我非常喜欢用 [RSS](https://www.benkuhn.net/index.xml) 获取我[最喜欢的作者](https://www.benkuhn.net/blogroll)的更新。但最近我注意到，我的 RSS feed 开始像 Hacker News 一样，让人忍不住分心去看。我依然受那个 [little red badge](https://www.benkuhn.net/product) 的摆布！

为了摆脱这种状态，我决定试着通过 [Kindle4RSS](http://kindle4rss.com/) 在我的 Kindle 上阅读 RSS feeds。Kindle4RSS 会把每天的文章 bundles up 成一期 Kindle “magazine issue”，每天在一个可以预期的时间发送给我。到目前为止，体验非常棒——simple，而且完全 non-addictive。[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/krss/#fn:1)

最有意思的是，我也变得更享受阅读了。在 Feedly 上，我很容易 tab away，然后分心；而在 Kindle 上，眼前的内容会得到我全部的注意力。这让我面对比如 [sam\[\]zdat](https://samzdat.com/) 写的大部头文章时，不那么容易望而却步。

一些随记：

-   你需要 premium version，才能每天 send articles（而不是 manually），或者拥有超过 25 个 feeds。对我来说，每月花“$2.9”非常值得。

-   它有个很棒的 feature，可以 extract 那些 summary-only feeds 的 full text，这样你就不必在糟糕的 Kindle browser 里阅读它们了。

-   你也可以直接在 Kindle4RSS site 上阅读 RSS feeds。好笑的是，它比几乎所有“modern web app”都快得多，也没那么复杂，以至于它或许同时也是最好的 web-based RSS reader。

-   听起来 Instapaper 也有类似的 feature，但用起来糟糕透顶。别试。[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/krss/#fn:2)

## 原文

I really enjoy using [RSS](https://www.benkuhn.net/index.xml) to get updates from my [favorite writers](https://www.benkuhn.net/blogroll). But recently I noticed that my RSS feed was starting to get the same distracting pull as Hacker News. I was still under the thumb of the [little red badge](https://www.benkuhn.net/product)!

To escape, I decided to experiment with reading RSS feeds on my Kindle via [Kindle4RSS](http://kindle4rss.com/). Kindle4RSS bundles up each day’s articles into a single Kindle “magazine issue” and sends it to me once a day at a predictable time. So far, it’s been really awesome—simple and completely non-addictive.[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/krss/#fn:1)

Most interestingly, I’ve also been appreciating the reading more. On Feedly it’s easy for me to tab away and get distracted; on a Kindle, everything has my full attention. It makes me a lot less likely to bounce off a doorstopper from, say, [sam\[\]zdat](https://samzdat.com/).

Random notes:

-   You need the premium version to send articles daily (vs manually) or to have over 25 feeds. It’s well worth the “$2.9” per month for me.

-   There’s a sweet feature where it can extract the full text of summary-only feeds so that you don’t have to read them in the crappy Kindle browser.

-   You can also read RSS feeds directly on the Kindle4RSS site. Hilariously, it’s so much faster and less complicated than almost every “modern web app” that it might be the best web-based RSS reader as well.

-   Instapaper sounds like they have a feature like this, but it works terribly. Don’t try.[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/krss/#fn:2)

* * *

1.  I also found myself really appreciating the Kindle4RSS app. The first time I used it I thought “man, this thing really just works!” And then, “man, I don’t remember the last time I said that about software with a graphical user interface!” It took me a while to figure out why, but then I realized that the site is _absurdly_ technically minimalist: a ~2002-era multi-page webapp with no images and no Javascript. And it’s _screaming_ fast. The initial load is under half a second… from Senegal. It’s enough to make me sad that I only have to use the site once every few weeks! [↩︎](https://www.benkuhn.net/krss/#fnref:1)

2.  Instapaper’s Kindle integration sends down every unread article every day. You can only mark articles as read by visiting a link. So if you read halfway through Monday’s articles, then _all_ of them will appear in Tuesday’s download too. Do this enough and you can get to a point where it’s impossible to mark anything as read without unread articles slipping through the cracks.

    Also, it randomly failed to actually deliver articles on many days and the support team acted like this was an unfortunate but normal thing to happen. [↩︎](https://www.benkuhn.net/krss/#fnref:2)
