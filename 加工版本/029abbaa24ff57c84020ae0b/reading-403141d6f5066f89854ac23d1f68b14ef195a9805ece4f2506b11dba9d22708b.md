# Effective altruism and growth

---

## 正文

有些人[认为](http://effective-altruism.com/ea/jb/solving_donation_coordination_problems/3wh)，effective altruist organizations“在 evaluating 上花的时间已经太多，而用于 growing 的时间还不够”。有些人读了[我关于待人友善的文章](http://www.benkuhn.net/welcoming)，担心对人过于友善会 dilute EA movement 的 quality，或者迫使我们 compromise 我们的 principles。这两种说法似乎有些矛盾。EAers 应该在多大程度上 prioritize 对 movement growth 的 explicit attempts？

在我看来，不应该太多，尤其是在需要以 evaluation 为代价的情况下。

首先，我们需要践行自己所宣扬的理念。effective altruism 对如何 improve the world 的 discourse 所作的 main contribution，一部分就在于：我们在做 altruism 时，应该更加 thoughtful、更加 careful，也更加 smarter。对 transparency、self-evaluation、thoughtfulness 和 caution 的追求，是这一主张不可或缺的一部分。

从这个意义上说，transparency 和 self-evaluation 甚至未必会以 growth 为代价：它们本来就是让 EA 具有吸引力的部分原因。GiveWell 之所以 appealed to 他们的 niche，并且 grew fast，_正是因为_他们惊人的 depth 和 thoroughness，而不是尽管如此仍能做到。他们几乎没有花时间在 growing 上，却取得了极大的成功：自成立以来，money moved 几乎每年都在 doubling。

更重要的是，我认为，出于 first-order reasons，transparency 和 self-criticism 仍然极其重要。它们是让我确信 effective altruism 长远来看会做出非凡之事的不可或缺的一部分。不仅仅是我们现在对如何 improve the world 有好的 ideas，更在于，当我们意识到自己正在做的事情并非 optimal 时，我们有能力、也有意愿去 _change our minds_——如果我们的做法是对的，这种情况就应该经常发生！

认为我们不再需要像以前那样关注 transparency 和 self-evaluation，是一种很有吸引力的想法，因为我们已经弄明白了许多关于如何 improve the world 的事情，而且还没有人针对 core EA arguments 提出非常 credible 或 convincing 的 attacks。认为我们应该从 [exploration mode 转入 exploitation mode](http://www.benkuhn.net/exploration)，也很诱人——也就是说，我们应该停止专注于_弄清楚该做什么_，开始针对_付诸行动_进行 optimize。

但我认为，这终究是一个诱人的错误。从全局来看，effective altruism 的 lifespan 仍处于非常早期的阶段。我们的 ideas 还有如此多的 improve 空间！如果我们还困在山丘顶上，远处仍有群山可以向上 optimize，却过早转入 exploitation，那将是极大的遗憾。

## 原文

Some folks [think](http://effective-altruism.com/ea/jb/solving_donation_coordination_problems/3wh) that effective altruist organizations “spend already too long evaluating and not enough time growing.” Some people read [my post on being welcoming](http://www.benkuhn.net/welcoming) and worried that being too welcoming to people would dilute the quality of the EA movement, or require us to compromise on our principles. These two statements seem to be somewhat in tension. How much should EAers prioritize explicit attempts at movement growth?

In my opinion, not much, especially if it comes at the expense of evaluation.

For one thing, we need to practice what we preach. Part of effective altruism’s main contribution to the discourse about how to improve the world is that we should be more thoughtful, more careful, and smarter about how we do our altruism. The drive towards transparency, self-evaluation, thoughtfulness, and caution is an integral part of this message.

In that sense, transparency and self-evaluation may not even come at the expense of growth: they’re part of what makes EA compelling in the first place. GiveWell appealed to their niche and grew fast _because_, not despite, their incredible depth and thoroughness. They’ve barely devoted any time to growing, yet they’ve been incredibly successful, nearly doubling in money moved every year since their founding.

But what’s more, I think transparency and self-criticism are still incredibly important for first-order reasons as well. They’re integral part of what makes me confident that effective altruism is going to do awesome things in the long term. It’s not just that we have good ideas for how to improve the world right now, it’s that we have the ability and desire to _change our minds_ when we realize the stuff that we’re doing isn’t optimal—which should happen often if we’re doing things right!

It’s compelling to think that we don’t need to focus on transparency and self-evaluation as much any more, because we’ve already figured out a ton about how to improve the world, and nobody has mounted very credible or convincing attacks on the core EA arguments. It’s tempting to think that we should shift from [exploration mode into exploitation mode](http://www.benkuhn.net/exploration)—that we should stop concentrating on _figuring out what to do_ and start to optimize for _doing it_.

But I think that’s ultimately a seductive error. In the grand scheme of things, we’re still very early in the lifespan of effective altruism. We have so much more room left to improve our ideas! It would be a terrible shame if we shifted prematurely into exploitation when we were still stuck at the top of the hill, when there are still mountains in the distance to optimize up.
