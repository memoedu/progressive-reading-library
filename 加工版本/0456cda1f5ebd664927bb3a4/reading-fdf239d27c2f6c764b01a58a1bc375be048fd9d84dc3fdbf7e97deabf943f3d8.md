# Reprogramming：目前唯一通向 systemic、permanent 的 rejuvenation 的 approach

---

## 正文

这篇文章的标题会引起一些争议。但在合理的理解下，它并没有错，只是有点辛辣 🌶️。

不妨先从一个或许争议较小的观点——“rapamycin 是一种 aging drug”——说起，并解释为什么这个说法也不对。

对于那些把 rapamycin 称为“(anti)aging drug”或“geroprotector”的人，我的问题在于，他们说的究竟是下面哪一种意思，往往并不清楚

1.  存在一个叫作 aging 的 unified 事物，它 affects 随 chronological time 而出现问题的一切。Rapamycin modulates 那一个杠杆，从而 improves health 和 lifespan。
2.  Rapamycin increases lifespan，并具有 broad health effects。aging drug 就是任何能广泛做到这一点的东西。

(2) 是一个完全 valid 的 statement。(1) 则已知是 false。但我觉得，阅读某些 rapamycin papers，或者有时阅读大众媒体对它们的报道时，很容易陷入 (1) 的思维框架。所以：rapamycin 在 (2) 的意义上是 aging drug，在 (1) 的意义上则不是 aging drug。我们应当更倾向于说“rapamycin slows aging 的某些 aspects”，而不是“rapamycin slows aging”或“rapamycin slows aging process”。

正如我将在下文提到的，rapamycin 并不只是让时钟倒转或停止。它不会把每一个 organ 都 resets 到若干年前的状态。Rapamycin 让某些 functions 保持 untouched，却让另一些变得 worse。

说到这里，有些人可能会说：“这不是当然的吗，aging 是 multifactorial，而 rapa 正在 addressing 它的一部分。”另一些人则可能会说：“异端！那些表明 rapa 并非对一切都有帮助的 papers，肯定是错了或者怎么回事。”第二种说法显然更像一种夸张 caricature。即便是那些主张把 rapamycin 视为 aging drug 的知名支持者，也认为它有 downsides 是理所当然的（[mouth ulcers](https://www.facebook.com/watch/?v=381108460841940) 在 humans 中已得到充分确认）。但这样一来，如果我们同意“rapamycin 只是 affecting aging 的某些 aspects”，那么在读到“rapamycin slows aging”这样的 claims 时，就必须非常谨慎，因为这听起来像是它在 slowing aging 的 _所有_ aspects，甚至可能意味着所有 aging 都是 mTOR-driven。

我怀疑，在一些人的概念框架中，rapamycin 与 aging 的紧密 relationship 可以追溯到这个 field 的起源。这个 field 正是从研究 caloric restriction 的 mTOR-IGF axis 起步的。CR 也具有 lifespan-increasing 和 broad health-promoting effects，因此，认为存在一个 regulating lifespan 和 health、并且与 mTOR 相联系的 master program，这种想法可能就源于此。例如，[Blagosklonny](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6084391/) 就提出了这种 mTOR maximalism 观点，但它并不是一个得到明确表达的多数派观点。

先简要介绍一下，[rapamycin](https://nintil.com/longevity/#:%7E:text=addressing%20competing%20diets.-,Rapamycin,-Among%20the%20longevity) 是一种 mTOR inhibitor，能够在常用的 model organisms——yeast、flies、worms 和 mice——中 extend lifespan。它的 lifespan-extending effects 或许是这个 field 中得到最 consistently replicated 的 findings 之一，在 Interventions Testing Program 中也取得了成功；这是一项使用 outbred mice、针对各种旨在 extend lifespan 的 interventions 开展的 large sample size study。

在 worms、yeasts 和 flies 中，rapamycin 似乎是由于一种 autophagy-inducing effect 而 extend lifespan（即它要发挥作用，依赖于 key autophagy-related genes 的存在）；而在 mice 中，还存在额外的 effects，可能包括 anti-inflammatory（Rubinsztein et al., [2011](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674\(11\)00828-2))，并且肯定包括 anti-cancer。除了活得更久，rapamycin 还 slows down muscle、tendons 或 liver 中各种 age-related phenotypes 的 appearence（Wilkinson et al., [2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1474-9726.2012.00832.x)）。

既然 rapamycin extends lifespan 并且 improves health，怎么还能说 rapamycin 不是 aging drug 呢？它难道没有在 impacting biological aging 吗？

> 我实在不理解这种观点。在 middle age 开始使用时，Rapamycin increases lifespan，并且在多个（每一个？）aged tissues 中 delays declines 或 improves function。除了 impacting biological aging，还能有什么 alternative explanation？
> 
> — Matt Kaeberlein (@mkaeberlein)，[2022年12月4日](https://twitter.com/mkaeberlein/status/1599238886395621377?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw)

首先，我们必须质疑这样一种叙述：rapamycin improves 作为 unified entity 的“health”。Rapamycin improves health 的某些 aspects，同时又 _worsens_ 另一些。Hallmarks of Aging paper 引用了前面提到的 Wilkinson paper，称“rapamycin delays mice 中 aging 的多个 aspects”。然而，它完全没有提到，那篇 paper 还说 rapamycin accelerates cataracts 的 appearence，并且 leads to testicular degeneration。在 humans 中，mouth ulcers 同样是一种常见的 observation（Peterson et al., [2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4971919/)）。考虑到每个人可能正在接受的其他 interventions，这些取舍对自己是否值得，应由各人自行判断。

在我看来，对 rapamycin 在 mice 中的 effects 所作的最 rigorous、最 comprehensive 的 examination，是 Dan Ehninger 在以下 papers 中完成的：

-   [Deep phenotyping 和 lifetime trajectories reveal longevity regulators 对 C57Bl/6J mice 中 aging process 的 limited effects](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34515-y) (2022)
-   [Longevity、aging 与 rapamycin](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00018-014-1677-1) (Ehninger et al., 2014)
-   [Rapamycin extends murine lifespan，但对 aging 的 effects 是 limited](https://dm5migu4zj3pb.cloudfront.net/manuscripts/67000/67674/JCI67674.v2.pdf) (Neff et al. 2013)

Ehninger 在这些 papers 中提出的总体观点是，rapamycin 在 mice 中 extending lifespan，部分是通过 cancer suppression 实现的；除此之外，它只对 age-related phenotypes 的一个 subset 具有 effects，而且它也会 improve young animals 中的 function。因此他声称（用他自己的话说），_目前几乎没有 evidence 可以 support mTOR inhibitors slow mammalian aging 的 rate 这一 notion_。Ehninger 承认，rapa 的 lifespan-extending effects _并不只是_ 源于 anti-cancer effect：在同样观察到 lifespan effects 的 yeast、flies 或 worms 中，cancer 并不是 cause of death。但 rapa 在这些 organisms 中 increases lifespan 的原因，可能不同于它在 mice 中 increases lifespan 的原因。

说得更清楚一些：要在 mice 中 extend lifespan，**最终就必须 slow down cancer，因为大多数 mice 都死于它**；mTOR inhibition 会以 indirectly（例如 inflammation reduction）或 directly（slowing down cancer 本身的 growth）的方式做到这一点。此外，还有一种独立的 autophagy-enhancing effect，它在 mortality 方面对 mice 的作用可能很小，但对 lower organisms 的作用却很大。

重要的是，在某个 organism 中 extend lifespan，可以有许多不同的方式。一种 intervention 是否正在 addressing aging，取决于你使用的 definition。也就是说，一种高度看重“lifespan extension”的 definition，会把 chemotherapy 和 statins classify 为 aging drugs，因为它们 address 随 age 而变得更 common 的 phenotypes（分别为 cancer 和 CVD），并因此 extend lifespan。

Ehninger 指出，如果一种 drug 在 young 和 old 中具有相同的 effect，那么它实际上并不是在 addressing aging，而是在通过 aging-independent mechanisms reversing age-related phenotypes。

![图片](https://nintil.com/images/2023-12-09-rapamycin-not-aging/FnQzz5vakAE3WmK.jpeg)

以 muscle mass 为例；在 development 之后，它会 declines。Reducing myostatin 的 activity 可以 increase muscle mass，而且这种 increase 在 young 和 old 中都可能发生。这种 treatment 或许能够 reverse old 中的 sarcopenia（即 shifting muscle mass %，使之更接近 young levels）。

这时有人可能会说：myostatin knockdown 并不真正是一种 aging intervention，因为它是在通过一个非常 proximate 的 muscle mass regulator，directly addressing 一种 disease（sarcopenia）。它并不是 systemic。对此，也可以回答：啊，但你知道吗，follistatin overexpression（它 reduces myostatin signalling）或许会 increases lifespan（Kumar et al., [2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.2121499119)）。或许 follistatin treatments 比人们最初想象的更 systemic！或许它是通过 cell-to-cell signalling 而 acting，而后者是一种 [hallmark of aging](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3836174/)。既然如此，follistatin 是否和 rapamycin 一样，也算是一种 anti-aging therapy？同样，这取决于你如何 define aging。我个人认为，不应纠缠于这个问题，而应关注什么有效、什么无效，才能以 sustained 的方式 improve healthy lifespan。

Rapamycin 的行家会说，在后一篇 paper（Neff）中，rapa dose 比其他研究使用的更低（[Johnson](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4510413/) et al., 2015），这可能就是 effects 没有那么 systemic 的原因。即便如此，我们仍然有其他研究发现的 negative effects，以及有时在 humans 中发现的 negative effects，例如 mouth ulcers。Ehninger 在后来的 2022 年 paper 中使用了一种 mTOR deficient mouse，其中 mice 会 constitutively produce 更少的 mTOR。这个 model recapitulates rapamycin 的已知 effects：lifespan extension (~20%)，同时伴随 broad health effects（[Wu](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211124713003926) et al. 2022）。Ehninger 的 paper measures 208 个 phenotypes，其中 117 个会随 age 而 change（age-sensitive phenotypes, ASP）。随后，他们根据这些 effects 是否具有 aging interaction（即在 aged animals 中 show an effect，而在 young animals 中没有），或者只有 genotype interaction（即 effect 在相同 genotype 的 aged 和 young 中都存在），来 classify 这些 effects。结果如下：

-   16% 的 ASPs 在 old 中得到 improved，但在 young 中没有（在 Ehninger 看来，这是“对 aging 的 true effect”）
-   31% 的 ASPs 在 old 和 young 中都得到 improved（在 Ehninger 看来，这是“health-promoting、aging-independent effect”）
-   36% 的 ASPs 在 mTOR-deficient genotype 下是 unchanged
-   13% 的 ASPs 被 mTOR-deficient genotype _made worse_

虽然我们可以争论他们 measuring 的具体 phenotypes，以及某个数字究竟是 15 还是 10%，但结合其他 papers 来看，在我看来，这确实是其他 papers 也支持的一个 robust finding：简单说，rapamycin extends lifespan，并且 improves healthspan 的 _某些_ aspects，同时又 worsening 另一些。

## Reprogramming：目前唯一通向 systemic、permanent 的 rejuvenation 的 approach

归根结底，rapamycin 是一种 small molecule drug。它能做的事情是有限的：它可以 bind FKBP12 并 jam mTOR，仅此而已。这又会 turns up autophagy 和其他有助于 general cellular health 的 cellular programs，从而带来 lifespan 和 healthspan 的 increases。但它也会 reduces protein synthesis 的 rate，并产生其他 negative effects。Rapamycin 并不是一种随 age 而 goes down 的 endogenous metabolite（[taurine](https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/taurine) 就属于这种情况），而是一种用来 crank up pathways 的 hack，这些 pathways 产生的 effects 大多是我们想要的。然而，rapamycin 的 effects 既不是 permanent 的，也不会在 cellular level 上 reverse aging，而是会将 aging 的某些 aspects slow 下来（[Kabacik](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-022-00220-0.pdf) et al. 2022; Horvath et al. [2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6555449/)）。我们主要应将 rapamycin 看作一种 transiently slow down aging 的某些 aspects 的方式，而不是通向 robust age reversal 的道路。在我看来，与其他选择相比，一种“更好的 rapamycin”并不特别值得追求。

请注意，这是一个 empirical observation：有可能某种 intervention，比如 boosts autophagy 的 intervention，会让 cell 在 garbage collection 上“赶上进度”，从而 permanently resetting 这个 cell 的 age。但实际上，rapamycin 似乎并不是在做这件事。它所做的是 stops 和 delays，而不是 repairs 和 reverses。

截至今天，据我们所知，唯一能够在 cellular level 上 permanently 且 systemically reverse aging 的 intervention（DNA mutations 等少数情况除外）是 [reprogramming](https://nintil.com/aging-solved-in-vitro/)。我希望自己在这一点上是错的：interventions 越多越好，所以如果你想到了什么能够 permanently reverse aging 的办法，请通过 [\[email protected\]](https://nintil.com/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection) 发给我。

与 targeting 各种 aging hallmarks 的 therapies 不同，reprogramming 是 permanent 的。一旦一个 cell 被 made into 一个 iPSC，它几乎在所有可能的方面都会 stays young；而一旦这个 cell 被 made back to 它的 type of origin，clock 就会被 reset，不再需要原来的 OSKM factors 继续 present。在一种非常严格的 definition 下，如果“age reversal”意味着每一样 measurable 的东西都已被 reverted back to 一个 young state，那么 DNA mutations 无法被 reprogramming 逆转这一 irreversibility 就会使它不符合要求。但在一种较宽松的“general reversal”definition 下，它无可匹敌。

reprogramming（或 partial reprogramming）的问题在于，它比 rapamycin 更难在 in vivo 中 control，因此 applying 这种 intervention 并不像给一些 mice 喂一份 [Eudragit](https://www.drugs.com/inactive/eudragit-28.html)\-coated 的 rapa 那么容易。要让它在那里发挥作用，还需要一些 engineering。另一种办法是，每次对一种 ex vivo cell types 进行 reprogram，然后将它们 engraft 回去，也就是将 fully rejuvenated cells engraft 回去。

但 hallmarks of aging 难道不是 connected 的吗？在某种程度上是，但并非 fully：根据前面提到的 Kabacik paper，rapamycin 的确 affects 其他 hallmarks。但我们已经知道，cells 中 epigenetic information 的 deterioraton 是 rapamycin 无法 reverse 的。在这些 hallmarks 中，有些比另一些更 upstream，而 rapamycin 正在 affecting 的那些，并不像 reprogramming 所 targets 的那些那么 root-cause-y。

## Systemic rejuvenation 还不够

即使 reprogramming 也不能做到一切。我认为，无论进行多少 reprograms，最终还是会得 cancer（毕竟，cancer 甚至能 overwhelm 一个 young immune system，看看 child leukemias 就知道了！）。因此，要让 healthy lifespan continued increases，还需要一系列非常有针对性地作用于我们 biology 的具体方面的东西：cancer therapies，包括像 [6-thio](https://maiabiotech.com/pipeline/thio/) 这样的 next gen chemotherapies 或 immunotherapies（比如我们自己的 [T-cell therapy](https://youtu.be/iOvVMow4jJU?si=SrPQaiP4eOwu2hiA&t=405)！）；amyloid plaque targeting therapies（如果 cellular rejvenation 无法 clear 它）；像 [Repair](https://www.repairbiotechnologies.com/) Bio 的那种 atherosclerosis targeting therapies；用于 add 已经 lost 的 new cells 的 therapies（已经不存在的东西是无法 rejuvenate 的！）；以及可能的其他办法。

## 最后再谈几句 rapamycin-like drugs

虽然我把时间花在思考未来以及 aging 问题的 comprehensive solutions 上，但在较短期内，有些 drugs 或许可以服用，并有可能帮助人们在当下活得更久、更健康。rapamycin 有 downsides，也有 upsides；对某些处于某些情境中的人来说，它可能值得使用，而我也欢迎针对这一个 molecule 开展更多 humans 中的 trials。

## 原文

The title of this post will spark some controversy. But it is, under a reasonable interpretation, not false, just spicy 🌶️.

It make sense to start from the perhaps less controversial view that "rapamycin is an aging drug" and explain why that is wrong as well.

My issue with those that talk about rapamycin as an "(anti)aging drug", or a "geroprotector" is that it's often unclear what of this is being said

1.  There's a unified thing called aging that affects everything that goes wrong with chronological time. Rapamycin modulates that one lever and as a result improves health and lifespan.
2.  Rapamycin increases lifespan and has broad health effects. An aging drug is anything that broadly does this.

(2) is a perfectly valid statement to make. (1) is known to be false. But I think it's easy to fall into the (1) frame of thinking when reading some of the rapamycin papers, or sometimes popular press coverage thereof. So: rapamycin is an aging drug in the sense of (2) and it is not an aging drug in the sense of (1). We should prefer to say that "rapamycin slows some aspects of aging" than "rapamycin slows aging" or "rapamycin slows the aging process".

As I will mention below, rapamycin does not simply turn back or stop the clock. It doesn't take every organ and resets it by some number of years. Rapamycin leaves some functions untouched and makes others worse.

Now at this point some may go "duh of course, aging is multifactorial and rapa is addressing parts of it" whereas other might go "Heresy! Surely the papers that show that rapa doesn't help with everything must be wrong or something". The second is more clearly a caricature. Even well known proponent of rapamycin as an aging drug take it as a given that it has its downsides (the [mouth ulcers](https://www.facebook.com/watch/?v=381108460841940) are well established in humans). But then, if we agree that "rapamycin is just affecting some aspects of aging" then we have to be very careful when reading claims that "rapamycin slows aging" as that sounds like it is slowing _all_ aspects of aging and perhaps even that all aging is mTOR-driven.

I suspect that the intimate relationship between rapamycin and aging in some's conceptual schemes dates back to the origins of the field. The mTOR-IGF axis is where the field was born, studying caloric restriction. CR also has lifespan-increasing and broad health-promoting effects, so the idea that there was a master program regulating lifespan and health and that this is tied to mTOR can be coming from there. This mTOR maximalism view is presented by eg. [Blagosklonny](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6084391/) but it is not an explicit majority view.

As a brief introduction, [rapamycin](https://nintil.com/longevity/#:%7E:text=addressing%20competing%20diets.-,Rapamycin,-Among%20the%20longevity) is an mTOR inhibitor that is able to extend lifespan in the usual model organisms: yeast, flies, worms, and mice. Its lifespan-extending effects are perhaps one of the most consistently replicated findings in the field, showing success in the Interventions Testing Program, a large sample size study using outbred mice of various interventions aiming to extend lifespan.

In worms, yeasts, and flies, rapamycin seems to extend lifespan because of an autophagy-inducing effect (i.e. it depends on the presence of key autophagy-related genes to work) whereas in mice there are additional effects present, possibly anti-inflammatory (Rubinsztein et al., [2011](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674\(11\)00828-2)) and surely anti-cancer. In addition to living longer, rapamycin slows down the appearence of various age-related phenotypes in muscle, tendons or liver (Wilkinson et al., [2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1474-9726.2012.00832.x)).

Given that rapamycin extends lifespan and improves health, how could one possibly say that rapamycin is not an aging drug? Is it not impacting biological aging?

> I honestly don't understand this view. Rapamycin increases lifespan and delays declines or improves function in multiple (every?) aged tissues when started in middle age. What is the alternative explanation other than impacting biological aging?
> 
> — Matt Kaeberlein (@mkaeberlein) [December 4, 2022](https://twitter.com/mkaeberlein/status/1599238886395621377?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw)

First, one has to question the narrative that rapamycin improves "health" as a unified entity. Rapamycin improves some aspects of health and _worsens_ others. The Hallmarks of Aging paper cites the Wilkinson paper from earlier to say that "rapamycin delays multiple aspects of aging in mice". However it completely fails to mention that the paper also says that rapamycin accelerates the appearence of cataracts and leads to testicular degeneration. In humans, mouth ulcers are a common observation as well (Peterson et al., [2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4971919/)). It's up to each individual to judge if the traeoffs will be worth to them, given other interventions they may be undergoing.

In my view the most rigorous and comprehensive examination of the effects of rapamycin in mice has been done by Dan Ehninger in these papers:

-   [Deep phenotyping and lifetime trajectories reveal limited effects of longevity regulators on the aging process in C57Bl/6J mice](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-34515-y) (2022)
-   [Longevity, aging, and rapamycin](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00018-014-1677-1) (Ehninger et al., 2014)
-   [Rapamycin extends murine lifespan but has limited effects on aging](https://dm5migu4zj3pb.cloudfront.net/manuscripts/67000/67674/JCI67674.v2.pdf) (Neff et al. 2013)

The overall point made by Ehninger in these papers is that rapamycin is extending lifespan in mice in part via cancer suppression, and that in addition to that, it is having effects on only a subset of age-related phenotypes and furthermore that it does also improve function in young animals so he claims that (his words) _there is currently little evidence available to support the notion that mTOR inhibitors slow the rate of mammalian aging_. Ehninger acknowledges that the lifespan-extending effects of rapa are _not just_ due to the anti-cancer effect: cancer is not a cause of death in yeast, flies, or worms, where lifespan effects are also seen. But it may be that the reason that rapa increases lifespan in those is different from the reason it increases lifespan in mice.

To make this more clear: To extend lifespan in mice **one must ultimately slow down cancer because most mice die of that**, which mTOR inhibition does either indirectly (inflammation reduction, for example) or directly (slowing down growth of the cancer itself). Then there's a separate autophagy-enhancing effect which may be doing little for the mice in terms of mortality, but a lot for the lower organisms.

Importantly, it is possible to extend lifespan in a given organism in many ways. Whether an intervention is addressing aging depends on the definition you are using. That is, a definition that puts a heavy weight on "lifespan extension" will classify chemotherapy and statins as a aging drugs in that they address phenotypes that are more common with age (cancer and CVD respectively) and they extend lifespan as a result.

Ehninger points out that if a drug is having the same effect in young than it does in old, it is not really addressing aging but rather reversing age-related phenotypes via aging-independent mechanisms.

![Image](https://nintil.com/images/2023-12-09-rapamycin-not-aging/FnQzz5vakAE3WmK.jpeg)

Take muscle mass; after development it declines. Reducing the activity of myostatin can increase muscle mass, and this increase can happen in both young and old. This treatment could plausibly reverse sarcopenia in the old (ie shifting muscle mass % closer to young levels).

Now one might say: A myostatin knockdown is not really an aging intervention because it's addressing one disease directly (sarcopenia) through a very proximate regulator of muscle mass. It is not systemic. To which one could say: ah but did you know follistatin overexpression (which reduces myostatin signalling) perhaps increases lifespan (Kumar et al., [2022](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.2121499119)). Perhaps follistatin treatments are more systemic than one thought at first! Perhaps it's acting through cell-to-cell signalling which is a [hallmark of aging](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3836174/). Given this, is follistatin as much of an anti-aging therapy as rapamycin is? Again, depends on how you define aging. I personally think one should not obsess over this question, but rather about what works and what doesn't to improve healthy lifespan in a sustained way.

Rapamycin connoiseurs will say that in the latter paper (Neff), the rapa dose was lower than what has been used elsewhere ([Johnson](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4510413/) et al., 2015) and that this might by why the effects are not as systemic. Even with this, we still have the negative effects found elsewhere and found in humans sometimes like mouth ulcers. Ehninger uses in the later 2022 paper an mTOR deficient mouse, where mice constitutively produce less mTOR. This model recapitulates known effects of rapamycin: lifespan extension (~20%) coupled with broad health effects ([Wu](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2211124713003926) et al. 2022). The Ehninger paper measures 208 phenotypes out of which 117 change with age (age-sensitive phenotypes, ASP). They then classify the effects according to whether they have an aging interaction (ie they show an effect in aged but not young animals) or only a genotype interaction (if the effect is present in both aged and young of the same genotype). The result here:

-   16% of ASPs are improved in old but not young ("true effect on aging" in Ehninger's view)
-   31% of ASPs are improved in both old and young ("health-promoting, aging-independent effect" in Ehninger's view)
-   36% of ASPs are unchanged by the mTOR-deficient genotype
-   13% of ASPs were _made worse_ by the mTOR-deficient genotype

While one can quibble with the exact phenotypes they are measuring and whether a number is 15 or 10%, given other papers, it does seem a robust finding to me from other papers: put it simply rapamycin extends lifespan and improves _some_ aspects of healthspan while worsening some others.

## Reprogramming as only current approach to systemic, permanent, rejuvenation

Ultimately rapamycin is a small molecule drug. What it can do is limited: It can bind FKBP12 and jam mTOR and that's it. That in turn turns up autophagy and other cellular programs that contribute to general cellular health, leading to increases in lifespan and healthspan. But it also reduces the rate of protein synthesis and has other negative effects. Rapamycin is not an endogenous metabolite that goes down with age (the case of [taurine](https://www.science.org/content/blog-post/taurine)), but rather a hack to crank up pathways that have effects that we mostly want. However, the effects of rapamycin are not permanent nor they reverse aging at the cellular level, rather they slow some aspects of aging down ([Kabacik](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43587-022-00220-0.pdf) et al. 2022; Horvath et al. [2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6555449/)). We should think of rapamycin mostly as a way to slow down aspects of aging transiently, but not as a path towards robust age reversal. A "better rapamycin" in my view, is not particularly worth seeking, compared to alternatives.

Note that this is an empirical observation: It could be the case that an intervention that say boosts autophagy lets the cell "catch up" on garbage collection, resetting the age of the cell permanently. But in practice, this is not what rapamycin seems to be doing. It stops and delays, not repairs and reverses.

The only intervention that can permanently and systemically reverse aging at a cellular level (with minor exceptions like DNA mutations) that we know of as of today is [reprogramming](https://nintil.com/aging-solved-in-vitro/). I'd like to be wrong on this one: more interventions are better so if you have something in mind that can reverse aging permanently, send it my way at [\[email protected\]](https://nintil.com/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection) .

Unlike therapies targeting various aging hallmarks, reprogramming is permanent. Once a cell has been made into an iPSC, it stays young in almost every possible way, and once the cell is made back to its type of origin, the clock is reset, without the original OSKM factors having to be present anymore. Under a very strict definition where "age reversal" means that every measurable thing has been reverted back to a young state, the irreversibility by reprogramming of DNA mutations would disqualify it. But under a more relaxed "general reversal" definition, it is second to none.

The issue with reprogramming (or partial reprogramming) is that it's harder to control in vivo than rapamycin so applying the intervention is not as easy as giving some mice an [Eudragit](https://www.drugs.com/inactive/eudragit-28.html)\-coated serving of rapa. It's going to take some engineering to make it work there. Alternatively, one can reprogram ex vivo cell types one at a time and then engraft them back, fully rejuvenated cells.

But aren't the hallmarks of aging connected? Yes to some extent but not fully: per the Kabacik paper earlier rapamycin indeed affects others. But we already know the deterioraton of epigenetic information in cells is something rapamycin does not reverse. Within the hallmarks, some are more upstream than others, and that which rapamycin is affecting is not as root-cause-y as that which reprogramming targets.

## Systemic rejuvenation is not enough

Even reprogramming doesn't do everything. I think no matter how much one reprograms one will eventually get cancer (cancer can overwhelm even a young immune system after all, see child leukemias!). Thus there are a series of things that are required for continued increases in healthy lifespan that are very targeted to specific ways to our biology: cancer therapies being next gen chemotherapies like [6-thio](https://maiabiotech.com/pipeline/thio/) or immunotherapies (Like our own [T-cell therapy](https://youtu.be/iOvVMow4jJU?si=SrPQaiP4eOwu2hiA&t=405)!), amyloid plaque targeting therapies (if cellular rejvenation can't clear that), atherosclerosis targeting therapies like [Repair](https://www.repairbiotechnologies.com/) Bio's, therapies to add new cells that were lost (Can't rejuvenate what's not there anymore!) and potentially more.

## One last word on rapamycin-like drugs

Though I spend my time thinking about the future and comprehensive solutions to the aging problem, in the shorter term there are drugs that potentially one could take that could help one live longer healthier lives here and now. With its downsides and upsides, rapamycin might be worth using for some people in some situations, and more trials of this one molecule in humans are something I welcome.
