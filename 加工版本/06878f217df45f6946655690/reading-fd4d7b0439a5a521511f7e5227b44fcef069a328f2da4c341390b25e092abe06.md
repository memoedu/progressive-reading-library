## 正文

\[Epistemic status：Possible（认识状态：可能）\]

nuclear chain reaction 的想法大约出现在 1930-40 年，第一个 reacton（Chicago Pile）于 1942 年出现，其 design 非常简陋。后来，1956 年位于 [Calder Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sellafield#Calder_Hall_nuclear_power_station) 的一座 small（按今天的标准而言）plant，以及早几年位于 Obninsk 的一座更具 experimental 性质的 plant，成为最早为 civilian purposes 提供 power 的 nuclear fission plants。

从那以后，reactors 的 fundamental physics 并没有新的 advances 发生改变。Fundemental physics 后来有所 advanced，但比如说，quarks 的 discovery 并不 impact nuclear reactor design。reactors 的 Advances 来自对相关 systems 的 higher-level study，以及 improved engineering。另一方面，reactor design 的 progress 一直受到人们对 nuclear energy 的普遍恐惧的 hampered，也受到这些项目是 highly capital intensive projects 这一事实的 hampered，这 reduces 了 learning by doing 的 potential gains。其他 factors 也可能在起作用，因为各国的 construction costs 曾 varied wildly：在 USA，一次 sharp decrease 之后出现了 substantial increase，时间大约在 Three Mile Island accident 前后，以至于到 1978 年，最后一座 nuclear reactor 已经 built（但有[这个](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts_Bar_Nuclear_Plant)值得称道的例外）。不过在 South Korea，costs 一直呈 steady decline（[Lovering, Yip, and Nordhaus, 2016](http://sci-hub.tw/http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301421516300106)）

> 最令人惊讶的 feature 是 trends 的 large diversity，其中 US 和 South Korea 位于两个 extremes。较晚开始 building reactors 的国家，尤其是 construction starts 在 1980 年之后的国家，其 trend shapes 与早期的 nuclear pioneers 不同。显然，nuclear technology 并不存在 singular cost trend，而是存在多种不同的 country-specific experiences；并非 nuclear technology 所 intrinsic 的 complexity 会“inevitable”地 increases，从而 lead to cost escalation，或是“invariable exhibition of negative learning”（Grubler, 2010）。Grubler（2010）所提出的 cost escalation 的一致“rhythm”并不 match historical record

这些 changes 看来很可能并不与对 safety 的担忧 tightly related。comparatively 最不 safe 的那个 reactor type（RBMK design）只在 Russia 使用，而且自 USSR 解体以来没有新的此类 reactor 被 built。普遍并不认为 South Korea 各地使用的 designs 更不 safe。

如果你相信了上面的描述，那你并不孤单，因为我最初也觉得它说得通：先有一个悲观的 initial prior（secular cost increase），随后转向 nuanced view（并非到处都必须如此！），听起来不错；但我刚刚 cited 的 study 存在几个 issues，[Koomey, Hultman and Grubler (2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301421516301549) 指出了这些问题，包括 flawed cost metric——它没有 take into accont 漫长 construction period 中的 interest accred——以及过于 heterogeneous、无法 compare 的 data sources。并不是说它必定全错，但第二篇 paper 的作者提醒我们要谨慎。例如，Korean reactor data 来自该国 nuclear operator 的 unaudited data。

原 paper 的作者作出了回应（[Lovering et al., 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Jessica_Lovering/publication/310814563_Apples_and_oranges_Comparing_nuclear_construction_costs_across_nations_time_periods_and_technologies/links/59dfe4cc0f7e9bc51256c828/Apples-and-oranges-Comparing-nuclear-construction-costs-across-nations-time-periods-and-technologies.pdf)），arguing 说，Korean data 还与两个 independent Korean public bodies 以及 International Atomic Energy Agency 持有的 data 进行了 cross-checked。他们还为自己的 cost metric 辩护，arguing 说，纳入 financial considerations 将会 bias 他们的 estimates，因为一个机构是 public 还是 private，或处于较稳健还是较不稳健的 financial position，都会 affect 其 loans 所收取的 interest rates。他们说，自己的 results 或许不适合用于 compare 各国的 absolute values，但适合用于 assess 各国内部的 trends。即使 Korean data 是 biased，只要 bias 是 constant，observed trend 就会 match real trend。最后，他们公开了自己的 data。

总之，measuring costs 比人们想象的更困难！

如果不看金钱，而看 performance，即便在 US，capacity factor 也显示出 improvement，因为现有 plants improved procesures，并进行了 retrofitted。capacity factor 是一座 plant 的 actual energy produced 与该 plant 假如一年都以 100% capacity 运行所能 produced 的 total energy 之间的 ratio。100% 几乎是 unreachable，因为 plant 必须停下来 refuel 或进行 maintenance。（[Koomey and Hultman, 2007](http://sci-hub.tw/http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301421507002558)）

还要记住：US 到处都是老旧的 reactors！并不是因为它们使用了最新的 designs，这个值才那么 high。（[World Nuclear Association, 2018](http://www.world-nuclear.org/getmedia/b392d1cd-f7d2-4d54-9355-9a65f71a3419/performance-report.pdf.aspx)）。该 report 还提到，随着 reactor age 的 increasing，capacity factors 仍是 relatively constant。

至于 construction times，以 Pressurised Water Reactor（PWR，与 Boiling Water Reactors，即 BWR，并列为最常见的 design types 之一）来看，历史上它们似乎一直 constant 在大约 10 年，但如今已 decreased 到原来的 more than half。不过，这很可能是 compostition effect：随着 US built 的 reactors 更少，而其他国家 build 的更多，global average construction times 已经 gone down。（[Moreira et al, 2013](http://sci-hub.tw/http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S030142151201097X)）。

![2018-10-05 22-12-19 的屏幕截图.png](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2018/10/06-5.png)

一个值得考察的有趣 engineering metric 是，各国的 energy extracted per gram of uranium 如何随时间变化，但我没能找到它。因此，对于 overall end-to-end operational efficiency，我们只好采用 cost of energy produced。但这也有自身的问题：[Cost 看来基本上是在 paying back interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics_of_nuclear_power_plants#Capital_costs)，再加上 fuel costs，而 fuel costs——buying uranium——并不是 plant 能够 controled 的。

至少[在 US](https://www.nei.org/CorporateSite/media/filefolder/resources/fact-sheets/nuclear-by-the-numbers-20180412.pdf)，nuclear power 的 electricity costs 曾 gone up，随后又 down，但仍然 over 它们的 peak。人们或许会认为，即将出现的一批新 reactor designs（[Generation III 及以上](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_III_reactor)）将进一步 bring down costs。但现在下结论还为时过早。目前我们知道，Westinghouse 的 AP1000 reactor 本应更 cheaper，并以 pre-fababricated modules 来 built，最终却出现了极其巨大的 cost overruns，以至于让它们 [bankrupted](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-toshiba-accounting-westinghouse-nucle/how-two-cutting-edge-u-s-nuclear-projects-bankrupted-westinghouse-idUSKBN17Y0CQ)。

如今的 Nuclear fission 看起来像是新的 vacuum tube，将被一种在相同 fuction 上 replaces 它的新 technology 所 superseded。那可能是 nuclear fusion；如果当前的 cost decrease trends 持续，而且 multijunction cells 能够被制造得比现在更 cheaper，也可能是 solar。

总之，nuclear 的案例清楚表明，institutional factors 会影响一项 innovation 随时间如何发展。甚至有[一篇 paper](https://afinetheorem.wordpress.com/2011/11/16/nuclear-power-reactors-a-study-in-technological-lock-in-r-cowan-1990/) argued，nuclear energy 的整个 landscape 之所以呈现如今的样子，是因为 institutional reasons（military secrecy 和 subsidies）locked-in 了某个特定的 design family（PWR 和 BWR）。此外，organisational changes 以及对现有 infrastructure 的 tweaks，已经能够通过 increasing capacity factor，从相同 reactors 中 squeeze 出几乎 2x 的 power。Thi

space 的情况可能也曾如此（Cost-plus payments，以及 large military satellites 所要求的 large launchers 的需求），直到 SpaceX 出现。对于 nuclear fusion，类似的历史可能[也](https://www.nature.com/news/fusion-furore-1.15596)[发生过](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10894-015-0053-y)。

## 原文

\[Epistemic status: Possible\]

The idea of a nuclear chain reaction arose around 1930-40, the first reacton (Chicago Pile), a very barebones design, in 1942. Later, in 1956, a small (by today's standards) plant at [Calder Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sellafield#Calder_Hall_nuclear_power_station) and a more experimental plant at Obninsk some years earlier were the first nuclear fission plants providing power for civilian purposes.

Since then, no new advances in the fundamental physics of reactors have changed. Fundemental physics advanced later, but the discovery of, say, quarks does not impact nuclear reactor design. Advances in reactors have come through higher-level study of the relevant systems, and improved engineering. And on the other hand, progress in reactor design has been hampered by general fear of nuclear energy, and the fact that these are highly capital intensive projects, which reduces the potential gains from learning by doing. Other factors may have been at play, as construction costs have varied wildly between countries: In the USA a sharp decrease was followed by a substantial increase, around the time of the Three Mile Island accident, so that by 1978 the last nuclear reactor was built (With [this](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watts_Bar_Nuclear_Plant) honorable exception). But in South Korea, costs have followed a steady decline ([Lovering, Yip, and Nordhaus, 2016](http://sci-hub.tw/http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301421516300106))

![Captura de pantalla de 2018-10-05 20-49-05.png](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2018/10/06-1.png)

All together, the picture looks like this:

![Captura de pantalla de 2018-10-05 20-54-34.png](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2018/10/06-2.png)

> The most surprising feature is the large diversity in trends, with the US and South Korea at the two extremes. Countries building reactors more recently, particularly those with construction starts after 1980, have different trend shapes than the early nuclear pioneers. Rather than an “invariable exhibition of negative learning” and “inevitable” increases in complexity intrinsic to nuclear technology that lead to cost escalation (Grubler, 2010), it is clear that there is not a singular cost trend for nuclear technology, but a plurality of different country-specific experiences. A consistent “rhythm” of cost escalation suggested by Grubler (2010) does not match the historical record

Most likely, these changes do not seem tightly related to concerns for safety. The one reactor type that is comparatively the least safe (the RBMK design) is used only in Russia and no new ones have been built since the fall of the USSR. The designs used through South Korea are not generally believed to be less safe.

If you believed the picture above, you are in good company because it also made sense to me initially: initial prior of doom (secular cost increase) followed by a nuanced view (it doesn't have to be that way everywhere!) sound nice, but the study I just cited has several issues pointed out in [Koomey, Hultman and Grubler (2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301421516301549), including a flawed cost metric, one that doesn't take into accont interest accred during the lengthy construction period,  and data sources too heterogeneous too compare. Is not that it has to be all false, but the author of this second paper tells us to be careful. The Korean reactor data comes from unaudited data from the country's nuclear operator, for example.

The authors of the original paper replied ([Lovering et al., 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Jessica_Lovering/publication/310814563_Apples_and_oranges_Comparing_nuclear_construction_costs_across_nations_time_periods_and_technologies/links/59dfe4cc0f7e9bc51256c828/Apples-and-oranges-Comparing-nuclear-construction-costs-across-nations-time-periods-and-technologies.pdf)), arguing that the Korean data was also cross-checked against data held by two independent Korean public bodies, plus the International Atomic Energy Agency. They also defended their cost metric, arguing that including financial considerations will bias their estimates, as whether a body is public or private or is on a more or less solid financial position will affect the interest rates charged to the loans they take. Their results, they say, may not be useful to compare absolute values across countries, but they are useful to assess trends within countries. Even if the Korean data is biased, if the bias is constant, the observed trend will match the real trend. Lastly, they made their data available.

In sum, measuring costs is more difficult than one might think!

Looking not at money, but at performance, the capacity factor shows improvement even in the US, as existing plants improved procesures and were retrofitted. The capacity factor is the ratio of the actual energy produced by a plant over the total energy the plant could have produced over a year had it been running at 100% capacity. 100% is almost unreachable because the plant has to stop to refuel, or for maintenance. ([Koomey and Hultman, 2007](http://sci-hub.tw/http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0301421507002558))

![Captura de pantalla de 2018-10-05 21-08-45.png](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2018/10/06-3.png)

These values also vary by region:

![Captura de pantalla de 2018-10-05 21-50-38](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2018/10/06-4.png)

And remember: the US is full of old reactors! It's not like it's that high because they are using the latest designs. ([World Nuclear Association, 2018](http://www.world-nuclear.org/getmedia/b392d1cd-f7d2-4d54-9355-9a65f71a3419/performance-report.pdf.aspx)). The report also mentions that capacity factors are relatively constant with increasing reactor age.

As for construction times, considering Pressurised Water Reactor (PWR, one of the most common design types along with Boiling Water Reactors, BWR) historically they have seemed to be constant at around 10 years, but have now decreased to more than half of that. However, this is likely a compostition effect: As the US has built less reactors and other countries build more, global average construction times have gone down. ([Moreira et al, 2013](http://sci-hub.tw/http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S030142151201097X)).

![Captura de pantalla de 2018-10-05 22-12-19.png](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2018/10/06-5.png)

One interesting engineering metric to look at would be energy extracted per gram of uranium over time by country, but I couldn't find that. Instead, for overall end-to-end operational efficiency, we'll have to take the cost of energy produced. But that has its own issues: [Cost seems to be basically paying back interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics_of_nuclear_power_plants#Capital_costs) plus fuel costs, and fuel costs -buying uranium- is something that cannot be controled by the plant.

[In the US](https://www.nei.org/CorporateSite/media/filefolder/resources/fact-sheets/nuclear-by-the-numbers-20180412.pdf) at least electricity costs for nuclear power have gone up and then down, but still over their peak. One might think that the bunch of new reactor designs coming up ([Generation III and above](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generation_III_reactor)) will bring down costs even more. But it's too early to tell. For now we know that Westinghouse's AP1000 reactor, which was supposed to be cheaper and built in pre-fababricated modules ended up in cost overruns so massive that [bankrupted](https://www.reuters.com/article/us-toshiba-accounting-westinghouse-nucle/how-two-cutting-edge-u-s-nuclear-projects-bankrupted-westinghouse-idUSKBN17Y0CQ) them.

Nuclear fission as of today seems like the new vacuum tube, to be superseded by a new technology that replaces it in the same fuction. That may be nuclear fusion, or solar if the current cost decrease trends continue, and if multijunction cells can be made cheaper than they currently are.

In sum, the case of nuclear is a clear case of institutional factors at play in how an innovation plays out over time. There is even[a paper](https://afinetheorem.wordpress.com/2011/11/16/nuclear-power-reactors-a-study-in-technological-lock-in-r-cowan-1990/) that has argued that the whole landscape of nuclear energy looks the way it does because of institutional reasons (military secrecy and subsidies) that locked-in a particular design family (PWR and BWR). Furthermore, organisational changes and tweaks to existing infrastructure have been able to squeeze almost 2x the power out of the same reactors by increasing the capacity factor. Thi

The same might have been true for space (Cost-plus payments and the need for large launchers required by large military satellites) until SpaceX came in. And for nuclear fusion a similar history may have [also](https://www.nature.com/news/fusion-furore-1.15596) [happened](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10894-015-0053-y).
