# Slutcon, or the attractiveness of embodied authenticity

---

## 正文

我喜欢住在湾区的原因之一，是这里有在其他地方很少能遇到的社交聚会。我最近参加的一场就是 _[Slutcon](https://www.slutcon.com/)_（关于这场活动的更多内容见[这里](https://aella.substack.com/p/the-vision-of-slutcon)）。第一次在 twitter 上看到活动公告时，我毫不犹豫地买了票。我奉行的一条 heuristic 是：像自己是某部小说的主角那样生活，去做既符合整体故事走向、读起来又有趣的事。而这一次，情节要求我去。

我不想按小时写一篇活动流水账，而更想琢磨一下我认为贯穿全程、最突出的共同线索：embodied authenticity 或 self-acceptance。

在其中一场讨论中，一位名叫 I. 的女性熟人说，她讨厌男人仅仅为了取悦她，就把自己扭曲成某种样子（这是比喻），也讨厌他们试图摸清该按她身上的哪些按钮，好让她做他们想要的事。她说这话时，我心想：“就是这个！”我能理解这为什么会让她不舒服，也能理解为什么许多男人不明白这有什么问题。她的话让我想起组织者之一 Aella 过去[写过](https://aella.substack.com/p/the-difficulty-in-dating-good-men)的一些内容：

> 但我的 body 不喜欢他们。一个男人谈起自己的失败时，那种语气暗示着他对自己感到 uncomfortable，仿佛在内心深处，他的一部分相信自己是个坏人；而他许多寻求 social approval 的举动，似乎都是为了得到 reassurance，确认自己其实是 okay 的。$$...$$
> 
> 另一个男人……我不太确定他究竟有什么问题，但他似乎会围着我扭曲自己。他赞同我说的话，速度有点太快了。我一讲笑话，他立刻就笑。他的手因 nervous energy 而抽动。他看起来人挺好，但似乎很怕我，又像是在花极大的力气，让自己显得并不怕我。他的 body tension 让我想起自己上那些 high-pressure public shows 时的感觉：我不想让别人知道我_此刻真的很害怕_。我感觉自己的存在仿佛凌驾于他之上，不得不小心翼翼地对待他，仿佛只要我说得太坦白，他就会在我手里碎掉。

就在同一场讨论里，大家还谈到了“男人问女人的问题不够多”这个话题（我链接的那篇文章里也谈到了）。那一刻我也在想：是啊，但许多女人也喜欢男人在谈论某个 technical topic 时表现出 competence（而在 infodumping 发生的那些时刻，这与 asking questions 是 at odds 的）。西方女人啊，你到底想要哪一种？

我个人更偏向“我喜欢 asking questions”这一边（我的意思是，我的默认生存状态就是对世界感到 confused/wonder，不断 generating 和 answering questions，再在这个博客里写下来，哈哈）。在活动上，我对某个人说下面这句话时，是有几分认真的：“我想像你了解自己一样深入地了解你，却不拿关于我自己的任何东西作为回报（因为我已经讲过很多次了，觉得这件事没那么有趣，但我很喜欢了解别人！）”

有一次在派对上，我不停地向一个叫 K. 的女孩抛出关于她的问题。我开玩笑说，这感觉一定像面试，又问她是否享受这个过程（她是享受的）。但我也能想象，在别的情境下，另一个人可能只想听我就自己熟悉的话题侃侃而谈。不过，这似乎就像在问：跳舞时，下一步该是 X 还是 Y？在当下，这个问题有一个 skillful 的答案，但人无法提前预见。Conversations 是 improvisational 的，所以你必须“read the vibe”：如果一个笑话没有奏效，那就是 information；如果对方看起来 bored，那就是 information；如果对方显得 enthusiastic，那也是 information。理论上，一个人可以在心里运行一张 internal lookup table，比如“如果对方 enthusiastic，就记下我刚才在做什么，再做一次”，但聪明的女人会看穿这一点。

相反，正确的做法是_什么也不做_。如果你曾尝试深入 meditation，就会更容易理解这一点：其中有些 states 是你起初想要 reach 的，但如果在试图 achieve 它们的过程中，你非常 crave 它们，就无法 achieve。到了 retreat 之后，如果你一心想要 jhana，就无法 [jhana](https://nintil.com/jhanas)；但你可以决定去参加 retreat，从而 increase 达成那个 goal 的 odds。你必须相信，只要你允许它们到来，它们就会到来；但即使它们不来，你也要从内心深处感到 okay。

本质上，如果你 trust yourself（或者是“all the way down”地 fully self-accepting），这就会 reflected 在 body language、言语和 actions 中。在前面的例子里，一个对 being single 根本上感到 insecure 的人，不会对 being rejected 从内心深处感到 okay；而这种 okayness 的缺失，又会 emits 一种糟糕的 needy vibe，让这个人继续 single（此处请出 Joe Hudson 的 [golden algorithm](https://x.com/FU_joehudson/status/1917987459809648668)）。如果一个人有点 on the spectrum，或是 disembodied，情况就更糟，因为这样一来，你自己不会察觉。

有人可能会回答：“但我想要一个女朋友！你是说，只要我不再想要，就会有了吗？这也太荒唐了！”我并不完全是这个意思；更准确地说，我是说，craving 它才是让你一直 single 的原因。在跳舞的例子里，想象一个人真的、真的、真的、真的很想做这些 dips 中的[一个](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7VKGIVpxms)，但 follow 不愿意，也许觉得 unsafe。一个好的 dancer 会 pick up 那种 vibe，转而做别的动作。他们内心深处确实想做一个那样的 dips，但并不_非做不可_。

然而，这难道不就是让 I. 产生 ick 的那种“扭曲自己”的做法吗？并不完全是，但这个问题问得很好。让人觉得 icky 的做法，是问她每一步想做什么，然后只跳那些。这意味着你没有 preferences，你是一面镜子，除了她已经拥有的东西，什么也提供不了。不会让人觉得 icky 的做法，是有一系列 options，并在合适的时候做到适度 flexible。不会让人觉得 icky 的做法，是在不强迫自己 vibe 的情况下 vibing。如果你不能打心底确信自己正在享受那个当下，就很难“all the way down”地 vibe。理想情况下，你正用整个自己 fullsending 每一刻的 experience，这意味着你[从不 bored](https://x.com/ArtirKel/status/1903997796287762746)。

为了说明 vibe 的重要性，就连“多问女人一些问题”这条 recommendation，如果出发点不好，也可能 backfire。前段时间，我问如今已经不再与我合住的室友：_她为什么喜欢电影？_ 。这让她非常恼火。原因在于，当时我问这个问题，并不是为了更好地了解她，而是处于这样一种 mode of being：

1.  我没有她那么喜欢电影 \[我当时 aware 到了这一点\]
2.  我不理解为什么有人会这么喜欢电影 \[我当时 aware 到了这一点\]
3.  也许她错了，她应该少喜欢一点 \[起初我并未 aware 到自己正在 embodying 这个 belief\]
4.  又或许是我错了，我应该开始多看些电影 \[起初我并未 aware 到自己正在 embodying 这个 belief\]
5.  我_非得_从她那里得到这份 information，弄清电影到底有什么值得喜欢的 \[起初我并未 aware 到自己正在 embodying 这个 belief\]

也就是说，我当时在 craving 那份 information；尽管那时我心里觉得“我只是在 asking questions”，但 body language 和 vibe 很可能泄露了这些不那么 wholesome 的 intentions。这也是让我开始对各种形式的 therapy 感兴趣的事情之一。把 asking questions 的习惯从出于 fear 转为出于 wonder，让生活变得更愉快，也第一次让我觉得：“咦，therapy 真的 works。”（当时我找的是 [theo](https://untanglingself.com/) 和 [georgina](https://www.georginadorothea.com/)，如果你想要推荐的话。）

其中一场 workshops 试图教会大家关于 vibes 和 embodiment 的这一课：coach [Nick Grant](https://x.com/FullyKnownExp) 请几位女性坐在一起聊天，然后让一个男人朝她们走去，向其中一人要电话号码。接着，他问那些女性感受到了怎样的 vibe。然后，他让那个男人反复推他。起初推得有点不痛不痒，但推了几次之后，那个人的推搡至少带上了一丝 genuine aggression。之后，他让那个人再试一次，屋里的每个人都注意到 vibe 已经 shifted：声音稍微 lower 了一点，走路的样子更 confident，说出来的话似乎与自己的愿望更 coherent，整个人也没那么 timid 了。

没有人告诉他们该怎么走路、该说什么，或者该 embody 什么 vibe；他们所做的，只是推了别人几次。最初的 approaches 中大概带着一点 fear 和 anxiety，而这与最初“拿到她们电话号码”的 goal 是相悖的。他们没有用整个自己 fullsending 自己的 intention，而 playful aggression 则让这些男人进入了一种更接近“我要得到我想要的东西”的 mindspace。这样做的目的不是找个 wingman 来推着玩（哈哈），而是举例说明：body 和 mind 是一个 integrated system，通过对其中一方、另一方或双方进行 interventions，就能 shift 整体的 state。由此，人可以设想建立一整棵 skill tree：通过 attuned to oneself 来“知道自己想要什么”，同时不 losing 对自身所处位置的 awareness（自己还想要什么，environment 里还有什么），进而采取 skillful action。如果想进一步 investigate，这一切听起来也许有点像 [Alexander's Technique](https://expandingawareness.org/)、[Art of Accomplishment](https://www.artofaccomplishment.com/)，或 [Gendlin's Focusing](https://focusing.org/sixsteps)。

我还没弄明白那个 exercise 背后的 theory；某种程度上，这感觉就像一位米其林级别的厨师成功教会你做一道美味的菜，却留你自己琢磨食谱中某个具体步骤背后的用意。我期待在接下来的几个月里弄清楚。

## 原文

One of the reasons I like living in the Bay Area are the social occasions one rarely finds anywhere else. One such recent one I attended was _[Slutcon](https://www.slutcon.com/)_ (more on the event [here](https://aella.substack.com/p/the-vision-of-slutcon)). When I first saw the event announcement showing up on twitter, I bought tickets without thinking it twice. A heuristic I live by is living as if you're the main character in some novel and do what would go along with the overarching story while being fun to read. And in this case the plot demanded that I go.

I didn't feel like writing an hour-by-hour summary of the event, but rather muse on what I think was the most salient common thread throughout: embodied authenticity or self-acceptance.

During one of the sessions, an acquaintance, a woman called I. commented that she hates it when men contort themselves (metaphorically speaking) into a shape just to please her, or when they try to figure out which buttons to press in her to get her to do what they want. When she said that I thought "that's it!" This is something that I can see why it would bother her and I can see why many men would not get why is it a problem. What she said reminded me of something Aella, one of the organizers, had [written](https://aella.substack.com/p/the-difficulty-in-dating-good-men) in the past:

> But my body does not like them. One man talks about his failures in a tone that implies he's uncomfortable with himself, like somewhere deep down a part of him believes he's a bad person, and it seems that many of his bids for social approval are attempts to be reassured that he is in fact okay. \[...\]
> 
> Another guy… I’m not sure what his problem is exactly, but he seems to warp around me. He agrees with what I say a little too fast. He laughs at my jokes immediately. His hands twitch with nervous energy. He seems nice enough, but he seems afraid of me, and like he’s putting in a huge amount of effort to make himself seem not afraid of me. His body tension reminds me of the way I feel when I’ve appeared on high-pressure public shows and I don’t want people to know that I’m _really scared right now_. I feel as though my presence towers above him, and I have to be delicate with him, like if I speak too honestly he'll crumble in my hands.

In that very same session, the topic of "men don't ask women enough questions" also came up (as it does in the piece I linked). In that moment I also thought: yeah but many women like it when the guy expresses competence talking about some technical topic (which is at odds with asking questions during the instants where the infodumping is happening). Which way, western woman?

I personally sit more in the "I like asking questions" side of things (I mean my default existence is being confused/wonder at the world, generating, and answering questions and writing about them in this blog lol). I was somewhat serious when I told someone at the event "I want to know you as deeply as you know yourself and give you nothing about me in return (because I've done it many times and find it not super fun to do, but I love learning about people!)"

Once I found myself at a party barraging a girl, K. with question after question about her, I joked that it must feel like an interview and asked if she was enjoying it (she was). But I could also imagine some other situation where someone else might have wanted to just listen to me being smart about topics I know about. But this seems like asking: during a dance should the next step be X or Y? There is a skillful answer to that question in the moment, but one cannot foresee that upfront. Conversations are improvisational so you have to "read the vibe": If a joke doesn't land, that's information, if the other person looks bored, that's information. If they seem enthusiastic that's information. One could, in theory, be running an internal lookup table like "If enthusiastic then note what I was doing and do it again" but smart women will see through that.

Instead, the right thing to do is _to not do anything_. This will make more sense if one has tried to go deep into meditation where there are states that initially you want to reach that you can't achieve if during the process of trying to achieve them you really crave them. You can't [jhana](https://nintil.com/jhanas) if you want to jhana once you get to a retreat but you can decide to go to a retreat, which will increase the odds of getting to that goal. You have to trust that they will come if you allow them, but be deeply okay if they don't come.

Essentially, if you trust yourself (or are fully self-accepting "all the way down") that will be reflected in body language, words, and actions. In the example earlier, someone who is fundamentally insecure about being single won't be deeply okay with being rejected and that lack of okayness then emits a bad needy vibe which keeps the person single (cue Joe Hudson's [golden algorithm](https://x.com/FU_joehudson/status/1917987459809648668)). This is even worse if one's somewhat on the spectrum or disembodied because then you won't notice.

"But I want a girlfriend!" one might reply. "Are you saying that once you stop wanting it you'll have it? That's deranged!". I'm not quite saying that; more precisely I am saying that craving it is what will keep you single. In the dance example, imagine someone really really really really want to do [one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t7VKGIVpxms) of these dips, but the follow is not into it, maybe it feels unsafe. A good dancer will pick up the vibe and do something else. They deep down do want to do one of those dips, but they don't _have to_.

Isn't that, however, doing that "contorting oneself" move that gave I. the ick? Not really, but that is a really good point. The icky move would be to ask her what is every step she wants to do and then dance just that. That would mean you have no preferences, you are a mirror offering nothing but what she already has. The non-icky thing is having a range of options and being flexible the right amount at the right time. The non-icky thing is vibing without forcing yourself to vibe. It's hard to vibe "all the way down" if you are not really sure, in your bones, that you are enjoying that very moment. Ideally you are fullsending every moment of your experience with all your being, which means you are [never bored](https://x.com/ArtirKel/status/1903997796287762746).

To illustrate the importance of vibe, even the "ask women more questions" recommendation can backfire if done from a bad place. A while back I asked my now ex-roommate _why did she like movies?_ . That annoyed her greatly. The reason is that, at the time, I wasn't asking it to undertand her better but rather I was in a mode of being where:

1.  I don't like movies as much as she did \[I was aware of this\]
2.  I don't understand why someone would like movies this much \[I was aware of this\]
3.  Maybe she's wrong and she should like them less \[I was initially unaware I was embodying this belief\]
4.  Or maybe I'm wrong and I should start watching more movies \[I was initially unaware I was embodying this belief\]
5.  I _have to_ get this information about what is there to be liked about movies from her \[I was initially unaware I was embodying this belief\]

ie I was craving the information, though at the time, in my mind "I was just asking questions", but probably the body language and vibe was letting through these not so wholesome intentions. This was one of the things that got me to start being interested in various forms of therapy. Switching my habit of asking questions from a place of fear to a place of wonder was something that makes life more pleasant and the first thing that made me go "huh therapy works". (That was with [theo](https://untanglingself.com/) and [georgina](https://www.georginadorothea.com/), if you want recommendations)

One of the workshops tried to teach this lesson of vibes and embodiment: The coach, [Nick Grant](https://x.com/FullyKnownExp) asked some women to sit together and chat to each other, then he asked a guy to talk towards them and ask one for their number. Then he asked the women for the vibe they got. Then he asked the guy to push him repeatedly. Initially the pushes were kinda meh but after a few the guy was pushing with at least a hint of genuine aggression. After that he asked him to go again and everyone in the room noted that the vibe had shifted: the voice was a bit lower, the way they walked more confident, and what they said seemed more coherent with what they wanted, they seemed less timid.

They were not told how to walk or what to say or what vibe to embody, all they did was push someone a couple of times. In the initial approaches there was probably a bit of fear and anxiety, and that goes against the initial goal of "getting their number". They were not fullsending their intention with all their being, whereas the playful aggression got the guys in a more "I'm going to get what I want" mindspace. The point of that was not to have a wingman to push around (lol) but rather it served as an example that body and mind are one integrated system and that one can shift the state of the whole thing by interventions on one, the other, or both. Given this, one could imagine building an entire skill tree: being attuned to oneself to "know what you want" and not losing awareness of where one is (what other things one wants, what else is in the environment) to then take the skillful action. This all sound a bit like [Alexander's Technique](https://expandingawareness.org/) or [Art of Accomplishment](https://www.artofaccomplishment.com/) perhaps or [Gendlin's Focusing](https://focusing.org/sixsteps) if one wants to investigate more.

I have yet to figure out the theory behind that exercise; in a way it felt like having a Michelin-tier chef successfully teaching you how to cook a delicious dish, while leaving you to wonder the motivation behind a specific step in the recipe. I expect to find out over the coming months.
