# 228 What to Do

## 阅读前置卡

- 价值判断：高。文本围绕‘一个人应该做什么’提出并论证‘做出好的新事物’这一生活原则，结合历史对比与反例，具有跨领域的启发性和可迁移性，但属于个人随笔式论证，未提供系统证据或可操作框架，故未达 very_high。
- 信息密度判断：中。核心论点集中，但穿插大量历史举例、脚注和修辞性展开，信息密度中等；关键概念（should 的两种类型、newness 的本质性）有实质讨论，但部分段落以叙事和类比为主。
- 是否继续阅读：3 全文。论证有清晰主线且逐步推进，从提出问题到区分两种 should、回应历史反例、讨论新事物的判定，读者有较强动力读完；但篇幅较长且部分历史细节可略读，故为 3。

## 核心洞察

一、两种 should 的区分：文本区分了两种‘应该’：照顾他人和世界属于 duty 意义上的 should，而做出好的新事物属于活出全部 potential 意义上的 should。历史上大多数生活规则混合了二者，但通常前者多于后者。这一区分把道德义务与自我实现分开，为后文解释传统答案为何不提‘做出新事物’提供了框架。
[[#^pr-81df7d337ebc|正文跳转]]

二、传统答案针对的是‘如何存在’：传统智慧（如 Cicero、Confucius）回答的是‘如何 be’而非‘做什么’。其受众是拥有土地同时参与政治的阶级，他们的工作被预先规定：管理庄园、参与政治、必要时战斗。闲暇做其他工作可以，但理想状态是没有闲暇。因此传统答案不涉及在物理学和写小说之间做选择，也不提‘做出新事物’。
[[#^pr-ca736cc05e04|正文跳转]]

三、新事物是本质性的：文本主张 newness 在科学和艺术中都是本质性的：抄袭他人论文不仅不令人印象深刻，而且不诚实；复制一幅好画虽令人愉悦，但不如原作令人印象深刻。由此推出，一遍又一遍做出同样的东西并不令人印象深刻，无论做得多好，因为那只是在复制自己。
[[#^pr-7b399f0d1178|正文跳转]]

四、措辞偏向创造而非批评：选择‘做出好的新事物’而非‘拥有好的新 ideas’，是为了偏向 creation。它使人偏好自然被视为做出事物的 ideas，而不是对别人已做出的事物做批判性观察。批评看似 sophisticated，做出新事物往往显得 awkward，尤其在初期；但最初几步最为罕见和宝贵。
[[#^pr-acdb6d5739c1|正文跳转]]

五、表达即理解：文本认为，试图表达你所理解的，不仅是证明你理解它的一种方式，也是更好地理解它的一种方式。如果一个人不能为某事物做出 model 或就它写作，他对它的理解能有多好？因此‘做出好的新事物’这一措辞也涵盖了通过创造来深化理解的过程。
[[#^pr-4e53f7d2eb9b|正文跳转]]

六、新工作的鄙视模式可作配方：新种类的工作一开始常常受到鄙视，Raymond Chandler 写的是字面意义上的 pulp fiction，如今却被公认为二十世纪最好的作家之一。这个模式如此常见，以至于可以当作配方：如果你对某种不被认为 prestigious 的工作感到兴奋，并且能解释其他人忽略了它的什么，那么这不仅是一种可以做的工作，而且是一种值得 seek out 的工作。
[[#^pr-271c211c02ae|正文跳转]]

七、做出 amazing 之物常附带助益：文本指出，如果你做出某种 amazing 的东西，你常常会帮助他人或世界，即使你并非有意。Newton 被 curiosity 和 ambition 驱动，而非被实际效果驱动，然而他工作的实际效果一直是巨大的。这似乎是 rule 而不是 exception，因此如果你认为你能做出某种 amazing 的东西，你大概就应该直接去做。
[[#^pr-f1a679351a8d|正文跳转]]

八、新事物创造者与社会层级正交：Archimedes 知道自己是第一个证明球体积是最小外接圆柱的 2/3 的人，并对此非常高兴，但古代作家更多把他视为 prodigy 而非 model。如今更多人可追随他的 example，把大部分注意力投入一种工作。做出新事物的人的那条 vein 与社会 hierarchy 成直角相交，因此同时代人会觉得把他们视为一个 distinct group 很奇怪。
[[#^pr-29f3a1b0e3cb|正文跳转]]

九、照顾世界与做出新事物的关系：文本提出一套生活原则：照顾他人和世界，并做出好的新事物。不同人会有不同侧重，有人完全专注于照顾他人，少数人主要专注于做出新事物。即使专注于后者，也至少应确保做出的新事物不会净 harm 他人或世界；若更进一步试着做出帮助他们的东西，可能会在 trade 上占先，受到更多约束但会以更多 energy 做出它。
[[#^pr-ce7da823be50|正文跳转]]

十、传统答案未提照顾世界的原因：文本不担心传统答案没有提到照顾世界，因为人们只有在清楚我们可能毁掉它之后才开始关心这一点。但传统答案没有提到‘做出好的新事物’则更令人担忧，因为这个问题似乎应该是一个 timeless 的问题，答案不应该变化太多。这一对比引出对传统答案适用范围的进一步分析。
[[#^pr-b92b8a2ca93d|正文跳转]]

## 正文

# 228 What to Do

2025年3月

一个人应该做什么？这似乎是个奇怪的问题，但它并非没有意义，也不是无法回答。这是孩子们在学会不问大问题之前会问的那种问题。我自己也只是在调查别的事情的过程中偶然碰到它。但一旦碰到了，我想我至少应该试着回答它。

那么一个人_应该_做什么？一个人应该帮助他人，并照顾这个世界。这两点是显而易见的。但还有别的吗？当我这样问时，冒出来的答案是_做出好的新事物_。

我无法证明一个人应该做这件事，就像我无法证明一个人应该帮助他人或照顾这个世界一样。我们在这里谈论的是 first principles。但我可以解释为什么这个原则说得通。人类能做的最令人印象深刻的事情是思考。它可能是所能做的最令人印象深刻的事情。而最好的思考方式，或者更准确地说，一个人思考得好的最好证明，就是做出好的新事物。

我说的新事物是非常广义的。Newton 的 physics 就是一件好的新事物。事实上，这个原则最初的版本是拥有好的新 ideas。但那似乎不够 general：例如，它没有包括创作 art 或 music，除非它们体现新的 ideas。而尽管它们可能体现新的 ideas，那也不是它们所体现的全部，除非你把“idea”这个词拉伸得毫无用处地稀薄，以至于它包括流经你 nervous system 的一切。

不过，即使对于一个人有意识地拥有的 ideas，我也更喜欢“做出好的新事物”这个措辞。还有其他方式描述最好的思考。例如，做出 discoveries，或者比其他人更深入地理解某事物。但如果你不能为某事物做出 model，或者就它写作，你对它的理解能有多好？事实上，试图表达你所理解的，不仅是证明你理解它的一种方式，也是更好地理解它的一种方式。

^pr-4e53f7d2eb9b

我喜欢这个措辞的另一个原因是，它使我们偏向 creation。它使我们偏好那种自然被视为做出事物的 ideas，而不是，比如说，对别人已经做出的事物做出 critical observations。那些也是 ideas，有时还是有价值的 ideas，但人很容易欺骗自己相信它们比实际更有价值。Criticism 似乎很 sophisticated，而做出新事物往往显得 awkward，尤其是在一开始；然而恰恰是那些最初几步最为罕见和宝贵。

^pr-acdb6d5739c1

newness 是本质性的吗？我认为是的。显然它在 science 中是本质性的。如果你抄袭别人的一篇 paper 并作为自己的发表，那不仅显得不令人印象深刻，而且显得 dishonest。在 arts 中也是类似的。一幅好 painting 的 copy 可以是一件令人愉悦的东西，但它不像原作那样令人印象深刻。这反过来意味着，一遍又一遍地做出同样的东西并不令人印象深刻，无论做得多好；你只是在复制你自己。

^pr-7b399f0d1178

不过请注意，用这个原则时，我们谈论的是一种不同类型的 should。照顾他人和世界是 shoulds，其意义在于它们是一个人的 duty，但做出好的新事物是一种 should，其意义在于这是如何活出一个人全部 potential 的方式。从历史上看，大多数关于如何生活的规则都是两种 should 的混合，尽管通常前者多于后者。[1](#fn1)

^pr-81df7d337ebc

在历史的大部分时间里，“一个人应该做什么？”这个问题在任何地方都得到大致相同的答案，无论你问的是 Cicero 还是 Confucius。你应该 wise、brave、honest、temperate 和 just，uphold tradition，并 serve the public interest。有很长一段时间，在世界某些地方，答案变成了“Serve God”，但在实践中，wise、brave、honest、temperate 和 just、uphold tradition、serve the public interest 仍然被认为是好的。而事实上，这个配方对大多数 Victorians 来说会显得正确。但其中没有任何关于照顾世界或做出新事物的内容，这有点令人担忧，因为这个问题似乎应该是一个 timeless 的问题。答案不应该变化太多。

我并不太担心传统答案没有提到照顾世界。显然，人们只有在清楚我们可能毁掉它之后才开始关心这一点。但如果传统答案没有提到它，做出好的新事物又怎么可能重要呢？

^pr-b92b8a2ca93d

传统答案是针对一个略有不同的问题的答案。它们是对如何 be 这个问题的答案，而不是对做什么的答案。受众对于做什么没有很多选择。直到最近几个世纪，受众都是 landowning class，它同时也是 political class。他们不是在 doing physics 和 writing novels 之间做选择。他们的工作是 foreordained：manage their estates、participate in politics、fight when necessary。在 spare time 做某些其他种类的工作是可以的，但理想情况下一个人根本没有 spare time。Cicero 的 _De Officiis_ 是关于如何生活的伟大 classical 答案之一，在其中他明确说，如果不是因为最近的政治动荡使他被排除在 public life 之外，他甚至不会写它。[2](#fn2)

^pr-ca736cc05e04

当然，有人在做我们现在会称为“original work”的事情，他们常常因此受到钦佩，但他们不被视为 models。Archimedes 知道他是第一个证明一个 sphere 的体积是最小 enclosing cylinder 的 2/3 的人，并对此非常高兴。但你找不到古代作家敦促他们的读者去 emulate 他。他们更多把他视为一个 prodigy，而不是一个 model。

现在，我们中更多的人可以追随 Archimedes 的 example，把大部分注意力投入到一种工作中。他终究成了一个 model，还有一群其他人，而他的同时代人会觉得把他们视为一个 distinct group 很奇怪，因为做出新事物的人的那条 vein 与社会 hierarchy 成直角相交。

^pr-29f3a1b0e3cb

哪些种类的新事物算数？我宁愿把这个问题留给它们的 makers。试图定义任何种类的 threshold 会是一件有风险的事，因为新种类的工作一开始常常受到鄙视。Raymond Chandler 写的是字面意义上的 pulp fiction，而他现在被公认为 twentieth century 最好的 writers 之一。事实上，这个模式如此常见，以至于你可以把它当作一个 recipe：如果你对某种不被认为 prestigious 的工作感到兴奋，并且你能解释其他所有人忽略了它的什么，那么这不仅是一种可以做的工作，而且是一种值得 seek out 的工作。

^pr-271c211c02ae

我不想定义任何 thresholds 的另一个原因是，我们不需要它们。做出好的新事物的那类人不需要规则来让他们保持 honest。

所以这就是我对一套生活原则的猜测：照顾他人和世界，并做出好的新事物。不同的人会以不同程度做这些事。想必会有很多人完全专注于照顾他人。会有少数人主要专注于做出新事物。但即使你是其中之一，你也至少应该确保你做出的新事物不会净 _harm_ 他人或世界。而如果你更进一步，试着做出帮助他们的东西，你可能会发现你在 trade 上占了先。你在能做出什么方面会受到更多约束，但你会以更多 energy 做出它。

^pr-ce7da823be50

另一方面，如果你做出某种 amazing 的东西，你常常会帮助他人或世界，即使你并非有意。Newton 是被 curiosity 和 ambition 驱动的，而不是被他工作可能产生的任何 practical effect 驱动，然而他工作的 practical effect 一直是巨大的。而这似乎是 rule 而不是 exception。所以如果你认为你能做出某种 amazing 的东西，你大概就应该直接去做。

^pr-f1a679351a8d

## 原文

# 228 What to Do

March 2025

What should one do? That may seem a strange question, but it’s not meaningless or unanswerable. It’s the sort of question kids ask before they learn not to ask big questions. I only came across it myself in the process of investigating something else. But once I did, I thought I should at least try to answer it.

So what _should_ one do? One should help people, and take care of the world. Those two are obvious. But is there anything else? When I ask that, the answer that pops up is _Make good new things_.

I can’t prove that one should do this, any more than I can prove that one should help people or take care of the world. We’re talking about first principles here. But I can explain why this principle makes sense. The most impressive thing humans can do is to think. It may be the most impressive thing that can be done. And the best kind of thinking, or more precisely the best proof that one has thought well, is to make good new things.

I mean new things in a very general sense. Newton’s physics was a good new thing. Indeed, the first version of this principle was to have good new ideas. But that didn’t seem general enough: it didn’t include making art or music, for example, except insofar as they embody new ideas. And while they may embody new ideas, that’s not all they embody, unless you stretch the word “idea” so uselessly thin that it includes everything that goes through your nervous system.

Even for ideas that one has consciously, though, I prefer the phrasing “make good new things.” There are other ways to describe the best kind of thinking. To make discoveries, for example, or to understand something more deeply than others have. But how well do you understand something if you can’t make a model of it, or write about it? Indeed, trying to express what you understand is not just a way to prove that you understand it, but a way to understand it better.

Another reason I like this phrasing is that it biases us toward creation. It causes us to prefer the kind of ideas that are naturally seen as making things rather than, say, making critical observations about things other people have made. Those are ideas too, and sometimes valuable ones, but it’s easy to trick oneself into believing they’re more valuable than they are. Criticism seems sophisticated, and making new things often seems awkward, especially at first; and yet it’s precisely those first steps that are most rare and valuable.

Is newness essential? I think so. Obviously it’s essential in science. If you copied a paper of someone else’s and published it as your own, it would seem not merely unimpressive but dishonest. And it’s similar in the arts. A copy of a good painting can be a pleasing thing, but it’s not impressive in the way the original was. Which in turn implies it’s not impressive to make the same thing over and over, however well; you’re just copying yourself.

Note though that we’re talking about a different kind of should with this principle. Taking care of people and the world are shoulds in the sense that they’re one’s duty, but making good new things is a should in the sense that this is how to live to one’s full potential. Historically most rules about how to live have been a mix of both kinds of should, though usually with more of the former than the latter. [1](#fn1)

For most of history the question “What should one do?” got much the same answer everywhere, whether you asked Cicero or Confucius. You should be wise, brave, honest, temperate, and just, uphold tradition, and serve the public interest. There was a long stretch where in some parts of the world the answer became “Serve God,” but in practice it was still considered good to be wise, brave, honest, temperate, and just, uphold tradition, and serve the public interest. And indeed this recipe would have seemed right to most Victorians. But there’s nothing in it about taking care of the world or making new things, and that’s a bit worrying, because it seems like this question should be a timeless one. The answer shouldn’t change much.

I’m not too worried that the traditional answers don’t mention taking care of the world. Obviously people only started to care about that once it became clear we could ruin it. But how can making good new things be important if the traditional answers don’t mention it?

The traditional answers were answers to a slightly different question. They were answers to the question of how to be, rather than what to do. The audience didn’t have a lot of choice about what to do. The audience up till recent centuries was the landowning class, which was also the political class. They weren’t choosing between doing physics and writing novels. Their work was foreordained: manage their estates, participate in politics, fight when necessary. It was ok to do certain other kinds of work in one’s spare time, but ideally one didn’t have any. Cicero’s _De Officiis_ is one of the great classical answers to the question of how to live, and in it he explicitly says that he wouldn’t even be writing it if he hadn’t been excluded from public life by recent political upheavals. [2](#fn2)

There were of course people doing what we would now call “original work,” and they were often admired for it, but they weren’t seen as models. Archimedes knew that he was the first to prove that a sphere has 2/3 the volume of the smallest enclosing cylinder and was very pleased about it. But you don’t find ancient writers urging their readers to emulate him. They regarded him more as a prodigy than a model.

Now many more of us can follow Archimedes’s example and devote most of our attention to one kind of work. He turned out to be a model after all, along with a collection of other people that his contemporaries would have found it strange to treat as a distinct group, because the vein of people making new things ran at right angles to the social hierarchy.

What kinds of new things count? I’d rather leave that question to the makers of them. It would be a risky business to try to define any kind of threshold, because new kinds of work are often despised at first. Raymond Chandler was writing literal pulp fiction, and he’s now recognized as one of the best writers of the twentieth century. Indeed this pattern is so common that you can use it as a recipe: if you’re excited about some kind of work that’s not considered prestigious and you can explain what everyone else is overlooking about it, then this is not merely a kind of work that’s ok to do, but one to seek out.

The other reason I wouldn’t want to define any thresholds is that we don’t need them. The kind of people who make good new things don’t need rules to keep them honest.

So there’s my guess at a set of principles to live by: take care of people and the world, and make good new things. Different people will do these to varying degrees. There will presumably be lots who focus entirely on taking care of people. There will be a few who focus mostly on making new things. But even if you’re one of those, you should at least make sure that the new things you make don’t net _harm_ people or the world. And if you go a step further and try to make things that help them, you may find you’re ahead on the trade. You’ll be more constrained in what you can make, but you’ll make it with more energy.

On the other hand, if you make something amazing, you’ll often be helping people or the world even if you didn’t mean to. Newton was driven by curiosity and ambition, not by any practical effect his work might have, and yet the practical effect of his work has been enormous. And this seems the rule rather than the exception. So if you think you can make something amazing, you should probably just go ahead and do it.
