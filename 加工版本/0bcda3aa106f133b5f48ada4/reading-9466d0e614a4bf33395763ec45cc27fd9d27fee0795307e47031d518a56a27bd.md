# 每年有多少 acres 会 burn？情况正在恶化吗？

---

## 正文

California 总是年年燃起大火，留下堪比 Cyberpunk 的场景，作为其[结果](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x_m9TUP_t_Y)。为什么会发生这种情况，今后又会如何发展，是本文的主题。

## 每年有多少 acres 会 burn？情况正在恶化吗？

> scientific community 已形成 broad consensus：整个 American West 的 wildfire behavior 正在 changing，California 尤其如此。Figure 2.3 突出了 California 自 1990 以来 wildfire activity 的 trends。所展示的所有 indicators——wildfire occurrence、total area burned 和 average fire size——在过去几十年里都在全州 display an upward trend（[California 的 Wildfire Costs](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)）

从下面的 chart 来看，每年 [burned](https://www.usatoday.com/pages/interactives/news/california-wildfires-carr-fire-data/) 的 acreage 存在 increasing trend，这似乎很清楚。例如，graph 所涵盖的 (67) 年中，最糟糕的 5 年都发生在近些年。但我们也应注意另一个特点：即使存在 overall increasing trend，也远不能确定任何一年都会出现特别糟糕的情况。

![image-20210617134951704](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210617134951704.png)

情况并未止步于此，如果把之后直到 2020 年（含）剩下的年份也加进来，就会得到[有史以来](https://lao.ca.gov/Publications/Report/4285)最糟糕的一年。还要注意，每年 fires 的 distribution 是 highly variable；每年的 number of fires 和 area burned 正在 increasing，但这并不意味着任何一年都必然会发生许多 fires。

## 这些 data 是 valid 的吗？

我们如何知道每年有多少 acres 会 burn？data 有多种 sources：

-   Landsat satellites 及其他 satellites 会定期拍摄 satellite imagery。接下来只需 comparing 前后的 pictures、drawing 一个 polygon，并对它的 area 进行 [measuring](https://www.esri.com/content/dam/esrisites/en-us/media/pdf/teach-with-gis/measuring-fires.pdf)。data 来自哪个 satellite 似乎并不重要，results 非常相似（[Edwards et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425717306223)）。第一颗 Landsat satellite 于 1972 年 launched，因此在那之前，我们根本没有关于 fire 的 satellite data。
-   其次，各个 fire agencies 的 written records 可以一直追溯到 1900s。其中一些 records 使用了来自 airborne cameras 的 data
-   对于更早的 data，可以从 trees 中的 [fire scarring](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Tree-ring-evidence-of-fires-includes-cross-dated-fire-scars-and-abrupt-increases-or_fig1_228116871) 来 inferred area burned。这种方法可追溯到 ~1700，再往前，data 就会变得 noisier
-   还可以对 soil 进行 sample，并 measure charcoal accumulation。我稍后讨论的一篇 paper 使用这种 method，追溯到了一千多年前

literature 中曾就 written records 的 reliability 提出这个问题，consensus 是它们是 trustworth 的（Keeley & Syphard [2018](https://www.publish.csiro.au/wf/pdf/WF18026)），

> 大多数 investigators 不熟悉这些历史 fire records，有时会怀疑其 accuracy。例如，Stephens (2005) 主张 1940 年之前的 USFS data 是 inaccurate 的，但 cited 的 source (Mitchell 1947) 并未提供这方面的 evidence。这种看法很可能来自 Donoghue’s (1982b) 的评论：“1940 标志着 fire reporting 的现代时代。”然而，那句评论指的是：“当时发布的 report 是第一份为 automated data processing 和 easy readability 而 designed 的 report”，而不是指 reliability。Historians 通常对这些早期 California fire records 抱有信心 (Brown 1945; Show 1945; Clar 1969; Cermak 2005)。第一位 author，J. E. Keeley，examined 了存放在 state 和 federal archives 中所有与 California fire-related 的 materials，并认为它们总体上表明，managers 始终认真对待 reporting accuracy 和 completeness。例如，从 1905 年开始，USFS recordkeeping 要求在 fire reporting Form 944 上填写 15 项 information，包括 specific cause (Donoghue 1982b)。在 state-protected lands 上还存在一项 incentive：1911 Federal Weeks Law 根据 fire protection 的 statistics 向各州提供 fiscal aid（见 http://www.calfire.ca.gov/ about/about\_calfire\_history2，访问于 23 May 2018）。
> 
> 1919 年，California state legislature 为 fire prevention 和 suppression 拨款，state archive 中的 records 表明，到 1920 年，全州分布着超过 400 名 fire wardens，负责 firefighting 和 fire reporting。1920 年，Army’s 9th Aero Squadron fire patrol 在持续 5-month 的 California fire season 期间执行了 800 次 flights，覆盖 426 500 km (Cermak 1991)。

### Fire scars 与 ground records 的对照

对于从 fire scarring 中 inferred 的更早 fire areas，这是一种 imperfect metric，原因有多个：trees 即使 burned，也可能没有 scars；trees 可能全部死亡，没有留下 fire 的痕迹；某个 forest 的 composition 也可能已经 changed：如果发现少量带有 fire scars 的 trees，那是一场 small fire，还是一场 larger fire 的一部分，只是 forest 的其余部分都已死亡？于是就不得不借助 statistical techniques 来 correct for 这些 factors。有些 studies 旨在 measure：通过观察 fire scars 得到的结果，与早期 fires 的 written record 之间 correlate 得有多好（Fule et al., [2003](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/viewdoc/download?doi=10.1.1.516.7826&rep=rep1&type=pdf)）。下面展示了 written record 与 fire scar-derived data（通过两种不同 methods 得到）之间的 correlation；它表明，在一个 studied area 的 1937-2000 期间，两者有 reasonable agreement（Farris et al., [2010](https://www.fs.fed.us/rm/pubs_other/rmrs_2010_farris_c001.pdf)；较不乐观的看法见 Shapiro-Miller [2007](https://cdnsciencepub.com/doi/10.1139/X07-054)，不过这里似乎是缺乏良好的 written records，而不是 fire-scars 有误），

### 追溯到更久远的过去

有些人根据 yearly burned acreage 的 _extended_ record，[主张这并不是因为 climate change](https://climaterealism.com/2021/05/national-fire-center-disappears-inconvenient-u-s-wildfire-data/?fbclid=IwAR0cvur78oJKeIRkE8JYeiExy0EirDqUBwa0xFVW7qW3xHHEgxL38H617zI)。上面的 plot 只追溯到 1950。他们在那里指出的 data 确实是真实的：National Interagency Fire Center 的确说过，一个世纪前的 fires 是最糟糕的，此后一直改善，或许直到 1996。事实上，即便看上面的 chart，如果仔细看 1950-1996 的 data，仍可能 conclude 它正在 trending down。这些 data 的 [original source](https://web.archive.org/web/20201224043154/https:/www.nifc.gov/fireInfo/fireInfo_stats_totalFires.html) 对 figures 作出了如下限定：_1983 之前，这些 figures 的 sources 并不为人所知，或无法 confirmed，而且并不是 derived 自当前的 situation reporting process。因此，不应把 1983 之前的 figures 与后来的 data 进行 compared。_

这个 finding 令人意外，因为经常听说 climate change 正在导致 wildfires 的 increase。但如果并没有 increase，那么 climate change 在这里或许没有多大 impact？也就是说，我们是否正在经历一个 fire amount 异常低的时期，而我们现在看到的只是恢复正常？

## climate change 的作用

这是 Michael Shellenberger 等人持有的立场，参见[这里](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2020/09/10/why-environmental-alarmism-makes-forest-fires-worse/)、[这里](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2020/08/24/stop-blaming-climate-change-for-californias-fires-many-forests-including-the-redwoods-need-them/)，尤其是[这里](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2019/11/04/why-everything-they-say-about-california-fires--including-that-climate-matters-most--is-wrong/)。他指出了 driving 近期 fires increase 的其他 factors，尤其是 _fire suppression_，或者更准确地说是 fire exclusion：不允许 environment 中出现任何 fire 的 practice（我稍后会讨论），这会导致 fuels 的 accumulation，以及多年后更大的 fires。Shellenberger 很多地方说得对，但整体传达的信息是错的；有足够的 evidence 表明，climate change 在 California 的 wildfire situation 中发挥着关键作用。稍后再详细讨论。

其他人指出的另一个 factor 是 wildland-urban interface (WUI) 中 housing construction 的 substantial increase，文中引用的一位 scientist（Keeley）提到，这导致了更多 fires。更多人住在 wildland areas 附近，也意味着更多 roads（vehicles 是 ignitions 的一个 nontrivial source）、更多 powerlines、更多 campfires（自然环境离家近时，露营更方便），以及总体上更多可能导致 fire 的 accidents。

另一个会 modulates climate 对 wildfires 的作用的 factor（Keeley & Syphard, [2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42408-019-0041-0)），至少在短期内，是某一年的 higher rainfall 会导致下一年更多 fires（更多 vegetation grows）。事实上，California 有史以来的“[wettest](https://www.mercurynews.com/2021/05/24/whats-causing-californias-worsening-drought/)”年份（2016-2017）之后，出现了 intense fires（2018）。

而这些 higher 或 lower rainfall 的时期，又可能是由 climate 中的 natural oscillations 所 driven 的（例如 Pacific Decadal Oscillation 或相关的 Atlantic Multidecadal oscillation）。2007 的一篇 paper，[Kitzberger](https://www.pnas.org/content/104/2/543) et al. 指出：_AMO 当前的 warming trend suggests，未来几十年，我们可能会看到 western U.S. 各地 widespread, synchronous fires 的 increase。_而实际发生的正是如此。

\[2\]. paper 中还有一个更 conservative 的 estimate，会使 climate change 的 contribution 减少 19%；这意味着，climate change 导致的不是多出 55% 的 burned area，而是多出 40% 的 burned area，这仍然令人担忧。

如果想知道这一 trend 有多少 attributable to climate change，就必须 model 那些 oscillations（并 control away 诸如 forest management 等 human activities 的 impact）。大多数此类 analyses 使用 1950 及以后的 data，例如 [Abatzoglou & Williams](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/113/42/11770.full.pdf)（2016）。这没有任何问题！那个时期应该 cover 一部分 cold 和 warm Pacific Decadal oscillations，但理想情况下，我们希望看到一个 reconstructs 整个 observed dataset of fires 的 model。无论如何，作者们 find，US forests（想必也包括 California）中现有 fuels 的 aridity increase，有 _half_[2](https://nintil.com/wildfires-california/#sidenote-2) 是由 anthropogenic climate change 导致的，其余则是 natural variation（可能就是前面描述的 oscillations）。而 aridity 又与某一年的 burned area 存在 strongly correlated 的关系，

![image-20210617211630880](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210617211630880.png)

请注意，Abatzoglou et al. 的 paper 没有 account for 一长串 effects，包括 preventing 或 fighting fires 的 human activity，而其中的 causes 既可能 increase，也可能 decrease attributable to climate change 的 wildfires fraction。

> 在只 focusing exclusively on ACC 对 fuel aridity 的 direct impacts 时，**我们没有 address ACC 可能 affected wildfire activity 的其他几个 pathways。**例如，我们使用的 fuel aridity metrics 可能无法 adequately capture mountain snow hydrology 对 soil moisture 的作用。我们也没有 account for climate change 对 lightning activity 的 influence，而后者可能随着 warming 而 increase (40)。我们同样没有 account for，atmospheric CO2 的 increases (41)、drought-induced vegetation mortality (42) 或 insect outbreaks (43) 所导致的 biomass/fuel changes，可能如何 affected fire risk。**此外，除了 fire management（例如 suppression 和 wildland fire use policies）、ignitions、land cover（例如 exurban development）以及 vegetation changes 对 fuel aridity 与 forest fire area 之间 relationship 的 modulate 程度之外，我们把 ACC 对 fire 的 impact 视为 independent from 这些因素的 effects。**近几十年来，这些 factors 很可能 added to western US forests 各地的 area burned，并可能 amplified wildfire activity 对 climate variability and change 的 sensitivity (2, 22, 24, 44)。这些 confounding influences，加上 burned area 与其 drivers 之间的 nonlinear relationships（例如 Fig. 1），给我们将 regional burned area 归于 ACC 的 empirical attribution 带来了 uncertainty。我们的方法 depends on 在 western United States 的 large regional scale 上 observed 的、burned area 与 fuel aridity 之间的 strong regional relationship，因此，这项 attribution 工作的 quantitative results 不一定 applicable 于 finer spatial scales、individual fires，或 nonforested areas 的 changes。

这里的 Aridity 是八种不同的“fuel aridity”metrics 的 mean，

> 有 Four 个 metrics 是根据 1948–2015 的 monthly data calculated 的：(i) reference potential evapotranspiration (ETo)、(ii) VPD、(iii) CWD，以及 (iv) Palmer drought severity index (PDSI)。另外四个 metrics 是为 1979–2015 calculated 的 daily fire danger indices：(v) 来自 Canadian forest fire danger rating system 的 fire weather index (FWI)、(vi) 来自 US national fire danger rating system 的 energy release component (ERC)、(vii) McArthur forest fire danger index (FFDI)，以及 (viii) Keetch– Byram drought index (KBDI)。

顺带一提，如果我们选其中一个，例如 vapor pressure deficit，得到的 results 也是一样的。

## Vapor Pressure Deficit（水汽压亏缺）

VPD _measures_ _air 中 moisture 的 amount 与 air 在 saturated 时可能 hold 的 moisture 量之间的 difference。它通常以 pounds per square inch (psi) 或 kilopascal (kPa) 来 measured。high VPD（greater than 1.0 kPa）意味着 air 仍然可以 hold 大量 water_。High VPD 意味着，water 的 droplets 不会留在 plants 上或内部，而会被 sucked up 到 air 中（warmer air 可以 carry 更多 water），因此 high VPD leads to drier terrains，而 drier terrains 一般又 leads to 更多 fire。VPD 也与整个 Western United States，尤其是 California 的 burned area 存在 reasonably well 的 correlates 关系（Higuera & Abatzoglou, [2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/gcb.15388), Park Williams et al. [2019](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1029/2019EF001210?utm_source=mp-fotoscapes)）。这种 relationship 似乎是 exponential：VPD 越多，fires 就会 exponentially 越多。:

![image-20210625174439995](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210625174439995.png)

![image-20210625174717818](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210625174717818.png)

![image-20210625174812700](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210625174812700.png)

说 VPD 随着 temperatures rise 而上升，并不会太令人惊讶，但也要注意，它还呈现出一个 oscillatory component（黑色），而 CMIP5 climate model ensemble（红色）并没有很好地 captured 这一部分。这种 oscillation 或许有助于解释，为什么 1950 之前有那么多 fires，为什么中间那些年份的 fire 那么少，以及为什么现在的 fire 又这么多。目测这个 oscillation 的 period，它可能是由 [Pacific Decadal Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_decadal_oscillation#/media/File:PDO.svg) 引起的。如果是这样，我们可能已经 reached a peak，接下来几年能暂时从 rising trend 中喘口气，但考虑到 climate change 带来的 underlying rising trend，这至多只是 increase 的暂时放缓，而不是彻底的喘息：

![image-20210625175012334](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210625175012334.png)

请注意，在 sufficiently high VPDs 下，这种 correlation 应该会 break down：一旦 California 有很多地方已经 burned，这自然就会 delays 后续 fires 的 occurrence。

California 这些年来确实在变得 more dry，即便考虑追溯到 100 多年前、理应 cancel 我们的 periodic oscillations 的 data，结论也是如此：朝向 dryness 的 trend 仍然 [remains](https://www.ncdc.noaa.gov/cag/divisional/time-series/0402/pdsi/1/5/1895-2021?base_prd=true&begbaseyear=1901&endbaseyear=2000&trend=true&trend_base=10&begtrendyear=1895&endtrendyear=2021&filter=true&filterType=binomial)。,

![image-20210618105458861](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210618105458861.png)

所以 climate change 正在朝预期的 direction 起作用：California 正在变得 [warmer and drier](https://twitter.com/RARohde/status/1061932205327429632)，而根据 thermodynamics of combustion，这两点将使未来出现更多 fire，前提是 ceteris paribus。请注意，这种 effect 一般成立，但并非总是如此。虽然 drier fuels 会 burn 更多，但如果 weather 足够 arid，plants 就会停止 growing：在非常极端的情况下，这就是 Sahara desert 尽管极其 hot 和 dry，却没有多少 fire activity 的一个原因。后文我会讨论一项 study，它在 California 的某个特定 area 发现了这种 dynamic。

## 历史 fire record

正如我前面提到的，“这不是 climate change”这一 argument 的一个关键点是：在 1800 年代之前，fire 曾经比现在多得多。

2020 年约有 4.4M acres burned，这被认为是 record 中历来 acres burned 最多的一年。事实也确实如此，除非我们追溯到 1950 之前。如果这样做，一直追溯到 1800 之前，我们就会发现，任何一年 burn 的 **average** acres 是 1.8M hectares，也就是大约 4.4M acres（Stephens et al., [2007](https://www.sierraforestlegacy.org/Resources/Conservation/FireForestEcology/FireScienceResearch/FireHistory/FireHistory-Stephens07.pdf)）。这个 average 等于近代历史上最糟糕一年的数值，因此我们可以在这里讲一个故事：California 在经历了一段试图对抗不可避免之事的时期之后，正在恢复正常（也就是 climate-given 的每年 burned acres 的 steady state）。我们应该讲这个故事吗？Michael [Shellenberger](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2020/09/10/why-environmental-alarmism-makes-forest-fires-worse/) 选择了这种说法：反常的不是最近 fires 的 uptick，而是当前时期之前 anomalously low 的 burns 数量；后者源于一个世纪以来 aggressively fighting fires，这 leads to overgrown forests，为 fires 的 spread 创造了更多 fuel。这并不意味着 climate change 没有 contributing to 这种 effect，increased temperatures 和 aridity 会 contributing to 每年 burned acres 的那个 steady state 的 increase；只不过我们现在正从一个 unsustainably low level 被拉回来。[Shellenberger](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2020/08/24/stop-blaming-climate-change-for-californias-fires-many-forests-including-the-redwoods-need-them/) 甚至说，我们大概应该停止将 fires 归咎于 climate change。我希望上面的讨论已经说清楚：climate change 正在发挥作用。

1950 之前的 fire records 并不只有 1800 年代的 data。那也是修建 railroads、通过 burning forests 来 clear up land 以供 farming 的时期，当时人们对 firefighting 的了解更少，甚至缺少 suppress 其中一些 fires 的手段。我们应该追溯到更早的过去，而确实有一些研究在这样做：Marlon et al.（[2011](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/109/9/E535.full.pdf)）说，考虑更长的 time scales，虽然 fire activity 在 ~1890 左右出现了一个 peak，但过去并非始终如此。再往前，fire 确实比现在更多，但没有中间那个时期那么多。

Marlon et al. 指出，截至撰写这篇 paper 时，相对于根据 historical climatic patterns 得出的预期，存在一个“fire deficit”（Baker 也曾指出这一点，[2015](https://europepmc.org/backend/ptpmcrender.fcgi?accid=PMC4564206&blobtype=pdf)）：burning 得还不够。十年后，我们或许正在看到，这个 gap 如何通过历来最具 damaging 的 fire seasons 被 closed。

![image-20210618205952246](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210618205952246.png)

如果将利用 climate 得出的 predicted charcoal influx（fire 的一个 proxy）的 historical record 与 observed record 一起 plots 出来（就像 Marlon et al. 在 supplement 中所做的那样），这个 fire deficit 就更加显著，

![image-20210628132403254](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210628132403254.png)

如果你对那里关于 historical fires 的“[Reynolds and Pierson (1941)](https://www.fs.fed.us/research/docs/rpa/pre-1989/1941%20SAWTIMBER%20RESOURCE%20OF%20THE%20US%201630-1930.pdf)”这条 reference 感到好奇，我设法找到了一个自称是其扫描文本的东西。它包含下面这张 figure，应该就是 figure “A”，只不过沿着一个更长的 axis 展开了。我们该在多大程度上相信它？我不知道！它包含了 fire 以外的 tree destruction 的 causes，但将每 decade 的 board feet converting 为 acres 并不是一件简单的事。假设每 acre 有 5-15k board feet，那么 1600 年代的 surface burned 可能在每年 1.3 到 4 million acres 之间（后一个数值更接近前面 Stephens 给出的 figure，因此 order of magnitude 是对得上的）。

不过，那个 bump 中的某些部分[或许](https://ti.org/antiplanner/?p=15127)并不真实，而是非法进行的 prescribed fire（或 woods clearing）（尽管以 controlled 的方式进行），Forest Service 无论如何都会将其 classify 为 wildfire。

## 为什么 fires 会 start？

Climate change 和 forest management 可能在 fires start 后使其 worse，但是什么 triggers 它们？

通常，wildfire 造成的大部分 damage 都来自少数几场 fires（[Strauss et al., 1989](https://www.fs.fed.us/psw/publications/strauss/psw_1989_strauss001.pdf)）；在 2020 California wildfire season 中，组成 August Fire Complex 的合计 38 场 fires 所 burned 的 surface area，几乎占当年全部 burned surface area 的四分之一，尽管这 38 场 fires 仅占 2020 年 reported 的 9917 场 fires 的 0.38%。这场 fire 是由 lightning storms 引起的。2020 年其余的 mega fires 也是如此，包括 SCU Lightning Complex、LNU Lightning Complex、North Complex 或 SQF Lightning Complex。

情况并非总是如此：2018 年，有史以来最严重的 fire（按 insured losses 而非 area burned 衡量）Camp Fire 是由一条 faulty power line 引起的；而从更广的范围来看，由 electric power lines 引起的 fires 比由 lightning 引起的还多，

![ 摄影：Todd Trumbull](https://s.hdnux.com/photos/01/02/15/40/17356840/5/920x1240.jpg)

但我们应该记住，重要的不一定是什么 starts 最多的 fires，而是什么 leads to 最多的 damage。

根据 [Cal FIRE](https://www.fire.ca.gov/media/t1rdhizr/top20_destruction.pdf)，最具 destructive 性的前 20 场 fires 中，有 6 场是由 faulty powerlines 引起的（按 structures burned 和 deaths 衡量）。其余一些则源于不起眼的小事，例如 [Carr fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carr_Fire) 的情况：_与这起事件的名称巧合的是，这场 fire 起于一辆 vehicle 的 flat tire 导致 wheel's rim scrape against asphalt，产生了 sparks，继而 set off 了 fire。_又如 [Valley fire](https://www.cnn.com/2016/08/11/us/california-valley-fire-faulty-hot-tub/index.html)：_Cobb town 的一户住宅中，一个 poorly connected hot tub 的 wire overheated、melted，并 ignited 了附近的 dry brush。_再如 [Mendocino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendocino_Complex_Fire) fire：_这场 \[Ranch\] Fire 是一位牧场主引起的；他在寻找 wasp nest 时敲打 metal stake，无意间 sparked 了 dry grass。_

因此，从 prevention 的角度来看，尽管难以 control 的 potential causes of fire 存在一条 long tail，但 maintaining powerlines 或将其 under grounding，是可以用来 reduce 那些在人命和 damaged structures 方面最具 devastating 性的 fires 的一项措施。

\[3\]. 我检查了这篇 paper 是否存在明显错误，据我所能判断，它是 robust 的，不过有一些 potential caveats：他们在一处使用 random forests 来 estimate California 的 background PM2.5（RFs 有时可能很 finnicky），并采用了 8.8M$ which is not unreasonable. Even when assuming differential value per age and assuming that most of the risk is borne by older people, it would still be a large number. They used a model to predict the health impacts of fire-derived particulates called BenMAP produced by the EPA which is supposed to account for all sorts of things and which I have not examined directly. The 95% CI for their estimate of health impact is [13.33-75.92] bn$ 的 statistical value of life。从这篇 paper 的视角来看，BenMAP 是一个 black box model，这使得 health cost per acre 的数值难以 determine，因为它很可能取决于 fire 的 location 和 size，或者当天的 wind conditions；不过，可以通过调整 model 的 inputs 来 derive 此类数值的一个 range

## 自然引起的 fires 也很重要：health costs

另一方面，如果我们关心 acres burned，就需要关注 lightning-started fires；而我们确实应该关心这一点，因为用 structures that burned+deaths 来 quantifying damage 的 approach，很可能 underestimates 了 wildfires 的 true costs。还必须加上个人 wildfire mitigation 的 economic costs（购买 air purifiers）、无法享受相关 natural areas 的损失（许多人从中获得很大价值），以及数百万人 [breathing](https://patrickcollison.com/pollution) polluted air 所遭受的 damage。Acreage 不应成为需要 reduce 的“唯一”metric（如[这里](https://www.npr.org/2020/10/07/921209244/four-million-acres-have-burned-in-california-why-thats-the-wrong-number-to-focus)所建议的那样），但它仍是 equation 的一个重要部分。为了把这一点说得更清楚，已有研究尝试 quantify wildfire-produced air pollution 对 health 的 impact；[Wang et al. (2021)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-020-00646-7) estimate，仅 2018 年的 California wildfires 就通过 increases in mortality risk，造成了 3652 人死亡，是直接死于 fires 的人数的 35x[3](https://nintil.com/wildfires-california/#sidenote-3) 。这一点非常重要：这种由 pollutants 造成的 indirect damage，_并不总会在 wildfire equation 的 cost-benefit analyses 中得到 considered_：National Fire Protection Association 经常被引为 cost of wildfires 方面的权威，但[他们的 calculations](https://www.nfpa.org/%7E/media/fd0144a044c84fc5baf90c05c04890b7.ashx)并不 include health-related costs。又如 wikipedia 对 2018 wildfires 的 cost calculation，引用的数值是 26.35bn$ loss whereas the Wang et al. paper, adding in health losses and indirect (counterfactual loss of economic activity) losses has a total of 148.5bn$。[这份资料](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)的 Chapter 5 对 wildfires 的 health effects 做了广泛的 review。之所以如此，可能是因为 damage 的存在更容易被注意到（一栋 burning building 比 inflamed lungs 更容易看见），而为 rebuilding a building assigning a value，也比 modeling particulate matter 进入人们 lungs 所造成的 cost 更容易。幸运的是，这一点正开始得到承认，[California's Wildfire and Forest Resilience and Action](https://fmtf.fire.ca.gov/media/cjwfpckz/californiawildfireandforestresilienceactionplan.pdf) plan 已有一节讨论 reducing smoke 的 health effects（p.34）。_[Cost of Wildfire in California](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)_（2020）report 也考虑了 health effects。

这只是对该问题非常基础的理解，但已经使我们比[这篇 PBS 文章](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/californias-catastrophic-wildfires-in-3-charts)掌握了更多信息；该文声称 95% 的 fires 都源于 human causes。情况可能确实如此，但大多数 fires 都是 small 且无关紧要的；我们关心的是按 seriousness 加权的 causes：由 lightning strikes 引起的 fires 仍应是一个主要的关注对象。

## WUI 的难题

California 的许多人住在所谓的 Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI)。我最近去了一趟 Larkspur (Marin County)，做了一些 [research](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Posie/@37.9160269,-122.545898,12z/data=!4m5!3m4!1s0x0:0x76995d24f2f80820!8m2!3d37.9330498!4d-122.53573)。那个地方的 WUI 可能就是这样的，

WUI 在 California wildfires 的 discourse 中反复出现：

首先，有一种观点认为，WUI 的 new construction 一直在 [rapidly](https://www.fs.fed.us/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2018/nrs_2018_radeloff_001.pdf) increasing，既 increasing 了对人的 damage（在 tree-less 的 San Francisco 很难有 wildfires）——因为 urban dwellers 因 increasing house prices 而被 [pushed out](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2019/10/can-california-save-itself/601135/) 城市——也 increasing 了 human activity 会 cause 更多 fires 的 odds。这个观点是正确的。

其次，作为上述 diagnosis 所产生的 prescriptive measure，有人 [呼吁让人们搬出](https://gabekleinman.medium.com/how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying-and-love-the-smoke-90d61c8f2572) WUI。我前面提到的几场 fires 就始于这里：引发 Valley Fire 的 faulty hot tub 是另一个 [WUI](https://www.google.com/maps/place/38%C2%B049'20.0%22N+122%C2%B043'23.0%22W/@38.8217209,-122.7224955,419m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m5!3m4!1s0x0:0x0!8m2!3d38.822222!4d-122.723056?hl=en) 的例子。人越多，就越有可能有人做出可能 start a fire 的事情。forests 附近有更多人，也就需要更多 electric lines（出于 cost 原因，它们往往建在 overground），这也可能 lead to accidental fires。在我看来，关键问题不是人们在那里 building 是否会让自己的人身和 property 面临 risks；而是他们住在那里，正在让_其他人_面临巨大的 risks。

我非常不愿意尝试让人们搬出 WUI。正如你从链接中的 StreetViews 所能看到的，WUIs 是很棒的居住地点！为了拯救 California 而剥夺一些人这样的机会，目前是一个 false dilemma：[progress](https://nintil.com/progress-in-progress/) 意味着不必做 tradeoffs，我们或许可以在 WUI 这件事上鱼与熊掌兼得。

这件事有一条出路：Insurance。如果住在 WUI 的人既是 fires 的一个 major cause，又是 victims，insurance 难道不能解决这个问题吗？更高的 premia 会劝退想搬到那里的人，而且——在一种值得探讨的更极端情形中——可以将造成 damage 后的 paying responsibility 从 State 转移给 insurers，再通过它们转移给 potential culprits，就像 nuclear reactors 或 cars 的做法一样。Mandatory third-party insurance 或许是一种 solution，可以将针对 wildfires 的这项 insurance 纳入 location-dependent property taxes。

我们现有的，或者更准确地说，[曾经有过的](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/fire-prev-fee.htm)，最接近这种做法的东西，是对 state land 上未 incorporated 地区的 habitable buildings 征收的 CAL FIRE fee。这项 fee 的问题有两个：第一，它对所有人都一样（与 regular insurance 不同），而 higher risk areas 本应被收取 higher premium。第二，它并不适用于所有人！[Cobb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobb,_California) 的 hot tub owners 需要缴纳，而 [South Lake Tahoe](https://www.google.com/maps/@38.93958,-119.9460768,3a,60y,330.88h,90t/data=!3m6!1e1!3m4!1sXEpKa-t-Am2tq7-boXzZug!2e0!7i13312!8i6656) 的则不需要。

遗憾的是，victim 一侧的 insurance mechanism（向遭受 wildfire damage 的人 paying）因 government regulation 而失灵。本来应该发生的是，fire-prone areas 的 premia 应当 increase，进而在 on the margin 上帮助劝退那里的 new construction。例如，California government [prevents](http://www.insurance.ca.gov/0250-insurers/0300-insurers/0200-bulletins/bulletin-notices-commiss-opinion/upload/Bulletin-2020-11-Mandatory-Moratorium-on-Cancellations-and-Non-Renewals-of-Policies-of-Residential-Property-Insurance-After-the-Declaration-of-a-State-of-Emergency-Amended-11_6_20.pdf) insurers 在 wildfire-prone areas dropping coverage。在可以这样做的地方，insurers 实际上正在 [dropping](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2020/12/04/592788.htm) customers。insurers 为什么想要 drop 有价值的 customers？因为 California government 同样不允许它们将 premia increase 到 actuarially fair levels：它们必须先申请 [permission](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RBA635-1.html) 才能 raise premia。这里没有让 insurers 通过 compete 来 fairly price risk 的 free market，反而是 imposition of price caps 导致了 absence of service。有一个 state-operated insurer of last resort，即 FAIR plan，幸好它 offers 的 coverage 不如 private plans 那么 comprehensive（因此它最终显得 expensive；或许还没有达到它应有的 expensive 程度，但总比 coverage 更 comprehensive 时的情况好）。当然，California 毕竟是 California，它的计划正是往那个方向走，实际上是在向住在 fire-prone areas 的人提供 [subsidy](https://calmatters.org/commentary/my-turn/2020/05/states-insurer-of-last-resort-should-not-become-the-primary-insurance-market/)。

\[4\]. 在我的家乡 Spain，大多数 construction 都采用 concrete blocks 或 bricks。我搬到 UK 和 US 后，得知如此多的 housing 仍在使用 wood，在我看来，这种做法和自助洗衣店的存在一样野蛮。Concrete 或 brick construction 不仅 fire-proof，而且更 soundproof，floors 不那么吱呀作响，overall quality 也更高。更发达的国家仍有这种 construction，一直令我困惑。好吧，[这里](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wpxLLCdW_Gc)有一些解释。但还是要这样吗？认真的吗？都这个年代了还住 Wooden houses？而且是在 wildfire-prone areas？更新：[这里](https://constructionphysics.substack.com/p/wood-construction-and-the-risk-of)有一篇讨论此事的文章。

另一个可能很容易的 solution 是制定 building standards，强制在房屋周围设置所谓的 _defensible space_，clearing up 建筑周围的 vegetation，或者使用 fire-resistant materials 来 building（当然，我们知道 timber 很便宜，但 concrete blocks 也没有那么贵[4](https://nintil.com/wildfires-california/#sidenote-4)）。遗憾的是，California 又一次表现得很 California：尽管在 San Francisco，crime 是 de facto legal，现行 construction codes _已经_要求 house builders 以合理的方式 build houses，以 reduce fire 带来的 damage，并要求 owners 维持这些 standards。但只要看看任何 WUI area，就会发现_许多人根本不照着做[这些](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/news/environment/story/2019-06-11/californias-not-enforcing-wildfire-prevention-rules-for-homeowners-leaving-tens-of-thousands-of-properties-vulnerable-to-big-blazes)_。这是 California 一个更普遍的问题：制定 laws 和 action plans，与真正让它们得到 executed 之间存在 gap。无论如何，即便这些要求得到 complied with，也总可以在这里要求更加 restrictive 的 standards。

> 在 State Responsibility Areas 以及 Local Responsibility Areas 内的 High Fire Hazard Severity Zones，state law 和／或 local ordinance 要求按照特定的 horizontal and vertical spacing criteria，在 property 周围 creation of defensible space。关于 defensible spaces 的具体 requirements 见 California Building Code: Chapter 7A 内的 Public Resources Code 4290- 4294。2005、2008 (SB 1595 Kehoe, Chapter 366, Statutes of 2008) 和 2018 (AB 2911 Friedman, Chapter 641, Statutes of 2018) 对 CA building code 的 Amendments 为该 code 带来了 major updates，提高了 defensible space requirements，包括将 defensible space increasing 到最多 100 feet，并要求在 structure 周围最初的 30 feet 内进行更 intense 的 fuel management。对于第三次 infraction，State law 处以不低于 \$100 for a first infraction, increasing to a potential misdemeanor charge and a fine of no less than \$500 的 fines。State law 还允许 insurance carriers 要求比 California’s building code 中 100-foot criteria 更 restrictive 的 defensible space criteria。\*\*目前没有 statewide audit 来检查 defensible space 对 state requirement 的 compliance，而 anecdotal evidence 表明 compliance 并不均衡。\*\*（[The Cost of Wildfire in California](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)）

关于这些 trends 中的一些：是我们看到了更多 lightning，还是相同数量的 lightning 正在 striking 更 arid 的 fuels，从而 leading to 更多 fire？我们知道后者是对的，那么前者呢？似乎并非如此，lightning 的 amount（至少在 1985-2000 期间）一直 remained constant（van Wagtendonk & Cayan，[2008](https://fireecology.springeropen.com/articles/10.4996/fireecology.0401034)）。lightning 可能总体上正在 increasing，但至少那段时期提供了 evidence，说明我们可以看到 increasing lightning-caused fires，而不必是 lightning 本身作为 driving 它的 variable。这并不是要排除 lightning strikes 总体上（或在 2000-2021 期间）正在 trending up 的可能性。

human causes 的情况也一样吗？要 disentangle 这一点更难，因为 lightning 是一种 discrete event，而 human factors 包括 fire suppression、urban construction、roads 或 power lines。Mann et al.（[2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153589)）尝试 disentangle 这些 relative contributions，并发现，即使在 controlling away 了 climate change 的 effect 之后，human activity 仍然 on net contributed to 更多 fire。

由此得到的一些 conclusions：

-   存在着朝向更多 area burned、更多 fires，以及最大的 fires 出现 increase 的 trend；过去，在某一年，30% 的 fires 造成了 90% 的 area burned，而现在 less than 10% 的 fires（最近，某一年 less than 25 fires）就 account for 大部分 area burned。由此得到的启示是：如果我们有好的 modeling tools，并 focus on 最先被 predicted 为最严重的那些，就有可能 avoid 大部分 damage。不过，这未必总是可取的，因为我们也可以利用 wildfires 作为 clear up vegetation 的 tools（managed wildfire）。后文会进一步讨论。
-   area burned 最主要的 cause，遥遥领先于其他 causes，是 lightning strikes，其次是 fraction 高得令人惊讶的 'Unknown fires'。即使是 2020 年的 large fires，例如 [Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creek_Fire_\(2020\)) Fire，我们至今仍不知道其 cause。再之后是 miscellaneous 和 equipment use。
    -   Miscellaneous 是一个包罗各种情况的类别。一些例子包括：La Brea fire（2009，一起 marijuana drug trafficking 活动中的 cooking fire）、Ferguson fire（2018，Faulty vehicle catalytic converter）、Detwiler fire（2017，firearm-related），或者 Rocky fire（2015，一座存有 flammable liquids 的 outbuilding 附近的 gas water heater 出现 failure）
    -   Equipment use 似乎与 miscellaneous 有点相似，因为这个 category 下汇集了许多不同的情况。包括 Cedar fire（2003，signal fire out of control）、Zaca fire（2007，private property 上用于 repair 一根 water pipe 的 grinding machine 产生的 sparks），或者 Moonlight fire（2007，与一家 logging company 有关）
-   我们前面不是说过，根据某些 sources，95% 的 fires 是由 humans started 的吗？然而，FRAP data 却说，在任何一年，humans 都从未 account for more than 20% 的 fires（即使把所有 unknown causes 都视为 human-related）。这是因为 FRAP 只考虑规模明显较大的 fires（我也找到了由 sheds 引发的 fires，所以不清楚它们所谓 large 的含义）。这些较小的 fires 大部分很容易 put off，也很少登上新闻。如果我们考虑那些较大的 fires，那么 lightning strikes 又要被 attributed 很大的 role（按 number of fires 和 area 而言），而 unknown cause 的 fires 数量也大得吓人。我确实尝试获取 [MTBS](https://www.mtbs.gov/)，也就是那篇“主要是我们造成的”[paper](https://www.pnas.org/content/114/11/2946) 的 authors 使用的 dataset，但这个 dataset 不包含 fires 的 cause。不过确实，大多数 sources 倾向于同意，在 fires 的 known causes 中，less than 10% attributable to nature，因此我们原本可以 preventing 相当多的那些 wildfires（例如，见[这里](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)的 p. 55）。[所有 fires 的 98%](https://www.vox.com/2015/9/17/9347361/wildfire-management-prescribed-burn) 都在 grow larger than 300 acres 之前被 stopped，而其中大多数很可能位于 firefighters 最容易抵达的 areas，更靠近 urban areas，并且大多 caused by humans。
-   与其他 causes 相比，Power line fires 在 total fires 中所占的 amount 小得令人惊讶（除非那些 'Unknowns' 中有许多实际上是 power lines。我在 dataset 中发现了两起 fires，Thomas 和 Witch，它们曾被 classified 为 Miscellaneous，但现在被认为是 caused by faulty power lines）
-   在 Los Angeles 一带，几乎每一起 fire 都是由 human-causes 引起的。在 North California，大部分 fire 往往由 lightning 引起，而在 Bay Area 一带，两者都有。
-   在 2000-2020 这段时期，fires 之间通常没有太多 overlap：已经 burned 的地方不会再次 burn（或许在原先那场 fire 后长达 10 年内都是如此）。如果有更多时间，应该可以用 FRAP data 对此进行更 detailed analysis。在某些 areas，trees 没有重新长回来（Stevens-Rumann，[2019](https://fireecology.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s42408-019-0032-1)），terrain 正在被 converted 为 [chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaparral)。而 chaparral 又正在被 converter 为 [grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_coastal_prairie#:%7E:text=California%20coastal%20prairie%2C%20also%20known,California%20up%20into%20southern%20Oregon.) 和 sage [scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_sage_scrub)（Syphard [2018](https://d2k78bk4kdhbpr.cloudfront.net/media/content/files/K2018_Syphard_type_conversion_chapter.pdf)）
-   Highways 是良好但 imperfect 的 firebreaks；I-5 有一段 4-lane 宽的路段，曾有 fire spreading 穿过它。即使是这条有多个 levels、lanes 多得数不清的 [freeway](https://www.google.com/maps/place/San+Fernando+Rd,+California/@34.3321153,-118.5170132,3678m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m5!3m4!1s0x80c2910bd789f097:0xf319f00457897e3f!8m2!3d34.2264051!4d-118.3788733)，也未能 stop 2019 年的 [Saddle Ridge](https://wildfiretoday.com/tag/saddle-ridge-fire/) fire。
-   Prescribed fires 正在增加，与 public authorities 的各种声明和承诺一致。或许还没有达到应有的程度（后文讨论），但方向是对的。

## 哪里由谁负责？

California 的 [land](https://www.nrcm.org/documents/publiclandownership.pdf) [ownership](https://fas.org/sgp/crs/misc/R42346.pdf)，简化来说，分属于

-   Private landowners（私人土地所有者，~40%）
-   Government（政府）
    -   Local government（地方政府）
    -   The State of California（加利福尼亚州，2.25%）
    -   Federal government（联邦政府，占所有 land 的 45.4%，主要 agencies 如下）
        -   Bureau of Land Management（土地管理局，BLM, 33%）
        -   US Forest Service（美国林务局，FS, 45%）
        -   US Park Service（美国公园管理局，PS, 16%）
        -   Department of Defense（国防部，DoD, 3.7%）

Starrs et al.（[2018](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aaaad1/meta)）有一张方便查阅的 chart，展示了谁 owns 什么，更重要的是，谁负责在哪里 fighting fires。在 State areas，由 CALFIRE 进行 firefighting，而 federal land 则由 manage 相关 land 的各个 agencies 的 firefighters 负责：

![图 2.](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/erlaaaad1f2_online.jpg)

作者暗示，谁 manages 这些 land 可能很重要，

California 没有单一的“fire czar”；取而代之的是一份 _[California Master Cooperative Wildland Fire Management and Stafford Act Response Agreement](https://gacc.nifc.gov/oscc/cwcg/docs/2013cfma/!!2018%20-%202023%20CFMA%20FINAL%20SIGNED.pdf)_，其中陈述并旨在解决一些显而易见的问题，例如

>     
> 2.  对本 Agreement 的 Agencies 而言，在其 areas of responsibility 内及 adjacent 地区，协调 wildland fire 的 investigation、prevention、detection 和 response 工作，以及与 fuels management 相关、包括 prescribed fire 在内的 projects，符合各方共同利益。这会 improves efficiency 和 effectiveness，并 limits duplication。
>     

它还设立了一批 cross-agency entities，主要是 [California Wildland Fire Coordinating Group](https://gacc.nifc.gov/oscc/cwcg/)，这是 California 最接近负责整个 territory 的单一 entity 的机构。这份 agreement 实际上将所有 firefighting resources pools 到一个松散的 coalition 中。然而，它并没有 effectively unify 各 agencies 的 forest management practices。这就是为什么，如果一个 agency 的 prevention activities 经费用尽，另一个 agency 的 [firefighters](https://www.kqed.org/news/11619715/wildfires-burning-through-cash-not-just-landscape) 不能直接过来代替他们完成工作，

> 因此，实地 reduce fires 的 intensity 的实际工作没有得到完成，或正在被推迟，”Cal Fire 的 director Ken Pimlott 说。“这实际上只会进一步 exacerbates fires 的 intensity，因为我们无法进入尤其是 federal ground，去对 fuels 进行 treated。

To make it worse, tree NIMBYism is a thing, and so proposed activities to manage a forest (e.g. prescribed burns) often face [opposition](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/california-managing-its-forests-president-managing-its-federal-lands-n942581);

> wildlands 的众多 overseers——交织在一起的 federal、state 和 local agencies，以及数千名 private owners——让制定 solutions 变得复杂。如果 public officials 无法回应有关 air quality、water purity、wildlife preservation 以及 cultural and historical preservation 的担忧，那么对任何 parcel 进行 cut 或 burn 的 permit 都可能被搁置。

## 其他地方的 Trends。总体上是否正在恶化？

无论是 US 总体，还是 California 本身，都没有关于遥远过去的良好 records。但其他国家可能有。Canada 的 wildfires 的 number 和 area 多年来呈现 [declining](https://cwfis.cfs.nrcan.gc.ca/ha/nfdb)，此前则经历了一段在 90s 达到顶点的 [increasing](http://www.fao.org/3/ae158e/AE158E06.htm) trend。与 US 一样，Canada 直到 70s 都在 [engaged](https://www.nrcan.gc.ca/our-natural-resources/forests-forestry/wildland-fires-insects-disturban/forest-fires/fire-management/13157) 于 fire suppression 的 policy，但也许他们向 prescribed burns 的转变更为积极？Spain（[Urbieta](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02925832/document) et al., 2019; Montiel-Molina et al. [2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-6255/2/3/44)）以及更 [broadly](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345) 的 Mediterranean region，尽管 fire 的 risk increase，fire 却正在减少（这是由于更好的 management techniques），但 [Australia](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2019-09-10/more-than-climate-change-driving-queensland-fires/11493950) 并非如此（那里的一些 areas 存在 upward trend，部分但并非完全是由于 climate change）。

在 Globally 的层面，存在 fires 更少、但更大的 trend（Doerr & Santin, [2016](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345)）。其 concluding section 与前面提到的“fire deficit”观点一致，值得长篇引用，因为他们当时声称不存在 trend：这也许曾经是真的，但现在已不再如此（这篇文章写于 2016 年）。

> 明确 examined fire severity 的 trends 的 studies 很少。这些 studies 主要 focused 于 western USA，该 area 尤其担忧 increased fire activity $$[42](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C42),[70](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C70)$$。在 Examining north- and southwest USA 的 large ecoregions 从 1984 到 2006 的 trends 时，Dillon _et al_. $$[71](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C71)$$ found，**在所 considered 的 six 个 regions 中，five 个的 annual area burned at high severity 的 proportion 没有 significant increase**，例外是 southern Rockies。对于此前 study $$[71](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C71)$$ 未 covered 的 Sierra Nevada region（California），Hanson & Odion $$[72](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C72),[73](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C73)$$ found，在 1984–2010 期间，fire severity 没有 general increase。在 Considering 同一时期 California 的 ten 个 national forests 时，**Miller & Safford $$[74](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C74)$$ found，yellow pine–mixed conifer forests 的 burn severity 存在 significant increase。他们主要将此 attribute 于数十年的 fire suppression 和其他 management practices，而非 climate；这些 practices 导致了 forest composition 和 structure 的 major changes、density 和 fuel-loading 的 increases，进而也改变了 fire behaviour。**Baker $$[75](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C75)$$ 的研究 Covering 了 western USA 的 dry forest landscapes 这一大得多的 area，其中包括上述 studies 所 examined 的大部分地区；他 found，1984–2012 期间 high-severity fire 的 rate 处于或低于 historical century- to millennial-scale estimates 的水平。
> 
> 因此，虽然有 evidence 表明 California 某个 specific forest type 的 proportional fire severity 最近存在 increase，但这些 independent studies 并不 support 这样一种 notion：过去几十年里，western USA 的 fire-adapted forested landscapes 的 fire severity 存在 overall increase。**事实上，Mallek _et al_. $$[49](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C49)$$ 针对 Californian Sierra Nevada 和 Cascades 的 longer term perspective suggests，1984 至 2009 年间的 annual area burned at high severity 仅为 European settlement 之前（约 1500–1850）的 half，这与相较 pre-European times 而言 overall 更小的 area burned 相关。**burn severity 总体上缺乏 change 的情况是否也 applies 于其他同样被认为存在 fire severity 的 increases 的 regions，在出现 robust data 来 test 这一 notion 之前，仍然无法回答。

## Fire suppression、fire exclusion 与 prescribed fire

以 extinguishing fires 为目标的一组 activities 称为 fire suppression。另一方面，Fire exclusion 是一种尽快 putting off 所有 fires、且不 starting 任何新 fires 的 policy。在默认情况下，wildfires 会 burn，直到再没有可供 burn 的 fuel，但这并不理想，因为这些 fire 可能 generate pollution，并造成 loss of life and property。因此，想要 suppress 每一场 fire 似乎很自然。Fire 有点吓人，而且难以 control。

但这属于 [Talebian](https://fs.blog/2011/06/suppressing-volatility/) 情形之一：systems 需要一定的 volatility，才能保持比没有 volatility 时更 stable。如今人们普遍认为，这类 policies（fire exclusion）是不好的：它们让 _过多的_ fuel 得以 accumulate，导致 larger、也更 devastating 的 fires。

在 [1910 年的一系列 large fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_1910) 之后，US 决定自己[已经受够了](https://foresthistory.org/research-explore/us-forest-service-history/policy-and-law/fire-u-s-forest-service/u-s-forest-service-fire-suppression/)，并力图 prevent 任何及所有 fires 的发生。1935 年，一项 policy 得以 instituted，规定 _每一场 fire 都应在其 initial report 后一天的 10 a.m. 前被 suppressed_。最初为什么要 fight wildfires 呢？与其说是出于 safety 或 health concerns，不如说是出于 economic 考量：burnt forests 不是你能通过 [logging](https://foresthistory.org/research-explore/us-forest-service-history/policy-and-law/fire-u-s-forest-service/u-s-forest-service-fire-suppression/) 来 profit 的 forests。如今 logging 和 wood-related activities 只占 California 的 GDP 的一小部分（~1%，即 [39 billion](https://anrcatalog.ucanr.edu/pdf/8670.pdf)/ 3 trillion），与其他考量（例如 health 和 protecting life and property）相比，这一 justification 就更弱了。

1970 年，这些 policies 开始 reversal，而如今的 consensus position 是，starting 一些 fires 可以成为一种有价值的 tool（Stephens & [Ruth](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1890/04-0545), 2005）。

早期的 Forest Service 并不愚蠢；他们确实委托开展了 studies，探讨是否应进行 prescribed burns，而在多年的 debate 之后，当时的答案是否定的：任何 fire 都被认为太 dangerous，

> maximum protection 或 fire exclusion 会通过促进 undergrowth 而 inevitably increases hazard，这当然是真的，但这种 added hazard 绝不会使 protection 的理由失效。这是一种 additional danger，但也是可以自愿接受的 danger。（[Show & Kotok](https://ir.library.oregonstate.edu/concern/defaults/05741x809?locale=en), 1924；另见 [Keeley](https://www.publish.csiro.au/WF/pdf/WF20112), 2021，他指出，这项 policy 得以实施的部分原因，似乎是 logging industry 的一定程度的 lobbying：burnt trees 的价值更低）

但尽管官方宣称 prescribed fire 如今已获认可，它似乎仍未像大多数 reports 所说的那样得到充分 practiced。我猜，这是因为改变 firefighting culture，不能只靠说一句“我们的 culture 如今不同了”。

> 在 NPS revised 其 fire policy 后不久，USFS 也这样做了。USFS 的 Director of Fire and Aviation Management Henry Debruin 表示：“我们决心保留过去最好的部分，同时把一个 basic concept 从 fire 是不好的，改为 fire 既是好的也是不好的”（DeBruin 1974:12）。虽然这番话代表着 USFS 的 philosophy 发生了重大 shift，但在接下来的数十年里，fire suppression 仍将 dominate agency policy（Franklin and Agee 2003）。（[Stephens](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1890/04-0545) & Ruth, 2005）

即使在 2020 年，仍能找到讨论进一步推行这项 policy 所面临 bottlenecks 的 articles（Miller et al., [2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-019-0451-7)）。California 不仅没有进行足够的 prescribed burns，甚至连已经 planned 的那些都没有完成（dashed line 是 planned，black 是 actual）。至少它正在 trending up：

![image-20210621211929080](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210621211929080.png)

但 2018 年 burned 的 30k acres 并不足够：California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection 在 2010 年 estimated，有 20 million acres 需要 treatment。即使将每年的 burn 提高到每年 100k acres，仍需 200 年才能完成全部 burning。Miller et al. 实际呼吁的 number 是每年 1.01 million，这相当可观，约为 [2020 wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_California_wildfires) 中 burned 的 total surface 的 20%。

那么，是什么在阻碍 California 达到这一 target 呢？Miller et al. 询问了一系列 stakeholders：

-   在 federal lands 上，如果认为有必要，可以让 wildfires burn（这可以用来 clear up fuels）。在 CAL FIRE managed 的 areas，这却是 illegal 的：该 agency _必须_ extinguish 所有 unplanned fire。让 CAL FIRE 能够利用 wildfires 来达到 treatment 目的（“managed wildfire”），将是一大进步。不过，需要 treatment 的 land 大部分是 federally owned，因此这并不是一个特别重大的关键 bottleneck。在 Sierra Nevada，US National Park Service（而 US Forest Service 则没有这么多）一直在利用 wildfires 进行 treatment，效果很好，以至于在 1968-2017 年间，通过 managed wildfire 得到 treated 的 area，与进行 prescribed burn 的 area 相近（[Keeley](https://www.publish.csiro.au/WF/pdf/WF20112) et al., 2021）
    -   虽然大多数 interviewees 将 managed wildfire 誉为 fuel treatments 的未来，但由于 legal responsibilities 和 cross-jurisdictional agreement 的需要，在不久的将来，它仍不太可能用于 state and private land。因此，Interviewees 强调了扩大 prescribed burns 的重要性，尤其是在 non-federal lands 上，以实现 fire 的 ecological benefits。
    -   需要 prescribed burn 的 land 大部分是 federally owned
-   如果一场 prescribed burn escapes（其中 1.76% 确实如此），burner 就会面临重大的 legal and financial penalties。[SB 1260](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=201720180SB1260) 对此作出了处理：如果 burn 满足某些 safety criteria，包括通过从 2018 年开始提供的 certification course，就可免除他们的 liability。
-   在 federal lands 上，使用 prescribed burns 的 managers 得不到任何好处，但如果他们的 fires escape，就要承担 personal liability。
-   没有足够的 trained crews 来完成所有需要进行的 prescribed burning
-   California Air Resources Board (CARB) 的 burn windows 太 narrow。CARB 说，他们提供的 burn days 比最终被使用的更多；另一方面，landowners 则说 CARB 太 restrictive。想必是因为有 team 可以进行 burn 的时候，并不是 CARB 允许 burn 的时候。可以为 prescribed fires 放宽 air quality regulations：按计划暂时 worsening（这样 affected population 就能做好准备），比持续数月的 bad air quality 更好。但 interviewees 不愿要求为 prescribed fires 弱化 environmental regulations。California Air Resources Board [知道](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/prescribed-burning/about)需要更多 prescribed fire，而且早在 2019 年，他们就在[呼吁](https://ww3.arb.ca.gov/smp/CARB%20Informational%20Hearing%20on%20Prescribed%20Fire.pdf)“Significantly increase 通过 prescribed fire 得到 treated 的 acres 数量。”或“Streamline regulatory and permitting processes。”目前，prescribed fires 都在 favorable weather 下进行（想想 wind），这就是它们对 nearby populations 的 impact 较小的原因（[Xiaoxi](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2016JD026315) et al., 2017），但要 burning 所有需要 burn 的地方，恐怕就需要对这些 air quality regulations 作出 transitory exceptions。
    -   NEPA 和 CEQA 等 [Environmental](https://reason.com/2021/04/06/why-isnt-california-safer-from-wildfires/) regulations 给 burns 的 justifying 带来沉重 burden，减少了它们的使用。如果错过 approved burn window，就必须重新申请 approval。
-   state-level 的高层似乎对 prescribed burns 很支持
-   作者们认为，正在实施的 policies 和 solutions 的整体 mix 过于 conservative，不太可能达到所需的 fuel treatment goal。

## firefighting trap：消防陷阱

在任何一年，firefighting agency 都必须在 fighting fires 与 preventing 它们之间 allocate resources。在某种理想化的情境中，人们会 models 开展每项 activity 将会发生什么，以及这如何随时间 affects fire hazard，并据此 allocates resources。

现实中，agencies 并不是这样 operate 的。例如，无论还有什么其他考量，CAL FIRE 都_别无选择_，必须 extinguish 其 area of responsibility 内的所有 fires，唯一真正的 constraint 是 firefighting crew availability。从事 prevention 的 crews 也从事 suppression，而随着 fire season 的延长，原本会用于为下一个 season 做准备的 money 和 crews，正在应对当前这个 season。所谓 _firefighting trap_（Moreira et al., [2020](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ab541e/pdf)），就是在 short term 内 fighting fires，却以忽视 prevention 和为未来 fires 做 preparation 为代价，结果反而产生越来越多 fire 的情形。要打破这种 self-defeating firefighting practices 的 cycle，可能需要采取带有政治风险的行动，例如修改 CAL FIRE 的 charter，允许其不 intervene 于 wildfires，甚至_默认_不 intervene，除非某场 fire 被 predicted 会超过某个 expected damage 的 threshold。这项 policy 带来的 Savings 将使 prevention activities 能够更频繁地开展。更现实地说，CAL FIRE 应当为这两类 activities 分别设立 ring-fenced budget，但即便如此，让一些 crews 不受干扰地开展 prevention projects，而数英里外的其他 crews 正拼命拯救生命、急需 reinforcements，在政治上能被接受吗？将 resources 转向 suppression activities 的诱惑始终存在。

## Native Americans 当时在这样做吗？

我反复遇到的一种说法是，在 ~这件事流行起来~ Europeans 抵达 America 之前，Native Americans 就已经在 [engaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_use_of_fire_in_ecosystems) 于 [prescribed](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2019/nov/21/wildfire-prescribed-burns-california-native-americans) burns。围绕 prescribed fires，似乎存在某种 political coding：“prescribed fires 才是解决问题的办法，government 把事情弄得更糟了”是 conservative position，而“实际上，wildfires 是由 climate change 导致的”则是 progressive position。

补上一句“Native Americans 当时就在这样做”，或许是想让这一 position 听起来更 progressive、更 natural，或诸如此类（在这种说法中，prescribed fire 是回归 ancestral practices）。无论如何，他们当时真的在这样做吗？

Native Americans 确实出于各种原因 used fire，从 religious 原因，到 clearing land、increasing agricultural yield，再到减少 rival tribes 能够 hide 和 ambush 的地方（[Keeley](http://communitiesandlandscapes.org/files/original/af0f908e9fb310ada5934ab914543605a0c42c1e.pdf), 2002）。但这些 practices 在多大程度上 affected 大片 territory，仍有争议。不管怎样，Keeley 的确认为，这些 practices 带来了相当多的 fire，足以 shape 这一 region 的大片区域。

这一主题上似乎最 authoritative 的书籍 _Indians, Fire, and the Land in the Pacific Northwest_（[Boyd](https://www.amazon.com/Indians-Fire-Land-Pacific-Northwest/dp/0870714597), 1999）指出，Indians 确实并非真正在有意开展 fire 的 prevention；相反，即使他们确实使 larger fires 变得更少，这也只是他们为其他目的 use of fire 所产生的意外结果，

> burning 和其他 traditional management practices 的其他 ecological effects，今天看来显而易见，但在 Native Americans 的世界中，可能并没有这么明显或重要。在几种 forest types 中，understory and spot burning 作为 wildfires、disease outbreaks 和 extensive forest burns 的 preventive 所具有的 beneficial effects，如今对 Northwest forest managers 而言如此明显；这些 effects 之所以变得切合当前需要，是因为 fire suppression 这种不明智、unnatural（且 culturally specific）的 practice。

对于这些 practices 是否足以 substantially alter 整个 California 的 landscape，我不持确定看法。归根结底，我认为 prescribed burning 这种 practice 的目的，并不是给予 environment 它“naturally needs”的东西；nature 并不进行 prescribed burning。相反，这是试图先 undo 长期以来的 fire suppression policy 所造成的后果（这项 policy 同样是对事物 natural course 的 alteration），再让 California 成为一个比 nature 所允许的更宜居的地方：也许 fires 更多，但方式更安全，pollution 和 damage 更少。

## 那么，是 fire exclusion，还是 climate change？

只有少数 paper 尝试 jointly 考虑 climate change 和 fire exclusion。[Hanan](https://www.niskanencenter.org/megafires-climate-change-or-land-management/) et al.（[2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/abd78e/pdf)）使用我目前见过最 detailed 的 coupled hydrology-fire-ecology-climate 的 model（[RHESSys-WMFire](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014AGUFM.H54D..06K/abstract)），examine 了 Idaho 的两个不同 locations（Johnson Creek 和 Trail Creek），而……情况很复杂。Fire exclusion（针对 fire 的 zero-tolerance policy）既可能 worsen，也可能 improve fires 的 number 和 area。climate change 也是如此！后一个 result 的原因在于，虽然 increasing aridity 会使 fuels 更 flammable，但当 aridity 增加到某个程度时（对于给定的 vegetation type），新 vegetation 的 growth rate 会受到抑制，因此 fires 就会更少。

> Fire regimes——由 fire 随时间变化的 frequency、intensity、size、season 和 severity 来 characterized——存在于一条 aridity continuum 上，其范围从受 flammability（即 climate）limited，一直到受 fuel limited（Krawchuk and Moritz 2011, Pausas and Paula 2012）。在 climate-limited systems 中，通常有足够的 fuel 供 fire spread，但 moisture 过高；这些 systems 的特点是 infrequent、severe fires，它们发生在 unusually arid conditions 期间。在 fuel-limited systems 中，climate 是 relatively arid，且 conducive to fire，因此 fire activity 主要由现存 live and dead plant material 的 amount 所 driven；这些 systems 通常经历 frequent、low-severity surface fires。

那么，有没有可能“fire deficit”实际上是由 climate change 而不是 fire exclusion caused 的？这极不可能：如果 California 整体已经达到 aridity 的 vegetation-reducing effect overwhelms 其 fire-inducing effect 的程度，我们就会在过去几十年中看到 aridity 与 fire 之间存在 inverse correlation；但上文引用的 Abatzoglou et al. papers 显示情况并非如此：目前，更多 aridity 就意味着更多 fire。

## 结论（Conclusion）

在这里，我回顾了关于 California 的 wildfires 正在发生什么变化、以及未来走向的各方面 evidence。我明确_没有_讨论我们应该对此采取什么行动，这将在本系列的第 2 部分展开。总而言之，目前的情况如下：

-   California 每年的 Burned acres 相对于 1950 正在 increasing。据 predicted，这将继续 increase，但这种 increase 不会是 linear。有些年份的 fire 会很少，另一些年份则会出现 2020-scale mega fires。
-   这种 fire quantity 在 California 的历史上并非从未出现过：在 pre-1800 时期，fires 曾经更多，而在 European colonization 期间，California 的 fires 还要更多。
-   大多数（>80%）fires 的 immediate cause 是 attributable to humans。不过，对于 _large_ fires，情况并非如此。Lightning strikes 仍在 igniting fires 中发挥 significant role，不应被忽视。wildfires 并没有某一个 main contributor。请看那些五花八门的 "miscellaneous" explanations。
-   zero-tolerance fire suppression，也就是 fire exclusion，这种 practice 已导致整个 California 的 field buildup。这又与一个格外 wet 的时期叠加，带来 strong plant growth，同时伴随着 lower fire incidence。这个 wetter 时期的结束，加上 climate change 导致的 rising temperatures，共同将 California 带到了当前的境地。
-   即使没有 climate change，我们仍会看到比 1950-1980 时期更多的 fire；也就是说，通常用来说明 climate change 对 wildfires increase 的 effects 的 graphs，_overstate_ 了 climate change 的 effect magnitude，同时 understate 了 natural variation 和 human firefighting practices。
-   在我看来，Californian institutions 是 weak 的，尽管要充分论证这一点，会让这篇文章变得更长。某些 regulations（让 PG&E 规矩行事、building fire-safe WUI houses）无论在 individual 还是 corporate level，都没有得到 enforced。这解释了各种详细描述正在实施哪些 measures 的 action plans 和 roadmaps，与实际情况之间的 disconnect。想法已经写进了正确的文件，却没有落实为行动。
-   California 正在阻碍 market mechanisms（即 insurance）充分 operating，以 prevent highly fire-prone areas 的 construction
-   总体而言，关于 wildfires 究竟发生了什么，存在 systematic 且 comprehensive 的 good quality information 缺失，其中甚至包括_许多 wildfires 的 cause！_。_[Cost of Wildfire in California](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)_ (2020) report 反复提到这一点。理想情况下，应该有一个 central website，包含便于获取的 datasets、predictive models、incident reports、budgets 等，并提供充分的来源依据与更新。
-   正确的问题，不是回头追问 driver 究竟是 climate change 还是 fire suppression。正确的问题是今后该怎么做，尤其要 accounting for 各种 political 和 social constraints。即使没有 climate change、fire exclusion 或 weather oscillations，_California 的大片地区也会 naturally burn_。因此，问题就变成了：
    -   考虑到 Californians 所关心的事情，California 究竟有多少地方_应该_ burn？而不是接受一个 pristine、未经我们触碰的 California 中默认的 acres burned 数量。
    -   我们该如何实现这一点？

## Is this data valid?

How do we know how many acres burn per year? There are various sources of data:

-   There is satellite imagery taken regularly by the Landsat satellites and others. It's then a matter of comparing before/after pictures, drawing a polygon, and [measuring](https://www.esri.com/content/dam/esrisites/en-us/media/pdf/teach-with-gis/measuring-fires.pdf) its area. It doesn't seem to matter from which satellite the data comes from, the results are very similar ([Edwards et al., 2018](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0034425717306223)). The first Landsat satellite was launched in 1972, so prior to that we just don't have satellite data for fire.
-   Then there's the written records from various fire agencies which go all the way to the 1900s. Some of these records use data derived from airborne cameras
-   For even older data, area burned can be inferred from [fire scarring](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Tree-ring-evidence-of-fires-includes-cross-dated-fire-scars-and-abrupt-increases-or_fig1_228116871) in trees. This works up until ~1700, and beyond that the data gets noisier
-   One can also sample the soil and measure charcoal accumulation. A paper I discuss later uses this method to go over a millennia into the past

This question has been raised within the literature regarding the reliability of the written records, the consensus is that they are trustworth (Keeley & Syphard [2018](https://www.publish.csiro.au/wf/pdf/WF18026)),

> Most investigators are unfamiliar with these historical fire records and are sometimes sceptical of their accuracy. For example, Stephens (2005) contended that USFS data before 1940 were inaccurate, but cited a source (Mitchell 1947) that provided no evidence of this. Likely, the idea comes from Donoghue’s (1982b) comment ‘1940 marked the modern era of fire reporting’. However, that comment was in reference to the fact that ‘the report issued at this time was the first designed for automated data processing and easy readability’ and was not in reference to reliability. Historians have generally been confidant in these early California fire records (Brown 1945; Show 1945; Clar 1969; Cermak 2005). The first author, J. E. Keeley, examined all of the California fire-related materials stored at the state and federal archives and believes collectively they show managers have always been conscientious about reporting accuracy and completeness. For example, beginning in 1905, USFS recordkeeping required 15 items of information on the fire reporting Form 944, including the specific cause (Donoghue 1982b). On state-protected lands there was an incentive in that the 1911 Federal Weeks Law provided fiscal aid to states based on statistics of fire protection (see http://www.calfire.ca.gov/ about/about\_calfire\_history2, accessed 23 May 2018).
> 
> In 1919, the California state legislature appropriated money for fire prevention and suppression, and records in the state archive show that, by 1920, there were more than 400 fire wardens distributed throughout the state who were charged with firefighting and fire reporting. In 1920, there were 800 flights of the Army’s 9th Aero Squadron fire patrol that covered 426 500 km during the 5-month California fire season (Cermak 1991).

## The role of climate change

This is a position held by some like Michael Shellenberger, see [here](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2020/09/10/why-environmental-alarmism-makes-forest-fires-worse/), [here](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2020/08/24/stop-blaming-climate-change-for-californias-fires-many-forests-including-the-redwoods-need-them/), and especially [here](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2019/11/04/why-everything-they-say-about-california-fires--including-that-climate-matters-most--is-wrong/). He points to other factors driving the recent increase in fires, especially _fire suppression_, or rather fire exclusion: the practice (that I will discuss later) of not allowing any fire in the environment, which leads to the accumulation of fuels, and larger fires years later. Shellenberger has many things right but the overall message is wrong, there is enough evidence that climate change is a key player in the wildfire situation in California. More on this later.

Another factor others point to is a substantial increase in housing construction in the wildland-urban interface (WUI), which is mentioned by a scientist cited in the piece (Keeley) leading to more fires. More people living near wildland areas means also more roads (vehicles are a nontrivial source of ignitions), more powerlines, more campfires (easier to go camping when nature is close to home), and more accidents that can lead to fire in general.

Yet another factor that modulates the role of climate on wildfires (Keeley & Syphard, [2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42408-019-0041-0)), in the short term at least, is that higher rainfall in one year leads to more fires the next (more vegetation grows). Indeed the "[wettest](https://www.mercurynews.com/2021/05/24/whats-causing-californias-worsening-drought/)" year in California ever far (2016-2017) was followed by intense fires (2018).

And in turn these periods of higher or lower rainfall may be driven by natural oscillations in the climate (like the Pacific Decadal Oscillation or the related Atlantic Multidecadal oscillation). A paper from 2007, [Kitzberger](https://www.pnas.org/content/104/2/543) et al. noted that _A current warming trend in AMO suggests that we may expect an increase in widespread, synchronous fires across the western U.S. in coming decades._, which is precisely what happened.

\[2\]. There's a more conservative estimate in the paper that would reduce the contribution of climate change by 19%; that means that instead of 55% more burned area we got 40% more burned area due to climate change which is still alarming

If one wanted to see how much of the trend is attributable to climate change one would have to model those oscillations (and control away the impact of human activities like forest management). Most such analyses use data from 1950 onwards, like [Abatzoglou & Williams](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/113/42/11770.full.pdf) (2016). Nothing wrong with that! That time period should cover a chunk of cold and warm Pacific Decadal oscillations, but ideally we'd like to see a model that reconstructs the entire observed dataset of fires. Be that as it may, what the authors find is that _half_[2](https://nintil.com/wildfires-california/#sidenote-2) of the increase in aridity of the fuels present in US forests (and presumably, California) was due to anthropogenic climate change, with the rest being natural variation (possibly the oscillations described earlier). In turn, aridity is strongly correlated with burned area in a given year,

![image-20210617211630880](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210617211630880.png)

Note that the Abatzoglou et al. paper does not account for a long list of effects, including human activity preventing or fighting fires, and this list contains causes that could both increase or decrease the fraction of wildfires attributable to climate change.

> In focusing exclusively on the direct impacts of ACC on fuel aridity, **we do not address several other pathways by which ACC may have affected wildfire activity.** For example, the fuel aridity metrics that we used may not adequately capture the role of mountain snow hydrology on soil moisture. Nor do we account for the influence of climate change on lightning activity, which may increase with warming (40). We also do not account for how fire risk may be affected by changes in biomass/fuel due to increases in atmospheric CO2 (41), drought-induced vegetation mortality (42), or insect outbreaks (43). **Additionally, we treat the impact of ACC on fire as independent from the effects of fire management (e.g., suppression and wildland fire use policies), ignitions, land cover (e.g., exurban development), and vegetation changes beyond the degree to which they modulate the relationship between fuel aridity and forest fire area.** These factors have likely added to the area burned across the western US forests and potentially amplified the sensitivity of wildfire activity to climate variability and change in recent decades (2, 22, 24, 44). Such confounding influences, along with nonlinear relationships between burned area and its drivers (e.g., Fig. 1), contribute uncertainty to our empirical attribution of regional burned area to ACC. Our approach depends on the strong observed regional relationship between burned area and fuel aridity at the large regional scale of the western United States, so the quantitative results of this attribution effort are not necessarily applicable at finer spatial scales, for individual fires, or to changes in nonforested areas.

Aridity here is a mean of eight different metrics of "fuel aridity",

> Four metrics were calculated from monthly data for 1948–2015: (i) reference potential evapotranspiration (ETo), (ii) VPD, (iii) CWD, and (iv) Palmer drought severity index (PDSI). The other four metrics are daily fire danger indices calculated for 1979–2015: (v) fire weather index (FWI) from the Canadian forest fire danger rating system, (vi) energy release component (ERC) from the US national fire danger rating system, (vii) McArthur forest fire danger index (FFDI), and (viii) Keetch– Byram drought index (KBDI).

As an aside, if we pick one of them, like vapor pressure deficit, we get the same results.

## Vapor Pressure Deficit

VPD _measures_ _the difference between the amount of moisture in the air and how much moisture the air could potentially hold when it’s saturated.It’s often measured in pounds per square inch (psi) or kilopascal (kPa).A high VPD (greater than 1.0 kPa) means that the air can still hold a large amount of water_. High VPD means that rather than droplets of water staying on or in plants, they will be sucked up into the air (warmer air can carry more water), thus high VPD leads to drier terrains and drier terrains leads to, in general, more fire. VPD also correlates reasonably well with burned area across the Western United States in general and California specifically (Higuera & Abatzoglou, [2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/gcb.15388), Park Williams et al. [2019](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1029/2019EF001210?utm_source=mp-fotoscapes)). The relationship seems exponential: More VPD, exponentially more fires.:

![image-20210625174439995](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210625174439995.png)

![image-20210625174717818](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210625174717818.png)

![image-20210625174812700](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210625174812700.png)

It won't be too surprising to say that VPD is going up as temperatures rise, but also note that it also presents an oscillatory component (black) that is not well captured by the CMIP5 climate model ensemble (red). This oscillation may help explain why there were so many fires pre 1950, why there was so little fire in the intermediate years, and why there is so much fire now. Eyeballing the period of the oscillation, it may be caused by the [Pacific Decadal Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_decadal_oscillation#/media/File:PDO.svg). If so, we may have reached a peak and will get some rest from the rising trend from a few years, but given the underlying rising trend from climate change, that won't be more than a temporary slowdown of the increase rather than an absolute respite:

![image-20210625175012334](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210625175012334.png)

Note that the correlation should break down at sufficiently high VPDs: once a lot of California has burned, that naturally delays the occurrence of further fires.

California is indeed getting more dry over the years even when considering data going back more than 100 years which should cancel our periodic oscillations: there [remains](https://www.ncdc.noaa.gov/cag/divisional/time-series/0402/pdsi/1/5/1895-2021?base_prd=true&begbaseyear=1901&endbaseyear=2000&trend=true&trend_base=10&begtrendyear=1895&endtrendyear=2021&filter=true&filterType=binomial) a trend towards dryness.,

![image-20210618105458861](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210618105458861.png)

So climate change is acting in the expected direction: California is getting [warmer and drier](https://twitter.com/RARohde/status/1061932205327429632) and these two, by virtue of the thermodynamics of combustion will lead to, ceteris paribus, more fire in the future. Note that effect is true in general but not always. While drier fuels burn more, if the weather is sufficiently arid, plants will just stop growing: in the very extreme case, that's a reason why the Sahara desert doesn't see much fire activity despite it being an extremely hot and dry place. Later on I discuss a study that finds this dynamic in a specific area of California.

## The historical fire record

As I mentioned earlier, a key point of the "it's not climate change" argument is that there used to be substantially more fire in the pre-1800s past than there is now.

In 2020 around 4.4M acres burned which was considered to be the most acres ever burned in the record. And indeed it is, unless we go before 1950. If we do that, going all the way pre-1800, we find that the **average** acres that burn in any given year is 1.8M hectares, or around 4.4M acres (Stephens et al., [2007](https://www.sierraforestlegacy.org/Resources/Conservation/FireForestEcology/FireScienceResearch/FireHistory/FireHistory-Stephens07.pdf)). This average is equal to the worst year ever in recent history, so one could tell a story here of California returning to normal after a period of trying to fight the inevitable (the climate-given steady state of burned acres per year). Should we tell this story? Michael [Shellenberger](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2020/09/10/why-environmental-alarmism-makes-forest-fires-worse/) goes for that, where the rarity is not the recent uptick in fires, but the anomalously low amount of burns that preceded the current era, fruit of a century of aggressively fighting fires, which leads to overgrown forests, creating more fuel for fires to spread. This wouldn't mean that climate change is not contributing to the effect, increased temperatures and aridity would contribute to an increase in that steady state of burned acres per year, it's just that we are now being pulled back from an unsustainably low level. [Shellenberger](https://www.forbes.com/sites/michaelshellenberger/2020/08/24/stop-blaming-climate-change-for-californias-fires-many-forests-including-the-redwoods-need-them/) goes as far as saying that we should probably stop blaming climate change for the fires. As I hope my discussion above clarifies, climate change is playing a role.

There's more to pre-1950 fire records than the 1800s data. That was also an era of building railroads, burning forests to clear up land for farming, and having less knowledge of firefighting or even having means to suppress some of those fires. We should go even further into the past, and there's some work doing that: Marlon et al. ([2011](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/109/9/E535.full.pdf)) say that considering even longer time scales while there was a peak in fire activity centered in ~1890 that wasn't the situation all the way into the past. Prior to that there was, it is true, more fire than there is now, but not as much as that intermediate era.

Marlon et al. note that there is, as of the date of writing the paper, a "fire deficit" (noted also by Baker, [2015](https://europepmc.org/backend/ptpmcrender.fcgi?accid=PMC4564206&blobtype=pdf)) relative to what one would expect from historical climatic patterns: not enough is burning. A decade later, we're perhaps now seeing how that gap is being closed by means of the most damaging fire seasons ever.

## Why do the fires start?

Climate change and forest management may make fires worse once they start, but what triggers them?

Typically most of the damage caused by wildfire is due to a handful of fires ([Strauss et al., 1989](https://www.fs.fed.us/psw/publications/strauss/psw_1989_strauss001.pdf)); in the 2020 California wildfire season, the aggregated 38 fires that comprised the August Fire Complex burned almost a quarter of the entire surface area that burned that year, despite those 38 fires being just 0.38% of the 9917 fires reported during 2020. This particular fire was due to lightning storms. The same is true for the rest of the mega fires of 2020, the SCU Lightning Complex, LNU Lightning Complex, North Complex, or SQF Lightning Complex.

This is not always true: In 2018 the worst fire ever (by insured losses rather than area burned), the Camp Fire was started by a faulty power line; and more broadly there are more fires that start due to electric power lines than there are due to lightning,

![ Photo: Todd Trumbull](https://s.hdnux.com/photos/01/02/15/40/17356840/5/920x1240.jpg)

But we should remember that what matters is not necessarily what starts the most fires, but rather what leads to the most damage.

According to [Cal FIRE](https://www.fire.ca.gov/media/t1rdhizr/top20_destruction.pdf), 6 out of the top 20 most destructive fires (by structures burned and deaths) were due to faulty powerlines. Some of the other ones are due to trivial incidents as in the [Carr fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carr_Fire) case where _Coincidental to the name of the incident, the fire was started when a flat tire on a vehicle caused the wheel's rim to scrape against the asphalt, creating sparks that set off the fire._ Or the [Valley fire](https://www.cnn.com/2016/08/11/us/california-valley-fire-faulty-hot-tub/index.html) where _a wire at a poorly connected hot tub overheated, melted and ignited dry brush nearby at a home in Cobb town._ Or the [Mendocino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendocino_Complex_Fire) fire where _the \[Ranch\] Fire was started by a rancher who had inadvertently sparked dry grass while hammering a metal stake while trying to find a wasp nest._

Thus, from a prevention side, whereas there is a long tail of potential causes of fire that are hard to control, maintaining or under grounding powerlines is the one thing that could be done to reduce the most devastating of fires in terms of human lives and damaged structures.

## Natural fires matter: health costs

On the other hand if we care about acres burned the thing to do is worry about lightning-started fires, and we should care about this because it's likely that the structures that burned+deaths approach to quantifying damage underestimates the true costs of wildfires. One would have to add the economic costs of individual wildfire mitigation (buying air purifiers), the inability to enjoy the relevant natural areas (from which many derive great value), or the damage from millions of people [breathing](https://patrickcollison.com/pollution) polluted air. Acreage should not be "the" metric to reduce (as suggested [here](https://www.npr.org/2020/10/07/921209244/four-million-acres-have-burned-in-california-why-thats-the-wrong-number-to-focus)), but it is still a major part of the equation. To make the point more clear, there is work attempting to quantify the impact of wildfire-produced air pollution on health; [Wang et al. (2021)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-020-00646-7) estimate that the California wildfires of 2018 alone, via increases in mortality risk, caused the death of 3652 people which is 35x more than the lives lost directly due to the fires[3](https://nintil.com/wildfires-california/#sidenote-3) . This is a very important point: this kind of indirect damage due to pollutants is _not always considered in cost-benefit analyses of the wildfire equation_: The National Fire Protection Association, which is often quoted as an authority for cost of wildfires does not include health-related costs in [their calculations](https://www.nfpa.org/%7E/media/fd0144a044c84fc5baf90c05c04890b7.ashx). Or the wikipedia cost calculation figure the 2018 wildfires cites a 26.35bn$ loss whereas the Wang et al. paper, adding in health losses and indirect (counterfactual loss of economic activity) losses has a total of 148.5bn$. Chapter 5 of [this](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf) has an extensive review of the effects of wildfires on health. The reason why this might be is that it's easier to notice that the damage is there (a burning building is easier to see than inflamed lungs) and assigning a value to rebuilding a building is easier than modeling the cost of that particulate matter getting into people's lungs. Fortunately, this is starting to be acknowledged and the [California's Wildfire and Forest Resilience and Action](https://fmtf.fire.ca.gov/media/cjwfpckz/californiawildfireandforestresilienceactionplan.pdf) plan has a section on reducing the health effects of smoke (p.34). The _[Cost of Wildfire in California](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)_ (2020) report considers health effects as well.

This is a very basic understanding of the issue, but one that gets us to a more informed level than [this PBS article](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/science/californias-catastrophic-wildfires-in-3-charts) that claims that 95% of fires are due to human causes. That may be the case, but most of fires are small and irrelevant, what we care about is causes, weighted by seriousness: fires caused by lightning strikes still should be a major cause of concern.

## The WUI conundrum

Many people in California live in the so-called Wildland-Urban Interface (WUI). I recently took a trip up to Larkspur (Marin County) to do some [research](https://www.google.com/maps/place/Posie/@37.9160269,-122.545898,12z/data=!4m5!3m4!1s0x0:0x76995d24f2f80820!8m2!3d37.9330498!4d-122.53573). This is what the WUI might look like at that particular place,

The WUI features repeatedly in the California wildfires discourse:

First, there's the point that new construction at the WUI has been increasing [rapidly](https://www.fs.fed.us/nrs/pubs/jrnl/2018/nrs_2018_radeloff_001.pdf) increasing both the damage caused to humans (hard to have wildfires in tree-less San Francisco) because of urban dwellers being [pushed out](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2019/10/can-california-save-itself/601135/) of cities due to increasing house prices as well as increased odds that human activity will cause more fires. This point is correct.

Second, as a prescriptive measure resulting from the above diagnosis, there are [calls for moving people out](https://gabekleinman.medium.com/how-i-learned-to-stop-worrying-and-love-the-smoke-90d61c8f2572) of the WUI. A few of the fires I mentioned earlier started here: the faulty hot tub that started the Valley Fire is another example of [WUI](https://www.google.com/maps/place/38%C2%B049'20.0%22N+122%C2%B043'23.0%22W/@38.8217209,-122.7224955,419m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m5!3m4!1s0x0:0x0!8m2!3d38.822222!4d-122.723056?hl=en). More people, more chances someone does something that could start a fire. Having more people near forests also requires having more electric lines (that for cost reasons tend to be built overground) which can also lead to accidental fires. To me the key issue is not whether people expose themselves to risks to their person and property by building there; rather the issue is that they are exposing _others_ to huge risks by being there.

I am very reluctant to try to get people out of the WUI. As you can see from the linked StreetViews, WUIs are a great place to live! Depriving some of that opportunity to save California is for now a false dilemma: [progress](https://nintil.com/progress-in-progress/) means not having to make tradeoffs and it may be that we can have our WUI cake and eat it as well.

There is a way out of this: Insurance. If people living in the WUI are both a major cause of fires as well as victims, can't insurance solve the problem? Higher premia would dissuade people from moving there, and -in a more extreme case worth exploring- could shift the responsibility of paying for causing the damage from the State to the insurers and through them, to the potential culprits, the same way it is done for nuclear reactors or cars. Mandatory third-party insurance, perhaps baked into location-dependent property taxes for wildfires would be a solution.

The closest we have, or rather [used to have](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/fire-prev-fee.htm), to this is the CAL FIRE fee levied on habitable buildings in state land that are not incorporated. The problem with this fee is twofold: one, it's the same for everyone (unlike regular insurance), where higher risk areas should be charged a higher premium. Second, it doesn't apply to everyone! While hot tub owners of [Cobb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobb,_California) would be paying it, those of [South Lake Tahoe](https://www.google.com/maps/@38.93958,-119.9460768,3a,60y,330.88h,90t/data=!3m6!1e1!3m4!1sXEpKa-t-Am2tq7-boXzZug!2e0!7i13312!8i6656) would not.

The insurance mechanism on the victim side (paying those that suffer from wildfire damage) is sadly broken due to government regulation. What should happen is that premia should increase in areas prone to fire which in turn help dissuade new construction there on the margin. The California government, for example [prevents](http://www.insurance.ca.gov/0250-insurers/0300-insurers/0200-bulletins/bulletin-notices-commiss-opinion/upload/Bulletin-2020-11-Mandatory-Moratorium-on-Cancellations-and-Non-Renewals-of-Policies-of-Residential-Property-Insurance-After-the-Declaration-of-a-State-of-Emergency-Amended-11_6_20.pdf) insurers from dropping coverage in wildfire-prone areas. Where they can, insurers are in fact [dropping](https://www.insurancejournal.com/news/west/2020/12/04/592788.htm) customers. Why would insurers want to drop valuable customers? Because again the California government doesn't let them increase premia to actuarially fair levels: they have to ask for [permission](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_briefs/RBA635-1.html) to raise them. Instead of a free market where insurers compete to fairly price risk, the imposition of price caps leads to the absence of service. There's a state-operated insurer of last resort, the FAIR plan which fortunately offers less comprehensive coverage than the private plans (So it ends up being expensive; perhaps not as expensive as it should be, but better than it would be if it were more comprehensive). Of course, California being California, the plan is to move in that direction, in effect offering a [subsidy](https://calmatters.org/commentary/my-turn/2020/05/states-insurer-of-last-resort-should-not-become-the-primary-insurance-market/) to those living in fire-prone areas.

\[4\]. In Spain—where I'm from—most construction is done with concrete blocks or bricks. When I moved to the UK and the US, upon learning that so much housing is still using wood, the practice seemed to me as barbaric as the existence of launderettes. Concrete or brick construction is not only fire-proof but also more soundproof, with less creaky floors and overall higher quality. The presence of this kind of construction in more developed countries is a continued cause of puzzlement for me. Ok [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wpxLLCdW_Gc) are some explanations. Another potentially easy solution is to have building standards that enforce so called _defensible space_ around the house, clearing up vegetation around the building, or building using fire-resistant materials (Sure we get it timber is cheap but concrete blocks are not that expensive[4](https://nintil.com/wildfires-california/#sidenote-4) ). Unfortunately again California is California: Whereas in San Francisco crime is de facto legal, current construction codes _already_ ask for house builders to build houses in a reasonable way to reduce damage from fire and for owners to upkeep those standards. But if you look at any WUI area _lots of people just don't do [that](https://www.sandiegouniontribune.com/news/environment/story/2019-06-11/californias-not-enforcing-wildfire-prevention-rules-for-homeowners-leaving-tens-of-thousands-of-properties-vulnerable-to-big-blazes)_. This is a broader theme in California: there is a gap between instituting laws and action plans and actually getting those executed. In any case, even when complied with, one could always demand even more restrictive standards here.

Regarding some of these trends; is is that we are seeing more lightning, or that the same amount of lightning is striking more arid fuels, leading to more fire? We know the latter is true, is the former true? Seemingly not, the amount of lightning (at least in the 1985-2000 period) has remained constant (van Wagtendonk & Cayan, [2008](https://fireecology.springeropen.com/articles/10.4996/fireecology.0401034)). It may be that lightning is overall increasing, but at least that period is evidence that we can see increasing lightning-caused fires without lightning itself being the variable that's driving it. This is not to rule out that lightning strikes are trending up in general (or in the 2000-2021) period.

Is the same the case for the human causes? Trying to disentangle that is harder because lightning is a discrete event whereas human factors include fire suppression, urban construction, roads, or power lines. Mann et al. ([2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153589)) tried to disentangle these relative contributions and find that even after controlling away the effect of climate change, on net human activity has still contributed to more fire.

Some conclusions from this:

-   There is a trend towards more area burned and more fires as well as towards an increase in the largest fires; whereas 30% of the fires were responsible for 90% of the area burned in a given year, now less than 10% of the fires (recently, less than 25 fires in a given year) account for most of the area burned. The takeaway from this: If we had good modeling tools and focus on the first predicted to be the worst, it's possible to avoid most of the damage. However this may not always be desirable, it's possible to leverage wildfires as tools to clear up vegetation (managed wildfire). More on this later.
-   The leading cause of area burned, by far, is lightning strikes, followed by a surprisingly high fraction of 'Unknown fires'. Even for large fires in 2020, like the [Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creek_Fire_\(2020\)) Fire we still don't know their cause. After that there's miscellaneous and equipment use.
    -   Miscellaneous is a grab bag of things. Some examples: La Brea fire (2009, A cooking fire at a marijuana drug trafficking operation), Ferguson fire (2018, Faulty vehicle catalytic converter), Detwiler fire (2017, firearm-related) or the Rocky fire (2015, failure of a gas water heater near an outbuilding that contained flammable liquids)
    -   Equipment use seems a bit like miscellaneous in that there are many things bunched under the category. Cedar fire (2003, signal fire out of control), Zaca fire (2007, sparks from a grinding machine on private property being used to repair a water pipe), or the Moonlight fire (2007, related to a logging company)
-   Didn't we say earlier that, according to some sources, 95% of the fires are started by humans? And yet the FRAP data says that humans never account for more than 20% of fires in a given year (Even if one considers all the unknown causes to be human-related). This is because FRAP only considers meaningfully large fires (I was able to find fires from sheds so it's unclear what large means for them). It's easy to put off most of those smaller fires and rarely these make it to the news. If we consider those larger fires then we are back to a large role to be attributed to lightning strikes (by number of fires and area) and a scarily large fires of unknown cause. I did try to get the [MTBS](https://www.mtbs.gov/) the authors of the "it's mostly us" [paper](https://www.pnas.org/content/114/11/2946) used but the dataset doesn't contain the cause of the fires. But yes, most sources tend to agree that out of the known causes of fires, less than 10% are attributable to nature, so we could be preventing a good amount of those wildfires (see e.g. p. 55 of [this](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)). [98% of all fires](https://www.vox.com/2015/9/17/9347361/wildfire-management-prescribed-burn) are stopped before they grow larger than 300 acres and plausibly most of those are in areas most accessible to firefighters, closer to urban areas and mostly caused by humans.
-   Power line fires as a surprisingly small amount of the total fires, compared to other causes (unless many of those 'Unknowns' are actually power lines. In the dataset I found two fires (Thomas and Witch) that had been classified as Miscellaneous but are now seen to be caused by faulty power lines)
-   Around the Los Angeles area, almost every fire is due to human-causes. In North California most of the fire tends to be due to lightning and around the Bay Area there's a combination of both.
-   In the span of 2000-2020 there doesn't tend to be much overlap between fires: what has burned does not burn again (perhaps up to 10 years after the original fire). It should be possible to do a more detailed analysis of this with the FRAP data given more time. In some areas trees are not coming back (Stevens-Rumann, [2019](https://fireecology.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s42408-019-0032-1)), with the terrain being converted to [chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaparral). In turn, chaparral is being converter to [grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_coastal_prairie#:%7E:text=California%20coastal%20prairie%2C%20also%20known,California%20up%20into%20southern%20Oregon.) and sage [scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_sage_scrub) (Syphard [2018](https://d2k78bk4kdhbpr.cloudfront.net/media/content/files/K2018_Syphard_type_conversion_chapter.pdf))
-   Highways are good but imperfect firebreaks; there is a 4-lane wide section of the I-5 that had a fire spreading across it. Even this [freeway](https://www.google.com/maps/place/San+Fernando+Rd,+California/@34.3321153,-118.5170132,3678m/data=!3m1!1e3!4m5!3m4!1s0x80c2910bd789f097:0xf319f00457897e3f!8m2!3d34.2264051!4d-118.3788733) with multiple levels and more lanes than you can possibly count was not able to stop the [Saddle Ridge](https://wildfiretoday.com/tag/saddle-ridge-fire/) fire of 2019.
-   Prescribed fires are on the rise, in accordance to various proclamations and commitments by public authorities. Perhaps not as much as there should be (discussed later) but directionally in the right direction.

## Who is in charge where?

California's [land](https://www.nrcm.org/documents/publiclandownership.pdf) [ownership](https://fas.org/sgp/crs/misc/R42346.pdf), at a simplified level is split between

-   Private landowners (~40%)
-   Government
    -   Local government
    -   The State of California (2.25%)
    -   Federal government (45.4% of all land, main agencies below)
        -   Bureau of Land Management (BLM, 33%)
        -   US Forest Service (FS, 45%)
        -   US Park Service (PS, 16%)
        -   Department of Defense (DoD, 3.7%)

Starrs et al. ([2018](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aaaad1/meta)) have a convenient chart showing who owns what, and importantly, who is responsible for fighting fires where. In State areas, it will be CALFIRE doing the firefighting whereas for federal land it'll be firefighters from any of the agencies that manage the relevant land:

The author hint at the possibility that who manages the land matters,

> Our results clearly demonstrate that non-federal lands have seen far less of an increase in fire probability than have federal lands. Our results also showed a consistent pattern of lands with federal firefighting—regardless of ownership or reserve status—nearly always having a higher fire probability than lands with state firefighting

There is no single "fire czar" in California; in lieu of that there's a _[California Master Cooperative Wildland Fire Management and Stafford Act Response Agreement](https://gacc.nifc.gov/oscc/cwcg/docs/2013cfma/!!2018%20-%202023%20CFMA%20FINAL%20SIGNED.pdf)_ that states and aims to solve the obvious problems like

>     
> 2.  It is to the mutual advantage of the Agencies to this Agreement to coordinate efforts in the investigation, prevention, detection and response to wildland fire, and projects related to fuels management including prescribed fire in and adjacent to their areas of responsibility. This improves efficiency and effectiveness and limits duplication.
>     

And institutes a bunch of cross-agency entities, mainly the [California Wildland Fire Coordinating Group](https://gacc.nifc.gov/oscc/cwcg/) which is the closest California have to a single entity in charge for the entire territory. The agreement effectively pools all firefighting resources into a single loosely held coalition. However what it does not do is to effectively unify forest management practices across agencies. This is why, if an agency runs out of money for prevention activities, [firefighters](https://www.kqed.org/news/11619715/wildfires-burning-through-cash-not-just-landscape) from another can't just jump in to do their job in their stead,

> So real work on the ground to reduce the intensity of fires isn't getting done or is being delayed," says Ken Pimlott, the director of Cal Fire. "It really just further exacerbates the intensity of fires because we can't get on the federal ground in particular to get the fuels treated.

To make it worse, tree NIMBYism is a thing, and so proposed activities to manage a forest (e.g. prescribed burns) often face [opposition](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/california-managing-its-forests-president-managing-its-federal-lands-n942581);

> Creating solutions is complicated by the array of overseers of wildlands — a tangle of federal, state and local agencies and thousands of private owners. A permit to cut or burn any parcel might stall if public officials can’t answer concerns about air quality, water purity, wildlife preservation and cultural and historical preservation.

## Trends elsewhere. Is it getting worse in general?

There are not good records of the distant past in the US in general or California specifically. But there might be for other countries. Canada shows a [declining](https://cwfis.cfs.nrcan.gc.ca/ha/nfdb) number and area of wildfires over the years after an [increasing](http://www.fao.org/3/ae158e/AE158E06.htm) trend culminating in the 90s. Like the US, Canada [engaged](https://www.nrcan.gc.ca/our-natural-resources/forests-forestry/wildland-fires-insects-disturban/forest-fires/fire-management/13157) in a policy of fire suppression up until the 70s, but maybe they transitioned more aggressively to prescribed burns? Spain ([Urbieta](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02925832/document) et al., 2019; Montiel-Molina et al. [2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-6255/2/3/44)) and the Mediterranean region more [broadly](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345) are seeing less fire despite increase risk of fire (due to better management techniques), but not in [Australia](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2019-09-10/more-than-climate-change-driving-queensland-fires/11493950) (Where there's an upward trend in some areas that's partially but not fully due to climate change).

Globally, there is a trend towards fewer, but larger fires (Doerr & Santin, [2016](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345)). The concluding section, in line with the "fire deficit" idea from earlier is worth quoting at length because back then they make the claim that there was no trend: and that may have been true once but not anymore (the article was written in 2016).

> Few studies exist that have explicitly examined trends in fire severity. These have mainly focused on the western USA, an area where there are particular concerns about increased fire activity \[[42](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C42),[70](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C70)\]. Examining trends from 1984 to 2006 for large ecoregions in the north- and southwest USA, Dillon _et al_. \[[71](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C71)\] found **no significant increase in the proportion of annual area burned at high severity for five of the six regions considered**, with the southern Rockies being the exception. For the Sierra Nevada region (California), which was not covered in the previous study \[[71](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C71)\], Hanson & Odion \[[72](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C72),[73](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C73)\] found no general increase in fire severity within the period 1984–2010. Considering ten national forests in California for the same period, **Miller & Safford \[[74](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C74)\] found a significant increase in burn severity for yellow pine–mixed conifer forests. They attribute this largely to decades of fire suppression and other management practices rather than climate, which have led to major changes in forest composition and structure, increases in density and fuel-loading, and hence fire behaviour.** Covering the much larger area of the dry forest landscapes of the western USA, including large parts of those examined in the aforementioned studies, Baker \[[75](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C75)\] found that the rate of high-severity fire in the period 1984–2012 was within or below that of historical century- to millennial-scale estimates.
> 
> Thus, while there is evidence of a recent increase in proportional fire severity for a specific forest type in California, these independent studies do not support the notion of an overall increase in fire severity over the past few decades in the fire-adapted forested landscapes in the western USA. **Indeed, a longer term perspective focused on the Californian Sierra Nevada and Cascades by Mallek _et al_. \[[49](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2015.0345#RSTB20150345C49)\] suggests that the annual area burned at high severity between 1984 and 2009 was only half that prior to European settlement (approx. 1500–1850), associated with an overall smaller area burned compared to pre-European times**. Whether or not the overall lack of change in burn severity applies also to other regions where perceptions of increases in fire severity exist too has to remain unanswered until robust data emerge to test this notion.

## Fire suppression, fire exclusion and prescribed fire

The set of activities that have as a goal extinguishing fires called fire suppression. Fire exclusion on the other hand the a policy putting off all fires as fast as possible, and not starting any new fires. By default wildfires would burn until it there is no more fuel to burn but this is not ideal as all that fire can generate pollution, and loss of life and property. So it seems natural to want to suppress every single fire. Fire is kind of scary and hard to control.

But this is one of the [Talebian](https://fs.blog/2011/06/suppressing-volatility/) cases where systems require some volatility to stay more stable than otherwise. It is now commonly agreed that such policies (fire exclusion) are bad: they allow for _too much_ fuel to accumulate, leading to larger and more devastating fires.

After a [series of large fires in 1910](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_1910) the US decided [it had gotten enough](https://foresthistory.org/research-explore/us-forest-service-history/policy-and-law/fire-u-s-forest-service/u-s-forest-service-fire-suppression/) of that and aimed to prevent any and all fires from ever happening. In 1935 a policy was instituted such that _every fire should be suppressed by 10 a.m. the day following its initial report_. Why even fight wildfires in the first place? It was not so much safety or health concerns, but an economic one: burnt forests are not forests you can profit from by [logging](https://foresthistory.org/research-explore/us-forest-service-history/policy-and-law/fire-u-s-forest-service/u-s-forest-service-fire-suppression/) them. Now that logging and wood-related activities are a minor part (~1%) of California's GDP ([39 billion](https://anrcatalog.ucanr.edu/pdf/8670.pdf)/ 3 trillion) this justification is weaker, compared to the other considerations (like health and protecting life and property).

In 1970 a reversal of these policies started, and now the consensus position is that starting some fires is can be a valuable tool (Stephens & [Ruth](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1890/04-0545), 2005).

The early Forest Service was not stupid; they did commission studies about whether or not to engage in prescribed burns, and after years of debate the answer back then was no: Any fire was deemed too dangerous,

> That maximum protection or fire exclusion inevitably increases hazard by the encouragement of undergrowth is, of course, true, but such added hazard in no way vitiates the reasons for protection. It is an additional danger, but one that can willingly be accepted. ([Show & Kotok](https://ir.library.oregonstate.edu/concern/defaults/05741x809?locale=en), 1924; see also [Keeley](https://www.publish.csiro.au/WF/pdf/WF20112), 2021 who notes that part of the reason the policy was put into place seems to be some amount of lobbying by the logging industry: burnt trees are less valuable)

But despite official proclamations that prescribed fire is okay now, it still seems to be not as practiced as most reports say it should. I guess because changing firefighting culture takes more than saying 'We have a different culture now'.

> Shortly after the NPS revised its fire policy, the USFS did so as well. Henry Debruin, Director of Fire and Aviation Management for the USFS, stated ‘‘we are determined to save the best of the past as we change a basic concept from fire is bad to fire is good and bad’’ (DeBruin 1974:12). While this statement represented a major shift in the philosophy of the USFS, fire suppression was still to dominate agency policy for the coming decades (Franklin and Agee 2003). ([Stephens](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1890/04-0545) & Ruth, 2005)

Even in 2020, one can still find articles talking about bottlenecks in further pursuing this policy (Miller et al., [2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41893-019-0451-7)). Not only California is not doing enough prescribed burns, but it's not even doing the ones that are planned (dashed line is planned, black is actual). At least it's trending up:

![image-20210621211929080](https://nintil.com/images/2021-06-17-wildfires-california/image-20210621211929080.png)

But the 30k acres burned in 2018 are not enough: The California Department of Forestry and Fire Protection estimated in 2010 that 20 million acres would require treatment. Even when upping the yearly burn to 100k acres per yer it would still take 200 years to get all the burning done. The number Miller et al. actually call for is 1.01 million a year, which is substantial, around 20% of the total surface that burned in the [2020 wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_California_wildfires).

So what's preventing California from reaching that target? Miller et al. asked a range of stakeholders:

-   In federal lands, it's possible to let wildfires burn if deemed necessary (it can serve to clear up fuels). In areas managed by CAL FIRE, that is illegal: the agency _has to_ extinguish all unplanned fire. Enabling CAL FIRE to leverage wildfires for treatment purposes ("managed wildfire") would be a big step forward. However, most of the land in need of treatment is federally owned, so this is not a huge key bottleneck. In Sierra Nevada, the US National Park Service (and not so much the US Forest Service) has been leveraging wildfires for treatment to great affect, to the point where the area treated by managed wildfire is similar to the area undergoing prescribed burn in the years 1968-2017 ([Keeley](https://www.publish.csiro.au/WF/pdf/WF20112) et al., 2021)
    -   Although heralded as the future of fuel treatments by most interviewees, it remains unlikely that managed wildfire will be used on state and private land in the near future due to legal responsibilities and the need for cross-jurisdictional agreement. Interviewees therefore underscored the importance of expanding prescribed burns, particularly on non-federal lands, to achieve the ecological benefits of fire.
    -   Most of the land in need of prescribed burn is federally owned
-   If the a prescribed burn escapes (and 1.76% does), there are substantial legal and financial penalties imposed on the burner. This was addressed by [SB 1260](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=201720180SB1260) which exempts them from liability if the burn meets some safety criteria, including passing a certification course that was made available starting in 2018.
-   In federal lands, managers that use prescribed burns don't receive any upside, but are personally liable if their fires escape.
-   There are not enough trained crews to do all the needed prescribed burning required
-   The California Air Resources Board (CARB)'s burn windows are too narrow. CARB says they offer more burn days than the ones that end up being used; landowners on the other hand says CARB is too restrictive. Presumably because when there's availability of a team to do the burn is not when CARB is allowing it. One could relax air quality regulations for prescribed fires: temporary worsening on a schedule (so the affected population can prepare) is better than multi-month bad air quality. But interviewees declined to ask for weaker environmental regulations for prescribed fires. The California Air Resources Board is [aware](https://ww2.arb.ca.gov/our-work/programs/prescribed-burning/about) of the need for more prescribed fire and back in 2019 they were [calling](https://ww3.arb.ca.gov/smp/CARB%20Informational%20Hearing%20on%20Prescribed%20Fire.pdf) for "Significantly increase the number of acres treated with prescribed fire. " or to "Streamline regulatory and permitting processes.". Currently prescribed fires take place in favorable weather (think wind), which is why there is less of an impact to nearby populations ([Xiaoxi](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2016JD026315) et al., 2017), but burning all that is required to burn probably will require making transitory exceptions to this air quality regulations.
    -   [Environmental](https://reason.com/2021/04/06/why-isnt-california-safer-from-wildfires/) regulations like NEPA and CEQA impose a heavy burden on justifying the burns, decreasing their use. If the approved burn window is missed, they have to reapply for approval.
-   There seems to be high level state-level support for prescribed burns
-   The authors judge the overall mix of policies and solutions being implemented at too conservative and unlikely to meet the required fuel treatment goal.

## The firefighting trap

In any one given year, a firefighting agency has to allocate resources between fighting fires and preventing them. In some platonic scenario, one models what's going to happen by doing each activity and how that affects fire hazard over time, and allocates resources accordingly.

This is not how agencies operate in reality. For example CAL FIRE _has no choice_ but to extinguish any and all fires under its area of responsibility regardless of any other consideration, the only real constraint being firefighting crew availability. The crews that do prevention also do suppression and with a lengthening fire season, the money and crews that would otherwise be preparing for the next season are fighting the current one. The _firefighting trap_ (Moreira et al., [2020](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/ab541e/pdf)) is the situation that happens when fighting fires in the short term, at the expense of disregarding prevention and preparation for future fires, yields an increasing amount of fire. Breaking this cycle of self-defeating firefighting practices would involve potentially politically hazardous actions, like modifying CAL FIRE's charter to allow them not to intervene in wildfires, or even not to intervene _by default_ unless there fire is predicted to cross a certain threshold of expected damage. Savings from this policy would allow prevention activities to take place more often. More realistically, CAL FIRE should have a ring-fenced budget for one and other kinds of activities, but even then, is it politically acceptable to have some crews working unmolested on prevention projects while other crews are desperate for reinforcements miles away, trying to save lives? The temptation to shift resources to suppression activities is always there.

## Were Native Americans doing it?

A point I encountered repeatedly is that Native Americans were [engaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_use_of_fire_in_ecosystems) in [prescribed](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2019/nov/21/wildfire-prescribed-burns-california-native-americans) burns before ~it was cool~ the arrival of Europeans to America. There seems to be some political coding around prescribed fires, where "prescribed fires is how to solve the problem, the government made it worse" is the conservative position and "actually, wildfires are due to climate change" is the progressive position.

Adding that "Native Americans were doing it" may be an attempt to make the position sound more progressive and natural sounding or something (where prescribed fire is a return to ancestral practices). Be that as it may, were they really?

Native Americans indeed used fire for various reasons, from religious to clearing land to increasing agricultural yield to reducing places where rival tribes could hide and ambush ([Keeley](http://communitiesandlandscapes.org/files/original/af0f908e9fb310ada5934ab914543605a0c42c1e.pdf), 2002). But the extent to which these practices affected large swathes of territory remains debated. For what's worth Keeley does think that the practices did lead to substantial amounts of fire, enough to shape large parts of the region.

What seems to be the authoritative book on the subject _Indians, Fire, and the Land in the Pacific Northwest_ ([Boyd](https://www.amazon.com/Indians-Fire-Land-Pacific-Northwest/dp/0870714597), 1999), says that indeed the Indians were not really engaged in the deliberate prevention of fire, rather to the extent that they were causing there to be fewer larger fires, it was an accidental outcome of their use of fire for other purposes,

> Other ecological effects of burning and other traditional management practices that are apparent today may not have been so obvious or primary in the world of the Native Americans. The beneficial effects of understory and spot burning in several forest types as a preventive to wildfires, disease outbreaks, and extensive forest burns, now so apparent to Northwest forest managers, have become timely because of the ill-advised, unnatural (and culturally specific) practice of fire suppression

I'm agnostic as whether these practices were enough to substantially alter the landscape of California at large. Ultimately I think of the practice of prescribed burning as aiming not to give the environment what it "naturally needs"; nature does not do prescribed burning, rather it's an attempt to first undo what the longstanding fire suppression policy (also an alteration over the natural course of things) did, and make California a more livable place than what nature would let us have: maybe more fires, but in safer ways with less pollution and damage.

## So is it fire exclusion or climate change?

There is only a handful of paper trying to consider climate change and fire exclusion jointly. [Hanan](https://www.niskanencenter.org/megafires-climate-change-or-land-management/) et al. ([2021](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/abd78e/pdf)) examine two different locations (Johnson Creek and Trail Creek) in Idaho using the most detailed model of coupled hydrology-fire-ecology-climate that I've seen so far ([RHESSys-WMFire](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2014AGUFM.H54D..06K/abstract)) and... it's complicated. Fire exclusion (zero-tolerance policy against fire) can both worsen and improve the number and area of fires. And the same is true for climate change! This latter result is because while increasing aridity makes for more flammable fuels, at some point of increased aridity (and for a given vegetation type), the growth rate of new vegetation is stunted and so one gets less fires.

> Fire regimes—characterized by the frequency, intensity, size, season, and severity of fire over time— exist along an aridity continuum that ranges from being limited by flammability (i.e. climate) to being limited by fuel (Krawchuk and Moritz 2011, Pausas and Paula 2012). In climate-limited systems, there is typically enough fuel for fire to spread, but moisture is too high; these systems are characterized by infrequent, severe fires, which occur during unusually arid conditions. In fuel-limited systems, the climate is relatively arid and conducive to fire, so fire activity is mostly driven by the amount of live and dead plant material present; these systems typically experience frequent, low-severity surface fires.

Could it then be that the "fire deficit" is actually caused by climate change, rather than fire exclusion? This is very unlikely: If California as a whole were at the point the vegetation-reducing effect of aridity overwhelms the fire-inducing effect we would see an inverse correlation between aridity and fire in the last few decades; but the Abatzoglou et al. papers cited above show otherwise: Right now more aridity means more fire.

## Conclusion

Here I have reviewed various strands of evidence regarding what's going on with wildfires in California and where it is going. I have explicitly _not_ gone into what should we do about the matter, that will come in part 2 of this series. To sum up, this is what is going on:

-   Burned acres per year in California are increasing relative to 1950. This is predicted to continue to increase, but the increase won't be linear. Some years will see very little fire and others will see 2020-scale mega fires.
-   This quantity of fire is not unseen in the history of California: pre-1800 there used to be more fires, and during the European colonization California saw even more fires.
-   The immediate cause of most (>80%) fires is attributable to humans. However, this is not true for _large_ fires. Lightning strikes continue to play a significant role in igniting fires and should not be disregarded. There is no one main contributor to wildfires. See the plethora of "miscellaneous" explanations.
-   The practice of zero-tolerance fire suppression, or fire exclusion, has led to a buildup of field throughout California. This compounded with a particularly wet period leading to strong plant growth coupled with lower fire incidence. The combination of the end of this wetter period, and rising temperatures due to climate change is what has led California to the current situation.
-   Even in the absence of climate change, we would still see more fire than the 1950-1980 period; that is, the graphs typically used to point to the effects of climate change on the increase of wildfires _overstate_ the magnitude of the effect of climate change and understate natural variation and human firefighting practices.
-   It seems to me, though showing this at length would require making this post even longer, that Californian institutions are weak. Some kinds of regulations (Getting PG&E to behave, building fire-safe WUI houses) and are not enforced either at the individual or the corporate level. This explains the disconnect between various action plans and roadmaps detailing measures being implemented, and what is actually happening on the ground. The ideas are in the right documents, but they are not being translated into actions.
-   California is preventing market mechanisms (i.e. insurance) from fully operating to prevent construction in highly fire-prone areas
-   There is a systematic and comprehensive lack of good quality information in general about what's going on with wildfires, including _the cause of many of them!_. This is repeated throughout the _[Cost of Wildfire in California](https://ccst.us/wp-content/uploads/The-Costs-of-Wildfire-in-California-FULL-REPORT.pdf)_ (2020) report. Ideally there should be a central website containing easy to access datasets, predictive models, incident reports, budgets, and so forth, adequately sourced and updated.
-   The right question to ask is not whether the driver is climate change or fire suppression looking back. The right question to ask is what to do moving forward, especially accounting for various political and social constraints. Even without climate change, fire exclusion, or weather oscillations, _a lot of California is going to naturally burn_. The questions then become:
    -   How much of California _should_ burn, given what Californians care about? Rather than accepting the default amount of acres burned in a pristine California untouched by us.
    -   How do we get there?
