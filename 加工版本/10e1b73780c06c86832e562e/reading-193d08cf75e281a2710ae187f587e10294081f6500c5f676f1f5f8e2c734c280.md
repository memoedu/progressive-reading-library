# 如何变得如此有创意，以至于感觉像是在违法

说实话。

过去这几周，我感觉脑子彻底烧干了。

你知道的，就是那种感觉：仿佛同时什么都没想，又什么都在想。

当你试着思考、brainstorm，或想出一个好点子时，无论多努力，脑子里都一片空白。

这更像是 cognitive burnout，而不是 emotional burnout。我当然还能继续工作，但感觉自己不太像个人。

可能是 stress。

也可能是 AI 用得太多了（我最近挺常用 cursor 的）。

也可能是我脱离了 writing routine（这源于把注意力转向其他公司，而那又带来了更多 stress）。

就在上个月，写出好点子和写作对我来说还轻而易举。我可以坐下来尽情写作，_感觉_内容有质量，思考也接近原创。

这种情况持续得越久，这种感觉就越发加重。

我为什么写不出来？我的点子都去哪儿了？_我怎样才能恢复？_

这封信的首要目标就是这个。

我想给你我一份指南，帮助我们回到最具 creative 的状态；你会发现，这非常重要。

我的第二个目标是让你明白，即使你不觉得自己是“creative person”，也能进入一种极其愉悦的 state of consciousness。它类似于 flow state，但可能更强烈。你的注意力并不在于轻松完成一组 tasks。相反，你会以一种截然不同的方式看世界，就像一只第一次看到草地的狗。

我的第三个目标是给你一套 7-day protocol。只要你严格遵循它，就能从 brain-fried 的状态变得鲜活起来。这会很难，但绝对值得；你的 work quality 也会大幅提升。

因为在当今世界，creativity 是最稀缺的 resource。

任何人都能 build anything。任何人都能 think anything。任何人都能 write anything。一如既往，在 business、writing、art 和整体 quality of life 上胜出的人，将是那些能走出最 creative path 的人。那条别人都没想到要走的路。

### I – 你没有点子，是因为受到的干扰太多。

“我不是 creative person。”

这句令人遗憾、而且往往未经深思的话，让人觉得 creativity 仿佛是一种天赋或 skill。

某种程度上确实如此，但从根本上说，creativity 是一种自然的存在方式。它是一种 state of consciousness。它是每个人都有的 capacity，但这种 capacity _会随着时间推移而被埋没。_

它是怎么被埋没的？

任何会让你的思维变狭窄的事物都会如此。Creativity 是一种非常开放、放松的状态，在这种状态中，你能看到那些并非立刻显而易见的 connections、patterns 和 possibilities。它是_留意到未被留意之物_的行为，这与多数人认为的 creativity 不同：凭空创造某种东西。

在我看来，有三种 Mind Narrowers：

**1) Conditioning** **是 wonder 的敌人。**

想到 creativity，你就会想到孩子。

他们用如此新鲜的眼光看世界。如果一个孩子让 ChatGPT build 一台 teleportation device，好带朋友去另一个 galaxy，没人会觉得奇怪；但如果你这么做，人们会觉得你只是个不懂“世界如何运作”的白痴。

孩子还没有承受来自父母、老师和同伴不断累积的负面反馈。他们还没有内化这样的观念：要融入一个破败又无聊的 society，他们_必须_以某种方式行事。

_你必须去上学。_

_你必须尽力找一份高薪工作。_

_你必须赞美这位 God；如果违抗，你就会下地狱。_

等大多数人到了 20 岁，他们就和其他人一样了。想法、行动和信念类型都一样。他们走上了分配给自己的 life path，而不是自己选择创造的那条路。

Creativity 要求你不要把 beliefs 抓得太紧，并且愿意考虑一个 idea，而不是立刻否定或妖魔化它（社交媒体上的人都这么做，因为这能带动 engagement，助长 groupthink）。

2**) 把 Productivity 当作优先事项，是注定失败的游戏。**

工业化时期，9-5 job 成为常态后，productivity 成了最高价值。每个人都成了 specialist，只学会如何拼上 puzzle 的一块；因为要是他们懂得如何解决整个问题，他们就会是 entrepreneur，而不是 employee。

如今，每个人都觉得自己在落后（说实话，在一场不是你创造的游戏里，你永远追不上。Creativity 是唯一的出路）。

你总觉得有个 deadline 一直近在眼前。

stress 下的 mind 只担心生存；当你的 nervous system 受 deadlines 支配时，你就看不到新的 connections。

如果你的生活不是围绕 optimization 和 efficiency 来安排的（换句话说，如果你不是 robot），所有人都会觉得你没用。但这恰恰是 creativity 所要求的：漫无目的地闲逛，真正的无聊，为恰当的 idea 腾出空间。它会带你走得比那些困在同一场竞赛里的 productivity bros 远得多。

把每个小时都排满的人，什么也不会偶然发现

优先事项本身就会干扰 creative thought 所需的条件。

**3) 无限 input，零 processing time。**

你的 metabolism 速度终究有限。

很显然，吃太多食物会让你开始觉得迟钝，看起来也没精神。

没错，你会发胖。

但大多数人没意识到，这同样适用于 mind。

他们觉得，如果一周不 consume 10 个 podcasts，就会“跟不上”，可事实恰恰相反。他们的 mental metabolism 没有时间 digest information。

经过筛选、能激发更多 ideas 的 information 确实有用武之地，但若不加以严格控制，很快就会变得危险。

Creativity 很少是 input 问题，不过话说回来，你只能用冰箱里的东西做饭。问题是，大多数人的冰箱都塞满了冰淇淋和汽水。

### II – 你并非无聊，而是 overstimulated。

“Dan，我一直都觉得无聊，而且我没有 creativity。”

长期 overstimulated 和 overcaffeinated 并不是无聊。你已经疲惫不堪，甚至走到了另一个极端，还把那种状态与无聊联系起来，因为你太习惯 euphoria，以至于它也变得无聊。你真的已经无法再往前走了，必须回到另一边。

真正的无聊（withdrawal period 之后）会带来几种作用。

**1) 无聊为 novelty 打开一扇门。**

Carl Jung 这位 OG psychologist 一再强调 _shadow work_ 的重要性——直面我们通常会回避的、令自己不舒服的方面。

与无聊共处正是如此。

当 rational mind 不再试图解决一切时，它会激活 breakthrough insights。

它会揭示 external conditioning 之下我们 authentic desires。

它为 3 flow triggers 创造条件，让你更有可能进入 intense learning and building 的阶段：

*   **Deep embodiment** – 与不适共处、活在当下

*   **Novelty** – 无聊会迫使你寻求新的、更健康的 stimulation

*   **Unpredictability** – 不知道虚无之中会浮现什么

如果你不知道人生中该做什么，也许你就该什么都不做。

不是每个人都会陷入的那种默认的无所事事，而是真正的 _什么都不做。_

**2) 被剥夺时，大脑会 upregulate dopamine receptors**。

_Hedonic adaptation_ 就是你的 psychological thermostat。

无论温度升得多高或降得多低，它总会试图回到 set point。

这就形成了 psychologists 所说的“hedonic treadmill”。你总是在奔向下一个 pleasure source，但满足感从不会持久。每次体验都会成为你的新常态，要获得同样的 emotional high，就需要更强烈的 stimulation。

但当你剥夺自己的 pleasure 时，情况就会反过来。hedonic treadmill reversal。

起初缓慢，随后迅速，简单的 pleasures 又会变得令人愉悦。你会体验到 Buddhists 所说的 _beginner’s mind._

你散步时会注意到天空中的细节。你会尝到烹调得恰到好处的一餐里淡淡的 rosemary 香气。生活会变得鲜活起来，本该如此。

**3) 你不需要 motivation，而需要 clarity**。

> 人类所有的问题都源于人无法独自安静地坐在房间里。
>
> – Naval Ravikant

无聊会创造 sensemaking 的空间。也就是说，_processing and integrating experience._ 这就是大多数人没有意识到其重要性的 digestion。

在 Information Age，modern technology 会造成“context collapse”。我们的大脑通过 conscious attention 每秒最多只能处理约 50 bits of information。

当你把自己剥夺到无聊的程度时，你几乎会被迫直面这些年来一直压抑的所有问题。

你需要坐下来，留意自己的 mind 里发生了什么。

这会很痛苦，但如果你与之共处足够久，就会获得一阵 clarity，带你进入人生的新阶段。

通过 chaos，或对 chaos 的 perspective 转变，order 会浮现。

### III – 让自己彻底慢下来、重新感到鲜活的 7-day protocol

好了，你明白了。

Creativity 是一件了不起的事，你或许应该更优先考虑它。

但要怎么做？

好，我们看看这个 _problem_（overstimulated、overcommited、mentally bloated），再设计一个能缓解这些状况的 _system_。事情进展不顺时，你就该这么做；但当你困在这种 narrow-minded state 里，首先很难识别问题是什么，更难改变自己的 behavior。这就是为什么这样的信会有帮助。它会照亮 awareness（你不会去问 ChatGPT 自己想不到要问的问题）。

现在，我们不需要在这里进行完整的“dopamine detox”，但确实需要下定决心。

如果有帮助，就对自己的感受感到愤怒，并用这种 negative energy 全力投入接下来的 7 天：

**第 1–2 天：迅速减少输入**

这相当于做 intermittent fasting，只不过对象是 mind。

这一切都很重要；即使感觉不对劲也没关系。

*   **为工作日安排严格的 timeblocks。** 如果可以，这周每天把工作限制在 4 小时以内。如果做不到，也没关系。设置一个标记结束时间的 alarm。alarm 响起时，你就 _结束了_。不要“再做最后一项任务”。不工作时，你的任务就是 _不要_ 想工作或 productivity。你在练习一种 skill：让事情处于未完成状态，同时不感到 anxiety。

*   **切断你的主要 input source。** 就像夜里把橱柜里的 junk food 拿走一样，挑出你最不假思索就会去接触的那个 source。它可以是通勤时听的 podcast、睡前刷的内容，或早上的 news。用 _什么都没有_ 来替代它。安静地坐着，留意是否有 idea 浮现。

*   **去散步**。不是因为它会带来什么神奇效果，而是因为 ideas 会在行动中被捕捉到。不戴 headphones。见鬼，甚至可以把 phone 留在家里。你可能是第一次这么做，所以这次散步不会给你带来太多，但要 trust the process。

就这样。三个简单的改变。

从心理层面来说，移除持续不断的 input，会让你大脑的 default mode network（大脑的“wandering”系统）启动。这个 network 负责 random insight、self-reflection 和 imagining the future。

当你正在 consuming 时，它无法 active。

**第 3–4 天：消化已有的东西**

现在你已经创造了空间，各种东西会开始浮现。

未完成的 thoughts、被压抑的 feelings、随机的 connections，以及你已经忘了曾经存在过的 ideas。你的 mind 终于有时间拆解自己一直在收集、却始终没有处理的东西。

在这个阶段（同时继续保持第 1、2 天做的事），我们要练习留意 reality 更深层的部分。

*   **慢慢读完一本书的一章。** 你不是要读完它或学到什么，只是想留意某个句子何时让你停下来思考。一旦出现这种情况，就把书放下。坐下来体会那句话为什么触动了你。在我看来，这是最好的 reading 方式。别想着快速读完一本书，只要获得你需要的东西，然后把书放下。那一个 idea 对你的影响，会超过整本书。

*   **静坐 10 分钟，什么也不做。** 你可以称之为 meditation，当然可以，但我不希望你使用 meditation app 或任何 guided breathing technique。只要找个地方坐下，让 mind 随心所欲。起初会很混乱，但那就是 digestion 正在发生。不要对它做任何事，只管坐着。

*   **继续散步。** 规则相同，不戴 headphones，也不接受任何 stimulation。但这一次，试着真正 _看见_ 周围的事物。你已经从这些东西旁边经过上百次了，但你有没有注意过 sidewalk 的细节、树枝的结构，或天空的辽阔？我敢打赌，上次散步时你甚至没有抬头看。

我们要做的，是放开对 subconscious 的控制（它负责在后台工作，并让“aha”时刻浮现）。离开 task-oriented work 是做到这一点的最好方式。

如果这些 insight 时刻带来一个 idea，让你能够做得更好、从事更高杠杆率的 work，那么不工作反而不是做得更多吗？

**第 5–6 天：重新对生活产生兴趣**

有件事我常常会想：

当人们说自己“觉得什么都没意思”时，我会想他们是否真的看看周围。_一切都很有意思。_

你的 mind 只是被毫无意义的狗屁占据了，以至于你没有注意到这一点。

到现在，你的 mental fog 应该正在消散。你会觉得头脑清晰了一些，颜色更鲜明，对话更有意思，小事也重新变得有意义。你走到户外，终于又能享受一口清新的空气。

*   **相信 ideas 会回来。** 克制住给一切做笔记的冲动。如果它很重要，自然会找到你。当你有了一个有趣的想法，就让它继续发展。留在那股 stream of consciousness 里。别伸手拿 phone。在人们不相信自己 mind 的世界里，学会相信自己的 mind。

*   **进行一次** _**真正的**_ **conversation**。下次 meeting 前别只“叙叙旧”5 分钟。也别为了建立 business connection 而“networking”。我们在这里不是要表演。倾听其他人的 perspectives，并尽可能保持 present。你的 brain 也许就会亮起来。

*   **延长散步时间。** 你可能会发现自己不想停下来。

在这个阶段，你的 dopamine receptors 正在 resensitizing。你开始做某件事，是因为它本身就很有意思；这对 creative output 是一个重要的 predictor，也恰好会引向第 7 天。

**第 7 天：用浮现出的东西进行创作**

大多数人试图从 depletion 的状态进行创作，然后纳闷为什么一切都显得勉强。

我很熟悉这种感觉。过去 5 年，我几乎每周都会写一份 newsletter。但上个月，我找不到可以写的内容。写起来很勉强。我已经脱离了 creative way of life。

现在，我们已经花了 6 天创造空间、processing，并让 connections 形成；你可以从 abundance 而不是 obligation 出发进行创作。

*   不做计划，随手创作点什么。写作、绘画、录一段 20-minute voice note，或者不看 recipe 做饭。唯一的规则就是没有规则。不要 strategic thinking，也不要试图寻找完美的 angle。开启一条 thread，顺着它走下去。

*   不要分享它。我知道这与我宣扬的理念相悖，但我们需要记住，没有别人默默的 opinions 影响你的方向，是什么感觉。留意做出一件属于自己的东西时，感觉如何。

至少过 24 小时后，如果你想重新拿起它并发到某处，那就请便。

如果你不知道该发布或创作什么，再说一次，[writing / content system challenge](https://stan.store/thedankoe/p/build-a-2hour-content-ecosystem-in-30-days) 很快就要开始了。

我们在这里要做的，是把 generative thinking（producing novel ideas）和 evaluative thinking（judging and editing them）分开。人们通常会试图同时做这两件事，但这样做会压制 generative 的 potential。

### IV – 要变得 creative，你需要有 something to create。

还记得我们上小学时怎么参加 spelling tests 吗？

每当我学会一个 new word，就会开始到处听见那个 word。

当我看到一辆以前没见过的 car 时，也会发生同样的事。我先看到一次，接着就到处都能看到它。

接触到某些 business opportunities 时，也发生过同样的事。我甚至不知道它们存在，因为我一直被灌输这样的观念：9-5 就是一切；而当我开始探索后，就发现到处都有它们。

这就是我们大脑中的 reticular activating system。

它会过滤我们接收到的数百万条 inputs，并呈现那些符合你告诉它哪些事情重要的 inputs。

这正是我们变得更 creative 所需要的。

你需要一个值得投入的 meaningful project。你需要一个待解决的 problem。你需要一个待建立的 business。你需要去 create。可以是一篇 essay、一项 design，或其他任何东西。

你正在创造一个 lens，借此由 _you_ reprogram your mind。

因为，如果你是自己接受进脑海的 ideas 的总和，而你接受哪些 ideas 又取决于你认为什么重要，并且对你来说，唯一重要的只有 industrial culture 灌输给你的父母、老师和同伴的 school、job 和 retirement，那么追求 rare life 的主要方式，就是简单地质疑并选择对你而言什么重要。

当你有了重要的东西——一个 project、design 或 product——甚至是在街上无意听到的一段 conversation，也会成为 creative fuel。你读一本 book，某个 sentence 会突然跃入眼帘；但另一个人读到它时，却不会有同样的感受。

没有 project，你的 mind 就像一艘在茫茫水面上漂流的 boat。

我们经历的 7 day protocol 平息了 storm，但即使 sea 平静，如果你没有 direction，也只是在漂浮。这感觉不错，但总有一天你会想做点什么。

那么，什么样的 frame 才算好？

什么样的 project 值得 create？

*   它必须是对你来说尚未解决的事情。它不必完全 original 或 novel，但必须是一个 challenge。必须有某件事是你尚不知道答案的，这样才能让你的 subconscious 成为 relevant and useful ideas 的磁石。

*   它必须对你重要。你的 pattern recognition 由 emotional investment 驱动。你选择一个 project，如果只是因为它“看起来很体面”——比如一个你其实并不在乎的高薪学位——它就不会像那个真正让你夜不能寐的 project 一样，激活同一套 radar。

*   它必须是 shareable。换句话说，它必须有某种 form。它必须存在于 reality 中。它可以是 words、visuals、code、一段 conversation、一个 business，或一顿 meal。你必须把脑海中的 abstract thoughts 带入 reality，并检验它们的 worth。我们在这里不是只想象那些自己做不到的事情。

这就引出了最后一个问题……

你要怎样找到这个 meaningful project？

嗯，这需要经历一番摸索，过程并不愉快；但如果你把 problem 倒过来看，会有所帮助。

你要深入思考眼下为了填满时间而勉强忍受的所有 _meaningless_ projects、tasks 和 activities，因为如果你没有投入某件 meaningful 的事情，你觉得自己的人生最终会走向何处？

如果你培养起对自己在生活中 _不想要_ 什么的深刻 awareness，它就会开始构成你的 frame。你会开始到处看见它。一旦看见了，就更容易开始朝相反方向前进。

– Dan

> ### I – You don’t have ideas because there’s too much interference.
>
> “I’m not a creative person.”
>
> That unfortunate and often unthought-through statement makes creativity seem like it’s some sort of talent or skill.
>
> In some ways, it is, but at its core, creativity is a natural way of being. It’s a state of consciousness. It’s a capacity that everyone has, but that capacity _gets buried as time goes on._
>
> How does it get buried?
>
> With anything that narrows your mind. Creativity is a very open, relaxed state where you see connections, patterns, and possibilities that aren’t immediately obvious. It’s the act of _noticing the unnoticed,_ which is not the same as what most think creativity is: creating something from nothing.
>
> In my eyes, there are Three Narrowers of the Mind:
>
> **1) Conditioning** **is the enemy of wonder.**
>
> When you think of creativity, you think of children.
>
> They see the world through such fresh eyes. If a child asked ChatGPT to build a teleportation device so they can take their friends to another galaxy, nobody would bat an eye, but if you did that, people would think you’re just an idiot who doesn’t understand “how the world works.”
>
> Kids haven’t yet received the compounding negative feedback from their parents, teachers, and peers. They haven’t internalized that they _have_ to act a certain way to fit into a broken and boring society.
>
> _You must go to school._
>
> _You must do your best to get a high-paying job._
>
> _You must praise this God, and if you disobey, you’re going to hell._
>
> By the time most people turn 20 years old, they are the same as everyone else. Same thoughts, actions, and types of beliefs. They are going down the life path assigned to them rather than the one they chose to create.
>
> Creativity requires holding beliefs loosely and entertaining an idea without immediately rejecting or demonizing it (as everyone does on social media, where it drives engagement and facilitates groupthink).
>
> 2**) Productivity as a priority is a losing game.**
>
> When the 9-5 job became a thing during industrialization, productivity became the highest value. Everyone became a specialist who only learned how to place one piece of the puzzle, because if they understood how to solve the entire thing, they would be the entrepeneur not the employee.
>
> Today, everyone feels like they’re falling behind (and if you’re being real, you’re never going to catch up in a game you didn’t create. Creativity is the only way out).
>
> You have this perpetual deadline that’s always looming.
>
> A stressed mind only worries about survival, and you can’t see new connections when your nervous system is ruled by deadlines.
>
> If your life isn’t structured around optimization and efficiency (in other words if you aren’t a robot) everyone thinks you’re useless. But that’s exactly what creativity demands. Useless wandering. True boredom. Creating space for the right idea to emerge that will take you much further than the productivity bros stuck in the same race as everyone else.
>
> People who schedule every hour don’t stumble onto anything
>
> The priorities themselves interfere with the conditions creative thought needs.
>
> **3) Infinite input and zero processing time.**
>
> Your metabolism can only go so fast.
>
> It’s obvious that if you eat too much food, you start to feel slow and look slow.
>
> Yes, you get fat.
>
> But most people don’t realize this applies to the mind as well.
>
> They feel as if they don’t consume 10 podcasts a week, they won’t be able to “keep up,” even though the opposite is true. Their mental metabolism doesn’t have time to digest the information.
>
> There’s a time for curated information that helps spark more ideas, but if it isn’t kept under tight control, it gets dangerous very quickly.
>
> Creativity is rarely an input problem, but then again, you can only cook with what’s in the fridge. The problem is that most people’s fridges are overflowing with ice cream and soda pop.

> ### II – You’re not bored, you’re overstimulated.
>
> “Dan, I’m bored _all the time_ and I’m not creative.”
>
> Being chronically overstimulated and overcaffeinated is not boredom. You’re so fried that you’ve gone all the way off the other end and associate that with boredom because you’re so used to euphoria that it’s become boring. You quite literally can’t go any further, you must come back the other way.
>
> True boredom (after your withdrawal period) does a few things.
>
> **1) Boredom provides a gateway to novelty.**
>
> Carl Jung, OG psychologist, harped on the importance of _shadow work_ – confronting the uncomfortable aspects of ourselves we typically avoid.
>
> Sitting with boredom does just this.
>
> It activates breakthrough insights when the rational mind stops trying to solve everything.
>
> It reveals our authentic desires beneath external conditioning.
>
> It sets the scene for 3 flow triggers, making you more likely to enter a season of intense learning and building:
>
> *   **Deep embodiment** – being present with discomfort
>
> *   **Novelty** – boredom forces you to seek new, healthier stimulation
>
> *   **Unpredictability** – not knowing what will emerge from the void
>
> If you don’t know what to do in your life, maybe you should do nothing.
>
> Not the default nothing that everyone falls into, but truly _nothing._
>
> **2) The brain will upregulate dopamine receptors when deprived**.
>
> _Hedonic adaptation_ is your psychological thermostat.
>
> No matter how high or low the temperature goes, it always tries to return to the set point.
>
> This creates what psychologists call the “hedonic treadmill.” You’re always running toward the next source of pleasure, but the satisfaction never lasts. Each experience becomes your new normal, requiring more intense stimulation to achieve the same emotional high.
>
> But when you deprive yourself of pleasure, the opposite happens. A hedonic treadmill reversal.
>
> Slowly, then rapidly, simple pleasures become enjoyable again. You experience what Buddhists call the _beginner’s mind._
>
> You notice the detail in the sky when you’re on a walk outside. You notice the hint of rosemary in the well-cooked meal. Life becomes electric, as it should be.

> ### III – The 7-day protocol to slow the fuck down (how to feel alive again)
>
> Alright you get it.
>
> Creativity is an incredible thing, and you should probably prioritize it more.
>
> But how?
>
> Well, we look at the _problem_ (being overstimulated, overcommited, mentally bloated) and design a _system_ that results in the alleviation of those things. That’s what you do when something isn’t going well, but when you’re stuck in this narrow-minded state, it’s hard to first identify what your problem is, and even harder to change your behavior. That’s why a letter like this can be helpful. It shines a light of awareness (you can’t ask ChatGPT what you don’t think to ask).
>
> Now, we don’t need a full “dopamine detox” here, but we do need to commit.
>
> If it helps, get upset with how you feel, and use that negative energy to pursue these next 7 days full force:
>
> **Day 1-2: Reduce The Input Fast**
>
> This is the equivalent of doing intermittent fasting, but for the mind.
>
> All of this is important, it’s okay if it doesn’t feel right.
>
> *   **Impose strict timeblocks on your workday.** If you can, limit work to 4 hours a day for this week. If you can’t, that’s fine. Set an alarm that marks the end. When it goes off, you’re _done._ No “one last task.” Your job is to _not_ think about work or productivity when you’re not working. You’re practicing the skill of letting something feel unfinished without anxiety.
>
> *   **Cut out your primary input source.** Like the junk food in the cabinet at night, pick the one source you reach for the most mindlessly. This could be the podcast on the commute, the scroll before bed, or the news in the morning. Replace it with _nothing._ Sit in silence. Listen for an idea.
>
> *   **Go on a walk**. Not because it will do anything magical, but because ideas are caught in motion. No headphones. Hell, even leave your phone at home. This walk won’t do much for you since it may be your first time, but trust the process.
>
> That’s it. Three simple changes.
>
> Psychologically, removing constant input allows your brain’s default mode network (the brain’s “wandering” system) to fire. This is the network responsible for random insight, self-reflection, and imagining the future.
>
> It cannot be active while you’re consuming.

> **Day 3-4: Digest What’s Already There**
>
> Now that you’ve created space, things will start surfacing.
>
> Unfinished thoughts, suppressed feelings, random connections, and ideas you forgot existed. Your mind finally has time to break down what it’s been collecting but doing nothing with.
>
> During this phase (and while maintaining what you did in days 1 and 2), we’re going to practice noticing deeper layers of reality.
>
> *   **Read one chapter of a book slowly.** You aren’t trying to finish it or learn something, but simply trying to notice when a sentence makes you stop and think. When it does, put the book down. Sit with why that line hit you. In my eyes, this is the best way to read. Don’t try to quickly finish a book, just get what you need and put it down. That one idea will impact you more than the entire book.
>
> *   **Sit with nothing for 10 minutes.** You can call it meditation, sure, but I don’t want you to use a meditation app or any guided breathing technique. Just sit somewhere and let your mind do whatever it wants. It will be chaotic at first, but that’s the digestion happening. Don’t do anything with it. Just sit.
>
> *   **Keep going on a walk.** Same rules, no headphones or any stimulation. But this time, try to actually _see_ things. You’ve passed by these things a hundred times, but have you noticed the detail in the sidewalk, the structure of the tree’s branches, or the vastness of the sky? I bet you didn’t even look up the last walk.
>
> What we’re trying to do is release your grip on your subconscious (the thing responsible for working in the background and surfacing “aha” moments). Stepping away from task-oriented work is the best way to do this.
>
> And if those moments of insight lead to an idea that allows you to do better, higher-leverage work, then are you not getting more done by _not_ working?
>
> **Day 5-6: Become Interested In Life Again**
>
> Something I think about often:
>
> When people say they “don’t find anything interesting,” I wonder if they’ve actually looked around. _Everything is interesting._
>
> Your mind is just so occupied with meaningless shit that you don’t notice it.
>
> By now, your mental fog should be lifting. You feel a bit sharper, colors are more vivid, conversations are more interesting, and small things feel meaningful again. You walk outside and finally just enjoy a breath of fresh air again.
>
> *   **Trust that ideas will come back.** Resist the urge to take notes on everything. If it’s important, it will find its way to you. When you have an interesting thought, let it play out. Stay in that stream of consciousness. Don’t reach for your phone. In a world where people don’t trust their own minds, learn to trust yours.
>
> *   **Have one** _**real**_ **conversation**. No “catching up” for 5 minutes before your next meeting. No “networking” to form a business connection. We’re trying not to perform here. Listen to other people’s perspectives and attempt to be as present as possible. Your brain may just light up.
>
> *   **Extend the walk.** You might find that you don’t want to stop.
>
> During this phase, your dopamine receptors are resensitizing. You start doing something because it’s inherently interesting, which is a massive predictor for creative output, and leads perfectly into day 7.
>
> **Day 7: Create With What’s Emerged**
>
> Most people try to create from a state of depletion and then wonder why everything feels forced.
>
> I know this feeling well. I’ve written a newsletter almost every week for the past 5 years. But this last month, I couldn’t find what to write about. It felt forced. I’d fallen out of the creative way of life.
>
> Now that we’ve spent six days making space, processing, and letting connections form, you can create from abundance rather than obligation.
>
> *   Make something with no plan. Write, draw, record a 20-minute voice note, or cook without a recipe. The only rule is no rules. No strategic thinking or trying to find the perfect angle. Start a thread and follow it.
>
> *   Don’t share it. I know this is antithetical to what I preach, but we need to remember what it’s like to not have silent opinions influencing your direction. Notice how it feels to have made something that’s yours.
>
> After at least 24 hours, if you want to return to it and post it somewhere, be my guest.
>
> If you don’t know what to post or create, again, the [writing / content system challenge](https://stan.store/thedankoe/p/build-a-2hour-content-ecosystem-in-30-days) starts soon.
>
> What we are doing here is separating generative thinking (producing novel ideas) from evaluative thinking (judging and editing them). It’s typical for people to try to do both at the same time, but by doing so, you suppress the generative’s potential.

> ### IV – To be creative, you need something to create.
>
> Remember how we used to take spelling tests in grade school?
>
> Whenever I learned a new word, I would start to hear that word everywhere.
>
> The same happens when I see a car I’ve never seen before. I see it once, then I see it everywhere.
>
> The same happened when exposed to certain business opportunities. I didn’t even know they existed, because I was made to believe that a 9-5 was the end-all be-all, and once I started exploring, I noticed them everywhere.
>
> This is the reticular activating system in our brains.
>
> It filters the millions of inputs we receive and surfaces the ones that match what you’ve told it matters.
>
> This is exactly what we need to be more creative.
>
> You need a meaningful project to work on. You need a problem to solve. You need a business to build. You need to create. An essay, a design, whatever.
>
> You’re creating a lens by which _you_ reprogram your mind.
>
> Because if you are the culmination of the ideas you’ve accepted into your head, and the ideas you accept are based on what you deem important, and the only things that were important to you were the school, job, and retirement that industrial culture permeated into your parents, teachers, and peers, then the primary way to pursue a rare life is to simply question and choose what is important to you.
>
> When you have something important - a project, design, product - even a conversation you overhear on the street becomes creative fuel. You read a book and a sentence pops out at you, but when another person reads it, they don’t get the same effect.
>
> Without a project, your mind is a boat drifting in open water.
>
> The 7 day protocol we went through calmed the storm, but even with a calm sea, if you don’t have a direction you’re just floating. It’s nice, but at some point you’ll want to do something.
>
> So what makes a good frame?
>
> What makes a project worth creating?
>
> *   It has to be something unsolved for you. It doesn’t have to be completely original or novel, but it must be a challenge. There must be something you don’t know the answer to yet, which allows your subconscious to become a magnet for relevant and useful ideas.
>
> *   It has to matter to you. Your pattern recognition is powered by emotional investment. A project you chose because it “looks good on paper” - like a high paying degree that you don’t actually care about - won’t activate the same radar as the one that genuinely keeps you up at night.
>
> *   It has to be shareable. In other words, it has to take some form. It has to exist in reality. It can be words, visuals, code, a conversation, a business, or a meal. You have to take the abstract thoughts in your head, ground them in reality, and test their worth. We aren’t just imagining things we can’t do here.
>
> That begs the final question...
>
> How do you find this meaningful project?
>
> Well, it takes a bit of floundering, which is unpleasant, but it helps if you flip the problem on it’s head.
>
> You think deeply about all of the _meaningless_ projects, tasks, and activities you currently tolerate to fill your time, because if you aren’t engaged in something meaningful, where do you think your life will end up?
>
> If you cultivate this deep awareness of what you _don’t_ want in life, that starts to create your frame. You start to see it everywhere. And once you see it, it’s much easier to start moving in the opposite direction.
