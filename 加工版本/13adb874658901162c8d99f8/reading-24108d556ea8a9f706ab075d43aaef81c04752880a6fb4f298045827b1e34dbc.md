# Squashing blips

---

## 正文

前些时候，我了解到 [couchsurfing.org](https://www.couchsurfing.com/)：Internet 上的人去各个城市游玩，由 Internet 上的其他人免费接待。我考虑过接待上面的人——别人告诉我这很棒，而且这似乎是个有趣的办法，能让我暑期住处多出来的那间卧室不至于闲置。另一方面，这可能有危险，或者有人会偷我的东西；我和网站上的许多人似乎也没多少共同点，而且接待他们大概会很麻烦，还有——_等等，我刚才列出的所有理由都很傻，而且都是 speculative_，因为我完全可以很轻松地_试试看_。

于是我就试了试，结果棒极了！没有人拿刀捅我，也没有人偷我的东西；我和接待的客人相处得非常好，而且一点也不麻烦。此外，这还开阔了我的眼界，总体而言就是很好玩。呼！真高兴我意识到了那种 aversion 有多傻。

……等等。我只是_差一点才_在彻底打消这个念头之前发现了这一点。我敢打赌，我还有上百种类似的愚蠢 aversions 没能被发现。不知道我能不能弄清楚，怎样才能 reliably notice 它们？

嗯，我没有什么 algorithm，不过我在这方面确实有了一点进步，所以可以试着解释一下我是怎么做的。

我喜欢这样理解刚才发生的事：我的 brain 中，在祖先还是 lizards 时 evolved 出来的那部分正在害怕、闹脾气，因为在 ancestral environment 中，把随便什么陌生人迎进家门会是个非常糟糕的主意。当然，lizard-brain 是 pre-verbal 的，所以这个 objection 不会以“这在五百万年前会是个坏主意！”的形式被 _consciously_ 察觉到。（要是这样，事情就太容易了。）相反，lizard-brain 会 hijacks 我的 verbal loop，开始做一些 [motivated reasoning](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivated_reasoning)，让 brain 中更 reasonable 的部分也同意，于是我感觉自己只是在想出一堆不去做某件事的 legitimate reasons。（这会很不方便，我们大概没多少共同点，他们可能会把我的房间弄乱……）

事实证明，这种事在我身上发生得相当频繁——我的 lizard brain 经常闹脾气，因为它就是不明白，_如今 failing at things 的 cost 远比我的 brain 所 adapted for 的要 lower_。（如果我在 ancestral environment 中 showed weakness，可能会被撕开喉咙。如果我现在 show weakness，别人也就是稍微笑话我一下。）幸运的是，我越来越善于察觉这类情况了。这种感觉有点 subtle，很难描述，不过它就像 trepidation、fear 或 worry 的一个小小“blip”；当我的 aversion 是 rational 的时候，就不会有这种感觉（rational aversions，比如我今晚不想去参加宿舍的正式舞会，感觉更像是“没什么兴趣”）。所以，我正在试着 train 自己去察觉这样的 blips：每次发现它们，就有意识地享受 squashing 它们的乐趣——弄清楚是什么在 causing 这个 blip，然后去做那件事，再提醒 lizard brain：我既没有被老虎吃掉，也没有被自己的 tribe 所 ostracized。

为了更清楚地说明我所说的“blip” sensation，下面列出了一些过去曾对我 triggered 它的事情。如果你想到其中某件事，立刻就有冲动列出一堆理由来说明它为什么是个坏主意，请留意你的 brain 是什么感觉——那就是我说的 blip。我希望其中一些能让你产生共鸣，或者至少能帮你 generate 一份自己的类似清单，用来 train：

-   接待 couchsurfers

-   和陌生人交谈

-   玩 improv games

-   对别人进行 cold-calling 和 cold-emailing

-   跳布鲁斯舞（blues dancing）

-   冥想（meditation）

-   瑜伽（yoga）

-   选修“水”课（sociology、English、history……）

-   管理一个学生团体

-   替人照看宠物（pet-sitting）

当然，这些事情并不全都是好主意。有时候，我的 lizard brain 闹脾气时确实是对的。但它只是碰巧对了——如果它对所有事情都惊慌失措，那偶尔总会碰对。所以，即使我不喜欢那些事情，我仍然会因为自己尝试过而给自己加分。事实上，遇到这种情况，我一定会额外表扬自己一下，免得自己灰心。

到目前为止，这个办法相当有用。一部分原因是，我发现了一些以前不会发现的好玩事情（比如 rock climbing）。另一部分原因是，仅仅拥有这样一个 concept 就已经很有帮助：_事情可以在我的 comfort zone 之外，而我仍然可以去做_——比如，我变得更愿意向商店店员寻求建议，更愿意向街上的人求助或问路，玩 improv games 时不那么容易僵住，也更能在众人面前讲话，等等。

祝你愉快地消除这些小小的恐惧波动，享受 de-blipping！

## 原文

A little while ago I learned about [couchsurfing.org](https://www.couchsurfing.com/), in which people from the Internet visit cities and are hosted for free by other people from the Internet. I thought about hosting people from it—people told me it was awesome, and it seemed like an interesting way to not waste the extra bedroom in my summer housing. On the other hand, it might be dangerous, or people could steal my stuff, and it didn’t look like I had very much in common with a lot of the people on the site, and it would probably be a hassle to host them, and _wait a minute, all of the reasons I just listed were silly and speculative_, since I can very easily just _try it and see_.

So I tried it and saw, and it was awesome! Nobody knifed me or stole my stuff, I got along really well with the folks I hosted, and it was no hassle at all. Plus it was horizon-expanding and just generally fun. Phew! I’m glad I realized how silly that aversion was.

…wait a minute. I just _barely_ spotted that before I dismissed the idea entirely. I bet I have like a hundred more silly aversions that didn’t make it. I wonder if I can figure out how to reliably notice them?

Well, I don’t have an algorithm or anything, but I have gotten a bit better at it, so I can try to explain how.

I like to think of what happened there as the following: whatever part of my brain evolved while my ancestors were lizards is getting scared and throwing a tantrum, because welcoming random strangers into your house would have been a Really Bad Idea in the ancestral environment. Of course, the lizard-brain is pre-verbal, so the objection doesn’t register _consciously_ as “that would have been a bad idea five million years ago!” (That would make things too easy.) Instead, the lizard-brain hijacks my verbal loop and starts doing some [motivated reasoning](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivated_reasoning) to get the more reasonable parts of the brain to agree, so it feels like I’m just coming up with a bunch of legitimate reasons not to do something. (It would be inconvenient, we probably wouldn’t have that much in common, they might mess up my room…)

It turns out this happens to me pretty frequently—my lizard brain throws a lot of trantrums, because it just doesn’t understand that _the cost of failing at things is way lower now than my brain is adapted for_. (If I showed weakness in the ancestral environment, I might get my throat torn out. If I show weakness now, people laugh at me a little.) Fortunately, I’m getting better at noticing that kind of event. It’s a bit subtle and hard to describe, but it feels like a little “blip” of trepidation or fear or worry that isn’t there when my aversion is rational (rational aversions, like my desire not to go to my dorm’s formal tonight, feel more like just “meh”). So I’m trying to train myself to notice such blips by taking conscious pleasure in squashing them wherever I find them—figuring out what’s causing the blip, and then doing it, and reminding the lizard brain that I haven’t been eaten by tigers or ostracized by my tribe.

To give a better sense of what I mean by the “blip” sensation, here’s a list of things that have triggered it in the past for me. If you think about one of these things and immediately feel an urge to list a bunch of reasons why it’s a bad idea, pay attention to what your brain feels like—that’s the blip I’m talking about. I hope some of these will resonate with you, or at least help you generate a similar list of your own to train on:

-   hosting couchsurfers

-   talking to strangers

-   playing improv games

-   cold-calling and cold-emailing people

-   blues dancing

-   meditation

-   yoga

-   taking “fluffy” courses (sociology, English, history…)

-   running a student group

-   pet-sitting

Of course, not all of these turn out to be good ideas. Sometimes my lizard brain is right when it throws tantrums. But it’s right by accident—if it freaks out about everything, it’s bound to be right occasionally. So I still give myself points for trying the things out, even if I don’t like them. In fact, I make sure to give myself an extra pat on the back in that case, so that I don’t get discouraged.

So far, it’s been pretty useful. Partly this is because I’ve discovered enjoyable things that I wouldn’t have before (rock climbing, e.g.). Partly it’s been helpful just to have the concept that _things can be outside my comfort zone and I can do them anyway_—I’ve become, e.g., more likely to ask clerks in stores for advice, more likely to ask people on the street for help or directions, less prone to seizing up during improv games, more able to speak in front of people, etc.

Happy de-blipping!
