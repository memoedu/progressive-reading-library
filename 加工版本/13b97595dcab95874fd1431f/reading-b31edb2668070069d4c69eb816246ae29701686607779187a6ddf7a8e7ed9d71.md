Medical nihilism：[dentistry](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/04/evidence-based-dentistry.html) 篇。

> 想想这条格言：每个人都应该每年去看 dentist 两次，进行 cleanings。我们如此频繁地听到这句话，而且从很小的时候就开始听，以至于已经把它当作真理接受了。但这条所谓的 oral health 戒律没有 scientific grounding。Scholars 已将它的起源追溯到几个可能的来源，包括 1930s 的一则 toothpaste 广告，以及 1849 年的一本插图小册子，后者讲述了一个患有 severe toothache 的男人的苦难经历。如今，越来越多的 dentists 承认，具有 good oral hygiene 的 adults 只需要每隔 12 到 16 个月看一次 dentist。许多 standard dental treatments 同样没有得到 research 的充分 substantiated——更不用说近来的各种 innovations 和 cosmetic extravagances 了。其中许多从未在 meticulous clinical trials 中接受过 tested。而现有的 data 也并不总能让人放心。

然而，这一 norm 似乎因国家而异，在西班牙是每年一次，而不是两次。；同样，dental sealants 也在使用中（根据个人经验，也许我通常去的地方是个 outlier）

[Science nihilism](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2019/04/09/how-sloppy-science-creates-worthless-cures-crushes-hope-and-wastes-billions-and-still-stays-around-even-after-its-been-retracted/)：当 studies 被 retracted 后，它们仍然继续被 cited。这些 citations 中有 91% 是 positive。Nintil 的 research 的 basic rules 之一，是在 citing papers 之前，必须 checked 它们是否存在这类问题。 Academics 仍有改进的余地！

人们[早已](https://twitter.com/gwern/status/954386991956848645)知道 Candidate gene studies 不太可靠，如今这一点变得[非常](https://www.theatlantic.com/science/archive/2019/05/waste-1000-studies/589684/)明显：至少，就连经过 meta-analysed 的整套 candidate gene literatures 也都是[wrong。](https://slatestarcodex.com/2019/05/07/5-httlpr-a-pointed-review/) 最初是在 [SSC](https://slatestarcodex.com/2019/05/07/5-httlpr-a-pointed-review/) 上看到的。

令人惊讶的是，一项相对 [simple educational intervention](http://uweb.cas.usf.edu/%7Edrohrer/pdfs/Rohrer_et_al_InPressJEdPsych.pdf) 在一项 RCT 中 shows 出相当 large 的 effect size（d=0.83）。这只是 comparing 将 similar 的 math problems 按 blocks 进行 assigning，与 assigning alternating kinds 的 problems。这 shows，至少还是有一些事情可以做，来 improve education。但这些 techniques 会出现在实际的 education 中吗？

Medical nihilism: [dentistry](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/04/evidence-based-dentistry.html) edition.

> Consider the maxim that everyone should visit the dentist twice a year for cleanings. We hear it so often, and from such a young age, that we’ve internalized it as truth. But this supposed commandment of oral health has no scientific grounding. Scholars have traced its origins to a few potential sources, including a toothpaste advertisement from the 1930s and an illustrated pamphlet from 1849 that follows the travails of a man with a severe toothache. Today, an increasing number of dentists acknowledge that adults with good oral hygiene need to see a dentist only once every 12 to 16 months. Many standard dental treatments—to say nothing of all the recent innovations and cosmetic extravagances—are likewise not well substantiated by research. Many have never been tested in meticulous clinical trials. And the data that are available are not always reassuring.

This norm, however, seems to vary by country, in Spain it's once a year, not twice.; similarly dental sealants are in use (from personal experience, maybe the place I usually go to is an outlier)

No plane-based rocket launch [stagnation](https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2019/04/stratolaunch-flies-has-first-flight-of-worlds-largest-airplane.html)

People don't really care about [privacy](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/04/privacy-sentences-to-ponder.html), in the context of, say, Google or FB having data about you, as I imagined. This is also why I am pessimistic re blockchain, one of the genuinely new things that it facilitates is private transactions, but if people don't value that...

[Science nihilism](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2019/04/09/how-sloppy-science-creates-worthless-cures-crushes-hope-and-wastes-billions-and-still-stays-around-even-after-its-been-retracted/): When studies are retracted, they continue to be cited. 91% of those citations are positive. One of the basic rules of research at Nintil is that papers have to be checked for these sort of things before citing them.  Academics still have room to improve!

Candidate gene studies have been known for a [while](https://twitter.com/gwern/status/954386991956848645) to be fishy, now it becomes [very](https://www.theatlantic.com/science/archive/2019/05/waste-1000-studies/589684/) obvious that at least even entire candidate gene literatures that had been meta-analysed are [wrong.](https://slatestarcodex.com/2019/05/07/5-httlpr-a-pointed-review/) As originally seen in [SSC](https://slatestarcodex.com/2019/05/07/5-httlpr-a-pointed-review/).

Surprisingly, a relatively [simple educational intervention](http://uweb.cas.usf.edu/%7Edrohrer/pdfs/Rohrer_et_al_InPressJEdPsych.pdf) shows a fairly large (d=0.83) effect size in an RCT. This is just comparing assigning math problems that are similar i blocks vs assigning alternating kinds of problems. This shows that at least there are some things that can be done to improve education. But will these techniques show up in actual education?

[Self-driving cars may take longer than it seems](https://rodneybrooks.com/agi-has-been-delayed/), decades rather than 1--2 years.

Latest review paper on the [minimum wage wars](https://twitter.com/wwwojtekk/status/1128499022011011073): Econ 101 is still right, minimum wages are bad, especially for low skilled workers. A survey of economists (in the thread) from this year shows that consensus still seems to be that effects are mostly harmful.

US state Idaho legislators, possessed by the ghost of Murray Rothbard just [repealed all regulations](https://fee.org/articles/idaho-repealed-its-entire-regulatory-code-now-what/?fbclid=IwAR0vQFuMEMy2eU2BifiNQ7NfWPNtWzymQjVjL4BpLmjRFCNtUhltDSW8PKk). Now they have to decide what to keep on a case by case basis.

The benefits of electric cars are usually [discussed](https://twitter.com/hausfath/status/1127969484671553536) in terms of CO2 savings, which vary with the electricity generation mix. What is often forgotten, as I point out in a reply to the thread is that EVs more importantly move away pollutants from cities to powerplants, where they can be safely addressed.

One of the [best reviews](https://twitter.com/glenweyl/status/1127988864906350593) of Radical Markets so far, by David Levine. On data as labour, he joins the consensus that it is the worst part of the book.

[Open-sourcing civil society](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3387154), essay by Vlad Tarko

Jason Collins reviews [The Mind is Flat.](https://jasoncollins.blog/2019/05/13/nick-chaters-the-mind-is-flat-the-illusion-of-mental-depth-and-the-improvised-mind/) There are some issues similar to what I found in The Elephant in the Brain: Extrapolating too much from relatively niche studies like split-brain patients, studies that don't replicate.

[Critique](https://twitter.com/FinchesofDarwin/status/1127669069677678592) of "The Mating Mind" by @Evolving\_Moloch. I too side with the view that a sexual-selection explanation for art and music is all not that obvious.

What is Ethereum actually used for? [Gambling and cryptokitties](https://twitter.com/rohamg/status/1126929056371314688).

[Good review on graphical models,](https://journals.sagepub.com.sci-hub.tw/doi/abs/10.1177/2515245917745629?journalCode=ampa) clearer than Judea Pearl's own exposition in The Book of Why

Why Popper ([falsificationism](https://twitter.com/phl43/status/1126510571401035777)) is wrong, essay from Philippe Lemoine. This also seems to me the consensus view in philosophy in general. A pro-Popperian [paper](http://nathanoseroff.com/files/papers/popperdemarcation.pdf) posted in the previous thread as a counterpoint.
