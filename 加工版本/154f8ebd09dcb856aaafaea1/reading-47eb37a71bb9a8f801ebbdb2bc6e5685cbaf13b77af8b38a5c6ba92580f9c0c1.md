# Post-apocalyptic education

## 阅读前置卡

- 价值判断：高。文章围绕 AI 对作业、学习评估及教学的影响，结合调查和学校实验数据，提出检测误觉、学生知识错觉及教学应用方向，具有较强教育实践参考价值。
- 信息密度判断：高。全文包含多项调查与研究结果、具体比例和成绩变化，并展开作弊成因、检测局限、学习损害与有效教学用法等论证，信息较密集。
- 是否继续阅读：3 全文。论证层次完整，研究例证和教学路径对理解结论有帮助，适合阅读全文；精细阅读可按需关注研究设计与证据边界。

## 核心洞察

一、AI普及未改变教育结果：文章称，AI 已能在大多数测试中取得高分，学生使用 AI 也几乎普及，但教育结果几乎没有改变。
[[#^pr-ce98d1a786e7|正文跳转]]

二、AI或将改变传统作业：一种可能到来的现实是，AI 能够完成大多数传统作业，使这些作业不再适合作为学习工具和评估手段。
[[#^pr-ce98d1a786e7|正文跳转]]

三、学生使用AI已相当普遍：文中引用一项美国调查：八个月前，82% 的本科生和 72% 的 K12 学生曾在学校使用 AI；使用者中，56% 用 AI 帮助完成写作作业，45% 用于其他学业。学生可能把借助 AI 解决难题视为求助，而许多教师则视其为作弊。
[[#^pr-a8d843776ea4|正文跳转]]

四、AI采用速度：AI 在教育中的采用速度快得惊人；当前段落未提供支持这一判断的具体数据或比较依据。
[[#^pr-a8d843776ea4|正文跳转]]

五、调查涉及AI积极用途：调查还发现了 AI 的许多积极用途，但当前段落没有列出具体用途，并表示后文会再讨论。
[[#^pr-a8d843776ea4|正文跳转]]

六、教育中的努力不能被外包：作弊并非由 AI 单独造成；文章将其联系到学业难度、较高利害以及学习并不总是有趣等因素。人们会设法避开不喜欢的任务，而一项分析显示多数人不喜欢脑力努力。作者总体赞成把任务委托给 AI，但认为教育有所不同：努力本身正是学习的重点。
[[#^pr-ab265f27f276|正文跳转]]

七、学习需要外在动机：学习并不总是有趣，为了促使人们学习，往往需要成绩这类外在动机；这被用来解释 schoolwork 为何困难且 stakes 高。
[[#^pr-ab265f27f276|正文跳转]]

八、将部分心智努力交给 AI：人们会把自己不喜欢做的部分心智努力委托给 AI。
[[#^pr-ab265f27f276|正文跳转]]

九、困难的脑力任务令人更难受：文章引用一篇新论文，指出脑力任务越难，人们往往感觉越糟。
[[#^pr-a6c513f2ab71|正文跳转]]

十、家庭作业效果变化：一项针对 Rutgers 数千名学生的研究显示，做家庭作业时 test grades 得到提高的学生比例从 2008 年的 86% 降至 2017 年的 45%；文中将这一变化归因于互联网兴起后，更多学生抄用网上答案而非自行完成作业。
[[#^pr-5ca83a231da5|正文跳转]]

十一、问题并非新出现：这并不是一个新问题。
[[#^pr-5ca83a231da5|正文跳转]]

十二、新技术常用于作业：帮助完成作业一直是新技术最初用途之一。
[[#^pr-5ca83a231da5|正文跳转]]

十三、两种错觉解释忽视危机：更多人没有把这一情况视为紧急事件，可能与两种错觉有关；此处尚未说明两种错觉的具体内容。
[[#^pr-b68db485492c|正文跳转]]

十四、识别 AI 的错觉：家庭作业危机可能在生成式 AI 出现前就已发生。教师认为自己仍能轻易识别 AI 使用，可能使教育者继续依赖过时的评估方式；材料称，专用检测器难以同时保持高准确率并避免误报，尤其在多轮提示后，水印也帮助有限。
[[#^pr-ddac8cd9e9b6|正文跳转]]

十五、识别 AI 的错觉：教师可能高估自己稳定识别 AI 生成作业的能力；当技术发展超出这种识别能力时，继续依赖旧式评估方式便难以有效阻止 AI 被用于学校作业。
[[#^pr-ddac8cd9e9b6|正文跳转]]

十六、AI写作难以可靠识别：人们，包括顶尖语言学期刊编辑和教师，都不擅长识别 AI 写作；简单文本或许较容易被发现，但提示词可以让 AI 模仿不同写作风格。材料还提到，读者认为经过良好提示的 AI 写作比人类写作更像人类写作。
[[#^pr-4708580de053|正文跳转]]

十七、AI检测也会误判：让 AI 判断一段人类写作是否由 AI 生成，并不能解决检测问题：材料援引的研究称，GPT-4 在这类判断中有 95% 的时候会出错。
[[#^pr-4c1167ec5b9e|正文跳转]]

十八、评估方式需要随之调整：保留旧作业的一种办法是采用课堂写作，或通过密切监考等方式减少 AI 作弊；但这需要教师改变做法，而截至材料所述，真正因 AI 检测可靠性受质疑而调整教学和评估方式的教师仍然很少。
[[#^pr-a88c24f9ba55|正文跳转]]

十九、作业表现不等于学会：学生可能没有意识到，借助 AI 获得答案和解题建议会让作业过程显得流畅，却可能损害实际学习。材料引用的研究学生可能会合理但错误地推断：能提高作业测验成绩的策略也会提高考试成绩。
[[#^pr-87083f25ca2a|正文跳转]]

二十、直接代做可能损害学习：一项在土耳其高中开展的实验比较了学生使用标准 ChatGPT、使用带 tutor prompt 的 ChatGPT 等情形。使用未经提示设计的标准 ChatGPT 帮助完成作业时，学生作业分数显著提高，但期末考试成绩低了 17%；学生虽觉得自己学到了很多，实际学得更少。
[[#^pr-2ab01f701309|正文跳转]]

二十一、引导式使用可能更有益：材料所引调查中，59% 的教师认为 AI 对学习有积极作用。土耳其研究显示，与学生自行使用 ChatGPT 相比，配有基础 tutor prompt 的 GPT 能提高作业分数而不降低期末考试成绩；另有一项斯坦福大型编程课研究发现，使用 ChatGPT 后考试成绩提高。
[[#^pr-0127df8252d6|正文跳转]]

二十二、AI 不应只围绕作业：学生使用 AI 还包括理解复杂主题、构思、复习知识、创作，以及获取反馈和建议。若只聚焦作业及其可能助长的错觉，可能难以推动教育实践取得进展。
[[#^pr-c6f0dd416c94|正文跳转]]

二十三、教师应成为 AI 使用的核心：近四分之三的教师已在工作中使用 AI，但有效用法仍待探索。一项近期深入定性研究发现，同时用 AI 生成内容并处理输入、辅助思考的教师，比只用 AI 生成内容能获得更多价值；这指向将 AI 作为协助人类更好思考的工具。
[[#^pr-5961e52d049e|正文跳转]]

二十四、用 prompts 揭示知识盲区：面向学生的 prompts 可以推动思考，而非让 AI 成为拐杖。相关 prompts 采用免费的 Creative Commons license，供教师按课堂需要定制或修改；其设计目标是揭示“幻觉式知识”，促使学生面对自己知道与不知道的内容。此类练习也体现了 AI 提供贴合学生实际水平、且可广泛普及的个性化学习体验的潜力。
[[#^pr-8b3e1f75aa96|正文跳转]]

[[#^pr-8b3e1f75aa96|正文跳转]]

[[#^pr-d8a39e077f59|正文跳转]]

27、教育应主动塑造 AI 变革：AI 融入教育已是当下现实；教育不应只被动接受、徒劳抵制，或假装 AI 不存在，而应重新思考教学、学习和知识评估，并利用 AI 的潜力增强教育、减轻负面影响，塑造更有效、公平且有吸引力的学习环境。

## 正文

去年夏天，我写过[the Homework Apocalypse,](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-homework-apocalypse)：一种即将到来的现实，在这种现实中，AI 可以完成大多数传统 homework assignments，使它们无法再作为 learning tools 和 assessment measures。我的预言已经成真，如今 AI 已经能在大多数 tests 中取得高分。然而，令人惊讶的是，即便 AI use 在 students 中几乎普及，结果却几乎没有任何改变。

^pr-ce98d1a786e7

截至八个月前，[a representative survey](https://8ce82b94a8c4fdc3ea6d-b1d233e3bc3cb10858bea65ff05e18f2.ssl.cf2.rackcdn.com/bf/24/cd3646584af89e7c668c7705a006/deck-impact-analysis-national-schools-tech-tracker-may-2024-1.pdf) 在美国发现，82% 的 undergraduates 和 72% 的 K12 students 曾在 school 中使用 AI。这种 adoption 速度快得惊人。在使用 AI 的 students 中，56% 用它来帮助完成 writing assignments，45% 用它来完成其他类型的 schoolwork。这项 survey 还发现了 AI 的许多积极用途，我们之后会再谈到；不过现在，让我们先聚焦于 AI assistance on homework 这个问题。Students 并不总认为寻求 AI 帮助是在 cheating（他们只是从中获得某个棘手问题或 essay 中某个难点的答案），但许多 teachers 却这么认为。

^pr-a8d843776ea4

需要明确的是，AI 并不是 cheating 的 root cause。Cheating 发生，是因为 schoolwork 很难，而且 stakes 很高。而 schoolwork 之所以困难且 stakes 很高，是因为[learning is not always fun](https://x.com/emollick/status/1756396139623096695)，而且为了让人们 learn，往往需要 grades 这类 extrinsic motivation。人们极其擅长想办法避开自己不喜欢做的事情；而且，[正如一项重要的新 analysis 所显示的](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037%2Fbul0000443)，大多数人都不喜欢 mental effort。于是，他们把其中一部分 effort 委托给 AI。总体而言，我赞成把 tasks 委托给 AI（这也是我在 MasterClass 开设的[新课程](https://urldefense.com/v3/__http://masterclass.com/ethanmollick__;!!IBzWLUs!SLXZqxW7DajpsWupHPF8ZBtTaO8Tm_5QvrSCO0k-zoETwmpdJK6DQOdD55YtHk01aCzjUvRT8faWqUuI6-GY3BJXVAtc2RnRcPXaHQ$)的主题），但 education 不同——effort 正是重点。

^pr-ab265f27f276

根据[这篇新 paper](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037%2Fbul0000443)，mental tasks 越难，我们往往感觉越糟。

^pr-a6c513f2ab71

这不是一个新问题。任何一项新 technology 最初的用途之一，一直都是帮助完成 homework。[一项针对 Rutgers 数千名 students 的 study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343624164_Fewer_students_are_benefiting_from_doing_their_homework_an_eleven-year_study) 发现，2008 年做 homework 时，86% 的学生 test grades 得到了提高（看吧，homework 确实有帮助！）；但到了 2017 年，homework 只对 45% 的 students 有帮助。为什么？因为 Internet 的兴起。到 2017 年，大多数 students 都在抄 internet answers，而不是自己完成作业。

^pr-5ca83a231da5

[

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!X4kj!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6455b64c-3b39-4834-bd60-d8f5b8696c0f_1242x688.jpeg)

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!X4kj!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F6455b64c-3b39-4834-bd60-d8f5b8696c0f_1242x688.jpeg)

The Homework Apocalypse 已经发生，甚至可能在 generative AI 出现之前就发生了！为什么更多人没有把这看作紧急事件？我认为这与两种 illusions 有关。

^pr-b68db485492c

第一种 illusion 是 Detection Illusion：teachers 认为自己仍然能轻易 detect AI use，因此能够阻止它被用于 schoolwork。这种 Detection Illusion 让 educators 继续依赖过时的 assessment methods；他们认为自己很容易识别出 AI-generated work，但事实上，technology 已远远超出了我们持续稳定地 identify 它的能力：

^pr-ddac8cd9e9b6

-   没有任何 specialized AI detectors 能够在保持 high accuracy 且不带来 t[he risk of false positives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819) 的情况下 detect AI writing，[尤其是在经过多轮 prompting 之后。](https://arxiv.org/ftp/arxiv/papers/2403/2403.19148.pdf)[即便是 watermarks 也帮不了多少](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156)。

-   人们并不擅长 detect AI writing。[顶尖 linguistics journals 的 editors 也做不到](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2772766123000289?via%3Dihub)。[Teachers 也做不到（尽管他们以为自己做得到——又是那个 Illusion）](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X24000109)。虽然简单的 AI writing 可能可以被 detect（“delve”，有人注意到了吗？），但只需简单的 prompting，就有很多办法可以伪装“AI writing”的 styles。事实上，读者认为经过良好 prompting 的 AI [writing 比人类写作更像人类写作](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08853)。 ^pr-4708580de053

-   你不能让 AI detect AI writing（尽管人们一直在尝试）。当被问到一段由人类写的内容是否由 AI 写成时，[GPT-4 有 95% 的时候会判断错误。](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14724) ^pr-4c1167ec5b9e

仍有一些办法可以保留旧的 assignments。Teachers 可以回到 in-class writing，让 students 当面展示自己的 skills；也可以采用其他可能通过密切监考来缓解 AI cheating 的 techniques。但对绝大多数 teachers 来说，这样做需要进行调整和改变，而这些改变至今尚未完成。迄今为止，鲜有 teachers 真正因 AI detection 的 illusion 破灭而作出回应，转而改变 teaching 和 assessment 的方式。

^pr-a88c24f9ba55

Teachers 正在应对 Detection Illusion，与此同时，students 也面临着自己的误解：Illusory Knowledge。他们实际上没有意识到，获得 homework 帮助会损害自己的 learning。毕竟，他们从 AI 那里获得 advice 和 answers，帮助自己 solve problems，而这会让他们觉得自己掌握得很流利。正如 Rutgers 那项 study 的 authors 所写：“没有理由认为 students 意识到，他们的 homework strategy 会降低 exam score……他们会作出符合常理的推断：任何能提高 homework quiz score 的 study strategy，也会提高 exam score。”

^pr-87083f25ca2a

我在 Penn 的一些同事所做的一项 study 发现，AI 似乎也在发生同样的事情。[他们在土耳其的一所 high school 做了一项 experiment](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4895486)，让一些 students 使用 GPT-4 来帮助完成 homework：有些人使用标准的 ChatGPT interface（没有 prompt engineering），另一些人则使用带有 tutor prompt 的 ChatGPT。Students 的 homework scores 大幅上升，但使用未经 prompting 的标准 ChatGPT 来帮助完成 homework，却像 crutch 一样损害了 learning。尽管 students 觉得自己从使用 ChatGPT 中学到了很多，实际上他们学得更少——final exam 的成绩低了 17%。

^pr-2ab01f701309

尽管如此，我前面引用的 survey 发现，59% 的 teachers 认为 AI 对 learning 有积极作用；我不认为他们错了。只把 AI 当作 crutch 使用可能会损害 learning，但更谨慎地使用 AI 则不同。Turkey study 显示了这一点：与让 students 自行使用 ChatGPT 相比，给他们一个搭配基础 tutor prompt 的 GPT，能够提高 homework scores，同时不降低 final exam grades。此外，[一项在 Stanford 的大型 programming class 中开展的 study 发现，使用 ChatGPT](https://osf.io/download/6628930d80d25c0de8f919e6/) 带来的 exam grades 是提高，而不是降低。

^pr-0127df8252d6

当然，students 使用 AI 并不只是为了做 homework。他们还借助 AI 理解 complex topics、brainstorming ideas、refreshing their knowledge、创造新的 creative work、获取 feedback 和 advice，以及做更多事情。只聚焦于 homework 这个问题及其助长的 illusions，可能会让我们难以取得进展。

^pr-c6f0dd416c94

要取得进展，我们需要让 teachers 成为使用 AI 过程的核心，而不是只把 AI 留给 students（或那些梦想彻底取代 teachers 的人）。我们知道，近四分之三的 teachers 已经在工作中使用 AI，但我们才刚开始了解 teachers 使用 AI 的最有效方式。一项近期的[深入定性研究](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4924786)发现，同时用 AI 产出内容（制作 worksheet、设计 quiz），并借助 AI 处理输入（帮助我思考什么成就了一部 Great American novel，给我一些解释正数和负数的方法）的 teachers，比只用 AI 产出内容能获得更多价值。这为 AI in education 指明了一条有用的前进路径：把它用作 co-intelligence 和 tool，帮助人类更好地思考。

^pr-5961e52d049e

如今，AI 越来越多地以同样的方式用于 students，推动他们思考，而不是把 AI 当作 crutch。[例如，我们发布了多个 prompts，采用免费的 Creative Commons license，instructors 可以为自己的 classrooms 定制或修改这些 prompts](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4802463)（这里深入介绍了[其中一个——simulator prompt](https://hbsp.harvard.edu/inspiring-minds/using-generative-ai-to-create-role-play-scenarios-for-students)）。这类 prompts 旨在揭示 Illusory Knowledge，迫使 students 正视自己知道和不知道的内容。许多其他 educators 也在设计类似的 exercises。这样一来，我们就能利用 AI 在 teaching 方面的潜力：它能够提供定制的 learning experiences，贴合 students 的实际水平，并且能够以过去的 educational technology 从未实现过的方式广泛普及。

## 原文

Last summer, I wrote about [the Homework Apocalypse,](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-homework-apocalypse) the coming reality where AI could complete most traditional homework assignments, rendering them ineffective as learning tools and assessment measures. My prophecy has come true, and AI can now ace most tests. Yet remarkably little has changed as a result, even as AI use became nearly universal among students.

As of eight months ago, [a representative survey](https://8ce82b94a8c4fdc3ea6d-b1d233e3bc3cb10858bea65ff05e18f2.ssl.cf2.rackcdn.com/bf/24/cd3646584af89e7c668c7705a006/deck-impact-analysis-national-schools-tech-tracker-may-2024-1.pdf) in the US found that 82% of undergraduates and 72% of K12 students had used AI for school. That is extraordinarily rapid adoption. Of the students using AI, 56% used it for help with writing assignments, and 45% for completing other types of schoolwork. The survey found many positive uses of AI as well, which we will return to, but, for now, let’s focus on the question of AI assistance on homework. Students don’t always see getting AI help as cheating (they are simply getting answers to some tricky problem or a challenging part of an essay), but many teachers do.

To be clear, AI is not the root cause of cheating. Cheating happens because schoolwork is hard and high stakes. And schoolwork is hard and high stakes because [learning is not always fun](https://x.com/emollick/status/1756396139623096695) and forms of extrinsic motivation, like grades, are often required to get people to learn. People are exquisitely good at figuring out ways to avoid things they don’t like to do, and, [as a major new analysis shows](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037%2Fbul0000443), most people don’t like mental effort. So, they delegate some of that effort to the AI. In general, I am in favor of delegating tasks to AI (the subject of my [new class on MasterClass](https://urldefense.com/v3/__http://masterclass.com/ethanmollick__;!!IBzWLUs!SLXZqxW7DajpsWupHPF8ZBtTaO8Tm_5QvrSCO0k-zoETwmpdJK6DQOdD55YtHk01aCzjUvRT8faWqUuI6-GY3BJXVAtc2RnRcPXaHQ$)), but education is different - the effort is the point.

As mental tasks get harder, we tend to feel worse, according to [this new paper](https://psycnet.apa.org/doiLanding?doi=10.1037%2Fbul0000443).

This is not a new problem. One of the first uses of any new technology has always been to get help with homework. [A study of thousands of students at Rutgers](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343624164_Fewer_students_are_benefiting_from_doing_their_homework_an_eleven-year_study) found that when they did their homework in 2008, it improved test grades for 86% of them (see, homework really does help!), but homework only helped 45% of students in 2017. Why? The rise of the Internet. By 2017, a majority of students were copying internet answers, rather than doing the work themselves.

The Homework Apocalypse has already happened and may even have happened before generative AI! Why are more people not seeing this as an emergency? I think it has to do with two illusions.

The first illusion is the Detection Illusion: teachers believe they can still easily detect AI use, and therefore can prevent it from being used in schoolwork. This Detection Illusion leads educators to rely on outdated assessment methods, believing they can easily spot AI-generated work when in reality, the technology has far surpassed our ability to consistently identify it:

-   No specialized AI detectors can detect AI writing with high accuracy and without t[he risk of false positives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819), [especially after multiple rounds of prompting.](https://arxiv.org/ftp/arxiv/papers/2403/2403.19148.pdf) [Even watermarks won’t help much](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.11156).

-   People can’t detect AI writing well. [Editors at top linguistics journals couldn’t](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2772766123000289?via%3Dihub). [Teachers couldn’t (though they thought they could - the Illusion again)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666920X24000109). While simple AI writing might be detectable (“delve,” anyone?), there are plenty of ways to disguise “AI writing” styles through simples prompting. In fact, well-prompted AI [writing is judged more human than human writing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.08853) by readers.

-   You can’t ask an AI to detect AI writing (even though people keep trying). When asked if something written by a human was written by an AI, [GPT-4 gets it wrong 95% of the time.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14724)

There are still options that preserve old assignments. Teachers can return to in-class writing, asking students to demonstrate their skills in person, or other techniques that might mitigate AI cheating through close monitoring. But, for the vast majority of teachers, doing so requires adjustment and changes that have yet to be made. To date, few have actually reacted to the shattering of the illusion of AI detection by shifting how they approach teaching and assessment.

While teachers grapple with the Detection Illusion, students face their own misconception: Illusory Knowledge. They don’t actually realize that getting help with homework is undermining their learning. After all, they are getting advice and answers from the AI that help them solve problems, which feels like fluency. As the authors of the study at Rutgers wrote: “There is no reason to believe that the students are aware that their homework strategy lowers their exam score... they make the commonsense inference that any study strategy that raises their homework quiz score raises their exam score as well.”

The same thing appears to be happening with AI, as a study by some of my colleagues at Penn discovered. [They conducted an experiment at a high school in Turkey](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4895486) where some students were given access to GPT-4 to help with homework, either through the standard ChatGPT interface (no prompt engineering) or using ChatGPT with a tutor prompt. Student homework scores shot up, but the use of unprompted standard ChatGPT to help with homework undermined learning by acting like a crutch. Even though students thought they learned a lot from using ChatGPT, they actually learned less - scoring 17% worse on their final exam.

Despite this, the survey I quoted earlier found that 59% of teachers see AI as positive for learning, and I don’t think they are wrong. While just using AI as a crutch can hurt learning, more careful use of AI is different. We can see signs of this in the Turkey study, which found that giving students a GPT with a basic tutor prompt for ChatGPT, instead of having them use ChatGPT on their own, boosted homework scores without lowering final exam grades. Plus, a study done in a [massive programming class at Stanford that found use of ChatGPT](https://osf.io/download/6628930d80d25c0de8f919e6/) led to increased, not decreased, exam grades.

And, of course, students are not using AI just to do their homework. They are getting aid in understanding complex topics, brainstorming ideas, refreshing their knowledge, creating new forms of creative work, getting feedback, getting advice, and so much more. Focusing just on the question of homework, and the illusions it fosters, can discourage us from making progress.

To do so we need to center teachers in the process of using AI, rather than just leaving AI to students (or to those who dream of replacing teachers entirely). We know that almost three-quarters of teachers are already using AI for work, but we have just started to learn the most effective ways for teachers to use AI. A recent [deep qualitative study of teachers](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4924786) found that teachers who used AI for both output (create a worksheet, develop a quiz) and to help with input (help me think through what makes a Great American novel, give me ways to explain positive and negative numbers) get more value than if they use AI for producing output alone. This points to a useful path forward in AI for education, using it as a co-intelligence and tool for helping humans do better thinking.

Increasingly, AI is being used in the same way for students, pushing them to think, rather than use AI as a crutch. [For example, we have released multiple prompts, all under a free Creative Commons license, that instructors can customize or modify for their classrooms](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4802463) (here is deep dive into [one of them - a simulator prompt)](https://hbsp.harvard.edu/inspiring-minds/using-generative-ai-to-create-role-play-scenarios-for-students). These sorts of prompts are designed to expose Illusory Knowledge, forcing students to confront what they know and don’t know. Many other educators are designing similar exercises. In doing so, we can take advantage of what makes AI so promising for teaching - its ability to produce customized learning experiences that meet students where they are, and which are broadly accessible in ways that past forms of educational technology never were.

The integration of AI in education is not a future possibility—it's our present reality. This shift demands more than passive acceptance or futile resistance. It requires a fundamental reimagining of how we teach, learn, and assess knowledge. As AI becomes an integral part of the educational landscape, our focus must evolve. The goal isn't to outsmart AI or to pretend it doesn't exist, but to harness its potential to enhance education while mitigating the downside. The question now is not whether AI will change education, but how we will shape that change to create a more effective, equitable, and engaging learning environment for all.
