# Post 28: On Creativity - the joys of 5 minute timers — Neel Nanda

## 正文

Introduction（引言）

我在自己和别人身上观察到的一种非常常见的 failure mode，是陷入 _stuck_ 状态，然后放弃。正在处理一个 problem，或试图 generate ideas，却觉得很难，于是不再寻找。我发现，这与一个大得多的 problem 密切相伴：被困在自己的 preconceptions 中——只注意到你的 problem 的 obvious solution、眼前那条显而易见的人生道路，以及处在你这个角色的人显然_应该_做的事，却从未意识到还可能有更多选择。我认为这是一个_非常_大的 problem，因为世界广阔而复杂。possibilities 的 space 是_巨大的_，而超越那些显而易见之处进行 search 的 ability，对于察觉自己何时正在 [wasting motion](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-22-the-8020-rule) 非常有价值。

我认为，这些 problems 的核心是 **lack of creativity**。我认为 creativity 是一种极其宝贵的 skill，而且重要的是，它是一种 _learnable_ 的 _skill_，而不是 inherent trait。这种 skill 我现在仍然很不擅长，但随着时间推移，我已经进步了很多；这篇文章就是我对最适合自己的 approach 所做的一次 outline 尝试。

在 art 和 research 等场景中，这显然是一种有用的 skill。但我发现，它在我生活中的许多领域都很有用。几个突出的例子是：

-   [Solving personal problems](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)（解决个人问题）

-   给别人提供 advice

-   对我在工作时遇到的小 problems 进行 Solving

-   寻找让自己开心的方式

-   寻找[让我感到兴奋的](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about) projects

5 Minute Timers（5 分钟计时器）

我认为这里有两个主要的 insights：

-   先 Generate，_然后_再 filter ideas

    -   我是个十足的 perfectionist，我发现自己很容易滑入这样一种 failure mode：试图 generating 那个 _perfect_ 的 idea。这极其无益，因为它在实际中会令人 paralysing，意味着我很难想出任何东西。任何 weak、half-formed 的 ideas，还没来得及被 fleshed out 成有价值的东西，就已经被 discarded 了

    -   这是一个普遍的 failure mode 的 example——试图同时进行 generate 和 filter。

        -   我发现，这两件事涉及我 mind 中非常不同的部分——generating ideas 应该感觉是 free-flowing 和 diffuse 的，而 filtering 应该感觉是 constrained、directed 和 focused 的。而这些不同的 mindsets 无法同时被 activated，所以在实际中，我不是忽略其中一个，就是在它们之间 rapidly switching

    -   对于任何让人觉得害怕或不堪重负的 decision，这一点尤其适用——比如思考 life decisions 时

        -   而这非常、非常糟糕，因为最吓人的往往正是 unknown 和 fuzzy 的东西！让 unknown 的东西变得 clear 和 concrete，往往就会让人觉得更容易去 pursue，所以这让我陷入了 catch-22。

        -   例如，我最近在考虑休一个 gap year，却觉得这个想法有点令人不堪重负。于是我决定坐下来花一个小时，generate 一份 list，列出那一年里我可以做的 50 件不同的事。这是一次非常有用的 exercise；如果那一个小时里，我一直因为想到真的要_做_其中任何一件事而感到不堪重负，那就完全不可能做到这一点

-   Hard 并不 mean impossible

    -   一种常见的 failure mode 是觉得自己 _stuck_ 了，然后放弃。这完全可以理解！Feeling stuck 令人不快，也很难熬，而撑过去需要 [willpower](https://neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)

    -   但是，在实际中，尽管你可能_感觉_ being stuck 意味着某件事是 impossible，它真正意味着的只是某件事是 hard。这一点很重要，因为面对 impossible task 的 solution 应该是放弃，而面对 hard task 的 solution 往往只是继续尝试！这是一种极其常见而 subtle 的 error，重要的是在当下察觉它

    -   我发现，通过 practice，overcoming 这一点会变得容易得多——即使你知道一切只是自己的心理感受，要让自己继续做一项感觉 impossible 而且 fruitless 的 task，仍然很难。只有在真正取得一些成功之后，你才能开始改变自己对此的感受。

    -   更一般地说，我发现，overcoming 这一点，是成为一个[真正会动手做事的人](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things)，以及做成任何特别有趣或不同寻常的事情所需的一项 _key_ subskill。如果某件事是大多数人不做或做不到的，那么通常就会有某种 mental barrier 挡在路上。而且，虽然了解自己的 limits 很有价值，但你应该努力[了解自己 _true_ 的 limits](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/26-accurate-self-image)，而不是把你以为自己的 limits 是什么的 cached thoughts 当成 limits。

当然，与任何需要 willpower 才能 apply 的 insights 一样，solution 不是[更努力地尝试](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)，而是[将它们 systematise](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)！我在这里的主要 tool 是，每次注意到自己想要 creative 时，就设一个 5 minute timer，打开一份空白 document，或拿一张纸，然后 generate solutions，直到 timer 响起。

这是我最喜欢的 life hacks 之一：一方面，它听起来简单得不可思议，似乎根本不可能真的有效；另一方面，它在实际中又极其有用。这让我能够以一种极其 low-willpower 的方式 implement 这两个 insights！我会用完整的 5 minutes 进行 generating，而且由于 time limit 相当紧，保持 focused 很容易。这意味着我的 mind 很少走神，我也能够在心理上保持 generating 与 filtering 之间的 distinction，根本没有足够的_时间_像那样分心！而且，它迫使我在 getting stuck 之后继续下去，因为只有 5 minutes！如果实在有必要，我完全可以坐在那里，盯着一张白纸看上 5 minutes。但在实际中，我总能想出点什么。

此外，你会希望这件事是 systematised 的——它应该成为一种 _reflex_。我尽量始终在伸手可及的地方放一个 physical timer（我推荐[这种样式](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=time%20timer&ref=nb_sb_noss_2)，它的视觉呈现很棒），让这件事尽可能容易做到（我也喜欢直接说“Alexa，5 minute timer”这个功能）。而且，我已经培养了这样的 habit：每当注意到自己感觉 stuck、没有 ideas，或是在做任何哪怕稍微_重要_一点的事情时，就开始一次 5 minute brainstorm。具体采用 5 minutes，其实相当 arbitrary，4 或 6 minutes 也一样有效。关键是有一个固定的 default time，从而 minimise 设置 timer 所需的 activation energy。

_尽管_我完全相信 5 minute timers 很棒，但我仍然常常发现自己在抱怨或纠结某个 problem，却连至少 5 minutes 都没有花来尝试 solve 它；这种情况多得让我难为情！我也经常在别人身上发现这一点，以至于我最喜欢问别人的问题之一就是：“你有没有花至少 5 minutes 来思考这个 problem？”5 minutes 是如此_短_的一段时间，我认为你对这个问题的回答根本就不应该是否定的。我认为，一种很好的 social norm 是：如果你注意到自己或别人出现了这种 failure mode，就设一个 5 minute timer，让你们_两个人_都来思考这个 problem。（读到这里的任何人，只要觉得我没有足够努力地尝试，都随时可以问我这个问题！）

坦白说，这么简单的东西竟然如此有用，让我觉得有些沮丧。而且我认为，读到文章这里时心存怀疑很合理。所以，如果你确实怀疑，我提议做一个 test：设一个 5 minute timer，列一份你生活中的 problems 的 list——那些令你烦恼的事、你想着手处理的事，以及本可以变得更好的事。然后逐项查看这份 list，把那些你确信自己已经花过至少 5 minutes 的 focused 时间尝试 solve 的划掉。如果你和我有几分相似，剩下的 list 会长得令人难为情。我会很想知道，如果你对剩下的任何一项尝试做一次 5 minute brainstorm，会发生什么。

其他来源

creativity 的另一个绝佳 source，是借助其他人！正如 [Nate Soares 所指出的](http://mindingourway.com/obvious-advice/)，那些_我_觉得 obvious 的 advice，别人往往并不觉得 obvious，反过来也一样。我们都有自己惯用的 toolkits，以及看待世界的方式。而这些在人与人之间差异很大！当你想要拓展 possible ideas 的视野时，向别人寻求 advice 或 suggestions 几乎总是有用的。已经数不清有多少次，别人给了我一个_他们_觉得 obvious、我却绝对想不到的 suggestion，而我一听就立刻觉得很有 insight。

你也可以在 groups 中使用 5 minute timers！每当我参与 group discussion 时，我都觉得一开始先设一个 5 minute timer，让每个人独自 generate ideas，然后再分享，是极其有用的。我们太容易被别人的想法 anchored，而这样做能够确保我们一开始就拥有广泛的 ideas。

Conclusion（结论）

我认为，creative 是一种_非常_有用的 skill。而且，如果你还没有给自己设 5 minute timers，那么你就错过了一些极其 low-hanging fruit。培养这个 habit，显著提高了我 creative 的 ability，让我能够想出第一遍没能想到的 ideas。

更一般地说，在当下_注意到_该开始一个 5 minute timer，并不容易。我认为，practice 并有意识地 develop 这个 habit 会很有用！我发现，把它用于 open-ended、introspective 的 prompts（比如在 weekly review 中），几乎一直都很有用。所以，如果这篇文章引起了你的共鸣，下面是一些我觉得有用的 examples。我建议你选一个自己觉得有意思的 prompt，用接下来的 5 minutes 来思考它！毕竟，[开始一个新 habit 的最佳时机就是现在](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-24-on-procrastination-the-art-of-shaping-your-future-actions)。

-   我在什么事情上 procrastinating？

-   我在为什么事情 unnecessarily stressing？

-   我目前正在犯的一个 mistake 是什么？

-   今天我能做的一件_特别棒_的事是什么？

-   我目前遗漏了哪些 obvious ideas？

-   我能为别人做的一件好事是什么？

-   我最近学到的最酷的东西有哪些？

-   我最大的 bottleneck 是什么？

-   我目前在为什么而感恩？

最后，如果你读完了整篇文章，却觉得它与你无关，想不出自己生活中的哪些领域需要更 creative。那么，嗯，这件事本身就像是一种 lack of creativity。我会很想看看，如果你设一个 5 minute timer，试着 generate 一些你_可以_运用这些 ideas 的地方，会发生什么。

## 原文

Introduction

A really common failure mode that I observe in myself and others is to get _stuck_ and then give up. To be working on a problem, or trying to generate ideas, find it hard and stop looking. I find that this goes hand-in-hand with the far larger problem of being trapped within your preconceptions - to notice the obvious solution to your problem, the obvious life path ahead of you, the obvious thing somebody in your role _should_ do, and never realising there can be anything more. I think this is a _really_ big problem, because the world is large and complicated. The space of possibilities is _enormous_ and the ability to search beyond what’s obviously visible is really valuable for noticing when you are [wasting motion](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-22-the-8020-rule).

I think these problems are, at their heart, a **lack of creativity**. I think creativity is an incredibly valuable skill and, importantly, a _learnable_ _skill_, not an inherent trait. This is a skill that I still suck at, but I’ve improved a lot at over time, and this post is my attempt to outline the approach that’s worked best for me.

This is obviously a useful skill in settings like doing art and research. But I’ve found it useful in a wide range of areas in my life. A few highlights:

-   [Solving personal problems](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)

-   Giving advice to others

-   Solving the small problems I run into when doing work

-   Finding ways to have fun

-   Finding projects [I feel excited about](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)

5 Minute Timers

I think there are two main insights here:

-   Generate _then_ filter ideas

    -   I’m a massive perfectionist, and I find it easy to slip into the failure mode of generating the _perfect_ idea. This is incredibly unhelpful, because this is paralysing in practice, and means I struggle to come up with anything. Any weak and half-formed ideas are discarded before they can be fleshed out into something worthwhile

    -   This is an example of a general failure mode - trying to both generate and filter at the same time.

        -   I find that these involve very different parts of my mind - generating ideas should feel free-flowing and diffuse, while filtering should feel constrained, directed and focused. And these different mindsets can’t be activated at once, so in practice I’m either neglecting one or rapidly switching between them

    -   This especially applies when it comes to any decision that feels scary or overwhelming - eg thinking about life decisions

        -   And this is super, super bad, because often the scariest thing is something unknown and fuzzy! By making the unknown feel clear and concrete, often it’ll feel a lot easier to pursue, so this leaves me stuck in a catch-22.

        -   For example, I was recently considering the idea of taking a gap year, and feeling a bit overwhelmed by the idea. So I decided to sit down for an hour, and generate a list of 50 different things I could do in that year. This was a really useful exercise, and would’ve been completely impossible if I’d spent that hour overwhelmed by the thought of actually _doing_ any of them

-   Hard does not mean impossible

    -   A common failure mode is to feel _stuck_ and give up. This is completely understandable! Feeling stuck is unpleasant and hard, and pushing through it takes [willpower](https://neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)

    -   But, in practice, while it may _feel_ like being stuck means something is impossible, all it really means is that something is hard. This is important, because the solution to an impossible task should be to give up, while the solution to a hard task is often to just keep trying! This is a super common and subtle error, and it’s important to notice it in the moment

    -   I’ve found that overcoming this gets a lot easier with practice - it’s difficult to make yourself continue on a task that feels impossible and fruitless, even if you know it’s all in your head. Only after having some genuine successes can you begin to change how you feel about this.

    -   More generally, I find that overcoming this is a _key_ subskill to becoming a [person who Actually Does Things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things), and to do anything particularly interesting or unusual. If it’s something that most people don’t or can’t do, then there will normally be some kind of mental barrier in the way. And while it’s valuable to know your limits, you should try to [know your _true_ limits](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/26-accurate-self-image), not the cached thoughts of what you think your limits are.

Of course, as with any insights that take willpower to apply, the solution is not to [try harder](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button), it’s to [systematise them](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)! And my main tool here is to notice every time I want to be creative, set a 5 minute timer, get a blank document up or a piece of paper and generate solutions until the timer goes off.

This is one of my favourite life hacks, for both sounding so ridiculously simple that there’s no way it actually works, and being incredibly useful in practice. This lets me implement both insights in a super low-willpower way! I spend the full 5 minutes generating and, because I’m under a fairly sharp time limit, it’s easy to remain focused. This means my mind rarely wanders, and I can maintain the mental distinction between generating and filtering, there isn’t enough _time_ to get distracted like that! And it forces me to continue past getting stuck, because it’s only for 5 minutes! If I really need to, I can just sit there for 5 minutes staring at a blank piece of paper. But, in practice, I always come up with something.

Further, this is something you want to be systematised - it should be a _reflex_. I try to always have a physical timer within arms reach (I recommend [this style](https://www.amazon.co.uk/s?k=time%20timer&ref=nb_sb_noss_2) for great visuals) to make it as easy to do as possible (I also like the ability to say “Alexa, 5 minute timer”). And I’ve cultivated the habit of noticing any time I feel stuck, or out of ideas, or when I’m doing anything remotely _important_, and starting a 5 minute brainstorm. The fact that it’s 5 minutes, specifically, is pretty arbitrary, 4 or 6 minutes would work equally well. The point is to have a single default time, to minimise the activation energy of setting one.

_Even though_ I’m fully convinced that 5 minute timers are awesome, an embarrassing amount of the time I still notice myself complaining about or dwelling on a problem without having spent at least 5 minutes trying to solve it! And I also notice this a lot in other people, to the point where one of my favourite questions to ask people is “have you spent at least 5 minutes thinking about this problem?”. 5 minutes is such a _short_ amount of time, that I don’t think your answer to that question should ever be no. I think a great social norm, if you ever notice this failure mode in yourself or somebody else, is to set a 5 minute timer and have _both_ of you think about the problem. (Anyone reading this has an open invitation to ask me this question if you ever think I’m not trying hard enough!)

Frankly, I find it somewhat depressing that anything as simple as this is so useful. And I think it’s reasonable at this point in the article to feel skeptical. So, if you do, I propose a test: Set a 5 minute timer, and make a list of problems in your life - things that annoy you, things you want to work on, things that could be better. And then, go through that list, and cross off any you’re confident you’ve spent at least 5 minutes of focused time trying to solve. If you’re anything like me, you’ll have an embarrassingly long list left over. I’d be pretty curious about what happens if you try doing a 5 minute brainstorm for anything left.

Alternate sources

Another great source of creativity is using other people! As [Nate Soares notes](http://mindingourway.com/obvious-advice/), often the advice that feels obvious to _me_ doesn’t feel obvious to somebody else, and vice versa. We all have our standard toolkits, and ways of seeing the world. And these vary a lot between people! Asking people for advice or suggestions is almost universally useful when you want to expand your horizons of possible ideas. I’ve lost count of the number of times someone has given me a suggestion _they_ thought was obvious that I’d never have come up with, but seemed instantly insightful when I heard it.

You can also use 5 minute timers in groups! Whenever I’m part of a group discussion, I think it’s extremely useful at the start to set a 5 minute timer, and have everyone generate ideas on their own, and then share. It’s super easy to get anchored on other people’s thoughts, and this ensures we can start out with a wide range of ideas.

Conclusion

I think being creative is a _really_ useful skill. And that if you aren’t setting 5 minute timers for yourself, you’re missing out on some incredibly low-hanging fruit. Cultivating this habit has significantly increased my ability to be creative, and come up with ideas I missed on the first pass.

More generally, it’s difficult to _notice_ in the moment to start a 5 minute timer. I think it can be useful to practice and explicitly develop the habit! I find it pretty consistently useful to try it on open-ended, introspective prompts (in, eg a weekly review). So, if this article has resonated with you, here are some examples I’ve found useful. I recommend picking a prompt that seems fun to you, and spending the next 5 minutes thinking about it! After all, [the best time to start a new habit is right now](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-24-on-procrastination-the-art-of-shaping-your-future-actions).

-   What am I procrastinating about?

-   What am I stressing unnecessarily about?

-   What’s a mistake I’m currently making?

-   What’s something _really awesome_ I could do today?

-   What obvious ideas am I currently missing?

-   What’s something nice I could do for somebody else?

-   What are the coolest things I’ve learned recently?

-   What’s my biggest bottleneck?

-   What am I currently grateful for?

Finally, if you’ve gotten through this entire article, and it doesn’t feel relevant to you. That you can’t come up with areas of your life where you’d like to be more creative. Then, well, that in itself feels like a lack of creativity. I’d be pretty curious to see what happens if you set a 5 minute timer, and try to generate places you _could_ use these ideas.
