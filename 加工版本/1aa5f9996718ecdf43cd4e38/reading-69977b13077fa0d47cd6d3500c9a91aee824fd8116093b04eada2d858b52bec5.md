# Mini Blog Post 15: The illusion of doing nothing — Neel Nanda

---

## 正文

_（这篇文章很大程度上延续了_[_成为一个 Actually Does Things 的人_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things)_的精神，但我试图着重更充分地阐明 underlying errors 和 next actions）_

在 finance industry 实习给我带来的一个较为意外的好处，是我现在对于 probabilities、expected values，以及 uncertainty 下的 reasoning 和 decision-making，有了清晰得_多_的 intuition。这也凸显了我头脑中一个强烈的 bias：面对一个 risky opportunity，什么都不做会让我觉得舒服得多。面对 risk 时，我总想坚持 safe option。拒绝新的 opportunities。不让自己迈出去。

而我觉得这_非常、非常_愚蠢。这个世界_充满_了能让我的生活好得多的 opportunities！可能成为亲密朋友或伴侣的陌生人。我还不知道、却可能真正热爱的工作。能让我大展身手的新爱好和热情所在。我可以做的精彩演讲。我可能喜欢的新食物。

然而，面对 uncertainty，迈出答应尝试、大胆行动的那一步_很难_。这让人害怕，我的注意力会牢牢盯着所有的 costs、risks、所需的 effort 和 downsides，却忽视了我可能错过的所有美好 benefits。而且，downsides 往往比 upsides 更重要，这_确实_是真的——比起 gaining £1000，我更希望 avoid losing £1000。但这里的 effect 比那要明显得_多_。这严重阻碍了我 achieving 我的 goals，因为这意味着我会 systematically 错过让事情变得更好的途径。我把这叫作 **illusion of doing nothing**——什么都不做_感觉_很 safe，但这归根结底[全在我的头脑里，而我的 goals 存在于现实世界中](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-12-the-map-and-the-territory)。

用 economics 的术语来说，我忽视了 [**opportunity costs**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_cost)。每当我花钱买东西，问题都不是“我想要这个东西吗？”，而是“这个东西是否比这笔钱的其他花法，也就是那些 _alternatives_，更好？”这一点极其容易被忽视，因为 opportunity costs 是 invisible 的，而不是 visceral 的。但它们仍然_存在_。我发现，opportunity costs 是理解世界以及如何 make decisions 的一种根本重要的方式，因为它们_无处不在_。当我什么都不做时，我忽视了自己本_可以_做的事情所对应的全部 opportunity costs，因为它们感觉并不 visceral。

例如，假设我遇到了一个有趣的人，我们将来可能成为亲密朋友，但我感到 socially anxious，缺乏和对方保持联系的勇气与主动性。这样感觉 safe，也感觉舒服。但我是在用失去一段亲密友谊这一_重大_ loss，换取那种暂时的 safety 感。这种 loss 感觉并不 _visceral_，因为它只是未来一件潜在而模糊的事情。但 visceral 的感觉存在于我的头脑中，并不会 affect 我的 long-term happiness。放过这样的 opportunity，感觉上或许没什么，但归根结底，我已经 _lost_。而这种 safety 感不过是 illusion of doing nothing。做一个 thought experiment：现在想象一位亲密朋友，问问自己，这段友谊对你而言值得付出多少 effort、承受多少 social anxiety。再问问自己，假如有 10% chance 获得另一段同样亲密的友谊，又值得付出多少？而当你遇到一个有趣的人时，你_实际上_又投入了多少？你的 actions 是否符合你的 values，还是说，你只是在做感觉最 safest 的事？

这是一个_非常_ _common_ 的 error。我的 intuitions 中，许多重大问题都源于它。我是一个相当 loss averse、risk averse 的人，因为坚持 status quo、什么都不做，感觉很 _safe_；而让自己迈出去，感觉很_可怕_。这在一定程度上源于 self-justification 和 avoiding blame。什么都不做感觉是 _justifiable_ 的，好像那就不是我的错。而如果我迈出去了，事情却出了岔子，那就感觉像是 _failure_，而且我知道自己会感到 regret。但这些 guilt、blame、justification 的 emotions，全都只是 illusions。我在乎的是 _results_，是那些能够 achieve 我的 long-term goals 的事情。

这一点还有另一种表现，就是对 _default_ action 的感觉。无论 default、符合社会惯例的事情是什么，照着做都会感觉 safe，而 deviate from 它就会感觉_可怕_——这不过又是 illusion of doing nothing 的一个 example。我是个极端的 perfectionist，部分原因是，埋头死磕自己的工作感觉像是 _default_；不这样做、直接把东西拿出来，反而让人害怕。Cambridge 到处都是把所有时间都用来学习、不断逼自己的学生。有时候，这_确实_是正确的 decision！但根据我的经验，更常见的原因是，学习感觉像是 _default_，是他们的角色、他们的义务。这样做感觉 safe。而做些不同的事情，比如出去加入一个社团，或者培养新爱好，就让人觉得_可怕_、_不负责任_。学习时间减少这一 cost，感觉很 _visceral_。而他们错过的所有令人兴奋的新事物所对应的 opportunity costs，却没有这种感觉。

我认为这是一个极其 common 的问题，但每个人都有不同的 values、neuroses 和 opportunities。所以说到这里，我强烈建议设一个 5 分钟的计时器，试着想出你自己的 examples。对我而言（我在这个问题上已经取得了很大进展，所以这些大多是“3 年前的 Neel”的问题）：

-   我会 default to 拒绝事情。比如有趣的 project ideas、活动邀请、认识新朋友的机会。costs，以及自己可能不喜欢它的 possibility，感觉很 _visceral_，而 uncertain benefits 则不然

-   在社交或恋爱中不主动：与刚认识的人 initiate a friendship、organise events，或约别人出去，都让人觉得_可怕_。打扰对方，或者在对方没兴趣时造成 emotional labour 的 chance，感觉非常 _visceral_。而未来重大的 upside 却感觉并不 visceral

-   不去 [Actually Doing Things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things)

-   不让自己迈出去：拒绝那些感觉有点可怕，或者在我的 comfort zone 之外的事情。比如做演讲、办聚会、启动 projects。

-   不去 investing money，而且想要完全 0 risk 的东西，即使 rates 差得多也如此（这一点我现在仍然做得不好）

-   接受一份违背我的 self-image 的工作或课程——成为一个生活在 pure abstraction 世界中的 pure mathematician，感觉像是 _default_。而做这之外的事情就感觉_奇怪_，那不是 default。

说到这里，一个很自然的 objection 是，risks _确实_有 cost。失去东西是痛苦的。生活充满 opportunities，而 evaluate 和 prioritise 它们需要 mental energy 和 effort。接受_每一个_ risky opportunity，on average 很可能会有所损失。我认为这些都是很好、很重要的 points！你_不_应该把_所有_ risky 的事都做一遍。你不应该对_一切_都说好。evaluating things 确实是一种 skill，而这需要时间、经验和练习。

但我认为，归根结底，这里的 error 是人们不想_选择_如何应对 opportunities。必须制定一个 policy，并且 think explicitly about 这些问题，是困难而累人的。但归根结底，你_总是_有一个 policy。也许你的 policy 是：“面对一个 opportunity，完全遵从我的 intuitions，以及 default、safe 的 action。”有时候这效果很好，有时候却很差。而 changing 你的 policy，会涉及加入一个对应于“花费时间和 effort 来_思考_事情”的 term；也许它糟糕到足以让结果变得更差。但你无法选择完全没有 policy，_那_正是 illusion of doing nothing。这里存在一个 trade-off；如果你不思考它，你就是为了省去思考所需的 mental effort，而任由自己的 choice 被夺走。**不存在真正 safe 的 option，只有深浅不同的灰色。**

希望到现在，我已经让你相信：这是一个重要而重大的 bias，它存在于你的思维中，并且阻碍着你实现 goals。但这还不足以 _solve_ 这个 bias。我们很容易陷入这样的误区：以为理解这个 bias 就已大功告成，下次只要[更努力一点](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)，就能靠意志力 avoid 它。这基本上从来不管用。所以，在文章余下的部分，我将概述自己如何让克服它变得 _actionable_。

靠更加努力来 correct 这个 error 之所以困难，一个原因是，在当下很容易忘记它——你的脑海里还有许多其他事情。所以，如果你遇到了一个重大的 decision，明确留出一些时间来 prioritise 并仔细思考相关事情，在_那时_提醒自己注意这些 biases，可能会很有帮助。如果你的生活充满这样的 decisions，就安排定期的 reflection time，比如 weekly review，在其中问自己：“我目前正在拖延哪个 decision？”这可能感觉像是在浪费时间，但花时间认真考虑事情_极其_有价值——花费 5% 的时间，让其余时间的 effective 程度提高 10%，是一笔_极好_的 trade。这就是你的 intuitions 出错、阻碍你实现 long term goals 的一个 instance。

这里很大一部分影响，来自你把 _default_ expectations 设成什么——什么都不做感觉 safe，是因为世界当前的 state 感觉像是 default，而走出 comfort zone 的念头让人觉得_可怕_。让生活变得更好，感觉并没有那么令人兴奋；而让生活变得更差，却让人觉得_可怕_。仅仅意识到这一点就可能有所帮助，因为我认为，你可以在一定程度上 consciously control 自己把 [Zero Point](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rMfpnorsMoRwyn4iP/choosing-the-zero-point) 设在哪里。

这里更深一层的原因是，risks 感觉很 _concrete_，而 benefits 则不然。所以，很好的第一步就是让 benefits 变得 concrete！试着明确 plan out 一个 best case scenario，以及它为什么会很棒。让 default action 的 costs 感觉 concrete。这种 mindset 与 [status quo bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo_bias) 关系密切——也就是觉得世界当前的 state 享有特殊地位、与众不同。应对 status quo bias 的一个好 solution，是 _viscerally_ imagine 一个 alternate world。想象自己就生活在那个世界中，问问自己是否愿意 switch 到当前这个世界。这也能很好地 transfer 到这里！不要问自己，启动一个雄心勃勃的 coding project 是否值得花时间；想象一个你已经_做出_了某个雄心勃勃的东西的世界，再问问自己，是否愿意用失去那份 achievement，换取一些额外时间。向黑暗中望去，并意识到，你无法_不_作出 choice，你只需要 choose 自己要放弃什么。

让事情变得 concrete 的一个好办法，就是[直接去做，并 gather data](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/the-value-of-experiments)！看看某件事是_真的_可怕，还是仅仅在你的头脑中如此。并提醒自己，无论它是否是一次 failure，你仍然赢了！因为你正在 improving 自己一生中所做的全部 decisions 的 sum total。

在合适的 social environment 中，这往往会容易得多。我 _真的_ 非常喜欢 Comfort Zone Expansion workshops（参见[这里](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1UZYBtOJ3QZ7FTI_4eKjVzBSNUqC_Uba3/view)的 p121），我在上学时参加的 [summer camps](https://espr-camp.org/) 中体验过这些 workshops。在那里，大家花几个小时，列出那些感觉处在自己 comfort zone 边缘、但又希望自己能做得更好的事情。然后就在 safe & supportive environment 中去做，看看会发生什么！通过这些 workshops，我练习过：就自己的 feelings 进行 open, honest and vulnerable conversations；向别人寻求 sincere criticism；以及请别人帮忙。结果，我不仅对这些具体的 actions 感到更加 comfortable，而且也更加 _visceral_ 地体会到：**我的 intuitions 会 systematically hold me back，使我无法做对自己最有益的事**。即使不在 workshop 的情境中，我也认为，仅仅为了看看会发生什么而去做一些让自己不舒服的事情，这种精神也可能非常有价值。这会将 question 从 success 或 failure 的问题，reframes 为一个 _每种_ outcome 都是 success 的问题。因为归根结底，goal 是 gather information。

针对 biases 的另一个 general solution，是认识到它们是你 thinking 中的一种 systematic flaw，并有意识地向相反的方向用力——create 一个 artificial counter-bias！如果你 _确实_ 会 systematically avoid opportunities 和让你害怕的事情，那么采用“每当我对一个 opportunity 感到犹豫不决时，就抓住它”这样的 policy，可能会 systematically improve 你的生活。

如果你能为这个 counter-bias 感到[_兴奋_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)，这往往会更容易！不要把它看成强迫自己去做一件可怕的事，而要将它 reframe 为一件正在改变你的 self-image 的事，使你 systematically 越来越接近[那种 Actually Does Things 的人](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things)。和朋友聊聊这个 opportunity，聊聊它可能有多么 _棒_。尽可能让自己为它热血沸腾！

我发现，给自己设定一个 **quest** 往往很有帮助——一个宏大、open ended 的 goal，推动自己实现 behaviour change。每个学期，我都会试着给自己设定一个 goal，一个自己努力追求的宏大、有雄心的 project 或 change（[很契合这里的精神](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NVGuFdX5guE)），而且我发现，这能极其有效地让我对事情感到兴奋。其中一个非常棒的 quest，就是用一个学期 seeking novelty——为了做新鲜事本身而去做，对遇到的任何 opportunity 都 defaulting to yes。现在回想起来，我觉得那是我在 Cambridge 最快乐的几个学期之一！其中一些亮点是：我去玩了 gliding、pole-dancing、trampolining，做了造型，彻底改变了自己的个人穿衣风格，尝试了 amateur rapping，独自旅行，开始使用 Tinder，还学起了 baking

所以，下次你感觉自己动弹不得时，请留意这种感觉。下次你遇到一个 opportunity，或者有机会 deviate from the default，却又感到一股 compulsive urge，想要坚持 safe option，避免主动迈出一步时，请留意。提醒自己，你的 intuitions 困在 illusion of doing nothing 之中，正在阻碍你实现真正的 goals。试着让 trade-off 变得 concrete，resist status quo bias，reframe 这个 question，看看自己能否为它感到兴奋。提醒自己，你正在错过多少事情。什么都不做，真的还让你感觉是 safe option 吗？

## 原文

_(This post is very much in the spirit of_ [_Become a person who Actually Does Things_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things)_, but I try to focus on fleshing out the underlying errors and next actions better)_

One of more unexpected benefits of interning in the finance industry is that I now have a _much_ crisper intuition for probabilities, expected values and reasoning and decision-making under uncertainty. And this highlighted a strong bias in my mind, that given a risky opportunity it feels much more comfortable to do nothing. When faced with a risk, I always want to stick with the safe option. To say no to new opportunities. To not put myself out there.

And I think this is _super, super_ dumb. The world is _full_ of opportunities that can make my life much better! Strangers who could become close friends or partners. Unknown jobs that I could really love. New hobbies and passions in which I could thrive. Awesome talks I could give. New foods I might love.

Yet, when faced with uncertainty, it’s _hard_ to take the step of saying yes and being bold. It feels scary, and my mind fixates on all of the costs, the risks, the effort required, the downsides. And it neglects all the amazing benefits I could be missing out on. And it _is_ true that often downsides are more important than upsides - I would prefer to avoid losing £1000 than to gaining £1000. But this is a _much_ more pronounced effect than that. And this significantly holds me back from achieving my goals, because it means I systematically miss out on ways things could be better. I call this the **illusion of doing nothing** - it _feels_ safe to do nothing, but this is ultimately [all in my head, while my goals live in the world](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-12-the-map-and-the-territory).

In the jargon of economics, I’m ignoring [**opportunity costs**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_cost). Whenever I spend money on something, the question is not “do I want this thing?” it’s “is this thing better than the _alternatives_ for how I could spend this money”. And this is extremely easy to ignore, because the opportunity costs are invisible, not visceral. But they’re still _there_. And I’ve found opportunity costs are a fundamentally important way to understand the world and how to make decisions, because they’re _everywhere_. When I do nothing, I’m ignoring all the opportunity costs of what I _could_ be doing, because they don’t feel visceral.

For example, let’s say I meet somebody interesting, and we could one day be close friends, but I feel socially anxious and lack the courage and initiative to keep in touch with them. This feels safe, and this feels comfortable. But I’m trading that temporary feeling of safety, for the _major_ loss of a close friendship. And this doesn’t feel _visceral_, because it’s a potential, vague future thing. But the feeling of being visceral lives in my head, and doesn’t affect my long-term happiness. It may feel OK to pass up an opportunity like that, but ultimately I have _lost_. And this feeling of safety is just the illusion of doing nothing. A thought experiment: picture a close friend now, and ask yourself how much effort and social anxiety that friendship would have been worth to you. And ask yourself how much it would be worth for, say, a 10% chance of getting another similarly close friendship? And how much do you _actually_ put in when you meet somebody interesting? Are your actions in accordance with your values, or are you just doing what feels safest?

This is a _really_ _common_ error. This underlies a lot of the significant problems with my intuitions. I’m a pretty loss averse and risk averse person, because it feels _safe_ to stick with the status quo, to do nothing, and it feels _scary_ to put myself out there. And this partly comes from a sense of self-justification and avoiding blame. It feels _justifiable_ to do nothing, like it isn’t my fault. While if I put myself out there and things go wrong, that feels like _failure_, and I know I’ll feel regret. But these emotions of guilt, blame, justification, are all just illusions. What I care about is the _results_, the things that achieve my long-term goals.

Another way this manifests is in a sense of the _default_ action. It feels safe to do whatever the default, socially conventional thing to do is, and it feels _scary_ to deviate from this - this is just another example of the illusion of doing nothing. I’m a massive perfectionist, and that’s in part because it feels like the _default_ to obsess over my work, and it feels scary not to, to just put something out there. Cambridge is full of students who spend all their time working, and pushing themselves. And sometimes this _is_ the right decision! But in my experience, it’s most often because working feels like the _default_, their role, their obligation. And it feels safe to do this. And it feels _scary_ and _irresponsible_ to do something different, like go out and join a society, or take up new hobbies. The cost of having less time to work feels _visceral_. While the opportunity costs of all the exciting new things they’re missing out on don’t.

I think this is a highly common problem, but everybody has different values, and neuroses, and opportunities. So at this point, I highly recommend setting a 5 minute timer and trying to generate your own examples. For me (I’ve made a lot of progress on this problem, so these are mostly problems of “Neel of 3 years ago”):

-   I default to saying no to things. Like interesting project ideas, invitations to events, new people to meet. The costs, and possibility I won’t enjoy it feels _visceral_, while the uncertain benefits do not

-   Not initiating things socially or romantically: it feels _scary_ to initiate a friendship with somebody I’ve just met, or to organise events, or to ask somebody out. There’s a really _visceral_ chance of bothering them, or causing emotional labour if they aren’t interested. And the major future upside doesn’t feel visceral

-   Not [Actually Doing Things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things)

-   Not putting myself out there: saying no to things that feel a bit scary or outside my comfort zone. Like giving a talk, throwing a party, starting projects.

-   Not investing money, and wanting things that are perfectly 0 risk, even at much worse rates (I’m still bad at this one)

-   Taking a job, or course that violates my self-image - it feels like the _default_ to be a pure mathematician, who lives in a world of pure abstraction. And it feels _weird_ to do something outside of that, it’s not the default.

At this point, a natural objection is that there _is_ cost to risks. That losing something is painful. That life is full of opportunities, and it takes mental energy and effort to evaluate and prioritise them. That taking _every_ risky opportunity probably loses things on average. And I think these are all good and important points! You should _not_ just do _everything_ risky. You shouldn’t say yes to _everything_. There is a definite skill to evaluating things, and this takes time, experience and practice.

But I think that ultimately the error happening is that people don’t want to _choose_ how to approach opportunities. Having to come up with a policy, and think explicitly about these questions is hard, and tiring. But ultimately, you _always_ have a policy. And maybe your policy is “given an opportunity, perfectly follow my intuitions and the default, safe action”. And sometimes this works well, sometimes it works badly. And changing your policy will involve adding a term for “expending time and effort in _thinking_ about things”, and maybe that would be bad enough to make it worse. But you don’t get to choose not to have a policy at all, _that_ is the illusion of doing nothing. There is a trade-off here, and if you do not think about it, you’re letting your choice be taken from you, to save the mental effort of not needing to think about it. **There is no truly safe option, just differing shades of grey.**

Hopefully I’ve now convinced you that this is an important and significant bias, is present in your thoughts, and holding you back from your goals. But this is insufficient to _solve_ the bias. It’s easy to fall into the trap of thinking that understanding the bias is the entire battle, and that you can just [Try Harder](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button) next time, and avoid it by force of will. This basically never works. So for the rest of the article, I’m going to outline how I’ve made overcoming this _actionable_.

One reason it’s hard to correct this error by trying harder is that it’s easy to forget in the moment - there’s lots of other things in your mind. So, it can be useful to explicitly block out some time to prioritise and think over things, if you ever have a big decision to make, and remind yourself _then_ of these biases. If your life is full of these decisions, then have regular reflection time, like a weekly review, where you ask yourself “what decision am I currently dragging my feet about?”. This can feel like a waste of time, but taking time to genuinely consider things is _extremely_ valuable - spending 5% of your time to make the rest of your time 10% more effective is an _awesome_ trade. This is an instance of your intuitions being wrong, and holding you back from your long term goals.

A lot of the work here is being done by what you set your _default_ expectations to be - doing nothing feels safe, because the current state of the world feels like the default, and the idea of going out of your comfort zone is _scary_. It doesn’t feel that exciting to make your life better, while it feels _scary_ to make your life worse. Just realising this can be helpful, because I think you can have some conscious control over where you set the [Zero Point](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rMfpnorsMoRwyn4iP/choosing-the-zero-point).

A further underlying thing here is that risks feel _concrete_, while benefits do not. So a good first step is to make the benefits concrete! Try to explicitly plan out a best case scenario, and why it would be awesome. Make the costs of the default action feel concrete. The mindset is quite related to [status quo bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo_bias) - when the current state of the world feels privileged and special. A good solution to status quo bias is to _viscerally_ imagine an alternate world. And imagine you lived in that world, and ask whether you’d switch to the current one. And this can transfer here quite well! Don’t ask yourself whether it’s worth your time to start an ambitious coding project, imagine a world where you’ve _made_ something ambitious, and ask whether you’d trade the loss of that achievement for some extra time. Look into the dark, and realise that you can’t _not_ make a choice, you just need to choose what you’re giving up on.

A good way to make things concrete, is to [just do them and gather data](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/the-value-of-experiments)! See if something is _actually_ scary, or just all in your head. And remind yourself that whether it’s a failure or not, you still win! Because you’re improving the sum total of all decisions you make over the course of your life.

This is often much easier in the right social environment. I’m a _really_ big fan of Comfort Zone Expansion workshops (see p121 [here](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1UZYBtOJ3QZ7FTI_4eKjVzBSNUqC_Uba3/view)), which I’ve done at [summer camps](https://espr-camp.org/) I attended while in school. Where you take a few hours for everyone to list things that feel on the edge of their comfort zone, but which they want to get better at. And then just doing them, in a safe & supportive environment, and seeing what happens! As a result of these workshops, I’ve practiced: having open, honest and vulnerable conversations about my feelings; seeking sincere criticism from people; and asking people for favours. And as I result, I both feel more comfortable with these specific actions, and it’s become far more _visceral_ to me that **my intuitions systematically hold me back from doing what’s best for me**. Even outside of a workshop context, and I think spirit of doing something uncomfortable just to see what happens can be super valuable. This reframes the question from one of success or failure, to one where _every_ outcome is a success. Because ultimately the goal is to gather information.

Another general solution to biases is to recognise them as a systematic flaw in your thinking, and to intentionally push the other way - create an artificial counter-bias! If you _do_ systematically avoid opportunities and scary things, then having a policy of “every time I feel borderline about an opportunity, take it” could systematically improve your life.

This is often easier if you can get [_excited_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about) about this counter-bias! Don’t see it as forcing yourself to do a scary thing, reframe it as something that is changing your self-image so that you’re systematically getting closer to [the kind of person who Actually Does Things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things). Talk to a friend about the opportunity, and talk about all the ways it could be _awesome_. Try to get yourself as pumped as possible about it!

I find it often helps to set myself a **quest** - a big, open ended goal to push myself towards a behaviour change. Every term I try to have a goal for myself, some big, ambitious project or change I’m striving for ([much in the spirit of this](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NVGuFdX5guE)), and I’ve found this can work extremely well for being excited about things. And an excellent quest was to spend a term seeking novelty - doing new things for the sake of doing them, defaulting to yes for any opportunity presented. And looking back, I think that was one of my most fun terms at Cambridge! A few highlights: I went gliding, pole-dancing, trampolining, got a makeover, overhauled my personal sense of style, tried amateur rapping, went travelling solo, got Tinder, took up baking

So, notice next time you feel paralysed. The next time you have an opportunity, or a chance to deviate from the default, and feel a compulsive urge to stick with the safe option and avoid putting yourself out there. And remind yourself that your intuitions are stuck within the illusion of doing nothing, and holding you back from your true goals. Try to make the trade-off concrete, resist status quo bias, reframe the question, and see if you can be excited about it. Remind yourself of all of the things you’re missing out on. Does doing nothing still truly feel like the safe option?
