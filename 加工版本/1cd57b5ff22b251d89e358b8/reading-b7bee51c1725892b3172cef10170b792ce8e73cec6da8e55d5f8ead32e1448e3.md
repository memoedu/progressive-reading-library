# Nintil - Humeans in the net

---

## 正文

Hume 有句名言：reason 是、也应当是 passions 的奴隶。Humeans 是 [moral non-cognitivists](http://www.iep.utm.edu/non-cogn/)（具体而言，是 emotivists），并且认为 moral judgements 归根结底只是 sentiments。“Inequality 是 bad”或“Agression 是 bad”并不意味着什么，而只是 express attitudes（呸，inequality！呸，violence！）。

我在网上阅读的作者中，有一些 Humeans。他们如下：

第一位 Humean 是 Chris Dillow，他写道

> 那些引发 taboos 的 emotions 能否接受 reason 的 tribunal 审判，同样并不显然。例如，David [Hume](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotions-17th18th/LD8Hume.html#ReaOugOnlSlaPas) 就认为[不能](http://ebooks.adelaide.edu.au/h/hume/david/h92t/B2.3.3.html)：
> 
> > Reason 是、也只应当是 passions 的奴隶，除了 serve 和 obey 它们之外，绝不能自称承担任何其他职责……当一种 passion 既不是 founded on false suppositions，也没有 chuses means insufficient for the end 时，understanding 既不能 justify，也不能 condemn 它。
> 
> 我承认，对我而言确实如此；**我对 inequality 的 antipathy，从根本上说是 emotional 的，而我那些 apparently rational arguments 是那种 passion 的奴隶**。我怀疑——尽管无法 prove——Dawkins 也是如此。阅读 _The God Delusion_ 给我的印象是，Dawkins 受到一种 emotion 的驱动，即对 religion 的某些 effects 感到 disgust。我碰巧在许多方面也有这种 feeling，但它终究是一种 feeling。（[Rationalism 的限度](http://stumblingandmumbling.typepad.com/stumbling_and_mumbling/2014/08/limits-of-rationalism-.html)，2014）

> 在那两篇文章中，我已经为 left-libertarianism 给出了十几条充分的 reasons。但 reason 只是 passions 的奴隶。以下才是我的 left libertarianism 的真正 causes。1. 我对 authority 有抵触。我最早的记忆之一，是父亲被捕；更早的记忆则是他的暴躁脾气。对我而言，power 和 authority 是拳头，而不是援手。2. 我曾在 financial markets 工作。因此，我认识到 Hayek 是对的。平均而言，Individuals 不可能比 market 知道得更多。3. 我是个独来独往的人。这有两个 effects。首先，相比 community，它让我更 preference 于 liberty——别来打扰我。其次，这意味着我的 political beliefs 并没有受到别人的 opinion 的太多 shaped。相反，它们更多来自 literature。而好的 literature 比 popular opinion 更 liberal，也更少 managerialist。4. 我从未真正融入 the rich。无论是在 grammar school、Oxford、the City，还是仅仅在 London 的富人区，我从未真正感到自在。upper-middle class 的 interests 和 attitudes 从来都不是我的 interests 或 attitudes。5. 我是 risk averse 的，而且生活口味并不昂贵。因此，我的 instinct 是，state 应当 provide a safety net，而不必太担心让 the rich 保留他们的数百万财富。\*\*因此，Rationality 并不是我的 political beliefs 的 cause。我也怀疑它并不是任何其他人的 political beliefs 的 cause。  \*\*（[我为什么是 left libertarian](http://stumblingandmumbling.typepad.com/stumbling_and_mumbling/2006/09/why_im_a_left_l.html)，2006）

第二位 Humean 是 Noah Smith，

> 当然，正如我在这番牢骚开头所说，这其实并不是我喜欢思考 ethics 的方式。在 right 和 wrong 的问题上，我是 Aristotelian，而不是 Platonist；我更多地把 ethical systems 看作对 fundamentally jumbled empirical reality 进行 order 和 classify 的尝试，而不是现实中的人们的 morals 从中 emerge 的 pure ideas。**而在我看来，[is-ought rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is%E2%80%93ought_problem) 意味着，internally self-consistent ethical systems 并不比 self-contradictory 的 ethical systems 更 intrinsically better**，因此，试图将人们的 beliefs 统统圈进一张 periodic table 的整个做法，几乎没有 practical benefit。我自己的 ethical system 有时是 libertarian，有时是 utilitarian，有时两者都是，有时两者都不是；而且\_我对此安之若素\_。  （[所有 ethical systems 都既是 deontological，又是 consequentialist](http://noahpinionblog.blogspot.co.uk/2011/02/all-ethical-systems-are-both.html)，2011）

> 现在，你可能会问：“Noah，你为什么认为应当有这样的活动？”嗯，我可以直接回答说，**这纯粹是我的 moral intuition，而且作为一个 [Humean](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume-moral/)，我不需要任何其他 justification。事实上，我给出的任何 justification 都会招致‘可为什么呢？’这样的追问，直到最后我只能说‘因为我就是这么觉得的’**，或者‘哎，得了吧！’。$$...$$ 不过，我并不认为这个 norm 是 universal 和 overriding 的。**我是 Humean，不是 deontologist——我无须 establish 涵盖所有 situations 的 \_a priori\_moral axioms**。我能想到一些自己会 violate 这个 norm 的 extreme situations。如果 Nazis 让你制造 nuke，那就尽管去破坏那个项目吧！（[Science 与 politics](http://noahpinionblog.blogspot.co.uk/2015/08/science-vs-politics.html)，2015）

> 这里其实有一个在智识上略有趣味的 philosophical question，那就是：“是否存在一个由 ethical principles 构成的 finite set，能够 yield 一个由 capitalism 的 rules 构成的 set，而后者的 cardinality is larger than 那个由 ethical principles 构成的 set 的 cardinality？”我怀疑存在，但我也不在乎，因为**我是 Humean，我 reject 所有 clearly delineated ethical rules，转而接受 fuzzily defined、intuitive principles。**（[Arbitrary value systems 是 arbitrary 的](http://noahpinionblog.blogspot.co.uk/2014/09/arbitrary-value-systems-are-arbitrary.html)，2014）

> \*\*这篇文章真让我想吐。它极其 morally repugnant，令人恶心至极。\*\* 它理应受到 strong condemnation，或许还该被扔一个装满非洲鬣狗恶臭排泄物的水气球。这是我在 econ blogosphere 读过的\_最\_令人作呕的东西，没有之一。
> 
> 但作为 economists，我们理应是 dispassionate 的。我们理应 overcome 自己的 biases，置身于 pure reason 的领域。因此，我将 rebut Landsburg 的文章，依据的不是\*\*它严重的 moral failings\*\*，而是它的 logical shortcomings，而这样的 shortcomings 有很多。因为这篇文章不仅 offensive，而且思考得很不周全。（\[Steve Landsburg 让我把午饭都吐了出来\](http://noahpinionblog.blogspot.com/2012/03/in-which-steve-landsburg-makes-me-lose.html)，2012）\[注意这里：Explicit emotion based moral judgement，与 Hume 一致\]

> 我认为自己是个 pragmatist。我自己并没有一套非常 self-consistent、well-defined 的 ideology。我实在没法——\*\*我认为自己是个 Humean \[David Hume\]。我有这些 \_moral instincts\_\*\*——我会想，‘哦，嗯，你知道，这对 individual liberty 的 restriction 太过了’，以及‘我不喜欢\_那样\_。’然后在另一些情况下，我又会想，‘哦，看看这些 poor people’，你知道，‘我们得让 government 做点什么来帮助那些 poor people。’\*\*而这未必有什么道理可循。只是有时我有一种 instinct，有时我有另一种。而且，我把那些 moral instincts 看作 morality 的某种 primitive、primal、basic elements，它们并不总是起作用——它们并不总能 form 一个 self-consistent whole。\*\* 现在，你知道，当我看什么对 nations 真正 works、什么在我看来对 nations 不错时，你知道，我觉得世界上的 rich countries 都过得相当不错：United States 是个很适合生活的地方；Canada、Japan、France、Germany、Singapore——我对 Singapore 确实没有那么了解。但这些都是\_确实\_很适合生活的地方。我见过的每一个地方都很好。当然，也存在一些细小的 taste differences。我的意思是，在 France 生活不会完全像在 Japan 生活。但它们都过得相当不错。而且，你知道，我是说，\_也许\_来一次 radical overhaul，让我们 \_dramatically\_ decrease the size of government，会更好。也许某个国家会试一试。你知道，我支持 experimentation；也许会有人试一试。但到目前为止，我们看到的所有拥有这些出色 qualities of life 的国家，这些非常 successful 的国家，\_里面\_都有 big government。而在我看来，这说明，在我们彻底抛弃 big government 之前，至少应当相当谨慎。（\[EconTalk（经济学访谈）\](http://www.econtalk.org/archives/2015/12/noah\_smith\_on\_w.html)，2015）

## 原文

Hume famously said that reason is and ought to be the slave of the passions. Humeans are [moral non-cognitivists](http://www.iep.utm.edu/non-cogn/) (emotivists, in particular), and believe moral judgements are, ultimately, just sentiments. "Inequality is bad" or "Agression is bad" wouldn't mean anything, but would just express attitudes (Boo inequality!, Ugh violence!).

There are some Humeans in the net among people I read. Here they are:

Our first Humean is Chris Dillow, who writes

> Nor is it obvious that the emotions which give rise to taboos can be subjected to the tribunal of reason. David [Hume](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/emotions-17th18th/LD8Hume.html#ReaOugOnlSlaPas), for one, thought [not](http://ebooks.adelaide.edu.au/h/hume/david/h92t/B2.3.3.html):
> 
> > Reason is, and ought only to be the slave of the passions, and can never pretend to any other office than to serve and obey them... Where a passion is neither founded on false suppositions, nor chuses means insufficient for the end, the understanding can neither justify nor condemn it.
> 
> I'll confess that this is true for me; **my antipathy to inequality is, at root, an emotional one and my apparently rational arguments are the slave of that passion**. I suspect - though cannot prove - that the same is true of Dawkins. Reading _The God Delusion_ gives me the impression that Dawkins is motivated by an emotion of disgust at some of the effects of religion. I happen to share that feeling in many ways, but it is a feeling. ([Limits of Rationalism](http://stumblingandmumbling.typepad.com/stumbling_and_mumbling/2014/08/limits-of-rationalism-.html), 2014)

> Between those two posts, I've given a dozen good reasons for left-libertarianism. But reason is only the slave of the passions. Here are the real causes of my left libertarianism. 1. I've got a problem with authority. One of my earliest memories is of my dad being arrested; my earlier memories are of his violent temper. For me, power and authority are fists, not helping hands. 2. I've worked in financial markets. I've therefore learned that Hayek was right. Individuals can't know more than the market, on average. 3. I'm a loner. This has two effects. First, it gives me a preference for liberty -  leave me alone - over community. Second, it means my political beliefs have not been shaped much by the opinion of other people. Instead, they come more from the literature. And the good literature is more liberal and less managerialist than popular opinion. 4. I've never properly fitted in with the rich. Be it grammar school, Oxford, the City or just a posh part of London, I've never felt truly at home. The interests and attitudes of the upper-middle class have never been my interests or attitudes. 5. I'm risk averse, with cheap tastes. My instinct, then, is that the state should provide a safety net, and not worry too much about letting the rich keep their millions. \*\*Rationality, then, isn't the cause of my political beliefs. And I doubt it's the cause of anyone else's.  \*\*([Why I'm a left libertarian](http://stumblingandmumbling.typepad.com/stumbling_and_mumbling/2006/09/why_im_a_left_l.html), 2006)

Our second Humean is Noah Smith,

> Of course, as I said at the beginning of this rant, this is not really how I like to think about ethics. I'm an Aristotelian rather than a Platonist when it comes to right and wrong; I see ethical systems more as attempts to order and classify a fundamentally jumbled empirical reality, rather than pure ideas from which real people's morals emerge. **And as I see it, the [is-ought rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is%E2%80%93ought_problem) means that internally self-consistent ethical systems are no intrinsically better than self-contradictory ones**, so the whole exercise of trying to corral people's beliefs into a periodic table yields little practical benefit. My own ethical system is sometimes libertarian, sometimes utilitarian, sometimes both, and sometimes neither; and\_I'm fine with that\_.  ([All ethical systems are both deontological and consequentialist](http://noahpinionblog.blogspot.co.uk/2011/02/all-ethical-systems-are-both.html), 2011)

> Now you might ask: "Noah, why do you think there ought to be such an activity?" Well, I could just reply that **it's purely my moral intuition, and as a [Humean](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hume-moral/), I don't need any other justification. In fact, any justification I give will open itself up to questions of "But why?", until I finally just say "Because that's just how I feel"**, or "Oh come ON!". \[...\] Now, I don't think this norm is universal and overriding.**I'm a Humean, not a deontologist - I have no need to establish \_a priori\_moral axioms that encompass all situations**. I can think of extreme situations where I'd violate this norm. If the Nazis tell you to build a nuke, go ahead and sabotage that project! ( [Science vs. politics](http://noahpinionblog.blogspot.co.uk/2015/08/science-vs-politics.html), 2015)

> There is actually a mildly intellectually interesting philosophical question here, which is: "Is there a finite set of ethical principles that will yield a set of rules of capitalism whose cardinality is larger than the cardinality of the set of ethical principles?" I suspect there is, but I also don't care, because **I am a Humean, and I reject all clearly delineated ethical rules in favor of fuzzily defined, intuitive principles.** ([Arbitrary value systems are arbitrary](http://noahpinionblog.blogspot.co.uk/2014/09/arbitrary-value-systems-are-arbitrary.html), 2014)

> \*\*This post really made me want to vomit. It is morally repugnant and disgusting in the extreme.\*\* It deserves strong condemnation, and possibly a hurled water balloon filled with the unsavory excretions of an African hyena. It is \_the\_ most sickening thing I have read in the econ blogosphere, bar none.
> 
> But as economists, we are supposed to be dispassionate. We are supposed to overcome our biases and dwell in a realm of pure reason. And so I will rebut Landsburg's post not based \*\*on its severe moral failings\*\*, but on its logical shortcomings, of which there are many. For the post is not merely offensive, it is poorly thought out. (\[In which Steve Landsburg makes me lose my lunch\](http://noahpinionblog.blogspot.com/2012/03/in-which-steve-landsburg-makes-me-lose.html), 2012) \[Note here, Explicit emotion based moral judgement, in line with Hume\]

> I think of myself as a pragmatist. I don't have a very self-consistent, well-defined ideology to myself. I can't really--\*\*I think of myself as a Humean \[David Hume\]. I have these \_moral instincts\_\*\*--I think, 'Oh, well, you know, this is too much a restriction on individual liberty' and 'I don't like \_that\_.' And then in some other case I'll think, 'Oh, look at these poor people,' you know, 'We've got to have the government do something to help those poor people.' \*\*And there's not necessarily a rhyme or reason to that. It's just sometimes I have one instinct and sometimes I have the other. And I think of those moral instincts as sort of the primitive, primal, basic elements of morality, and they don't always work--they don't always form a self-consistent whole.\*\* Now, you know, when I look at what really works for nations, what I think looks good for nations, you know I think the rich countries of the world are all doing pretty well: the United States is a great place to live; Canada, Japan, France, Germany, Singapore--I don't really know as much about Singapore. But these are all \_really\_ good places to live. Every place I have seen is good. And there are some small taste differences. I mean, life in France is not going to be quite like life in Japan. But they are all doing pretty dang good. And, you know, I mean, \_maybe\_ a radical overhaul where we \_dramatically\_ decrease the size of government would be even better. And maybe some country will try that. You know, I'm for experimentation; maybe someone will try that. But so far all the countries we see with these great qualities of life, these very successful countries, all have big government \_in\_ them. And to me, that says that we should at least be pretty cautious before we toss out big government completely. (\[EconTalk\](http://www.econtalk.org/archives/2015/12/noah\_smith\_on\_w.html), 2015)
