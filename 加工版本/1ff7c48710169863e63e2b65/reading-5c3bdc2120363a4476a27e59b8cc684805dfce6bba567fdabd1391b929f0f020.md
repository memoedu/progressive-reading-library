# The surprising Human Development Index of Cuba

---

## 正文

\[[本文改编并译自西班牙语](https://artir.wordpress.com/2014/12/20/el-sorprendente-indice-de-desarrollo-humano-de-cuba/)\]

~Cuba 的 HDI 为 0.815，在整个 Latin America 中 second highest，仅次于 Chile~。实际上是 0.759，在 Latin America 中 ninth highest。考虑到这个岛的 sociopolitical regime，我们或许会疑惑这为什么可能。castrism 是否 work？

首先，第一段之所以有一处 correction，是因为经常被引用的 0.815 这一 [HDI](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2009/05/against_the_hum.html) 将一个[是 wrong 的](https://katalepsisblog.wordpress.com/2016/03/31/la-paradoja-del-pib-de-cuba/) GDP figure 考虑在内。所以，首先这就是 HDI 比看起来 lower 的一个原因。你也可以阅读 [Ribeiro, Stein, and Kang (2013)](http://economics.ca/2013/papers/SG0030-1.pdf)，他们认为，Cuban GDP growth 被 socialism diminished，而并非在同等程度上被 American blockade diminished；他们还认为，counterfactually，假如 revolution 没有发生，Cuba 本会 grown more。

我们将探讨的第二个原因是，Cuba 在 revolution 之前，一开始就有 high HDI。那么，我们就必须将此作为 comparisons 的 baseline。

那么，Fidel 之前的 Cuba 是什么样的？Castrism 在 1959 年 begins。那时，Cuba 已经是一个相当 advanced economy。在 [Smith and Llorens (1998)](http://www.ascecuba.org/c/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/v08-30smith.pdf) 中，我们可以找到一些关于 precastrist Cuba 的相关 data。Cuba 的 infant mortality rate 为每 thousand births 中 32，是 Latin America 中 lowest，也是世界上 thirteenth lowest，甚至 surpassing France、Japan、Spain、Portugal 或 Italy。不过，到 1990 年，这些国家在这一 metric 上全都 surpassed Cuba。文章指出，考虑到 high abortion rate（1991 年每 birth 有 0.71 abortions），Cuban 的 infant mortality data 很可能是 biased。如果 abortion 在 high risk births 中更 common，那么本会在后来 died 的 children 的 number 就会 reduced，从而 improving 这一 statistic measure。其他国家的 abortion rate 大约是这一数值的一半。

关于 number of doctors and dentists per capita，Cuba 当时在 Latin America 中就已经是 rates 位居 third highest 的国家，在这方面也 better 于 United Kingdom 或 Finland。

关于 literacy rate，Cuba 当时在整个 Latin America 中为 fourth highest。到 1995 年，其位居 second，与 Argentina tied。

关于 nutrition，Cuba 当时是 Latin America 中 caloric intake 位居 third highest 的国家（data 来自 1948 年，其他国家的则来自 1954-57 年，因此 Cuban data 在 comparison 中本会更 higher）。现在，它在所 surveyed 的 eleven 个国家的名单中位列最后。

关于 transportation，Cuba 是 sample 中唯一一个 number of cars per inhabitant falls 的国家，从每 thousand 人 24 辆 falls 到 1988 年的每 thousand 人 23 辆。我们可以对 energy、agriculture 及其他 sectors repeat 这一 analysis，并确信 Cuba 在 Castro 之前确实是一个相当 advanced country。最后，引用文章中的一段话：

> 这篇 paper 的上一版本发表（State 1998）后不久，Washington Times 引用了 Cuban Interests Section 对 study 中 statistical presentation 的异议（Carter 1998）。它并未质疑 statistics 本身，其中大部分是由 Cuban government 自己向前面讨论过的 multilateral institutions reported 的。相反，一位发言人声称，这些 statistics 并未考虑 revolution 在 distribution of wealth 方面的 social achievements。例如，他说：“在 revolution 之前，rich 有更多 food，而 poor 有更少 food。”这一 statement 当然无可争议，但它并没有回答：一个几乎所有人都是 poor 的 society，为什么会 preferable 于一个有些人是 poor、另一些人是 middle class、还有一些人是 rich 的 society。

请注意，Cuban government 自己确实承认了这些 statistics 是 true 的。另一篇文章 [Ward and Devereux (2012)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/41353825) 也 analyses 了 post and pre-revolutionary Cuba，并 conclude：

> 所有迹象都表明，Republican Cuba 曾经是一个 prosperous middle-income economy。在 revolution 前夕，我们发现 Cuban incomes 为 European levels 的 fifty to sixty percent。它们在 Latin America 中属于 highest，约为 US 的 thirty percent。$$…$$ 与之形成鲜明对照的是，现有最好的 information 表明，在 revolutionary regime 之下，income 已经 declined，而且可能 significantly below 其在 1950’s 的 levels。$$…$$ 哪里出了问题？回过头来看，central planning 最终结果糟糕这一事实不应令人意外。在过去 fifty years 里，Cuba 尽管采用了一种独具 Cuban 特色的方式，却 replicated 了其他地方 command systems 的 failings。$$…$$ overall Cuban consumption 是 Costa Rica 的 forty seven percent。

作为一个小小的 comparison，我们可以说，在 1955 年，Cuba 的 GDP 是 USA 的 27%，Chile 为 23%，Haiti 为 3%，Costa Rica 为 16%，Dominican Republic 为 9%。Cuba 的 Life expectancy 为 64 years，Chile 为 53，Haiti 为 39，Costa Rica 为 59，Dominican Republic 为 48。如今，Cuba 的 life expectancy 为 79.4 years，Chile 为 79.5，Haiti 为 63，Costa Rica 为 78.9，Dominican Republic 为 72.5。这意味着 Cuba 的 increases 为 24%，Chile 为 50%，Haiti 为 61%，Costa Rica 为 35%，Dominican Republic 为 53%。我们可以将 sample 中所有国家的 data 进行 plot，得到的结果是：starting life expectancy 越 higher，increase 就越 lower。red square 是 Cuba。

![古巴2](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2014/12/20-1.jpg) X axis 是 life expectancy。Y axis 是 1955-2015 年 life expectancy 的 % increase

我们也可以 perform 同样的 regression，但只针对 plot 中接近 Cuba 的国家（它们在 1955 年的 life expectancy 与 Cuba 相差 +/- 5 years），

![古巴](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2014/12/20-2.jpg) X axis 是 life expectancy。Y axis 是 1955-2015 年 life expectancy 的 % increase

red square 仍然是 Cuba，而 green dots 是 Hong Kong、Singapore 和 Japan（就在 Cuba 上方）。light blue dot 是 Argentina。

这样来看，Cuban healthcare system 的表现 underperformed 了其他国家。当然，声称在这里发挥作用的只有 economic system 的 type，未免过于天真，因此我们不能立即跳到 Castrism 已经 costed Cuba 5 years 的 life expectancy 这一 conclusion。但我们可以说，Cuban 在 life expectancy 方面的 success 是由其 starting position 所 explained 的。如果其 economic system 所特有的某些东西，在 improving healthcare 方面确实 better，我们就会预期 Cuba 在上面的 charts 中 outperform。也有可能 castrism 确实 good for your health，只是某个 negative factor 对 life expectancy 起了拖累作用。我——这并不令人意外——确实支持这样的观点：castrism 对 life expectancy 要么是 neutral，要么是 bad。请记住，这里是按 data 的表面含义来理解的。

Red square 再次是 Cuba。Green dots 是 Bolivia 和 Singapore，这两个国家以 meaningful margin outperformed 了其余国家。

再次聚焦 Cuba 及其相邻国家，

![识字率图2](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2014/12/20-4.jpg) X axis 是 1955 年的 Literacy rate。Y axis 是 1955-2015 年的 Growth rate

这里，Cuba 的 progressed 本会超过 average，但幅度不大。如果 Cuba 的表现如同一个 average country，其 literacy rate 就会是 97.5%，而不是官方的 99.8%。

附加问题：Equality

Cuban Gini index 在 1953 年为 0.55。现在（2007）为 0.407（[Mesa-Lago, 2009](http://www.mesa-lago.com/uploads/2/7/3/1/27312653/balance_economico_social.pdf)）。它已经 improved，但如果我们将它与例如 US 的 Gini index（0.411）进行 compare，结果却是，Cuba 的 inequality 比 US 更多。作者们说，他们对 Cuban Gini 的 estimate 可能是 downwards biased

> 这些 estimates include foreign remittances，这是 two thirds 的 population 的重要 source of income，但 one third 并不 receive 它们。如果 included，Gini 就会 far higher。

不过，根据 World Bank，Cuba 仍然是 Latin America 中 second most egalitarian 的国家，仅次于 Trinidad y Tobago。他们并没有 Cuba 本身的 values，也没有所有国家近期的 values，因此其中一些 values 来自 1995 年，不要过于当真。

### Conclusion（结论）

根据这些 data，Cuba 相对于 similar countries 并没有 improved——literacy rate 除外——而且在某些 metrics 上已经 worsened。Cuba 总体上处于与 1955 年相同的 level，或者从 absolute terms 来看更好，但有理由认为，如果没有 socialist revolution，它的处境还会更好。在 starting conditions 的基础上 Improving 并不是一件特别困难的事；更值得关注的是，在一个 society 面临的 technological 和 material constraints 下，尽可能做得更好。

因此，我们可以 conclude，Cuba 的 high HDI，首先，由于其较低的 GDP per capita，实际上比宣传的更低；其次，它主要是由其 initial conditions 所 explained 的。Castro 的 rule 对 literacy 和 life expectancy 的 secular increase 没有产生 effect，但确实对 GDP growth 产生了 negative effect。

\[[Post adapted and translated from Spanish](https://artir.wordpress.com/2014/12/20/el-sorprendente-indice-de-desarrollo-humano-de-cuba/)\]

~Cuba has an HDI of 0.815, the second highest in all Latin America, just behind Chile~. Actually, 0.759, the ninth highest in Latin America. We could wonder why this is possible, given the island's sociopolitical regime. Does castrism work?

First, the reason there is a correction in the first paragraph is that the often quoted [HDI](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2009/05/against_the_hum.html) of 0.815 takes into account a GDP figure [that is wrong](https://katalepsisblog.wordpress.com/2016/03/31/la-paradoja-del-pib-de-cuba/). So there's one reason why the HDI is lower than it seems, to begin with. You can also read [Ribeiro, Stein, and Kang (2013)](http://economics.ca/2013/papers/SG0030-1.pdf), who argue that Cuban GDP growth was diminished by socialism, and not so much by the American blockade, and furthermore that Cuba would have, counterfactually, grown more had the revolution not happened.

The second reason we will explore is the fact that Cuba had a high HDI to begin with, before the revolution. We would have to,  then take this as a baseline for our comparisons.

So how was Cuba before Fidel? Castrism begins in 1959. Back then Cuba was already quite an advanced economy. In [Smith and Llorens (1998)](http://www.ascecuba.org/c/wp-content/uploads/2014/09/v08-30smith.pdf) we can find some relevant data about precastrist Cuba. Cuba had a infant mortality rate of 32 per thousand births, the lowest in Latin America, and the thirteenth lowest in the world, surpassing even France, Japan, Spain, Portugal, or Italy. By 1990, though, all of these contries surpassed Cuba in this metric. The article points out that Cuban data for infant mortality is probably biased, given the high abortion rate (0.71 abortions per birth in 1991). If abortion is more common for high risk births, the number of children who would have later died is reduced, improving this statistic measure. Other countries have about half this abortion rate.

Regarding number of doctors and dentists per capita, Cuba was already the third country with the highest rates in Latin America, and better in that sense than United Kingdom or Finland.

Regarding literacy rate, Cuba had the fourth highest in all Latin America. In 1995 they had the second, tied with Argentina.

Regarding nutrition, Cuba was the country with the third highest caloric intake in Latin America (data from 1948, other countries' are for 1954-57, so Cuban data would be even higher in comparison). Right now it is the last out of eleven in the list of countries surveyed.

Regarding transportation, Cuba is the only country in the sample where the number of cars per inhabitant falls, from 24 per thousand to 23 per thousand in 1988. One can repeat this analysis for energy, agriculture, and other sectors, and convince oneself that Cuba was indeed quite an advanced country before Castro. To finish off, a quote from the article,

> Not long after the previous version of this paper was published (State 1998), the Washington Times quoted the Cuban Interests Section’s objections to the statistical presentation found in the study (Carter 1998). It did not dispute the statistics themselves, the majority of which are reported by the Cuban government itself to multilateral institutions discussed earlier. Rather, a spokesman asserted that the statistics did not take into account the social achievements of the revolution in relation to the distribution of wealth. For example, he said, “\[B\]efore the revolution, the rich had more food and the poor had less.” This statement is, of course, indisputable, but it leaves unanswered how a society in which virtually everyone is poor is preferable to one in which some people are poor, others are middle class, and still othersare rich.

Note that the Cuban government itself did acknowledge that these statistics were true. Another article, [Ward and Devereux (2012)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/41353825) also analyses post and pre-revolutionary Cuba and conclude that:

> All indications are that Republican Cuba once was a prosperous middle-income economy. On the eve of the revolution, we find that Cuban incomes were fifty to sixty percent of European levels. They were among the highest in Latin America and were about thirty percent of the US. \[…\] In stark contrast, the best information available suggests that income has declined under the revolutionary regime and may be significantly below its levels of the 1950’s \[…\] What went wrong? With hindsight, the fact that the central planning has ended badly should come as no surprise. Over the last fifty years, Cuba has replicated the failings of command systems elsewhere albeit in a uniquely Cuban fashion. \[…\]  overall Cuban consumption is forty seven percent of Costa Rica.

As a small comparison, we can say that in 1955, Cuba had 27% of USA's GDP, Chile had 23%, Haiti 3%, Costa Rica 16%, and Dominican Republic, 9%. Life expectancy in Cuba was 64 years, Chile 53, Haiti 39, Costa Rica 59, and Dominican Republic 48. Today, Cuba has a life expectancy of 79.4 years, Chile 79.5, Haiti 63, Costa Rica 78.9, and Dominican Republic 72.5. This supposes increases of 24% for Cuba, 50% for Chile, 61% for Haiti, 35% for Costa Rica, and 53% for Dominican Republic. We can plot the data for all of the countries in the sample, and we get that the higher the starting life expectancy, the lower the increase. The red square is Cuba.

![Cuba2](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2014/12/20-1.jpg) X axis is life expectancy. Y axis is % increase in life expectancy 1955-2015

![Cuba](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2014/12/20-2.jpg) X axis is life expectancy. Y axis is % increase in life expectancy 1955-2015

The red square is again Cuba, and the green dots are Hong Kong, Singapore, and Japan (just on top of Cuba). The light blue dot is Argentina.

Seen like this, the Cuban healthcare system as underperformed other countries. Of course, it would be naïve to claim that the only thing at play here is the type of economic system, so we cannot inmediateley jump to the conclusion that Castrism has costed Cuba 5 years in life expectancy. But what we can say is that the Cuban success in terms of life expectancy is explained by its starting position. If something particular to its economic system was really better in improving healthcare, we would expect Cuba to outperform in the charts above. It could be that castrism is indeed good for your health, but that some negative factor has acted as a drag on life expectancy. I - not surprisingly - do support the idea that castrism has either been neutral or bad for life expectancy. This is taking the data at face value, remember.

### Conclusion

In light of the data, Cuba has not improved with respect to similar countries -except literacy rate- and it has worsened in some metrics. Cuba is in general at the same level it was in 1955, or better in absolute terms, but it is plausible to suppose that it would be even better off in the absence of the socialist revolution. Improving over starting conditions is not a particularly difficult thing, what is more interesting is doing as better as possible, given the technological and material constraints that a society faces.

Thus we can conclude that Cuba's high HDI is, first, lower than advertised due to its lower GDP per capita,  and second, that it is mostly explained by its initial conditions. Castro's rule has had no effect on the secular increase of literacy of life expectancy, but it did have a negative effect on GDP growth.
