# 这个 EA Giving Group

---

## 正文

我已经决定了 2016 年要向哪里 donate（以及 donate 多少）！先说结论：我将向 GiveWell donating \$20,000 to a donor-advised fund run by Nick Beckstead, $5,000，用于 discretionary re-granting，并向 GiveDirectly donating $1,000。下面是我作出这个决定的经过。

## 这个 EA Giving Group

我认为，我 do good 的主要方式是在 Wave 工作，而不是 donating，所以我不想花很多时间来 optimizing 我的决定。我也不认为，在 choosing where to give 方面，我比 effective altruism community 中的其他人更擅长。因此，我决定让别人替我选择我的 donations。

幸运的是，今年我了解到 EA Giving Group，这是一个 donor-advised fund，由 Nick Beckstead 和另一个人共同 directed，后者也提供了它的大部分 funding。我私下认识这两位 directors，也非常信任他们的思考过程，因此我很乐意让他们替我决定我的 giving。Nick 在 [GiveWell 关于 staff donations 的文章](http://blog.givewell.org/2016/12/09/staff-members-personal-donations-giving-season-2016/)中属于他的部分，写了一些关于 EA Giving Group 的内容。

在查看[他们之前的 donations](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1H2hF3SaO0_QViYq2j1E7mwoz3sDjdf9pdtuBcrq7pRU/edit#gid=0)时，我注意到一件有意思的事。Nick 和他的 co-director 向许多 organizations 提供了 grants，我现在认为这些 organizations 都在 doing good work——但几乎每一笔 grant 都发生在我仍对相应 organization 持怀疑态度的时候。这是相当清楚的 evidence，说明他们比我更擅长 grant-making！

今年还有其他几个人决定向同一个 fund 进行 give，而且正如 Nick 在 GiveWell 的文章中所提到的，Open Philanthropy Project 现在也在 funding 类似的 organizations，这可能让 Nick 更难找到好的 opportunities。不过，我预计，对他来说，这仍然会比对我来说更容易。

## GiveWell 和 GiveDirectly

尽管我认为 EA Giving Group Fund 是最适合我 give 的地方，我也向 [GiveWell 的 top charities](http://www.givewell.org/donate) 和 [GiveDirectly](https://www.givedirectly.org/) 进行了 donated。我这样做的 reasons，与 [Alex 和 Denise 在这里 cite 的 reasons](http://effective-altruism.com/ea/14u/eas_write_about_where_they_give/) 相似：

> \[我们向 global poverty causes 进行 donating 的 reasons\]稍微复杂一些，来自多种 interlinked considerations 的共同作用：
> 
> 1.  我们希望，我们的 donations 至少有一部分是在 tangibly and robustly helping 世界上一些 worst-off people。
>     
> 2.  我们不希望自己所做的事仅仅相当于（或者可能被视为相当于）给我们熟识的那些 ‘inner circle’ EAs 支付 salaries。
>     
> 3.  GiveWell charities 更容易向别人谈起，而且可以说，它们让我们能够向 outsiders 发出一个不那么 ambiguous 的 signal
>     

我决定将大约 20% 用于向 global poverty causes 进行 give，因为这个比例足够大，让我感觉自己对那些 organizations 作出了 strong commitment；同时又足够小，不至于让我觉得自己在 compromising 主要 donation 的 effectiveness。

我让 GiveWell allocate 我的 global-poverty donation 中的大部分，原因与我让 Nick allocate 我的 overall donation 中的大部分相同：我已经关注 GiveWell 多年，完全信任他们的 research。我破例直接向 GiveDirectly 进行 give $1,000，因为无论是他们所做的事，还是 GiveDirectly 本身的 run 方式，都让我个人非常振奋。

## 要 give 多少

我原本计划今年 donate 我的 income 的 50%，主要因为这是许多其他认真的 effective altruists 所设定的 benchmark。像 [Jeff 和 Julia](http://www.jefftk.com/donations) 这样的人，已经对自己的 giving habits 作过很多思考，而 50% 看起来是一个既不会 constrain 他们过上充实生活的能力，又仍能让他们 give 出 huge amounts 的 level。我的收入比这里所想到的大多数人都少，但因为我不用养家，我的 expenses 也更低，因此 50% 看起来完全做得到。

今年我离这个 target 差了很多，主要是因为我决定[对我的 Wave stock options 提前 exercise](http://www.investopedia.com/university/employee-stock-options-eso/eso5.asp)。这篇文章不适合解释 stock options，但基本上，提前 exercising stock options 意味着，如果它们的 value 上升，你需要为 gains 支付的 tax 会少得多。从 Financially 的角度看，这基本上 equivalent to 向 Wave 作出一笔 cash investment。

由于 early exercise terms 看起来非常好，我决定 exercise 我的所有 shares，这意味着要向 Wave 支付大约 $25,000。我的 savings 足够这样做，但不足以在此后仍保有充足的 cash buffer。（我尽量在 bank 里保留至少六个月的 expenses，以应付意外情况——比如 exercising 一大批 options。）既要重新建立 cash buffer，又要维持 retirement account contributions，我剩下的钱不足以达到原先的 50% target。

对此我并不太失望——我认为 early exercising 是正确的决定，而且我与 goal 之间的差额，几乎恰好就是 early exercise investment 的 amount。我预计明年能够 donate 50%，除非又出现同样大的一笔支出。

## 总体想法

明年，我希望从几个方面改进自己的过程：

-   我可以接受今年没有达到原先的 50% goal，但如果多做一些 budgeting，或者采取更有 strategic 性的 saving 和 investment，我或许能够更接近它。另一方面，如果能腾出注意力，投入到诸如我在 Wave 的工作这样更重要的事情上，那么我可能应该对 budgeting 操_更少_的心（代价可能是略微 constraining donations）。我希望明年能进一步完善自己对这一 trade-off 的看法。

-   我正在考虑向一些目前还没让我觉得有说服力、但很受欢迎的 cause areas 提供 small gifts。对我来说，这可能会是 animal welfare，也可能包括某种 political advocacy。虽然我对这些 areas 的热情不及对目前 giving 的那些地方，但我担心，这可能部分是因为我没有同样密切地关注它们。作出 small donations 看起来是让自己继续对新信息保持开放的一个好办法。

-   我的 donation logistics 还有改进空间。我直到几天前才最终作出决定，也直到 30 日才寄出 checks。如果一切正常，这些 donations 仍会 count towards 2016 tax year，但如果出现任何 delays（例如 bounced check），我最后可能就只能把它们 count 在 2017 年了。我希望明年能在圣诞节之前寄出 checks，以留下更大的容错余地。

另一方面，我对实际作出的 donations 非常满意。我认为 EA Giving Group 会把这笔钱 allocate 得比我自己尝试去做要好得多，我也很高兴能继续 supporting GiveWell 及其 top charities。

## 原文

I’ve decided where (and how much) to donate for 2016! The punchline: I’m donating $20,000 to a donor-advised fund run by Nick Beckstead, $5,000 to GiveWell for discretionary re-granting, and $1,000 to GiveDirectly. Here’s how I came to that decision.

## The EA Giving Group

I think the main way I do good is by working at Wave, not by donating, so I don’t want to spend a lot of time optimizing my decision. I also don’t think I’m better at choosing where to give than other people in the effective altruism community. Because of this, I decided to let someone else choose my donations for me.

Fortunately, this year I found out about the EA Giving Group, a donor-advised fund directed by Nick Beckstead and another person who also provides most of its funding. I know both directors personally and have a lot of trust in their thought process, so I’m happy to let them decide my giving for me. Nick wrote a bit about the EA Giving Group in his section of [GiveWell’s post on staff donations](http://blog.givewell.org/2016/12/09/staff-members-personal-donations-giving-season-2016/).

When I was looking at [their previous donations](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1H2hF3SaO0_QViYq2j1E7mwoz3sDjdf9pdtuBcrq7pRU/edit#gid=0), I noticed something interesting. Nick and his co-director made lots of grants to organizations that I now think are doing good work—but almost every grant happened while I was still skeptical of the organization in question. This is pretty clear evidence that they’re better at grant-making than I am!

A few other people have decided to give to the same fund this year, and as Nick mentioned in GiveWell’s write-up, the Open Philanthropy Project is now funding similar organizations, which might make it harder for Nick to find good opportunities. But I expect it will still be easier for him than it would be for me.

## GiveWell and GiveDirectly

Even though I think the EA Giving Group Fund is the best place for me to give, I donated to [GiveWell’s top charities](http://www.givewell.org/donate) and [GiveDirectly](https://www.givedirectly.org/) as well. I did this for similar reasons to the ones [Alex and Denise cite here](http://effective-altruism.com/ea/14u/eas_write_about_where_they_give/):

> \[Our reasons for donating to global poverty causes\] are a bit more complicated and have flowed from a mix of interlinked considerations:
> 
> 1.  We want at least part of our donations to be tangibly and robustly helping some of the worst-off people in the world.
>     
> 2.  We don’t want to solely be doing things which amount (or could be seen to amount) to paying salaries of ‘inner circle’ EAs who we know well.
>     
> 3.  GiveWell charities are easier to talk about and arguably allow us to send a less ambiguous signal to outsiders
>     

I decided to give about 20% to global poverty causes because it was big enough to feel like a strong commitment to those organizations, but small enough not to feel like I was compromising the effectiveness of my main donation.

For the same reason that I’m letting Nick allocate most of my overall donation, I’m letting GiveWell allocate most of my global-poverty donation: I’ve been following GiveWell for years and have complete trust in their research. I made an exception to give $1,000 straight to GiveDirectly because I’m personally really excited about both what they do, and how GiveDirectly itself is run.

## How much to give

I originally planned to donate 50% of my income this year, mostly because it’s a benchmark set by lots of other serious effective altruists. People like [Jeff and Julia](http://www.jefftk.com/donations) have put a lot of thought into their giving habits, and 50% seems like a level that doesn’t constrain their ability to have a fulfilling life, but still lets them give huge amounts. I earn less than most of the people I’m thinking of here, but I also have lower expenses since I’m not supporting a family, so 50% seems very doable.

This year I missed that target by a lot, mostly because I decided to [exercise my Wave stock options early](http://www.investopedia.com/university/employee-stock-options-eso/eso5.asp). This post is not the place to explain stock options, but basically, exercising stock options early means that if they go up in value, you pay much less tax on the gains. Financially, it’s mostly equivalent to making a cash investment in Wave.

Since the early exercise terms looked very good, I decided to exercise all my shares, which meant paying Wave about $25,000. I had enough savings to do that, but not enough to have a solid cash buffer afterwards. (I try to keep at least six months’ expenses in the bank to deal with unexpected things—like exercising a large lot of options.) Between building back up a cash buffer and keeping up retirement account contributions, I didn’t have enough left over to meet my original 50% target.

I’m not too disappointed about this—I think early exercising was the right decision, and I missed my goal by almost the exact amount of the early exercise investment. I expect to be able to donate 50% next year unless something else equally large comes up.

## General thoughts

There are a few ways I’d like to improve my process next year:

-   I’m okay with not meeting my original 50% goal this year, but I might have been able to come closer with more budgeting or more strategic saving and investment. On the other hand, it’s possible I should be worrying _less_ about budgeting (potentially at the expense of slightly constraining donations) if it frees up my attention for more important things like my work at Wave. I’d like to refine my views on this trade-off next year.

-   I’m thinking about making small gifts to popular cause areas that I haven’t found compelling so far. For me, this would probably be animal welfare and maybe some kind of political advocacy. While I’m not as excited about these areas as I am about the places I’m currently giving, I’m worried that this could be partly because I don’t follow them as closely. Making small donations seems like a good way to keep myself open to new information.

-   I have room to improve my donation logistics. I didn’t finalize my decision until a few days ago, and didn’t mail the checks until the 30th. The donations will still count towards the 2016 tax year if everything goes normally, but if there are any delays (for instance, a bounced check), I could end up having to count them in 2017 instead. I’d like to send out checks by Christmas next year to leave more room for error.

On the other hand, I’m very happy with the actual donations. I think the EA Giving Group will allocate the money much better than I could try to do on my own, and I’m excited to continue supporting GiveWell and their top charities.
