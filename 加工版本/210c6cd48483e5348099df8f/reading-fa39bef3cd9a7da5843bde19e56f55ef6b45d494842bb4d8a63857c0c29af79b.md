# I just sold half of a blog post

---

## 正文

我很兴奋地告诉大家，我的 [donation matching literature review](https://www.benkuhn.net/matching) 的 impact 中有 50% 刚刚 [been purchased](http://impactpurchase.org/2015/04/10/first-round-results/)，这是在 Paul Christiano 和 Katja Grace 的 [impact purchase](http://impactpurchase.org/) 第一轮中完成的！

这意味着，Paul 和 Katja 现在 own 这篇文章的 altruistic consequences 的 50%，所以你应该认为它作为 good deed 的分量只有你先前所认为的 50%。

### 啊？

Paul 和 Katja 正在 purchasing “certificates of impact”，这是他们发明的一种 philanthropic instrument。这就像 funding 某人去做某件事，只不过不是在他们做事之前给他们 money，而是在他们做完 _之后_ 给他们 money。

### 为什么这样更好？

把它与通常的 tradeable goods 作个 analogy 会有所帮助。

假设我正在 buying 一件衬衫。我通常会看看所有可以买到的衬衫，然后 buy 一件。（将这与 philanthropy 中常见的 funding model 作个 Contrast：在那种情况下，我会先为一件衬衫向某人 pay money，_然后_ 对方才会制作它。）

通常的 market model 将衬衫 production 的大部分 downside risk 放在 seller 身上。如果我的裁缝用完了布料，最终没能做出我的衬衫，那么我的裁缝就没法 sell 它，因此会损失 money。这能更好地 aligns incentives：如果我的裁缝还没有因结果而 gotten paid，就很可能会更努力地制作一件 saleable 的衬衫。（例如，如果 sellers 只有在其 product shipped 时才 paid，Kickstarter products 的 production delays 和 failures 可能就会更少。）

通常的 market model 还有一个更重要的方面：它让你更容易决定自己想要哪件衬衫。对于衬衫而言，这倒不是什么大事，因为要 _ex ante_ predict 未来那件衬衫有多好，并不那么困难。但在 philanthropic funding 中，要做到这一点就困难得多了！

关于 impact purchase market 为什么会有帮助，[网站上](http://impactpurchase.org/why-certificates/)有深入得多的解释。

### 这个 process 是如何运作的？

我通过 Paul 和 Katja 网站上的 form 提交了申请，简短介绍了这篇博客文章、我为什么认为它产生了 good consequences，以及我愿意 undo 这篇文章的 impact 所需的 minimum amount of money。

然后，Paul 和 Katja 自行 evaluation 了每个 applicant project 做出了多少成果，并运行了一个[复杂却巧妙的 auction process](http://impactpurchase.org/the-auction/)，以确保他们能 purchase 尽可能 maximum amount 的 impact，同时确保我有 incentivized 去 report 我愿意 sell 这篇文章的 impact 的 true minimum price。

（这个 auction 的 structure 使得我为文章报出的 stated price 对 Katja 和 Paul 以什么 price buy 它没有 effect，只会影响它是否 gets purchased。这相当酷。）

### 这不基本上就是 Paul 和 Katja 无缘无故地给你撒钱吗？你为什么会报出一个 greater than zero 的 minimum price？

对我来说，这并不是 free money，因为我现在有了更好的 incentivized，去做 Paul 和 Katja 认为对世界有益的事情。

不过，实际上，就目前而言，由于 Paul 和 Katja 是 impact purchases 唯一的 buyers，而且 market 非常 thin，我认为确实有一些 free money 可以拿。例如，如果 Paul 和 Katja 在年底停止这个项目，也没有其他人接手，那么 incentives 就会消失，也不会有人真的那么在意他们曾经 bought 我某篇文章的 50%。

另外，因为目前 entrants 很少，impact 正以相当大的 markup 被 sold——根据 Paul 和 Katja 的 impact evaluations，他们这一轮从 sellers 那里 purchased impact 所支付的 price，几乎是通过 donating 给 GiveWell top charities 获得同样 impact 所需金额的两倍。但我并不认为这种情况会持续下去。

不过，从长远来看，impact purchases 完全不涉及什么撒钱。

人们很容易认为它们会涉及撒钱，因为乍看之下，Paul 和 Katja 似乎并没有真的 buying 任何东西。claim 我的文章的 impact 的 50% 的 right 看起来并不那么值钱，因为人们可能并不买账。例如，人们可能仍会把做 altruistic 的事情所带来的接近 100% 的 social status benefits 给我。

但实际上，Paul 和 Katja 正在 buying 的更重要的东西，是让我去做更多他们将来会想 buy 的事情的 incentive。从这个角度看，给予他们对这篇文章的 claim，是为了确保我不能 sell 它的 100% 以上。（如果我能那样做，反而会 distort the market，并导致人们获得 _过多_ incentive 去做事情。）公开告知他们 own 一半的 consequences，只不过是 enforcing 这一点的一种 mechanism。

所以，从长远来看——如果 impact purchase market grows——我有一个非常充分的理由，为我的文章报出一个 nonzero minimum price：我可能认为，以后或者向其他人 sell 它，可以获得更多 money。

### 体验如何？你还会再参加吗？

会！我认为 impact purchase 项目真的很酷，而且可以在什么样的 EA projects 能 get funded/properly incentivized 去完成这方面填补一个重要空白。申请过程非常快。（而且，我还得到了报酬。）

每个月都有一轮 purchases，下一次截止时间是一周多以后的 April 25。你应该去了解一下，并[申请](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/1D1PlHNi_7ya9iI1StsR5vSraDvcjTFk-fk-DIUYFtu4/viewform)！

## 原文

I’m excited to report that 50% of the impact of my [donation matching literature review](https://www.benkuhn.net/matching) has just [been purchased](http://impactpurchase.org/2015/04/10/first-round-results/) in the first round of Paul Christiano and Katja Grace’s [impact purchase](http://impactpurchase.org/)!

This means that Paul and Katja now own 50% of the altruistic consequences of the post, so you should consider it only 50% as much of a good deed as you previously did.

### Huh?

Paul and Katja are purchasing “certificates of impact,” which are a philanthropic instrument they invented. It’s like funding someone to do something, except that instead of giving them money before they do the thing, you give them money _afterwards_.

### Why is that better?

It helps to make an analogy to normal tradeable goods.

Suppose I’m buying a shirt. What I generally do is look at all the shirts that are available and then buy one. (Contrast this with the funding model that’s normal for philanthropy, where I would pay someone money for a shirt, and _then_ they would make it.)

The normal market model puts most of the downside risk of shirt production on the seller. If my tailor runs out of cloth and can’t make my shirt after all, then my tailor doesn’t get to sell it so my tailor loses the money. This aligns incentives better: my tailor is likely to try harder to make a saleable shirt if they haven’t gotten paid for the result yet. (For instance, Kickstarter products might experience fewer production delays and failures if sellers were only paid when their product shipped.)

An even more important aspect of the normal market model is that it makes it much easier to decide what shirt you want. This isn’t such a huge deal for shirts, where it’s not so hard to predict _ex ante_ how good the future shirt will be. It’s much harder to do that in philanthropic funding!

There’s a much more in-depth explanation of why an impact purchase market would be helpful [on the website](http://impactpurchase.org/why-certificates/).

### How did the process work?

I applied through the form on Paul and Katja’s website, giving a short description of the blog post, why I thought it had good consequences, and the minimum amount of money for which I would undo the post’s impact.

Then Paul and Katja made their own evaluation of how much each applicant project did, and ran a [complicated yet nifty auction process](http://impactpurchase.org/the-auction/) that ensured they would purchase the maximum amount of impact possible while ensuring that I was incentivized to report the true minimum price at which I would be willing to sell the post’s impact.

(The structure of the auction is such that my stated price for the post has no effect on what price Katja and Paul buy it at, only on whether or not it gets purchased. It’s pretty cool.)

### Isn’t this basically Paul and Katja throwing money at you for no reason? Why would you name a minimum price greater than zero?

It’s not free money for me, because I’ve now been better incentivized to do things that Paul and Katja think are good for the world.

Well, actually, right now, since Paul and Katja are the only buyers of impact purchases and the market is very thin, I think there’s some free money to be had. For instance, if Paul and Katja discontinue the program at the end of the year and nobody else steps up, then the incentives will go away, and nobody will really care that much that once they bought 50% of one of my posts.

Also, because there are so few entrants right now, impact is getting sold at a substantial markup—according to Paul and Katja’s impact evaluations, the price at which they purchased impact from sellers this round was almost double what they could have gotten from donating to GiveWell top charities. But I don’t expect that to continue.

In the long term, though, impact purchases don’t involve any money-throwing at all.

It’s easy to think they would, because at first it seems like Paul and Katja aren’t really buying anything. The right to claim 50% of my post’s impact doesn’t seem worth that much because people might not play along. For instance, people might still give me close to 100% of the social status benefits from doing something altruistic.

But actually, the more important thing Paul and Katja are buying is an incentive for me to do more stuff they’d want to buy in the future. On this view, the point of giving them a claim to the post is to make sure that I can’t sell more than 100% of it. (If I could do that, it would distort the market instead and lead to people getting _too much_ incentive to do things.) The public notice that they own half of the consequences is simply a mechanism of enforcing this.

So in the long term—if the impact purchase market grows—there’s a very good reason for me to name a nonzero minimum price for my post, which is that I might think I could sell it later, or to someone else, for more money.

### How was the experience? Would you do it again?

Yes! I think the impact purchase program is really cool, and could fill an important gap in what EA projects get funded/properly incentivized to get done. The application was very quick. (And on top of that, I got paid.)
