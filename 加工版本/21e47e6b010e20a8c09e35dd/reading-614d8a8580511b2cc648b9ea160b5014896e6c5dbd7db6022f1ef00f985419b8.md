# A general framework for understanding biology

---

## 正文

理解 biology 中的某个事物意味着什么？

当然，我们可以通过 [Tinbergen's four questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinbergen%27s_four_questions) 来理解 biology 中的 systems。Tinbergen 将 explanations 分为：

1.  从 evolutionarily 的角度来说，X 的 function 是什么？
2.  X 是如何 evolve 的？
3.  X 如何 work？
4.  X 如何随着 organism 的 grows 而 develop？

那么，对于 vision，wikipedia 给出了以下 example：

> explaining [visual perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_perception) 的 Four 种方式：
> 
> -   Function：为了 find food 和 avoid danger。
> -   Phylogeny：[vertebrate eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_the_eye) 最初 developed 时带有 blind spot，但由于缺乏 adaptive intermediate forms，blind spot 的 loss 未能发生。
> -   Causation：eye 的 lens 将 light focuses 到 [retina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retina) 上。
> -   Development：[Neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurons) 需要 [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light) 的 stimulation，才能将 eye wire 到 brain（Moore, 2001:98–99）。

但这还是太 high level 了。我最近读了很多 biology papers，刚接触一个新领域并开始读 papers 时，感觉是这样的：

![图片-20200624205308660](https://nintil.com/images/2020-06-24-framework-biology/image-20200624205308660.png)

黑色部分代表知识。起初，它们像一座座分开的岛屿，core concepts 周围有一些额外细节，但彼此并不相连。

最后，它会变得更像这样：

![图片-20200624205452359](https://nintil.com/images/2020-06-24-framework-biology/image-20200624205452359.png)

我们开始在不同想法之间建立联系。这大致就是理解的感觉：把某个事物放进背景之中。带着这个想法，再回顾我自己最近阅读这些 papers 的经历，下面这些问题似乎构成了一个不错的 framework，能够近似呈现理解的体验：

1.  它有哪些 functions？（很少只有一个）
2.  还有其他事物 plays 相同的 functions 吗？
3.  它们在哪里被 made？它们通常的 lifecycle 是什么？
4.  有没有其他与它们 similar 的事物？有没有 subcategories？
5.  什么会 reduces/downregulates 这个 system 的 functions？
6.  什么会 enhances/upregulates 它们？
7.  在其他 animals 中也是如此吗？
8.  我们是怎么知道这一切的？通常涉及哪类 measurement devices 或 assays？它们有多 robust？
9.  其 evidence base 是否 broad 且 agreed-on，是不是基于少数几篇早年的 papers，还是仍有争论？
10.  这是一个 highly connected concept 吗？它是 system 中 relatively disconnected、只做一件事的 part，还是与许多其他 parts connected，并有 multiple functions？

在你自己探索 biological systems 时，你也可能会发现这个 framework 很有用。

_Ricón, José Luis, “A general framework for understanding biology”, Nintil (2020-06-24), available at https://nintil.com/framework-biology/._

## 原文

What does it mean to understand something in biology?

There are of course [Tinbergen's four questions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinbergen%27s_four_questions) to understand systems in biology. Tinbergen separates explanations into:

1.  What's the function of X, evolutionarily speaking?
2.  How did X evolve?
3.  How does X work?
4.  How does X develop as an organism grows?

So for vision, wikipedia gives the following example:

> Four ways of explaining [visual perception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_perception):
> 
> -   Function: To find food and avoid danger.
> -   Phylogeny: The [vertebrate eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution_of_the_eye) initially developed with a blind spot, but the lack of adaptive intermediate forms prevented the loss of the blind spot.
> -   Causation: The lens of the eye focuses light on the [retina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retina).
> -   Development: [Neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurons) need the stimulation of [light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light) to wire the eye to the brain (Moore, 2001:98–99).

But this is too high level. As I've been reading a lot of biology papers recently, initially coming to a new area and reading papers feels like this:

![image-20200624205308660](https://nintil.com/images/2020-06-24-framework-biology/image-20200624205308660.png)

The black bits represent knowledge. Initially there are separate islands, with some extra details around core concepts, but they are disconnected.

Eventually it becomes more like:

![image-20200624205452359](https://nintil.com/images/2020-06-24-framework-biology/image-20200624205452359.png)

One starts to build relations between the different ideas. That's roughly what understanding feels like, to put something in context. With that in mind, and reflecting over my own recent experiences reading these papers, these questions seem like a good framework that approximate the experience of understanding:

1.  What are the functions? (Rarely there is just one)
2.  Is there anything else that plays the same functions?
3.  Where are they made? What's their usual lifecycle?
4.  Are there other things similar to them? Are there subcategories?
5.  What reduces/downregulates the functions of the system?
6.  What enhances/upregulates them?
7.  Is it the same in other animals?
8.  How do we know all that? What kind of measurement devices or assays are usually involved? How robust are they?
9.  Is the evidence base broad and agreed-on, is it based on a handful of old papers, or are there ongoing disputes?
10.  Is this a highly connected concept? Is this a relatively disconnected part of a system that does one thing or is this connected to many other parts, with multiple functions?

You might also find this framework useful in your own explorations of biological systems.
