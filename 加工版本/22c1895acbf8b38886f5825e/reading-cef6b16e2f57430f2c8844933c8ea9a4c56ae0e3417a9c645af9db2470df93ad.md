引言

在 default 情况下，我是一个_出色的_ procrastinator。更糟的是，我是一名 mathematician，受过的训练让我对 existence proofs 和 theory 感到兴奋，却忽略 implementation details。因此，在 default 情况下，我_真的_很不擅长实际 taking actions，或 acting on 我的 desires。

我经常有很棒的 ideas，却很少 act on 它们。我可能听说一本很棒、似乎非常值得读的书，一篇我想看看的文章，想到一位我应该发消息联系的朋友，或者听说一个很棒的 idea，可以尝试用来 solving 我的某个 problems。它当时在我的脑海中一闪而过，而且听起来很值得做！这确实是我真心想做的事。但从 empirically 来看，我经常忘记这些事，而且极少、极少 act upon 它们。这是一个_真的_很大的 problem！人很容易落入 academically minded 的典型陷阱——总在 thinking，从不 doing。有一大堆我_想_做的事，却从来没真正去做。本文谈的就是我用来 solving 这些 problems 的 tools。

而且，我在别人身上也非常经常 observe 到这个 problem！如果你对这个 problem 有共鸣，我强烈建议你实际尝试我将在这里概述的 ideas，这种情况_真的_很常见。为了让感受更 visceral 一些，我建议你**现在就**设一个 5 minute timer，列出你目前一直 putting off、从来没真正去做的事——如果你和我有几分相似，那份清单会长得令人不安。

此外，我想有这样一类人：他们一直在读我的博客，而且读得很开心，觉得我有一些有趣的 ideas，也许值得 acting upon。然而，从 empirically 来看，他们至今什么也没做，而且很可能永远也不会真正去做。**这篇文章就是写给你的。**

planning 的 mindset

我认为，所有这些 failure modes 的 root cause 都是 **planning 的 failure**。人们很容易把 planning 理解为坐下来，想出一套仔细、缜密的 strategy，以确定如何着手处理某件事。但我把 planning 定义为：**通过 shaping 你未来的 actions 来 achieve 你的 goals 的 art**。制定仔细、详尽的 strategy 只是 means to an end。

我觉得这种 mindset 很有帮助，因为当我有一个 idea，却始终没有为它做任何事时，这就是 planning 的 failure。并不是我从未 made a plan，而是我的 implicit plan 是“assume 它会在 default 情况下发生”。**最糟糕的 plan，就是你从未 make 的那个**。而不幸的是，这远远是最常见的 planning 的 failure。因为**世界的 default state 就是你会忘记，而且永远不会 achieve 你的 goals**。因此，良好 planning 的第一项 skill，是首先学会如何 make a plan——如何让一个转瞬即逝的 idea 感觉像是**值得为之 making a plan 的事**。

这里的 underlying problem 在于 [willpower 和 attention](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)——记住要做某件事会 consumes attention。而 doing 它并不是 **default** action，因此需要 effort 和 willpower。此外，你的 intuitions _极其_ misleading——人很容易 optimistic，并 commit the planning fallacy，觉得自己总有一天_一定_会去做。然而，从 empirically 来看，事实并非如此！你脑子里有很多事，如果现在没有做，很可能就会被永远 put off，或者被忘记。因此，你_唯一的_ intervention point，就是**当下 notice，并为此做点什么**。你需要培养 **noticing** 和 **reacting** 的 skill。而 reaction _必须_在当下发生，因为**这是唯一的 intervention point**——如果你现在 fail，你就已经输了。

听完这些，一种自然的 reaction 是 guilt 和 obligation。你可能成功在当下 notice，感到 guilty，并下定决心确保这次一定去做！这就是 [pressing the Try Harder button](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)。Planning 不是关于 _trying_，而是关于 _doing_。你并不_想_在这件事上 spending willpower——任何需要长期 spending willpower 的 strategy 都不是 robust 的。这是一个需要用 systems thinking 来 solved 的 problem——在当下 reacting，意味着 **changing 你将采取的 default actions。**

一般来说，这是一个困难的 problem，因为这种 failure mode 发生在 [five second level](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JcpzFpPBSmzuksmWM/the-5-second-level)——不是仅靠 thinking 或 trying harder 就能 solve 的。doing 与不 doing 之间的 difference 在于 reflexes 和 mental habits，而 developing 恰当的 mental habits，与 understanding 它们_应该_是什么，是非常不同的 skill。这不是仅靠 trying harder 就能 solve 的 problem，你需要 deliberate，而且要小心！

Noticing：觉察

第一步是在你 fail to plan 时 noticing！我之前已经写过自己关于[如何 notice 事物](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GLPaZamxqkx7XJbXv/the-skill-of-noticing-emotions)的想法，现在正是应用它的绝佳时机！Noticing 可以 breaks down 为两个 parts——**aware** 到自己正在 failing to plan，以及感受到 **urgency**。我发现这里一个有用的 trigger，是把 noticing 与“我应该……”这句话挂钩——我_极少_在说过“我应该找个时间做 \_\_\_”之后真的去做。

我发现，在这里 awareness 往往比较容易——它经常表现为我说“我以后会做的”时的一种不安感：我知道这并不完全是真的，却不想面对。或者我感到有些 guilty，却没有 energy 去 act upon 它。

关键在于 **urgency** \- 感觉这件事是 _important_ 的 \- 值得 acting upon，是我在乎的事。一瞬间的**真正 caring**，就足以打破 inertia，让我愿意为此 spend 一些 willpower。世界的 default state 是我不会 act on 这份 awareness，因此我_需要_一个 trigger 来脱离那个 default。一些建议：

-   提醒自己，我很可能永远都不会去做——不是现在做还是以后做，而是**现在做，否则永远不做**。

-   Notice 那种想 ignore 这个 thought 的 reflex，并 challenge 它

-   试着让“overcoming procrastination”成为你 self-image 的一部分——成为一件让你引以为豪、也愿意为其本身而做的事

    -   一般来说，这是一件非常值得努力去[为之感到兴奋](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)的事

-   试着 hack in to intrinsic motivation——说：“我可以现在为此做点什么，也可以永远都不去做，这是我的 free choice。而我选择永远不做。”这里有什么让你觉得不对劲吗？如果有，就倾听你内心的那个部分，并试着把它 channel 成更强烈的东西

-   在 practicing [Murphyjitsu](https://dynalist.io/d/pgngbu6nDQbQzu1Gcof3Dj1i#z=4SdU4Sh5SSWbyF1vyi6uAnPG) 之后——想象一个世界：我现在什么也不做，却最终还是去做了。然后问：“这个世界会让我 surprised 吗？”

    -   这会 accesses 我的 intuitions 中更 accurate 的一层，而让它成为 _intuitive_ knowledge，会使它更 powerful——surprise 是一种相当 visceral 的 emotion，很擅长 hacking into 我的 motivation system

-   一个 ideal end goal——每当你 notice 到自己希望 future self 做某件事，就问自己：“如果这件事没发生，我会 surprised 吗？”——我已经朝这个方向走了一段，并发现它_超级_ powerful

注意，urgency 不必_非常_ strong——只要足以让你做点什么，而不是什么都不做就行。

Reacting：作出反应

一旦你成功唤起了暂时的 urgency，你能为此做些什么？这是一种可以让你 spend 一些 willpower 的 impulse。但 spending willpower _很难受_。你的 goal 是 achieve goals，而不是在某种 abstract 意义上“try hard”——results 才是唯一重要的。而你的 goal 是 **change 未来的 default action**。对于这些 problems，合适的 tool 就是 systems！你需要一些办法，用最少的 effort 来 change 这个 default——一项很值得的时间 investment，就是建立 scaffold systems 来帮助你，比如 to-do system 或 calendar。

我常用的办法：

-   把它 Add 到我的 to-do list，并在每周的“clearing 我的 to-do list 的时间”去做

    -   注意：**如果你不 regularly clear 这份 list，这就不是一个 plan**。这_远远_是使用 to-do lists 时最常见的 mistake

-   给朋友发消息，committing 会去做，或者请他们提醒我

    -   注意——试着让它 _concrete_。“我会在 \_\_\_ 时间之前做 \_\_\_。”这样就难得多，没法轻易找借口逃掉。

-   如果它是 time/location sensitive 的，就给自己 setting 一个 reminder

    -   更好的是，我有一个叫 Waiting 的 Trello board，里面全都是 reminders。这非常好，因为即使我错过了 reminder，仍然可以在 clear out 我的 to-do list 时看到它

-   Financial commitments：财务承诺

    -   我从这样的 committing 中获益良多：如果我不做某件事，就付钱给朋友；或者先把钱给他们，告诉他们只有在我做了某件事后才还给我。

        -   它的力量大到曾经让我在 100 多人面前_糟糕地_说唱

    -   如果找朋友这部分让你觉得别扭，[Beeminder](https://www.beeminder.com/) 和 [Stickk](https://www.stickk.com/) 这样的 Websites 可以让你不需要朋友也能这样做。

-   让它 _concrete_。_准确地_弄清楚我需要做什么、什么时候做、first step 会是什么、哪些 barriers 让这件事变得困难，以及我可以如何处理它们。

    -   这是一个_真的_ powerful 的 technique——fuzzy ideas 要难得多，做起来也需要多得多的 willpower。

    -   这尤其适用于更有雄心的事情！比如说，如果你想开始一个 daily writing project，其中涉及_很多_ decisions。如果你能决定_什么时候_做、_在哪里_做、_怎么_做、写_什么_，做起来就会容易_得多_。这会让它更可能真的发生！

    -   我发现，把这件事与给朋友发消息或找朋友聊聊结合起来，往往很好——与别人交谈时，更容易 clarify 我的 thoughts，也更省 energy

-   在我的 calendar 中为它找一个合适的时间，并把它 schedule 进去

    -   注意——只有在你 _stick_ 于自己的 schedule 时，这才有效

最后，只要有可能，_现在就做_。如果一个 action 能在 <5 minutes 内完成，就训练自己 **just doing it** 的 habit。[first step 总是最困难的 step](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-21-taking-the-first-step)，所以，如果你能在 5 minutes 内至少做个_开始_，那就现在做！设一个 5 minute timer，看看你能做到哪一步。

一个常见的 failure mode 是：这些事你做得还行，但正在与人交谈时，却觉得太尴尬，不好意思去做。“让我先 set 一个 reminder，提醒自己做这件事”，在社交上完全可以接受！（至少在我平时来往的人之中是这样。）说真的，如果你想采纳别人给你的建议或推荐，那么你说“嗯，这是个很棒的 idea，而我必然会忘记，让我现在就确保自己会去做”，其实挺让对方受用的

这还有一个有趣的 variant：当你给_别人_建议时，往往很容易 recognise 这种 failure mode。而通过问“如果这件事没发生，你会 surprised 吗？”，明确指出这一点，对 creating urgency 相当 effective。我认为，给建议最有价值的部分之一，并不是 transferred 的 information，而是让一个人从 **thinking** 转向 **doing**。提供 accountability，帮助他们把事情变得 concrete，并帮助他们让“真正 doing 它”成为 default。而且还有一个额外好处：如果事情没发生确实_会_让他们 surprised，你就可以退开，不会造成任何伤害！好的 social skills 应该能够 fail gracefully。

结语

总的来说，我在应对 procrastination 方面仍然很不擅长。但随着时间推移，我在 reacting in the moment 方面已经进步了_很多_，而且我认为这是一项非常有价值的 skill。我强烈建议你也尝试 cultivating 这项 skill，尤其是如果你对这些问题感同身受。**尤其**是，如果你一直喜欢读这个博客，也想 act upon 其中的 ideas，却还没有真正做过什么——你从不 act upon 的 ideas **比毫无用处还糟糕**。它们消耗时间和精力，却没有任何 results。

这很难，因为把一个 idea 付诸行动并不_总是_最好的选择——[prioritisation 很重要，而且很难](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/prioritisation)。我认为，重要的是_先_培养 reacting in the moment 这项 skill，再解决 prioritisation 的问题——反过来要困难得多。建立 systems，把 task 加入 to-do list，_然后_再 prioritise。Prioritising 和 doing 会用到你 brain 中非常不同的 parts，而你无法同时做这两件事。

归根结底，procrastination 是一种_真的_很难戒掉的 habit。部分原因在于，即使你知道该如何应对它，也很容易在_这件事_上 procrastinate。于是，你就陷入了 catch-22。而摆脱困境的唯一办法，就是**感受到那种 urgency**。你是否对所有事情都 procrastinate？如果你始终不对此采取任何行动，你会 surprised 吗？

这样的生活是你满意的吗？你_现在_能做些什么，作为迈向**你想成为的那种人**的第一步？

Introduction

By default, I’m an _excellent_ procrastinator. Worse, I’m a mathematician, and trained to be excited by existence proofs and theory, and to ignore the implementation details. As a result, by default, I am _really_ bad at actually taking actions, or acting on my desires.

I often have cool ideas, but rarely act on them. I might hear about a cool book that sounds super worth reading, an article I want to check out, think about a friend I should message, or hear about an awesome idea to try solving one of my problems with. And this flashes in my mind in the moment, and it sounds worthwhile! It’s something I genuinely want to do. But, empirically, I often forget about these, and very, very rarely act upon them. And this is a _really_ big problem! It’s so easy to fall into the classic trap of being academically minded - always thinking, never doing. Having a bunch of things I _want_ to do, but never get round to. This post is about my tools for solving these.

And this is a problem I very commonly observe in others! If you resonate with this problem, I strongly urge you to actually try the ideas I’ll outline here, this is _really_ common. To make it a bit more visceral, I recommend that you set a 5 minute timer **right now**, and list things you’re currently putting off, and never get round to - if you’re anything like me, that’s an uncomfortably long list.

Further, I think there’s a kind of person who’s been reading and enjoying my blog, who thinks I have some interesting ideas that might be worth acting upon. Yet, empirically, they have yet to do anything, and are unlikely to ever get round to it. **This post is aimed at you.**

The mindset of planning

I think the root cause of all these failure modes is a **failure of planning**. It’s easy to conceive of planning as sitting down and coming up with a careful, meticulous strategy for how to approach something. But I define planning as **the art of shaping your future actions to achieve your goals**. Making a careful, detailed strategy is just a means to an end.

I find this mindset helpful because, when I have an idea and never get round to doing anything about it, this is a failure of planning. It’s not that I never made a plan, it’s that my implicit plan was “assume it would happen by default”. **The worst plan is the one you never make**. And, unfortunately, this is by far the most common failure of planning. Because **the default state of the world is that you forget, and never achieve your goals**. Thus, the first skill of good planning is learning how to make a plan in the first place - how to have a fleeting idea feel like **something worth making a plan about**.

The underlying problem here is one of [willpower and attention](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower) - remembering to do something consumes attention. And doing it is not the **default** action, so it takes effort and willpower. Further, your intuitions are _highly_ misleading - it’s easy to be optimistic, and commit the planning fallacy. To think I’ll _definitely_ get round to it someday. Yet, empirically, this is not true! There are a lot of things on your mind, if it doesn’t happen now, it’s likely to be put off forever or forgotten about. As a consequence, your _one_ intervention point is to **notice in the moment and do something about it**. You need to cultivate the skill of **noticing** and **reacting**. And the reaction _needs_ to happen in the moment, because **this is the only intervention point** - if you fail now, you’ve lost.

Upon hearing all this, a natural reaction is one of guilt and obligation. You might manage to notice in the moment, feel guilty, and resolve to make sure you do it this time! This is [pressing the Try Harder button](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button). Planning isn’t about _trying_ it’s about _doing_. You don’t _want_ to be spending willpower on this - any strategy that involves spending willpower in the longterm is not robust. This is a problem that needs to be solved with systems thinking - reacting in the moment means **changing the default actions you will take.**

In general, this is a difficult problem because the failure mode happens on the [five second level](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/JcpzFpPBSmzuksmWM/the-5-second-level) - it’s not something you can solve by just thinking or trying harder. The difference between doing and not doing is one of reflexes and mental habits, and developing the right mental habits is a very different skill from understanding what they _should_ be. This is not a problem you can solve by just trying harder, you need to be deliberate and careful!

Noticing

The first step is noticing when you fail to plan! I have written my thoughts previously on [how to notice things](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/GLPaZamxqkx7XJbXv/the-skill-of-noticing-emotions), this is an excellent time to apply that! Noticing breaks down into two parts - being **aware** that you’re failing to plan, and feeling a sense of **urgency**. One trigger I’ve found useful here, is pegging the noticing to the word “I should …” - I _very_ rarely say “I should do \_\_\_ some time” and then actually do it.

I find awareness tends to be easier here - it often manifests as a sense of unease when I say “I’ll do that later” - I know this isn’t quite true, but don’t want to confront that. Or I feel a bit guilty, but don’t have the energy to act upon it.

The key part is the **urgency** \- the feeling that this is _important_ \- something worth acting upon, something I care about. A flash of **actually caring**, that’s enough to break the inertia and get me to spend some willpower on it. The default state of the world is that I won’t act on the awareness, so I _need_ a trigger to break from that default. Some tips:

-   Remind myself that I probably will never get round to it - it’s not now or later, it’s **now or never**.

-   Notice the reflex to ignore the thought, and challenge that

-   Try to make “overcoming procrastination” part of your self-image - something you feel proud of, and to do for its own sake

    -   Generally, this is an excellent thing to strive to [feel excited about](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)

-   Try to hack in to intrinsic motivation - say “I can do something about this now, or never get round to it, and this is my free choice. And I choose to never do it”. Does anything feel wrong here? If so, listen to that part of you, and try to channel it into something stronger

-   After practicing [Murphyjitsu](https://dynalist.io/d/pgngbu6nDQbQzu1Gcof3Dj1i#z=4SdU4Sh5SSWbyF1vyi6uAnPG) - imagine a world where I do nothing now yet eventually get round to it. And ask “am I surprised by this world?”

    -   This accesses a more accurate layer of my intuitions, and having it as _intuitive_ knowledge makes it more powerful - surprise is a pretty visceral emotion, and is good at hacking into my motivation system

-   An ideal end goal - every time you notice wanting your future self to do something, ask yourself “am I surprised if this doesn’t happen?” - I’m partway to this point, and have found it _super_ powerful

Note that the urgency doesn’t have to be _very_ strong - just enough to get you to do something rather than nothing.

Reacting

Once you’ve pulled off the temporary feeling of urgency, what can you do about it? This is an impulse that can get you to spend some willpower. But spending willpower _sucks_. Your goal is to achieve goals, not to “try hard” in some abstract sense - results are all that matters. And your goal is to **change the default future action**. And the right tool for this problems are systems! You want to have ways to use a minimal amount of effort to change this default - a great investment of time is setting up scaffold systems to help with this like a to-do system or calendar.

My go-tos:

-   Add it to my to-do list, and do it in my weekly “clearing my to-do list time”

    -   Note: **If you don’t regularly clear the list, this is not a plan**. That is by _far_ the most common mistake made with to-do lists

-   Message a friend committing doing it, or asking them to remind me

    -   Note - try to make it _concrete_. “I will do \_\_\_ by \_\_\_ time”. This makes it far harder to weasel out of.

-   If it’s time/location sensitive, setting myself a reminder

    -   Even better, I have a Trello board called Waiting which is nothing but reminders. This is excellent, because even if I miss the reminder, I can still get it when I clear out my to-do list

-   Financial commitments

    -   I get a lot of mileage out of committing to pay a friend if I don’t do something, or giving them money and telling them to only give it back if I do something.

        -   This was strong enough to once get me to rap _terribly_ in front of 100-odd people

    -   Websites like [Beeminder](https://www.beeminder.com/) and [Stickk](https://www.stickk.com/) let you do this without needing a friend, if that part feels weird.

-   Make it _concrete_. Figure out _exactly_ what I need to do, when I need to do, what the first step will be, what barriers make this hard and what I can do about them.

    -   This is a _really_ powerful technique - fuzzy ideas are much harder to do, and take a lot more willpower.

    -   This applies especially to more ambitious things! If you want to, say, start a daily writing project, there are a _lot_ of decisions involved in that. It’s _much_ easier to do if you can decide _when_ to do it, _where_ you’ll do it, _how_ you’ll do it, _what_ you’ll write about. This makes it more likely to actually happen!

    -   I find this is often good to combine with messaging a friend, or chatting to a friend about - it’s much easier to clarify my thoughts when talking to somebody else, and takes less energy

-   Find a good time in my calendar for it, and schedule it in

    -   Note - this only works if you _stick_ to your schedule

Finally, if at all possible, _do it right now_. If an action can be done in <5 minutes, train the habit of **just doing it**. [The first step is always the hardest step](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-21-taking-the-first-step), so if you can just make a _start_ in 5 minutes, do it now! Set a 5 minute timer, and see how far you can get.

A common failure mode is being OK at these things, but being in a conversation and feeling too awkward to do it. “Let me just set a reminder to do that” is totally socially acceptable! (At least, among the people I hang out with). Hell, if you want to follow advice or a recommendation somebody has given you, it’s pretty flattering if you say “huh, that’s an excellent idea, and I will inevitably forget about it, let me make sure I do it now”

A fun variant on this: When giving advice to somebody _else_, it’s pretty easy to recognise this failure mode. And explicitly calling them on it, by asking “are you surprised if this doesn’t happen?” is pretty effective for creating a sense of urgency. I think one of the most valuable parts of giving advice isn’t the information transferred, it’s shifting somebody from **thinking** to **doing**. Offering accountability, helping them make it concrete, and helping them make “actually doing it” become the default. And as a bonus, if they genuinely _are_ surprised if it doesn’t happen, you can back off with no harm done! Good social skills should fail gracefully.

Conclusion

Overall, I’m still pretty bad at procrastination. But I have gotten _much_ better at reacting in the moment over time, and I consider this a really valuable skill. And I strongly urge you to try cultivating it yourself, especially if you relate to these problems. And **especially** if you’ve been enjoying this blog, and want to act upon the ideas, but have yet to actually do anything - ideas you never act upon are **worse than useless**. They consume time and energy, with no results.

This is hard, because it’s not _always_ the best idea to do the idea - [prioritisation matters and is hard](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/prioritisation). I think it’s important to build the skill of reacting in the moment _first_, and solve prioritisation second - it’s much harder the other way round. Have systems to add the task to a to-do list, and prioritise _then_. Prioritising and doing take very different parts of your brain, and you cannot do both at the same time.

Ultimately, procrastination is a _really_ hard habit to kick. In part because, even if you know what to do about it, it’s easy to procrastinate about _that_. And you get stuck in a catch-22. And the only way to break out is to **feel that sense of urgency**. Do you procrastinate about everything? And are you surprised if you never do anything about this?

Is that a life you’re happy with? What could you do, _right now_, that would be a first step towards **the kind of person you want to be?**
