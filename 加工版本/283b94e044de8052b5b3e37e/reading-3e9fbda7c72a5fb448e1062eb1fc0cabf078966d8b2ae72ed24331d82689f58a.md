# Ed Zitron 的 AI 预测记录｜Ed Zitron's AI prediction track record

---

## 正文

我很好奇，我见过被引用得最广泛的 AI skeptic（Ed Zitron）的 predictions 表现如何，于是查看了他的 predictions 最终如何。先坦白我自己的 biases：我从未对支持或反对 AI progress 持有特别强烈的 position。例如，[在 2022 年，我全面考察了 Futurists 作出的 predictions，其中包括 Kurzweil 这样广受尊敬的人](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)，发现无论是 prediction results 还是 reasoning，他们通常都是 wrong。另一方面，[在 2015 年，我写过人们如何 underestimating AI 在 jobs 中 displace humans 的 ability](https://danluu.com/customer-service/)，而且曾多次公开表示，许多人正在 underestimating AI 从 jobs 中 displace humans 的 ability。我对 AI 的 position 一直极其无聊，基本就是：“如果某件事眼下正在发生，那么那些说它永远不可能发生的人大概是错的”。

当有人回应一位 AI skeptic 时，我见过许多 AI skeptics 发表的一种评论是：所有声称 AI 并非假的人，都是自私自利的骗子。就我个人而言（这显然让我吃了亏），我在 AI companies 中没有特别的 financial interest。我通过无聊的 index funds，持有其中通常会有的 share。我有一些 seed stage investments，但仅仅由于时机和哪些领域发展壮大了，我的 portfolio 中这一部分在 AI 上是 underweight。我不在 AI lab 工作，也不在为 AI labs 供货的 company 工作。我以前提过，自己在 interviews 中的表现差得令人发笑；多年前，我确实曾在一家 AI lab 参加 interview，并且没通过 phone screen，那次表现差到想必足以启发 Jeff Atwood 那篇著名的文章——[为什么 Programmers……不能 Program？](https://blog.codinghorror.com/why-cant-programmers-program/)。他在文中 concludes，外面一定有很多假的 programmers，因为如果一个人知道如何 program，就不可能在 coding interview 中失败得那么惨。如果 AI 发展得好，我不会以任何特别的方式 benefit，除了所有 holds broad index funds 的人都会 benefit 这一点之外；但我确实在乎 accuracy。

虽然这并不符合 Zitron 那番话的本意，但也可以 argue，Meta 确实正在衰亡，只是还没有死而已。然而，Zitron 的 argument 中的 reasoning 在这里是 incorrect——Meta、Google 和 Microsoft 的 ecosystems 并没有正在衰亡。考虑到这些 companies 的 growing 速度如此之快（就 revenue 和 profit 而言），AI 看起来并不像 Zitron 所 implied 的那样，是它们因为“不知道如何 grow”、又已经想不出任何办法，而抓来救命的某种绝望之举。我不认为值得为每一个 prediction 都花这么多篇幅，但 Zitron 在这里使用的 pattern 很有说明性。

为了 make the case，说明这些东西正在衰亡，他抓住了一些 minor issues；这些 issues 并不足以 cause 他所暗示即将发生的那些巨大 changes。对于 Meta，他 cited 了 Facebook 据称出现的某种 MAU drop。他似乎没有 use Meta 自己的 MAU figures，也没有 use 任何 revenue 或 profit numbers，而是 used 了 Similarweb 的 numbers。我对这类 3rd party tracking numbers 的经验是，它们相当 inaccurate，除了粗略的 order of magnitude comparison 之外，通常没有什么用，因此 Zitron 所 cited 的 decline 没有意义。FB 在 2023 年 12 月停止公开 reporting MAU，但大多数 estimates 都认为 FB MAU 随时间 increasing，而 Meta 确实 report 的 numbers 也显示，其 products 的 usage 总体上随时间 increasing；Zitron cherry-picked 了一个 outlier low estimate 来说明自己的观点。

对于 Google，他 cites Prabhakar Raghavan，说他对 Google search 造成了某种严重伤害，并称他真正邪恶，是“一个站在 management consultancy sect 那边的 computer scientist class traitor”。在那些针对 Raghavan 的怒斥中，他从未 credibly establishes，Raghavan 正在严重伤害 Google search，而且[那些对他的怒斥发表过评论的 Google search engineers，似乎并不 agree with 将 Raghavan 视为 search issues 的 sole 乃至 major reason 的 hypothesis](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=40133976)。[1](https://danluu.com/zitron/#fn:H)

但即便我们 posit，Zitron 是 right，反派 Prabhakar Raghavan 击败了英雄 Ben Gomes，给 Google search 带来了某种 issue，这仍然没有 make the case，说明 Google revenue growth 整体陷入了困境，因为它们还有若干其他 major products（例如 YouTube 和 Google Cloud），即便 search 没有 growing，这些 products 也能够 drive growth。

这里的 chain of reasoning 中，每一个 significant part 不仅是 incorrect；只要你对 Google 或一般的大 companies 有任何了解，就会觉得它并不 plausible。我会第一个说，[Google search quality 存在一些严重 problems](https://danluu.com/seo-spam/)，而且随着时间推移，Google 一直在 increasing revenue 相对于 user experience 的 relative priority。我在 2013 年供职于 Google 时，这让那里的许多 user-focused engineers 感到不安。

Zitron cites 的一个 issue 是 ads 让 users 感到 confusing。2013 年，我曾向一位 search engineer 问起 Google changing ads 的 background color、使其看起来更像 search results 的事情，因为此前有一项 study 显示，一条 ad 看起来越像 search result，users 就越容易弄不清某个 result 究竟是 ad 还是真正的 search result；而我听说，Google 曾故意让 ads 看起来不像 search results，以 avoid user confusion。那位 search engineer 说，因为有些人不希望 users get confused，所以不可能在一次 change 中就让 ads 与 search results nearly identical，那样意图就太明显了。

这件事将会这样发生：每当你通过 A/B test，尝试 tweaking ads，让它们看起来更接近 search results 一点，就会赚到多得多的钱；所以，这种 change 会分成多个部分，在多年中逐步发生，每一部分都小到足以让那些想反对这类事情的人很难 make a case。事情正如这位 engineer 所 predicted 的那样发生了，但无论 Raghavan 最终是否 overseeing search，它都会发生。而且，当然，这类事情的发生并不会 cause Google 耗尽 grow 的空间，继而绝望地试图在一个正在衰亡的 ecosystem 中 reignite growth。无论你是否认为 Google 应该这样做，这件事都会让 Google 赚到更多钱。

### 人们如何 cite Zitron？

据我对人们如何 cite Zitron 的观察，他们把他作为 authority 来 cite，这样就能说，这个看过 numbers 的人提出了这个 claim，因此他们的 claim 有 numbers 的支持。结果是，如果你看看 Zitron 提出的 claims，并且对这个 topic 有所了解，就会发现这些 claims 说不通，但我认为重点不在这里。重点是，人们可以说有人看过 numbers。另一个重点似乎是，这个人很愤怒[2](https://danluu.com/zitron/#fn:V)，而这很适合用来 drive engagement。

但人们提起他时，当然通常不是在 cite 他的愤怒；他们是在说，这里有个人看过 numbers，而且，如果你对 AI 感到愤怒，他也正和你一起对 AI 感到愤怒，并且 numbers 站在他那边。[3](https://danluu.com/zitron/#fn:S) 正如我上面所说，我不想对每个 claim 都讨论得这么细；这只是一个 illustrative example，展示下面那些 claims 大致是什么样。在我读过的他的每篇文章里，虽然到处都抛出了 numbers，但这些 numbers 实际上并没有构成 coherent argument。在许多情况下，正如我们上面看到的，numbers 甚至并不真正 support 他的 argument（例如，Facebook 的 MAU decline 导致 Meta 出现 financial problems，继而导致 Meta 在不该出现 AI 的地方 spuriously insert AI）。我怀疑他依赖的是，人们一看到 numbers 就两眼发直，根本不去想这些 numbers 意味着什么。

对于下面的 predictions，一个人完全可能有一模一样的 prediction record，同时有完全 reasonable 的 reasons，只是最终没有应验。或者，一个人也可能在每个 case 中都是 correct，却又是 wrong，因为他的所有 reasons 都是 wrong。后一种人可能有某种无法表达出来的 intuition，也可能只是运气好。幸运的是，我们不必作出这个困难的 judgement call，因为 Zitron 不仅在 predictions 上是 wrong，在 reasoning 上也是 wrong。

### 那些关注细节的人如何看待 Zitron

由于[我多年来一直两耳不闻窗外事，最近才开始了解 AI discourse](https://danluu.com/ai-coding/)，我此前其实没有读过或看过 Zitron 或任何知名 AI commentators 的作品，但在查找那些 judgement 很好的人怎么说时，发现他们似乎也认为，[Zitron 对 numbers 的运用只是障眼法，例如 Juho Snellman 的这条评论](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45402639)：

> 他的文风确实张扬，但那些攻击性言辞和脏话似乎更多是在煽动那些本来就相信他所写内容的人，而不是为了让人改变想法。他在 anti-tech grift 中找到了一个 niche，现在正竭尽所能地 exploiting 这个 niche。不过，你或许真该对他那些说服了你的说法挑几条做一下 fact-check，因为至少在他的书面文章里，基本上所有东西不是编造的，就是被 misrepresented 了。没错，确实有大量指向 sources 的 links，但如果你顺着它们一路找到 primary source，就会发现其中所说的内容与 Zitron 暗示的非常不同

[这里有个例子，commenters 似乎出于某种原因，默认 Zitron 的 analysis 很好，Juho Snellman 对此回复道](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45404088)：> 关键 problem 是，他的 economic analysis 完全就是垃圾。我以前以为他只是完全不擅长这件事，但考虑到 errors 中的 bias，这相当明显是 intentional deception。然而，要回应这些往往相当困难，因为他写的每篇文章都是一场 10k 词的 gish gallop。我曾几次尝试在 HN comments 中 debunking 关键 points，而且_只针对一个_他犯下的 intentional mistakes，人们就抱怨回复太长。

例如，[Timothy B. Lee 查看了 Zitron 用于 create Anthropic 的 revenue projection 的 spreadsheet](https://x.com/binarybits/status/2034377838883700953)，发现

-   [**2025 年 7 月**](https://bsky.app/profile/edzitron.com/post/3lsw4vatg3k2b): "我并非想危言耸听，但很容易得出 Cursor 即将 die 的 conclusion"
    -   Wrong（错误）（Cursor 获得了 $60B 的 exit）
-   [**2025 年 8 月**](https://www.wheresyoured.at/how-to-argue-with-an-ai-booster/): "这些 models 显然已经 hit a wall，training 正在 hitting diminishing returns"
    -   Wrong（错误）
-   [**2025 年 8 月**](https://www.wheresyoured.at/ai-is-a-money-trap/): Zitron 说 Cursor 正在 dying，并预计它会以 firesale price 售出；价格高达 $10B is not plausbie: "Is Cursor worth \$10 billion？不可能！不管它的 product 究竟有多好或没多好，都还不够好，不可能以一个无需 Cursor 无休无止地烧掉数亿美元的 price 被 sold。"
    -   Wrong（错误）
-   **2025 年 10 月**: 在回答“如果你必须猜测，我们预计 AI bubble 会在什么 timeline 内 pop？”这一问题时，Zitron 回答："不晚于 Q2 2026"
    -   Wrong（错误）（注意，Zitron 加上了一个“不晚于”，这就提出了更强的 claim：这是一个 upper bound，而不只是 best guess）
-   [**2025 年 11 月**](https://carnegieendowment.org/podcasts/the-world-unpacked/ais-biggest-skeptic-sees-a-bubble): "事实是，我们正在 running out of high quality training data，而且在 training paradigm 中，我们正在 hitting scaling laws 的 walls，这些 models 并没有 getting better。我们今天所看到的，基本上就是它们永远会有的样子"
    -   Wrong（错误）

在这之后，我看到的后续 predictions 大多要么是 non-falsifiable，要么要到未来才会 resolve。请注意，我没有尝试 catalogue 那些不知所云的 statements，或在当时就纯属 factually incorrect 的 statements，例如他在 2024 年 12 月声称“Generative AI 的 products 实际上已经被封在琥珀里一年多了”，或在 2026 年 1 月声称“\[models are\] 基本上和一年前一样。它们具有相同的 efficacy”。Zitron 不仅作出了 AI capabilities 将不会 improve 的 forward-looking statements，还一直在作出 capabilities 并未 improved 的 backwards-looking statements；这些话虽然在当时就显然是 false，却似乎很受他的支持者欢迎（他的其他 false statements 也是如此）。如果把他所有的 statements 连起来看，就会发现它们暗示 AI 在 2026 年 1 月具有的 capabilities 与 2023 年 12 月相同（而如果把后来的 statements 也连起来，实际上还暗示 2026 年 8 月的 capabilities 与 2023 年 12 月相同，不过，公平地说，Zitron 经常 contradicts himself，也曾在 2026 年年中承认存在 limited improvement）。

公平地说，我们可以认为 Zitron 说的是口语，所以当他说“实际上已经被封在琥珀里一年多了”这样的话时，并不意味着自 2023 年 12 月以来真的没有 change，因此这些 statements 并不是 transitive。如果我们谈论的是 100 年的 progress，有人说一年里没有 change，可能是想表达只有 1% 的变化，但这仍可能意味着 100 年里有 100% 的 change。然而，我们这里谈论的是三年。即使假定他在口语表达上有些不严谨，这些 statements 合在一起仍然暗示，从 2023 年 12 月到 2026 年 8 月，improvements 一直是 minimal（或许，正如上文所述，他 contradicts himself、承认某些 areas 存在 limited improvements 时除外）。

### 与受尊敬的 Futurists 比较

如果我们与[我们对 futurists 的 analysis 中 futurists 的表现](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)比较，在风格上，Zitron 比我们考察过的任何 futurists 都更依赖愤怒。在 reasoning 的 quality 上，与这些 futurists 相比，他大概处于 average。尽管他几乎什么都错了，但他并不比 Buckminster Fuller 这样的人更 unreasonable；后者曾提出，我们将能够通过 radio 发送人，因为 atoms 有 frequencies，而 radio waves 也有 frequencies，所以将有可能 pick up 我们所有的 frequencies，并通过 radio 发送它们。

就 reasoning 的风格而言，在接受考察的 futurists 中，他大概最接近 Kurzweil，因为他用 numbers 营造某种可信的光环，但如果你对他讨论的话题有所了解，或者看看那些 numbers，这套 reasoning 就站不住脚了。Zitron 的 reasoning 并不比 Kurzweil 的更差；后者（例如）不断作出关于极其快速的 progress 的新 predictions，而它们都未能兑现（比如在 2001 年 predicting 到 2011 年会有 unbounded lifespans）。不断因为 incorrect 的 reasons 而 predicting AI progress 将停止，只不过是在关于 progress 的赌局中押了另一面。我们将不会有 infinite progress，而是没有 progress。每当那个 prediction 被 proven wrong，你都可以再作一个类似的 prediction，然后把日期往后挪一点（两人的粉丝也使用相同的 techniques；Zitron 的粉丝直接声称他的 predictions 是 true，就像 Kurzweil 的粉丝援引他的 86% prediction accuracy，[尽管如果你实际查看那些据称有 86% 正确的确切 predictions 的 results，他在那些 predictions 上的实际 accuracy 是 7%](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)）。Michał Zalewski (lcamtuf) 对这种现象为何发生有一些想法：

> 建立 $$a$$ 大众追随群体最稳妥的办法，就是表达鲜明、强硬、不留任何怀疑余地的立场。靠着“嗯，market 可能朝任一方向发展”“两个 political parties 都有道理”“AI 有些 merit，但也有些 hype”，你很难获得多少上 podcast 或电视的机会。或者，借用这篇文章中的例子，“《哈利·波特》是一本还行的书”。
> 
> 事实上，这里存在一个 positive feedback loop。如果你采取挑衅、出格的立场，就会得到更多关注和点赞，于是你就有点……self-radicalize 了？到了某个时候，它就不再是一个能够改变的看法。它成了一种 identity，一种 personal brand。
> 
> 这……就是为什么 Ed Zitron 能靠一个宣称这一切不过是一场大骗局的博客大受欢迎。如果你采取更 nuanced 的看法，最好的结果是无人回应，最坏的结果则是招来双方的鄙视。[8](https://danluu.com/zitron/#fn:O)

对于任何想寻找 well-reasoned 的 anti-AI 观点的人，我觉得 [whitequark](https://social.treehouse.systems/@whitequark) 相当不错（并不是说我同意，而是我认为其 reasoning 是 sound，而且我能理解，如果某人的 premises 与我的略有不同，就可能同意），但当然，whitequark 并没有吸引到 Zitron 那样庞大的受众。

### 你能把一个 incorrect 的 position 维持多久？

我很好奇，人们在这样一组关于 progress 的 failed predictions 上站错边之后，会怎么做。对于那些 futurists，即使是几乎全错的人（[这里考察的每一个人都是如此](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)），他们仍然可以提出某种说辞，比如“我说会发生的事情，有四分之一发生了，只不过花的时间比我预期长了两倍到二十倍”；而如果他们不那么执着于 accuracy，就可以把这句话 round this up 成“我说会发生的事情发生了”，他们也常常这么做。这似乎对他们很管用，因为反正也没什么人真的愿意查看细节；比如，Kurzweil 据称有 86% prediction accuracy，就是这样变成了公认的事实；人们实际上并不关心那些 predictions 最终表现如何，[尽管在 2022 年就已 refuted 那个 86% 的 claim](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)。

但像 Paul Ehrlich 这样的人会怎样呢？他 predicted imminent catastrophe，而在他写作时，这显然并没有发生，后来也没有发生。只看 Ehrlich 的 Wikipedia 页面，我们就能看到

> 一种常见的 criticism 是，Ehrlich 的 predictions 屡屡未能成真；例如，《Reason》杂志的 Ronald Bailey 称他为一个“不可遏制的末日论者……据我所知，他关于 imminent catastrophe 的 forecasts 从未有过一次正确。”\[41\] 在 1970 年的第一个 Earth Day，他警告说：“$$i$$n ten years，海洋中所有重要的 animal life 都将 extinct。大片 coastline 将不得不因死鱼的恶臭而被疏散。”\[41\]\[42\]
> 
> 在 1971 年的一次演讲中，他 predicted：“到 2000 年，United Kingdom 将只剩下一小群贫困的 islands，上面住着约 70 million 饥饿的人。”Ehrlich 教授在登机前总结说：“如果我是个赌徒，我会以 even money 打赌，England 到 2000 年将不复存在。”\[41\]\[42\]
> 
> 当这个 scenario 并未发生时，他回应说：“当你 predict 未来时，你会弄错事情。错到什么程度是另一个问题。如果我当时打了那个赌，我就会输。不过，如果你仔细看看 England，我还能说什么呢？他们和所有其他人一样，正面临各种各样的问题。”\[41\]
> 
> Ehrlich 在 The Population Bomb 中写道：“India 到 1980 年不可能再 feed two hundred million 人。”\[27\] 在 1967 年，Ehrlich 呼吁 cut off 对 India 的 emergency food aid，称其“无可救药”。\[43\] 这个 position 后来受到 criticized，因为 India 的 food production 随后通过 Green Revolution in India 而 skyrocketed，而且在接下来的几十年里，即使其 population doubled，其 per capita caloric intake 仍 rose significantly。\[44\]
> 
> 自 1960s 以来，global food production 的大幅 increase 和 population growth 的 slowing，在当前 non-renewable resources 持续 depletion 的背景下，averted 了 Ehrlich 夫妇所 foretold 的那种规模的 food shortage、famine 和 catastrophe。
> 
> Canadian 记者 Dan Gardner 在其 2010 年著作 Future Babble 中\[45\] argues，Ehrlich 在承认自己所犯的 errors 时不够坦率，却在为那些他声称自己说“对”的事情邀功时，表现得 intellectually dishonest 或 evasive。例如，他很少承认自己在 predicting material shortages、starvation 导致的 massive death tolls（在出版物 Age of Affluence 中高达 one billion），或关于特定 countries 所受的 disastrous effects 时犯下的 mistakes。与此同时，他却乐于为“predicting” AIDS 的 increase 或 global warming 邀功。\[13\]
> 
> 在 disease 方面，Ehrlich 曾 based on overcrowding，或饥饿人群的 weakened immune systems，predicted 某种 disease 的 increase，因此，“把这视为 forecasting AIDS 在 1980s 的 emergence，是很牵强的。”同样，global warming 是 Ehrlich 描述过的 scenarios 之一，因此为它邀功，却拒绝为 failed scenarios 承担责任，是一种 double standard。Gardner 认为，Ehrlich 正在 displaying cognitive dissonance 的 classical signs，而且他不肯承认自己 judgement 中明显的 errors，使他当前的想法变得可疑。\[13\]
> 
> Barry Commoner 曾 criticized Ehrlich 在 1970 年的这番话：“当你到了某个时刻，意识到再努力也是徒劳时，你不妨照顾好自己和朋友，享受所剩不多的时光。对我来说，那个时刻就是 1972 年。”\[46\] Gardner 曾 criticized Ehrlich endorsing William 和 Paul Paddock 在他们的著作 Famine 1975! 中提出的 strategies。他们曾提出一种“triage” system，将 end 对 India 和 Egypt 等“无可救药”的 countries 的 food aid。在 Population Bomb 中，Ehrlich suggests：“就 food distribution 而言，除了 adopt 某种形式的 Paddocks' strategy 之外，没有 rational choice。”如果这个 strategy 当时在 India 和 Egypt 等依赖 food aid 的 countries 被 implemented，它们几乎肯定会遭受 famines。\[13\] 然而，Egypt 和 India 都大幅 increased 了自己的 food production，如今不依赖 food aid 就能 feed 大得多的 populations

令人惊讶的是，Ehrlich 在 1968 年写作 The Population Bomb 期间及之后作出了一连串 incorrect predictions 后，又在 1990 年写出了 The Population Explosion，并一直声称，我们存在 global overpopulation，它正在造成或将会造成一场 dire crisis，除非我们 cut worldwide population。本世纪，甚至本十年，他都一直在说同样的话。看起来，他今天没有继续这么说的唯一原因，就是他在今年早些时候去世了。

如果我没有去查，我大概会猜，他最近的 position 会是这样：“嗯，我确实弄错了一些事情，但正是因为我的工作激发了人们采取行动，crisis 才得以 averted”，或者“虽然 crisis 得以 averted，但那只是运气好；在大多数 universes 中，那些 staved off 我所 predicting 的 mass starvation deaths 的 agricultural advancements 都不会发生”，而不是“你们等着瞧吧，crisis 现在正在发生，我马上就会被 proven right”；在 2015 年，谈到他那本 incorrect 的 1968 年著作时，他说：“$$m$$y language 今天会更加 apocalyptic。”这就是我们在 Zitron 身上看到的 pattern，但我没想到，我查的那一个人竟然会把它又维持了 50 年。也许我们还会再听 50 年 Zitron predicting AI progress 的终结。

### 对 Zitron 的一些反应

在上面引用 Juho Snellman 的一段话中，Snellman 说他写了大量的 [gish gallop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gish_gallop)，这个 term 指的是有人用大量廉价（指的是产生这些内容的成本很低）的胡说八道淹没你，以至于谁都不愿意花时间费心去 refute。为了详细讨论 Zitron 演讲中的一小部分，我们花了超过 1000 words 来解释，为什么 Zitron 对 corporations 如何 work 的理解是 incorrect，以及 Zitron 的 reasoning 错在何处。有人读完后可以说，“但你没有回应演讲中的 X”，这确实没错。我第一次看这场演讲时，实际上在 90 seconds 后就关掉了 tab，因为里面的胡说八道太多，似乎不值得再花时间继续看下去。光是 video 的前 90 seconds，我就能写出 5k words。因为 Zitron 只是在胡说八道，他这么做的成本非常低，而即使我手头已经有全部 facts，要 refute 他 90 seconds 的胡说八道，也得花上 30-60 minutes。算上查找确切信息的时间，大概还得再花 double 或 triple 的时间。一个有良好 judgement 的人看到这种东西，往往会立刻认定这个人不值得理会。举个例子，我跟一位朋友提到自己正在写这篇文章，对方说：

> 我在听有这家伙参加的一期 podcast，实在听不下去。我的 heart rate 都上来了，因为他先说一件 false 的事，然后那个还算讲道理的采访者会追问：“那 X 呢？”，接着我们就又跳到了另一个 falsehood……
> 
> ……在我听不下去退出之前，他说 LLMs 在过去一年里并没有好多少，采访者说人们在用它们，而它们在过去一年里肯定已经好得多了，Zitron 却否认，说“是吗？”，采访者只能说：“是啊……”到那一刻，我就在想，我为什么要听这段对话？

我们主要讨论的是 predictions，而不是关于过去或现在的 incorrect statements，但我读过或看过的 Zitron 的所有内容，也都充斥着这种东西。许多人看到这些，会认定这家伙是个怪人，不再关注。但还有很多人看到这些，看到有人 refute 了一组说法，就会说：“但你没有 refute X。”一般来说，进行 refuting 的人或许会回应其中几个，但最终还是会放弃，因为 gish gallop method 与 amplification DoS attack 有相同的 properties。产生新的胡说八道非常廉价，但要 refute 它却需要花些力气。如果你[观看 Natalie Wynn 对 conspiracy theory reasoning 的这段 analysis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=teqkK0RLNkI)，其中的 reasoning 风格和 rhetorical techniques 会让你觉得出奇地熟悉，因为 Zitron 和 Zitron 的辩护者用的是相同的 techinques。

顺便说一句，我很好奇这是哪次采访，于是把上面的引文输入 ChatGPT，让它找到这次采访。它找出了一段包含相关对话的采访（实际上找出了多段，因为这似乎是 Zitron 常见的问答 pattern），以及采访中每句相关 statements 的 timestamp（[整体 argument 的开头在这里](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lf5oqGOCRCM&t=5601s)，而[“是吗”的回应在这里](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lf5oqGOCRCM&t=5888s)。在那句“是吗？”之前，采访者试图 establish 一个 baseline，即 agents 的 capability 已经 improved。Zitron 否认发生过这种事，随后，当采访者提到那些在工作中使用这些东西的人时，Zitron 就用“是吗？”来否认这一点（实际上，他在这段对话中说了多句 contradictory statements）。

这里还需要注意的是，Zitron 所声称的 confidence 极高。我们注意到的例子包括，他把 OpenAI 的 forecast 说成是“一句如此离谱的 statement，以至于我很惊讶，把它说出口竟然不算某种 financial crime”（而他们迄今已经实现了这个 forecast），以及他声称 Google 对 Gemini users 的 forecast 是“一个如此 unrealistic 的 number，以至于 Google 应该有人因此被解雇，而那个人就是 Sundar Pichai”（他们最终 exceed 了 forecast，幅度达到 50%，而 Zitron 的 claim 是，他们哪怕只是达到那个 number 都完全荒谬）。

我做过不少 predictions，其中也有不少 predictions 是 wrong。当我真正做出一项 prediction 时，我会为自己给这项 prediction 附上一个 confidence level，这样回头再看时，就能知道这些 predictions 的 calibrated 程度如何。我从未在任何 confidence 接近 Zitron 为自己某些 predictions 所赋予的 confidence 的 prediction 上出过错。考虑到他声称的 confidence level，哪怕只有一项 incorrect prediction，都足以表明极其严重的 overconfidence。以那种 confidence level 说出的 prediction，实际上应该几乎从不出错，但 Zitron 在 rhetorically 几乎以最高可能的 confidence 程度做出的 predictions 上，却经常出错。

关于 Gemini 达到 500M users 被说成“如此 unrealistic，以至于 Google 应该有人因此被解雇，而那个人就是 Sundar Pichai”，有件好笑的事：Zitron 还曾经（incorrectly）说，Google 不知道如何 grow，所以才把 AI 塞得到处都是。Dennis Snell 指出，如果 Zitron 认真对待自己的 statement，那么 Google 只需要做 Zitron 说他们会做的那件事——把 AI 塞得到处都是——就能让 Gemini 的 user numbers 达到它想要的任何 number。

你实际上不可能既认真对待 Zitron 关于 Google 缺乏 growth 导致其在 AI 上孤注一掷的 statement，又认真对待他所说的 Sundar 提出 500M users 这样的 number 就构成了某种严重 malpractice。这也是只要看他的一场演讲或读他的文章，就会立刻发现的另一件事。里面有一堆 disconnected statements，彼此无法吻合，除了它们都在说 AI companies、使用 AI 的人和 companies 有多邪恶、多糟糕。那些 statements 中的实际 numbers 和 logic 是 contradictory 的。似乎只要是脑中冒出来、能把反派描绘得邪恶的东西，他就会说。好笑的是，Gemini 达到的 750M user number 表明，Zitron 的两项 statements 都是 incorrect。如果 Google 真像 Zitron 所说的那样，急于抬高 numbers，那么只要把 Gemini 塞得到处都是，他们就能轻易让这个 number 超过 1B，而且当然，750M > 500M。

我第一次停下这场演讲，是听到这里的时候：

> 一个痴迷于 year-over-year revenue growth 的 market。而这样的 progression 是 natural 的。它糟糕透了。你可以怪 Marc Andreessen。他是个糟糕的人。你可以怪许多糟糕的男人。眼下有那么多家伙值得你生气。

最后那句话真是概括了 Zitron 的立场。“眼下有那么多家伙值得你生气。”在这场演讲中，他顺手挖苦了 Andreesen，并把 Meta、Google 和 Microsoft 追求 growth 归咎于 Andreesen。实际上，即使 Marc Andreesen 从未存在过，Meta、Google 和 Microsoft 也几乎肯定仍然会设法 grow，所以我们当然不能真的把这些 companies 试图 grow 归咎于 Andreesen。无非是有这么一件他认为糟糕的事，然后他就按照一贯的风格，拉出某个人，说这就是该为此负责的邪恶反派，接着再转向下一个 non sequitur。

### 人们怎么会认真看待这些？

因为我喜欢自找罪受，我还真去读了大量关于 Zitron 的讨论（我想我读了 HN 和 lobsters 上每一场主要讨论，以及不少其他讨论），看看那些认真看待 Zitron 的人都在说些什么。一种常见的 defense 就是上面那种：没错，你 refuted 了一些 points，但你没有涉及 X。更常见的 defense 则是泛泛地说，人们 attack Zitron 是因为 Y（通常是他的风格），却从不 address 他的 points，“这已经告诉了我所有需要知道的事”（或者类似的话）。根据这些消息的 timestamps，即便只看当时正在讨论的文章，通常也早已有 comments 在讨论 Zitron 的实际 errors，但 Zitron 的 defenders 会无视这些，直接 claim 人们根本无法指出 Zitron 犯过的 mistakes。这是一种非常 Zitron 式的招数，喜欢他这种风格的人也会使用这种招数，倒也不难理解。毕竟，什么人会觉得 Zitron 有说服力？就是认为这种东西算得上 valid reasoning 的人。

其次常见的“招数”，就是直接否认 Zitron 说过那些已经被 refuted 的话。每当有人提到，Zitron 在 2024 和 2025 年曾反复公开表示，LLMs 由于 fundamental reasons 而不可能进一步 improve，他的 defenders 就会说他从来没这么说过；对于他之前的 predictions 或 factually incorrect statements，也是如此。

我看到的另一类 defense，是诸如“但那些说错了的 AI hypists 又怎么说？”这样的 comments。就像我之前说过的，[我写过一篇 34k 字的文章，讨论一批最受尊敬的 futurists 如何错了，不仅是因为他们作出了 incorrect predictions，还因为他们的 methods 和 reasoning 本身就是错的](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)。但一群吹捧未来的人错了，并不会让 Ed Zitron 或 Paul Ehrlich 这样的人少错一点。即使那些 futurists 从未存在过，Zitron 和 Ehrlich 也仍然会错得一模一样。

我的一位朋友还注意到，在有人指出 Zitron 关于 models 从 2023 到 2026 年没有 improving 的说法是错的那些讨论中，comments 有这样一个特点（没错，这里特指那些讨论 Zitron 关于 capabilities 没有 improving、而且已经被 disproven 的 statements 的文章或 comments）：

> 看到这么多人说“Zitron 没错，他只是说早了！”，真是不可思议。我猜其中的 implication 是，我们最终会意识到，2026 年的 models 其实并不比 2024 年的那些更好，还是说？？

发表这篇文章后，有一件有意思的事：给我发消息说我错了的人当中，有相当一部分人说，我错就错在，到了 2026 年的今天，models 其实并没有 models 从 2023 或 2024 年以来并没有 gotten better。我原本猜想，大多数说出上面引语那种话的人，只是在使那种“招数”：不去读别人究竟说了什么，就用一套现成的话来回应，而任何真正读过他们所回应内容的人都会觉得这套话毫无意义。但事实证明，确实有很多人相信 Zitron 的那一连串 statements，相信其中所 imply 的 models 从 2023 或 2024 年以来没有 improved。

如果一个人确实看过正在发生什么，却还在如此程度上否认现实，我觉得你实在没什么办法能说服他。不过，对于只是还没见过事情发生了哪些变化的人，要直观看看那段时间里的 improvements，[这里有 2023 和 2025 年 video generation 的 comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZBOgtTCfnGs)，[这里还有一个 2026 年 8 月的例子](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TudmZN3h8_s)。Video 并不是一个特别好的例子，因为 models 在 coding 上 improved 得更多，例如，[只需 on the order of minutes 的 human time，就可以 create 一个新的 regex engine，其中带有一个 interpreter 和一个 native code compiler，然后 fork ripgrep，让它在我机器上 codex 的实际 ripgrep calls 中变得更快](https://danluu.com/no-slow/)。如果按具备相关 expertise 的人的薪酬来计价，这样一个项目在 pre-LLM 时代可能需要 7 figures 的费用。不过，video 又是个不错的例子，因为在上面链接的采访里，那段“有吗？”的对话之后，Zitron 曾经这样“rebuttal”：“你不会拿它来拍 _movie_，对吧？”现在，人们正在“shooting”相当多的 AI-generated digital footage，而这正在颠覆大量 lower end video work。根据 historical patterns，很容易作出一个 prediction：随着时间推移，这会继续 move upmarket。但仅看人们今天已经在用 AI video 做什么，即使 Zitron 只是在迎合那些坚信 models 从 2023 或 2024 年以来没有 improved 的忠实信徒，他大概也该换一种 rebuttal 了。当然，这个话题可不能涉及 math 或 sciences，因为那些领域的 improvements 一直非常 rapid，其他许多 fields 也是如此。

### 未来的 predictions

尽管 Zitron 过去的 predictions 通常都错了，也许他将来会在某件事上说对。也许其中一些 companies 会因为某种原因出现 valuations decline。但是，即使发生某种大规模 AI crash，OpenAI 和 Anthropic 都 go to zero，从 societal impact 的角度说，哪怕在此之上又发生某件事，使 models 无法在 AI labs 目前内部已经拥有的水平之上取得进一步 progress，仍然会带来相当多的变化。哪些 companies successful，会改变谁能发财，但个别 companies failing 并不会阻止由当前或 next generation model capabilities 带来的变化发生；这只会改变谁获益最多。

就我个人而言，是这家 company 的人还是另一家 company 的人发财，对我没有什么关系。如果一家 company 在某件事上做得比另一家更好（在某种 abstract sense 上），我会有些兴趣，但对于任何一家特定 company 所声称的、他们会比另一家 company 更多地去做“正确的事”，我都抱有一些 skepticism（这件事上我可以被说服，但据我所知，那些公开的 claims 并不怎么有说服力）。

如果 Zitron 最终说中了某家 company 会 collapsing，相比于 capabilities 已经如何 developed、将来又会如何 develop，我对此实在没什么兴趣，而在后一个问题上，他迄今一直是错的。恰好，他迄今作出的 financial predictions 也一直是错的，背后的 reasoning 同样是 flawed，但这些其实并不怎么令我感兴趣，尽管为了 completeness，我还是纳入了不少 financial predictions。

### 附录：Ed Zitron 谈人们为什么不喜欢 Ed Zitron

在寻找关于 Zitron 作品的讨论时，reddit 上排名 #2 的结果是 [Zitron 的这条评论](https://archive.is/EdOKE)：

> ……有些男人不喜欢我，因为 emotional honesty 和 introspection 对他们来说很困难。男人被告诫要 repress 或 compress 自己的 Feelings。我拒绝这样做，任何人叫我这样做，都会让我感到恶心……
> 
> ……先从 emotions 说起，因为这是最明显的一点。人们真的不喜欢我现在的样子，觉得我是在“为了表演效果而发怒”，甚至把我形容为 psychotic、out-of-control，诸如此类。我发现，这种反应常见于那些自身就 emotionally repressed 的人，尤其是在自己的工作中如此的人。既 emotional **又**有深思熟虑的观点，是很难的……
> 
> ……我也没有走那条你“理应走”的路来到这里。你“理应”是大媒体里的体制内作家，或者 analyst，或者从事 finance，又或者走其他种种不同的“正途”，这样你才“配得上”你理应得到的东西。我没有以传统意义上的方式“熬出资历”，而那些熬出资历的人认为，我没有凭本事走到这里……
> 
> ……我的工作也很周全，这让那些做事不周全的人感到恼火。我的每个观点都经过深思熟虑，我也煞费苦心地充分了解所谈的主题。看看还有多少人声称“这就和 Uber 一样”或者“这就和 dot com boom 一样”。从不核实真假，只管瞎他妈假设，要容易得多！这时候冒出个做事周全、又满怀热情的混蛋，确实让人恼火。这会衬得你的工作很差……
> 
> ……我拍照上镜，接受采访也表现得好，我会抓住时事的机会，而且不会卑躬屈膝，因为我通常会带来几千字的想法，或者一期讨论某件事的节目。我相信，有些人也想得到这种程度的关注或声望，却不愿为此付出努力，这让他们很不痛快……
> 
> ……我爱得热烈，爱得深沉，我就是我，任何“圈内”群体都从未让我感到自己受到欢迎。我拼命工作，我写得比任何人都多，我始终在场。无论我创造出什么样的空间，我都会反抗“圈内群体”或小圈子。我讨厌他们，他们也同样讨厌我。而且，我从根本上知道自己为什么相信自己所相信的东西。这让那些不知道的人感到不快。

我不知道这些话他究竟是不是真心的（[如果 Wired 这篇关于 Zitron 及其经营的 PR firm 的人物特写是 accurate 的，那就不得不倾向于认为不是](https://archive.is/m9pHl)），但 Zitron 似乎很擅长说受众想听的话，所以这大概能让我们对他的受众有所了解。

关于小圈子和“圈内群体”的那段话，有一点值得注意：只要在 reddit 上搜索他的名字，就能看到评论者指出，如果你在他的 subreddit 发布任何表明 AI 已经 improved 的内容（比如 benchmarks 的链接），就会因此被 banned，最终形成一个小圈子气息极重的 echo chamber。我曾公开[说过，METR 的 progress benchmark 并不 meaningful](https://www.patreon.com/posts/149123122)，与其依赖 misleading analysis，还不如凭感觉判断；我也说过，[被广泛 cited 的 AI evals 经常是 flawed 的](https://danluu.com/exercise-7/)。所以，并不是说我认为 benchmarks 总体上很好，但我从评论中了解到的情况是，只要你不遵循统一口径，就会很快被 banned，这和上面描绘的图景恰好相反。这并不是 Zitron 独有的现象；前一阵子我查另一位网红时发现，如果你在那位网红的 subreddit 表示不同意见，对方会写一条评论感谢你的评论，说自己多么喜欢收到人们的反馈，说这个世界是多么美好的和平与爱的盛会，我们都应该彼此相爱，同时却把你从他们的 subreddit 里 banned。

Zitron 说人们不喜欢他的作品，是因为他们不喜欢周全的工作；我觉得这些话也很有意思，原因有两个。

其一，我自己的作品经常因为严谨、周全而被正面引用。也有很多人不喜欢我的作品，但我不仅不知道有谁说过自己因为它周全而不喜欢它，就算真有人暗地里因为它周全而不喜欢它，我也会感到惊讶。一般来说，这似乎根本不是人们不喜欢某样东西的理由。有人因为别人知道自己为什么相信某件事，或者因为别人工作努力而感到不快，也是同样的道理。这似乎正是 Zitron 的受众想听的话，实在让我觉得很有意思。

其二，我个人不会认为自己的工作很周全。[我在这里提到的、不觉得自己的工作有多好这件事](https://danluu.com/bug-blind/)，同样适用于我不觉得自己的工作很周全。我确实会对自己的工作做一定程度的 checking。就投入时间而言，我不知道能否说自己比大多数人投入得更多，但就 effectiveness 而言，我怀疑 methods 和投入时间的 combination 比 average 更有效。不过，每次发表作品时，我总是不满意，因为我可以永无止境地继续做更周全的 checking，结果什么也发表不了，所以我强迫自己在一个我认为 thoroughness 高于 average、却远称不上周全的程度上发表。如果把我的工作与 Gary Bernhardt 这样我认为做事周全的人的工作相比，我不知道自己怎么能把自己的工作称为周全。我有几个朋友能做出 Bernhardt 水准的作品，而我选择的是产出多得多、但质量也更低的作品。我觉得，在 quality-speed tradeoff space 中处于这样的位置没什么不好，但这并不意味着我的工作很周全。按照我在 2026 年 7 月之前的 baseline 标准，要做到像 Gary 那样周全，我每份产出就得投入 10x-100x 的时间（按照我最近发表作品的方式，还得再增加 10x 或更多）。然而，我的 fact checking process 似乎仍比 Zitron 的周全得多。

即使把 Timothy Lee 那个例子中严重的 arithmetic errors 放在一边，看看 Facebook MAU 这样的例子：他挑选的 number 在 directionally 上与其他 estimates 和 Meta 自己的 numbers 相反，而且考虑到已有的其他 data，这个方向本身也 implausible；我实在不明白，这样一个 figure 怎么可能通过任何 fact checking。他的大量 factual statements 都存在这种情况（我猜大多数都是，尽管我没有尝试 randomly sample 来确认）。似乎任何你能想象得到的 fact checking process，都会查出 contradictory results。

### 附录：为什么要写这篇文章？

其实没什么充分的理由。我只睡了四个小时，脑子基本干不了什么事，随后看到有人发了一张 reddit 帖子的截图，嘲讽 Ed Zitron 的 prediction record。在我[写这篇关于 futurist prediction accuracy 的 review](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)时，我尽量确保自己没有以任何方式对 review 的内容施加 bias。从帖子里看不出来，那位 review 了 Zitron 的 predictions 的 redditor 是以 unbiased 的方式挑选 predictions，还是带有某种 bias（既然 AI 已经成了 culture war issue，有人挑选带有 bias 的 predictions 也不足为奇），所以我决定在闲暇时间读一些 Zitron 的文章，同时摆弄 agents，让它们去做我想让它们完成的一项无关任务。写 futurist 那篇帖子时，我完整读了好几本书来提取 predictions，通常只有在某个人开始反复说同样的话时才停下来。而在这里，Zitron 所做的就是不断重复，因此，沿用那篇 futurist review 的 methodology，就意味着我 review 几个 predictions 后便会立刻停下来。为了解决这个问题，我让 ChatGPT 给我一份 predictions 列表（不刻意偏向 correct 或 incorrect predictions），然后略读或通读了 ChatGPT 链接的帖子。有些情况下，我认为 ChatGPT 对帖子的解读是 incorrect 的（通常是它标出了一个 prediction，按照它的解读，这个 prediction 会是 incorrect 的，但我不同意它的解读）；此外，我也移除了那些并非 falsifiable，或因为是 tautological 而显得毫无意义的 predictions（下文会进一步讨论；我在 futurist 那篇帖子里也提到过类似的情况）。

如果认真想想，我大概能找到更值得花时间做的事，不过事情就这样了；有时候，我的待办清单上会留一些太累、做不了正经工作时可以做的任务，但当时没有。我不认为他们 cherry picked 了特别糟糕的 predictions，尽管他们确实挑了一些听起来比较荒谬的。不过，如果去细看那些没那么无可辩驳的“打脸”案例（比如声称 Gemini 到 EOY 达到 500M users 是如此荒谬，以至于 Sundar 应该因为提出这个想法而被解雇，结果 Gemini 到 EOY 实际达到了 750M），就会发现，这些也同样是 wrong 的，和声称 Cursor 没有 realistic buyer、暗示它甚至卖不到 $10B when "everybody" (who cares about AI exits) knows they sold for $60B 的说法一样。

那位 redditor 挑选的是备受关注的 failed predictions，但 Zitron 的 prediction corpus 中还有多得多的 failures，而且，如上所述，更大的问题是他的 reasoning。

关于那条 reddit 评论，还有一点：不管评论本身是否 unbiased，人们都可能怀疑它带有某种 bias，因为它被一个看起来信奉 r/accelerate 理念的人发到了 r/accelerate。查看实际的 predictions 后，它们确实与存在某种 bias 的情况相符（也与无心之失的情况相符，因为单凭这些记录无法区分两者）。例如，其中一个“refutations”针对的是 Zitron 关于 OpenAI 会在 12-24 个月内 collapse 的 statement。OpenAI 没有 collapse，所以表面上看，这似乎是证明 Zitron 是 wrong 的绝佳方式；但如果读过 Zitron 的帖子，就会发现，他实际的 claim 是 OpenAI 要么 collapse，要么 raise a lot more money，而它们确实 raised a lot more money。我不同意 Zitron 暗示这必然只会导致日后的 collapse，但他明确提出的 prediction 并未被 falsified。

这个 prediction 不在本文 scored 的 predictions 的 set 中。有人会认为，本文应该对它进行 scored。之所以没有这样做，是因为除了为 Zitron 更宽泛的论点提供支持之外，这个 prediction 似乎毫无意义（那个论点就是 OpenAI 注定完蛋，而且必定 collapse）。

想想我们能提出哪些 predictions：一个必然为 true 的 tautological prediction（如果你把我为了节省篇幅而略去的所有 edge cases 都写出来）是，OpenAI 要么有足够的钱来 operate，要么没有；如果没有，就必须想办法 raise the money。我可以提出一百万个这样的 tautological predictions，但如果有人要对我的 prediction record 进行 scoring，把这些纳入其中毫无道理，因为它们没有意义。一般来说，一家还活着的 company 会 cover its costs。如果没有，它就会尝试 raise money。如果做不到，它就会 shut down 或 get acquired。一个说 company 要么 cover its costs、要么不会 cover its costs 的 prediction，什么也没说。

OpenAI 自己的 projections 就是，它尚不会 profitable，而且它的 costs 会 exceed 它的 revenue。这看起来几乎是 certain 的，因此，如果你 assume 这件几乎 certain 的事情是 true 的，就会得到一个近乎 tautological 的 prediction：OpenAI 要么 collapse，要么 raise money 来 cover its costs。以非常高的 confidence（99.9% 或以上）提出这样的 prediction，本来是合理的。如果你使用任何 prediction scoring methodology，比如 [Brier score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brier_score)，那么，只要 Zitron 有相当数量的 high-confidence incorrect predictions，这些 predictions 除了在 wrong 的时候之外，基本不会有什么贡献。

而且，如上所述，Zitron 对那些被他赋予最高可能 confidence 的 predictions 一再判断 incorrect（根据他的措辞，我会把其中不少评为 6 9s 或以上），所以在 Brier score 之类的任何 scoring mechanism 下，Zitron 的 record 都非常差。而这样的 summary metric，其实还远远低估了这类 predictions 有多么毫无意义。Hypothetically，假设 Zitron 提出了 unbounded number 的 correct、99.99% certainty、近乎 tautological 的 predictions，那么，在 Brier score 这样的指标下，他其余 bounded number 的 predictions 所得的 score 就会变得毫无意义。但这仍然不会让你对他的任何并非近乎 tautological 的 predictions 产生丝毫 confidence，而人们通常讨论的恰恰就是那些 predictions（AI progress 已经结束，AI companies 必须 collapse，而且这还会拖垮 major tech companies，等等）。

回到那位 reddit 评论者可能存在的 bias 与我的 bias 的比较上，如前所述，我并不特别偏向 rapid progress 是 inevitible 这一看法，而且也指出过人们 overly optimistic 的情况，这篇关于 [futurist predictions](https://danluu.com/zitron/\(/futurist-predictions/\) 的帖子就是证明）。我也[不是那种需要或想要通过编造某个人是 wrong 或坏人的理由来 farm engagement 的人](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rlcd5m4FY-o)，也不会一概把每个 predictor 都评得很差，[这篇对 Steve Yegge 的 prediction record 的 review](https://danluu.com/yegge-predictions/) 就是证明；我在其中指出，他的 scored 结果很好，而且实际表现比 raw score 所显示的好得多，因为即便 precise prediction 是 incorrect 的，那些 predictions 总体上仍然是 well reasoned、directionally correct 的。我觉得，如果有人对未来有很好的 insight，并愿意公开分享，那真是件很棒的事，所以当我注意到这些案例时，我很乐意把它们指出来。只不过，在这个案例中，Zitron 是某种 anti-Yegge：他的 prediction record 很差，而他的 predictions 实际上比单看 record 所呈现的还要糟。

### 附录：本文中的 errors

我认为，本文几乎肯定有多个 errors。一般来说，我发现，读完一长串 incorrect reasoning 后，再寻找其中的 errors 时，很难不变得草率。[我在 reviewing futurist predictions 时也遇到过完全相同的问题](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)。这让我想起为某个 system 进行 programming 的时候：它的 compiler 有很多 bug，你每天从早到晚都会碰到 compiler bugs（在处理 embedded systems 时，这并不少见，至少在 pre-LLM 时期如此；现在，你可以比较容易地 fix 这些 bugs）。我发现，自己很难不变得草率，心想“嗯，这可能是一个 compiler bug”，尽管偶尔那实际上是你自己的 bug，而不是 compiler bug。在查看这类 predictions 的 predictions 时，这个问题严重得多，因为 compiler 通常基本上还是能 works 的，而且往往是 right 的；而在阅读这里讨论的这类文字时，你只是在不断被胡说八道淹没，偶尔才夹杂着一个不错且 accurate 的 point。

I was curious how well the predictions of the most widely cited AI skeptic I've seen (Ed Zitron) have done, so I looked at how his predictions panned out. My position on AI has been extremely boring and is basically, "if something is currently happening, the people who are saying that it's impossible that it will ever happen are probably wrong".

One comment I've seen from a lot of AI skeptics when someone responds to an AI skeptic is that all of the people who are saying that AI isn't fake are self-interested liars. Personally (to my obvious detriment), I have no particular financial interest in AI companies. I own whatever the standard share of them is via boring index funds. I'll be the first person to say that [Google search quality has some serious problems](https://danluu.com/seo-spam/) and that Google has been increasing the relative priority of revenue over the user experience over time. For one of the issues Zitron cites, ads being confusing to users, in 2013, I asked a search engineer about Google changing the background color of ads to look more like search results because there was a previous study that showed that more an ad looked like a search result, the more users got confused over whether a result was an ad or a real search result, and I'd heard that Google deliberately made the ads not look like search results to avoid user confusion. The search engineer said that because some people didn't want users to get confused, it was impossible to make ads nearly identical to search results in a single change because it would be too obvious what's going on.

The way this was going to happen was that every time you A/B test tweaking ads to look a bit closer to search results, you make a lot more money, so the change would happen over multiple years in multiple parts, each small enough that the people who want to fight back against this kind of thing would have a hard time making a case. That happened just as this engineer predicted, but it was going to happen whether or not Raghavan ended up overseeing search. And, of course, that kind of thing happening doesn't cause Google to run out of room to grow and become desperate to reignite growth in a dying ecosystem. Whether or not you think Google should do it, it's something that makes Google more money.

### How do people cite Zitron?

From what I can tell of how people cite Zitron, they cite him as an authority so they can say that this guy who looked at the numbers has made this claim, so their claim is backed up by the numbers. It turns out that if you look at the claims Zitron makes and know anything about the topic, the claims don't make sense, but I don't think that's the point. The point is one can say that someone looked at the numbers. The other point seems to be that this guy is angry[2](https://danluu.com/zitron/#fn:V), which is a good way to drive engagement.

But when people bring him up, they're of course not generally citing his anger; they're saying here's this guy who's looked at the numbers and, if you're angry about AI, he's right there with you being angry about AI, and he's got numbers on his side.[3](https://danluu.com/zitron/#fn:S) Like I said above, I don't want to go into this level of detail on each claim; this is just an illustrative example about how the claims below look. For any of his posts that I read, while there are numbers thrown around, the numbers don't actually connect to a coherent argument. In many cases, as we saw above, the numbers don't even really support his argument (such as an MAU decline in Facebook causing Meta financial problems which would then cause Meta to spuriously insert AI in places it doesn't belong). I suspect he's relying on people's eyes glazing over when they see numbers and just not thinking about what the numbers mean.

With the predictions below, someone could have the exact same prediction record and have completely reasonable reasons that just didn't pan out. Someone like the latter person might have some kind of intuition that they're unable to articulate, or perhaps they're someone who just got lucky. Fortunately for us, we don't have to make this difficult judgement call because Zitron is wrong on the predictions and also wrong on the reasoning.

### Comparing to respected Futurists

If we compare to [how futurists did in our analysis of futurists](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/), on style, Zitron relies much more heavily on anger than any of the futurists we looked at. On the quality of reasoning, he was probably about average compared to the futurists. Despite being wrong on roughly everything, he's not more unreasonable than someone like Buckminster Fuller, who suggested we'll be able to send people by radio because atoms have frequencies and radio waves have frequencies so it will be possible to pick up all of our frequencies and send them by radio.

In terms of the style of reasoning, of the futurists reviewed, he's probably closest to Kurzweil, in that he uses numbers to give a kind of aura of credibility, but if you know something about the topic he's discussing or look at the numbers, the reasoning falls apart. Zitron's reasoning isn't worse than Kurzweil's, who (for example) continually made new predictions of extremely fast progress that didn't pan out (such as, in 2001, predicting unbounded lifespans by 2011). Continually predicting that AI progress will stop for reasons that are incorrect is just taking the flip side of the bet on progress. Instead of having infinite progress, we're going to have no progress. Every time that prediction is proven wrong, you can just make another similar prediction and then move the date forward a bit (fans of both use the same techniques as well; fans of Zitron simply claim that his predictions are true, just like fans of Kurzweil cite his 86% prediction accuracy [even though his actual accuracy on those predictions is 7% if you actually look at the results on the exact predictions he allegedly got 86% right](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)). ### How long can you maintain an incorrect position for?

I'm curious what people do after being on the wrong side of a set of failed predictions about progress like this. For the futurists, even the ones who were nearly completely wrong ([which was every single one reviewed here](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/)), they can still make some kind of case like "a quarter of the things I said would happen happened, it just took two to twenty times longer than I expected" and if they're not so stuck on accuracy, they can round this up to "the things I said would happen happened", which is often what they've done. That seems to have served them well as nobody really cares to look at the details anyway, which is how, for example, Kurzweil's alleged 86% prediction accuracy became a well-established fact; people don't actually care how the predictions panned out [even though refuted the 86% claim in 2022](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/).

But what happens to someone like Paul Ehrlich, who predicted imminent catastrophe when this clearly was not happening as he was writing and then did not happen? Just looking at Ehrlich's Wikipedia page, we have

> A common criticism is that Ehrlich's predictions routinely failed to come true; for instance, Ronald Bailey of Reason magazine has termed him an "irrepressible doomster ... who, as far as I can tell, has never been right in any of his forecasts of imminent catastrophe."\[41\] On the first Earth Day in 1970, he warned that "\[i\]n ten years all important animal life in the sea will be extinct. Large areas of coastline will have to be evacuated because of the stench of dead fish."\[41\]\[42\]
> 
> In a 1971 speech, he predicted that: "By the year 2000 the United Kingdom will be simply a small group of impoverished islands, inhabited by some 70 million hungry people." "If I were a gambler," Professor Ehrlich concluded before boarding an airplane, "I would take even money that England will not exist in the year 2000."\[41\]\[42\]
> 
> When this scenario did not occur, he responded that "When you predict the future, you get things wrong. How wrong is another question. I would have lost if I had had taken the bet. > 
> Ehrlich wrote in The Population Bomb that, "India couldn't possibly feed two hundred million more people by 1980."\[27\] In 1967, Ehrlich called to cut off emergency food aid to India as "hopeless".\[43\] This position was later criticized, as India's food production subsequently skyrocketed through the Green Revolution in India, and its per capita caloric intake rose significantly in the following decades, even as its population doubled.\[44\]
> 
> A large increase in global food production since the 1960s and a slowing of population growth have, within the current context of continued depletion of non-renewable resources, averted the scale of food shortage, famine and catastrophe foretold by the Ehrlichs.
> 
> Canadian journalist Dan Gardner, in his 2010 book Future Babble,\[45\] argues that Ehrlich has been insufficiently forthright in acknowledging errors he made, while being intellectually dishonest or evasive in taking credit for things he claims he got "right". For example, he rarely acknowledges the mistakes he made in predicting material shortages, massive death tolls from starvation (as many as one billion in the publication Age of Affluence) or regarding the disastrous effects on specific countries. Meanwhile, he is happy to claim credit for "predicting" the increase of AIDS or global warming.\[13\]
> 
> In the case of disease, Ehrlich had predicted the increase of a disease based on overcrowding, or the weakened immune systems of starving people, so it is "a stretch to see this as forecasting the emergence of AIDS in the 1980s." Similarly, global warming was one of the scenarios that Ehrlich described, so claiming credit for it, while disavowing responsibility for failed scenarios is a double standard. Gardner believes that Ehrlich is displaying classical signs of cognitive dissonance, and that his failure to acknowledge obvious errors of his own judgement render his current thinking suspect.\[13\]
>

That point for me is 1972."\[46\] Gardner has criticized Ehrlich for endorsing the strategies proposed by William and Paul Paddock in their book Famine 1975!. They had proposed a system of "triage" that would end food aid to "hopeless" countries such as India and Egypt. In Population Bomb, Ehrlich suggests that "there is no rational choice except to adopt some form of the Paddocks' strategy as far as food distribution is concerned." Had this strategy been implemented for countries such as India and Egypt, which were reliant on food aid at that time, they would almost certainly have suffered famines.\[13\] Instead, both Egypt and India have greatly increased their food production and now feed much larger populations without reliance on food aid

Amazingly, following the series of incorrect predictions Ehrlich made in and after writing The Population Bomb in 1968, he followed this up with The Population Explosion in 1990 and has continued saying that we have global overpopulation that is causing or will cause a dire crisis unless we cut worldwide population. He has said the same thing this century and even this decade. It appears the only reason he's not saying that today is that he died earlier this year.

If I didn't look it up, I would've guessed that his recent position would be something like "well, I got some things wrong, but it was only due to actions that were inspired by my work that crisis was averted" or "while crisis was averted, it was a lucky roll of the dice and, in most universes, the agricultural advancements that staved off the mass starvation deaths I was predicting don't happen", not "just you wait, the crisis is happening now and I'm about to be proven right"; in 2015, referring to his incorrect 1968 book, he said "\[m\]y language would be even more apocalyptic today". That's the pattern we've seen from Zitron, but I wouldn't have guessed that the one person I looked up would've kept that up for 50 more years. Maybe we'll get 50 more years of Zitron predicting the end of AI progress.

### Some reactions to Zitron

In one of the quotes from Juho Snellman, above, Snellman says that he writes a large amount of [gish gallop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gish_gallop), which is a term for when someone floods you with so much cheap (as in cheap to produce) nonsense that no one would want to take the time to bother to refute it. In discussing one small part of Zitron's talk in detail, we spent more than 1000 words explaining why Zitron has an incorrect understanding of how corporations work and how Zitron got the reasoning wrong. Someone can read that and then say, "but you didn't address X" in the talk, which is true. When I first watched the talk, I actually closed the tab after 90 seconds because there was so much nonsense that it didn't seem worth the time to go any further. I could write 5k words on the first 90 seconds of the video. Because Zitron is just saying a bunch of nonsense, he can do that very cheaply and it would take 30-60 minutes to refute 90 seconds of his nonsense if I had all the facts at hand. With time to look up the exact right information, it would probably take double or triple the amount of time. When someone who has good judgement sees something like this, they tend to immediately write the person off. Just for example, I mentioned to a friend of mine that I'm writing this post and they said

> I was listening to this podcast with the guy and I couldn't get through it. My heart rate was going up because he would just say this false thing and then the interviewer, who was reasonable, would ask about it, "what about X?", and then we would just jump to another falsehood ...
> 
> ... before I ducked out, he talks about how LLMs haven't gotten a lot better over the past year, and the interviewer says people use them and they've definitely gotten a lot better in the past year, and Zitron denies it and says 'have they?', and the interviewer is just like, "yes..." At that point, I'm just like, why am I listening to this conversation?

We mostly discussed predictions and not incorrect statements about the past or present, but everything I've read or watched by Zitron is also full of things like this. Many people will look at something like this and decide the guy is a crank and stop paying attention. But many other people will look at something like this, see someone refute a set of things, and then say, "but you didn't refute X" and, in general, the person doing the refuting may respond to a couple of these, but they eventually give up because the gish gallop method has the same properties as an amplification DoS attack. It's very cheap to generate new nonsense, but it takes some effort to refute it. If you [watch this analysis of conspiracy theory reasoning by Natalie Wynn](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=teqkK0RLNkI), the style of reasoning and rhetorical techniques will seem oddly familiar because Zitron and Zitron's defenders use the same techinques.

BTW, I was curious what this interview was, so I put the above quote into ChatGPT and asked it to find the interview. It was able to identify an interview with the relevant exchange (it actually identified multiple, as this appears to be a common question and response pattern by Zitron) and the timestamp of each relevant statement in the interview ([the start of the general argument is here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lf5oqGOCRCM&t=5601s) and a ["have they" response is here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lf5oqGOCRCM&t=5888s). Prior to the "have they?" comment, the interviewer tries to establish a baseline that agents have improved in capability. Zitron denies that this has happened, and then when the interviewer notes that people who use these things for their jobs Zitron denies this with the "have they?" comment (he actually makes multiple contradictory statements in the sequence).

Another thing to note here is Zitron's extremely high level of stated confidence. Some that we noted were OpenAI's forecast that is "a statement so egregious that I am surprised it's not some kind of financial crime to say it out loud" (which they've achieved so far) and his claim that Google's forecast for Gemini users is "a number so unrealistic that someone at Google should have been fired, and that someone is Sundar Pichai" (they managed to exceed the forecast by 50% when Zitron's claim was that it would be completely absurd for them to reach the number at all).

I've made quite a few predictions, and quite a few of those predictions are wrong. When I'm really making a prediction, I attach a confidence level to the prediction just for my own sake, so I can look back at these things and see how well calibrated the predictions are. I have never been wrong about a prediction that has anywhere near the confidence Zitron gives to some of his predictions. Given the stated level of confidence, even a single incorrect prediction would be a sign of an extremely high degree of overconfidence. One should effectively never be wrong about a prediction delivered with that level of confidence but Zitron is routinely wrong about predictions he makes with what is rhetorically pretty much the highest possible degree of confidence.

A funny thing about Gemini hitting 500M users being "so unrealistic that someone at Google should have been fired, and that someone is Sundar Pichai" is that Zitron has also (incorrectly) said that Google doesn't know how to grow, and that as a result they're shoving AI everywhere. Dennis Snell pointed out that, if Zitron takes his own statement seriously, Google can make Gemini's user numbers go to any number it wants by doing the exact thing Zitron said they would do, sticking AI everywhere.

You can't actually take Zitron's statement about Google's lack of growth leading to AI desperation seriously and also take it seriously when he says that Sundar is committing some kind of gross malpractice by naming a number like 500M users. This is another thing that is immediately obvious on watching one of his talks or reading his writing. There are a bunch of disconnected statements that don't fit together, except insofar as they're statements about how AI companies and people and companies that are using AI are evil and bad. The actual numbers and logic of the statements are contradictory. It seems to be whatever comes to mind that can be used to paint the villains as evil. And, funnily enough, the 750M user number Gemini hit shows that both of Zitron's statements were incorrect. If Google were as desperate to juice the numbers as Zitron claimed, they could've easily gotten the number above 1B by sticking Gemini everywhere, and of course 750M > 500M.

The point at which I stopped the talk for the first time was

> a market obsessed with year-over-year revenue growth. And this progression was natural. It was horrible. You can blame Marc Andreessen. He's a horrible man. You can blame many horrible men. There are so many guys to be mad at the moment.

That last sentence really sums up Zitron's position. "There are so many guys to be mad at the moment". In this talk, he throws in this jab at Andreesen and blames Andreesen for Meta, Google, and Microsoft pursuing growth. In reality, if Marc Andreesen had never existed, Meta, Google, and Microsoft would almost certainly still be trying to grow so we of course cannot actually blame Andreesen for these companies trying to grow. There's just this thing that he says is bad, and in his usual style, he pulls some person and says they're the evil villain that's to blame for this, and then moves on to the next non sequitur.

### How can people take this seriously?

Because I'm a masochist, I actually went and read a bunch of Zitron discussions (I believe I read every major discussion on HN and lobsters, and a bunch of other ones as well) to see what people who take Zitron seriously are saying. One common defense was the one above, sure, you refuted some points, but you didn't cover X. A more common defense is to say, just in general, people attack Zitron because of Y (usually his style), but they never address his points, "which tells me everything I need to know" (or something along those same lines). Based on the timestamps of the messages, just scoping to the stories that were being discussed, there were generally already comments discussing Zitron's actual errors, but Zitron's defenders would ignore this and just claim that people were unable to point to mistakes Zitron had made. This is a very Zitronian move and it makes sense that people who like his style would also use this move. After all, who would find Zitron convincing? Someone who thinks this kind of thing is valid reasoning.

The next most common "move" was to simply deny that Zitron said something that was refuted. When people would mention that Zitron was repeatedly on the record in 2024 and 2025 as having said LLMs couldn't improve further for fundamental reasons, Zitron's defenders would say that he never said that, and likewise for previous predictions or factually incorrect statements.

Another class of defense I saw were comments like "but what about all the AI hypists who are wrong?". Like I said before, [I wrote a 34k word post about how a bunch of the most respected futurists have been wrong, not just because they made incorrect predictions, but their methods and reasoning were wrong](https://danluu.com/futurist-predictions/). But a bunch of people who hype the future being wrong doesn't make people like Ed Zitron or Paul Ehrlich any less wrong. Zitron and Ehrlich are still exactly as wrong as they would be if those futurists never existed.

A friend of mine also noted this about comments on cases where people point out that Zitron was wrong about models not improving from 2023 to 2026 (and yes, this is specifically on stories or comments that discuss Zitron's disproven statements on capabilities not improving):

> It's incredible to see so many people saying, "Zitron isn't wrong, he's just early!" I guess the implication is that we'll eventually realize that the models we have in 2026 are actually no better than the ones we had in 2024 or ??

An interesting thing about publshing this post is that a decent fraction of the people who've message me to tell me that I'm wrong say that I'm wrong because, today in 2026, models haven't actually models haven't gotten better since 2023 or 2024. My guess would be that most people who are saying things like the quote above are just doing the "move" where you don't read what was actually said and respond with a canned response that's nonsensical to anyone who's actually read what they're replying to, but it turns out there are plenty of people who actually believe Zitron's string of statements that imply models haven't improved since 2023 or 2024.

If someone's actually looked at what's happening, I don't think there's anything you can really do to convince someone who's denying reality at that level, but for anyone who's just hasn't seen how things have changed, for a visual example of improvements over that time period, [here's a comparison of 2023 and 2025 video generation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZBOgtTCfnGs) and [here's an example from August 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TudmZN3h8_s). Video isn't a great example since models have improved a lot more at coding, e.g., [with on the order of minutes of human time, it's possible to create a new regex engine with an interpreter and an native code compiler and then fork ripgrep to make it faster for codex's actual ripgrep calls on my machine](https://danluu.com/no-slow/), a project that would probably cost 7 figures pre-LLM if you price out how much people with the expertise for that are paid. But video is a nice example because, in the interview linked above, after the "have they?" exchange, at one point Zitron's "rebuttal" is, "you wouldn't make _movie_ with it would you?". People are "shooting" quite a bit of AI-generated digital footage now and this is upending a lot of lower end video work. An easy prediction based on historical patterns is that this will continue to move upmarket over time, but just based on what people are using AI video for today, Zitron should probably find a new rebuttal even if he's just playing to true believers who don't think models have improved since 2023 or 2024. Of course that topic can't be related to math or the sciences, where improvements have been very rapid, and the same goes for numerous other fields.

### Future predictions

Although Zitron's past predictions have generally been wrong, maybe he'll be right about something in the future. Perhaps some of these companies will have valuations decline for some reason. But, even if there's some kind of massive AI crash and OpenAI and Anthropic go to zero, in terms of the societal impact, if on top of that, some other event occurs that prevents further progress in models beyond whatever AI labs have internally right now, that's still going to result in a fair amount of change. Which companies are successful will change who gets rich, but particular companies failing won't stop changes that fall out of current or next generation model capabilities from happening; it just moves around who benefits the most.

Personally, it doesn't matter to me if folks at one company vs. another get rich. If one company does something better (in some abstract sense) than another, that's of some interest to me, but I have some skepticism about any particular company's claims that they'll do more of "the right thing" than another company (I could be convinced on this one, but I don't find the public claims that I know of very convincing).

If Zitron ends up being right about some company or other collapsing, that's pretty uninteresting to me compared to how capabilities have developed and will develop, where he's been wrong to date. It also happens that he's been wrong about the financial predictions he's made to date and the reasoning for those is flawed as well, but that doesn't really interest me, though I included a number of financial predictions for completeness.

It's not obvious from the post if the redditor who reviewed Zitron's predictions was pulling predictions in an unbiased fashion or if they were biased in some way (since AI has become a culture war issue, it wouldn't be surprising if someone pulled biased predictions), so I decided to read some Zitron in my spare time while poking at agents to get them to do an unrelated task I wanted them to do. For the futurist post, I read multiple entire books to pull predictions and generally only stopped when someone was being repetitive and kept saying the same thing over and over again. In this case, all Zitron does is be repetitive, so the methodology in the futurist review would mean that I review a few predictions and then stop immediately. To overcome this, I had ChatGPT give me a list of predictions (with no attempted tilt towards correct or incorrect predictions) and then I skimmed/read the posts that ChatGPT linked to. There were some cases where I thought ChatGPT's reading of the post was incorrect (these were generally cases where it flagged a prediction that would be incorrect if its reading was correct, but I disagreed with its reading) and (discussed further below) I also removed predictions which weren't falsifiable or seemed pointless because they were tautological (I noted something similar to this in the futurist post).

If I really thought about it, I probably could've found something better to do with the time, but here we are; I sometimes have tasks on my todo list for when I'm too tired to do real work, but I didn't have one. I don't think they cherry picked particularly bad predictions, although they did pick some that are among the more absurd sounding. However, if you go and look into the details of ones that aren't such ironclad "dunks" (like saying that Gemini hitting 500M by EOY users is so absurd Sundar should be fired for the idea, when Gemini actually hit 750M by EOY), these are just as wrong as claims that Cursor has no realistic buyer with the implication they won't even sell for $10B when "everybody" (who cares about AI exits) knows they sold for $60B.

The redditor picked the high-profile failed predictions, but Zitron's prediction corpus has many more failures and, as noted above, the bigger issue is his reasoning.

Another thing about the reddit comment is, whether or not the comment is unbiased, one might have the suspicion of a kind of bias because it was posted to r/accelerate by someone who apparently is an r/accelerate believer. On looking at the actual predictions they are consistent with some bias (they would also be consistent with an honest mistake as there's no way to distinguish these from the record). For example, one of the "refutations" is a statement by Zitron that OpenAI will collapse in 12-24 months. OpenAI didn't collapse, so this would appear on the surface to be a great way to show that Zitron was wrong, but if you read Zitron's post, Zitron's actual claim was that OpenAI will either collapse or raise a lot more money and they raised a lot more money. I disagree with Zitron's implications that this is inevitable just leading to a later collapse but his stated prediction was not falsified.

This prediction wasn't in the set of predictions scored in this post. Some would argue that this should be scored in the post. The reason this wasn't scored is because the prediction seems meaningless except insofar as it contributes to Zitron's broader point (that OpenAI is doomed and must collapse).

If we think about predictions one could make, a tautological prediction (if you write out all the edge cases I'll elide for space reasons) that has to be true is OpenAI has enough money to operate or it doesn't, and if it doesn't, it must raise the money somehow. I could make a million such tautological predictions, but if one were scoring my prediction record, it wouldn't make sense to include these because they're meaningless. In general, a company that's alive will cover its costs. If it does not, it will try to raise money. If it fails to do that, it will shut down or get acquired. A prediction that a company will either cover its costs or it will not cover its costs says nothing.

OpenAI's own projections were that it would not yet be profitable and its costs would exceed its revenue. That seemed nearly certain, so if you assume that this nearly certain thing is true, then you have the nearly tautological prediction that OpenAI will either collapse or it will raise money to cover its costs. It would have been reasonable to make a prediction like this at very high confidence (99.9% or above). If you use any kind of prediction scoring methodology, such as [Brier score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brier_score), these predictions contribute essentially nothing except when they're wrong as long as Zitron has a significant number of high-confidence incorrect predictions.

And, as we noted above, Zitron is repeatedly incorrect on predictions he gives the highest possible confidence (given his wording, I would rate a number of these at 6 9s or above), so on any kind of scoring mechanism like Brier score, Zitron's record is very poor. And a summary metric like this really understates how meaningless predictions like this are. Hypothetically, let's say Zitron made an unbounded number of correct 99.99% certainty near tautological predictions, which would make the score from the bounded number of other predictions he made meaningless on something like Brier score. This would still give you zero confidence for any of his non-near tautological predictions, and those are the predictions people generally talk about (AI progress is done, AI companies must collapse and this will bring down major tech companies as well, etc.).

Back the topic of the reddit commenter's potential bias vs. mine, as noted above, I don't have a particular bias towards a view that rapid progress is inevitible and have called out cases where people are overly optimistic, as evidenced by this post on [futurist predictions](https://danluu.com/zitron/\(/futurist-predictions/\)). I'm also [not someome who needs to or has any desire to farm engagement by manufacturing reasons that someone is wrong or bad](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rlcd5m4FY-o) and don't consistently rate every predictor as bad, as evidenced by [this review of Steve Yegge's prediction record](https://danluu.com/yegge-predictions/), in which I note that he scored well and also actually performed much better than the raw score indicated because the predictions are generally well reasoned and directionally correct even if the precise prediction was incorrect. I think it's actually awesome if someone has good insight in the future and shares it publicly, so I'm happy to call these cases out when I noticed them. It's just that, in this case, Zitron is a kind of anti-Yegge: someone with a poor prediction record whose predictions are actually worse than they seem from the record alone.
