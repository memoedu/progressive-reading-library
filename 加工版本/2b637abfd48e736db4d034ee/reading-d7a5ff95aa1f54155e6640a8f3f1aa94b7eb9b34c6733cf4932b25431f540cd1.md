# The Future of Assessment Is a Thousand Years Old

## 阅读前置卡

- 价值判断：高。文章提出 AI 可能把大学传统的口试式评估扩展给更多学生，并说明其对学习、认证和教师职责的意义，也列出尚待研究验证的问题。
- 信息密度判断：高。全文围绕评估方式的历史、探究式评估、AI 委员会的运作设想、学习与认证及研究议程展开，包含多学科例子和具体研究问题。
- 是否继续阅读：3 全文。文章的核心论证由历史背景、评估设计、实施边界和研究问题逐步展开，阅读全文有助于理解其设想及尚未验证之处。

## 核心洞察

一、AI引发教育危机感：人工智能已使许多人认为，现代教育正面临最严重的危机之一。
[[#^pr-8d8e6325ef93|正文跳转]]

二、AI引发评估危机：当学生能够借助 AI 流畅地完成论文、编程作业、文学分析和考试题目，大学教师便面临一个核心难题：如何判断学生是否真正理解所提交的答案。
[[#^pr-6d759229531f|正文跳转]]

三、评估支撑学位认证：大学评估是其认证职能的基础：学位不仅代表课程完成，也向公众表明毕业生具备相应职业或学术领域所需的知识与判断力。若评估失去可信度，认证价值也会随之减弱。
[[#^pr-8f708ee6d7bb|正文跳转]]

四、人工智能或扩大深度评估：人工智能确实扰动了一种评估方式，但也可能使大学把历史悠久且受信任的评估形式带给每位学习者。看似威胁学术诚信的技术，也可能成为恢复学术诚信的技术。
[[#^pr-a1d84d5a2044|正文跳转]]

五、教育技术史的三重交织：教育技术的历史同时也是评估与教学的历史，三者的发展彼此关联。
[[#^pr-fdef0b5a57ff|正文跳转]]

六、评估形式受规模制约：在计算机出现以前，教师评估论文、实验记录、演讲、设计项目、辩论和口试等丰富且贴近真实表现的成果，经验丰富的教育者能够据此判断推理、沟通、创造力与判断力。限制在于规模：学生人数增长后，大学越来越依赖技术能够支持的评估形式。
[[#^pr-f01930def233|正文跳转]]

七、计算机推动选择题普及：计算机使大规模评分切实可行，改变了教育评估。选择题考试广泛普及，并非因为教育者认为它代表学习的最高形式，而是因为计算机能够可靠判断是否选中了正确选项。
[[#^pr-1a0f709a19ff|正文跳转]]

八、技术塑造评估方式：评估围绕技术能够测量的内容形成；书面考试和客观测试，是教育质量与机构规模之间的一种务实折中。
[[#^pr-f5bc223f04dc|正文跳转]]

九、从答案转向探究：AI改变了既有折中：技术不再只是检查答案，也能参与有意义的对话，因此评估可以从答案延伸到探究本身。
[[#^pr-5308b300d9e3|正文跳转]]

十、传统阅读理解流程：传统阅读理解练习中，学生阅读一段材料，再回答教师预先准备的问题。
[[#^pr-ef04dc24b0dd|正文跳转]]

十一、以提问构建理解：阅读理解活动可以倒转流程：学生从文章出发，与机构的AI平台互动并提出自己的问题；目标由给出预设答案转为建构理解。
[[#^pr-722d7f1edcea|正文跳转]]

十二、观察探究如何发展：评估可观察探究过程是否连贯推进：问题是否自然承接先前回应，学生是否识别矛盾、逐步精炼假设，以及调查是否逐渐汇聚到可辩护的结论。
[[#^pr-aa732616f889|正文跳转]]

十三、探究过程也能证明理解：探究的推进轨迹本身可成为理解的证据。相较于只奖励回忆，这种评估还奖励好奇心、分析、综合与反思；学生不仅从答案中学习，也通过提出越来越有意义的问题来学习。这种模式具有教育价值，并可能改变作业、案例研究、阅读任务和项目式学习。
[[#^pr-f2ff4a358a47|正文跳转]]

十四、探究式认证仍有漏洞：在高风险认证中，探究式评估仍容易受到预先准备的影响：学生可能事先咨询另一个 AI 系统，并带着精心设计的问题序列参加。因此，探究仍有价值，但尚未成为大学的黄金标准。
[[#^pr-f4454e62b998|正文跳转]]

十五、口头追问揭示书面考试之外的理解：大学做出最重要的学术决定时，很少只依赖书面材料。博士候选人要在委员会面前答辩论文，教职申请者要作研究报告并接受提问，医学专科人员要完成口试，建筑专业学生要在评审团面前答辩设计，研究生则要答辩研究计划。这些对话影响职业发展；大学信任它们，因为追问中展现的理解，是书面考试无法揭示的。
[[#^pr-4480a467531d|正文跳转]]

十六、口试观察超越答案正误：评估者不仅判断答案是否正确，也会观察候选人能否解释假设、连接观点、应对问题变化、识别不确定性、从错误中恢复、捍卫立场而不陷入防御，以及承认自身所知有限。
[[#^pr-150d6857f32b|正文跳转]]

十七、理解可从思维互动中显现：即使不属于相关学科，观察者也往往能辨认谁真正理解材料；关键不在于跟上每个技术细节，而在于观察其智识投入如何展开。
[[#^pr-3d7fc016867d|正文跳转]]

十八、对话是评估理解的标杆：对话始终是评估的黄金标准；其局限在于难以规模化，而材料认为这一局限可能正在改变。
[[#^pr-15883fbf2ab7|正文跳转]]

十九、扩大口试不等于削减投入：规模化的目标不是取代教师或减少教育投入，而是让更多学生获得内容丰富的评估形式。口试因能通过对话揭示理解而受到重视，其限制在于实践可行性而非教育质量；AI可能使这种真实性得以保留并广泛提供。
[[#^pr-6519b04fa01d|正文跳转]]

二十、AI可成为学术委员会：AI 除了担任导师、助教或评分系统，也可能成为大学长期需要却无力负担的学术委员会：通过对话追问学生的假设、理由与结论，动态改变条件并探索其他解释。
[[#^pr-619f2133ccda|正文跳转]]

二十一、评估思考过程而非回忆答案：自适应考试不再只判断学生能否复述答案，而是观察其如何思考；对话中的每个回答影响后续问题，评估方式由此类似于博士论文委员会的探究。
[[#^pr-d68f2f734f79|正文跳转]]

二十二、追踪每名学生理解的边界：评估目标是发现每名学生理解的前沿，而不是让所有人回答同一套问题。已展现掌握的学生可以接受更复杂的挑战；其他学生则可能需要不同的问题序列来显现理解。这种自适应对话可能比许多现行标准化笔试更个性化。
[[#^pr-2c32925dfcda|正文跳转]]

二十三、对话贯通不同学科评估：同一框架可用于不同学科：编程学生为软件架构答辩，文学学生解释改写理由，历史学生为相互竞争的史料解释提供依据，经济学学生则在假设改变后修订结论。其共同原则是通过对话显现理解。
[[#^pr-cb1dfcefc60e|正文跳转]]

二十四、AI委员会让评估融入学习：学生可像使用题库练习一样借助 AI 委员会练习；随着理解加深，委员会的挑战程度也会提高。模拟口试因此有望从博士生扩展到每一位学习者，使评估与学习相互促进。
[[#^pr-714c4ed0ba89|正文跳转]]

25、教师判断扩展而非被取代：AI 的作用是把专家判断带给更多学习者，而非自动化判断或取代教师。教师仍负责课程设计、学生指导、标准制定和评估验证，并继续承担教学、激励、实验室督导与智力发展指导等职责；AI 扩展其影响力，而不是削弱其角色。
[[#^pr-9bf194cb3e16|正文跳转]]

26、教育与认证并行强化：大学承担教育与认证两项不同责任，AI 委员会被视为能够同时加强这两项职责的工具。
[[#^pr-edd212f96c4a|正文跳转]]

27、AI 评估的关键在于经验检验：AI 委员会是否会普及，最终是一个经验问题。研究需检验其评价能否预测教师判断、对话模式能否区分真实理解与记忆、系统能否识别理解边界的变化，以及提问方式和对话行为是否与深层掌握或未来职业成功相关；还需探讨如何评价从错误中恢复，以及是否应纳入口头回答、信心或肢体语言。
[[#^pr-e679796fcdea|正文跳转]]

28、评估问题也是教育问题：AI 评估所涉及的议题不仅是工程问题，也关系到教育评估本身将如何发展。
[[#^pr-196b2bfb3f4e|正文跳转]]

29、口试的限制曾是规模：几个世纪以来，大学把最丰富、最真实的评估形式留给博士候选人、教师招聘候选人、医学专家和研究生答辩等少数人。口试未必更好，而是难以规模化，这限制了它的广泛使用。
[[#^pr-5b1525699eee|正文跳转]]

30、AI 或成评估信任的转机：AI 既能生成论文，也可能进行有深度的对话。它曾被视为威胁大学评估的技术，而历史或许会留下讽刺性的记忆：看似削弱评估信任的技术，最终可能成为社会重新信任评估的原因。
[[#^pr-05df2cde1491|正文跳转]]

31、AI与古老考试：人工智能可能不会取代大学考试，而是或许能让每名学生都参加大学最古老的考试，从而使这类考试恢复。
[[#^pr-d84387af535b|正文跳转]]

32、创新让传统惠及更多人：有时，变革性创新并非抛弃传统，而是让最古老的理念终于能够惠及所有人。

## The Future of Assessment Is a Thousand Years Old

_How Artificial Intelligence May Democratize the University's Most Trusted Form of Evaluation_

![学生正在使用 AI 辅助完成 coursework。](https://www.buffalo.edu/content/shared/www/research/venu-essays/the-future-of-assessment-is-a-thousand-years-old/jcr:content/par/image_copy.img.680.auto.jpg/1786131094140.jpg)

### Venu Govindaraju

Artificial intelligence 已造成许多人认为是 modern education 中最严重的 crisis。

^pr-8d8e6325ef93

如今，Students 可以流畅地 generate essays、solve programming assignments、analyze literature 并 answer examination questions。各所 universities 的 faculty 都在焦虑地问同一个问题：_如果 AI 能 produce the answers，我们怎么知道 students 真正 understand 什么？_

^pr-6d759229531f

这种担忧可以理解。Assessment 并非 university 的边缘活动，而是它的 foundation。Society 信任 universities，是因为它们 certify understanding。A degree 不只是已完成 courses 的集合；它还向公众宣告，graduate 拥有 physician、engineer、scientist、teacher 或 scholar 所应具备的 knowledge 和 judgment。

^pr-8f708ee6d7bb

如果 assessment 失去 credibility，那么 certification 的价值就会 diminished。

因此，大多数 discussions 都从这样的 assumption 开始：AI 已经破坏了 assessment。我认为，最终可能证明事实恰恰相反。

Artificial intelligence 确实 disrupt 了一种 assessment，但它也使 universities 能够做到以前从未做到的事情。它让我们能够把 university 最古老、最受信任的 evaluation 形式带给每一位 learner。

^pr-a1d84d5a2044

具有讽刺意味的是，看似威胁 academic integrity 的 technology，可能会成为恢复它的 technology。

## Every Generation Assessed What Technology Allowed

educational technology 的历史，既是 assessment 的历史，也是 teaching 的历史。

^pr-fdef0b5a57ff

在 computers 出现以前，teachers 会 evaluate essays、laboratory notebooks、presentations、design projects、debates 和 oral examinations。这些 assessments 内容丰富且 authentic，因为有经验的 educators 能够 evaluate reasoning、communication、creativity 和 judgment。

^pr-f01930def233

限制在于 scale。随着 student populations 增长，universities 越来越依赖 technology 能够支持的 assessment 形式。

Computers 让 large-scale grading 变得切实可行，从而 transform 了 educational assessment。Multiple-choice examinations 变得无处不在，并非因为 educators 认为它们代表 learning 的最高形式，而是因为 computers 能够可靠地 determine 是否选中了 correct option。

^pr-1a0f709a19ff

Technology 围绕 technology 能够 measure 的内容来 shape assessment。Written examinations 和 objective tests 成为 educational quality 与 institutional scale 之间务实的 compromise。

^pr-f5bc223f04dc

Artificial intelligence 改变了这种 compromise。Technology 首次不只是能够 check answers，还能够参与有意义的 dialogue。这一区别改变了一切。

^pr-5308b300d9e3

## Assessment Through Inquiry

AI 的一个 consequence 已经逐渐显现。我们不再只 evaluate answers，而是可以 evaluate inquiry 本身。

设想一道 reading comprehension exercise。传统上，students 阅读一段 passage，然后回答 instructor 准备的问题。

^pr-ef04dc24b0dd

假设我们把这个过程倒过来。Students 从这段 passage 开始，同时与 institutional AI platform 互动并 formulate 自己的问题。

^pr-722d7f1edcea

目标不再是 produce predetermined answers，而是 construct understanding。Assessment 会 observe inquiry 如何 develop。每个 question 是否都自然地从上一个 response 中产生？Student 是否 recognize contradictions？Assumptions 是否变得更加 refined？Investigation 是否逐渐 converge 到一个 defensible conclusion？

^pr-aa732616f889

Inquiry 的 trajectory 成为 understanding 的 evidence。与其只 reward recall，这种 approach 还会 reward curiosity、analysis、synthesis 和 reflection。

^pr-f2ff4a358a47

Students 不仅从 answers 中 learn，也会通过学习如何提出越来越有意义的问题而 learn。这种 model 具有重要的 educational value，并可能 transform homework、case studies、reading assignments 和 project-based learning。

然而，在 high-stakes certification 中，它仍然容易受到影响。Students 可能事先 consult another AI system，并带着精心准备好的 prompts sequence 到场。Inquiry 仍然有价值，但它还不是 university 的 gold standard。

^pr-f4454e62b998

## The Gold Standard Was Never the Written Examination

当 universities 做出最重要的 academic decisions 时，它们很少只依赖 written work。

Doctoral candidates 会在 committees 面前 defend dissertations。Faculty applicants 会 present research seminars，随后接受 questioning。Medical specialists 会完成 oral board examinations。Architecture students 会在 juries 面前 defend designs。Graduate students 会 defend proposals。

^pr-4480a467531d

这些 conversations 决定 careers。Universities 信任它们，因为它们能 reveal written examinations 无法揭示的东西：在 questioning 中展现的 understanding。

Committee members 当然会 evaluate answers 是否 correct。但与此同时，他们也在 observe 丰富得多的东西。Candidate 能否 explain assumptions？他们能否 connect ideas？当 problem 改变时，他们能否 respond？他们能否 recognize uncertainty？能否从 mistakes 中 recover？能否 defend a position 而不变得 defensive？能否 acknowledge 自己不知道的内容？

^pr-150d6857f32b

令人惊讶的是，即使是 discipline 之外的 observers，往往也能 recognize 谁真正 understands the material。这不是因为他们能 follow 每个 technical detail，而是因为他们 observe intellectual engagement 的 dynamics。

^pr-3d7fc016867d

Gold standard 从来不是 written examination，而始终是 conversation。它唯一的 limitation 是 conversations 无法 scale。直到现在。

^pr-15883fbf2ab7

准确说明这里的“scale”是什么意思很重要。目标不是 replace faculty 或 reduce 对 education 的 investment，恰恰相反。目标是把 university 内容最丰富的 assessment 形式扩展给更多 students。Oral examinations 一直受到重视，因为它们能通过 dialogue reveal understanding。它们的 limitation 从来不是 educational quality，而只是 practicality。AI 让我们有可能保留这种 authenticity，同时使其广泛 accessible。

^pr-6519b04fa01d

## The AI Committee

Artificial intelligence 通常被描述为 tutor、teaching assistant 或 grading system。也许更有意思的可能性是，AI 成为大学一直需要、却始终负担不起的东西：academic committee。

^pr-619f2133ccda

设想一名学生参加 Physics 101 assessment。AI 不会提出固定的问题序列，而是进行一场 conversation。每个 response 都会影响下一个 question。committee 探究 assumptions，提出意想不到的 scenarios，要求 justification，质疑 conclusions，改变 conditions，探索 alternative explanations。

examination 变得 adaptive。目标不再是确定学生是否能 recall 一个 answer，而是观察学生如何思考。正如 dissertation committees 几代以来所做的那样。

^pr-d68f2f734f79

目标不是向每名学生提出相同的问题，而是发现每名学生 understanding 的 frontier。一名学生可能很快 demonstrate mastery，随后面对越来越 sophisticated problems 的挑战。另一名学生可能需要不同的问题序列，才能让其 understanding 显现出来。从这个意义上说，adaptive dialogue 可能比当今许多 standardized written examinations 更 individualized。

^pr-2c32925dfcda

同一 framework 自然延伸到各个 disciplines。programming 学生为 software architecture 答辩。literature 学生解释为什么应该改写一段文字。historian 为关于 historical evidence 的相互竞争的 interpretations 提供 justification。economics 学生在 assumptions 改变后修订 conclusions。

^pr-cb1dfcefc60e

不同的 disciplines，同一个 educational principle。Understanding 通过 dialogue 显现。

## Learning and Certification

这种 approach 的妙处在于，assessment 与 learning 相互促进。学生会像现在练习 question banks 一样，使用 AI committees 进行练习。

随着 understanding 增长，committee 会变得越来越 challenging。每一位 learner 都能参加 mock oral examinations，而不再仅限于 doctoral candidates。

^pr-714c4ed0ba89

Faculty 仍然是 education 的核心。他们继续 teach、mentor、inspire、supervise laboratories，并指导 intellectual development。

Artificial intelligence 不会取代 teachers，而是将他们最有价值的 practices 之一扩展到此前不可能实现的规模。

目的不是 automate judgment，而是让 expert judgment 更广泛地可及。Faculty 仍负责 designing curricula、mentoring students、defining standards 和 validating assessment。AI 扩展他们的影响力，不会削弱他们的 role。

^pr-9bf194cb3e16

大学一直承担着两项 distinct responsibilities：educate 和 certify。AI committees 会加强这两项职责。

^pr-edd212f96c4a

## A New Research Agenda

AI committees 是否会变得 commonplace，归根结底是一个 empirical question。

-   AI evaluations 能否 predict faculty judgments？ ^pr-e679796fcdea
-   dialogue 中的哪些 patterns 能区分 genuine understanding 与 memorization？
-   应如何奖励从 error 中恢复？ 
-   AI committees 能否像 experienced faculty 一样可靠地识别学生 understanding 不断变化的 boundary？
-   哪些 patterns of questioning 能区分 deep conceptual mastery 与 rehearsed performance？
-   哪些 conversational behaviors 与日后的 professional success 相关？
-   未来的 systems 是否应纳入 spoken responses、confidence 或 body language？

这些不只是 engineering questions，也是关于 educational assessment 本身未来的问题。

#### Insights: A series of essays by Venu Govindaraju

## The Future of Assessment Is a Thousand Years Old

_How Artificial Intelligence May Democratize the University's Most Trusted Form of Evaluation_

![Student working on laptop using AI to assist with coursework. ](https://www.buffalo.edu/content/shared/www/research/venu-essays/the-future-of-assessment-is-a-thousand-years-old/jcr:content/par/image_copy.img.680.auto.jpg/1786131094140.jpg)

### Venu Govindaraju

Artificial intelligence has created what many believe is the greatest crisis in modern education.

Students can now generate essays, solve programming assignments, analyze literature and answer examination questions with remarkable fluency. Across universities, faculty ask the same anxious question: _If AI can produce the answers, how can we know what students truly understand?_

The concern is understandable. Assessment is not a peripheral activity of a university. It is its foundation. Society trusts universities because they certify understanding. A degree is more than a collection of completed courses; it is a public declaration that a graduate possesses the knowledge and judgment expected of a physician, engineer, scientist, teacher or scholar.

If assessment loses its credibility, the value of that certification is diminished.

Most discussions, therefore, begin with the assumption that AI has broken assessment. I believe the opposite may ultimately prove true.

Artificial intelligence has certainly disrupted one form of assessment, but it also enables something universities have never been able to do before. It allows us to bring the university's oldest and most trusted form of evaluation to every learner.

Ironically, the technology that appears to threaten academic integrity may become the technology that restores it.

## Every Generation Assessed What Technology Allowed

The history of educational technology is as much the history of assessment as it is the history of teaching.

Before computers, teachers evaluated essays, laboratory notebooks, presentations, design projects, debates and oral examinations. These assessments were rich and authentic because experienced educators could evaluate reasoning, communication, creativity and judgment.

The limitation was scale. As student populations grew, universities increasingly relied upon forms of assessment that technology could support.

Computers transformed educational assessment by making large-scale grading practical. Multiple-choice examinations became ubiquitous, not because educators believed they represented the highest form of learning, but because computers could reliably determine whether the correct option had been selected.

Technology shaped assessment around what technology could measure. Written examinations and objective tests became the practical compromise between educational quality and institutional scale.

Artificial intelligence changes that compromise. For the first time, technology is capable not merely of checking answers, but of participating in meaningful dialogue. That distinction changes everything.

## Assessment Through Inquiry

One consequence of AI is already becoming apparent. Instead of evaluating only answers, we can evaluate inquiry itself.

Imagine a reading comprehension exercise. Traditionally, students read a passage and answer questions prepared by the instructor.

Suppose we reverse the process. Students begin with the passage but formulate their own questions while interacting with an institutional AI platform.

The objective is no longer to produce predetermined answers. It is to construct understanding. The assessment observes how inquiry develops. Does each question emerge naturally from the previous response? Does the student recognize contradictions? Do assumptions become more refined? Does the investigation gradually converge toward a defensible conclusion?

The trajectory of inquiry becomes evidence of understanding. Rather than rewarding recall alone, this approach rewards curiosity, analysis, synthesis and reflection.

Students learn not only from answers but from learning how to ask increasingly meaningful questions. This model has significant educational value and may transform homework, case studies, reading assignments and project-based learning.

Yet it remains vulnerable for high-stakes certification. Students may consult another AI system beforehand and arrive with a carefully prepared sequence of prompts. The inquiry remains valuable. But it is not yet the university's gold standard.

## The Gold Standard Was Never the Written Examination

When universities make their most consequential academic decisions, they rarely rely upon written work alone.

Doctoral candidates defend dissertations before committees. Faculty applicants present research seminars followed by questioning. Medical specialists complete oral board examinations. Architecture students defend designs before juries. Graduate students defend proposals.

These conversations determine careers. Universities trust them because they reveal something that written examinations cannot. Understanding under questioning.

Committee members certainly evaluate whether answers are correct. But they are simultaneously observing something much richer. Can the candidate explain assumptions? Can they connect ideas? Can they respond when the problem changes? Can they recognize uncertainty? Can they recover from mistakes? Can they defend a position without becoming defensive? Can they acknowledge when they do not know?

Remarkably, even observers outside the discipline often recognize who genuinely understands the material. Not because they follow every technical detail. But because they observe the dynamics of intellectual engagement.

The gold standard has never been the written examination. It has always been the conversation. Its only limitation has been that conversations do not scale. Until now.

It is important to be precise about what "scale" means here. The objective is not to replace faculty or reduce investment in education. Quite the opposite. The objective is to extend the university's richest form of assessment to many more students. Oral examinations have always been valued because they reveal understanding through dialogue. Their limitation has never been educational quality. It has simply been practicality. AI offers the possibility of preserving that authenticity while making it broadly accessible.

## The AI Committee

Artificial intelligence is usually described as a tutor, a teaching assistant or a grading system. Perhaps the more interesting possibility is that AI becomes something universities have always needed but could never afford. An academic committee.

Imagine a student entering a Physics 101 assessment. The AI does not ask a fixed sequence of questions. Instead, it conducts a conversation. Each response influences the next question. The committee probes assumptions. Introduces unexpected scenarios. Requests justification. Challenges conclusions. Changes conditions. Explores alternative explanations.

The examination becomes adaptive. The objective is no longer to determine whether a student can recall an answer. It is to observe how the student thinks. Exactly as dissertation committees have done for generations.

The objective is not to ask every student the same questions. It is to discover the frontier of each student's understanding. One student may demonstrate mastery quickly and be challenged with increasingly sophisticated problems. Another may require a different sequence of questions before their understanding becomes visible. In that sense, adaptive dialogue may become more individualized than many of today's standardized written examinations.

The same framework extends naturally across disciplines. A programming student defends software architecture. A literature student explains why a paragraph should be rewritten. A historian justifies competing interpretations of historical evidence. An economics student revises conclusions after assumptions change.

Different disciplines. One educational principle. Understanding becomes visible through dialogue.

## Learning and Certification

The beauty of this approach is that assessment and learning reinforce one another. Students would practice with AI committees just as they currently practice with question banks.

The committee becomes increasingly challenging as understanding grows. Mock oral examinations become available to every learner rather than only to doctoral candidates.

Faculty remain central to education. They continue to teach, mentor, inspire, supervise laboratories and guide intellectual development.

Artificial intelligence does not replace teachers. It extends one of their most valuable practices to a scale that was previously impossible.

The purpose is not to automate judgment. It is to make expert judgment available more widely. Faculty remain responsible for designing curricula, mentoring students, defining standards and validating assessment. AI extends their reach. It does not diminish their role.

Universities have always performed two distinct responsibilities. They educate. And they certify. AI committees strengthen both.

## A New Research Agenda

Whether AI committees become commonplace is ultimately an empirical question.

-   Can AI evaluations predict faculty judgments?
-   What patterns of dialogue distinguish genuine understanding from memorization?
-   How should recovery from error be rewarded?  
-   Can AI committees identify the evolving boundary of a student's understanding as reliably as experienced faculty?
-   Which patterns of questioning distinguish deep conceptual mastery from rehearsed performance?
-   Which conversational behaviors correlate with later professional success?
-   Should future systems incorporate spoken responses, confidence or body language?

These are not engineering questions alone. They are questions about the future of educational assessment itself.

## The Great Irony

For the past several years, artificial intelligence has been portrayed as the technology that threatens university assessment. History may remember it ironically.

For centuries, universities reserved their richest and most authentic form of evaluation for a privileged few: doctoral candidates, faculty recruits, medical specialists and graduate defenses. Not because oral examinations were better. But because they were impossible to scale.

Artificial intelligence changes that equation. The same technology that can generate essays may also conduct thoughtful dialogue. The same technology that appeared to undermine trust in assessment may ultimately become the reason society trusts it again.

Artificial intelligence may not replace the university examination. It may restore the university's oldest examination by making it available to every student.

Sometimes the most transformative innovations are not those that abandon tradition. They are those that finally allow our oldest ideas to reach everyone.
