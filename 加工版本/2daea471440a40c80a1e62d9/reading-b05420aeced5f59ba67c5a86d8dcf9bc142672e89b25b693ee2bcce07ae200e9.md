## 核心洞察

一、王沪宁被定位为'隐性统治者'：文章认为王沪宁作为政治顾问，在决定重大政治问题上行使了前所未有的权威，其建言对政治的影响不亚于最重要的政治领导人，因此可被称为'hidden ruler'。这种影响力并非仅归因于个人能力，更源于迅速变化的政治经济条件、中共面临的合法性挑战以及围绕中国未来方向的争论等独特境况。

二、三位领导人意识形态的幕后策划者：中国媒体将王沪宁誉为他所服务的三位领导人的主要意识形态旗帜的幕后策划者，包括江泽民的'三个代表'、胡锦涛的'科学发展观'和习近平的'中国梦'。文章认为他具备构想此类表述的能力，且每项倡议的成功为其晋升提供资历，进而赋予其设计新倡议的权威，因此极有可能确实制定并发展了这些重大公共政策倡议。

三、'旗帜'是容纳冲突的总体叙事：文章指出这些构想并非具体政策方案，而是关于中国是什么及渴望成为什么的总体愿景。它们不追求科学社会主义的精确性，而更渴望成为总体'叙事'，试图把稳定要求与可能分裂政权的变革离心力结合起来，代表界定政权、容纳深刻不同且相互冲突的合法性主张的复杂尝试。
[[#^pr-6d45df0241b0|正文跳转]]

四、顾问权威与政权不确定性相吻合：文章认为思想家作为顾问行使权威只在建国、危机和过渡等不寻常境况下才可能。最近三位领导人都需要王沪宁的建言，表明当代中国面临的问题并非仅技术性或官僚性；其影响力恰恰与当代中国的不稳定、脆弱性以及对'中国梦'的深刻不确定性相吻合。

五、王沪宁兼具儒者与智识分子角色：文章分析王沪宁最初是复旦学者，可视为儒家传统中的智者谋士或向中共建言的智识分子；一旦就任公职便停止公开写作，成为党内'思想工作者'。其角色和行动既可从儒家士人、也可从马克思主义智识分子角度理解，但在重要方面已超越这些角色，更接近西方传统中间接统治的顾问。

## Abstract

这篇文章首次对 Wang Huning 的生平与思想进行了全面考察，他是 Politburo 的成员、三位中国领导人的 advisor，也是当代中国重大 political conceptual formulations 的重要贡献者。在此过程中，文章试图深入理解 intellectuals 在中国所扮演的角色，以及这说明了中国政治的什么。文章认为，尽管 Wang 起初不愿进入政治，但他实际上已成为一位‘hidden leader’，对中国政治的性质施加了深远的影响，从而揭示了当代中国政治中的根本张力，这些张力由围绕 stability、economic growth 和 legitimacy 的重大 political debates 所塑造。

理解中国最高领导人的性格与抱负，以及最高层政治所特有的联盟与权威的微妙而复杂的变动，长期以来一直是中国政治研究者的关注焦点。但人们对那些为这些领导人提供建议的人关注要少得多，或许是因为这些 advisors 的匿名性，以及认为他们在党和国家 hierarchy 内只掌握有限 authority 的观点。然而，就 Wang Huning 而言，这种忽视是没有道理的。Wang 曾是 Fudan University 的 academic，于 1995 年受邀为 President Jiang Zemin（1993–2003）提供建议，此后在 advice 后续领导人 Hu Jintao（2003–2013）和 Xi Jinping（2013–present）方面发挥了越来越重要的作用。他目前是 Politburo 成员，据说在制定‘Three Represents’、‘Scientific Outlook on Development’和‘Chinese Dream’这些当代中国重大 political policy initiatives 中发挥了关键作用。因此，无论以何种标准衡量，Wang 在塑造当代中国政治方面都举足轻重。然而，关于他作为 intellectual 和 political advisor 的角色，系统的研究却很少。这篇文章首次对 Wang 的生平、思想背景和政治生涯进行了全面叙述，其中大部分内容只能从一系列零散的来源中获得，而且只有中文版本。对于那些希望了解 Wang 是谁，以及他在担任正式职务、不再以自己的名义发表文章之前的思想观点的人来说，这一叙述将很有用。但 Wang 及其思想值得更仔细的考察，还有两个相关的额外原因。由于他的任期和影响力，Wang 让我们得以看到 intellectuals 在当代中国政治中可以说具有多大的影响力。同样重要的是，Wang 的政治角色为

理解当代中国的性格提供了一种宝贵的手段，尤其是其关于自身是什么以及渴望成为什么的核心关切。

这篇文章认为，Wang 作为 political advisor，在决定重大 political questions 方面已开始行使前所未有的 authority。这种影响力如此之大，以至于可以把他描述为一位‘hidden ruler’，他的 advice 和 counsel 能够对政治产生巨大影响，其影响力不亚于最重要的 political leaders。然而，这种影响力并非仅仅归因于他的能力，尽管他的能力相当可观。正是因为那些独特的 circumstances，包括迅速变化的 political and economic conditions、CCP（Chinese Communist Party）面临的 legitimacy challenges，以及围绕 PRC 未来方向的 contest，Wang 才得以施加这种影响力。就此而言，Wang 作为 hidden ruler 之所以可能，仅仅是因为中国在 stability 和 legitimacy 方面面临的关键挑战，以及它在应当采取何种未来方向上的不确定性。

在接下来的讨论中，第一节简要介绍 Wang 的生平，然后通过回顾中国和西方在 intellectuals 在政治中的角色方面所采用的不同 theoretical approaches，试图理解他作为 academic 和 advisor 的角色。这包括概述 advisors 和 public intellectuals 在现代中国政治中的角色。在建立了这一理解 thinkers 在政治中的角色的 conceptual framework 之后，下一节接着考察 Wang 作为当代中国 public intellectual 和 political advisor 的双重角色，详细概述他主要以中文写成的大量 publications，以及他作为三位中国领导人的 advisor 对中国政治的贡献。结语部分指出，独特的当代 circumstances 如何为 advisors 提供了前所未有的、尽管往往转瞬即逝的机会，使其能够在塑造 political landscape 中施加非凡的影响力，从而揭示当代中国政治中的重大挑战与不确定性。

## Who is Wang Huning?

Huning Wang 的祖先可追溯到山东省，那里是 Confucius 的诞生地；他于 1955 年出生于上海的一个 revolutionary cadre family，在四十岁被调入 Beijing leadership 之前，他一生的大部分时间都在那里度过。$^{2}$ 他的出生地被永久地编码在他所取的名字‘Hu-ning’的选择中，字面意思是‘Shanghai tranquillity’。但他的 formative years 一点也不平静。作为一名 military officer，Huning 的父亲在 1959 年 Mao 针对 Marshall Peng Dehuai 的 campaign 中受到牵连，随后在始于 1966 年、长达十年的 Cultural Revolution 中遭受迫害，当时 Huning 还是个青少年。$^{3}$ 一个安静而内向的男孩，

Wang Huning 养成了阅读的嗜好。由于在 Cultural Revolution 期间 intellectuals 受到压制，academic pursuits 遭到排斥，书籍供应短缺，除了一份有限的官方认可书目，包括 Mao's little red books 和 Marxism 的入门读物。Wang 设法从 Shanghai Yongqiang School 的老师们那里获得了课外书籍，包括被禁的外国文学经典，这些老师被他对知识的渴求所感动。$^{4}$

1972 年高中毕业后，他当了近三年的学徒工（xuetugong）。$^{5}$ 作为一个体弱多病的年轻人，他部分逃脱了被‘up to the mountains and down to the countryside’（shang shan xia xiang）的命运，当时大多数其他城市毕业生都按照 Mao 在 1960 年代和 1970 年代要求让受过学校教育的城市青年接受 peasants 再教育的 campaign 而如此。相反，Wang 在上海附近的农村地区待了一段时间，并参加了 Shanghai Normal University（后更名为 East China Normal University）的外语培训项目，在那里学习了几年，主修 French。$^{6}$ 1977 年完成这个培训项目后，他在 Shanghai News and Publication Bureau 工作了一年，随后他的人生出现了重大转折点。$^{7}$

1978 年，随着破坏性的 Cultural Revolution 结束后 Deng's reforms 的启动，Wang 是最早利用中国大学体系复兴的人之一，他参加了竞争激烈的 university entrance examinations（Gaokao）并表现突出。尽管他在 East China Normal University 的培训项目不足以使他获得 bachelor's degree，但他的 Gaokao 表现如此令人印象深刻，以至于他被直接录取进入上海 Fudan University 竞争非常激烈的 international politics master's program。$^{8}$ 他在那里的主要导师是 Chen Qiren，一位研究 Marx 著作尤其是 Das Kapital 的著名权威，这可能解释了 Wang 的 dissertation 标题‘From Bodin to Maritain: on sovereignty theories developed by the Western bourgeoisie’。$^{9}$

以优异成绩完成 master's degree 后，Wang 被留任为 teaching staff，承担其他人不喜欢教的课程，并针对蓬勃发展的改革时代课程设置的快速增长开发新课程。他在 academic journals 以及受过教育的精英阅读的报纸和杂志上广泛发表文章。他的课很受欢迎，出勤率很高。他喜欢休闲的穿着和简单的食物，对教职工和学生显得随和而平易近人。$^{10}$

在 Fudan 求学期间，Wang 遇到了 Zhou Qi，一位 international politics 的同学，她后来也证明是一位 competent scholar，毕业后被该大学聘为教师，后来成为他的第一任妻子。他们的爱情生活被描绘成两个 philosophical pedants 之间的爱情，没有 fun 和 romance，两人独自待在狭小的宿舍里时都埋头于自己的 reading，偶尔会就双方都强烈关注的话题发生争执。据一则轶事，在他们婚礼前夕，Zhou Qi 一天早上递给 Huning 一张简单的购物清单，让他去买些 groceries，外加一束 flowers，作为难得的 luxury 来点亮这一天。当两人在一天结束时回到家，Wang 已经忘了他的差事，取而代之的是拿出一份他需要购置的 books 清单，潦草地写在 Zhou 那天早上递给他的购物清单背面。$^{11}$ 但他确实记得在他的一些 books 中表达对妻子支持他 research 和 writing 的感谢。

Wang 对 academic pursuits 的 dedication 和他 down-to-earth 的 style，以及他不断增长的 publications 清单，并没有被他的 superiors 忽视，他们提出推荐他 accelerated promotion。Wang 起初试图拒绝他们的提议，说他还年轻，还有很多要学。同样地，他拒绝了大学提供更大 apartment 的提议，说其他人更需要它。无论如何，在 1985 年他刚满 30 岁时，Wang 被提升为 Associate Professor，而无需先担任 lecturer，从而成为 Fudan 有史以来最年轻的 professor。在那个年代，中国的 academia 等级森严，age 和 experience 上的 seniority 几乎被视为 promotion 的先决条件，这是一项了不起的成就。$^{12}$ 在三年内，他被任命为 full professor，并接着成为 Head of the Law School，之后被 Beijing leadership 挖走。

Wang 在 academia 的迅速崛起得益于他成功参与了一系列 international debating championships，这些比赛在中国国家电视台上播出，成为街头巷尾的话题。他首先带领 Fudan team 在 1988 年的 Asian intercollegiate Chinese language debating contest 中获胜，作为 student contestants 的 coach 发挥了关键作用。然后在 1993 年的 International Intercollegiate Debating Championship 中，汇集了 Asia-Pacific region 一些最好的 universities，他担任 Fudan team 的 advisor。该 team 在新加坡举行的 grand final 中以惊人的表现夺得冠军，这场比赛被国内数百万观众观看，激发了全国上下的巨大 national pride。$^{13}$

## Zhishifenzi 与 Modern China 中的 ‘Intelligentsia’

要理解 Wang Huning 在当代政治中的角色，不仅有必要理解上文详述的 intellectuals 的不同角色与类型，还必须考虑 Mao 与 post-Mao 时代中国对 intellectuals 的特定处理。‘intelligentsia’，即 zhishifenzi，实际上是上文所述 outsider-insider dichotomy 的新表述。在采纳这些 categories 时，Chinese intellectuals 接受了新的 ideological positions，但这样做也失去了 Confucian ‘literati’ 的 dignity 或 authority，使他们的新角色变得 precarious。$^{30}$ 对 Mao（1949–1975）而言，thinkers 或 ‘intelligentsia’ 的政治重要性存在一种核心的 ambiguity。作为 ‘mental workers’、official ideology 与 values 的 propagators 和 guardians，他们被视为革命事业中有用的 workers。然而当他们开始批评他时，Mao 至多将他们视为对 proletariat 的利益认识不足，至坏则视为 ‘stinking ninth’（chou laojiu），需要教育，因而遭受 disgrace、ostracism、imprisonment 甚至 death。对 intelligentsia 的 instrumental use 与 rectification 导致了 relaxation（fang）与 repression（shou）的循环，这 evident 于 1957 年的 Hundred Flowers campaign（‘let a hundred flowers bloom; a hundred schools of thought contend’）鼓励对 bureaucratic abuses 的批评，随后不久便是 Anti-Rightist campaign（1957）、Great Leap Forward（1958–1960）以及 Cultural Revolution（1966–1976），在其中所有 intellectuals 都被归咎于 leadership 的错误，成千上万的人被 purged、self-criticized 或被送往 countryside 向 proletariat 学习。$^{31}$ Maoism 造就了爱国的、nationalistic 的 Chinese intellectuals 的爱恨交织——作为 critical outsider 是危险的；然而从内部服务同样 precarious，因为他们的 patriotism 能否被承认并不确定。$^{32}$

对 Wang 作为 Fudan 的 scholar 与 public intellectual 的已发表著作进行细读，揭示了若干重要的 philosophical 与 political themes。$^{39}$ 进入享有盛誉的 Fudan University 后，Wang 不仅有了更广泛的书籍可读，还能更好地接触博学的 professors。他最初打算研究 French politics，随后转向 economics 与 Western political thought 的历史，最终专注于 political science，这是中国一门新近复兴的 discipline。如前所述，Wang 的 master's dissertation 的主题是 sovereignty 这一 concept。虽然他的 dissertation 未公开，但可以从他第一本独著 $State/National\ Sovereignty$（$Guojia Zhuquan$）中得出一些关于其内容的线索。$^{40}$ 该书开篇阐明了 sovereignty 作为 political science 中一个 key concept 的重要性，接着追溯了该 concept 在 western thought 中的起源与演变，并将其与中国的 $zhuquan$ concept 相对照，后者早于 western concept。$^{41}$ 该书随后追溯了 sovereignty 在不同时代的 genesis 与 formation，解释了其在国内 supremacy 与对外 independence 方面的双重面向。该书采用 Marxist perspective，进而阐述了 sovereignty 的 class-based nature，因为 supreme power 由特定社会的 ruling class 所掌握。$^{42}$ 该书的大部分篇幅致力于通过详细回顾关于该主题的理论文献来阐明 sovereignty 的内部与外部面向，引用了从 Socrates 到 Augustine 与 Machiavelli，以及从 Bodin 到 Hegel 与 Austin 的作者。$^{43}$ 该书最后探讨了 sovereignty 对更当代议题的意义，包括 national independence、Third World、international order，以及更贴近本土的 Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence，并引用 Marx、Engels 与 Lenin 关于 national equality 与 national self-determination 对维护 international peace 的重要性的论述。$^{44}$ 因此，$State/National\ Sovereignty$ 揭示了 Wang 对 political philosophy 的掌握深度，以及他受 Marxism 影响、关注其洞见对当代政治实践的直接意涵的焦点。

## 在北京的 Wang

这一看法似乎得到了他转变为北京 CCP 的一名雇员这一事实的印证，在那里他看起来与 literati high-level intellectual（gaoji zhishifenzi）以及向 emperor 进谏的 high-level official 并无二致，或者用现代的说法，就是尽职的 Communist ‘mind worker’。的确，他的个人背景似乎印证了这一点。在 Cultural Revolution 中幸存下来后，他抓住机会学习，并迅速通过大学体系晋升，作为 professor 和 Law School 的 head 获得了加速提拔。他的 publications 以及指导 debating team 的成功使他引起了 ‘Shanghai Gang’ 的注意，后者说服他前往北京，在那里他用自己的 scholar 生活换取了 bureaucrat 的生活。他此后的服务证明了他作为一名政治上精明的 bureaucrat 和 Party 的忠诚 servant 的能力。

但是，把 Wang 说成仅仅是又一位凭借运气、关系和精明的算计而成为 bureaucrat 的 scholar，这种叙述并未充分承认他的崛起、长久以及随后的政治影响所具有的前所未有的性质。要做到这一点，还必须注意到他非凡的才能、他对 bureaucrats 生活的模糊看法、他非凡的晋升，以及或许最重要的是，他对最高层 Chinese public policy 所作贡献的性质。Wang 的才能从他的学术生涯、他学术的广度和深度以及随后他的能力在最高层得到的认可中显而易见。正如本文试图表明的那样，知识和学术对 Wang 很重要，这一点从他早年自我教育的努力中显而易见，尤其是在 Cultural Revolution 期间，以及他大量的教学和 publications。吸引 ‘Shanghai Gang’ 走向 Wang 的是他的学术能力，而非其他影响来源，正如他的简要传记所表明的那样。$^{58}$ 对他生平的叙述表明，Wang 必须被说服才用自己的学术生活换取 bureaucrat 的生活，这表明他持续热爱学术，缺乏政治野心。可以推测，他迁往北京的最终原因，既有从内部施加更大影响以实施有用的 policy initiatives 的雄心，也有一种公共责任或爱国主义意识，这从他关于 ‘responsibility, conscience and yearning’ 的言论中显而易见。$^{59}$

这些构想不仅仅是 public policy formulation 或具体经济、金融或技术官僚 programs 或 initiatives 的简单练习，而是关于中国是什么以及渴望成为什么的总体愿景。严格来说，它们不是 ‘ideologies’，因为它们并不追求先前 forms of scientific socialism 的那种精确性或技术术语。的确，在其笼统和模糊之中，它们更渴望成为总体的 ‘narratives’，试图将当代中国明显处于深刻张力之中的东西——稳定的要求与有可能使政权分裂的变革的离心力——结合起来。‘banner’ 的每一次迭代都让人想起 Maoist doctrinal statements，但就其范围和雄心而言，它们显示出一种更复杂的尝试，即超越教条主义，走向一个能够容纳深刻不同且相互冲突的合法性主张的未来。因此，它们代表了界定该政权的尝试：对中国是什么以及渴望成为什么的复杂而雄心勃勃的表述。

thinkers 或 intellectuals 的政治角色，在东方与西方一直是一个重要且持久的问题。那些始终处于 political authority 之外的 thinkers——要么不参与，要么提供 critical counsel——与那些就任公职、成为 bureaucrats 或 advisors 的人之间的主要区分，可以在以下观念中找到，并且已经以特定方式被构想出来：儒家对 shi 的构想、西方对 advisor 以及 philosophe 或 public intellectual 的理解，以及马克思主义对 intelligentsia 的看法。Wang Huning 最初是 Fudan University 的一名 academic，因此是儒家传统中的 wise counsellor，或者是向 CCP 提供建议的 intelligentsia。然而，一旦他在 CPP 就任公职，他就停止了公开写作，成为党内的 ‘thinking worker’，服从于党及其领导人的需要和要求。然而，对他在中国当代政治中角色的这一细致考察表明，他的影响远不止于一名 mere advisor。Wang 的任职时间之长，跨越三位领导人；他的 authority 上升之快，以至于他如今已是 Politburo 的成员；以及他对 ‘Three Represents’、‘Scientific Outlook on Development’ 和 ‘Chinese Dream’ 等 seminal political formulations 的贡献，都表明：尽管他并不掌握任何直接的 political power，而且他的位置始终 precarious，但他在塑造当代中国政治方向方面的作用，已使他与 political leaders 平起平坐。因此，在这一角色中，Wang 似乎已成为一名 insider，其角色和行动在某种意义上既可以从 Confucian literati 的角度、也可以从 Marxist intelligentsia 的角度来理解。但在一些重要方面，Wang 似乎已经超越了这些角色。更准确地说，他属于西方学术传统中那位间接进行统治的 advisor。换言之，他已经成为一名 hidden leader。$^{65}$ 应当清楚的是，本文并不是在声称 Wang 已经掌握了 political authority——后者显然掌握在 General Secretary、Politburo 的 Standing Committee 以及党和国家的 hierarchy 内各 office 这一更大的架构之中。相反，本文的提法是：他的 counsel 如此重要，以至于尽管他并不掌握直接的 political authority，但作为一名 advisor，他已经显著地塑造了现代中国未来的政治方向。当然，Wang 并非完全 hidden，即并非处于视线之外或默默无闻——尽管他试图保持匿名，但 Xi（如同之前的 Hu 和 Jiang 一样）坚持要 Wang 陪同他出席重大活动和国际访问，公开宣示了这种 hidden influence。这证实了一个显而易见的事实：作为 advisor 和 hidden ruler，Wang 并不拥有 Xi 等领导人那样的 authority，因而也没有那样的 security。然而，正是这种在缺乏 political authority 与从他的 insights 和 counsel——从他的 intellectual authority——中获得的力量之间的微妙平衡，揭示了 ideas 和 thinkers 在无情的政治世界中的力量与 precariousness。

一名 thinker 作为 advisor、因而作为 hidden ruler 的可能性，不仅揭示了 Wang 本人的重要方面，也为理解当代中国能够容纳这样一种职位的独特境况提供了宝贵的洞见。$^{66}$ 只有在不寻常的境况下，尤其是在 foundings、crises 和 transitions 的时期，advisors 才能行使这样的 authority。最近三位领导人都需要 Wang 的 counsel，这表明当代中国面临着一些深刻的问题，而这些问题并非仅仅是技术性或官僚性的。Wang 对 ‘banners’ 的贡献，仅仅是围绕中国应当走何种未来方向、同时又不危及其根本 security 和 stability 的深刻 tectonic struggles 的外在表现。换言之，由 ‘three belief crisis’ 所引发的 legitimacy 之争，在为 thinkers 提供了前所未有的 political authority 的同时，也是这个国家所面临的艰巨 constitutive questions 和挑战的不可否认的证据。因此，Wang Huning 的影响力和 authority 本身，恰恰与当代中国的不稳定和 precariousness 相吻合，也与对何为 ‘China’s Dream’ 的深刻不确定性相吻合。

> ## Abstract
>
> The article provides the first comprehensive examination of the life and thought of Wang Huning, member of the Politburo, advisor to three Chinese leaders and important contributor to major political conceptual formulations in contemporary China. In doing so, it seeks to derive insights into the role of intellectuals in China, and what this says about Chinese politics. It argues that, although initially reluctant to enter politics, Wang has become in effect a ‘hidden leader’, exercising far-reaching influence on the nature of Chinese politics, thereby revealing the fundamental tensions in contemporary Chinese politics, shaped by major political debates concerning stability, economic growth, and legitimacy.
>
> Understanding the character and aspirations of China's top leaders and the subtle and complex shifting of alliances and authority that characterizes politics at this highest level has long been the focus of students of Chinese politics. But much less attention is paid to those who advise these leaders, perhaps because of the anonymity of these advisors and the view that they wield limited authority within the hierarchy of the state and the Party. Such neglect, however, is not justified in the case of Wang Huning. Wang, former Fudan University academic, was invited to advise President Jiang Zemin (1993–2003) in 1995 and since then has assumed an increasingly important role in advising subsequent leaders Hu Jintao (2003–2013) and Xi Jinping (2013–present). He is presently in the Politburo and is said to have been instrumental in formulating the ‘Three Represents’, ‘Scientific Outlook on Development’, and ‘Chinese Dream’, some of the major political policy initiatives in contemporary China. By any measure, therefore, Wang has been significant in shaping contemporary Chinese politics. Yet there have been few systematic studies of his role as intellectual and political advisor. This article presents the first comprehensive account of Wang, his life, his intellectual background and political career, much of which is only available from a range of fragmented sources and only in Chinese. This account will be useful for those who wish to understand who Wang is and what his intellectual views were before taking up formal office when he ceased publishing in his own name. But Wang and his thought warrants closer examination for two additional, related reasons. Because of his tenure and influence, Wang allows us to see the extent to which intellectuals can be said to be influential in contemporary Chinese politics. Equally important, Wang’s political role provides a valuable means for

Ma Kai, an economics graduate of People’s University, started his career as an economic official in 1982, first in the Beijing Municipal Government and then in the central government after 1993. He rose through the ranks, serving almost exclusively in the economic and financial areas through three regimes or administrations. He came to be known as one of the ‘big four’ economic and financial officials of the Hu (President Hu Jintao) – Wen (Premier Wen Jiabao) era, before becoming a vice-premier under Premier Li Ke Qiang during the Xi era. Unlike Wang, however, Ma did not have an academic career, nor an established academic reputation before entering officialdom. Zheng Bijian comes closer to Wang, having studied and taught at People’s University in the early 1950s. He went on to work in the central government and party institutions, including the State Council, the Secretariat of the CCP Central Committee and the Central Party School, culminating as a ministerial level official in charge of the party’s propaganda/publicity work in the Deng and Jiang eras. He stepped back into second-tier roles after Jiang handed over to Hu Jintao. Zheng had also worked as an advisor to General Secretary Hu Yaobang in the 1980s and was known to have played a significant role in editing and compiling some of Mao's later works and Deng's speeches. In some respects, Zheng's role is reminiscent of Wang Huning, but Zheng's career was marked and dented by the political vicissitudes of an earlier era, with setbacks and detours caused by the Cultural Revolution and the constraints characteristic of the Mao and Deng eras. Unlike Wang, Zheng's political career was not as smooth, and his influence was limited to the Party's ideological or theoretical work while Wang's trajectory has expanded steadily from theoretical to other spheres of the top leadership, including strategic planning and national security under Xi Jinping.

> ## Zhishifenzi and ‘Intelligentsia’ in Modern China
>
> To understand Wang Huning's role in contemporary politics, it is necessary not only to understand the different roles and types of intellectuals as detailed above, but also to take into account the specific treatment of intellectuals in China in the Mao and post-Mao era. The ‘intelligentsia’, or zhishifenzi, was in effect the new formulation of the outsider-insider dichotomy noted above. In adopting these categories, Chinese intellectuals embraced new ideological positions but in doing so lost the dignity or authority of the Confucian ‘literati’, making their new role precarious. $^{30}$ For Mao (1949–1975) there was a core ambiguity regarding the political importance of thinkers or ‘intelligentsia’. As ‘mental workers’, propagators and guardians of official ideology and values, they were seen as useful workers for the cause of the revolution. Yet when they began to criticize him, Mao saw them at best as insufficiently aware of the interests of the proletariat or at worst as the ‘stinking ninth’ (chou laojiu), in need of education and therefore subject to disgrace, ostracism, imprisonment and even death. The instrumental use and rectification of the intelligentsia resulted in a cycle of relaxation (fang) and repression (shou), evident in the 1957 Hundred Flowers campaign (‘let a hundred flowers bloom; a hundred schools of thought contend’) encouraging criticism of bureaucratic abuses, soon followed by the Anti-Rightist campaign (1957), the Great Leap Forward (1958–1960), and the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) where all intellectuals were blamed for the errors of the leadership and thousands were purged, self-criticized or were sent to the countryside to learn from the proletariat. $^{31}$ Maoism produced the love-hate of patriotic and nationalistic Chinese intellectuals — dangerous to be the critical outsider; yet equally precarious to serve from the inside, where it was not certain that their patriotism would be recognized. $^{32}$

As mentioned earlier, the topic of Wang's master's dissertation is the concept of sovereignty. While his dissertation is not publicly available, it is possible to derive some clues to its content from his first sole-authored book $State/National\ Sovereignty$ ( $Guojia Zhuquan$ ). $^{40}$ The book starts by making clear the importance of sovereignty as a key concept in political science and goes on to trace the origin and evolution of the concept in western thought, contrasting it with the Chinese concept of $zhuquan$ , which predated the western concept. $^{41}$ The book then traces the genesis and formation of sovereignty through different epochs, explaining its dual aspect in domestic supremacy and external independence. > ## Wang in Beijing
>
> This view seems to be confirmed by his transition to an employee of the CCP in Beijing, where he appears no different from the literati high-level intellectual (gaoji zhishifenzi) and high-level official who counsels the emperor, or in modern terms the dutiful Communist ‘mind worker’. Indeed, his personal background seems to confirm this. Having survived the Cultural Revolution, he seizes the opportunity to learn and rise swiftly through the university system, gaining accelerated promotions as professor and head of the Law School. His publications and success in mentoring the debating team brings him to the attention of the ‘Shanghai Gang’, who persuade him to go to Beijing, where he exchanges the life of the scholar for the life of the bureaucrat. His subsequent service testifies to his abilities as a politically adroit bureaucrat and a loyal servant of the Party.

> But this account of Wang as merely another scholar who through luck, connections, and shrewd calculation becomes a bureaucrat does not sufficiently acknowledge the unprecedented nature of his rise and longevity and subsequent political influence. To do so, it is also necessary to note his remarkable talents, his ambiguous view of the life of bureaucrats, his extraordinary ascendency, and perhaps most importantly, the nature of his contribution to Chinese public policy at the highest level. Wang's talent is evident from his academic career, from the breadth and depth of his scholarship and the subsequent endorsement of his abilities at the highest level. As this article has sought to show, that knowledge and scholarship is important for Wang is evident from his early attempts to educate himself, especially during the Cultural Revolution and his extensive teaching and publications. It was his academic ability, rather than other sources of influence that attracted the ‘Shanghai Gang’ to Wang, as demonstrated in his brief biography. $^{58}$ The account of his life suggests that Wang had to be persuaded to exchange his scholarly life for the life of a bureaucrat, suggesting his continuing love of scholarship and lack of political ambition. His ultimate reasons for moving to Beijing, one may surmise, were due to a mixture of ambition to have greater influence to implement useful policy initiatives from the inside, as well as a sense of public duty or patriotism, evident from his remarks regarding ‘responsibility, conscience and yearning’. $^{59}$

Confronting new political and economic questions and challenges that could not be simply answered by traditional Marxist-Leninist-Mao Zedong Thought, a Chinese leadership confronted the need for a non-bureaucratic intelligentsia that would refashion new ways to accommodate the evolving concepts of sovereignty, democracy, and rule of law in China. What was especially important was determining the new foundation of legitimacy that would reconcile innovative economic initiatives with the evolving role of the Party. In many respects, therefore, Wang was — and is — the man for the times. But his success is also attributable to the fact that he fulfilled the duties and obligations imposed upon him. Wang has been credited by the Chinese media with masterminding the major ideological banners of the three leaders he has served (or has been serving), such as Jiang’s ‘Three Represents’ (sange daibiao), Hu’s ‘Scientific Outlook on Development’ (kexue fazhan guan) and Xi’s ‘Chinese Dream’ (zhongguo meng). They are not strictly speaking ‘ideologies’, to the extent that they do not seek the exactness or technical jargon of the preceding forms of scientific socialism. Indeed, in their generality and vagueness, they aspire more to be overarching ‘narratives’ that seek to combine what is obviously in profound tension in contemporary China, the demands of stability and the centrifugal forces of change that have potential to fragment the regime. Each iteration of the ‘banner’ is reminiscent of Maoist doctrinal statements, but in their scope and ambition, they show a more sophisticated attempt to go beyond doctrinal dogmatism to a future that can accommodate the profoundly different and conflicting claims for legitimacy. As such, they represent attempts to define the regime: complex and ambitious articulations of what China is and what it aspires to be.

> The political role of thinkers or intellectuals has been an important and enduring question in the East and West. The main distinction between thinkers who remain outside political authority, either not engaging or providing critical counsel, and those who take office, as bureaucrats or advisors, can be found and has been conceived in specific ways in the Confucian conception of the shi, the western understanding of the advisor and the philosophe or public intellectual, and in the Marxist view of the intelligentsia. Wang Huning was originally an academic at Fudan University and therefore was the wise counsellor in the Confucian tradition, or the intelligentsia who provided advice to the CCP. Once he took office with the CPP, however, he ceased to write publicly and became the ‘thinking worker’ in the Party, who deferred to the needs and demands of the Party and its leaders. Yet this close examination of his role in contemporary Chinese politics suggests that his influence has been much more significant than a mere advisor. Wang’s length of tenure, spanning three leaders, his rapid rise in authority, so that he is now a member of the Politburo, and his contribution to seminal political formulations such as the ‘Three Represents’, ‘Scientific Outlook on Development’, and ‘Chinese Dream’ suggest that, though he does not wield any direct political power, and his position is ever-precarious, his role in shaping the political direction of contemporary China has made him the equal of political leaders. In this role, Wang, therefore, seems to have become an insider whose role and actions in some sense can be understood both in terms of the Confucian literati and Marxist intelligentsia. But in important respects, Wang seems to have gone beyond these roles. More accurately, he is in the tradition of the western scholarship the advisor who governs indirectly. He has, in other words, become a hidden leader. $^{65}$ It should be clear that the article is not claiming that Wang has assumed political authority, which clearly is held within the larger architecture of the General Secretary, the Standing Committee of the Politburo, and the offices within the hierarchy of the Party and the state. Rather, the suggestion is that his counsel has been so significant that though not wielding direct political authority, he has as an advisor come to shape significantly the future political direction of modern China. Of course, Wang is not altogether hidden in the sense that he is out of sight or obscure — in spite of his attempts at anonymity, Xi (like Hu and Jiang before him) has insisted that Wang accompany him to major events and international visits, publicly declaring this hidden influence. This confirms the obvious fact that as advisor and hidden ruler, Wang does not have the authority and therefore security of leaders such as Xi. Yet it is this delicate balance between lack of political authority, and strength gained from his insights and counsel — from his intellectual authority — that reveals both the strength and precariousness of ideas and thinkers within the ruthless world of politics.
>
> The possibility of a thinker as advisor and therefore hidden ruler not only reveals important aspects of Wang himself but also provides valuable insights into the unique circumstances of contemporary China that can accommodate such an office. $^{66}$ It is only in unusual circumstances, especially in times of foundings, crises, and transitions that advisors can come to exercise such authority. That all three recent leaders have needed Wang's counsel suggests there are profound questions confronting contemporary China that are not simply technical or bureaucratic. Wang's contributions to ‘banners’ is simply the external manifestation of the profound tectonic struggles concerning the future direction China should take without jeopardizing its fundamental security and stability. In other words, the contests for legitimacy due to the ‘three belief crisis’ that have provided unprecedented political authority for thinkers are also inconvertible evidence of the formidable constitutive questions and challenges facing the country. The very influence and authority of Wang Huning therefore coincides with the instability and precariousness of contemporary China, and a deep uncertainty of what is ‘China’s Dream’.
