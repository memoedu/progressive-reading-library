# Don't have conversations on Facebook

---

## 正文

Brian Tomasik [建议](http://reducing-suffering.blogspot.com/2012/07/why-i-prefer-public-conversations.html)，而且[许多](http://www.givewell.org/conversations)[人](https://sites.google.com/site/nbeckstead/conversations)也[赞同](http://www.goodventures.org/research-and-ideas/conversations)，为你的重要交谈和通信保留 public records 确实很有用：这让其他人更容易 discover 它们、对它们 give feedback、search for 它们以及 link to 它们，也能确保这些 information（通过 Internet Archive）长期留存。

我完全赞同。更进一步说，我认为我们还应该做得更多。并非所有“public”通信都同样有用。想想在 [Effective Altruists](https://www.facebook.com/groups/effective.altruists/) 这样的 Facebook groups 中进行的许多有用讨论：

-   **它们更难被 discover。** 据我所知，Facebook groups 不会被 Google indexed。我怀疑 StumbleUpon 这样的其他 sites 也没有对它们进行 tracked。这似乎极大地妨碍了 discoverability。虽然在 Facebook 上讨论有助于你 immediate social network 中的人 discover 这些 content，却完全无助于内容传播开来。

-   **它们更难获得 feedback。** 当文章被 shared 多次时，尤其如此。我有时写完 posts，会看到同一场讨论同时出现在两三个 Facebook comment threads 中，以及那篇 post 本身的 comments 中。如果这些讨论能够 merged，情况会好得多。

-   **它们更难被 search for。** 当然，discoverability 并不是 Facebook 的 custom search 唯一的问题。即便你已经看过正在寻找的那场交谈，如果你不知道它究竟发生在哪个 group 中（可能还得知道确切时间），想找到它可就只能祝你好运了。

-   **它们更难被 link to。** 有些 Facebook groups 是 private。即使 linking 到 public 的那些，URL schema 也可能随 Facebook 的心意而改变。

-   **它们未必能长期留存。** Facebook 以前已经多次更改自己的 user interface 和 data model。谁知道这个过程中会有什么 information 被 clobbered？而当这种事情发生时，有人在保留 backup 吗？Internet Archive 肯定没有，而[这个 help thread](https://www.facebook.com/help/community/question/?id=560590287326922&answer_id=580694768649807) 表明，这甚至是不可能的。

因此，Tomasik 的文章所指出的 public 通信的好处，Facebook 上的讨论几乎一样也不具备。只要有可能，我都建议把讨论和通信从 Facebook、Google+ 以及其他 corporate-owned media 转移到 blog posts 和 comment threads 中。例如，如果你准备对别人 shared 的一篇 blog post 进行 comment，在其他条件相同的情况下，你应该在那篇 post 本身进行 comment，而不是在 Facebook 上的那条“share”下进行 comment。[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/fb/#fn:1) 这只是一点小小的不便，却能带来许多 positive externalities。

* * *

1.  显然，如果你预期自己的朋友会比那篇 post 的 average audience 更善于评论，或评论会有趣得多，那么上述说法就不成立。不过，对我认识的许多人来说，他们的 blogs 主要就是朋友在读，所以这个 objection 并不适用。 [↩︎](https://www.benkuhn.net/fb/#fnref:1)

## 原文

Brian Tomasik [suggests](http://reducing-suffering.blogspot.com/2012/07/why-i-prefer-public-conversations.html), and [many](http://www.givewell.org/conversations) [people](https://sites.google.com/site/nbeckstead/conversations) [agree](http://www.goodventures.org/research-and-ideas/conversations), that keeping public records of your important conversations and correspondence is really useful: it makes them easier for other people to discover, give feedback on, search for, and link to, and makes sure (via the Internet Archive) that the information sticks around for a long time.

I completely agree. What’s more, I think we should go further. Not all “public” correspondence is equally useful. Consider the many useful discussions held in Facebook groups like [Effective Altruists](https://www.facebook.com/groups/effective.altruists/):

-   **They’re harder to discover.** Facebook groups aren’t indexed by Google, as far as I can tell. I doubt that it’s tracked by other sites like StumbleUpon either. This seems to hamper discoverability a lot. While discussing on Facebook helps people in your immediate social network discover the content, it doesn’t help things spread at all.

-   **They’re harder to get feedback on.** This is especially true when articles are shared multiple times. I’ve sometimes written posts and seen the same discussion happen in two or three Facebook comment threads and on the comments of the post itself. It would be much better if these discussions could be merged.

-   **They’re harder to search for.** Of course, discoverability isn’t the only problem with Facebook’s custom search. Even if you’ve already seen the conversation that you’re looking for, good luck finding it if you don’t know exactly which group it happened in (and maybe what exact time it was).

-   **They’re harder to link to.** Some Facebook groups are private. Even when linking to public ones, the URL schema might change at Facebook’s whim.

-   **They may not stick around.** Facebook has changed their user interface and data model many times before. Who knows what information will get clobbered in the process? And when it does, is anyone keeping a backup? The Internet Archive certainly isn’t, and [this help thread](https://www.facebook.com/help/community/question/?id=560590287326922&answer_id=580694768649807) suggests it’s not even possible.

As a result, Facebook discussions have almost none of the benefits of public correspondence that Tomasik’s essay noted. Wherever possible, I recommend moving discussions and correspondence off Facebook, Google+, and other corporate-owned media and into blog posts and comment threads. For instance, if you’re going to comment on a blog post that someone shared, all else equal you should comment on the post itself, not the “share” on Facebook.[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/fb/#fn:1) It’s a small inconvenience with many positive externalities.

* * *

1.  Obviously this doesn’t hold if you expect your friends to be significantly better/more interesting commenters than the post’s average audience. For many people I know, though, their blogs are mostly read by their friends, so that objection doesn’t apply. [↩︎](https://www.benkuhn.net/fb/#fnref:1)
