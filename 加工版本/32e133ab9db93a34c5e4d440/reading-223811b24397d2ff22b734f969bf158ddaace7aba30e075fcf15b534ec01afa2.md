# Late employee equity

---

## 正文

Dustin Moskovitz [认为](http://genius.com/Sam-altman-lecture-1-how-to-start-a-startup-annotated)，尝试成为一家看起来非常有前景的 startup 的 early 或 mid employee，往往比自己创办 startup 更 financially rewarding。最近这种说法流传得很广——我看到很多人声称，相对于 founders，early employees 的短缺程度很不合理。这种说法往往暗示，这是一种 market inefficiency，因为成为 founder 很风光，而成为 early employee 则不然。实际情况在多大程度上确实如此？

如果这_确实_是真的，那就太好了，因为找到下一个 Dropbox-at-100-employees，比 build 你自己的 startup 容易得多。但这也正是我觉得它不太可信的原因

Dustin Moskovitz 随口估计，Dropbox 可能会给他们的第 100 位 hire 以 10bp 的 equity。根据各种来源来看，这似乎偏高（例如 Wealthfront startup salary tool，可惜如今已经下线）。另外，Dropbox 的 employee #100 应该是在 [2011 年末或 2012 年初](http://www.quora.com/How-many-employees-does-Dropbox-have)加入的，当时 Dropbox 的 valued at 已经是 [\$4 billion](http://techcrunch.com/2011/08/30/index-leads-4-billion-valuation-round-in-dropbox/). That would make 10bp of equity worth $4m，而且是_当时就已经_如此，这有点令人难以置信。如果他们实际 paying 的确实是这么多，那么 Moskovitz 的建议更像是“去一家 pays 非常好的地方工作”，而不是“利用 market inefficiency”——你本来能从 Dropbox made money，更多是因为 equity _当时就已经_很值钱，而不是因为它有 high risk-adjusted returns。（Dropbox 在 [2014 年初的 valued at 为 $10b](http://techcrunch.com/2014/01/17/dropbox-closes-250m-round-at-10b-valuation-wsj-says/)，对应的 return 为每年 35%。）

其他 companies 呢？对于明显值得加入的“Dropbox-level”公司，我会用那些在 [‘08](http://techcrunch.com/2009/01/10/congratulations-to-the-crunchies-winners-facebook-takes-top-prize-for-second-year/)、[‘09](http://techcrunch.com/2010/01/08/crunchies-winner/)、[‘10](http://techcrunch.com/2011/01/21/congratulations-crunchies-winners-twitter-takes-best-startup-of-2010/)、[‘11](http://techcrunch.com/events/crunchies-2011/finalists/) 或 [‘12](http://techcrunch.com/2013/01/31/congratulations-crunchies-winners-github-wins-best-overall-startup/) 年获得 TechCrunch “Crunchie” awards 的“Best Overall Startup”提名的 startups，作为一个 rough proxy。弄清它们各自实际给 employee #100 多少 equity 很麻烦，而且其中一些还没有 exited，因此尚不清楚最终的 payout 会是多少，但我们仍然可以把一个人在 company 中的 equity 当作 investment，并弄清它 grew 了多少。如果这些 companies 给每位 hire 的 equity 都具有大致相同的 nominal dollar value（这至少也是个值得怀疑的 assumption！），那么这就能很好地作为 proxy，反映在每家公司工作相对而言会有多 lucrative。我把自己的工作放在了一份 [Google Sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1M1RzyvC4yVIYdzoEHqdEu1h-kHbIele4Lujrh9N3mbw/edit?usp=sharing) 中。

这些 companies 中，有两家 acquired 得太快，以至于在那个阶段加入的人无法获得多少 return（Instagram 和 ngmoco）；两家迄今为止毫无疑问是失败的（Zynga 和 Fab），另外两家在获得 Crunchie 提名时，加入它们_大概_已经不是好主意了，但这更难确定（Tumblr、Groupon）。还有四家（Gilt Groupe、Hulu、Quora 和 Square）已经一年多没有 raised，因此尚不太清楚 employees 最终能 realize 多少 return。

其余 9 家的 geometric mean IRR 为 58%。尚不清楚是否应该把那 8 家存疑的公司算作平均而言基本没有 return、但也没有 loss（这会把 overall IRR lower 到 27%），还是应该采用其他处理方式。无论如何，这样的 return 都相当不错。如果将它与另一种 counterfactual 相比，这一点尤其成立：在一个有 high cash salary 的地方工作，并将其 investing；如果你在 startup 工作，你能以加入时的 price 获得相当于 4 年 vesting 的 equity，这实际上让你可以大幅提前获得能够 invest 的 principal。当然，这里有很多 caveats，比如 IRR 在这里是一个相当糟糕的 metric，因为 VC equity 和 employee equity 很不一样；又比如，你应该考虑 option grants 的 present value，而不只是 future returns。考察 IRR 只是一次非常粗略的初步分析，仅此而已。

最后，如果你今天采用这种 strategy，那么值得留意你可能会向哪些 companies apply。2014 Crunchies 的 nominees（目前能找到的最近一届）是 GoPro、Snapchat、Stripe、Tinder 和 Uber。

## 原文

Dustin Moskovitz [thinks](http://genius.com/Sam-altman-lecture-1-how-to-start-a-startup-annotated) it’s often more financially rewarding to try and be an early or mid employee at a really good-looking startup than start your own. This meme has been going around a lot recently—I’ve seen lots of people make claims that there’s an unreasonable dearth of early employees relative to founders. Often there’s some implication that this is a market inefficiency because it’s glamorous to be a founder and not to be an early employee. How true is this in practice?

If it _were_ true, it would be pretty cool, since it’s much easier to find the next Dropbox-at-100-employees than to build your own startup. But that’s why it seems implausible to me

Dustin Moskovitz spitballed that Dropbox might give 10bp of equity to their 100th hire. That seems high based on various sources (e.g. the Wealthfront startup salary tool, unfortunately now taken offline). For another thing, employee #100 at Dropbox would have joined in [late 2011 or early 2012](http://www.quora.com/How-many-employees-does-Dropbox-have), when Dropbox was already valued at [$4 billion](http://techcrunch.com/2011/08/30/index-leads-4-billion-valuation-round-in-dropbox/). That would make 10bp of equity worth $4m _already_, which is kind of unbelievable. If that’s what they were actually paying, Moskovitz’s advice is more like “work somewhere that pays really well,” not “take advantage of a market inefficiency”—the reason you would have made money from Dropbox is more that the equity was _already_ worth a lot than that it had high risk-adjusted returns. (Dropbox was valued at [$10b in early 2014](http://techcrunch.com/2014/01/17/dropbox-closes-250m-round-at-10b-valuation-wsj-says/), for a return of 35% annually.)

What about other companies? As a rough proxy for being “Dropbox-level” obviously a good idea I’ll use startups that got nominated the TechCrunch “Crunchie” awards for “Best Overall Startup” in [‘08](http://techcrunch.com/2009/01/10/congratulations-to-the-crunchies-winners-facebook-takes-top-prize-for-second-year/), [‘09](http://techcrunch.com/2010/01/08/crunchies-winner/), [‘10](http://techcrunch.com/2011/01/21/congratulations-crunchies-winners-twitter-takes-best-startup-of-2010/), [‘11](http://techcrunch.com/events/crunchies-2011/finalists/), or [‘12](http://techcrunch.com/2013/01/31/congratulations-crunchies-winners-github-wins-best-overall-startup/). It’s annoying to figure out how much equity each of them actually gave employee #100, and some of them haven’t exited yet so it’s not clear what the eventual payout will be, but we can still treat someone’s equity in a company as an investment and figure out how much it grew. If the companies all give roughly the same nominal dollar value of equity to each hire (a questionable assumption to say the least!) then that will be a good proxy for how relatively lucrative it would have been to work at each one. I’ve put my work in a [Google Sheet](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1M1RzyvC4yVIYdzoEHqdEu1h-kHbIele4Lujrh9N3mbw/edit?usp=sharing).

Of those companies, two were acquired too quickly for someone joining at that stage to see much return (Instagram and ngmoco); two were unequivocally duds so far (Zynga and Fab) and two more were _probably_ bad ideas by the time they got nominated for a Crunchie but it’s harder to say (Tumblr, Groupon). Four more (Gilt Groupe, Hulu, Quora and Square) haven’t raised in over a year and so it’s not as clear how much of the return employees will eventually realize.

The other 9 of them had a geometric mean IRR of 58%. It’s not clear whether you should count the questionable 8 as giving essentially no return but no loss on average (which would lower the overall IRR to 27%) or what. Either way, it’s not too shabby of a return. That’s especially true if you compare it to the counterfactual of working somewhere with a high cash salary and investing it: if you work at a startup you get 4 years of vesting worth of equity at the price when you joined, which effectively gives you a big advance on the principal you can invest. Of course, there are lots of caveats, like that IRR is a pretty bad metric here since VC equity and employee equity are pretty different, or that you should take into account the present value of the option grants, not just future returns. Looking at the IRR is just a very rough first pass, nothing more.

Finally, it’s worth keeping track of which companies you might apply to if you were using this strategy today. The nominees for the 2014 Crunchies (the last set available) were GoPro, Snapchat, Stripe, Tinder and Uber.
