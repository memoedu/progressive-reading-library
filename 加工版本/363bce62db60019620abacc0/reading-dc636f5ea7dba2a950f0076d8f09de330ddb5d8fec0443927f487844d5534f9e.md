# The Slippery Utilitarian

---

## 正文

Boston Review 最近举办了一场关于 [Effective Altruism 的 Logic](http://bostonreview.net/forum/peter-singer-logic-effective-altruism) 的论坛，先由 Singer 撰文，再由一群人回应，最后由 Singer 作出 counter-response。阅读这些回应相当有意思，因为这或许是 effective altruism 迄今收到的最 thorough、cogent 且 intellectually solid 的一组 criticisms。

在他的 counter-rebuttal 中，Peter Singer 使出了一个 rhetorical move，我喜欢称之为“slippery utilitarian”：

> \[Angus\] Deaton、Daron Acemoglu、Iason Gabriel 和 Jennifer Rubenstein 都 suggest，effective altruists 很可能 neglect 那些能够 treat poverty 的 causes、而非 symptoms 的 large-scale political and economic reform。确实，我们无法通过 randomized trials 来 assess 这样的 action，但如果 large-scale reform 有一些 reducing poverty 的前景，那么 effective altruists 就会尝试 assess 它 doing good 的 chance，而如果这种 action 的 expected value 高于更 limited 的 interventions 的 expected value，他们就会 advocate 为这些 large-scale reforms 而努力。
> 
> 同一个 point 也适用于 Leila Janah 的 suggestion：helping the poor 的最 effective 的方式，是 support fair trade programs，或 start 那些 environmentally sustainable 且 pay a living wage 的 social businesses。它也同样适用于 Rubenstein 的 claim：一旦“low-hanging fruit”被摘完，reduce poverty 的努力就只有在与 poor countries 的 activists 合作并 follow the lead of 这些 activists 时才会 succeed。Effective altruism 不可能被某种 evidence 所 refuted，即其他某种 strategy 会比 effective altruists 正在使用的 strategy 更 effective，因为 effective altruists 到时就会 adopt 那种 strategy。

确实，“effective altruism”_作为_一个相对 uncontroversial 的 claim——你应该尝试尽可能 effectively 地 improve the world——无法以这种方式被 refuted。但 effective altruism 并不只是那个 claim；它也是一群试图弄清那个 claim 的 implications 并 act on 这些 implications 的人，而这些人当然可能把这一部分弄错！与任何 community 一样，EAers 都会就其 uncontroversial basic principles 之外的 claims 达成 consensus 或 near-consensus。而我认为，通过 criticizing 关于其 implications 的 consensus view、而不是 basic claim 本身来 criticize effective altruism，是 fair 的。

在我看来，这似乎正是 Deaton、Acemoglu、Gabriel 和 Rubenstein 试图表达的 point。Peter Singer 试图通过 arguing 来躲开他们的批评：如果他们偏爱的 strategies 真的 effective，那么 EAers 就会 adopt 它们。但这恰恰就是 critics 所指的问题——尽管这些 strategies 很有前景，EA community 却_没有_ adopted 它们（至少在 critics 看来如此）。一个 cogent 的 effective altruism defense 应该在这里作出回应：要么 explaining 为什么 critics 的 strategies 实际上并不是最好的前进道路，要么 conceding，EA community 在弄清自己应该如何 improve the world 时，已经 collectively gone astray。

## 原文

The Boston Review recently held a forum on [The Logic of Effective Altruism](http://bostonreview.net/forum/peter-singer-logic-effective-altruism), with an initial piece by Singer, responses by a bunch of people, and a final counter-response by Singer. Reading the responses was pretty interesting, as this is perhaps the most thorough, cogent, and intellectually solid set of criticisms that effective altruism has received.

In his counter-rebuttal, Peter Singer pulls a rhetorical move that I like to call the “slippery utilitarian:”

> \[Angus\] Deaton, Daron Acemoglu, Iason Gabriel, and Jennifer Rubenstein all suggest that effective altruists are likely to neglect the large-scale political and economic reform that would treat the causes, rather than the symptoms, of poverty. It is true that we can’t assess such action by randomized trials, but if large-scale reform offers some prospect of reducing poverty, then effective altruists will try to assess its chance of doing good, and if the expected value of such action is higher than the expected value of more limited interventions, they will advocate working for the large-scale reforms.
> 
> The same point holds for Leila Janah’s suggestion that the most effective way of helping the poor is to support fair trade programs or start social businesses that are environmentally sustainable and pay a living wage. It holds as well for Rubenstein’s claim that once the “low-hanging fruit” has been picked, efforts to reduce poverty will succeed only if they work with and follow the lead of activists in poor countries. Effective altruism cannot be refuted by evidence that some other strategy will be more effective than the one effective altruists are using, because effective altruists will then adopt that strategy.

It’s true that “effective altruism,” _qua_ relatively uncontroversial claim that you should try to improve the world as effectively as possible, can’t be refuted in this way. But effective altruism isn’t just that claim; it’s also a group of people trying to figure out and act on the implications of that claim, and those people are certainly able to get that part wrong! Like any community, EAers are going to reach a consensus or near-consensus on claims other than their uncontroversial basic principles. And I think it’s fair to criticize effective altruism by criticizing the consensus view of its implications rather than the basic claim itself.

To me, that seems to be the point that Deaton, Acemoglu, Gabriel and Rubenstein are trying to make. Peter Singer tries to get out of their sights by arguing that, if their favorite strategies were really effective, then EAers would adopt them. But that’s precisely what the critics are getting at—that the EA community _hasn’t_ adopted these strategies despite their promise (at least, in the critics’ views). A cogent defense of effective altruism should respond here by either explaining why the critics’ strategies aren’t really the best way forward, or conceding that the EA community has collectively gone astray when figuring out how they should improve the world.
