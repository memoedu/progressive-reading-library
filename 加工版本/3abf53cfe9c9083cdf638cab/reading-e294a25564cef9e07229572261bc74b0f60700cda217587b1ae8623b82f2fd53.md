# Nintil - There was only mom

---

## 正文

一天早上，我接到了弟弟 Jaime 的电话。他在哭。他告诉我，妈妈的健康状况在过去一个月里因 advanced cancer 而缓慢恶化，如今已经到了时日无多的地步。我惊呆了。我感到日常生活的惯性拉扯着自己：不想让工作中的人失望，还有我预订在下周参加的 meditation retreat。我在淋浴时哭了。上班路上，听着这些 Zen [chants](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bt9tOEHM14I)，我又哭了。

一切似乎都变得更加触动人心。singing bowls 的敲击声听起来斩钉截铁，仿佛在说：“快点！快点！时间快要耗尽了！凡有开始，必有终结！”碰巧那天我预约了与 therapist 的一次 session，我又哭了。“怎么会有人这样爱我，\[unconditionally\]？我不明白。”我的 therapist 几年前也失去了自己的母亲。“这就是母亲的爱。”她说。

我订了一张去她居住的加那利群岛的单程机票，搭了三趟飞机，20 小时后终于抵达。在飞机上，我也有许多次哭了出来。“要是我到了，她已经不在了怎么办？”“我还能和她说话吗？”抵达时，兄弟们来接我，我们开车回了家。爸爸刚做完 surgery，没法过来，所以只有我们兄弟三个，以及妈妈那边的家人，她有 13 个以上的兄弟姐妹。

![一家人在一起](https://nintil.com/images/2026-05-03-mom/family_together.jpg)

妈妈没有上过大学，和爸爸结婚后不久，她就留在家里抚养我们兄弟几个，不知疲倦地操劳。我记得，这几乎就是她生活的全部。她有时也会和朋友聚聚、看看电视，但除此之外就没什么了。她以前还抽很多烟，这也是我们这么早就走到这一步的原因。她才 61 岁，是兄弟姐妹中最小的，如今年纪最大的已经 82 岁了。我记得自己很小的时候，看见她对 tobacco 如此 addicted，心里十分害怕，而她同时又会提醒我们，千万不要开始抽烟。我试着帮她，只要看见烟盒，就藏起来或者扔掉，但都没有用，她真的很想抽烟。那时我不明白抽烟满足了她什么需要，现在我明白了。

过去几年，我们兄弟几个都已独立，她过得快乐多了：她戒了烟，也更愿意与人来往。她甚至加入了一个摩托车俱乐部！年轻时，她曾是个爱玩爱聚会的姑娘，如今那个她又回来了。

她一直爱我。即使我笨手笨脚，或者不善交际，或者觉得自己是 broken，她也一直爱我。即使我自己给自己设障，看不见她爱我的种种方式，她仍然爱我。无论我做什么，无论我说什么，她都爱着本来的我。

* * *

到了病房，我又哭了。

我在她身边埋下头，她抚摸着我的头发。她已经不能说话了，一根从喉咙里伸出的管子让她得以 breathe，一块白板让她能写下几个字与我们交流。至少她还是完全 lucid 的。她知道我们是谁，也能清楚地交流。她脸上没有太多表情，也许是因为一台不时发出刺耳哔哔声的 peristaltic pump，正在不断地将 painkillers pumped into 她体内。我哭着说：“原来是这样的。”“本来就该是这样的。”“原来缺失的是这个。”我觉得自己变得非常、非常小。

自从她在 2019 年第一次被 diagnosed with cancer 以来，我没有更常去看她，我为此请求她原谅。“你原谅我吗？”她写下“我原谅你”，我又哭了。她对我没有丝毫 ill will。

接下来的三天，我几乎没怎么睡，也没怎么吃。我们兄弟几个轮流在医院过夜，但即使身体躺在家里自己的床上，我的心也仍在医院。

我觉得自己仿佛身处一个梦境，只有妈妈是真实的。我心里的某些部分会问，为什么还有其他人？在那个世界里，我发了一条推文：“[只有我和妈妈](https://x.com/ArtirKel/status/2047950138526536064)”。有时，我会一边流泪，一边用 third person 对自己低声说话，躺在自己的床上时说：“José 喜欢鸟叫声。”躺在医院的沙发床上时说：“José 不喜欢医院里的声音。”“José 爱他的妈妈。”“José 的妈妈爱 José。”有一次，妈妈的一位朋友来探望她，问我还记不记得她是谁。我说：“不记得，你不存在，只有我妈妈和我的家人存在。”

我的 conceptual mind 试图理解这一切。那是 dissociation，还是 derealization？是向更年幼的 self 的 regression 吗？Toddlers 有时会用 third person 说自己。这让我觉得很亲切。事实上，从前，在很久很久以前，甚至在我的兄弟们出现之前，在爸爸见到我之前，那确实就是真相：只有妈妈。

![妈妈与 José](https://nintil.com/images/2026-05-03-mom/mom_jose.jpg)

我给她看自己生活中的一些照片，一边指，一边解释。把自己的生活带到她面前给她看时，我有些兴奋，但那种压倒一切的悲伤依然在。“看，这是我最好的朋友，也是以前的室友。”“看，我和她一起去了 burning man。”“看，这是我的第一任女朋友。”“看，这是我最近的一任女朋友。”我给她读自己写的那篇[我在 33 岁时学到的 33 件事](https://nintil.com/33-things)，一边读，一边翻译成西班牙语。我还给她读了 [Metta sutta（慈经）](https://x.com/ArtirKel/status/2048087340522913982)。

> 无论有怎样的 living beings；无论它们是 weak 还是 strong，一个也不遗漏，无论是 great 还是 mighty、medium、short 或 small，无论是 seen 还是 unseen，无论住得近还是远，无论是 born 还是 to-be-born，愿所有 beings 都能 at ease！
> 
> 愿没有谁 deceive 别人，也不 despise 任何 state 中的任何 being。愿没有谁因 anger 或 ill-will 而 Wish harm upon 别人。正如母亲以自己的生命 protects 她的孩子，她唯一的孩子，人也应怀着 boundless heart，cherish 所有 living beings；向整个世界 Radiating kindness。
> 
> 向上 Spreading 至天空，向下至深处；向外，且 unbounded，从 hatred 和 ill-will 中 Freed。

我一边读，一边哭。我告诉她，这就是现在的我。她写道：“你有很好的原则。”然后，她提醒我喝水。直到最后，她都一直想着我。

爸爸得以打来视频电话，我这辈子第一次看见他哭。我告诉他，我为他感到骄傲。我内心一个很年幼的部分很高兴：“一家人在一起！”虽然我们是在悲伤中相聚，但终究在一起，我们所有人都在一起。我已经很多年没有觉得自己和家人如此亲近了。

我告诉她，我正在写你此刻读到的这篇文章。不只是我永远不会忘记她，世界也不会。很多妈妈渐渐被人遗忘。如今已有无数人的一生被人遗忘。也许只要我写下来，你就真的永远不会被遗忘。“也许只要我写下来，你就永远不会死？”我内心一个很年幼的部分这样想着。

我说，我一直在想，如果我要纹身（我原本一个也没有），那一定会是与妈妈有关的东西，因为她的爱似乎是世上再确定不过的事。我们问她，应该纹什么。她写下“Siempre juntos <3”（永远在一起），于是我们兄弟三个一起纹了这句话。

![妈妈写下的永远在一起](https://nintil.com/images/2026-05-03-mom/siempre_juntos_mom.jpg)![我身上的永远在一起纹身](https://nintil.com/images/2026-05-03-mom/siempre_juntos_yo.jpg)

我告诉她，我一直在 emotions 和 meditation 上下很多功夫，现在我能 feel 自己的 feelings 了。我哭着告诉她，如今我相信，去 feeling anger、fear、sadness 都是可以的。在成长过程中，我曾 consciously chosen 去 repress 它们所有，我以为它们是 bad、irrational 的。我记得，年幼的 José 曾为自己能如此好地 suppress 自己的 strong emotions 而骄傲。

我觉得自己像个小孩，捡到了一块形状漂亮的石头，拿来给妈妈看：“看，妈妈！看！看！我找到自己的 feelings 了！这不是很棒吗？这不是很棒吗？？”她很开心。对她而言，一生中最重要的事就是让我们兄弟几个过上好日子。知道自己最想要的那一件事确实已经实现，她就能安心地离开了。

* * *

第二天或第三天发生了一件事，不过在那个梦里，时间已经不太重要了。

在她床边，我望着她的眼睛，说有时我觉得自己是 broken。然后，她看着我，脸上的神情，我只能用 fierce 或 wrathful love 来形容。她在说：‘别再说那些屁话了，你是我的儿子，你本来的样子就已经完全足够好了。’那份 wrath 不是冲着我，而是冲着那些 delusions，它们让我无法像她看我那样看待自己。我顿时伏在她身边哭了起来，我的 ignorance 被那双眼睛 obliterated，被妈妈的 unconditional love 的火焰 purified。她又抚摸了一会儿我的头发，我抬头时，看见了温暖。我坐到椅子上，闭上眼睛，试着 ground。我低声说“我是 broken”，那张脸便以 flashback 的方式浮现出来。“想都别想。”我又哭了，那个 thought 随之消散。“我是 broken。”我又低声说了一次，同样的事再次发生。我不停地说，直到“我是 broken”听起来成了某种傻话，就像说“我是奶酪做的”之类，明显错得如此离谱，根本没多少必要去想。

我曾有过 deep meditative experiences，曾因一种令人困惑却又确定无疑的 recognition 而哭泣：love 编织在 reality 最根本的纹理之中。那种 recognition 是，在一切存在之下，有一片 inmovable、炽烈燃烧着 purifying love 的火海，在它面前不可能有任何胜利：所有 delusions 都被 incinerated，关于自身 true nature 的所有 doubts，都被 thoroughly cut through。然而，即便如此，仍然有一种距离感。

而在妈妈面前，我觉得那份 vastness 正在看着我，具体地看着 José，一切存在都透过那双眼睛爱我，也透过那双眼睛看见它自己。我尽可能多地接纳这一切。我过去做 [jhanas](https://nintil.com/jhanas/) 和经历 [kensho](https://nintil.com/nonduality/) 的体验，与凝视那双眼睛相比，甚至根本不值一提。

你能望着妈妈的眼睛，郑重其事地告诉她你是 broken，而不哭成一摊泪水，不在瞬间 realising 自己对自身 nature 是 deluded 的吗？我当然做不到。我想起了和 therapist 的那次谈话。“怎么会有人这样爱我，\[unconditionally\]？”现在我明白了。我曾想直视太阳，直到自己的 retinas 被灼伤，而这个愿望实现了。

其中有一天，我陪了她整整一夜。我睡不着，这已经是连续第三天几乎没睡，甚至完全没睡。这些文字正浮现在我脑海里，自己写着自己。夜里，我两次扶她走去卫生间，让她小便。她走得很慢。后半夜，她尿在了尿布里，我帮她换了。我哭了：这曾是她为我做的第一件事，如今，在最后的时刻，我正为她做同样的事。她笑了。

一天，工作人员打扫病房时，我们在外面等候，一位 _celadoras_ 向我们做了自我介绍，原来她就是我很小时来家里打扫过卫生的人。我不记得她了，那已经是太久以前的事。但她还记得我。她说，她碰巧听见有人提到妈妈的名字，看到妈妈在这里，感到很意外。这得有多巧啊，我想，多么不可思议的巧合。而且，这是多么温暖的一幕，多么可爱的人：虽然我不记得她，却还记得家里曾经请过的另一位保洁阿姨，也记得妈妈和她是朋友。妈妈就是这样的人，她看得见那双眼睛背后的人。她曾有机会见到西班牙国王，我确信，她依然看见的是那个身份背后的人，就像她看待每一个人那样。

日子一天天过去，她越来越难以书写。她得把字反复重写许多遍，我们才能看懂。她在白板上写下的字中，各种 painkillers 的 brand names 占了越来越大的比例。她依然在那里，承受着 suffering。我们和她的 care team 谈过，一致同意将她 transitioned 到附近一家小医院的 palliative care unit。

第一晚，我陪着妈妈。这家医院原先是一座老旧的 tuberculosis sanatorium，收治的是一种如今在 developed countries 已不再令人担忧的 disease。我想，我看待它的方式——把它看作过去某种陌生的事物——也许有一天会成为人们看待 HIV、Alzheimer’s，乃至 cancer 的方式。医院的房间很大，工作人员温暖而友善。他们真诚地敞开着心扉。他们的眼里含着悲伤的泪水。我抽泣着问其中一位：“这怎么可能？”“你每天都看着这一切吗？”她回答：“是的，我在这里工作了 30 年。”我睁大眼睛说：“30 年？？30 年来每天都看着这些？？这怎么可能？”“我们是用不一样的东西做成的。”她温暖地笑了笑。他们也许真是如此。

那天，也就是 4 月 27 日晚些时候，妈妈接受了 heavy sedation。我一夜没睡，靠一大瓶健怡可乐撑着，让自己保持清醒。她最后一个 conscious action，是提出自己想要的事：小便。那时她已经不能动了，所以 nurses 得用一根 tube 来 drain 她的 urine。之后，她最后一次用温暖的目光望进我的眼睛。我握着她的手，她的手也握着我的。那里依然有一个人。

第二天早上，就没有了。也许还有 sensations 和 reactions。她在 breathing，但没有 moving。doctors 告诉我们，到了这个时候，已经没有 thoughts 或 emotions，就像 deep sleep。我上网查了查，似乎确实如此。她的脸上、身体上都没有 suffering 的 signs，没有因 discomfort 而皱眉，什么都没有。我们的一位表亲来看她，我们一边抚摸着她的身体，一边播放她最喜欢的歌曲之一，_[You’re Beautiful](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oofSnsGkops)_。“是啊，你很美。”我心想。即使在这样病弱的状态下也是如此。

* * *

回家后，我读了她之前用来交流的笔记本。快翻到末尾时，我看到：“我 struggle to breathe。我有很多 phlegms。我感到 anxious。”我想起了 Teaching of Ptahhotep 中的一段话。妈妈的 condition 与其中描述的完全一样：life ends 的方式，伴随着 mind 和 senses 的 dimming，以及 body 的 rotting away，如今和 4000 年前这些诗句写下时并无不同。

> Old age 已袭来，age 已降临，Feebleness 已到来，weakness 又在眼前。他整日都在 discomfort 中 Sleep。Eyes 已变得 small，ears 已 deaf，Mouth 已 silent，无法 speak，Heart 已 emptied，无法 recall 昨天。Bones 从头到脚都在 ache。Goodness 已变成 evil，一切 taste 都已消失。old age 对人所做的一切，无一不是 evil。Nose 已 blocked，无法 breathe，无论站着还是坐着，都觉得如此衰老。

妈妈的 faculties 正在一个接一个地 fading away：兄弟们和我不禁想，她还在那里吗？她是 conscious 的吗？consciousness 是什么？她在 suffering 吗？death 是什么？一个人究竟什么时候才算真的 die？

她似乎没有在 suffering。能有 pain 而没有 suffering 吗？我想起了 [Heart Sutra](https://appamada.pbworks.com/f/Heart%20Sutra-Red%20Pine.pdf) 中的几句话。此前几个月，我一直痴迷于它，如今却觉得它像一种预兆，仿佛正在一步步描述妈妈的离世。

> 因此，Shariputra，在 emptiness 中没有 form，没有 sensation，没有 perception，没有 memory，也没有 consciousness；没有 eye，没有 ear，没有 nose，没有 tongue，没有 body，也没有 mind；没有 shape，没有 sound，没有 smell，没有 taste，没有 feeling，也没有 thought；没有 element of perception，从 eye 到 conceptual consciousness 都没有

接着，我又想起了此前几个月里觉得格外意味深长的另一段话：

> Absolute light，在整个 universe 中 luminous；unfathomable excellence 正在到处 penetrating。愿 penetrating light 能 dispel ignorance 的 darkness，让一切 karma 都被 wiped out，让 mind flower 在 eternal spring 中 bloom

我又哭了。那些话听起来如此美丽，如此纯净。一切都显得如此珍贵。

经历了那些日子，我最终开始感激 brain 的工具箱里还有 derealization、depersonalization 和 dissociation，能够 cope with 那些强烈到根本无法 feel 的 emotions。此前几个月，我一直努力做到“feel 一切，而不 resist 任何东西”，但似乎有时候，skillful 的做法恰恰是不去 feel 全部。强迫自己去 feel 超出自身所能 feel 的东西，就是不对的。

与这一切同时进行的，还有 paperwork：我一边努力保持 emotionally present，一边翻阅成堆的旧文件，学习 Spain 如何 handles inheritances，以及当你住在国外时 taxes 如何 work。4 月 29 日早上 10:15，我正在 notary’s office，弟弟发来消息，说她刚刚[去世了](https://x.com/ArtirKel/status/2049421290310643730)。那是一种奇怪的感觉。时间停住了。但我周围的生活仍在继续。什么都没有改变，一切却都改变了。

她的 funeral 是一场 Catholic mass，这是当地的习俗。家里很多人信奉 Catholic faith，虽然我从小接受 Catholic 教养，但如今已不再信奉。过去我会觉得 mass 有点傻，可这一次，我发现自己也加入了 prayers。多年在 Catholic school 就读时学过的词句，我依然记得。我曾体验过包含 drums、fire 和 chanting 的 rituals 所具有的 transformational power，相比之下，这一场显得平淡。但我们拥有的就是这些。兄弟们和我都有机会发言。通常，我会提前准备些什么，没完没了地排练，然后又感到 anxious，担心自己会说错。但这一次，我选择不去想，相信无论从心里涌出什么，都会是恰当的话语，哪怕很简短。涌出的是：

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Todo lo que tengo se lo debo a mi madre | Everything I have, I owe to my mom |
| Todo lo que conseguiré en mi vida se lo debo a mi madre | Everything I will accomplish in my life, I owe to my mom |
| He tenido la suerte de tener una madre que me ha querido desde que nací hasta que falleció | I have been lucky to have a mom that loved me from the time I was born to the time she passed away |
| Que su amor viva a través de todos nosotros | May her love live through all of us |

## 原文

One morning I got a call from my younger brother Jaime. He was crying. He tells me my mom’s health, which had been slowly declining over the past month from advanced cancer, had reached the point where not much time was left. I was shocked. I felt the inertia of my regular life: not wanting to let people at work down, the meditation retreat I had booked for next week. I cried in the shower. I cried on the way to work as I listened to these Zen [chants](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bt9tOEHM14I).

Everything just seemed to hit harder. The strikes of the singing bowls sounded definitive, saying “Hurry up! Hurry up! Time is running out! Everything that begins ends!” By chance I had a session booked with my therapist that very day and once again I cried. “How can someone love me like this \[unconditionally\]? I don’t understand”. My therapist had lost her own mother some years prior. “That’s a mother’s love”, she said.

I booked a one way ticket to the Canary Islands where she lived, and three flights and 20 hours later I got there. There were many moments where I also cried on the plane. “What if I arrive and she’s not alive?” “Will I be able to talk to her?” When I arrived I was greeted by my brothers, and we drove home. My dad just had surgery and couldn’t come, so it’d just be the three of us, and my mother’s own family, over 13 siblings.

![family\_together](https://nintil.com/images/2026-05-03-mom/family_together.jpg)

My mom never went to college, and shortly after marrying my dad she stayed at home raising me and my brothers, working tirelessly. I remember that was most of what her life was. Sometimes she would hang out with her friends and watch TV, but that was it. She also used to smoke a lot, which is why we were here this early, she was only 61, the youngest of her siblings, with the oldest being 82 now. I remember being very young and horrified at seeing her addicted to tobacco while at the same time she would remind us to never start smoking. I tried to help her by hiding or throwing away the packs of cigarettes if I could see them, to no avail, she really wanted to smoke. At the time I didn’t understand what need the smoking was serving, but now I do.

Her past few years, with my brothers and I now independent, were much happier: she quit smoking, became more social. She even joined a motorbiking club! In her youth she used to be a party girl and she was back.

She always loved me. Even when I was clumsy or socially awkward, or when I thought I was broken, she always loved me. Even when I got in my own way and was blind to the ways she loved me, she loved me still. No matter what I did, and no matter what I said, she loved me as I was.

* * *

When we got to the hospital room, I cried yet again.

I sank my head by her side and she stroked my hair. She could no longer speak, a tube coming out of her throat let her breathe, and a whiteboard let her write a few words to communicate. At least she was fully lucid. She knew who we were, she could communicate well. Her face didn’t have much expression, perhaps as a result from the constant stream of painkillers pumped into her by an ungently-beeping peristaltic pump. I cried “this is how it was”, “this is how it is supposed to be” “this is what was missing”. I felt very very small.

I asked her for forgiveness for not having seen her more often since she was first diagnosed with cancer back in 2019. “Do you forgive me?” She wrote “I forgive you” and I cried yet again. She had an utter lack of ill will towards me whatsoever.

I didn’t sleep or eat much for the following three days. My brothers and I took turns to stay at the hospital overnight, but even when my body was in my own bed at home my mind was still in the hospital.

I felt like I was in a dreamworld where only my mother was real. Parts of me asked why were there other people? From within that world I tweeted “[there’s just me and my mom](https://x.com/ArtirKel/status/2047950138526536064)”. At times I was whispering to myself in third person amid the tears “José likes the sound of the birds” as I lied in my bed, “José doesn’t like the sounds of the hospital” as I lied in a sofa-bed at the hospital. “José loves his mom”. “José’s mom loves José”. At some point a friend of my mom that was visiting her asked me if I remembered who she was. I said “No, you don’t exist, only my mom and my family exist”.

My conceptual mind was trying to make sense of all of this. Was that dissociation or derealization? A regression to a younger self? Toddlers speak about themselves in the third person sometimes. It felt endearing. The truth is that once upon a time, a long long time ago, even before my brothers were there, even before my dad saw me, that was the truth: There was only mom.

![mom\_jose](https://nintil.com/images/2026-05-03-mom/mom_jose.jpg)

I showed her some pictures from my life, I pointed and explained. There was some excitement there as I brought her my life for her to see but the overwhelming sadness was still there. “Look this is my best friend and ex roommate”, “Look I went with her to burning man”, “Look this was my first girlfriend”, “Look this was my last girlfriend”. I read her my [33 things I’ve learned at 33](https://nintil.com/33-things) post, translating to Spanish as I went. I read her the [Metta sutta](https://x.com/ArtirKel/status/2048087340522913982).

> Whatever living beings there may be; Whether they are weak or strong, omitting none, The great or the mighty, medium, short or small, The seen and the unseen, Those living near and far away, Those born and to-be-born May all beings be at ease!
> 
> Let none deceive another, Or despise any being in any state. Let none through anger or ill-will Wish harm upon another. Even as a mother protects with her life Her child, her only child, So with a boundless heart Should one cherish all living beings; Radiating kindness over the entire world.
> 
> Spreading upwards to the skies, And downwards to the depths; Outwards and unbounded, Freed from hatred and ill-will.

I cried through my reading. This is who I am now, I told her. She wrote “You have good principles”. Then she reminded me to drink water. Always thinking about me, to the very end.

My dad was able to videocall and for the first time in my life I saw him crying. I told him I was proud of him. A very young part of me was happy “A family together!” Together in the sadness as we were, but together nonetheless, all of us. I hadn’t felt as connected to my family in years.

I told her I was writing this very piece you are reading right now. That not only I would never forget her but the world wouldn’t either. Many moms go forgotten. There are uncountable lives that are now forgotten. Maybe if I write this you will truly never be forgotten. “Maybe if I write this you will never die?”, thought a very young part of me.

I said that I had been thinking that if I were to get a tattoo (I didn't have any) it would be something about my mom, as her love seemed as certain as things go. We asked her what it should be. She wrote "Siempre juntos <3" (Always together) and all three brothers got that tattoo together.

![siempre\_juntos\_mom](https://nintil.com/images/2026-05-03-mom/siempre_juntos_mom.jpg)![siempre\_juntos\_yo](https://nintil.com/images/2026-05-03-mom/siempre_juntos_yo.jpg)

I told her I’ve been doing a lot of work with emotions and meditation and that now I can feel my feelings. I told her, as I cried, that now I believe feeling anger, fear, sadness are all okay to feel. Growing up I had consciously chosen to repress them all, I thought they were bad and irrational. I remember the young José feeling pride at how well he could suppress his strong emotions.

I felt like a small child coming to show her mom a nicely shaped rock he found “Look mom! Look! Look! I found my feelings! Isn’t this great? Isn’t this great??” She was happy. For her, the most important thing in her life had been to give me and my brothers a good life. She would go in peace knowing that the one thing she had wanted the most is something she actually got.

* * *

There was something that happened on the second or third day, but time didn’t matter much in that dream.

By her bed I said, holding eye contact, that sometimes I think I’m broken. She then looked at me with an expression that I can only describe as fierce or wrathful love. She was saying ‘drop that bullshit, you are my son, you are perfectly good as you are’. The wrath wasn’t directed at me, it was directed at the delusions that kept me from seeing myself as she sees me. I instantly collapsed crying by her side, my ignorance obliterated by those eyes, purified by the flames of my mom’s unconditional love. She stroked my hair a bit more, when I looked up there was warmth. I sat on a chair and closed my eyes, trying to ground. I whispered “I’m broken” and had a flashback of that face. “Don’t even dare”. I again cried, as the thought evaporated. “I’m broken”, I whispered again, and the same happened. I kept saying it until “I’m broken” ended up sounding like something silly, like saying “I’m made of cheese” or something like that, something so obviously wrong that there isn’t much point thinking about it.

I’ve had deep meditative experiences where I’ve cried at the confusing yet certain recognition that love is woven into the very fabric of reality. That’s the recognition that underneath all there is, there's an inmovable blazing inferno of purifying love against which there is no victory possible: all delusions are incinerated, all doubts about one's true nature, thoroughly cut through. But still, there's a sense of distance.

With my mom it felt like that vastness was looking at me, at José specifically, all of what there is loves me through those eyes and sees itself through those eyes. I tried to take in as much of that as I could. My past experiences of doing the [jhanas](https://nintil.com/jhanas/) and having a [kensho](https://nintil.com/nonduality/) don’t even begin to compare to looking into those eyes.

Can you look into your mom’s eyes and in all seriousness tell her that you are broken and not collapse into a puddle of tears, realising instantly that you are deluded about your own nature? I certainly couldn't. I remembered the conversation I had with my therapist. “How can someone love me like this \[unconditionally\]?”. Now I understand. I wanted to stare into the sun until my retinas burned, and I got that wish granted.

One of the days I stayed with her the whole night. I couldn’t sleep, it was the third day in a row of not sleeping much if at all. These very words were appearing in my mind, writing themselves. At night I helped her walk twice to the bathroom so she could pee. She walked slowly. Later in the night she peed in her diaper and I helped change it. I cried: that was the first thing she did for me, and now I’m doing it for her at the very end. She smiled.

As we waited outside when they were cleaning the room one day, one of the _celadoras_ introduced herself to us, and it turned out she was the one that used to come clean our house when I was very young. I didn’t remember her, it was too long ago. But she did remember me. She said it was a coincidence that she heard my mother’s name around and was surprised to see her there. What are the odds, I thought, what a miraculous coincidence. But also, what a lovely situation, what a lovely person: Though I didn’t remember this person, I did remember another cleaning lady we had, and I remember my mom and her were friends. That’s how she was, she saw the person underneath the eyes. She once got to meet the King of Spain and I am sure she still saw the person underneath just like she does with everyone.

As the days went she struggled to write. She had to rewrite words many times for us to understand. A growing fraction of the words she wrote in her whiteboard were the brand names of the various painkillers. She was still there, suffering. We talked with her care team and agreed that she would be transitioned to the palliative care unit, located at a small nearby hospital.

I stayed the first night with my mom. The hospital used to be an old tuberculosis sanatorium, a hospital for a disease that no longer is a concern in developed countries. I thought that the way I looked at it, something foreign in the past, may one day be how people think of HIV, Alzheimer’s, or even cancer. The hospital had large rooms, the personnel were warm and kind. They were truly open hearted. Their eyes were wet with sadness. I asked one of them, sobbing, “how is this possible?” “You see this every day?” she replied “yes, I’ve worked here for 30 years”. My eyes opened and said “30 years?? 30 years of seeing this daily?? How is this possible?” “We are made of something else”, she warmly smiled. They may as well be.

My mom was put on heavy sedation that day, later on the 27th of April. I stayed up all night, armed with a large bottle of diet coke to stay awake. Her last conscious action was asking for what she wanted: to pee. She couldn’t move at that point, so the nurses had to drain the urine with a tube. After that with a final warm look, she looked into my eyes, I held her hand, her hand held mine. There was still a person in there.

The next morning there wasn’t. There were sensations and reactions perhaps. She was breathing, but not moving. The doctors told us that at that point there are no thoughts or emotions, it’s like deep sleep. I searched online, and it seemed to be the case. There were no signs of suffering in her face or her body, no frowns of discomfort, nothing. One of our cousins came to visit, and while touching her body, we played one of her favorite songs, _[You’re Beautiful](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oofSnsGkops)_. “Yes you are”, I thought. Even in that sickly state.

* * *

When I got home I read the notebook she used to use to communicate. By the end of it I found “I struggle to breathe. I have lots of phlegms. I feel anxious” A quote from the Teaching of Ptahhotep came to mind. My mom’s condition was exactly the very same described there: the way life ends, with a dimming of the mind and senses and a rotting away of the body is the same now as it was 4000 years ago when these verses were written.

> Old age has struck, age has descended, Feebleness has arrived, weakness is here again. Sleep is upon him in discomfort all day. Eyes are grown small, ears deaf, Mouth silent, unable to speak, Heart emptied, unable to recall yesterday. Bones ache his whole length. Goodness has turned to evil, All taste is gone. What old age does to people is evil in every way. Nose is blocked, unable to breathe, how old it feels standing or sitting.

My mom’s faculties were fading away one by one: my brothers and I wondered is she still there? Is she conscious? What is consciousness? Is she suffering? What is death? When does someone really die?

She didn’t seem to be suffering. Can there be pain without suffering? Lines from the [Heart Sutra](https://appamada.pbworks.com/f/Heart%20Sutra-Red%20Pine.pdf) came to mind. I had been obsessed with it for the months prior and now it felt like a presage, like it was describing the passing away of my mom step by step.

> Therefore, Shariputra, in emptiness there is no form, no sensation, no perception, no memory and no consciousness; no eye, no ear, no nose, no tongue, no body and no mind; no shape, no sound, no smell, no taste, no feeling and no thought; no element of perception, from eye to conceptual consciousness

My mind then hopped to other words that had seemed particularly meaningful the months prior:

> Absolute light, luminous throughout the whole universe; unfathomable excellence penetrating everywhere. May penetrating light dispel the darkness of ignorance, let all karma be wiped out and the mind flower bloom in eternal spring

I cried yet again. It sounded so beautiful, so pure. Everything seemed so precious.

Through those days I ended up being grateful that derealization, depersonalization, and dissociation are there in the brain’s toolbox to cope with emotions so intense that just can’t be felt. For the few months prior I had been endeavoring to “feel everything and resist nothing”, but it seemed like sometimes the skillful thing to do is to not feel it all. Forcing oneself to feel what is beyond what one can feel is just wrong.

In parallel with all of this there was the paperwork: while trying to be emotionally present, I was also reviewing piles of old papers, learning how Spain handles inheritances, how taxes work when you live outside of the country. It was at a notary’s office, the morning of the 29th of April at 10:15 when my brother texted us that she had just [passed away](https://x.com/ArtirKel/status/2049421290310643730). It was a strange sensation. Time stopped. But also life kept going around me. Nothing had changed and everything had changed.

Her funeral was a Catholic mass, as is the local custom. Many in the family are in the Catholic faith, and though I was raised Catholic, I’m no longer. In the past I would have found mass kind of silly but this time I found myself joining the prayers. I still remembered the lines from years of Catholic school. Having experienced the transformational power of rituals involving drums, fire, and chanting, this one was underwhelming. But this was what we had. My brothers and I got to speak. I would usually have prepared something, rehearse it endlessly and then feel anxious that maybe I would say it wrong. But this time I chose to not think about it and trust that whatever came from my heart would be the right words, even if brief. What came was:

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| Todo lo que tengo se lo debo a mi madre | Everything I have, I owe to my mom |
| Todo lo que conseguiré en mi vida se lo debo a mi madre | Everything I will accomplish in my life, I owe to my mom |
| He tenido la suerte de tener una madre que me ha querido desde que nací hasta que falleció | I have been lucky to have a mom that loved me from the time I was born to the time she passed away |
| Que su amor viva a través de todos nosotros | May her love live through all of us |
