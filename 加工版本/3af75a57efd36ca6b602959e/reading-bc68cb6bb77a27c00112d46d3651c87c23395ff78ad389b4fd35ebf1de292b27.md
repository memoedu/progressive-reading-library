# 进一步的问题

---

## 正文

[许多 economists 说](http://www.bloombergview.com/articles/2014-08-28/is-technology-stagnating)，我们正经历一场 Great Stagnation。这个术语因 Tyler Cowen 的[同名著作](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Stagnation)而广为人知，而最新的版本当然就是 Robert Gordon 的 [The Rise and Fall of American Growth](http://press.princeton.edu/titles/10544.html)。

但他们通常关注的是 economic factors，例如 Total Factor Productivity (TFP) 或 GDP per hour worked。按照这些 measures，[我们正经历一个 stagnant period](http://www.brookings.edu/%7E/media/Research/Files/Interactives/2014/thinktank20/chapters/tt20-united-states-economic-growth-gordon.pdf?la=en)。有人原本预期，一旦我们恰当地 accounted for Internet 以及类似的难以 account for 的 innovations，stagnation 就会消失，但显然[这件事已经做过了](http://www.brookings.edu/about/projects/bpea/papers/2016/byrne-et-al-productivity-measurement)，而情况并没有太大变化。

在这篇文章中，我采用 engineering perspective，直接考察 technology 本身。我认为这种 approach 尚未得到充分 explored：我们应该全面考察 technology 随时间发生的 quantifiable changes。productivity 固然重要，但既然 technology 被认为是 TFP 的一个 substantial component，为什么不直接考察它呢？

我在这里的观点是，按照我们现有的 measures，并不存在严重的 _technological_ stagnation（但这与 economic growth 的 slowdown 是 compatible 的）。我们实际看到的是某些 sectors 的 stagnation（Aircraft speed）、另一些的 improvements（Gene editing、photovoltaics），以及少数的 worsening（Cost per new drug approved）。此外，这些 trends 似乎并没有在 1970 年发生 radically change，而 economic stagnation 看起来正是从那时开始的。我们面对的是一系列 drivers——将在后续文章中探讨——它们 **individually** influence 这些 technologies 的走向。因此，我认为重要的是 individually 考察它们，而不是像从 economic lens 出发时那样将它们 aggregate 起来，然后说一切都在 stagnating，因为事实显然并非如此，即使我们把 software 排除在考虑之外也是如此。

所以，并不存在全面、普遍的严重 technological stagnation，至少在这些 technologies 中没有。从现有的 timeseries 看不出它。对于某一项 technology，progress 往往会 follow 一个 constant law，直到它再也不可能 progress，此时就会发生向另一项 technology 的 switch。

即使对于更难 measure 的 breakthrough innovation，我也会说它并没有 slowed down，尽管[有人认为 rate of innovation per capita 已经](http://anthonyburgoyne.com/2011/10/12/jonathan-huebner-john-smart-and-the-rate-of-technological-change/)[declined](http://old.adamsmith.org/blog/economics/are-we-innovating-less/)（但 population 已经 grown，所以情况没那么糟）。而且，即使这是真的，它也不是从 1970 年开始的，而是从 1905 年开始的，因此它是否会成为 70 年代之后那场 great stagnation 的 cause，并不显而易见。

我并不是说 Robert Gordon 总体上错了。请阅读[这篇文章](https://growthecon.com/blog/Decomposition)，了解为什么 individual technologies 可以有 growth，同时总体上却出现 productivity slowdown。

    > Gordon 所说的 stagnation，是 innovation 带来的 economic growth 中的 stagnation，这里的 economic 是其根本意义：流向 households 的 goods 和 services。（Cowen 的思考不那么精确。）
    > 
    > 许多人都提出过与你相同的观点，但对于 innovation 的 reducing impact，还有一些其他 perspectives，我很少见到，甚至从未见到过。其中两个也许可以称为“ecological” argument 和 dispersive discovery model。
    > 
    > 不同 innovations 具有不同 impacts，Smil 等人反复提出过这一点。high-impact、pervasive innovations 很少见，而大部分 innovations 的 impact 或多或少是 infinitesimal 的。
    > 
    > 由于 diminishing marginal utility，economic growth 只能通过 increasing 提供给 households 的 services 的 range 和 diversity 来 sustained。但 increasing diversity 本身就会 reduces 每个 individual innovation 的 impact。
    > 
    > 要理解这一点，看看 18 世纪的 economy。economy 的 60% 是 agriculture，其中的 60% 是 grain growing。一项将 grain growing productivity improved 5% 的 innovation，会将整个 economy 的 productivity increased 1.8%。快进到 21 世纪。最大的单一 industry，即 healthcare，可能占 economy 的 10%，而 healthcare 本身又非常 diverse（如今所有 large industries 都是如此）。一项将整个 healthcare 的 productivity increases 5% 的 innovation 不太可能出现，即使真出现了，它的 global effect 也只有 0.5%。
    > 
    > 这可以与 forest ecosystem 不断 increasing 的 diversity 和 complexity 相比较：pioneer plants 占据 bare land，随后被范围越来越广的新 species 的 organisms 所 succeeded。起初 total biomass increases，但随着时间推移，它会 stabilises，而 new species 越来越多地与 existing ones compete，而非 complementing 它们。
    > 
    > 要点在于，我们应该预期 innovation 的 benefits 随着 economy grows 而 decline。我们看到的现象并不令人意外。
    > 
    > dispersive discovery model 设想在“innovation space”中进行 random search。Potential innovations 在 impact size 和 difficulty of production 上有所不同。有些 innovations 只有在其他 innovations 已被 exploited 之后才能发生——它们被其他 innovations masked 了。
    > 
    > 这里的 analogy 是 search for oil。Oil deposits 的 size、depth 和 cost of production 各不相同。Drillers 选择一个 location 和一个 target depth，然后 drill down，直到到达其 target depth 或 strike oil。接着，如果有所发现，他们就 exploit 所发现的东西。Large、easily found 且 high-quality（low cost）的 deposits 很罕见——其 distribution 顶多也就是 log-normal。
    > 
    > 同样，这一 perspective 表明，innovative effort 的 returns 应该随着时间推移而 decline。这正是我们所看到的。
    > 
    > 传统上，人们用一些夸大而空洞的 assertions 来 countered 这类 arguments，声称人类具有 infinite demands 和 infinite resourcefulness。对此，我要说，太阳底下没有什么是无限的。Strong assertions 需要 strong evidence，而这样的 evidence 从未被提供过；一旦你 introduce infinities，你就离开了 quantitative analysis 的领域，进入了 rhetoric。

## 原文

[Many economists say](http://www.bloombergview.com/articles/2014-08-28/is-technology-stagnating) we are living through a Great Stagnation. The term, was made famous by Tyler Cowen's[book of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Stagnation) and the latest iteration is, of course, Robert Gordon's [The Rise and Fall of American Growth](http://press.princeton.edu/titles/10544.html).

But they usually look at economic factors like Total Factor Productivity (TFP) or GDP per hour worked. By these measures, [we are living through a stagnant period](http://www.brookings.edu/%7E/media/Research/Files/Interactives/2014/thinktank20/chapters/tt20-united-states-economic-growth-gordon.pdf?la=en). Some expected that the stagnation would go away once we properly accounted for the Internet and similar hard to account for innovations, but apparently [this was done](http://www.brookings.edu/about/projects/bpea/papers/2016/byrne-et-al-productivity-measurement), and the picture doesn't change much.

In this post, I take an engineering perspective and look directly at technology itself. I think this approach has not been sufficiently explored: We should look at quantifiable changes in technology over time, across the board. Surely productivity is important, but since technology is supposed to be a substantial component of TFP, why not look at it directly?

My point here is that, by the measures we have, there is no great _technological_ stagnation (But this is compatible with a slowdown in economic growth). What we do have is stagnation in some sectors (Aircraft speed), improvements in others (Gene editing, photovoltaics), and worsening in a few (Cost per new drug approved). Furthermore, these trends don't seem to change radically in 1970, which is when the economic stagnation seemingly begins. What we have are a series of drivers -to be explored in later posts - that **individually** influence where these technologies go. Thus I think it is important to look at them individually rather than aggregate them as one would from an economic lens and say that everything is stagnating, because that's clearly not the case, even if we take software out of the equation.

So there is no great technological stagnation across the board everywhere, at least in these technologies. From available timeseries one cannot see it. For a given technology, progress tends to follow a constant law until it's not possible anymore for it to progress, in which case a switch to a different technology happens.

Even for breakthrough innovation, harder to measure, I would say that it has not slowed down, even when [some argue the rate of innovation per capita has](http://anthonyburgoyne.com/2011/10/12/jonathan-huebner-john-smart-and-the-rate-of-technological-change/) [declined](http://old.adamsmith.org/blog/economics/are-we-innovating-less/) (But population has grown, so it's not that bad). And even if that's true, it didn't began in 1970, but in 1905, so it's not obvious that it would be the cause of the post-70s great stagnation.

I do not say Robert Gordon is wrong in general. Please read [this post](https://growthecon.com/blog/Decomposition)for reasons why you can have growth in individual technologies and productivity slowdown in general.

    > The stagnation that Gordon talks about is in economic growth from innovation, in the radical sense of economic: the flow of goods and services to households. (Cowen is a less precise thinker.)
    > 
    > Many people have made the same points you have, but there are some other perspectives on the reducing impact of innovation that I have seen only rarely or not at all. Two of them might be called the "ecological" argument and the dispersive discovery model.
    > 
    > Different innovations have different impacts, a point made repeatedly by Smil and others. The high-impact, pervasive innovations are rare, while the bulk of innovations have more or less infinitesimal impact.
    > 
    > Because of diminishing marginal utility, economic growth can only be sustained by increasing the range and diversity of services provided to households. But increasing diversity intrinsically reduces the impact of each individual innovation.
    > 
    > To see this, consider the economy of the 18th century. 60% of the economy was agriculture, and 60% of that was grain growing. An innovation that improved grain growing productivity by 5% increased productivity of the whole economy by 1.8%. Fast forward to the 21st century. The largest single industry, healthcare, makes up maybe 10% of the economy, and healthcare in itself is very diverse (as are all large industries these days). An innovation that increases productivity in the whole of healthcare by 5% is unlikely to occur, and if one did eventuate, its global effect would be only 0.5%.
    > 
    > This can be compared to the increasing diversity and complexity of a forest ecosystem as pioneer plants take over bare land, and are succeeded by a bigger and bigger range of new species of organisms. At first total biomass increases, but over time it stabilises and new species increasingly compete with existing ones rather than complementing them.
    > 
    > The takeaway is that we should expect the benefits of innovation to decline as an economy grows. What we see is no surprise.
    > 
    > The dispersive discovery model imagines a random search through "innovation space". Potential innovations differ in impact size and in difficulty of production. Some innovations can only take place after others have been exploited - they are masked by the others.
    > 
    > The analogy here is the search for oil. Oil deposits vary in size, depth and cost of production. Drillers choose a location and a target depth, and drill down until they reach their target depth or they strike oil. They then exploit what they have found, if anything. Large, easily found and high-quality (low cost) deposits are rare - the distribution is log-normal at best.
    > 
    > Again, this perspective suggests that the returns to innovative effort should decline over time. Which is what we see.
    > 
    > Traditionally, arguments like these have been countered by pompous and vacuous assertions of the infinite demands and infinite resourcefulness of humans. To which I say, nihil sub solem infinita est. Strong assertions demand strong evidence, which has never been provided; and once you introduce infinities, you have left the realm of quantitative analysis and entered rhetoric.
