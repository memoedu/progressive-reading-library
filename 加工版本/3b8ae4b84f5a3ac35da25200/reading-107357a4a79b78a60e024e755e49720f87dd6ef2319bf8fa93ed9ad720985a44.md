# 如果我在 2026 年从头开始，我会如何建立 one-person business

观看这封信的视频版本：[YouTube](https://youtu.be/VyR8nqD3sQ8)。

收听这封信的音频版本：[Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/episode/2uMfHI1LjAI4NORsEHqR1w?si=IBxOKMeORliruAHRZGv5Rg)。

* * *

一切都在变化。

Business、internet、social media、coding、art，一切都在变化。去年才奏效的东西，今年奏效的可能性已经小得多。

我不会告诉你去创办 agency，也不会告诉你去 freelancing、coaching，或去做我已经深入谈过很多次的其他低垂果实。

相反，我要向你准确展示 _此刻_ 什么类型的 business 才有意义。

我一直看到有人说“value-based content 已死”，也有人说“info products 已死”。人们觉得，当一种他们尚未理解的新 paradigm 正在兴起时，作为 creator 开始已经不值得了（我不认为有人真正理解它。我不理解。这封信充其量只是一个经过研究的猜测）。

我们来谈谈 one-person business 正在如何变化、education 的未来会是什么样，以及如何真正利用这一点。

### I – 一人 business model 正在 evolving

> 财富需要 leverage。Business leverage 来自 capital、people，以及没有 marginal cost of replication 的 products（code 和 media）。
>
> – Naval

在过去十年左右，建立 cash-flow 高、利润率高得惊人的 business，最佳方式是做 information products 或提供 coaching services。

这很棒，因为有了 internet、social media 和 software，_一个人就能创办整个 business_。这简直不可思议。一个人就能拥有自己的 marketing、sales、product 和 design departments。显然，有了 agentic AI，这比以往任何时候都更可行——我们以后还会在其他信件中继续深入讨论。

在这个 digital world 里，你创作 content 来获取 traffic，不必在合适地点拥有实体 business。你可以在生活中做些有趣的事，或者充分学习自己的一项 passion，直到成为有价值的 resource，然后以教育性或娱乐性的方式与他人分享。你的 brand 就是你自己，你的 content 是你思想的一小片，你的 products 则是那些曾解决你生活中某个问题、并让生活变得更好的 processes。很多人把这件事想得过于复杂。这件事永远不会消失，但你用来做这件事的 _vessel_ 会持续变化，因此你需要持续关注一切如何演进。

写这些信的全部目标，是帮助你做到 future-proof。它们围绕 2 个 pillars 构建：

1.  你如何弄清自己在生活中想要什么（psychology、philosophy、personal development）

2.  在当今世界，你如何凭借这些实现成功并抵达目标（skill acquisition、business、technology、AI）

以前，第二部分主要通过在 social media 上打造 personal brand，以及某种 information product 来实现，比如简单的 ebook（level 1）、course（level 2）、cohort（level 3）、community（level 4）。

但既然人人都在谈论“info products 和 value-based content 已死”，我认为背后还有更深层的原因。这个 process 仍然一样。你仍然追寻自己的好奇心和兴趣，并与他人分享，但如今用来做这件事的 vessel 已不再是静态 PDF、video course 或 community。

> **我想把这点说得非常清楚**：我并不属于那种愤世嫉俗的一派，他们认为现在人人都有 info product，所以它们全是骗局。我认为它们能够改变人生，对我而言确实如此。我认为它们是传统 education 的负面方面（即从众，以及被训练去做自己讨厌的工作）的解药。我不认为它们会彻底消失；我在这封信里说的内容也并不适用于每一个 industry 或 niche。不过，和当下几乎所有 career sector 一样（这要归功于 AI），能取得成效的基准线已经提高了。

现在，development level 较低的普通人也能走“快速简便的路线”，让 AI 创作有说服力的 ebook、撰写 viral content、生成 images 和 assets。他们可以赚到某种收入，但永远无法与那些理解正在发生什么的人竞争。

换句话说，创办 one-person business 从未像现在这样容易。

但如果你不想处于 creator economy 的低端阶层（我不喜欢这么说，但它确实存在），就需要明白一件非常重要的事。

### II – 我们正在进入 market 的 stage 6

Markets 会经历可预测的 awareness 和 saturation stages。

Eugene Schwartz 将它们归为 5 个 stages。

在 level one，当市场上还没有其他人时，你只要说明 product 的作用，通常就足以奏效。接着 competitors 出现，你就得提出更大的 claims。然后 market 开始怀疑，于是你要解释自己的 mechanism（说“我会帮你在网上赚钱”开始听起来像骗局，你就必须更具体，比如“实施 [2 Hour Content Ecosystem](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-build-a-world-the-2-hour-content)”）。接着 competitors 会复制那个 mechanism。最后，所有人都厌倦了各种 claims，_brand_ 开始成为主要的 differentiator——这正是 personal branding 当下正在发生的事。人们渴望归属感，而加入一个有 mission 的 tribe 会带来这种感觉（我以前谈过[你的 mission 就是你的 niche](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/the-most-profitable-niche-in-the?lli=1)）。相比谁的 product 听起来最好，Community 和 authenticity 更重要。

我们已经全面进入最后一个 stage。甚至到了“authenticity”也不再是 differentiating factor 的地步。人们只是厌倦了那些老一套的 courses 和 coaching。这很糟，因为那些东西确实有影响力，但事情不止于此。

少数足够聪明、能够整体上 _超越_ info products 的人，才会成为赢家。超越并不意味着把它们抛在身后，而是意味着进化到下一个 level，并将它们整合进来，因为 education/learning 作为一个 domain 永远不会消失。

但因为你是以 _一个人_ 的身份在某个 space 里竞争，saturation 可能很快就会到来。我要告诉你的东西不会让你长久立于不败之地，因为下一个 level 就在不远处。Info products 持续的时间并不长（也许 10-15 年？），下一阶段也不会持续那么久（也许 2-3 年？）。谁知道之后的 business 会是什么样。我们要为明天而不是一年后而打造。Iteration cycles 会短得令人难以置信。Timescales 会被压缩。

作为 one-person business，最大的优势是 speed 和 adaptability。你必须持续行动和迭代，直到达到 escape velocity，能够超越 one-person business。

当然，AI 会让人们更快地复制 front runners，但这里也有一些好消息：

_大多数人就是不采取任何行动。_

这听起来很刺耳，但你大概身处 internet echo chamber。普通人仍然把 AI 当作提问或生成漂亮图片的地方；这也许永远不会改变，因为普通人不会利用空闲时间去学习和探索。普通人认为自己做成一件事、取得一些成功后，就可以再也不做任何事，因为他们正朝着一种虚幻的退休生活努力：所有付出都会得到回报，然后余生就能“放松”。不要把自己的未来押在 mechanical/linear model of work 上。

意义来自 struggle、status 和 curiosity。

当你停止追随这两种驱动力中的任何一种时，你的生活就会显著变糟。

### III – The future of education (products)

> 这不再是坐在一位 government-trained expert 面前，和其他所有人“learning”同样的东西，最后获得一套很快就会失去 relevance 的 skill stack。……而是找到一个你能产生共鸣的人，一个与你对未来有共同愿景的人。
>
> – [Purpose & Profit](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/purpose-and-profit-a-guide-to-discovering?lli=1)

我一直坚信——即使其他所有人都认为 courses 和 coaching 是骗局——creator economy 是一种 interest-based education system，在教人们掌握创造理想生活所需的 relevant skills 方面，远比 traditional schooling 有效。

我相信，人们跟随自己能够产生共鸣的人学习时，能学得更多、更快、更好。我相信，任何人只要围绕某个特定 topic 跟随 4-5 个人，就能比在 formal setting 中学习更快地成为该 domain 的 expert。

我仍然相信，存在只有 _你_ 才能获得的 knowledge 和 experience；我认为，获得它们并将其传承下去，都是高尚的。有些人甚至会说，这才是最有意义的生活方式，具体取决于你认同哪位 philosopher。

但传承这些 knowledge 的 _format or vessel_ 需要不断 evolve，正如它一直以来的变化：从围着火堆交谈，到寄送信件，再到建立图书馆，直到 internet 诞生、改变一切的那一天。

Static courses 已经不再奏效（好吧，它们仍然奏效，并不是说它们哪天就会突然归零，只是 general market sentiment 对它们有些倦怠）。人们不再那么想要那种 10-hour video library：有人观看、做笔记，并希望能把内容付诸实践。这感觉很慢。他们渴望更快、更高效的东西。此外，90% 的人无法完成 courses，更多人得不到 results，这也无济于事。而现在，有了 AI，任何人都能在一秒内生成那些 information（如果他们知道该问什么的话，而这不太可能）。

话虽如此，在我看来，education 的未来是 _learning experiences_，而不是 static courses。

你卖的不再只是 information。你实际上是在卖另一个版本的自己。你卖的是自己的 coaching services，只不过你本人并不在那里。相反，你把自己知道的一切交给 AI，让人们按自己的速度学习。

“可是，那些正在这样 implementing AI 的 schools 呢？你不是在和它们竞争吗？”

不是，因为 schools 是在教育公众，让他们了解在 society 中运作所必需的内容。理想情况下，你教的是极少有人做过、schools 也不会想到要教的东西。你是在为自己的 curiosity 和 self-development 标价。

### IV – How AI changes the way we learn

AI 很擅长 utility-based tasks，但不太擅长 meaning-based tasks，除非你能找到一种有用的方式，将它 implement 到自己的 workflow 中。（不，我不认为 AI 会让所有人变笨，或夺走我们所有的 human faculties。我认为，如果个人毫无克制，也不想维持这些 faculties，人们完全可能落入那个陷阱。）

举例来说，上个月我让 AI 为我写了大量内容。2 小时内大约写了 30 篇 articles。但这并不是典型的 writing（比如这封信）：对于那种 writing，我必须对 AI 生成的内容更加审慎。

这个小项目中，我搭建了一个 help center，这样我们的 support agent 就能 reference 由 30 篇 articles 构成的 knowledge base，并快速回答问题或解决 support requests。我们的新 AI agent 也会使用这个 knowledge base，快速解决那些不需要人工处理的问题。现在的 Response times 是即时的。

为了搭建这个 help center，我让 AI research 其他 help centers 的结构，提出应该收录哪些 articles 的想法，并采访我了解我的 company 和 product，以便写出所有 articles。我所做的只是回答问题，并修正 AI 在说明如何在 product 中完成特定操作时犯的一些错误：[Eden](https://eden.so/)。

现在，把同样的 concept 应用到 internet education/creator economy 上。

想象一门拥有涵盖全部 course content 的 knowledge base 的 course，但 chatbot 不是用来提供 support 的。它是用来帮助 student _learn, practice, and implement_ material 的。甚至还可以帮助他们完成实际工作。

一个能像坐在你身旁的 coach 一样帮你写 newsletter 的 AI，能比从 static course 中学习、并可能因为遇到难题而放弃的 student 更快取得 results。

这就是我所说的 learning experiences。student 不再只是买下一套 lessons，然后祈祷它们能改变自己的人生。相反，他们会在 guidance 下进行 interaction、获得 feedback，并真正 _doing_ 这件事。正如我相信你也知道的那样，Learning by doing 是最好的 learning 方式。希望以后不会再有困在 tutorial hell 里的日子。

顺便说一句，这并不新鲜。

在 mass education 出现之前，knowledge 正是以这种方式传承下来的。Apprenticeship。blacksmith 不会把 manual 递给 apprentice，然后说“自己琢磨吧”。他会和 apprentice 一起工作，在当下纠正他的握法，并在错误发生时指出来。

后来，我们把 education industrialized 了。我们需要快速 train 成千上万的 workers，于是创造了 lecture model。一个 teacher，许多 students，standardized curriculum。适合规模化。对真正的 learning 糟糕透顶（但结果证明，它很适合 indoctrinating）。

Courses 只是 digital lectures。它们也有同样的问题：student 遇到困难时，teacher 不在身边；而且 education 发生在实际应用 education 的环境之外。

自 apprenticeship model 以来，我们第一次能够实现大规模的 _personalized, interactive guidance_。那些认为所有 AI output 都和你与 ChatGPT 聊天一样的人，不会理解这一点。你的 AI 不会取代你作为 teacher 的角色。它只是又一种“build once, sell twice”的方式，只不过影响力更大。

如果由我亲自来做这件事（我目前不打算这么做，因为我正在打造两家规模更大的 companies。我认为 one-person business 是一个绝佳的起点，但我想继续向前推进），那么我会这样处理我的 2 Hour Writer course。

首先，我会为 course 创建 modules。这部分我已经完成了。

然后，我会搭建某种形式的 interactive chatbot，像一个小型 software。由于我不是 programmer，这会花我一些时间，但有了今天的 tools，我不认为这样简单的东西会很难做。

它会有 3 种不同的 modes 或 tabs：Learn、Practice、Create。每种 mode 都会有一系列 prompts，按正确顺序执行即可进行 teaching、提供 interactive practice，并“grade”他们的 work。这样一来，learning 和 writing 会变得很有趣。

### V – 如何构建 micro SaaS（非穷尽）

再说一次，我不认为 info products 会在短期内（彻底）消亡。

Education 对人类大脑太重要了，而基于你为自己推导出的 _goals_ 来_选择接受什么教育_，仍然是最后几道 moat 之一。Learning 对人类经验太根本，不可能被彻底 commoditized。

话虽如此，未来几年，我们今天所熟悉的 info products 会越来越像 software。你不再是提供可下载的 ebook，而是提供可访问的网站或可安装的 app。我的几位商业伙伴已经在这么做了。其中一位把自己的 offer creation process 做成了 app，帮助你找到合适的点子并构思 offer。

所以，与其卖类似“如何和女孩聊天”的 ebook、course 或 coaching program，不如创建一个 chatbot，_模拟_他们和女孩聊天。与其做 static productivity course，不如创建一个 AI，帮助他们明确自己的 vision、goals 和 priority tasks，然后通过通知提醒他们真正去做这些事。也许还能把它整合进某种 todo list app，这样你就有了比“买我的 course”更吸引人的东西，可以在 content 中推广。你打造一个 coach，成本远低于雇用你。

> 如果是 high-ticket service 而不是 product，这可以是以独特方式帮助其他 business 实施 AI system。你可以就 content writing process 为他人提供咨询，或像往常一样建立 agency，同时提供你以前会提供的服务，只是整合了 AI。

如果你一直有在关注我的 newsletters，或者看过我在这里分享的 prompts，那你就会完全明白我的意思。我发给这个列表的任何一条 [prompts](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/a-prompt-to-reset-your-life-in-30?r=of0ip&utm_campaign=post&utm_medium=web) 都很容易变成 micro software，每月收几美元。或者，如果它像我朋友的 offer creation app 一样属于一次性服务，就可以按一次性价格提供访问权限。我还在[这里教过如何 create good prompts](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-use-ai-better-than-99-of-people?lli=1)（这会是这个 process 中至关重要的一步）。这些 prompts 会成为 software 中 AI 部分的 system prompts。

与其创建一个非常通用的 ChatGPT，不如创建一个高度具体的 chat 或 software，帮助人们 learn、practice 并完成你最初用 info product 教他们做的事。

“可是 Dan，构建 ChatGPT wrapper 难道不算作弊吗？”

好吧，按这个逻辑，在互联网上构建_任何东西_都算作弊。Typeform 这家价值十亿美元的公司，不过就是一个 HTML wrapper。Cursor 是一个 GPT wrapper。你构建的任何 info product 都是由你搭建它的平台所封装的；如果 AI 就像下一代 cloud solution，那么互联网上的每个 software 都会是 wrapper。所以没错，对于一个必须精打细算、善用 third party tools 的个体来说，ChatGPT wrapper 是个很好的选择。而且这类产品通常相当简单。

有人告诉你不能创建 ChatGPT wrapper，就像在告诉你：如果你想做一把椅子，就不能用木头。

你可以像创建 help center 那样创建 course。把所有 articles 都补充完整——你实际上是在创建 course，可以借助 AI（如果你愿意），只是还要多几个步骤。

然后，你创建一个所谓的 _system prompt_。

就像我们刚才说的，system prompt 是你在 AI 与任何人交谈前交给它的一组 instructions。它定义 AI 的 personality、knowledge boundaries 和 behavior rules。可以把它想成是在为 AI 的 identity 编程。

例如，如果你要创建一个 AI writing coach，你的 system prompt 可能会包括：

*   AI 的 role：AI 应该如何 act

*   它的 instructions：具体来说，它应该按什么步骤做什么

*   它的 knowledge base：你的所有 frameworks、processes 和 examples

*   它的 boundaries：它应该和不应该提供哪些帮助

*   它的 personality：它如何说话、使用什么语气，以及表现得多严厉或多支持

这个 prompt 让_你的_ AI 与其他人的 AI 不同。它把你的 specific knowledge、frameworks 和 voice 打包进一个 tool，让它能同时帮助成千上万的人。

人们能偷走它吗？当然能。但他们也能偷走你构建的任何东西。你必须迅速行动，就像我前面谈到的那样。

### VI – Obsession 和 experimentation 就是 moat

使用 Replit 或 Cursor 这类 tool。

Replit 对 beginner 更友好，Cursor 则复杂一些。

你也可以试着只用 Claude Code 玩一玩，看看效果如何。

是的，你得学会如何使用它（很震惊吧，我知道，真不敢相信你居然得花时间学点东西，才能和那些什么都不学的人得到不同的 results）。最好的办法是跟着 YouTube tutorial 一起 build，遇到难题时把 AI 当作 study partner。问它，用“vibe coding”最好的方法是什么。让它制定一份全面的 game plan，一步步 build software。让它就你希望如何构建提出 clarifying questions。

然后，就开始折腾和 build 吧。

你最初几次尝试显然会很糟糕，你得学会如何修复。你可能得 build 3-4 个 apps，才会觉得其中一个值得标价。

现在的问题是，你真正的 advantage 在哪里？

你的 advantage 不在于做 AI 做不到的事。这是注定会输的游戏。

你的 advantage 在于用 AI 做只有_你_才会想到去做的事。

不是每个人都会在 ChatGPT、Replit 或 Cursor 中输入同样的内容。也就是说，这些东西的 output 都有无限种独特可能。你独特的 interests、experiences 和 insights 组合，会带来别人想不到的 prompts 和 products。

Naval 将这称为 specific knowledge：

*   Specific knowledge 无法通过训练获得

*   它来自追随 genuine curiosity

*   构建它对你来说会“像是在玩”

*   它往往位于“knowledge 的 edge”

一个人花了 10 年痴迷于 productivity，之后围绕这类 specific knowledge 构建 AI tool，做出来的东西会和一个只让 ChatGPT“做一个 productivity app”的人截然不同。如果这个痴迷者还凭借这类 specific knowledge 在 social media 上积累了 audience，你就成功突破了 market sophistication 的第 5 层，进入了不可替代的领域（暂时如此）。

你多年来的阅读、experimenting、失败和摸索_就是_这道 moat。

讽刺的是，AI 让 human knowledge 变得_更_有价值，而不是更不值钱。

AI 让每个人都变快了。谁都能做 course，谁都能写 content，谁都能复制别人。但真正无法被复制的人少之又少。很少有人花多年时间磨炼出如此精细的 experience（taste），以至于没人会想到去复制那些让它奏效的细节。

去 build 那件只有你才会想到去 build 的东西。

– Dan

> I’m not going to tell you to start an agency. I’m not going to tell you to start freelancing or coaching or any of the other low-hanging fruit that I’ve already talked about plenty of times in depth.
>
> Instead, I’m going to show you exactly what type of business makes sense _right now_.
>
> I’ve been seeing people say that “value-based content is dead” and that “info products are dead.” People feel like it isn’t worth it to start as a creator when there’s a new paradigm emerging that they don’t understand yet (I don’t think anyone truly understands it. I don’t. This letter is an educated guess at best).
>
> Let’s talk about how the one-person business is changing, what the future of education looks like, and how to actually take advantage of this.
>
> ### I – The one-person business model is evolving
>
> > Fortunes require leverage. Business leverage comes from capital, people, and products with no marginal cost of replication (code and media).
> >
> > – Naval
>
> For the last decade or so, the best way to build a cash-flow, absurdly high-margin business was with information products or coaching services.
>
> It was great because with the internet, social media, and software, _one singular person could start an entire business_. That’s insane. One SINGLE person could be their own marketing, sales, product, and design departments. And this is obviously more possible than it ever has been with agentic AI - as we’ll continue to dive into in future letters.
>
> In this digital world, you write content to get traffic without needing a physical business in the right location. You do something interesting with your life, or learn one of your passions enough until you become a valuable resource, and then you share it with others in an educational or entertaining way. Your brand is you, your content are little pieces of your mind, and your products are processes that solved a problem in your life and led to something better. Many overcomplicate that. This isn’t something that will ever go away, but the _vessel_ by which you do this will continue to change, so you will need to keep your finger on the pulse of how everything evolves.
>
> My entire goal with these letters is to help you become future-proof. They’re built around 2 pillars:
>
> 1.  How do you figure out what you want in life (psychology, philosophy, personal development)
>
> 2.  How do you succeed with that in today’s world to reach that (skill acquisition, business, technology, AI)
>
> Previously, the second part was largely handled with a personal brand on social media and with some kind of information product, be it a simple ebook (level 1), a course (level 2), a cohort (level 3), a community (level 4).
>
> But since everyone is talking about how “info products and value-based content are dead,” I think there’s something deeper at play. The process is still the same. You still pursue your curiosities and interests and share that with other people, but the vessel for doing that is no longer a static PDF, video course, or community.
>
> > **I want to make this very clear**: I am not on the jaded side that thinks every info product is a scam because everyone has one now. I think they can be life changing, they have been for me. I think they are the antidote to negative aspects of traditional education (i.e. conformity and getting trained into a job you hate). I do not think they are going away for good, and what I say in this letter will not apply to every single industry or niche, but as with almost every career sector right now (thanks to AI) the baseline that gets results has been raised.
>
> An average person at a low level of development can now take the “quick and easy route” and tell AI to create a persuasive ebook, write viral content, and generate images and assets. They can make some form of money, but they will never compete with those who understand what’s going on.
>
> In other words, it has never been easier to start a one-person business.
>
> But if you don’t want to be in the lower class of the creator economy (I hate saying that, but it absolutely exists), you need to understand something very important.
>
> ### II – We are breaching into stage 6 of the market
>
> Markets move through predictable stages of awareness and saturation.
>
> Eugene Schwartz categorized these into 5 stages.
>
> At level one, when nobody is in the market yet, you just state what your product does and that tends to do the job. Then competitors arrive and you have to make bigger claims. Then the market gets skeptical so you explain your mechanism (saying “I will help you make money online” started to sound like a scam and you had to be more specific like “Implement the [2 Hour Content Ecosystem](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-build-a-world-the-2-hour-content)”). Then competitors copy that mechanism. Finally, everyone is exhausted by claims altogether and _brand_ starts to be the major differentiator, which is happening right now with personal branding. People crave the sense of belonging they get from joining a tribe with a mission (I’ve talked about [how your mission is your niche](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/the-most-profitable-niche-in-the?lli=1) before). Community and authenticity matter more than who has the best sounding product.
>
> We’ve hit that final stage across the board. So much so that “authenticity” isn’t a differentiating factor. People are just tired of the same old courses and coaching. It sucks, because those things are impactful, but there’s more to it.
>
> The select few who are smart enough to _transcend_ info products as a whole will be the ones who win. Transcending doesn’t mean you leave them behind. It means you evolve to the next level and integrate them, because education/learning as a domain will never go away.
>
> But since you are competing in a space as _one singular person_, things can become saturated quite quickly. What I am going to tell you isn’t going to secure you for long, because the next level will be just around the corner. Info products didn’t last long (maybe 10-15 years?), and the next phase won’t last that long (maybe 2-3 years?). Who knows how business is going to look after that. We will be building for tomorrow rather than a year from now. Iteration cycles will be mind-bogglingly short. Timescales will be compressed.
>
> The main advantage of being a one-person business is speed and adaptability. You must constantly be moving and iterating until you reach escape velocity and can move beyond the one-person business.
>
> Of course, AI will make it so people can copy the front runners faster, but there is some good news here:
>
> _Most people just don’t do anything._
>
> That sounds harsh, but you’re probably in an internet echo chamber. The average person still treats AI as a place to ask questions or generate a pretty image, and that may never change because the average person doesn’t use their free time to learn and explore. The average person thinks that they can do one thing, see some success, and then never do anything again because they are working toward an illusory retirement where all of their effort pays off and they can just “chill” for the rest of their life. The mechanical/linear model of work is not something to bet your future on.
>
> Meaning is generated from struggle, status, and curiosity.
>
> The day you stop following either one of those drives is the day your life gets considerably worse.
>
> ### III – The future of education (products)
>
> > It’s no longer about sitting in front of a government-trained expert and ‘learning’ the same thing as everyone else to end up with a soon-to-be irrelevant skill stack... It’s about finding someone you relate with. Someone with a shared vision for the future.
> >
> > – [Purpose & Profit](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/purpose-and-profit-a-guide-to-discovering?lli=1)
>
> I’ve always held a firm belief – even when everyone else considered courses and coaching a scam – that the creator economy was an interest-based education system far more effective than traditional schooling at teaching people the relevant skills to create the life they want.
>
> I believe that people learn more, faster, and better when they learn from someone they can relate with. I believe that anyone can follow 4-5 people on a specific topic and become an expert in that domain much faster than they would by studying it in a formal setting.
>
> I still hold the belief that there is knowledge and experience that only _you_ can acquire, and I think it’s noble to both acquire it and pass it down. Some would even say that’s the most meaningful way to live, depending on which philosopher you look to.
>
> But the _format or vessel_ of how we pass down that knowledge needs to evolve as it always has from speaking by the fire to sending letters to building libraries to the day everything changed when the internet was born.
>
> Static courses don’t cut it anymore (well they do, it’s not like they’re just going to zero one day, but the general market sentiment is a bit burnt out from them). A 10-hour video library where someone watches, takes notes, and hopefully implements isn’t something that people want that much anymore. It feels slow. They crave something faster and more efficient. Plus, it doesn’t help that 90% of people don’t finish courses and even more don’t get results. And now, with AI, anyone can generate that information in a second (if they know what to ask, which is unlikely).
>
> That said, the future of education, in my eyes, is _learning experiences_ rather than static courses.
>
> You’re not only selling information anymore. You’re practically selling a second version of your mind. You’re selling your coaching services, but you aren’t there. Instead, you pass off everything you know to the AI and let people learn at their own speed.
>
> “But what about schools who are implementing AI like this? Aren’t you competing with them?”
>
> No, because schools are educating the public on what’s necessary to operate within society. Preferably, you’re teaching people something very few people have done and schools wouldn’t think to teach. You are placing a price tag on your own curiosity and self-development.
>
> ### IV – How AI changes the way we learn
>
> AI is great for utility-based tasks, but not so much with meaning-based tasks unless you find a way to implement it in your workflow in a useful way. (No, I don’t think AI makes everyone dumb and takes away all of our human faculties. I think people can absolutely fall into that trap on an individual level if they do not have any restraint or desire to maintain those faculties).
>
> As an example, I had AI do a ton of writing for me last month. Around 30 articles in 2 hours. But this isn’t the typical writing (like this letter) where I would have to be so much more critical of what the AI generates.
>
> For this little project, I built a help center so I could have our support agent reference the knowledge base of 30 articles and answer questions or resolve support requests quickly. This knowledge base is also used by our new AI agent that can quickly resolve questions that don’t need a human. Response times are now instant.
>
> To build this help center, I told AI to research how other help centers are structured, propose ideas for articles that should be there, and interview me about my company and product to write all of them. All I did was answer questions and fix some mistakes that the AI made when talking about how to do specific things within the product, [Eden](https://eden.so/).
>
> Now take that same concept and apply it to the internet education/creator economy.
>
> Imagine a course that has a knowledge base of all the course content, but the chatbot isn’t for support. It’s there to help the student _learn, practice, and implement_ the material. Hell, it can even help them do the work.
>
> An AI that helps you write the newsletter like a coach sitting next to you can get results faster than a student learning from a static course and potentially quitting because they got stuck on something.
>
> This is what I mean by learning experiences. The student no longer just buys a bundle of lessons and prays that they change their life. Instead, they’re interacting, getting feedback, and actually _doing_ the thing with guidance. Learning by doing is the best way to learn, as I’m sure you know. Gone are the days of being stuck in tutorial hell, hopefully.
>
> This isn’t new, by the way.
>
> Before mass education existed, this is exactly how knowledge was passed down. Apprenticeship. A blacksmith didn’t hand his apprentice a manual and say “figure it out.” He worked alongside him. Corrected his grip in real time. Pointed out mistakes as they happened.
>
> Then we industrialized education. We needed to train thousands of workers quickly, so we created the lecture model. One teacher, many students, standardized curriculum. Efficient for scale. Terrible for actual learning (but turned out to be great for indoctrinating).
>
> Courses are just digital lectures. And they have the same problem. The teacher isn’t there when the student gets stuck, and the education happens outside of the environment in which the education is to be applied.
>
> For the first time since the apprenticeship model, we can have _personalized, interactive guidance_ at scale. This won’t click for those who think that all AI output is the same as you chatting with ChatGPT. Your AI doesn’t replace you as the teacher. It’s yet another way to ‘build once, sell twice’ but on a more impactful scale.
>
> If I were personally doing this (I don’t plan to because I’m building two bigger companies right now. I think the one-person business is an incredible starting point, but I want to keep pushing) here’s what I would do with my 2 Hour Writer course.
>
> First, I would create the modules to the course. That’s already done on my end.
>
> Then, I would build some form of an interactive chatbot. Like a small software. This would take me a bit because I’m not a programmer, but with today’s tools I don’t think something that simple would be too difficult to do.
>
> It would have 3 different modes or tabs: Learn, Practice, Create. They would each have a series of prompts that can be executed in the right order to teach, give interactive practice, and “grade” their work so to say. It would make learning and writing very fun.
>
> ### V – How to build a micro SaaS (non-exhaustive)
>
> Again, I don’t think info products will be (completely) dead anytime soon.
>
> Education is too important for the human brain, and _choosing what to be educated on_ based on _goals you derived for yourself_ is still one of the last moats. Learning is too foundational to the human experience to ever be fully commoditized.
>
> With that said, the info products we know today will look more like software over the next few years. Rather than having an ebook to download, you have a website to visit or app to install. A few of my business friends are already doing this. One turned his offer creation process into an app that helps you find the right idea and formulate your offer.
>
> So, rather than selling something like a “how to talk to girls” ebook, course, or coaching program, you create a chatbot that _simulates_ them talking to girls. Rather than a static productivity course, you create an AI that helps them identify their vision, goals, and priority tasks, then pings them with notifications to actually do those things. Maybe integrate that with some kind of todo list app and now you have something much more enticing than “buy my course” to promote in your content. You build a coach that costs far less than it would to hire you.
>
> > For something like a high-ticket service rather than a product, this could look like helping other businesses implement an AI system in a unique way. You could consult people on a content writing process or build an agency as you normally would while offering what you would have offered before, but with AI integrated.
>
> If you’ve been following along my newsletters at all, or if you’ve looked at the prompts I have on here, then you’ll know exactly what I’m getting at. Any of the [prompts I’ve sent to this list](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/a-prompt-to-reset-your-life-in-30?r=of0ip&utm_campaign=post&utm_medium=web) could easily become a micro software that I charge a few bucks a month for. Or, if it’s a one-and-done type deal like my friend’s offer creation app, it could be a one-time price to access. I also taught how to [create good prompts here](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/how-to-use-ai-better-than-99-of-people?lli=1) (which will be a crucial step in this process). Those would become the system prompts of your AI portion of the software.
>
> Rather than creating a very general ChatGPT, you create a hyper-specific chat or software that helps people learn, practice, and do the thing you were teaching about in the first place with the info product.
>
> “But Dan, isn’t building a ChatGPT wrapper cheating?”
>
> Well, by that logic, building _anything_ on the internet is cheating. Typeform, a billion dollar company, is just an HTML wrapper. Cursor is a GPT wrapper. Any info product you build is wrapped by the platform you build it on, and if AI is like the next cloud solution, every piece of software on the internet will be a wrapper. So yes, as one person who has to be savvy with their resources and use third party tools, a ChatGPT wrapper is a great way to go. And they tend to be pretty simple.
>
> People telling you that you can’t create a ChatGPT wrapper is like telling you that if you want to build a chair, you can’t use wood.
>
> You create the course kind of like you would create a help center. You build out all of the articles – you practically create the course, with the help of AI if you want, but with a few more steps.
>
> Then you create what’s called a _system prompt_.
>
> Like we just talked about, a system prompt is the set of instructions you give to an AI before it talks to anyone. It defines the AI’s personality, knowledge boundaries, and behavior rules. Think of it as programming the AI’s identity.
>
> For example, if you were creating an AI writing coach, your system prompt might include:
>
> *   The AI’s role: How the AI should act
>
> *   Its instructions: What exactly it should do, step by step
>
> *   Its knowledge base: All of your frameworks, processes, and examples
>
> *   Its boundaries: What it should and shouldn’t help with
>
> *   Its personality: How it talks, what tone it uses, how harsh or supportive it is
>
> The prompt is what makes _your_ AI different from anyone else’s. It’s your specific knowledge, your frameworks, and your voice packaged into a tool that can help thousands of people at once.
>
> Can people steal it? Sure they can. But they could have stolen anything that you build. You have to act fast, as I discussed earlier.
>
> ### VI – Obsession and experimentation are the moat
>
> Use a tool like Replit or Cursor.
>
> Replit is more beginner friendly, Cursor is a bit more complex.
>
> You can also try playing around with just Claude Code and see how that does.
>
> Yes, you will have to learn how to use it (shocking, I know, I can’t believe you have to actually spend time learning something to get a different results from those who don’t learn anything). The best way to do this is to build alongside something like a YouTube tutorial and use AI as a study partner when you get stumped. Ask it what the best way to go about “vibe coding” is. Ask it to create a comprehensive game plan to build the software step by step. Have it ask clarifying questions about how you want it built.
>
> Then, just start tinkering and building.
>
> Your first few tries will obviously suck and you will have to learn how to fix it. You will probably have to build 3-4 apps before you feel like one is worth putting a price tag on.
>
> The question now is, where does your actual advantage lie?
>
> Your advantage isn’t doing what AI can’t do. That’s a losing game.
>
> Your advantage is doing what only _you_ would think to do with AI.
>
> Not everyone types the same thing into ChatGPT or Replit or Cursor. Meaning the output of those things are infinitely unique. Your unique combination of interests, experiences, and insights leads to prompts and products that nobody else would create.
>
> Naval explains this as specific knowledge:
>
> *   Specific knowledge can’t be trained for
>
> *   It comes from pursuing genuine curiosity
>
> *   Building it will “feel like play to you”
>
> *   It’s often at the “edge of knowledge”
>
> The person who spent 10 years obsessing over productivity and then builds an AI tool around that specific knowledge will create something fundamentally different than someone who just asked ChatGPT to “make a productivity app.” And if that obsessed person has built an audience on social media with said specific knowledge, you have successfully broken past the 5th level of market sophistication into the land of irreplaceability (for now).
>
> Your years of reading, experimenting, failing, and figuring things out _is_ the moat.
>
> The irony is that AI is making human knowledge _more_ valuable, not less.
>
> AI made everyone fast. Anyone can make a course. Anyone can write content. Anyone can copy anyone. But very few people are uncopyable. Very few people have spent years developing so much fine-tuned experience (taste) that nobody would think to copy the details that make it work.
>
> Build the thing only you would think to build.
>
> – Dan
