# Stereotypes and discrimination against women in STEM

---

## 正文

是的，又一篇关于 Google memo 的文章。在这里，我将 review 那些被用来 support 如下观点的 evidence：STEM participation（尤其是 engineering）的 gender gap 是由 stereotypes 和 discrimination 造成的。

诚然，我在之前的文章中没有太多讨论这个话题，因为我有一个反对这一 hypothesis 的 strong prior，而我已经呈现的 evidence 指向 biology 的 strong role（而且我还有其他想读、想写的东西！）。但如果有人认为 stereotypes 和 discrimination 的 role 很大，那么 by implication，他就会认为 biology 的 role 很小。正如 Moorean shift 所说，一个人的 modus tollens 是另一个人的 modus ponens。既然没有其他人打算这样做，我将在这里讨论我一直忽略的问题的另一面：支持 negative cultural hypothesis 的 evidence 究竟有多 strong？下文中，我只讨论这场争论中提出的 evidence。由于我之前一篇文章引用的那项近期 meta-analysis，我将直接 dismiss 对 stereotype threat 的引用。

最适合作为起点的资料汇集是 Rachel Thomas 的这篇[文章](https://medium.com/tech-diversity-files/if-you-think-women-in-tech-is-just-a-pipeline-problem-you-haven-t-been-paying-attention-cb7a2073b996)  以及另一[篇](https://www.wired.com/story/why-men-dont-believe-the-data-on-gender-bias-in-science/?mbid=social_twitter_onsiteshare)。

### 如果你认为 women 在 tech 中的处境只是一个 pipeline problem，那你就没有认真关注（Thomas）

> 当 researcher [Kieran Snyder interviewed 716 名 women](http://fortune.com/2014/10/02/women-leave-tech-culture/)时，她们都是在 average tenure 为 7 年后 left tech 的人；几乎所有人都说她们喜欢工作本身，但大多数人对 work environment 感到不满。

链接文章中写道：

> 午餐时，她向我透露，她正在考虑辞职。要兼顾一切实在太难了。她的经理曾施压让她提前结束休假回来上班，如今又逼她出差，把还在吃奶的婴儿留在家里。她睡不好觉。她觉得自己既没有做好工作，也没有照顾好孩子。$$...$$
> 
> 许多 women 说，毁掉她们 careers 的并不只是 motherhood，而是缺少 flexible work arrangements、unsupportive work environment，或不足以支付 childcare 的 salary。正如曾任 motion graphics designer 的 Rebecca 所说：“Motherhood 只是 amplifier。它让那些我一直忍受的问题变得真正无法忍受。”

到这里，这还只是不同人生安排——工作与家庭——之间的 tradeoff，并没有 discrimination。不过，下面这段确实涉及 discrimination：

> 有一百九十二名 women 将在让她们感到 overtly 或 implicitly discriminatory 的 environments 中工作的不适，列为她们决定 leave tech 的 primary factor。这仅略多于 surveyed women 的四分之一。

所以，仅略多于四分之一：绝大多数 women 并未 perceive 任何 discrimination。data 和/或 report 本身无法获取，因此无法进一步评论。回到 Thomas：

> 在 NSF-funded research 中， [Nadya Fouad surveyed 5,300 名 women](http://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2014/08/12/339638726/many-women-leave-engineering-blame-the-work-culture)，她们在过去 50 年间获得了 engineering degrees（各种类型），其中 38% 已不再作为 engineers 工作。Fouad 用“是 climate，笨蛋！”来 summarized 她关于她们为何离开的 findings。

那篇文章中写道：

> 从 aerospace sector 到 Silicon Valley，engineering 都存在 retention problem：拥有 engineering degrees 的 women 中，接近 40 percent 要么 leave profession，要么从未 enter field。

这份 [linked report](http://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2014/08/pushed-back.pdf) 没有 consider 那个显而易见的 control：men。有多少 men 会 leave engineering？如果我们发现，拥有 engineering degrees 的 men 中也有 40% 不在 engineering 工作，那么整体图景就会大为改变，因为这将表明，他们 leave 并不是因为 discrimination，而是因为更 benign factors（不喜欢工作本身，或在别处找到更好的工作）。

这份 report 实际上并没有提到 discrimination 或 hostility 是她们 leaving 的 factor。收录的 testimonies suggest，让她们却步的是 long working hours 和对 high availability 的 requirement。其中一张 slides 提到，对于不到 5 年前 left engineering 的 group，三分之二的人离开是为了在其他 fields 追求更好的 opportunities，三分之一则是为了留在家中。另一个有意思的 result 是，perceptions of supportiveness 并不存在 industry 之间的 differences：bioscience 这一 field 对 women 的 supportive 程度与 computer science 一样。因此，support 的不同无法 explain computer science 与 bioscience 之间的 gender gap。

有一些 work 探讨了 male 与 female 的 retention 问题。例如，[Kahn & Ginther (2015)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4541081/) cite 的其他 work 表明，许多 men 也会 leave STEM。对于 industry jobs，所 cite 的一项由 [Society of Women Engineers](https://web.archive.org/web/20160406111912/https://societyofwomenengineers.swe.org/images/swemagazine/RetentionStudyCompilation.pdf) 开展的 studies 发现，在 2003-2005 年 graduated 的 cohort 中，60% 的 women stayed in STEM，而 men 为 70%，因此 gap 为 10%。这项 study 还提到，engineering 中的 equality of treatment 正在 improving。1993 年，15.3% 的 women engineers reported “从事相同工作的 male 和 female workers 在 treatment 上存在 consistent inequities”。根据 women 的报告，2005 年这一比例降至 9.6%。根据 men 的报告，这两个 numbers 分别为 6.6% 和 3.2%。

这项 study 也讨论了 men 和 women 为何 leave STEM，而历年来只有少数 women 提到，离开是因为 negative work climate 问题（10%），同样因这一原因离开的 men 为 5%。两个 groups 提到的 main reason 都是对其他 careers 的 interest，其次是 advancement opportunities 和 lack of challenge。

Kahn 和 Ginther 的 paper concludes：

> Family status 至关重要。**有 children 的 Women 最有可能 leave labor force，因而也 leave engineering。没有 children 的 Single women 实际上比 men 更不可能 leave engineering**；在 5 个 cohorts 中的 4 个，情况都是如此（到 7–8 年这一 point 时）。
> 
> **同样，一直 working full-time 的 women，on average 比 full-time men 留在 engineering jobs 的可能性略微 _更_ 高**，直到 post-BSE 3–4 年这一 point；在五个 cohorts 中的四个，到 post-BSE 7–8 年时，她们留下的可能性也相同或更高。按 family status 进行 Dividing，在五个 cohorts 中的四个，到 7–8 年这一 point 时，没有 children 且 work full-time 的 single women 更有可能 remain；即使是有 children 的 women，在 5 个 cohorts 中的 3 个也同样有可能 remain。

这让 40% 的 women leave engineering 这一 statistic 显得没有那么灾难性，并 emphasises 了对 work hours 和 family 的 preferences 所起的 role。根据这些 studies，Discrimination 看起来只是一个 minor thing。

回到 Rachel Thomas：

> -   在 HBS、Wharton 和 MIT Sloan 联合开展的一项 study 中，[对于由 man pitched 的 entrepreneurial ventures，Investors preferred 的比例高于 woman 提出 identical pitch 的情况](http://www.pnas.org/content/111/12/4427.full.pdf+html)，两者的比例为 68% 对 32%。“与由 female voice narrated 的相同 pitches 相比，Male-narrated pitches 被 rated 为更 persuasive、logical 和 fact-based。”

这项 study 发现，由 male delivered 的 pitch 比由 woman delivered 的相同 pitch 更受 prefered，倍数为 1.57。

但这并不是唯一 analyses 这一问题的 study。[Boulton et al. (2017)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2886855) studied 了美国电视节目 _Shark Tank_ 中面向 angel investors 的 pitches，没有发现 gender effect。[Ewens & Townsend (2017)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2953011) 在一项关于 AngelList（一个允许 investors 与 startup founders 进行 interact 的 platform）的 study 中也没有发现这一 effect。不过，这两项 studies 发现的，并不是某种程度的 discrimination，而是 homophilic bias：male investors prefer male entrepreneurs，而 female investors prefer female entrepreneurs。

> -   在 Yale researchers 开展的一项 randomized、double-blind study 中，6 所主要 institutions 的 science faculty evaluated 了 lab manager position 的 applications。与被 assigned female names 的 identical applications 相比，[被 randomly assigned male name 的 Applications 被 rated 为 significantly 更 competent 和 hirable](http://www.pnas.org/content/109/41/16474.full.pdf+html)，并获得更高的 starting salary 和更多 career mentoring。

这就是 Moss-Racusin et al. (2012) study，幸好 [Lee Jussim](https://www.psychologytoday.com/blog/rabble-rouser/201707/why-brilliant-girls-tend-favor-non-stem-careers) 已经替我讨论过了（感谢 F[rancisco Boni](https://twitter.com/boni_bo) 提供线索），Jussim 说：

> “按照 conventional standards，Moss-Racusin study 是这些 studies 中最 weakest 的。它的 sample size 只是其他 studies 的一小部分。它 studies 的是一个 relatively minor situation（hiring lab managers）。它只是 single study（Su et al 是涵盖数十项 studies 的 meta-analysis；Williams 和 Ceci reported 了五项 separate studies）。与 Wang et al 相比，它只 studied 了 single time point 的一个 event；它没有 follow 人们的 career trajectories。
> 
> 这并不意味着 Moss-Racusin et al 是一项‘糟糕的’study；只是它在几乎所有重要 scientific grounds 上都比其他 studies weaker。这也不是说其他 studies 是‘完美的’；所有 studies 都有不足。但按照 conventional scientific standards，Su et al 的 meta-analysis、Williams 和 Ceci 中的 replications、longitudinal Wang et al study，以及这三者中远为 larger 的 sample sizes，都意味着，按照大多数 scientific methodological standards，它们优于 Moss-Racusin et al study。
> 
> 然而，看看 citation counts。其他人正在极其频繁地 citing Moss-Racusin et al study。当然，Wang et al 以及 Williams 和 Ceci 发表得更晚，因此最有用的 column 可能是最后一列。自 2015 年以来，weaker Moss-Racusin study 被 cited 的次数，比另外三项加起来还多 50%！这意味着，citing Moss-Racusin et al study 却完全忽略其他三项的 papers，很可能比 citing 其他三项中哪怕一项的 papers 还要多！我们这算什么‘science’，竟让如此多的‘scientists’能够在我们的 scientific journals 中如此 systematically 地 ignoring relevant data，却安然无事？
> 
> （再说一次，这并不意味着 Moss-Racusin study‘糟糕’。这里的 bias reflects 的是一个广泛得多的 field problem，并不 constitute 这篇 paper 本身的 weakness）。
> 
> 亲爱的读者，这就是一个巨大的 scientific bias。它甚至可能已经超越 bias。有些人可能会将其称为对 discrimination 和 bias 的一种‘痴迷’，严重到让我们 field 中的许多人看不到那些关于 gender differences 的 major findings，而这些 gender differences 会 contribute to 对不同类型 fields 的 preferences。”

Thomas 写道：

> -   在 Harvard 和 CMU 的一项 study 中，men 和 women 通过朗读 identical scripts 来 negotiated job offer 时，[要求更高 salary 的 women 被 rated 为更难共事、也更不 nice](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=779506)，但 men 并未因 negotiating 而被 negatively perceived。

这项 study 还 showed，当 evaluator 是 male 时，candidate 是 female 与更高的 willingness to work with her 存在 related 关系。当 evaluator 是 female 时，则没有观察到这种 effect。而且，这项 study 也 showed，某种程度的 homophilia 可能正在起作用（Table 2）。在 rating willingness to work 时，men 只对 women rated 得 lower（d=1.14），而 women 对 men（d=1.16）以及程度较轻的 women（d=0.6）都 rated 得 lower。在 niceness 方面，men 只在 women 要求更高 salary 时将她们 rated 为 lower（d=0.66），而 women 则将 men（d=1.41）和 women（d=1.23）都 ranked 为较不 nice。在 demandingness 中也存在类似的 effect。

诚然，这种事不应该发生。Homophilia 是一种 anti-women 和 anti-men bias，分别由 men 和 women 持有。

话虽如此，**我同意，这项 study 确实揭示了 overall discrimination against women**。但它与 explain STEM gap 有关吗？（这项 study 考虑的 scenario 是：participants 希望被安排到一个更 senior 的 management position，并正在为那个 position 接受 interviewing）

> -   Psychology faculty 收到了某位 applicant 的 CVs（randomly assigned male 或 female name），而 men 和 women 都[significantly more likely to hire a male applicant](http://advance.cornell.edu/documents/ImpactofGender.pdf)，而不是具有 identical record 的 female applicant。

我无法获取这篇 paper，它似乎发表于 1999，研究的是 psychology，而不是 STEM。不过，对于 STEM，我们确实有一项近期的 study（[Williams & Ceci, 2015a](http://www.pnas.org/content/112/17/5360.full.pdf)），他们 found，在 STEM tenure track positions 的 hiring decision 中，存在 2:1 的 preference，方向是 FOR women。这在 biology、engineering 和 psychology 中都 holds。对于 economics，preference 约为 1:1。

有些人对这项 study [提出了批评](http://womeninastronomy.blogspot.co.uk/2016/03/struggling-against-gender-bias-in-stem.html)，但同年稍后，他们在另一项 study（[Williams & Ceci 2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4611984/)b）中回应了部分 criticism。具体而言，一项 critique 是，由于 Williams and Ceci 的 study design，它无法 capture discrimination 的 full extent，因为它把 candidates 简化为 numbers，再加上一个带有 gender 的 tag。不过，他们预先回应了这项 critique：

> “_这样的 ranking experiments 与 real-world hiring 毫无关系_。”正如我们指出的，women 在实际的 real-world hiring 中具有 significant advantages——她们被 hired 的 rates 比 men 更高。我们的一些 critics 似乎不愿承认这一事实，而多项 audit studies 已清楚显示了这一点；这些 studies analyze 的是谁实际被 U.S. 和 Canada 的 universities hired（参见 Williams and Ceci，[2015](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4611984/#B28) 中的 cites）。鉴于现实中的 academic hiring 也以 disproportionate numbers chosen women 而不是 men，声称我们的 experiment 与 real-world hiring 毫无关联，似乎缺乏说服力。正如一位评论者在论证当前 experimental design 的 relevance 时指出的：“_人们将不得不同时声称，women 实际上是 stronger candidates（这是一个没有 direct evidence 支持的 strong assumption），这意味着 faculty 在 real hiring contexts 中并不 prefer 她们而不是 equally qualified men；但与此同时，faculty 在 hypothetical situations 中确实 DO prefer 她们（这又是一个没有 direct evidence 支持的 strong assumption）。最合理的 explanation 显然也是最 economical 的那个：faculty 在 hypothetical case 和 real case 中都 prefer women；他们的 preferences 不会从 actual 到 hypothetical 就剧烈摇摆。”_

> -   Wolfinger NH, Mason MA, Goulden M (2008) Problems in the pipeline: Gender, marriage, and fertility in the ivory tower. J Higher Educ 79(4):388–405
> -   Glass C, Minnotte K (2010) Recruiting and hiring women in STEM fields. J Divers High Educ 3(4):218–229
> -   Irvine AD (1996) Jack and Jill and employment equity. Dialogue 35(02):255–292

> -   Seligman C (2001) Summary of recruitment activity for all full-time faculty at the University of Western Ontario by sex and year. Available at: www.safs.ca/april2001/ recruitment.html. Accessed March 18, 2015.
> 
> 更多内容见[他们的 appendix](http://www.pnas.org/content/suppl/2015/04/08/1418878112.DCSupplemental/pnas.1418878112.sapp.pdf)，第 26 页。

回到 Thomas

> 在 [tech 中 high-performers 的 Performance reviews](http://fortune.com/2014/08/26/performance-review-gender-bias/) 中，negative personality criticism（例如 abrasive、strident 或 irrational）出现在 women 的 85% reviews 中，却只出现在 men 的 2% reviews 中。假定 85% 的 women 有 personality problems，而 men 中只有 2% 有，实在荒谬。

这又是我们前面提到的那位 Kieran Snyder，sample size 为 248，而且同样没有可获取的 source data，也没有背后的 peer-reviewed paper。我不是 peer-review 的狂热拥护者，但我会因为某项内容是 peer-reviewed 且 open 而给予它更大的 weight，你也应该如此。

> Bias 通常是 post-hoc justified 的。我们对 female applicant 最初的 subconscious impression 是 negative，随后便寻找 logical reasons 来 justify 它。例如，在上述 [Yale researchers 的 study](http://www.socialjudgments.com/docs/Uhlmann%20and%20Cohen%202005.pdf) 中，如果 police chief 的 male applicant 有更多 street smarts，而 female applicant 有更多 formal education，evaluators 就 decided，street smarts 是最重要的 trait；如果 names 被 reversed，evaluators 就 decided，formal education 是最重要的 trait。

这项 study 的 sample size 为 73，result 确如其所述，p~0.01。这一领域还有更近期的 work，包括一项 meta-analysis（[Koch et al., 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Amanda_Koch/publication/262682077_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Gender_Stereotypes_and_Bias_in_Experimental_Simulations_of_Employment_Decision_Making/links/57744b9d08aeb9427e2421b9.pdf)）。这项 meta-analysis 本身针对 hiring 中的 laboratory experiments，但他们也提到了 field studies，其中的 meta-analysis report，在现实世界中，bias 是 small 的，如果说有什么倾向，也是 in favour of women。

> 在 field studies 的 meta-analyses 中，Bowen, Swim, and Jacobs (2000) 与 Roth, Purvis, and Bobko (2012) 都 reported，male–female differences 的 overall effect sizes 是 small 的（overall d values 分别为 .01 和 .11，且 females receiving higher scores than males），但 gender differences 随 moderators 而 varied，例如 rating measure 的 gender stereotypicality、rater gender 和 rating 的 type。这些 assessing ratings 中 gender differences 的 field studies meta-analyses 的一个 advantage，是使用了实际的 employee evaluations，从而能对 findings 的 generalizability 更有 confidence。不过，一个 drawback 是无法将 field studies 中的 gender differences unambiguously attribute 给任何特定 cause，例如 gender bias 或 performance 中真正的 gender differences。

这项 study reports 了大量 findings，包括一种 overall small pro-male bias。但他们也 found，对于 highly experienced raters（相较于 undergrads），以及认为自己的 hiring decision 很重要的人，这些 effects 会变得非常 small；而这正是现实世界中 hiring decisions 的 setting，因此也就 explaining 了为什么 field studies 没有 found 任何 bias。

这项 study 还指出，当 applicant 的 skill 是 uncertain 时，bias 会更 greater。但即使这种 bias，也可能具有一定程度的 justification。如果一个人有这样的 background prior：men on average 会更好地 fit 那份 job（无论出于什么原因），那么，在收到关于两个不同 genders 的 equal candidates 的 noisy information 时，他就不得不——为了 comply with Bayes' theorem——opt for male，理由已在[这里](https://necpluribusimpar.net/politically-incorrect-guide-affirmative-action/)讨论。那么，持有这样的 prior 是否 justified？如果一个人接受 average man 在那份 job 中比 average woman fits better，并接受即便 applies for 那份 job 的 population 是 self-selected 的，这种 self-selection 仍存在一些 noise，那么他就可能是 justified 的。这里的 Noise 意味着，那些不会 fit 那份 job 的 women 或 men 仍然 apply。

我并不是声称这里的情况就是如此，而只是说它可能如此。

### 为什么 men 不相信 science 中关于 gender bias 的 data

这第二篇文章 cites 了以下内容：

> 一项早期的 [study](http://www.nature.com/nature/journal/v387/n6631/abs/387341a0.html) evaluated 了 biomedical sciences 中的 postdoctoral fellowship applications，并 found，women 必须比 men 更 productive，达到其 2.5 倍，才能被 evaluating 她们的 applications 的 senior scientists rated 为 equally scientifically competent。作者 concluded：“我们的 study strongly suggests，peer reviewers 无法 independent of gender 地 judge scientific merit。这些 peer reviewers over-estimated 了 male achievements，和/或 underestimated 了 female performance。”这项 study finds，“我们所 observed 的这一 magnitude 的 gender discrimination……可能 entirely account for female researchers 相较于 male researchers 在 attaining high academic rank 方面较低的 success rate。”

这项 study 是 correct 的，而且 proves 了他们所说的内容（N~100）。然而，文章随后由此继续说道

> 这只是数百项 peer-reviewed studies 中的几项，这些 studies clearly show，on average，science 对 women 设置的门槛比对她们的 male counterparts 更高。

但这似乎无法与 Williams-Ceci study 以及其中 citen 的 literature 很好地 cohere。事实上，在另一篇 paper（[Ceci et al. , 2014](http://sites.bu.edu/shulamitkahn/files/2016/03/Psychological-Science-in-the-Public-Interest-2014-Ceci-75-141.pdf)）中，他们直接 address 了这篇 paper，showing 这些 literature 总体上并不指向那个方向：

> grant funding rates 中的 Sex biases。许多评论者 claimed，grant review 中的 sex bias 导致较少的 women getting funded（或者 getting funded 的 levels 较低），而这种未能 gain grants 的情况又导致 women 较低的 rate of persistence 和较低的 rate of promotion。例如，Lortie et al. (2007) 写道：“如今人们已 recognized，(sex) biases 在 science 内部的许多 levels 上 function，包括 funding allocation、employment、publication，以及 general research directions”（p. 1247）。尽管存在这些 claims 以及无数其他类似说法（例如，Wenneras & Wold, 1997），grant-funding rates 中并不存在 systematic sex differences；虽然 men 的 grant awards 往往有更高的 dollar amounts，但这很可能是因为 men 更有可能担任 large center grants 和 program projects 的 principal investigators。然而，总体而言，men 与 women 的 grant proposals 的 funding rates 非常 similar（例如，Jayasinghe, Marsh, & Bond, 2003; Ley & Hamilton, 2008; Marsh, Bornmann, Mutz, Daniel, & O’Mara, 2009; Mutz, Bornmann, & Daniel, 2012; Pohlhaus, Jiang, Wagner, Schaffer, & Pinn, 2011; RAND, 2005）。下面，我们 summarize 这些 literature。在 Wenneras and Wold’s (1997) 关于 114 项 Swedish postdoctoral fellowships 存在 biased grant reviews 的 finding 之后，许多人 claimed，women 在 tenure-track positions 上的 success 受到 grant awards 中的 biases 的阻碍；他们 argued，一个 woman 要获得 funded，就必须比 comparable male competitors on average 多出 2.5 篇 major publications（即发表于 top journals），才能获得相同的 score。然而，对这些 data 的 comprehensive analysis 并不 accord with 这一 claim，而且完整的 corpus of evidence 也没有 reveal grant reviews 中存在 anti-female bias（[Ceci & Williams, 2011](http://www.pnas.org/content/108/8/3157.abstract)）。

文中也 cited 了 Moss-Racusin study，这项 study 已经讨论过了。

如果这些 studies 往多了说，只能 suggest anti-female biases 并不是很 strong（在 STEM 中），往少了说，则说明它们 nonexistent，那么为什么人们认为它们能起 strong role 呢？因为许多 scientist 确实这样想。也许 biased 的正是他们。这种情况的一个例子，可以在 Lee Jussim 的这一系列文章中找到（[1](https://www.psychologytoday.com/blog/rabble-rouser/201707/gender-bias-in-science-or-biased-claims-gender-bias)、[2](https://www.psychologytoday.com/blog/rabble-rouser/201707/gender-bias-in-science)、[3](https://www.psychologytoday.com/blog/rabble-rouser/201707/why-brilliant-girls-tend-favor-non-stem-careers)）。

评论者“Alex”也在我之前的文章下留下了一些支持 stereotypes 的 evidence：

[1\. 破解 gender code](https://www.accenture.com/t20161018T094638__w__/us-en/_acnmedia/Accenture/next-gen-3/girls-who-code/Accenture-Cracking-The-Gender-Code-Report.pdf)（Accenture report），但它没有提到 stereotypes 或 biases。

[2\. 为什么这么多 study engineering 的 women 会 leave 这个 field](https://hbr.org/2016/08/why-do-so-many-women-who-study-engineering-leave-the-field) 以已经讨论过的 Fouad 文章开篇，接着介绍作者本人开展的一项 panel survey。它 shows，women 的 self-confidence 比 men 更低，而且与 self-confidence 较低的 men 相比，她们更有可能向他人 seek reassurance。随后，它又列举了几起真实的 sexism instances，例如：

> 然而，对许多 women engineering students 而言，她们初次接触 collaboration 时，遭遇的就是 gender stereotypical 的对待，而这主要来自她们的 peers。虽然有些人起初 positively 描述 working in teams，但 reported negative experiences 的人要多得多。例如，在与 male classmates 一起工作时，她们常常谈到自己被 relegated 去做 routine managerial and secretarial jobs，并被 excluded 于“真正的”engineering work 之外。Kimberly 写道：“一个 group 中的两个女孩已经花了好几个小时，制作我们在那堂课上要造的 robot；她们 group 里的男孩们回来后，没几分钟就把她们打发去做 menial tasks，而男孩们则到 machine shop 里享受所有乐趣。”

这篇 paper 本身，即 [Seron, Silbey et al. (2015)](http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0730888415618728)，其 sample size 为 40 名 students（这是 diary section 的人数，该 section 被用作文章中 claims 的依据；此外还在第一年和第二年对 100 名 students 进行了 interviews），而且并没有 quantify 这种 discrimination 的 extent，只是说有人有时 experienced 过它。

文章和 paper 中提到的 700 这一 sample size 来自 [Cech et al. (2011)](http://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0003122411420815)，它并没有讨论 discrimination 或 sexism。

[3\. 将 women 推离 STEM 的 5 种 biases](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/resources/women-mathematics-stereotypes-identity-achievement?course=ap-physics-c-mechanics) 再次提到了 Moss-Racusin study，然后是 [Reuben, Sapienza, and Zingales (2013)](http://www.pnas.org/content/111/12/4403.full)。

这篇 paper studies 了 laboratory settings 中的 hiring decisions。Subjects 完成一项 arithmetic test，随后其中一些人被 chosen 为 employers，并必须从这个 group 中 ire 其他人。他们在不同的 scenarios 下这样做：No Information（只是看 employees）、Cheap Talk（candidates 向 employer communicate 自己的 performance）、Past Performance（Employers 被告知 employee 的 score）、Decision Then Cheap Talk（employers 只根据 candidates 的 looks，chose 一名 candidate 来 hire，随后再获得 candidates 的 self-reported scores，最后，Decision Then Past Performance 与前一种相同，但提供的是真实的 score。每个 condition 的 subjects 数量约为 50-60，而 picking decisions 的数量从 Cheap Talk 中的 160 到 Decision Then Cheap Talk 中的 269 不等。

当没有提供 information 时，women 被 chosen 的比例为 33.9%，与 Cheap Talk scenario 相似。当提供了 past performance 时，women 被 chosen 的比例为 43%。文章提到 men 被 chosen 的可能性是 women 的两倍，但这发生在没有任何 information 可用的 unrealistic condition 下。

第 3. 项中接下来 cited 的 paper 是 [Science 中针对 Women of Color 的 Gender Bias](http://www.uchastings.edu/news/articles/2015/01/double-jeopardy-report.pdf)。他们通过向 Association for Women in Science 的 members 发送电子邮件来 recruited subjects。这引入了一个 double self-selection problem，使我对这项 study 非常怀疑，无论它的 conclusions 是什么。首先，看起来，那些遭受过 sexism 的 women 既更有可能加入这个协会，也更有可能主动报名参与这项 study。

6\. [US millennial men 是否和他们的父亲一样 sexist？](https://hbr.org/2016/06/are-u-s-millennial-men-just-as-sexist-as-their-dads)

> 2016 年 2 月，National Institutes of Health 的 researchers 发表了一项 [study](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4749286/)，研究 college biology students 如何看待 classmates 的 intelligence 和 achievements。这些 researchers found，male students 相较于 women，systematically overestimated 了班上 men 的 knowledge。此外，随着 academic term 推进，尽管已有 clear evidence 表明 women 的 class performance 更 superior，men 对 classmates 的 abilities 的 faulty appraisal 仍在 increased。在 examined 的每个 biology class 中，被认为是最 renowned 的 student 的都是一个 man——即使有一个 woman 的 grades 好得多。相比之下，接受 surveyed 的 female students 没有表现出 bias，而是 based on performance 对 fellow students 进行 accurately evaluating。在 studying 这些未来 scientists 的 attitudes 后，researchers concluded：“women 所处的 chilly environment\[在 sciences 中\]可能不会很快消失。”

## 原文

Yes, one more post about the Google memo. Here, I review the evidence that was put forward to support the idea that the gender gap in STEM participation (especially in engineering) is due to stereotypes and discrimination.

Admittedly, I haven't discussed much this topic on my previous posts, as I have a strong prior against this hypothesis, and the evidence I have presented points to a strong role for biology (And there are other things I want to read and write about!). But if one thinks that the role of stereotypes and discrimination is big, then by implication one will think than that of biology is small. As the Moorean shift goes, one man's modus tollens is another man's modus ponens. Since no one else is going to do it, here I will discuss the other side of the question that I have ignored: how strong is the evidence for the negative cultural hypothesis? In what follows, I limit myself to evidence presented during the debate. Due to the recent meta-analysis cited in one of my previous posts, I will dismiss directly references to stereotype threat.

The best collection to start is Rachel Thomas'[post](https://medium.com/tech-diversity-files/if-you-think-women-in-tech-is-just-a-pipeline-problem-you-haven-t-been-paying-attention-cb7a2073b996)  and this other[one](https://www.wired.com/story/why-men-dont-believe-the-data-on-gender-bias-in-science/?mbid=social_twitter_onsiteshare).

### If you think women in tech is just a pipeline problem, you haven’t been paying attention (Thomas)

> When researcher [Kieran Snyder interviewed 716 women](http://fortune.com/2014/10/02/women-leave-tech-culture/) who left tech after an average tenure of 7 years, almost all of them said they liked the work itself, but most were unhappy with the work environment.

From the linked article:

> Over lunch, she confided in me that she was thinking of quitting. It was too hard to juggle everything. Her manager had pressured her to return from leave early, and was pushing her again to take a business trip and leave her nursing infant at home. She wasn’t sleeping. She felt like she was failing her job and her child at the same time. \[...\]
>

Rather, it was the lack of flexible work arrangements, the unsupportive work environment, or a salary that was inadequate to pay for childcare. As Rebecca, a former motion graphics designer, put it, “Motherhood was just the amplifier. It made all the problems that I’d been putting up with forever actually intolerable.”

So far, this is just a tradeoff between different life projects: work and family, no discrimination here. Here there is, though:

> One-hundred-ninety-two women cited discomfort working in environments that felt overtly or implicitly discriminatory as a primary factor in their decision to leave tech. That’s just over a quarter of the women surveyed.

So just over a quarter: The vast majority of women did not perceive any discrimination. The data and/or report itself are not available for further comment. Back to Thomas:

> In NSF-funded research, [Nadya Fouad surveyed 5,300 women](http://www.npr.org/sections/alltechconsidered/2014/08/12/339638726/many-women-leave-engineering-blame-the-work-culture) who had earned engineering degrees (of all types) over the last 50 years, and 38% of them were no longer working as engineers. Fouad summarized her findings on why they leave with “It’s the climate, stupid!”

In that article:

> From the aerospace sector to Silicon Valley, engineering has a retention problem: Close to 40 percent of women with engineering degrees either leave the profession or never enter the field.

The [linked report](http://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2014/08/pushed-back.pdf) does not consider the obvious control: men. How many men leave engineering? If we found that also 40% of men with engineering degrees are not in engineering, the picture would greatly change, as it would show that it is not because of discrimination that they leave, but due to more benign factors (not liking the job itself, or finding a better job elsewhere)

The report doesn't really mention discrimination or hostility as a factor for they leaving. The testimonies included suggest that it was the long working hours and requirement for high availability that discouraged them. One of the slides mentions that , for the group that left engineering less than 5 years ago, two thirds left to pursue better opportunities in other fields, and a third left to stay at home. Another interesting result is that there is no differences by industry in terms of perceptions of supportiveness: the field of bioscience is as supportive of women as computer science. Hence, different support wouldn't explain the computer science vs bioscience gender gap.

There is some work that addresses the male vs female retention issue. [Kahn & Ginther (2015)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4541081/) for example cite other work that shows that also many men leave STEM. On industry jobs, one of the studies cited, by the [Society of Women Engineers](https://web.archive.org/web/20160406111912/https://societyofwomenengineers.swe.org/images/swemagazine/RetentionStudyCompilation.pdf), finds that for the cohort who graduated in 2003-2005, 60% of women stayed in STEM, compared to 70% of men, so a gap of 10%. The study also mentions that equality of treatment in engineering is improving. In 1993, 15.3% women engineers reported "consistent inequities in treatment for male and female workers doing the same job". In 2005, it is down to 9.6%, according to women. According to men, the numbers are 6.6% and 3.2%.

The study also discusses why men and women leave STEM, and only a small number of women mentioned negative work climate issues for leaving throughout the years (10%), while 5% of men left for the same reason. The main reason cited by both groups were interest in other careers, followed by advancement opportunities and lack of challenge.

Kahn and Ginther's paper concludes:

> Family status is of key importance. **Women with children are most likely to leave the labor force and therefore engineering. Single women without children are actually less likely than men to leave engineering** (by the 7–8 year point) for 4 of the 5 cohorts.
> 
> **Similarly, women who remain working full-time on average are somewhat _more_ likely than full-time men to remain in engineering jobs** through the 3–4 year post-BSE point, and equally or more likely 7–8 years post-BSE for four of the five cohorts. Dividing by family status, single women without children who work full-time are more likely to remain for four of the five cohorts at the 7–8 year point and even women with children are equally likely to remain for 3 of the 5 cohorts.

This makes the 40% of women leave engineering statistic less catastrophic, and emphasises the role of preferences for work hours and family. Discrimination, as per these studies, looks like a minor thing.

The study finds that a pitch delivered by a male is 1.57 times more prefered than the same pitch delivered by a woman.

But this is not the only study that analyses this issue. [Boulton et al. What these two studies found, however, was some degree not of discrimination but homophilic bias: male investors prefer male entrepreneurs and female investors prefer female entrepreneurs.

> -   In a randomized, double-blind study by Yale researchers, science faculty at 6 major institutions evaluated applications for a lab manager position. [Applications randomly assigned a male name were rated as significantly more competent and hirable](http://www.pnas.org/content/109/41/16474.full.pdf+html) and offered a higher starting salary and more career mentoring, compared to identical applications assigned female names.

This is the Moss-Racusin et al. (2012) study, which fortunately has already been discussed for my by [Lee Jussim](https://www.psychologytoday.com/blog/rabble-rouser/201707/why-brilliant-girls-tend-favor-non-stem-careers) (ht/ to F[rancisco Boni](https://twitter.com/boni_bo) for the pointer), says Jussim:

> "The Moss-Racusin study is, by conventional standards, the weakest of the studies. Its sample size is a fraction of that of the others. It studies a relatively minor situation (hiring lab managers). It was a single study (Su et al is a meta-analysis of scores of studies; Williams and Ceci reported five separate studies). In contrast to Wang et al, it only studied an event at a single time point; it did not follow people’s career trajectories.

But by conventional scientific standards, Su et al's meta-analysis, the replications in Williams and Ceci, the longitudinal Wang et al study, and the far larger sample sizes in all three mean that, on most scientific methodological standards, they are superior to the Moss-Racusin et al study.

Now, Wang et al and Williams and Ceci came out later, so probably the most useful column is the last. Since 2015, the weaker Moss-Racusin study has been cited 50% more often than the other three combined! That means there are probably more papers citing the Moss-Racusin et al study and completely ignoring the other three, than there are papers citing even one of the other three! What kind of "science" are we, that so many "scientists" can get away with so systematically ignoring relevant data in our scientific journals?

> 
> And that, gentle reader, is a gigantic scientific bias. It might even be beyond bias. Some might call it an “obsession” with discrimination and bias so severe that it is blinding many in our field to major findings regarding gender differences that contribute to preferences for different types of fields"

> -   When men and women negotiated a job offer by reading identical scripts for a Harvard and CMU study, [women who asked for a higher salary were rated as being more difficult to work with and less nice](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=779506), but men were not perceived negatively for negotiating.

The study also showed that a candidate being female was related to a higher willingness to work with her when the evaluator was male. No such effect was seen when the evaluator was female. And the study also showed that some degree of homophilia may be at play (Table 2). In rating willingness to work, men only rated women lower (d=1.14), while women rated both men (d=1.16) and to a lesser extent women (d=0.6) lower. In niceness, men only rated women as lower whey asked for a higher salary (d=0.66), and women ranked both men (d=1.41) and women (d=1.23) as less nice. A similar effect was present in demandingness.

This is, to be sure, something that should not be happening. Homophilia is an anti-women and anti-men bias, held by men and women, respectively.

That said, **I agree that this study does reveal an overall discrimination against women**. But is it relevant to explain the STEM gap? (The study considers a scenario where participants want to be assigned to a more senior management position and they are interviewing for that position)

> -   Psychology faculty were sent CVs for an applicant (randomly assigned male or female name), and both men and women were [significantly more likely to hire a male applicant](http://advance.cornell.edu/documents/ImpactofGender.pdf) than a female applicant with an identical record.

I couldn't access the paper, which seems to be from 1999 and for psychology, not STEM. However, for STEM we do have a recent study ([Williams & Ceci, 2015a](http://www.pnas.org/content/112/17/5360.full.pdf)), and they found that there is a 2:1 preference FOR women in hiring decision for STEM tenure track positions. This holds across biology, engineering, and psychology. For economics, preference is about 1:1.

> [Performance reviews of high-performers in tech](http://fortune.com/2014/08/26/performance-review-gender-bias/), negative personality criticism (such as abrasive, strident, or irrational) showed up in 85% of reviews for women and just 2% of reviews for men. It is ridiculous to assume that 85% of women have personality problems and that only 2% of men do.

This is again the same Kieran Snyder we mentioned before, with a sample size of 248, and again there is no source data available, or no peer-reviewed paper behind. I'm no peer-review nazi, but I I use the fact that something is peer-reviewed and open to give it more weight, and so should you.

> Bias is typically justified post-hoc. Our initial subconscious impression of the female applicant is negative, and then we find logical reasons to justify it. For instance, in the above [study by Yale researchers](http://www.socialjudgments.com/docs/Uhlmann%20and%20Cohen%202005.pdf) if the male applicant for police chief had more street smarts and the female applicant had more formal education, evaluators decided that street smarts were the most important trait, and if the names were reversed, evaluators decided that formal education was the most important trait.

The study has a sample size of 73, and the result is what it says, with p~0.01. There is more recent work on that area, including a meta-analysis ([Koch et al., 2015](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Amanda_Koch/publication/262682077_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Gender_Stereotypes_and_Bias_in_Experimental_Simulations_of_Employment_Decision_Making/links/57744b9d08aeb9427e2421b9.pdf)). The meta-analysis itself is of laboratory experiments in hiring, but they mention field studies, where meta-analysis report that in the real world, bias is small, and if anything, in favour of women.

> In meta-analyses of field studies, both Bowen, Swim, and Jacobs (2000) and Roth, Purvis, and Bobko (2012) reported small overall effect sizes for male–female differences (overall d values of .01 and .11, respectively, with females receiving higher scores than males), but gender differences varied across moderators such as gender stereotypicality of the rating measure, rater gender, and type of rating. One advantage of these meta-analyses of field studies assessing gender differences in ratings is the use of actual employee evaluations, allowing for increased confidence in the generalizability of findings. However, one drawback is the inability to unambiguously attribute gender differences in field studies to any particular cause, such as gender bias or true gender differences in performance.

The study reports a ream of findings, including an overall small pro-male bias. But they also found that those effects become really small for highly experienced raters (vs undergrads), and people who felt that their hiring decision was important, which is the setting for hiring decisions in the real world, thus explaining why the field studies didn't found any bias.

The study also says that bias is greater when the skill of the applicant is uncertain. But even this bias could have some degree of justification. If one has a background prior that men on average will fit better in that job (for whatever reason), then on receiving noisy information about two equal candidates of different genders, one has to - to comply with Bayes' theorem - opt for the male for the reasons discussed [here](https://necpluribusimpar.net/politically-incorrect-guide-affirmative-action/). Now, is one justified in having such a prior? Well, if one accepts that the average man fits better than the average woman in that job, and accepts that, even when the population that applies for the job is self-selected, there is some noise in that self-selection, then one can be justified. Noise here means that women or men who won't fit in the job apply regardless.

I don't claim that it is the case here, but that it could be.

### Why men don't believe the data on gender bias in science

This second article cites:

> One early [study](http://www.nature.com/nature/journal/v387/n6631/abs/387341a0.html) evaluated postdoctoral fellowship applications in the biomedical sciences and found that the women had to be 2.5 times more productive than the men in order to be rated equally scientifically competent by the senior scientists evaluating their applications. The authors concluded, “Our study strongly suggests that peer reviewers cannot judge scientific merit independent of gender. The peer reviewers over-estimated male achievements and/or underestimated female performance.” The study finds that “gender discrimination of the magnitude we have observed… could entirely account for the lower success rate of female as compared with male researchers in attaining high academic rank.”

The study is correct and proves what they say (N~100). However, from that the article goes on to say

> These are just a few of the hundreds of peer-reviewed studies that clearly show, on average, the bar is set higher for women in science than for their male counterparts.

But this doesn't seem to cohere well with the Williams-Ceci study and the literature citen therein. Indeed, in another paper ([Ceci et al. , 2014](http://sites.bu.edu/shulamitkahn/files/2016/03/Psychological-Science-in-the-Public-Interest-2014-Ceci-75-141.pdf)) they directly address this paper, showing that it the literature does not in general point in that direction:

> Sex biases in grant funding rates. Numerous commentators have claimed that sex bias in grant review is responsible for fewer women getting funded (or getting funded at lower levels) and that this failure to gain grants is responsible for women’s lower rate of persistence and lower rate of promotion. For example, Lortie et al. (2007) wrote that “it is now recognized that (sex) biases function at many levels within science, including funding allocation, employment, publication, and general research directions” (p. 1247). Notwithstanding such claims and myriad others (e.g., Wenneras & Wold, 1997), there are no systematic sex differences in grant-funding rates, although men’s grant awards tend to be for higher dollar amounts, likely as a result of men’s greater likelihood of being principal investigators on large center grants and program projects. Overall, however, men and women have very similar funding rates for their grant proposals (e.g., Jayasinghe, Marsh, & Bond, 2003; Ley & Hamilton, 2008; Marsh, Bornmann, Mutz, Daniel, & O’Mara, 2009; Mutz, Bornmann, & Daniel, 2012; Pohlhaus, Jiang, Wagner, Schaffer, & Pinn, 2011; RAND, 2005) Below, we summarize this literature. ### Preferences: still the elephant in the room

The studies above deal with issues like sexism, inaccurate stereotypes and various biases, but they do not deal with what seems to be the root cause of the gender composition of the STEM fields: preferences. As we have seen, almost everyone who has looked into the issue, from psychologists to sociologists, seem to point to preferences as the key driver. But then the paths diverge: some argue that those preferences are just due to culture, others that biology also plays a role. Normatively, I have to emphasise, this does not settle anything. If it's all biology, one can still say that it has to be corrected, like male's greater propensity for violence.

So: Are girls and boys raised differently in those aspects that would later manifest in different choices of career? I welcome papers about that.
