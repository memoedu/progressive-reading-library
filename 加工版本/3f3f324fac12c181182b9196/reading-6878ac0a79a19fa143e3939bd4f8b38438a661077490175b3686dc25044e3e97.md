# Nintil - Comments on 'Fully Grown'

---

## 正文

你可能还记得我在 2016 年写的一篇文章，[没有 Great Technological Stagnation](https://nintil.com/no-great-technological-stagnation/)，其中我提出了几项值得重申、如今依然 valid 的论点：Technology 并不等同于 productivity growth，technology 也不存在 great stagnation；实际存在的是 localized slowdowns、accelerations 和 stagnations[1](https://nintil.com/fully-grown/#focus)，当然，TFP 也不只是 technology。去年我还写过 WWII 是否[有利于 growth](https://nintil.com/was-world-war-ii-good-for-growth/)，并链接了一些 evidence，表明 population 的 rate of growth 可能是 productivity growth 出现 stagnation 的背后原因。

当初我写第一篇文章时，Dietz Vollrath 写了一篇回应我 blogpost 的文章（另见这条 [comment](https://growthecon.com/blog/Decomposition/#comment-2652361775)），他当时就已经提出这样的观点：economy 中各个 sectors 有多 productive，以及它们在 economy 中所占的规模有多 large，对于 explain 所 observed 到的 economic growth 的 patterns 至关重要。

更早之前，Noah Smith 也写过[这篇文章](https://noahpinionblog.blogspot.com/2011/04/tfp-and-great-stagnation.html)，其中 shows，70s 的 TFP stagnation 实际上是 'nondurables' 的 stagnation（services 就在其中）。

把这些放在一起看，我喜欢这本书，并且觉得它的 explanation 是 plausible，也就不足为奇了。或许可以 criticise 某些具体的 numbers（例如 Piketty-Saez-Zucman data）和 assumptions，但总体而言，这本书的 thesis 是说得通的（[summary](https://mattsclancy.substack.com/p/maybe-there-is-no-technological-slowdown) 可参见 Matt Clancy 的这篇文章）。

话虽如此，读完这本书后，我还是想提出几点：

其一，对于“更多 human capital”这一方面，存在一些显而易见的 solutions：对 aging 开展更多 research，将有助于 delay 甚至 reverse 它；起初，成功可能表现为 extended life expectancy，同时伴随着 increased elderly population 带来的拖累，但未来或许就不会如此了。pro-natalist policies 或许也有其理由，但正如 Vollrath 所提到的，它们可能需要几十年才能 take effect，而且即便如此，其力度也必须比迄今任何 country 尝试过的措施都更加 drastic。我也预计，其他人会在其他书评中提出这样的 [argument](https://www.adamsmith.org/research/children-of-when-why-housing-is-the-solution-to-britains-fertility-crisis)：实际上，人们并没有 preference，想要像今天 observed 到的那样 small 的 families，因此，families 如此 small 并不是 progress 的标志。

另一点是，housing regulation 更多的 areas 与 high productivity 之间的 correlation，可能有 statistical explanation：如果一个地方的生活成本被推高，那么只有 highly productive（因而也是 well paid）的 workers 才有可能住在那里。有一些 anecdotic reports 说，[artists](https://www.sfgate.com/expensive-san-francisco/article/Artists-leaving-sf-bay-area-grass-is-greener-12382578.php) 已经离开了 SF，因为那里已经变得 unaffordable。这在本书的 argument 中并不起任何作用，但值得一提。

虽然这本书的书名是 'Fully Grown'，而且作者也作出了 caveats，指出关注 GDP 的 distributional aspect 依然重要，但即便如此，我仍认为，处于 growth frontier 的 countries 远未 fully grown：Services 仍然是 insufficiently automated 的；比如 [education](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Case-against-Education-System-Waste/dp/0691174652) 或 healthcare[2](https://nintil.com/fully-grown/#healthcare)。未来的确看起来会是工作很少、乐趣很多的样子，但我们离那一步还很远。这并不是说作者赞同自满；相反，这本书的观点是，_一旦这些都完成了_，它们就会给 GDP 带来 one-off boost，但也仅此而已。既然好东西多一些总比少一些好，那么这些额外的 improvements 就值得追求；最差的情况下，我们也会获得 GDP 那令人欣喜的 step jump，而最好的情况下，我们或许真能获得更多 growth。

1

直接关注 technology 而非 economic metrics，其意义在于 technology 更容易 measure（不过，代价是 technologies 之间的 comparisons 会变得更困难），而且这样可以依据相关 physical principles 以及有关 fields 的 knowledge，对各个 field 逐一开展 fine-grained assessment；这又有助于思考，哪些 fields 的 advancing 比人们可能预期的更 slower。我关于 [skyscraper construction speeds](https://nintil.com/building-skyscrapers-and-spending-on-major-projects/) 的文章，就是这类 analysis 的一个 example。[2](https://nintil.com/fully-grown/#healthcare)：等着有人出版一本 'The Case Against Healthcare'（《反对 Healthcare 的理由》）吧。我在对 [The Elephant in the Brain 的书评中有一些相关笔记，当然，书本身也有所讨论](https://nintil.com/this-review-is-not-about-reviewing-the-elephant-in-the-brain/)。

## 原文

You might remember a post I wrote in 2016, [No Great Technological Stagnation](https://nintil.com/no-great-technological-stagnation/) where I argued various things that are worth repeating and valid today: Technology is not the same as productivity growth, and there is no great stagnation in technology; rather there are localized slowdowns, accelerations, and stagnations[1](https://nintil.com/fully-grown/#focus), and of course TFP is more than technology. Also last year I wrote on whether WWII was [good for growth](https://nintil.com/was-world-war-ii-good-for-growth/), and I linked to some evidence that the rate of growth of population could be behind a stagnation in productivity growth.

Back when I wrote that first post, Dietz Vollrath wrote a reply to my blogpost (See also this [comment](https://growthecon.com/blog/Decomposition/#comment-2652361775)) where he was already putting out there the idea that how productive the various sectors of the economy are, and how large they are within the economy, matter to explain the observed patterns of economic growth.

From way earlier there was also [this](https://noahpinionblog.blogspot.com/2011/04/tfp-and-great-stagnation.html) from Noah Smith, that shows that the TFP stagnation of the 70s is actually an stagnation of 'nondurables' (Where services are).

This all put together it won't come as a surprise that I've liked the book and I find the explanation plausible. One could possibly criticise some particular numbers (e.g. the Piketty-Saez-Zucman data) and assumptions, but overall the thesis of the book makes sense (See this from Matt Clancy for [a summary](https://mattsclancy.substack.com/p/maybe-there-is-no-technological-slowdown)).

With this said, there are various points that I wanted to raise after my reading of the book:

One is that there are some obvious solutions to the "more human capital" side of things: More research into aging will help delay or even reverse it; at first success looks like extended life expectancy, with the drag of a increased elderly population but perhaps in the future that won't be the case. There could also be a case for pro-natalist policies, but as Vollrath mentions, that could take decades to take effect, and even then it would have to be something more drastic than anything that has been tried in any country so far. I also expect someone else in some other review to bring up the [argument](https://www.adamsmith.org/research/children-of-when-why-housing-is-the-solution-to-britains-fertility-crisis) that actually, there is no preference for families as small as the ones observed today, and thus them being that small is not a sign of progress.

Another point is that the correlation between areas with more housing regulation and high productivity may have a statistical explanation: If a place is made expensive to live, only highly productive (And thus well paid) workers could even live there. There are anecdotic reports of [artists](https://www.sfgate.com/expensive-san-francisco/article/Artists-leaving-sf-bay-area-grass-is-greener-12382578.php) having left SF, having become unaffordable. This doesn't play any role in the argument of the book, but it's worth mentioning.

Although the book's title is 'Fully Grown', even after the author's caveats that a focus on the distributional aspect of the GDP is still important, I think countries at the growth frontier are far from fully grown: Services are insufficiently automated; [education](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Case-against-Education-System-Waste/dp/0691174652) or healthcare[2](https://nintil.com/fully-grown/#healthcare). It is true that the future looks like little work and lots of fun, but we are far from there. Not that the author endorses complacency; instead the book's point is that _once this is all done_ then it's a one-off boost to GDP, but that's it. As more of the good stuff is better than less of it, those additional improvements are worth pursuing; at worst we'll get that juicy step jump in GDP and at best we might get actually more growth.

1

The point of focusing on technology directly rather than economic metrics is that it is easier to measure (Though one has to pay the price of making comparisons between technologies harder), and it allows for a fine-grained assessment field by field, informed by relevant physical principles and knowledge of the fields in question; which in turn helps thinking about what fields are advancing slower than one might expect them to. An example of this sort of analysis is my piece on [skyscraper construction speeds](https://nintil.com/building-skyscrapers-and-spending-on-major-projects/). [2](https://nintil.com/fully-grown/#healthcare): Wait until someone publishes 'The Case Against Healthcare'. Some notes on that in my review of [The Elephant in the Brain and of course the book itself](https://nintil.com/this-review-is-not-about-reviewing-the-elephant-in-the-brain/).
