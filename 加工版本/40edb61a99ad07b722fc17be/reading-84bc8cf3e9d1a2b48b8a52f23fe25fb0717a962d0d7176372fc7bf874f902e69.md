# Identity theory 与 functionalism

---

## 正文

我在上一篇关于 consciousness 的文章中收到了一些回复。这里我会回应这些回复，并补充一些关于 consciousness 的想法。我有意略过 David Pearce 的评论，因为我将在未来专门用一整篇文章讨论他提出的 consciousness 的 solution。

## Identity theory 与 functionalism

评论者 Simon 不同意我对 identity theory 的 characterisation（functionalism 将归入其中）。请注意，我在上一篇文章中没有讨论 functionalism。Functionalism 可以有两种理解方式，或者说有两种用法

Functionalism-A：perform 相同 function 的 Systems 都会是 conscious。按照这种 view，我会是 conscious，China brain 或 Chinese room 也会如此。在这里，consciousness 是 really real。因此，它就必须是 epiphenomenalism 或其他 non-materialist theories 的一个 subclass。

Functionalism-B：一个 system 的 function 就是全部。我们有一个 brain，并把 brain 所做的某些 processes 称为“consciousness”。这里的 consciousness 是 merely real，却不是 really real。一旦 easy problem of consciousness 得到 solved，就不会再有其他需要 explain 的东西。如果是这样，这种 view 就是 eliminativism 的一种 form。

Brian Tomasik 同意我的这一 analysis。我还想补充，identity theorist 想既保有 materialist 这块蛋糕，又享用 dualism，但这是不可能的。Claiming X physical property 与 Y phenomenal property 是 identical，就像 claiming 苹果与 [Catbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catbus) 是 identical 一样。摆脱这个问题的办法是澄清自己的意思：究竟是 A 还是 B types 的 functionalism。Carroll 的是 B type functionalism。我猜 A type functionalism 在 scientists 中很普遍。

> a) 你的五个 theses 中，我唯一 deny 的是 (5)。我认为，只要有足够的 understanding，consciousness 就能用 algorithmic language 来 described，这种 language 将 describe（human brain 中）neurons firing 的 behavior，就像我们今天能够用 describe transistors 中 electrons moving 的 behavior 的 algorithmic language，来 describe video game 中与 3D world interacting 的 character 一样。我们觉得这不可能，唯一的原因是我们距离所需的 understanding，还差一个或多个重大的 insights。

Thesis 5 的意思是，人不能从一组与某个 conclusion 毫无关系的 premises 中 logically derive 那个 conclusion。（is-ought gap 就是其中一个 example）。在 videogame character 的 example 中，只要有足够的时间和研究生，我们就能 explain 为什么玩 Starcraft 2 时，一个 [Archon](http://starcraft.wikia.com/wiki/Archon_\(StarCraft_II\)) 会在 screen 中那样 moved，一直解释到 transistor level。我们的 explanation 可以用 low level language 来完成（纯粹从 electrons 的 flows，以及 screen 中的 pixels 被 activated 时随之出现的 photons 来谈）。要得到 high-level explanation，例如“这个 Archon moved down，以便 get close to enemy 的 base”，我们就需要一条 premises 的 chain，逐步 introduce 那些 high level concepts。我们可以根据它当前的 position 来 define ‘down’，再根据它在 game-map 中的 position 来 define position，然后根据 computer 的 working 来 define 后者，最后转向 transistors。Archon 的 movement 并没有任何超出这些的东西，而这种 higher-level language，是对那条从 transistors 的 workings 到 videogame 中 behaviour 的 appearence 的 highly complex causal chain 的 short-hand。

Thesis 5 说，你不能只从 laws of physics 就得出“这个 Archon moves”。你需要先 introduce 能够 explain 什么是 Archon、什么是 moving 的 premises。这样做 respects Thesis 5。如果有人说 ‘Archon’ 是一种 high-level description，我们不会有异议；但如果有人说 consciousness 是 brain 中某些 processes 的 high level description，我们就会有异议。这会是 type-B functionalism，而它无法 account for real consciousness，所以我觉得它不能令人满意。

> b) 我不认为“poetic”是用来 describe ‘consciousness’ 这个 concept 与 human brain 中 neuronal activity 之间 relationship 的恰当词语。把 car 称为“car”，而不是谈论 engine 如何与 steering 和 transmission systems interacts 等等，这就是“poetic”吗？不是，它是用来指称一长串以 specific way assembled、能够表现出 specific behavior 的 specific components 的正确英语词语。

好吧，也许这个词并不恰当！:) 我之所以用它，基本上是因为 Carroll 在用它。这里的 ‘Poetic’ 是“对一个 phenomenon 的 High-level description，而这个 phenomenon 并没有任何超出我们今天大致理解的 physics 的东西”的 short-hand。

> c) 同样，如果某个东西只要是由 parts 构成的，就属于“merely real”（而非 really real），那么这意味着，除了 fundamental particles（以及，如果你是对的，consciousness）之外，一切 exists 的东西都是 merely real。Water molecules、bacteria、human bodies、human societies、stars，以及作为整体的 universe，全都是“merely real”。你刚刚宣布，对于自己每天思考和谈论的 concepts 中的 99.99%+，你都是一个 eliminativist，这难道不奇怪吗？

的确如此。我是一个 mereological nihilist，我会说，几乎一切都是 merely real。一直追究到底，都是 fields 和 quarks……再加上 consciousness。merely/really real distinction 之所以有用，正是因为它带来了非常鲜明的 contrast。我认为，说 car 除了它的 parts 以及将它们联系起来的 laws 之外别无其他，并不令人震惊。并不存在 intrinsic ‘carness nature’。我们拥有的最好的 theory of concepts（当然是，[Huemer 2015](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007%2F978-1-137-34455-7_3)）告诉我们，concepts 是 property space 中的 fuzzy regions（由我们自己划定）。

really/merely distinction 也帮助我们避开某些人使用的 linguistic ploy：他们并不真正相信 consciousness，却以另一种（non-realist）sense 使用这个词。当他们说“consciousness 当然是 real！Consciousness 与 brain activity 是 identical！”时，我们可以进一步探究，看他们使用的是 strong（really）还是 weak（merely）sense，并澄清他们真正的意思。

> 所以，作为一个 computational functionalist，我会说：1) Consciousness 是 really real，而且与其他一切一样，它也是由 parts 构成的。

我同意前半部分，也倾向于同意后半部分；尽管 consciousness 在我们看来似乎是 unified experience，我还是愿意考虑这样一种可能性：它并不是 really 那么 unified（只是 merely 如此）。

> 2.  我们不是 p-zombies，因为 p-zombies 并非 possible，尽管 human mind 能 conceive of 它们，就像不懂 cars 的人能 conceive of 一辆没有 engine 却在 moving 的 car 一样。然而……

如果我必须同时在脑中保有现有的 laws of physics 和那辆 engineless car，那么我就无法 conceive 它。如果我们提出这样一种东西，就需要考虑一个具有不同 laws 的 world。但这正是 zombie-ists 所说的：David Chalmers 说，zombies 在这个 universe 中并非 possible，但在另一个 universe 中，它们可能是 possible。那会是一个缺少 psychophysical laws（这些 laws define 哪些 systems 能获得 consciousness），或者缺少作为 fundamental property 的 consciousness 的 universe。他这样说，是为了强调 consciousness 并不是能从我们今天所知的 laws of physics 中直接 flow 出来的东西（难道有人曾在 [standard model 的 Lagrangian](http://www.symmetrymagazine.org/article/the-deconstructed-standard-model-equation) 中看到隐藏在某处的 consciousness 吗？）。

> 3.  也许有可能 assemble 一台 machine，它外表像我们、behaves 也像我们，却不是 conscious；但如果我们仔细查看它的 ‘brain’（或使它 act 和 react 的任何 material mechanism），就会发现它的 work 方式与我们的完全不同。因此，如果 Chinese room 只是在 implementing 一堆“if input X, output Y” rules，它就不是 conscious；但由非 neurons 的东西 implemented 的 human brain 的 replica 会是 conscious，无论那东西是四处 moving electrons 的 transistors，还是四处 passing messages 的人。

这很有意思，而且我在一定程度上赞同。问题在于，从 physics 的角度看，human brain 就是一堆 if input X, output Y。如果我们彻底采用 functionalism，那么 implementation 并不重要，function 才重要。我见过有人 claiming Chinese room 是 conscious！借用 Searle 的说法，Simon 心里想的也许是，replica 必须 replicate ‘brain 的 causal powers’。也就是说，parts 应当以与 human brain 中相同（或相似）的方式彼此 connected。我同意这一点：通过弄清 brain 中什么地方、什么东西与 consciousness 是 related，并 replicating 它，应当有可能在 non-neurons 中 replicate consciousness。

> 4.  当 Mary sees red 时，她确实 learn 到了新东西，算是吧——这里对“learn”采用了稍微宽松的 definition：她的 neurons 将以前所未有的方式 fire，不久之后，store 她的 memories 的 neurons 就会以一种新方式被 configured，以 hold 一个新的 memory。

我同意。但要点在于，她无法仅仅通过 studying physics、neurology 和一般的 cognitive science 来 learn 这一点。

> 5.  所有声称 consciousness 并非由 parts 构成的 theories of mind，都无异于拿一个神秘的 concept，把它放进盒子，给盒子贴上“fundamental”的标签，然后宣布 problem 已经 solved。这不是好的 philosophy。

Compare：所有声称 quarks 并非由 parts 构成的 theories of matter，都无异于把 problem 藏起来，等等。Reduction 想必总得从某处 begin。在 Chalmers 那里，微小的 conscious matter（parts！）通过 aggregating 来 form consciousness。

> 6.  我不确定该如何看待 eliminativists。我读过 Dennett 的一些文字，让我觉得他会同意我在这里写下的一切，或许除了关于 Mary’s room 的第 (4) 点；但如果 Tomasik 真的认为我们是 p-zombies，我就不知该说什么了。

是的，他就是这样想的，我直接以非常明确的方式问过他这个问题。我复制粘贴如下。我问的是：

> 只是想澄清一下，Brian，你是否同意：‘Consciousness 是一种 illusion，其 sense 与 Müller-Lyer illusion 是一种 illusion 相同。我（Brian）似乎是 conscious，但经过进一步思考，我（Brian）意识到事实并非如此’；以及‘Zombies 不仅是 possible 和 conceivable，而且每个人实际上都是 zombie，并且在这个 universe 的 laws of physics 下，no-zombies 并不 exist’ ![](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2017/04/18-1.png)

这就是我此前已经讨论过的 functionalism 或 identity theory 的问题：它们是 unstable 的，并且会 collapse 到 epiphenomenalism 或 eliminativism。

> > 你可以有用木头或钢造的桥，但你需要桥来让 external phenomena 进入你的 awareness。回想一下，你之所以在 doubting realism 并 embracing eliminativism，是因为在某个时候，一些 facts 进入了你的 awareness，而你思考了它们。

> 如果 zombies 是 possible 的，那么 zombies 就能在没有 consciousness 的情况下，让 facts 和 ideas 进入它们的 brains。Zombies 可以就自己是 conscious 的作出 statements，可以 know 关于世界的事情，等等。

按照 know 的 weak sense（不附带 mental experience）来说……是的，zombies 能做到所有这些，甚至更多。我在那里提出的 argument，是对 Rob Bensinger 的一个 argument 的回应：qualia 什么也不做，因为如果我们 invert 它们，它们仍然以相同方式 work。我当时 argued，如果 qualia 像由不同材料造的桥那样 work，情况就并非如此，但要有 knowledge，qualia 就必须存在（knowledge implies belief，而 belief implies phenomenal awareenss）。不过，我同意 Tomasik：如果我们以一种不那么 mental 的方式 interpret knowledge，那么是的，当然，zombies 可以和我们 know 得一样多。

## Emergentism（涌现论）

Seva Gunitsky 在 twitter 上说，现在 dismiss emergentism 还为时过早，ontological reductionism 这一基础并不牢靠。他引用了 chemistry 中的 evidence，并引用了[这篇论文](http://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2014/cc/c4cc02812k#!divAbstract)，它似乎提出了一个 strong emergentist thesis：

> 作为 membranous compartment systems 的 artificial vesicles 的所有各种 properties 都 emerge from molecular assembly，因为这些 properties 并不存在于组成该 system 的 individual molecules 中。

然而，这并不是反对 reductionism 的 evidence。

在博客中，我曾在[这里](https://nintil.com/2015/10/04/the-follies-of-holism-a-reductionist-critique/) argued against emergentism。我的 argument 基本上就是说，reductionism 意味着，一个 physical system 的 property 或 behaviour 是 reducible to 它的 components（parts）以及 govern 它们的 laws（laws of nature）的。在这一条件下，system 就是 fully specified 的（不然还能怎样呢？）。当然，前面那篇论文中 vesicles 的 properties 并不存在于 compose 这些 vesicles 的 molecules 中，但这些 properties 是由 molecules 和 laws of physics 所 implied 的。

考虑 water。这是一个常见的 example：water 是 wet 的，而 atoms 不是 wet 的，所以 wetness 是一种 emergent property。但它并不是。wetness 是什么？是说我们触摸它时，它给人 watery 的感觉吗？是说它 flows 吗？不管我们认为它意味着什么，我们都可以先想象一份具有“wetness”的 volume of water，然后再想象一份更小的 volume。如此继续，直到只剩一份非常小的 volume。我们会看到 water molecules 通过 hydrogen bonds 等方式 interacting，而且一路下去都会看到 wetness，直到只有一个 molecule 的情况，它并没有 hydrogen bonds。但这些 hydrogen bonds 虽然不存在于 hydrogen 或 oxygen 中，却是由 laws of physics 所 implied 的。wetness 还包含什么呢？当然，还包含 wetness 的 subjective feeling，但那 reduces to hard problem of consciousness，而不是 emergence 的问题。

对于 consciousness，要让 emergentism 成立，我们需要两样东西：

要么是“particles of consciousness”（类似 Monads），要么是 especify consciousness 如何 emerges 的 psychophysical laws。如果你愿意，可以把这称为 weak emergentism，因为我承认，有些发生的事情并不存在于 parts 中，而是由于 govern 它们的 interaction 的 laws 才 arise 的。我不会这样称呼它。Strong emergentism，也就是认为在我们已经 accounted for components 和 interactions 之后，还存在别的什么的 view，是我所 reject 的。我还没有看到支持它的好 argument，所以如果你知道一个，请留言。

## 一个 zombies 的 universe，一个 angels 的 universe

我喜欢清晰的语言，而且我可以想象，如果我是一个 eliminativist，我会怎样说话。如果我是，我就不会谈论 beliefs、qualia 或 thoughts。我也许会谈论它们，但方式会和我谈论鬼魂或上帝时一样。Eliminativist Artir 会说这样的话：

EA: 你谈论 consciousness 这个东西，却连 explain 它都做不到。你看，它不在 laws of physics 中，不在 elementary particles 中，而且我甚至无法大致理解你在说什么。它就像鬼魂、地精或其他神话生物一样。它不是 real 的。你谈论的其他这些东西，比如 beliefs 或 thoughts，也一样。你有你的 brain，你接收 inputs，它们在里面来回传递，然后在某个时候，你 output 一些什么。除此之外什么也没有。你愿意的话，可以把那些 electric impulses 叫作‘thoughts’，但它们并不是你所说的那种东西。

Realist Artir: 但我是 conscious 的！我会 experiment 事物，我看得到 red 的 redness！

EA: 这就和人们说自己‘相信’‘上帝’，或者说自己见过‘鬼魂’一样。这是你的 brain 的一种 malfunctioning，只要学一点 neuroscience，或者读读 brains 会如何 malfuntion，它就会消失。

RA: 但那些我已经做过了，我仍然这么想！

EA: 好吧，想象一下这个 thought experiment。一个 universe 中有和我们一样的 beings，但它们有这种所谓的‘consciousness’。我们把它们叫作 angels 吧（我从 Rob Bensinger 那里借用了这个 term）。它们做的事情会和我们有什么不同吗？

RA: 嗯，也许……很可能几乎所有人都会像我这样想，而你会成为少数派，说 consciousness 不是 real 的。

EA: 好，这说得通。但除了鹦鹉学舌般反复说自己有一种它们无法 explain 的 property，这些 angels 还能做什么我们——因为你不是 conscious 的，只是糊涂了！——做不到的事？它们能飞吗，它们的 senses 更 accurate 吗，还是怎样？

RA: 嗯，不能，它们只是会有 conscious experiences……但我不确定。

EA: 正是，你看！那会是一种 useless property，它不会 evolved，而如果它确实 existed，它就会是一种 spandrel。但一种只具有让你谈论它这一 effect 的 property of nature，算是什么 property 呢？你不觉得这听起来很荒唐吗？physics 中没有任何余地容许 consciousness 做任何事。如果没有，那它们又怎么会谈论它呢？angel world 是无稽之谈。consciousness 也是！

RA: 但似乎我喜欢听音乐，是因为它听起来那么美妙，我 experience 一个 sound，而它听起来令人愉悦。没有 consciousness，我就不会喜欢它。

EA: 不对：你喜欢音乐，是因为某些 patterns of sound 会 activate 你的 brain 的 reward centres，而这 causes 你去寻求 musical experiences。我也听音乐，原因相同。不需要 consciousness。

RA: 也许说不通的恰恰是_这个_世界。诚然，我还没有探索过 consciousness 能如何 work，但你告诉我的任何东西，或者我迄今读过的任何东西，都不能让我相信我不是 conscious 的。我确实相信，你和其他所有人都和我一样是 conscious 的，而且除了我自己以外，所有人都受了蒙蔽。Universal consensus 和 state of the art scientific knowledge 还不够。

EA: _叹气_

RA: 确实，_叹气_。

## Identity theory and functionalism

Commenter Simon disagrees with me on my characterisation of identity theory (under which functionalism will fall). Note that I didn't discuss functionalism in my previous post. Functionalism can be understood, or is used, in two ways

Functionalism-A: Systems that perform the same function will be conscious. In this view, I would be conscious, and so would be the China brain or the Chinese room. Here, consciousness is really real. And so it would have to be a subclass of epiphenomenalism or the other non-materialist theories.

Functionalism-B: The function of a system is all there is. We have a brain, and we call certain processes that the brain does "consciousness". Here consciousness is merely, but not really, real. Once the easy problem of consciousness is solved, there wouldn't be anything else to explain. If this is so, this view is a form of eliminativism.

Brian Tomasik agrees with me in this analysis. I would add that the identity theorist wants to have its materialist cake and eat the dualism too, but that cannot be. Claiming that X physical property is identical to Y phenomenal property is like claiming that apples are identical to the [Catbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catbus). The way to escape this is to clarify what one means: functionalism of the A or B types. Carroll's is a B type functionalism. I would guess that A type functionalism is widespread among scientists.

> a) The only one of your five theses that I deny is (5). I think that with sufficient understanding, consciousness will be described using algorithmic language that will describe the behavior of neurons firing (in a human brain), in much the same way that today we can describe a character interacting with a 3D world in a video game using algorithmic language that describes the behavior of electrons moving in transistors. The only reason that this seems impossible to us is that we are one or many major insights away from the required understanding.

Thesis 5 is the idea that one cannot logically derive a conclusion from a set of premises that have nothing to do with it. (The is-ought gap is an example of this). In the videogame character example, one can, given enough time and grad students, explain why an [Archon](http://starcraft.wikia.com/wiki/Archon_\(StarCraft_II\)) moved in the screen in that way while playing Starcraft 2, down to the transistor level. Our explanation could be done in a low level language (Talking purely in terms of flows of electrons and then photons when the pixels in the screen are activated). To get to a high-level explanation, like "The Archon moved down to get close to the enemy's base", we would need a chain of premises that would introduce, bit by bit, those high level concepts. We could define 'down' in terms of its current position, then define position in terms of its position in the game-map, then define that in terms of the working of the computer, and then turn to transistors. There is no movement of the Archon above and beyond that, and this higher-level language is a short-hand for that highly complex causal chain from the workings of transistors to the appearence of behaviour in a videogame.

Thesis 5 says that you cannot get to "The Archon moves" just from the laws of physics. You need to introduce premises that explain what is the Archon, and what is moving first. Doing this respects Thesis 5. We would not complain if one says that 'Archon' is a high-level description, but we would if someone says that consciousness is a high level description of certain processes in the brain. That would be type-B functionalism, and it wouldn't account for real consciousness, so I find it unsatisfying.

> b) I don’t think “poetic” is the right word to describe the relationship between the concept of ‘consciousness’ and the neuronal activity in a human brain. Is it “poetic” to call a car “a car” instead of talking about the way that the engine interacts with the steering and transmission systems, and so on? No, it’s the correct English word to refer to a long list of specific components assembled in a specific way, capable of specific behavior.

Well, maybe it's not the right word! :) I was using it because Carroll was using it, basically. 'Poetic' is here short-hand for "High-level description of a phenomenon that is nothing above and beyond physics roughly as we understand it today".

The merely/really real distinction is useful precisely because it leads to a very sharp contrast. I think it is not shocking to say that a car is nothing above and beyond its parts and the laws that tie them together. There is no intrinsic 'carness nature'. The best theory of concepts that we have (of course,[Huemer 2015](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007%2F978-1-137-34455-7_3)) tells us that concepts are fuzzy regions (that we make up) in property space.

## Emergentism

Seva Gunitsky on twitter said that it is too early to dismiss emergentism, that we ontological reductionism is a shaky foundation. He cites evidence from chemistry and cites [this paper](http://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlelanding/2014/cc/c4cc02812k#!divAbstract) which seem to state a strong emergentist thesis:

> All the various properties of artificial vesicles as membranous compartment systems emerge from molecular assembly as these properties are not present in the individual molecules the system is composed of.

However, this is no evidence against reductionism.

In the blog, I argued against emergentism [here](https://nintil.com/2015/10/04/the-follies-of-holism-a-reductionist-critique/). My argument, basically, is to say that reductionism means that a property or behaviour of a physical system is reducible to its components (parts) and the laws that govern them (laws of nature). Given this, the system is fully specified (How else could it be?). Of course, the properties of the vesicles in the previous paper are not present in the molecules that compose the vesicles, but those properties are implied by the molecules and the laws of physics.

Consider water. It is a common example: water is wet, and atoms are not wet, so wetness is an emergent property. But it ain't. What is wetness? That it we touch it, it feels watery? That it flows? Whatever we take it to mean, we can begin by imagining a volume of water that has "wetness" and then imagine a smaller volume. And so on till we are left with a very small volume. We'll see water molecules interacting via, among others, hydrogen bonds, and we will see wetness all the way down to the one molecule case, which doesn't have hydrogen bonds. But these hydrogen bonds, while not present in hydrogen or oxygen, are implied by the laws of physics. What else is there to wetness? A subjective feeling of wetness, sure, but that reduces to the hard problem of consciousness, but that's not an issue of emergence.

With consciousness, for emergentism to work we need two things:

Either "particles of consciousness" (a la Monads) or psychophysical laws that especify how consciousness emerges. You could call this weak emergentism if you wish, as I accept there are things happening that were not in the parts, but that arise due to the laws that govern their interaction. I wouldn't call it like that. Strong emergentism, the view that once that we have accounted for components and interactions, there is something else, is something I reject. I haven't seen a good argument for that, so if you know one, please leave a comment.

    > What is consciousness “for”? Why has consciousness evolved “if it’s as useless as it appears to be”, as Simon puts it (3)?
    > 
    > IMO, its fitness-enhancing role is so fundamental that we're mostly oblivious of its existence. Imagine a notional human without any capacity for phenomenal binding, whether "local" binding or “global” binding – the victim of a deficit syndrome more severe than simultanagnosia, cerebral akinetopsia (“motion blindness”) and florid schizophrenia combined. The central nervous system of this profoundly handicapped human has no capacity to bind neuronal feature-processors into conscious perceptual objects, and no capacity to run a phenomenally bound world-simulation apprehended by a unitary phenomenal self – what Kant forbiddingly calls the “transcendental unity of apperception”. In short, this creature is a micro-experiential zombie, 86 billion odd neuronal pixels of membrane-bound Jamesian “mind-dust”. Perhaps individual neurons of this notional micro-experiential zombie can be identified via neuroscanning as distributed feature-processors – edge-detectors, motion-detectors, colour-mediating neurons, etc – just as our artificial connectionist systems with a sub-symbolic architecture can analogously be “trained up” to perform different computational tasks. Yet this poor handicapped creature can't combine its neuronal micro-experiences into dynamic perceptual objects populating a real-time unitary world-simulation. The CNS of this notional micro-experiential zombie doesn’t even undergo the kind of inky introspective void that you or I experience when closing our eyes. This notional micro-experiential zombie is not a so-called p-zombie – far from it, it's severely handicapped – but like a p-zombie, it’s not even “all dark inside”.
    > 
    > By contrast, the real-life phenomenal binding of Darwinian minds is ridiculously computational powerful - regardless of how you believe that our own conscious minds / world-simulations carry it off, and regardless of whether you think our minds are effectively classical or non-classical information processors. Most people are implicitly perceptual direct realists. They don’t normally conceive of the world-simulation run by their minds as a _functional_ manifestation of consciousness. Instead they identify consciousness with e.g. self-awareness, or meta-cognition, or maybe wonder if consciousness has any real function at all - beyond facilitating interminable debates about consciousness. But a convergence of scientific evidence confirms that what we each pre-theoretically conceive as the “physical” – i.e. solid, medium-sized macroscopic objects “out there” - is as much a manifestation of consciousness as subtle introspective thought-episodes “in here”. This analysis of your phenomenally bound consciousness holds true whether you are dreaming or awake. When you are awake, however, the properties of your phenomenal world-simulation track gross fitness-relevant patterns in your mind-independent local surroundings.
    > 
    > The capacity of nervous systems to run real-time conscious world-simulations as a tool for navigating an unforgiving environment dates to the early Cambrian. In my view, Penrose et al are barking up the wrong end of the evolutionary tree. Yet highlighting the fitness-enhancing properties of phenomenally bound consciousness doesn’t _explain_ how such consciousness is physically possible for a pack of membrane-bound neurons. After all, telepathy would be fitness-enhancing for an organism too.
    > 
    > A non-materialist physicalist will say that, strictly speaking, consciousness per se isn’t evolutionarily “for” anything. Ultimately, all consciousness, and only consciousness, has causal efficacy: it’s the essence of the physical, the "stuff" of the world mathematically described by QFT. Plants, stock markets, digital computers and classically parallel connectionist systems are all examples of information-processing systems that are micro-experiential zombies. The textures of consciousness of their components are incidental, mere implementation details. By contrast, what makes biological minds special is how your consciousness is functionally bound.
    > 
    > Precisely how such functional binding is physically possible is really the topic for another comment / post. But before saying more, a word on Michael Huemer’s plausible-sounding, "1. For any system, every fact about the whole is a necessary consequence of the nature and relations of the parts.” (cf. https://nintil.com/2017/04/07/consciousness-and-its-discontents/)
    > 
    > In classical physics, yes, this assertion is almost a truism. The claim is inconsistent with modern physics. Quantum theory turns Huemer’s assertion of mereological priority on its head. Perhaps see e.g. Jonathan Schaffer's "Monism: The Priority of the Whole" (2.2): https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/ff0f/4e110da053d4ca1a2bacff43b42bb14ebdd3.pdf
    > 
    > Non-materialist physicalists who are wavefunction monists (cf. https://www.amazon.com/Wave-Function-Metaphysics-Quantum-Mechanics/dp/019979054X) face the phenomenal \_un\_binding problem. Why isn't the multiverse a single psychotic mega-mind, so to speak? In my view, the phenomenal \_un\_binding problem is solved by decoherence: the rapid, environmentally-induced scrambling of phase angles of the components of an individual superposition. Applying Zurek’s “quantum Darwinism” (cf. https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.5082.pdf) to the CNS yields a selection mechanism of unimaginable power, turning what would otherwise be nonsensical psychotic “noise” into the classical-seeming world you’re subjectively undergoing right now. Selection pressure more powerful than four billion years of Darwinian evolution (as naively understood) takes place in your CNS every moment of your life.
