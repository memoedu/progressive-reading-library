# 这令人困惑！

---

## 正文

下面是一些关于 venture capital 的事实，如果你学过一些 economics，它们可能会让你感到意外：

-   VC firms 的 returns 往往会 persist over time（[Kaplan and Schoar 2004](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-kaplan)）。换句话说，作为 investor，你可以通过向目前 top-performing VC firms 进行 investing 而获得“alpha”（market-beating returns）。一个 VC fund 的 rate of return 中，近 75% 可以由其最近 2 个 funds 的 performance 来 explained（[Cochrane 2001](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-cochrane)）。

-   排名最前的几家 VC firms 有着尤其惊人的 track records。例如，在 2004-2014 这十年间，虽然只有 14% 的 venture capitalists 曾 participated in 一次 billion-dollar exit，但 Sequoia、Greylock 和 NEA 各自都 participated in 了 8 次（[CB Insights 2013](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-cb2013)）。VC funds 的 top decile 的 median return 为 200%（[Janeway 2010](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-janeway)）。把这一点与前一点结合起来：通过向最近两个 funds 都是 top-decile 的人进行 investing，你应该能够 predictably get 150% 的 annualized return。[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:1)

-   Y Combinator 在其 fund 的 startups 上获得了令人难以置信的 deal。截至 2015，他们已 invested $120k in each of 940 companies ([Altman 2015](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-ycstats), [Altman 2014](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-ycterms)), for a total of about $120m。作为回报，他们获得了这些 companies 的 7%，这些 companies 目前的 valued in total 为 \$65 billion. Assuming that YC got diluted by 3x, they’ve turned their initial $120m into $1.5b already，即 >10x return。（这很可能大大 underestimate 了他们的 eventual return，因为最近那些规模较大的 batches——大部分 money 都投到了那里——仍有很大的 growing 空间；到目前为止，最大的 winners 大多来自 2010 或更早的年份（[CB Insights 2015](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-cb2015)）。）

-   看来，你甚至可能通过 investing 于 [TechCrunch“best startup”奖](https://www.benkuhn.net/late)的 nominees 来获得 alpha，而这些 nominees 是由……TechCrunch 读者投票选出的。

很难获得关于 fund returns 的真正可靠的 data，所以这些 data points 难免带有一些 speculative 的成分。但我认为，它们足以提供有力的 evidence，说明 venture capital 的 market 正在发生一些奇怪的事情——也就是说，(a) 如果你能够以看似 market rate 的条件 invest 于任意一家自己选择的 startup，你就能获得大量 free money；而且 (b) 这个 market 并没有“clearing”——capital 的 supply 远大于对它的 demand。

## 这令人困惑！

如果你相信 efficient market hypothesis，这听起来可能相当奇怪。你通常使用的 models 会 predict 这样的情况：

-   如果一家 startup 的 deal 好得如此显而易见，连普通 TechCrunch 读者都知道，那么它应该能够让 VCs 相互 bid，并 increase 自己的 valuation，直到它不再是一个显而易见的 win。

-   如果一家 VC firm 看起来显然将会 beat the market，那么 investor interest 就会蜂拥而至，它也会 increase 自己的 fund 的 size，直到它的 return 降至 market rates。（人们总说 hedge funds 就是这样做的。）

-   如果 Y Combinator 的 seed investments 在 on average 看来十拿九稳，那么要么 (a) 它的 investees 会要求更好的 terms，要么 (b) YC 会 accept 更多 companies，直到情况不再如此。

为什么这里没有发生这种情况？

## 有哪些解释？

我猜解释分为两个部分。

首先，_startups 可能是 predictably undervalued 的，因为相比于自己的 valuation，它们更在意 optimizing 自己的 chance of success。_这是因为 startups 受到 [power-law dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_law) 的驱动——一个 space 中排名最前的几个“winners”很可能与其余所有人加起来一样 valuable——所以 increasing 自己成为 winner 的 chance 比其他任何事情都重要得多。

下面列出了一些 startups 会为之 sacrifice 自己的 valuation 的事情（并不完整）：

-   **Minimizing distraction，即尽量减少分心。** Fundraising 是 [excruciatingly distracting](http://www.paulgraham.com/fundraising.html) 的。加入更多 players（以发起 bidding war 来 bid up valuation）会让情况糟糕 10x。我见过一些 startups 将自己的 valuation cut 了 1.5-2x，只为从已有的、low-maintenance investors 那里 raise，而不必四处比较并 manage 一场 bidding war。

-   **Getting good investors，即获得好的投资者。** 最好的 investors 确实会通过给出 good advice、介绍你认识合适的人等等，add 大量 value。这一点起初真的让我觉得很不直观——一个人发来的一封介绍邮件，怎么可能真的值那么多？但 Wave 一直都在经历这种效应：与某位 regulator/bank director/credit card processor 的一次面对面会谈，就能为我们省下数周或数月的 setup time。这就解释了，为什么像 [Ron Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Conway) 这样的 investors 值得你为之放弃相当多的 money。

-   **Minimizing risk of a down round，即尽量降低下一轮估值下降的风险。** Down rounds（以 below 上一 round 的 valuation 的 valuation 进行 raising）会让 startups 陷入 disproportionately bad position：它们会大幅 dilute common stock，伤害员工士气，而且通常意味着从糟糕的 adversarial investors 那里 raising。这一点如此重要，以至于 Sam Altman [称它为“fundraising strategy 的 prime directive”](https://blog.samaltman.com/fundraising-mistakes-founder-make)：

    > 一次 down round 的坏处怎么强调都不为过；事实上，这种威胁正是当有人给你 super high price 时不应接受它的最佳理由。如果你以这样的 price 来 raise，一切都必须进展得完美无缺，你的下一 round 才能是 up 的。

但即使我们能够解释为什么一些 startups 是 predictably undervalued 的，也不足以解释为什么一些 VCs 会 predictably earn excess returns。为什么最好的 venture capitalists 不 expand 自己的 funds，直到其 average return 不再是 market-beating 的？这里有一种 theory：

-   既然 startups 并不那么在意 valuation，VCs 就必须通过其他方式让 startups 希望它们来 invest：例如，good advice、connections，或是不让 founders 太过 distracting。

-   这些东西真的很难 evaluate，所以大多都被归结为一个 venture capitalist 的“brand”有多好。Startups 根据 (a) partners 有多出名，以及 (b) 它们曾 invested in 的其他 startups 有多 successful，来 judge 一家 VC 的 brand。[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:2)

-   **这意味着，好的 venture capital brand 无法 scale。** 如果一个好的 VC 让自己的 fund 变大，就会 decreases 其 companies 和 partners 的 average quality，从而 lowers 其 social capital，并使它们更难 compete for 最好的 deals。[3](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:3)

Y Combinator 是唯一一家可能已经解决 scaling problem 的 organization。我猜，它们是通过完全 monopolizing 自己在 fundraising pipeline 中所处的 stage 来“解决”这个问题的。如果你正在 raising 一轮 Series A，就有许多同样超级出名的 investors 可供选择，因此，如果其中一家 dilutes 自己的 brand，你可以去找别人。与此同时，如果你在寻找 accelerator，你的第二选择却[连“字面上”和“文雅地”都分不清](https://web.archive.org/web/20180224064744/https://angelpad.org/)。[4](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:4)

## 我的 free money 在哪里？

通常，当一个 asset class 被 inefficiently priced 时，你可以 buy 它并赚取 free money。但在这里，你不能这样做。你无法 invest 于最好的 startups，因为它们已经被最好的 VCs 抢先拿下；而你也无法 invest 于最好的 VCs，因为它们无法在不 diluting 自己的 brand 的情况下 increase 自己的 fund size。用 Yudkowskian terms 来说（[Yudkowsky 2017](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-yudkowsky)），尽管这个 market 是 _inefficient_ 的，它却不是 _exploitable_ 的（除非你是一家 top-decile VC firm）。[5](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:5) 真是苦了你了！

## 原文

Here are some facts about venture capital that might surprise you if you’ve learned some economics:

-   VC firms’ returns tend to persist over time ([Kaplan and Schoar 2004](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-kaplan)). In other words, as an investor, you could have “alpha” (market-beating returns) by investing with the currently top-performing VC firms. Almost 75% of a VC fund’s rate of return is explained by the performance of their last 2 funds ([Cochrane 2001](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-cochrane)).

-   The top few VC firms have particularly crazy track records. For instance, in the 2004-2014 decade, while only 14% of venture capitalists participated in a single billion-dollar exit, Sequoia, Greylock and NEA participated in 8 each ([CB Insights 2013](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-cb2013)). The top decile of VC funds has a median return of 200% ([Janeway 2010](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-janeway)). Combine this with the previous point: you should be able to predictably get a 150% annualized return by investing with someone whose last two funds were top-decile.[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:1)

-   Y Combinator gets an incredible deal on the startups they fund. As of 2015, they’d invested $120k in each of 940 companies ([Altman 2015](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-ycstats), [Altman 2014](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-ycterms)), for a total of about $120m. In return, they got 7% of these companies, which are now valued in total at $65 billion. Assuming that YC got diluted by 3x, they’ve turned their initial $120m into $1.5b already, a >10x return. (This is probably a huge underestimate of their eventual return, because the larger recent batches, where most of the money went, still have a lot of growing to do—the biggest winners so far are mostly from 2010 or earlier ([CB Insights 2015](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-cb2015)).)

-   It looks like you might even get alpha by investing in the nominees of the [TechCrunch “best startup” award](https://www.benkuhn.net/late), whose nominees are chosen by… the vote of TechCrunch readers.

It’s hard to get actual hard data on fund returns, so these data points are by necessity somewhat speculative. But I think they’re enough to provide strong evidence that something weird is going on in the market for venture capital—that (a) if you were able to invest in any startup of your choice, at what seems like the market rate, you’d be able to get substantial free money; and (b) the market isn’t “clearing”—the supply of capital is much bigger than the demand for it.

## This is confusing!

If you believe in the efficient market hypothesis, this probably sounds pretty weird. Your normal models would predict things like:

-   If a startup is such an obviously good deal that the average TechCrunch reader knows it, they should be able to bid VCs against each other and increase their valuation until it’s no longer an obvious win.

-   If a VC firm looks like it’s obviously going to beat the market, they’ll get swamped with investor interest and increase the size of their fund until their return goes down to market rates. (This is what people always claim hedge funds do.)

-   If Y Combinator’s seed investments look like a slam dunk on average, either (a) their investees would demand better terms, or (b) YC would accept more companies, until it stops looking that way.

Why doesn’t that happen here?

## Some explanations?

My guess at the explanation has two parts.

First, _startups can be predictably undervalued because they care more about optimizing their chance of success than their valuation._ That’s because startups are driven by [power-law dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_law)—the top couple “winners” in a space are likely to be as valuable as everyone else combined—so increasing your chance of being a winner is much more important than anything else.

Here’s an (incomplete) list of things that startups sacrifice their valuation for:

-   **Minimizing distraction.** Fundraising is [excruciatingly distracting](http://www.paulgraham.com/fundraising.html). Adding more players (to start a bidding war to bid up the valuation) makes it 10x worse. I’ve seen startups cut their valuation by 1.5-2x in order to raise from existing, low-maintenance investors instead of having to shop around and manage a bidding war.

-   **Getting good investors.** The best investors really do add an enormous amount of value by giving good advice, introducing you to the right people, and so on. This was really unintuitive to me at first—how could a single intro email from a single person really be worth that much? But Wave has this effect all the time, where a single face to face meeting with a certain regulator/bank director/credit card processor saves us weeks or months of setup time. Which explains why investors like [Ron Conway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Conway) would be worth leaving quite a lot of money on the table for.

-   **Minimizing risk of a down round.** Down rounds (raising at a valuation below the valuation of your last round) put startups in a disproportionately bad position: they dilute common stock by a lot, hurt employees’ morale, and generally involve raising from crappy adversarial investors. This is important enough that Sam Altman [calls it “the prime directive of fundraising strategy”](https://blog.samaltman.com/fundraising-mistakes-founder-make):

    > The badness of a down round is difficult to overstate; in fact, the threat of that is the best reason not to take a super high price when you’re offered one. If you raise at such a price, everything has to go perfectly in order for your next round to be an up one.

But even if we can explain why some startups are predictably undervalued, that’s not sufficient to explain why some VCs predictably earn excess returns. Why don’t the best venture capitalists expand their funds until their average return isn’t market-beating? Here’s one theory:

-   Since startups don’t care that much about valuation, VCs have to get startups to want them to invest in other ways: for instance, good advice, connections, or not distracting the founders very much.

-   These things are really hard to evaluate, so they mostly get rolled up into how good of a “brand” a venture capitalist has. Startups judge a VC’s brand by (a) how famous the partners are, and (b) how successful are the other startups they invested in.[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:2)

-   **That means a good venture capital brand doesn’t scale.** If a good VC makes their fund bigger, it decreases the average quality of their companies and partners, which lowers their social capital and makes it harder for them to compete for the top deals.[3](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:3)

Y Combinator is the only organization to have plausibly solved the scaling problem. My guess is that they “solved” it by completely monopolizing their stage of the fundraising pipeline. If you’re raising a Series A there are lots of equally super-famous investors to pick from, so if one of them dilutes their brand you can go to someone else. Meanwhile, if you’re looking for an accelerator, your second best choice [can’t tell “literally” from “literately”](https://web.archive.org/web/20180224064744/https://angelpad.org/).[4](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:4)

## Where’s my free money?

Normally, when an asset class is inefficiently priced, you can buy it and make free money. But in this case, you can’t. You can’t invest in the best startups because they’re already snapped up by the best VCs, and you can’t invest in the best VCs because they can’t increase their fund size without diluting their brand. In Yudkowskian terms ([Yudkowsky 2017](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#source-yudkowsky)), even though the market is _inefficient_, it’s not _exploitable_ (unless you’re a top-decile VC firm).[5](https://www.benkuhn.net/vc-emh/#fn:5) Too bad for you!
