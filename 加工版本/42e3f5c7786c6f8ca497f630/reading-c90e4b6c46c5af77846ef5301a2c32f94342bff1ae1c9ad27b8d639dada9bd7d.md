## 正文

[![](https://michaelnotebook.com/assets/home.png)](https://michaelnotebook.com/index.html)

_关于 Las Vegas 的随手笔记。这是我在 2018 年匆匆写给一位朋友的文字。_ _2022 年把它们放在这里，略有删节，这样分享起来比每次转发 WhatsApp 的_ _thread 更方便。_

去 Vegas 待了一个星期，实在非同寻常——绝对是我经历过的最有意思的 urban experiences 之一，或许就是最有意思的一次。

说几件事，主要围绕 Strip：

-   这里几乎没有 commons；一切都是 privatized。Security、use of force，等等等等。不付钱就连水也喝不到（这可是在 US 最炎热、最干燥的城市之一），甚至连个座位也没有。不可思议。

-   完全是 surveillance states。

-   这里的人是 middle class，而不是 uber-wealthy。尽管如此，人们的行为却仿佛自己是 uber-wealthy，在 designer clothes、食物等东西上花费数千。那些商店令人难以置信。Apple store 在其中算是_垫底_的；跟其他许多商店相比，它显得黯淡而且颇为破旧。

-   这里卖的究竟是什么？起初，我以为关键是金钱和性。但我越来越觉得是 glamour。

具体来说，Strip 围绕着一种非常明确的观念：什么才是 good life。这是一种以 revealed preference 来理解 utopia 的观念。

旅途中有一次，我开始写笔记，手能写多快就写多快，随后意识到自己能写出几十万字。在 Strip 的每一分钟，都有新鲜事物接连轰炸而来，远远超出我的消化能力。绝对令人惊叹的地方！

再说几个 observations：

-   所有 casinos 都由极少数 companies 所 owned。其 net effect 就像是 3 或 4 个 absolute dictatorships 之间的战争，每一个都以 run autocratically 的方式运作，对内部有着惊人的 control。我不太了解这里的 supply chain：这些餐厅等场所究竟有多大程度是它们 own 的？并不全是：brand name restaurants 和 shops（Spago、Versace _等_）可以充当 differentiators。

-   这些 casinos 作为 casinos，彼此只有 minor differences。那些 shows 似乎充当了主要的 differentiators（也许餐厅也是？）我完全不理解这背后的 economics。是 Caesars 付钱请 Celine Dion 来 perform 吗？还是她付钱给他们？这到底是怎么运作的？

-   Cirque du Soleil Mystere 开场的 20 分钟，是世间最辉煌的事物之一。有一刻，我才意识到泪水正顺着脸流下来，自己完全没有察觉。起了一身鸡皮疙瘩。show 后面的部分也很好，有时非常好。（恐怕我有点担心自己把它夸得太过了；这只是未经筛选的个人感受。）

-   有一段很棒的 Werner Herzog 访谈，里面有人问他对 lowbrow pop culture——Anna Nicole Smith、Wrestlemania 等——怎么看，他带着那种激情澎湃、执着强烈的劲头回答：“你_一定_不能移开目光。_这_就是正向我们扑来的东西。你_绝不能移开目光_。”这句话一直在我脑海里回响。不过对我来说，更像是我根本无法移开目光，实在太有意思了。

-   Penn and Teller 真的很棒。我还看了另一场 magic show（David Copperfield），两者的反差巨大。Penn and Teller 似乎正享受着人生中最快乐的时光。Copperfield 看上去却很无聊。关于 Penn and Teller，有一件有意思的事：Penn 声称，他们 routine 中几乎每一个 element 都在不断 changing。他们极其迅速地 turn things over，以各种方式 experiment，又 return to old ideas。这是看得出来的：感觉特别新鲜，而且就是纯粹好玩。

-   在 SF，我大概比 95% 的人都高[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/vegas/index.html#fn1)。在 LV，这个比例更接近 99%。我试着 estimate 一下，结果竟然觉得无聊了——前 77 个人全都比我矮（或者至少不比我高），于是我放弃了。我完全不知道该如何解释这一点——我有几种 theories，但没有一种是我相信的。

-   一个有趣的巧合：我特意独自前往，而且很庆幸自己这么做了（我进行了许多思考）。但我在一条 tweet-replied 中向 \[一位朋友\] 提到了这件事，\[共同的朋友\] 看到了。她正和家人一起游玩，于是发消息约我吃早午餐，再一起逛逛 Vegas。那几个小时很有意思，也让我对 Vegas 的 social experience 有了许多想法。（以前我也曾多次和别人一起去那里，但从未如此有意识地留意这些。）这里确实是 designed 来 elicit 朋友们为彼此作出的 performances 的。

还有好多想法！非常、非常值得一去。而且也就是纯粹好玩，先别管所有这些 analysis！

## 原文

[![](https://michaelnotebook.com/assets/home.png)](https://michaelnotebook.com/index.html)

_Quick notes on Las Vegas. I wrote them very quickly in text for a friend in 2018._ _Putting them here in 2022, lightly abridged, as a better way of sharing them than forwarding the WhatsApp_ _thread each time._

Went to Vegas for a week, which was really remarkable – by far one of the most interesting urban experiences I've ever had, perhaps the most interesting.

A few things, concentrating on the Strip:

-   There is almost no commons; everything is privatized. Security, use of force, etc etc. You can't get water without paying for it (in one of the hottest, dryest cities in the US), or even a seat. Amazing.

-   Complete surveillance states.

-   It's the middle class, not the uber-wealthy. Despite that, people behave as though they're uber-wealthy, spending thousands on designer clothes, food etc. The stores are incredible. The Apple store is at the _bottom_ of the pack; it seems drab and rather rundown, compared to many of the others.

-   What's being sold? At first, I thought money and sex were the keys. But increasingly I think it's glamour.

In particular, the Strip is about some very definite notion of what the good life is. It's a kind of revealed preference notion of utopia.

I started writing notes at one point on my trip, as fast as my hand could move, and realized I could write hundreds of thousands of words. Every minute on the Strip was just a bombardment with novelty, beyond my ability to process. Absolutely amazing place!

A few more observations:

-   All the casinos are owned by a tiny handful of companies. The net effect is of a war between 3 or 4 absolute dictatorships, each run autocratically and with remarkable control within. I don't understand the supply chain well: to what extent do they own the restaurants etc? Not entirely: brand name restaurants and shops (Spago, Versace _etc_) can act as differentiators.

-   The casinos qua casinos have only minor differences. The shows seem to act as the main differentiators (maybe the restaurants too?) I don't understand the economics at all around that. Does Caesars pay Celine Dion to perform? Does she pay them? How does it work out?

-   The first 20 minutes of Cirque du Soleil Mystere are one of the glories of the world. I realized at one point tears were running down my face, totally unconscious. Goosebumps. The later bits of the show are good too, sometimes very good. (I worry I've oversold it, I'm afraid; this is just unfiltered opinion.)

-   There's a great interview with Werner Herzog where he is asked about lowbrow pop culture - Anna Nicole Smith, Wrestlemania etc - and he replies with all his passionate driven intensity: "you _must_ not avert your eyes. _This_ is what is coming at us. You _must not avert your eyes_." That line kept running through my head the whole time. Although in my case it was more like I could not avert my eyes, it was so interesting.

-   Penn and Teller were really great. I saw one other magic show (David Copperfield), and the contrast was huge. Penn and Teller seemed to be having the time of their lives. Copperfield looked bored. An interesting thing about Penn and Teller: Penn claimed that almost every element in their routine is changing all the time. They turn things over extremely rapidly, experiment in all sorts of ways, return to old ideas. It shows: it felt extremely fresh and just plain fun.

-   In SF I'm taller than perhaps 95% of people[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/vegas/index.html#fn1). In LV it was more like 99%. I tried to estimate it, and actually got bored – the first 77 people were all shorter (or at least no taller) than me, and I gave up. I have no idea how to account for this – I have several theories, and none I believe.

-   Funny coincidence: I deliberately went on my own, and am glad I did (I did a lot of reflection). But I tweet-replied about it to \[a friend\], and \[mutual friend\] saw it. She was visiting with her family, and pinged me to have brunch and walk around Vegas. It was a very interesting few hours, and left me with a lot of thoughts about the social experience of Vegas. (I've been there socially many times before, but not nearly as self-consciously.) It's really designed to elicit performances from friends for each other.

Lots more thoughts! Very, very worth visiting. And just plain fun, too, never mind all this analysis!
