# Nintil - Scott Sumner's amusing philosophy

---

## 正文

他在[这里](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2016/05/youre_not_speci.html)和[这里](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2016/05/why_bryan_capla_1.html)发文。里面有许多奇怪的东西。

(I)

> 并不存在什么 \_objective \_reason，能让你或我的 political views 被视为 superior to 其他任何人的 political views。

不存在 objective reason。就是这样。Reasons 总是由某个人持有，因此是 subjective。我写这篇文章的 reason 是我自己的，而不是别人的。

Sumner 在那里似乎想要的是“某种 forces someone to believe in something 的东西”。但并不存在这样的东西。Arguments 可以或多或少地具有 persuasive 的性质，但据我所知，并不存在 universally working arguments，因为每个 argument 都 relies on premises，而不同人的 premises 并不相同。这并不是说 premises 不能 change，它们可以。

但按照这个 standard，也不存在什么 objective reason，能使 Science 比 wizardry works better。可以说，这个 standard 太高了。

> 不过，我并没有 objective reason 去 assume，仅仅因为我 inhabit Scott Sumner 的 body，我的 ideas 就有什么 special 之处。我只是在做自己的工作。

这与他的下一篇文章 contradicts

> 我最近对 personal identity 的 denial 引起了一些 confusion。大多数人 believe，存在一个 possesses a brain 的“你”，以及一个 possesses a body 的“你”。我不这样认为；我 believe，只有 brain 和 body，并没有“你”。这种 confusion 会出现在关于 free will 的 discussions 中。

如果没有“你”，前一句中的“我”是在 referring to 什么？这个 inhabits 一个 body 的 Scott Sumner 究竟是谁？

此外，这场 debate（personal identity）与 free will，prima facie，并没有多大关系。无论有没有它，都可以有 self。

> 虽然我倾向于 agree，small government 是 best，但我也知道，我的 opinion 并没有什么 special 之处。我并不比许多持有不同 views 的 economists 更聪明。**决定未来的 Americans 应该被 predisposed to prefer 我关于 economics 的 views，还是 Krugman 的 views，并不是我的工作**。

这也与他前面的段落 contradictory：

> 那么，我为什么要在 blogosphere 中 aggressively promote 我的 views？因为那是我的工作。我是一个名为“economics”的庞大 intellectual edifice 中的小工蚁。我的工作就是 study 这些 issues，并 report 我 believe 为 true 的东西。

Sumner 在写博客（并做着自己的工作）这一事实本身，确实会 marginally predispose 未来的 Americans 去 prefer 他的 economic views。如果没有 Sumner，就不会有这种 effect。如果他是个糟糕的作者，他的文章可能使他的 position 更不受尊重，但事实并非如此。

> 因此，在 deciding 哪些 groups 应该被 allowed to immigrate to the US 时，我不应该 take political inclinations into account。

但他确实这样做了！说 government 有 right 去 regulate inmigration，_就是_一种 political inclination。Sumner 是根据什么 basis 来 decide 哪些 groups 应该被 allowed 进入 US 的？“voters 说什么就是什么”？为什么要这样？因为 voters 是这么说的？

> 我的确明白，personal identity 的 illusion 会 creates 一种相关的 illusion，让我们觉得自己是 special，我们那些珍贵的 views 也是 special。但我们不是，它们也不是。

我们的 views，_对我们而言_，是 special。正如前面提到的，Reasons 是 subjective 且 individual。在 deciding 一个 issue 时，我们在 considered 相关 evidence 和 arguments 之后，会对它作出 judge。[如果在我们看来 P 是 right，那么我们 believing P 是 right 就是 justified。](http://www.iep.utm.edu/phen-con/)  [这并不会使 P 成为 true](http://www.oxfordscholarship.com.sci-hub.cc/view/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199899494.001.0001/acprof-9780199899494-chapter-15)。P 的 truth 并不 depend on 你对 P 的看法（除非 P 是一个关于你自己的 subjective fact）。因此，Sumner 可以说，inmigration 确实有这种 effect，而其他人可以说，inmigration 有那种 effect，双方 believing 各自的说法都可以是 justified。这样，Debates 就可以帮助 positions 更加接近，并 examine shared 和 unshared assumptions。

看来，他是在 advocating 我们采纳 consensus view。而[这无疑是一种明智的 heuristic](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/10/on-the-express-acceptance-and-rejection-of-beliefs/)。但正如他随后指出的，

> 总是将我们的 beliefs 按照 market consensus 进行 marking，存在一个可能的 flaw。有人指出，如果所有人都 believed，EMH 使 consistently beat the market 成为不可能，那么就不会有人进行使 EMH 成为 true 所必需的 research。因此，我们需要许多 individual actors 尝试 figure out，stocks 应该在什么水平上被 valued，并形成 _independent_ 于当前 market price 的 opinions。否则，EMH 就不会 work。但他们也应该足够明智，知道自己并没有什么 special，因此应该 advise 自己的母亲 invest in index funds，而不是自己的 hedge fund。这就是我所说的意识到自己并没有什么 special。你只是一个让 EMH 更加 true 的工蚁。

这很奇怪。我不 operate 一个 hedge fund 是有 reason 的，那就是我不认为自己会比 market do better。如果我 believed 自己能够 beat the market，我就会 start 一个 hedge fund，并在 taken into account 较高的 likelihood of failure 和母亲的 low risk preference 之后，advise 她 invest in 它。（也就是说，我不太可能 recommend 她 invest in 我的 hedge fund。但对于更 risk-friendly 的 investors，我当然会！）

EMH 是 markets 的一种 [emergent](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/10/04/the-follies-of-holism-a-reductionist-critique/) property，而不是 physical law。如果你在通读 balance sheets 时 detect 一个非常好的 opportunity，你可以想：“如果它真的那么好，肯定早就有别人想到过了”，然后放弃它。但有时候，你可以认为自己是第一个想到的人，这个 idea 很好，而你因为 realising 这一点而是 special。当然，并不是每个人的 guesses 都会是 right。这就是为什么你还应该将其他人的 judgement 与自己的 judgement 进行 weight：EMH 的一种 weak form 依然是 true。

请注意，如果 very-strong EMH 是 true，entrepreneurship 也会遇到同样的 problem（如果一个 business plan 那么好，为什么之前没有人想到过？）。

> 如果我们被任命为 dictator，就应该 implement 关于 optimal policy 的 consensus view，而不是我们自己的 view。人们很难接受这一点，因为它 implies 关于什么是 true 的 two levels of belief。一个是从我们 mind 内部出发的 view，另一个是从太空中 20,000 miles 之外出发的 view；在那里，我看到，并没有 objective reason 去 favor 我的 view 而不是 Krugman 的 view。

还是那句话，根本不存在 objective reasons。他要求的是某种 in principle 无法提供的东西。但确实有一个 reason 可以 favour 他的 view 而不是 Krugman 的 view：那是\_他的。\_ 自己的 beliefs 可以 ground justification。其他的 beliefs 则不是。如果在我看来 X 是 true，而我又没有 reasons 去 believe X 不是 true，那么我就 ought to believe X。事实上，Sumner 之所以对 specialness、identity 以及其他事情作出那些说法，就是因为在他看来事情就是那样。请注意，他在 espousing 自己的 views 时，正在 going against the consensus（有多少人，甚至有多少 philosophers，会说不存在 self，或者我们不是这里所 defended 的意义上的 special，或者你应该以他所说的方式 follow the consensus？），从而 contradicting 这些 views。

> 在 Middle Ages，人们 believed 自己的 religion 是唯一的 true faith，并利用 public policy，试图让 society 的大多数人 adhere to 这一种 faith。他们无法理解从 20,000 miles 之外出发的 view 这个 concept。在 enlightenment 时期（Copernicus 之后），philosophers 意识到，我们并没有 privileged position 来 decide 唯一的 true faith，而 public policy 不应该 favor 任何一种 religion。

不，他们并没有。不存在从 20,000 miles 之外出发的 view。只有 individual views。Public policy 不 favour 任何一种 religion，这的确没错。但它确实 favour 许多其他东西（law 所 upholds 的一切，而其中并非所有东西过去或现在都有 majoritarian approval；要求它们全都如此，也不是个好主意）

> 我也 believe，public policy 不应该试图将我们的 population reshape 得更加符合 Republican 或 Democratic 的路线，因为我们不知道哪个 party 的 views 是 correct。因此，immigration policy 应该是 neutral，只有那些存在 broad consensus、认为它们是 desirable 或 undesirable 的 characteristics 除外。（比如 Nazi views，或 totalitarian communist views，它们显然是 wrong。）

“neutral inmigration policy”究竟是什么意思？他真正的意思是“consensus inmigration policy”。谁的 Consensus？Voters？World citizenry？Economists？Philosophers？对于这些 groups 中的任何一个，你都可能得到不同的 result。那么，要 choose 哪一种 consensus 是 relevant，你又 appeal 什么？由哪一种 consensus 来 decide，究竟哪一种 consensus 的 view 可以得到 implemented？因为，如果我们竟然主张一个 polity 中的每一项 majoritarian decision 都 ought to be implemented，那么我们就是在 endorsing [早已被 refuted 的 political authority 的 views](https://www.amazon.com/Problem-Political-Authority-Examination-Coerce/dp/1137281650)。不过，看看 Sumner 为它们 arguing，倒会很有意思。

> 我并不 believe，Fed 应该把 interest rates set 在 Janet Yellen 认为 appropriate 的水平，或 John Taylor 认为 appropriate 的水平，或我认为 appropriate 的水平。相反，interest rates 应该被 set 在 market 认为最有可能 lead to good macroeconomic outcomes 的水平。Yellen 和 Taylor（还有我？）在 figuring out“good macroeconomic outcomes”是什么意思这件事上，确实有重要的 role to play。

如果你在这里放入他自己的名字，会怎样？“我并不 believe，Fed 应该把 rates set 在 Scott Sumner 认为 appropriate 的水平”。Scott Sumner 认为 using the market 是 appropriate。因此，（显然）他并不 believe in using markets（！）。

(II)

Sumner 在这里究竟想说什么？读过[这条](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2016/05/why_bryan_capla_1.html#357392)评论后，就更清楚了。

> 还是那句话，你在 confusing two levels of belief。回想一下我关于 stock investor 的 analogy。在一个 level 上，他认为自己的 hedge fund 是 best，但在一个 higher level 上，他认为自己的母亲应该 buy index funds。在一个 level 上，我可能认为 3.5% fed funds rate 是 best，但在另一个 level 上，我会 defer to market 关于 interest rates 应该处于什么水平的 view。我只是那个 market 中的一个人。

这说得通吗？让我们 suppose，Sumner 是世界上唯一的人。那么，无论他怎么想，那都是 consensus，所以这两个 levels 会 coincide。

但想象一下，Sumner 和我是世界上仅有的两个人，我们正在 arguing 他是否应该成为我的 slave。Assume 我是那种会 enslave 别人的人（其实我人很好，我保证！）。在这种 situation 下，他不会想成为我的 slave，而我会希望他成为我的 slave。这个 issue 上并不存在 consensus。我们 follow 什么 policy？按照他的 account，什么 policy 都不 follow。但并不存在“什么都不”。Either 你成为我的 slave，or 你不成为我的 slave。实际会发生的是，我会考虑将我的 policy imposing 于你，而你会试图将你的 policy impose 于我（Resisting 被 enslaved）（双方都会认为，我们各自的 policies 都是 justified）。这样做，恰恰就是我的 theory 所 predicts 的！

不过，Sumner 的 meta-rule 并不是“follow the consensus”。它更像是“其他人应该用 consensus 来 guide 他们的 decisions，而我应该用我自己的 beliefs 来 guide 我的 decisions”。

(III)

那么，为什么不把你自己的 beliefs set 成 consensus 的 beliefs 呢？因为这会把我们带向他所承认的 paradox。在一个人人都是旅行代理人的国家，没有人真正去旅行。

对我来说，也可能对大多数人（也就是 consensus！）来说，显而易见的 solution 是拥有一套 beliefs，并把“consensus 通常是 right”用作 informs 我的 actions 的 heuristic。Sumner 可以进入 third order meta，拥有一个认为 P is the case 的 1st order belief，一个认为“如果 consensus 说 P is the case，那么我就应该把 1st order belief set 为 P，除非我自己仔细 assess 它”的 second order belief，以及一个认为“Follow the consensus 是一条往往有效的 rule”的 third order  belief，以此 justify 那个 second order belief。请注意，我们最后总会落到一个因为是 commonsensical 而被 accepted 的 belief 上。

但 Sumner 的 solution 把我们引向了奇怪的 paradoxes：

我：Scott，听说你要开办一家 hedge fund。

Scott：是啊，我有几个不错的 investing ideas。

我：太好了！你告诉朋友们了吗？我是说，你那些可能喜欢 investing 的 investor 朋友。

Scott：嗯，没有。我建议他们 invest in 一只 ETF。

我：为什么呢？是 invest in 一只 ETF 更好，还是 invest in 你的 hedge fund 更好？

Scott：一只 ETF，这是 EMH 所 implied 的。

我：可这样一来，你大概会 lose money。你 expect 自己的 hedge fund 会 do better than the market，对吧？

Scott：对。

我：但如果你 expect 自己的 hedge fund 会 beat the market，为什么不告诉他们呢？

Scott：我并不 special，人们应该 follow the consensus。

我：那你自己为什么不 follow the consensus 呢？

Scott：我是一只工蚁，我知道，如果我这样做，EMH 就会是 true（而且 market 会因此 work better，这是一件好事）。

我：所以你的 hedge fund 更像是一项 public service？你 expect 自己 lose money，以便让 market 变得更加 efficient？

Scott：嗯，你知道……我们知道，如果人们都像我这样 behave individually，事情就会运转得很好。所以，我可以拥有 personal beliefs 并 act on 它们，但与此同时，我会给别人提供 based on consensus 的建议，以避免 propagating 我可能犯下的 mistakes。

我：但你知道，如果你直接选择 ETF，忘掉自己的 hedge fund，system 的运转基本上也不会有什么不同，对吧？

Scott：我是一个 utilitarian，我不认为自己的 preferences 是 special 的，我的 act 方式是为了让所有人都受益。

我：哦，好吧。这里还有另一种 policy 供你考虑：告诉别人，让他们 invest in 你的 hedge fund。只拥有一个 first order belief。告诉他们，你为什么认为这是个好主意，以及它为什么可能是个坏主意。向他们讲讲 EMH。然后，让他们自己决定。但我觉得，你这样做并不真的是出于慈善。当你在 economics 中做 research 时，你认为自己所做的事情包含某些 truth。

Scott：我不知道那样是否会 work best。你说的就像是在 betting against a prediction market。

我：没错，确实如此！在某些情况下，[prediction markets 是 beatable 的](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2660628)。尤其当我们谈论的是你想要 invest in 的 obscure assets，比如你正计划 invest in 的那些。究竟有多少人真正考虑过你现在正在考虑的事情？

(IV)

> Suppose 你有一个 belief——比如，认为正在下雨的 belief。再 suppose 你开始 reflect explicitly 自己的 belief 是否 constitutes knowledge。你可能得出什么 conclusion？你必须要么 conclude 自己 know 正在下雨，要么对自己是否 know 这一点 withhold judgment，要么 conclude 自己并不 know 这一点。如果你 conclude 自己并不 know 这一点，那么，你那个认为正在下雨的 first-order belief，与关于那个 first-order belief 的 second-order belief 之间，似乎就会存在某种 tension。如果你坚持自己的 first-order belief，同时又 denying 它 constitutes knowledge，那么，我认为，你就犯下了某种 irrationality。以下 fact 暗示了这一点：如果你用语言 express 自己的 attitudes，你就会说出一个 Moore-paradoxical sentence，大致如下：“正在下雨，但我不 know 是否正在下雨。”（[Huemer, 2011](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com.sci-hub.cc/doi/10.1111/j.1933-1592.2010.00388.x/abstract)）

Sumner 似乎处于类似的处境。他 believes 一些事情，却不 know 它们是否是 true。这是 nonsense。

他进一步说，process X（Science、public deliberation 等等……）会 delivers truth/good policy，尽管没有任何 individual participant 能够声称自己必定是 right。但一次又一次，Science 发生了变化，public policy 糟糕透顶，而人们指出了这一点，而且他们是 right 的。

不过，在当前这个例子中，情况似乎并非如此。Sumner 应该 default to the consensus，因为这样做有充分的 reasons：相较于世界上其他所有人的 judgement，要 trust 你自己的 reasoned judgement。

## 原文

He posts [here](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2016/05/youre_not_speci.html) and[here](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2016/05/why_bryan_capla_1.html). Lots of peculiar things.

(I)

> There's no \_objective \_reason to view your or my political views as being superior to those of any other person.

There is no objective reason. Period. Reasons are always held by a person, hence subjective. My reason to write this post is mine and no one else's.

What Sumner appears to want there is 'something that forces someone to believe in something'. But there are no such things. Arguments can be more or less persuasive, but as far as I know, there are no universally working arguments, because every argument relies on premises, and premises differ among people. This is not to say premises can't change, they can.

But by this standard, there is no objective reason in virtue of which Science works better than wizardry. Argueably, it is too high of a standard.

> Nonetheless, there is no objective reason for me to assume that just because I inhabit Scott Sumner's body, my ideas are somehow special. I just do my job.

This contradicts his next post

> There was some confusion about my recent denial of personal identity. Most people believe there is a "you" that possesses a brain, and a "you" that possesses a body. I don't, I believe there's merely a brain and a body, no "you". This confusion shows up in discussions of free will.

If there is no 'you', what is the 'I' in the previous sentence referring to? Who is this Scott Sumner that inhabits a body?

Additionally, this debate (personal identity) has little do do, prima facie, with free will. You can have a self with or without it.

> While I tend to agree that small government is best, I also know that there is nothing special about my opinion. I'm not smarter than lots of economists with different views. **It's not my job to decide whether future Americans should be predisposed to prefer my views on economics or Krugman's**.

This is also contradictory with his earlier paragraph:

> Then why do I aggressively promote my views in the blogosphere? Because that's my job. I am a small worker ant in a vast intellectual edifice called "economics." It's my job to study the issues and report what I believe to be true.

The mere fact that Sumner is blogging (and doing his job) does marginally predispose future Americans to prefer his economic views. If there were no Sumner, there would be no effect. If he were a terrible writer, his writings could make his position less respected, but this is not the case.

> Thus I should not take political inclinations into account when deciding on which groups should be allowed to immigrate to the US.

But he does! Saying that the government has a right to regulate inmigration _is_ a political inclination. On which basis does Sumner decide which groups should be allowed into the US? 'Whatever voters say'? And why that? Because voters say so?

> I do understand that the illusion of personal identity creates the related illusion that we ourselves are special, and that our precious views of things are special. But we aren't, and they aren't.

Our views of thing are special _to us_. Reasons, as mentioned earlier, are subjective and individual. When deciding on an issue, and after we have considered the relevant evidence and arguments, we judge it. [If it seems to us that P is right, we are then justified in believing that P is right.](http://www.iep.utm.edu/phen-con/)  [That does not make P true](http://www.oxfordscholarship.com.sci-hub.cc/view/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199899494.001.0001/acprof-9780199899494-chapter-15). The truth of P does not depend on what you think of P (except if P is a subjective fact about yourself). Sumner can then say that it is the case that inmigration has this effect, and others can say that inmigration has that effect, and both can be justified in believing that. Debates can then help bring positions closer and examine shared and unshared assumptions.

Seemingly, he is advocating us to take the consensus view. And [that is surely a wise heuristic](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/10/on-the-express-acceptance-and-rejection-of-beliefs/). But as he then notes,

> There is one possible flaw in always marking our beliefs to the market consensus. It's been pointed out that if everyone believed the EMH made it impossible to consistently beat the market, then no one would do the research necessary to make the EMH true. Thus we need lots of individual actors trying to figure out where stocks should be valued, and forming opinions _independent_ of the current market price. Otherwise the EMH does not work. But they should also be wise enough to know that they are nothing special, and hence they should advise their mother to invest in index funds, not their own hedge fund. That's what I mean by realizing that you are nothing special. You are merely a worker ant making the EMH more true.

This is weird. There's a reason why I don't operate a hedge fund, and it is that I don't think I would do better than the market. If I believed that I can beat the market myself, I would start a hedge fund, and I would advise my mother to invest in it once I've taken into account the high likelihood of failure and her low risk preference. (That is, it's unlikely that I would recommend her to invest in my hedge fund. But surely to more risk-friendly investors I would!)

The EMH is an [emergent](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/10/04/the-follies-of-holism-a-reductionist-critique/) property of markets, not a physical law. If you are reading through balance sheets and detect a very good opportunity, you can go and think "Surely someone else would have thought about this first if it were actually that good" and let it go. But sometimes, you can think that you are the first one, that the idea is good, and that you are special for realising that. Not everyone will be right about their guesses, of course. This is why you should also weight the judgement of the rest against your own: a weak form of EMH is still true.

Note that if the very-strong EMH were true, entrepreneurship would suffer from the same problem (If a business plan is that good, how is it that no one has thought of it before?).

> If we are made dictator, we should implement the consensus view of optimal policy, not our own. People have trouble with this, as it implies two levels of belief about what is true. The view from inside our mind, and the view from 20,000 miles out in space, where I see there is no objective reason to favor my view over Krugman's.

Again, no objective reasons at all. He's asking for something that cannot in principle be provided. But there is a reason to favour his view over Krugman's: that it is\_his.\_ Own beliefs can ground justification. Other beliefs aren't. If it seems to me that X is true, and I have no reasons to believe that X is not true, I ought to believe X. In fact, Sumner says what he says about specialness and identity and the rest because it seems that way to him. Note that he is going against the consensus (How many people or even philosophers say there is no self, or that we are not special in the sense defended here, or that you should follow the consensus in the way he says ) by espousing his views, thus contradicting them.

> In the Middle Ages, people believed their own religion was the one true faith, and used public policy to try to make most of society adhere to this one faith. They could not understand the concept of the view from 20,000 miles out. In the enlightenment (after Copernicus), philosophers realized that we have no privileged position to decide the one true faith, and that public policy should not favor any one religion.

No they didn't. There is no view from 20,000 miles out. Just individual views. Public policy does not favour any one religion, true indeed. But it does favour many other things (everything the law upholds, and not everything in that had or has majoritarian approval, nor it would be a good idea for it to be so)

> I also believe that public policy should not try to reshape our population more along Republican or Democratic lines, as we don't know which party's views are correct. Thus immigration policy should be neutral, except about characteristics for which there is a broad consensus that they are desirable or undesirable. (Say Nazi views, or totalitarian communist views, which are clearly wrong.)

What does a 'neutral inmigration policy' even mean?  He really means 'consensus inmigration policy'. Consensus of whom? Voters? World citizenry? Economists? Philosophers? For any of those groups, you may get a different result. And then, to choose which consensus is relevant, to what do you appeal? Which consensus does decide which consensus is the one that gets its view implemented? Because if we go as far as saying that every majoritarian decision in a polity ought to be implemented, we are endorsing [long refuted views of political authority](https://www.amazon.com/Problem-Political-Authority-Examination-Coerce/dp/1137281650). It'd be interesting to see Sumner arguing for them, though.

> I do not believe that the Fed should set interest rates where Janet Yellen thinks is appropriate, or where John Taylor thinks is appropriate, or where I think is appropriate. Rather interest rates should be set where the market thinks they are most likely to lead to good macroeconomic outcomes. Yellen and Taylor (and me?) do have an important role to play in figuring out what "good macroeconomic outcomes" means.

What happens if you include his own name there? "I don't believe that the Fed should set rates where Scott Sumner think is appropriate". Scott Sumner thinks using the market is appropriate. Hence, (apparently) he doesn't believe in using markets(!).

(II)

What is Sumner really saying here? It becomes clearer after reading [this](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2016/05/why_bryan_capla_1.html#357392) comment.

> Again, you are confusing two levels of belief. Recall my analogy with the stock investor. At one level he things his hedge fund is best, but at a higher level he thinks him mom should buy index funds. At one level I might think a 3.5% fed funds rate is best, but at another level I defer to the market view of where interest rates should be. I am merely one person in that market.

Does this make sense? Let us suppose Sumner is the only person in the world. Then, whatever he thinks is the consensus, so both levels coincide.

But imagine Sumner and I are the only people in the world and we are arguing about whether he should be my slave. Assume I'm the kind of person who would enslave another (I'm actually nice, I promise!). In this situation, he wouldn't want to be my slave and and I would want to. There is no consensus on the issue. What policy do we follow? On his account, none. But there is no 'none'. Either you become my slave or you don't. What would happen is that I would consider imposing my policy on you, and you would try to impose your policy on me (Resisting being enslaved) (Both would think each of us is justified in our respective policies). Doing this is precisely what my theory predicts!

Sumner's meta-rule is not 'follow the consensus', though. It's more like 'Others should guide their decisions by the consensus, I should guide mine by my own beliefs'.

(III)

Why not set your own beliefs to be that of the consensus, then? Because that leads us to the paradox ackowledged by him. In a country of travel agents, no one ever travels.

The obvious solution, for me and probably most of the people (the consensus!), is to have one set of beliefs, and use "The consensus is usually right" as a heuristic that informs my actions. Sumner could go third order meta and have a 1st order belief that P is the case, a second order belief that 'If the consensus says that P is the case, then I should set 1st order belief to P, unless I carefully assess it myself' and a third order  belief 'Follow the consensus is a rule that tends to work' to justify the second order belief. Note that we always end up with a belief that is accepted because it's commonsensical.

But Sumner's solution lead us to peculiar paradoxes:

Me: Scott, I heard you are going to start a hedge fund

Scott: Yeah, I have a few good investing ideas

Me: That's great! Have you told your friends about it? I mean, your investor friends who might like investing.

Scott: Well, no. I recommended them to invest in an ETF.

Me: Why is so? Is it better to invest in an ETF or in your hedge fund?

Scott: In an ETF, as is implied by the EMH

Me: But then, you are going to, probably, lose money. You expect your hedge fund to do better than the market, right?

Scott: Yeah

Me: But if you expect your hedge fund to beat the market, why not tell them?

Scott: I'm not special, people should follow the consensus.

Me: Why not follow the consensus yourself then?

Scott: I'm a worker ant, I know that if I do this, the EMH will be true (And the market will work better that way, and that's good)

Me: So your hedge fund is more of a public service? You expect to lose money so that the market becomes more efficient?

Scott: Well, you know... we know that if people behave individually like I am, things will work very well. So I get to have personal beliefs and act on them, but at the same time I give others advice based on consensus to avoid propagating my possible mistakes.

Me: But you know that the system will work basically the same if you just go for the ETF and forget your hedge fund, right?

Scott: I'm a utilitarian, I don't think my preferences are special, and I act in such a way as to make it good for everyone.

Me: Oh, well. Here's another policy for you: Tell others to invest in your hedge fund. Have just a first order belief. Tell them about why you think it's a good idea, and why it might be a bad idea. Tell them about the EMH. Then, let them decide. But I think that you are not really doing it out of charity. When you do research in economics, you think what you are doing has some grain of truth.

Scott: I don't know if that would work best. What you are saying is like betting against a prediction market.

Me: Yes, indeed! In certain cases, [prediction markets are beatable](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2660628). Especially when we are talking about obscure assets you want to invest in like the ones you are planning to invest in. How many people have really considered what you are considering now?

(IV)

> Suppose you have a belief–say, the belief that it is raining. And suppose that you come to reflect explicitly on whether your belief constitutes knowledge. What conclusion might you come to? You must either conclude that you know that it is raining, or withhold judgment about whether you know this, or conclude that you do not know it. If you conclude that you do not know it, then it seems that there will be a kind of tension between your first-order belief that it is raining, and your second-order belief about that first-order belief. If you hold on to your first-order belief while simultaneously denying that it constitutes knowledge, then, I think, you are guilty of some sort of irrationality. This is suggested by the fact that, were you to express your attitudes in words, you would utter a Moore-paradoxical sentence along the lines of, ‘‘It is raining, but I don’t know whether it is raining.’ ([Huemer, 2011](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com.sci-hub.cc/doi/10.1111/j.1933-1592.2010.00388.x/abstract))

Sumner seems to be in a similar position. He believes things, but he doesn't know if they are true. Which is nonsense.

He further says that process X (Science, public deliberation, etc...) delivers truth/good policy, even though no individual participant can claim to have to be right. But over and over again, Science has changed, and public policy has been horrible, and people have said so, and been right.

In the current case, however, it doesn't seem to be the case. Sumner should default to the consensus, as there are good reasons for it: trust your reasoned judgement over the rest of the world's.
