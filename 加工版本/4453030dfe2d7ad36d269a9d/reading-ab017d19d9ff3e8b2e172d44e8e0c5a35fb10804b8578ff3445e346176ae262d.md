# Nintil - Does every animal sleep?

## 正文

### sleep 的谜题

Sleep 可能显得 paradoxical：对于 humans 而言，一天中有三分之一的时间处于 inactive，意味着 acquire resources 或 mate 的机会 reduced；此外，这还 increases 被 preyed on 的几率，尤其是在我们的 evolutionary past 中。而且，我们也知道，就我们自身而言，sleep 很难避免：awake 太久会让我们 tired，并且我们会 progressively lose cognitive faculties；此外，我们保持 awake 越久，fall asleep 的 drive 就越强，直到它压倒不愿如此的意志。即便在那些情况下，也会发生被称为 microsleeps 的 wakefulnes lapses，因此我们已经 evolved 出一些 mechanisms，确实会 generate 强烈而难以逃避的 sleep need。人们已经提出了若干 theories 来解释我们为何 sleep，通常都与 memory consolidation 有关。自然，人们会问：我们能否 sleep 得更少？如果可以，我们为什么不这样做？例如，Elephants 或 horses 每天只 sleep 2-3 hours。

当初，我在 Alexey Guzey 针对 _Why We Sleep_ 的 [takedown](https://guzey.com/books/why-we-sleep/) 发表之前审阅它时，其中讨论的一个 claims 是，是否每一种 animal[1](https://nintil.com/everything-sleeps/#animal) 都 sleeps。这是书中的一个 background claim，可以用作 sleep 非常重要的部分 evidence。因此，以下引自 Guzey 的 review

> 在第 6 页，Walker 写道：
> 
> > 迄今 studied 的每一种 species 都 sleeps
> 
> **这是 false，**_至少，根据 Walker 自己的 source 来看如此_。在提出这个 claim 时，他 cites：
> 
> > Kushida, C. Encyclopedia of Sleep, Volume 1 (Elsever, $$_sic_$$ 2013)
> 
> ……结果发现，这是一本长达 2,736 页、售价 \$1,995 的书。幸好，Walker 告诉我们，应当在“Volume 1”，也就是这本书的前 _638 页_ 中寻找这项信息。
> 
> 总之，第 38 页写道：
> 
> > 现在看来，许多 species 在 normal conditions 下会长时间 reduce sleep，而**另一些根本不 sleep——按照 sleep 的 conventional definition 而言。**

The Encyclopedia of Sleep（2013 年出版）所说的与 Alexey 一致；而且，Walker 确实 cited 了一份与他相矛盾的 source，不过其中并未详细列举完全不 sleep 的 animals，必须翻到末尾，追查其中 cited 的一些 papers。

### 每一种 animal 都 sleep 吗？

首先，什么是 sleep？其中 cited 的一篇 2008 年的 paper，[_所有 animals 都 sleep 吗？_](http://profkramer.com/assets/siegel-2008-do-all-animals-sleep.pdf)（Siegel, 2008），将各种 related terms（如 torpor 或 rest）defined 为不同于 sleep，并将 sleep 本身 defined 为“一个 rapidly reversible state，具有 immobility 和 greatly reduced sensory responsiveness”。此外，或许还要加上：“要算作 sleep，它还必须是 homeostatically regulated；loss of sleep 之后必须出现 increased drive to sleep”。随后，还可以进一步 define 某些 animals 中这种 behaviour 的 correlates；具有 brain 的 animals 还可能呈现 sleep 的各种 subtypes，例如 REM（仅在 birds 和 mammals 中 observed 到）或 NREM。

有了这个 definition，这篇 paper 对不同 organisms 说了些什么：

-   Unicellular organisms：没有人 claimed 它们 sleep；但在其中一些中发现了 circadian rhythms
-   Insects：_Drosophila_ sleeps，cockroaches、bees 和 scorpions 满足 sleep 的 conditions，除了 homeostatic regulation。没有 insects 具有 REM sleep 的 evidence。
-   Fish：接受过 rest 或 sleep behaviour 检查的 species 少于 10 种。Zebrafish sleep，perches rest，但并不呈现针对 stimuli 的 elevated response threshold。
-   Amphibians：bullfrog 呈现具有 circadian rhythms 的 rest，但它们在 rest 期间也更加 vigilant，这会与上文 sleep 的 definition 相违背。不过，tree frog 似乎会 sleep。
-   Reptiles：即使对于同一种 species，关于 REM sleep 的 evidence 也是 Mixed 的。Turtles 似乎会 sleep。
-   Mammals：Mammalian 似乎全都 sleep，但这在不同 species 之间，以及随 environment 不同而有很大 variation（例如，hunger decreases sleep）。Sleep time 呈现很大的 variation，horses sleeping 2h，而 bats sleep 19 hours。
-   Marine mammals：众所周知，dolphins 每次用 [brain 的 half](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unihemispheric_slow-wave_sleep) 来 sleep，甚至会 closing 与正在 sleeping 的 hemisphere 相对的 eye。Fur seals 在陆地上也会 sleep，并呈现 dolphin-style sleep。重要的是，从未 observed 到 dolphins 像我们一样 sleep（即 bihemispheric sleep）。Sleep deprived dolphins（5 days 后）在一项 accuracy(?) task 中并未 show decline in performance，不过 dolphins 需要 progressively more stimulation 才能保持 awake。
-   Birds：Birds sleep，但在被 sleep deprived 后，它们似乎并不会遭受 cognitive impairment。少数 species 还能够每次让 brain 的一个 hemisphere sleep，即使在 flight 中也是如此

因此，作者 concludes，答案是否定的，sleep 并不是 universal；而且，鉴于 sleeping time 和 behaviours 的巨大 variation，sleep 可能在每种 species 中 serving 不同的 functions。

不过，同一年，[Cirelli & Tononi](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2525690/) assess 了这样一个 hypothesis：sleep 可能只是在所有 needs 都得到满足时的 default state。假设，例如，夜间四处游荡比与 pack 的其余成员待在安全的地方更 dangerous，那么 sleep 之所以会 evolve，就只是为了强加于 individual，从而 induce 更安全的 behaviour。作者指出，关于 mammals 和 birds 以外的 sleep，evidence 的质量并不很好；前面提到的 bullfrog example 仅 based on 一项 study，因此他们不认同这里的 evidence 很强。

关于 homeostatic regulation，预期 sleeping beings 会表现出的 behaviours 是 increased sleep pressure，以及 compensatory rebound（在 sleep deprivation 之后，会出现 additional sleep time）。Siegel 曾 argued，insects 并不 show 这些；但 Cirelli & Tononi argue，sleep compensation 可能以 deeper sleep 而非 longer sleep 的形式 occur。同样，支持或反对的 evidence 都不强。

关于 sleep loss 是否 leads to negative consequences，作者表示，只要 sleep deprivation 达到足够程度，大多数 studied 的 animals 都会 die，pigeons 可能是例外。话虽如此，作者承认，这可能是由于 constant stress，而非 lack of sleep 本身；而在 humans 中，一种 causes chronic insomnia 的 condition 也可能因其他原因而 deadly。不过，sleep deprivation 确实 leads to 两种 universal conditions：

首先，sleep intrusion 到 wakefulness 中（Increased sleep pressure、urge to sleep），直至无法避免，即使持续施加旨在实现 total sleep deprivation 的 stimulation 也是如此（microsleeps）；通常与 sleep associated 的 EEG wave patterns 会 leak 到 wakefulness 中，使 wakeful state 变得更加 sleeplike。作者在这里 concludes，没有 evidence 表明 total sleep deprivation 能够持续超过 24 hours，而不发生 microsleeps 和 mixed sleep-wakeful states 中的任一种。

其次是 cognitive impairment；作者在这里指出，对于 humans，individuals 对 impairment 的 susceptible 程度存在很大的 variability，即使在 task-level 也是如此。与 Spiegel 相反，作者 claim，除了 humans 以外，还有 evidence 表明 flies、birds 或 rodents 在 deprivation 之后会出现 cognitive impariment。

作者 concludes：

> null hypothesis 的这三个 corollaries 似乎与现有 evidence 并不十分吻合：没有 convincing case 表明某一种 species 不 sleep，没有 clear instance 表明某一种 animal 能够在没有任何 compensatory mechanism 的情况下放弃 sleep，也没有 indication 表明一个个体能够真正不 sleep，而无需付出高昂代价。

虽然他们并未 identify sleep 正在 serving 什么 function，但他们指出，它可能是 neuronal activity 的一个 intrinsic requirement，而且这个 requirement 也无法在 awake 时 fulfilled；在这里，他们提出，可能是在没有 new memories 进入所造成的 disturbance 的情况下进行 memory consolidation，或者 exercising/stimulating old memories，使其保持 recallable。

好吧，这已经是很久以前的事了。2020 年的 state of the art 如何？

The Handbook of [Behavioural Neuroscience](https://sci-hub.tw/10.1016/B978-0-12-813743-7.00024-4) 的 Chapter 24（2019）提到 [jellyfish](https://www.nature.com/news/jellyfish-caught-snoozing-give-clues-to-origin-of-sleep-1.22654)，将其作为即使没有 central nervous system 也会 sleeps 的 animal example。这种 behaviour 被 claimed 为 full sleep（rest+higher responsivity threshold+homeostatic response）；因此，如果 jellyfish 被 sleep-deprived，那么在有机会时，它们随后会 rest 得更多。

虽然该 chapter 将 sleep 介绍为“对于迄今 studied 的几乎所有 animals 都是 essential”，却并未提到不存在 sleep 的 examples（或许是对这个 claim 的一种 hedging）。该 chapter 还谈到了 mexican cavefish，即生活在 geographically close cave systems 中、evolutionarily related 的 fish。这些 fish 的 sleep time 呈现 substantial variation，从大约 6 hours（对于它们的 surface cousins 而言）到某些 cavefish 几乎完全不 sleep。总体而言，作者表示，他们很想 conclude 所有 fish 都 sleep，但没有这样做，因为我们仅 studied 了少量 fish，因此未来某项 study 也许会发现 sleepless fish。

一项 2018 年关于 sleep 的 origins 和 evolution 的 study（[Keene & Duboue](https://jeb.biologists.org/content/221/11/jeb159533.article-info)）同样指出，不同 species 之间及 species 内部存在很大的 variation。他们在我们的会 sleep 的 animals 清单中，加入了另一个知名的 animal model——只有 302 neurons 的 C. elegans，以及各种 mollusks 和 octopuses。与 longevity 一样，支撑 sleep behaviours 的 genes 似乎也是 conserved 的（他们提到具有 human homologs 的 genes：_Shaker_、_Sleepless_ 和 _Cyclin a_）。与 Tononi 一样，作者指出，sleep 在具有 neurons 的 animals 中是 conserved 的，这 suggests 它可能也是一种在 single-cell level，即 neurons 中就存在的 property。

对于 cockroaches 和其他 insects，该 review 确实表示，与 Spiegel 相反，它们确实呈现所有 sleep markers，包括 homeostatic regulation。

借助 artificial selection 的力量，人们也得以 study 同一种 species 内部的 sleep variation；在 Drosophila 中，可以在 60 generations 内 generate 出呈现 90% sleep loss 的 individuals，不过，sleep-deprived humans 中存在的 biomarkers，在这些 flies 中呈现 higher concentrations，而且这些 flies 也 showed reduced lifespan，与被 sleep deprived 的 wild type flies 相同。

因此，consensus 似乎已经转向 sleep 的 universality。如果回顾 Kavanau（[1998](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/9671258)），作者指出，许多 studies 表明，例如，有一种 cavefish 完全不 sleep；其中包括一项持续一年的 study，在此期间，这些 fish 始终在 swimming，而且总是愿意接受 food。不过，一篇较新的 review（[Kelly et al. 2019](https://www.karger.com/Article/FullText/504123)）cites 了这项 1-year-long study，并指出：

> 早期针对 sightless、cave-dwelling species 的 fishes 的 research \[Pavan, 1946\] 曾 suggested，这些 animals 可能是 sleepless，因为它们表现出 continuous swimming 的 evidence，并且缺乏 activity-based circadian rhythmicity。**不过，较新的 studies contradict 了这个想法。具体而言，虽然 cavefish（_Astyanax mexicanus_）确实 sleep，但相较于它们的 surface-dwelling conspecifics，它们实际上 sleep 得非常少** \[Zafar and Morgan, 1992; Duboué et al., 2011, 2012; Yoshizawa et al., 2015; Jaggard et al., 2017, 2018\]。$$...$$ 此外，buccal pumping sharks 和 rays 长时间的 restfulness 能否被视为 sleep，还是仅仅是 quiet wakefulness，仍然未知。遗憾的是，这篇 review 涵盖的 studies 没有一项回答这些问题。要做到这一点，未来的工作必须包括对 sleep 的 presence or absence 的 systematic investigation，最好涵盖广泛的 elasmobranchs。

H[andbook of Behavioural Neuroscience](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/B9780128137437000207) 第 30 卷的第 20 章 (2019) 考察了各种 species。部分 findings 如下：

-   Larger animals 的 sleep 更少（elephants sleep 2 hours，horses sleep 3 hours），具体来说，具有 larger brains（以 absolute terms 而言）的 animals 的 sleep 更少。考察 brain size relative to body size 时，会发现 opposite pattern：具有 relatively larger brains 的 Animals，例如 apes 或 dolphins，往往表现出 advanced cognitive abilities，它们的 sleep 更多；尤其是 humans，其 REM sleep 的 amount higher than expected。具有 shorter gestation periods 的 Animals 表现出更多 REM sleep，而 blood 中具有 higher antibody concentration 的 animals 的 sleep 更多。Sleep 是否可能向 immune system reallocate energy？
-   Birds 也会 sleep，包括 REM sleep，而且它们在 sleep deprivation 后也表现出 compensatory response，pigeons 也不例外；Spiegel 在上文说过，尚未观察到 pigeons 因 sleep deprivation 而 die；Handbook 指出，与 mammals 相比，它们尤其 resistant to sleep deprivation。
-   对于 Reptiles，即使在所研究的同一 species 内，evidence 似乎仍然是 mixed。
-   对于 fish，尤其是 Pavan 的 study，Handbook 也重复了 Kelly et al. 的 claim，即较新的 evidence 表明它们实际上会 sleeping。有些不需要 movement 来 breathe 的 shark 曾被观察到处于 immobile state，但它们是否正在 sleeping 尚不清楚。对于像 sharks 这样的 "ram ventilating" bony fish，它们必须保持 swimming，才能让 water 穿过 gills，即进行 "ram"，它们没有表现出 periods of inactivity，这构成了它们根本不会 sleep 这一 claim 的 basis；但这篇 review 的作者并不将此视为它们不会 sleep 的 evidence；相反，sleep 可能与 swimming 是 compatible 的。与其他章节不同，他们在这里没有提到任何 EEG measurement。上文的 Kelly paper 是专门针对这一具体情况的 review，其中他指出，_ram ventilating sharks 和 rays 不会 sleep 的 possibility，似乎是 unlikely 的_，而且他们提出，它们会像 dolphins 一样 sleep。
-   对于 invertebrates，他们提到，sleep 已在 arthropods、roundwordms、mollusk、flatworms 和 jellyfish 的各种 species 中得到 "convincingly demonstrated"，这表明 sleep 很可能很早就 evolved 了。

他们得出结论：

> **所有 animals 都会 sleep 吗？在 sleep scientists 所 studied 的所有 species 中，都已 observed 到 Sleep。**人们很容易 conclude 所有 animals 都会 sleep；然而，对于大多数 animal groups，并不存在 data。大约 30 个 animal phyla 尚未就 sleep 的 presence 接受 tested (Lesku & Ly, 2017)。即使在 studied phyla 内，phylogenetic coverage 也往往很 poor。invertebrates 如此，fishes 和 amphibians 也是如此。话虽如此，sleep 存在于非常 simple animals 中，例如 flatworms (Omond et al., 2017) 和 jellyfish (Nath et al., 2017)，这 indicates，sleep 在 animals 的 lineage 中很早便 evolved 了。它是否在 evolutionary time 中一直 persisted 于所有 species，尚不清楚。尽管如此，sleep 所表现出的 evolutionary longevity suggests，它 fulfills 一种 fundamental 且 inescapable need。这种 fundamental need 还通过以下方面进一步 revealed：(i) 尽管这种 state 带有 inherent vulnerability，sleep 仍然 persistence (Lima et al., 2005)；(ii) marine mammals 和 birds 中 unihemispheric SWS 的 evolution (Lyamin et al., 2008; Rattenborg et al., 2000)；以及 (iii) 当其他 demands favor sustained performance 时，某些 animals 能够 greatly reduce（但不能 eliminate）sleep (Lesku et al., 2012; Rattenborg et al., 2016)。sleep 很可能 serves many functions，其中有些可能是 evolutionarily “ancient”，存在于 jellyfish、flatworms 和 vertebrates 中 (Nath et al., 2017)，另一些则可能是 evolutionarily new，只存在于 derived species 中 (Lesku, Vyssotski, et al., 2011)。

因此，“迄今 observed 到的每一种 animal 都会 sleep”这一 claim 很可能是 correct 的；不过 REM sleep 并非 universal。Walker 在回应 Alexey Guzey 的评论时，cites 了我在这里提到的一些[相同 evidence](https://sleepdiplomat.wordpress.com/2019/12/19/why-we-sleep-responses-to-questions-from-readers/#species)。

所以我们可以回答：“每一种 animal 都会 sleeps”与当前 evidence 是 compatible 的，虽然尚未 confirmed；“我们深入 studied 过的每一种 animal 都会 sleeps”很可能是对的，而且我要补充，未来的 studies（针对 sharks、bullfrogs 等的 EEG）也将指向这一方向。

### 我们（究竟）为什么 sleep

这篇文章缘起于这条[讨论串](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1176872077825122304)，其中 Michael Nielsen 抱怨我们还没有关于 sleep 的好 explanation，而我想知道，什么才算一个好 explanation。这里我不打算 explain 我们为什么 sleep，那留待另一篇文章再谈；我将转而提出，一个答案必须满足什么条件才算合格。

要 explain 我们为什么 sleep，一种方式需要包含 evolutionary level（为什么这种 behaviour 或 trait 会 evolved），在这个 level 上，需要讨论 sleep 的 benefits 如何弥补其所需的 downtime。但它也需要 neuron、synapses、proteins 以及其中 regulatory pathways 等 levels 的 underpinnings，才能 explain 它究竟在为我们做什么。

Evolutionary explanations 可能令人失望；说“legs evolved 是为了 move”实在太容易了。之所以令人失望，是因为它忽略了可能的 counterfactuals；事实上，我们知道某些 animals 并没有用于 move 的 legs，因此，如果 “movement” 是 evolutionary requirement，那么具有 “legs” 的 phenotype 就不是唯一手段，于是问题便成了：“为什么是 legs，而不是别的东西？”所以，我们还需要用一个 counterfactual 答案来补充直接的 evolutionary 答案，思考这种 phenotype 本可以通过哪些不同方式 acquired。Biology 是一个非常丰富的 domain，因此，通常有多种方式可以满足同一个 “design goal”，所以我们最终可能不得不反复思考 counterfactuals。

在本文中，我提出 sleep 是 universal 的。这反过来为以下观点提供了一些 evidence：对于所有具有 neurons 的 animals，sleep 都是一种 necessity。那么，这是真的吗？

例如，假设 sleep 是 systems of neurons 的一种 need，而我们需要 explain 为什么会 observe 到 sleep。是否可能存在完全不 require sleep 的 systems of neurons？biology 中有一场[争论](https://www.quantamagazine.org/comb-jelly-neurons-spark-evolution-debate-20150325/)，讨论 neurons 是否不止一次 evolved，但无论如何，end-result 都是 similar 的，我们都有同样 basic kind 的 neurons（虽然我们有不同 kinds 的 ion channels；[每一种 animal 都 have K](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/pdf/S0960-9822\(16\)30489-4.pdf)，但只有一些具有 Ca 和 Na；variation 越多越好，因为这表明，即使不同的 designs 也不能完全 banish sleep）。Animals 曾 gained 和 lost limbs、scales、wings 及其他 traits，但它们都具有相同的 basic neuron design，而且都会 sleep。如果是这样，argument chain 就是：

1.  在 sufficiently complex environment 中的 Animals 需要 systems of neurons，才能 successfully occupy 它们的 ecological niche（这更像是一个必须接受的 premise。在这里，我们把 explanation 停在如下事实：某些 animals evolved 出了 neurons，而另一些没有。我们可以尝试进一步 explain，但不是现在）
2.  在 animal kingdom 中，只有一种制造 neurons 的方式。迄今的 Evidence 表明情况确实如此，它们都具有相同的 basic architecture，而且，虽然如上所述，对于它们 evolved 了多少次仍有争论，但这似乎是一个如此 fixed feature，以至于对于满足 control and information processing system 所需条件的 system 而言，它们在 possibility-space 中是 along 的。
3.  要么是 neurons 本身，要么是任何 system of neurons，都 requires sleep。Evidence 是，我们迄今考察过的每一种具有 neurons 的 animal 都 presents 某种形式的 sleep + 一些我没有在这里呈现的、关于 sleep 的 molecular basis 的 evidence。
4.  sleep 究竟满足了 neuronal systems 的哪些 needs？我们确实知道，lack of sleep 会 causes 各种形式的 cognitive impairment，因此，我们必须深入它们的 molecular biology，才能看清究竟发生了什么。
5.  但为什么我们 sleep 8 hours，而不是像 elephants 那样只需 2？我还没有研究这一点；但它将是 brain-specific architecture 与 environment 的 combination。对于一种 fixed brain architecture（例如 neurons 和 synapses 的 number、overall connectomic patterns 等），会有一个 optimal amount of sleep，使它能够履行 functions。然而，其他 evolutionary requirements 会使其发生变化。上文所述那些 slept 少了 90% 的 flies 就体现了这一点；这种 gains 是通过付出 shorter lives 以及类似 sleep deprivation 的 effects 这样的 cost 才实现的。无论如何，确实有一些 h[ealthy individuals](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2214505-dna-mutation-lets-some-people-live-healthily-on-only-4-hours-sleep/)，它们只需 sleep 4 hours，而没有任何明显的不利之处。individual sleep variation 的 causes 值得更多 research。

那么，接下来呢？Identifying 受到 sleep 影响的确切 systems，以及这些 relations 如何在不同 species 间 vary。然后看看它们在 sleep deprivation 期间会发生什么。那些因此 die 的 animals 究竟发生了什么？如果我们看到它们死于例如 pathogens，那便是 immune system playing a core role 的一个线索。但如果我们 observe 到 general dysregulation，而且 malfunctioning brains 能够 induce 它，那么也许只有 brain 才有这种 need。

1

在全文中，我所说的 animal，是指具有 neurons 的 animals。没有 neurons 的 animal 的一个例子是 sponges。

## 原文

### The puzzle of sleep

Sleep may seem paradoxical: Being inactive for a third of the day, in humans, means reduced chances to acquire resources or mate; plus it increases the odds of being preyed on, especially in our evolutionary past. Moreover, we also know that in our own case sleep is hard to avoid: Being awake for too long makes us tired and we progressively lose cognitive faculties, plus the more we stay awake the stronger there is of a drive to fall asleep, to the point it overrides the will not to. Even in those cases, lapses in wakefulnes known as microsleeps occur, so we have evolved mechanisms that do generate a strong and hard to elude need to sleep. Several theories have been proposed to explain why is it that we sleep, usually having to do with memory consolidation. Naturally, one wonders: Could we sleep less? If so why don't we? Elephants or horses for example sleep just 2-3 hours every day.

Back when I was reviewing prior to publication Alexey Guzey's [takedown](https://guzey.com/books/why-we-sleep/) of _Why We Sleep_ one of the claims that was discussed there was whether or not every animal[1](https://nintil.com/everything-sleeps/#animal) sleeps. This is a background claim in the book that can be used as part of the evidence for sleep being very important. So, from Guzey's review

> On page 6, Walker writes:
> 
> > \[E\]very species studied to date sleeps
> 
> **This is false,** _at least, according to Walker’s own source_. When making this claim, he cites:
> 
> > Kushida, C. Encyclopedia of Sleep, Volume 1 (Elsever, \[_sic_\] 2013)
> 
> ….which turns out to be a 2,736 page book that costs $1,995. Fortunately, Walker tells us that we should search for this information somewhere in “Volume 1” or the first _638 pages_ of the book.
> 
> Anyway, page 38 reads:
> 
> > It now appears that many species reduce sleep for long periods of time under normal conditions and that **others do not sleep at all, in the way sleep is conventionally defined.**

The Encyclopedia of Sleep (From 2013), says what Alexey says; and indeed Walker cited a source that contradicts him, but does not expand on examples of animals that do not sleep at all, one has to go to the end to follow some of the papers cited therein.

### Does every animal sleep?

First, what is sleep? A paper from 2008 that is cited there, [_Do all animals sleep?_](http://profkramer.com/assets/siegel-2008-do-all-animals-sleep.pdf) (Siegel, 2008) defines various related terms (Like torpor or rest) as different from sleep, and sleep itself as "a rapidly reversible state of immobility and greatly reduced sensory responsiveness". Plus perhaps "To count as sleep it must also be homeostatically regulated; loss of sleep must be followed by an increased drive to sleep". One can then further define correlates of this behaviour in certain animals; those with a brain can also present various subtypes of sleep, as REM (Only observed in birds and mammals) or NREM.

That defined, what does the paper say about different organisms:

-   Unicellular organisms: No one has claimed that they sleep; but in some circadian rhythms have been found
-   Insects: _Drosophila_ sleeps, cockroaches, bees, and scorpions meet the conditions of sleep except for homeostatic regulation. No evidence of REM sleep in insects.
-   Fish: Less than 10 species have been examined for rest or sleep behaviour. Zebrafish sleep, perches rest, but do not present the elevated response threshold to stimuli.
-   Amphibians: The bullfrog presents circadian rhythms rest but they are also more vigilant during rest, which would go against the definition of sleep above. The tree frog seems however to sleep.
-   Reptiles: Mixed evidence for REM sleep even for the same species. Turtles seem to sleep.
-   Mammals: Mammalian all seem to sleep, but this varies a lot across species and depending on the environment (eg. hunger decreases sleep). Sleep time shows great variation, with horses sleeping for 2h while bats sleep for 19 hours.
-   Marine mammals: Famously, dolphins sleep with [half of their brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unihemispheric_slow-wave_sleep) at a time, even closing the eye opposite to the hemisphere that is sleeping. Fur seals also sleep while on land, and show dolphin-style sleep. Importantly, dolphins have never been observed to sleep the way we do (i.e. bihemispheric sleep). Sleep deprived dolphins(after 5 days) do not show decline in performance in an accuracy(?) task, however dolphins required progressively more stimulation to stay awake.
-   Birds: Birds sleep, but they don't seem to suffer from cognitive impairment after being sleep deprived. A few species are also able to sleep one hemisphere of the brain at a time, even when in flight

So the author concludes that no, sleep is not universal; moreover given the great variation in sleeping time and behaviours, sleep may be serving different functions in each species.

From the same year however, [Cirelli & Tononi](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2525690/) assess the hypothesis that sleep may just be a default state when all the needs have been met. Assuming that, say, roaming around at night is more dangerous that staying with the rest of the pack in a safe place, then sleep would evolve for no reason other than to force itself upon the individual to induce a safer behaviour. The authors note that the quality of the evidence regarding sleep beyond mammals and birds is not very good; the bullfrog example noted before is based on a single study, so they don't agree that the evidence is strong here.

Regarding homeostatic regulation, the behaviours expected of sleeping beings were increased sleep pressure, and a compensatory rebound (after sleep deprivation, additional sleep time ensues). Siegel had argued that insects do not show this; but Cirelli & Tononi argue that sleep compensation may occur as deeper sleep rather than longer sleep. Again, the evidence for or against is not strong.

Regarding whether or not sleep loss leads to negative consequences, the authors say that given a sufficient amount of sleep deprivation, most animals studied die, with the possible exception of pigeons. This all said, the authors acknowledge that this may be due to constant stress, not lack of sleep itself, while in humans, a condition that causes chronic insomnia may be deadly for other reasons. However, sleep deprivation does lead to two universal conditions:

First, intrusion of sleep into wakefulness (Increased sleep pressure, urge to sleep) that goes to the point of being unavoidable, even with constant stimulation that aimed for total sleep deprivation (microsleeps); EEG wave patterns that are typically associated with sleep leak into wakefulness, so the wakeful state becomes more sleeplike. Here the authors conclude that there is no evidence that total sleep deprivation is possible for more than 24 hours without either microsleeps and mixed sleep-wakeful states occurring.

And second, cognitive impairment; here the authors note than in humans there is great variability in how susceptible individuals are to impairment, even at the task-level. Contra Spiegel, the authors claim there is evidence for cognitive impariment following deprivation in flies, birds, or rodents, in addition to humans.

The authors conclude that

> The three corollaries of the null hypothesis do not seem to square well with the available evidence: there is no convincing case of a species that does not sleep, no clear instance of an animal that forgoes sleep without some compensatory mechanism, and no indication that one can truly go without sleep without paying a high price.

While they don't identify what function is sleep serving, they point to the fact that it may be an intrinsic requirement of neuronal activity, a requirement that also cannot be fulfilled while being awake, here they suggest memory consolidation without the disturbance of new memories coming in, or exercising/stimulating old memories to keep them recallable.

Okay, this was a while ago. What's the state of the art in 2020?

The Handbook of [Behavioural Neuroscience](https://sci-hub.tw/10.1016/B978-0-12-813743-7.00024-4), Chapter 24 (2019) mentions [jellyfish](https://www.nature.com/news/jellyfish-caught-snoozing-give-clues-to-origin-of-sleep-1.22654) as an example of animal that sleeps, even when no central nervous system is present. This behaviour is claimed to be full sleep (rest+higher responsivity threshold+homeostatic response); so jellyfish if sleep-deprived will then rest more when given a chance to do so.

While the chapter introduces sleep as "essential to nearly all studied animals to date" it does not mention examples where it does not occur (Perhaps as a form of hedging the claim). The chapter also talks about mexican cavefish, evolutionarily related fish in geographically close cave systems. These fish present substantial variation in sleep time from around 6 hours (In the case of their surface cousins) to almost nothing for some of the cavefish. Overall, the authors say that they would be tempted to conclude that all fish sleep, but stop short of doing so as we have only studied a small number of fish, so maybe some future study will find sleepless fish.

A 2018 study on the origins and evolution of sleep ([Keene & Duboue](https://jeb.biologists.org/content/221/11/jeb159533.article-info)) also notes great variation across and among species. To our list of animals that sleep they add another well known animal model, C. elegans which has just 302 neurons, and various mollusks, and octopuses. As with longevity, the genes that underpin sleep behaviours also seem conserved (They mention the genes _Shaker_, _Sleepless_, and _Cyclin a_ which have human homologs). As with Tononi, the authors point to the fact that sleep being conserved across animals with neurons suggests that it may be a property that is also present at the single-cell level, i.e. in neurons.

For cockroaches and other insects the review does say that, contra Spiegel, they do present all the sleep markers, including homeostatic regulation.

Through the power of artificial selection, it has also been possible to study sleep variation within one species; in Drosophila it was possible to generate individuals that presented 90% sleep loss in 60 generations, though biomarkers present in sleep-deprived humans were present in higher concentrations in these flies, and these flies also showed reduced lifespan, same as wild type flies that are sleep deprived.

So the consensus seems to have moved towards the universality of sleep. If we look back at Kavanau ([1998](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/9671258)), the author points to numerous studies showing that, for example, a kind of cavefish does not sleep at all; including a one year long study where the fish were swimming at all times, and always disposed to accept food. But then again, a more recent review ([Kelly et al. 2019](https://www.karger.com/Article/FullText/504123)), that cites the 1-year-long study notes that

> Early research into sightless, cave-dwelling species of fishes \[Pavan, 1946\] suggested that these animals might be sleepless as they show evidence of continuous swimming and lack activity-based circadian rhythmicity. **More recent studies, however, contradict this idea. Specifically, while cavefish (_Astyanax mexicanus_) do sleep, they in fact sleep very little**, relative to their surface-dwelling conspecifics \[Zafar and Morgan, 1992; Duboué et al., 2011, 2012; Yoshizawa et al., 2015; Jaggard et al., 2017, 2018\]. \[...\] Furthermore, it remains unknown whether extensive periods of restfulness in buccal pumping sharks and rays can be considered sleep or simply quiet wakefulness. Unfortunately, none of the studies covered in this review answer these questions. To do so, future work must include a systematic investigation into the presence or absence of sleep, preferably on a broad range of elasmobranchs.

Chapter 20 of the 30th volume of the H[andbook of Behavioural Neuroscience](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/B9780128137437000207) (2019) looks at various species. Some findings:

-   Larger animals sleep less (elephants sleep 2 hours, horses sleep 3 hours), and specifically animals with larger brains (in absolute terms) sleep less. Looking at brain size relative to body size shows the opposite pattern: Animals with relatively larger brains such as apes or dolphins, which tend to show advanced cognitive abilities, sleep more; humans in particular show a higher than expected amount of REM sleep. Animals with shorter gestation periods show more REM sleep, and animals with a higher antibody concentration in blood sleep more. Sleep may reallocate energy to the immune system?
-   Birds also sleep, including REM sleep, and they also show the compensatory response after sleep deprivation, including in pigeons, which Spiegel said above had not been observed to die from sleep deprivation; the Handbook notes they are particularly resistant to sleep deprivation compared to mammals.
-   Reptiles, evidence seems still mixed even within the same species studied.
-   For fish, and particularly the Pavan study, the Handbook also repeats the claim from Kelly et al., that newer evidence points to them actually sleeping. Some kinds of shark that do not require movement to breathe have been observed in an immobile state, but whether they are sleeping or not is unclear. For "ram ventilating" bony fish like sharks, which must remain swimming to "ram" water through their gills, they don't show periods of inactivity, forming the basis of the claim that they do not sleep at all; but the authors of the review do not take this as evidence that they don't sleep; rather sleep may be compatible with swimming. Unlike in the other sections, they don't mention here any EEG measurement. The Kelly paper above is a review of this specific case in particular, where he notes that _the possibility that ram ventilating sharks and rays do not sleep, seems unlikely_, and they posit that they sleep just like dolphins do.
-   For invertebrates they mention that sleep has been "convincingly demonstrated" in various species of arthropods, roundwordms, mollusk, flatworms, and jellyfish, suggesting that sleep probably evolved very early.

And they conclude,

> **Do all animals sleep? Sleep has been observed in all species studied by sleep scientists.** There is a temptation to conclude that all animals sleep; however, no data exist for most animal groups. Around 30 animal phyla have yet to be tested for the presence of sleep (Lesku & Ly, 2017). Even within studied phyla, the phylogenetic coverage is often poor. This is true for invertebrates and also fishes and amphibians. That said, the existence of sleep in very simple animals, such as flatworms (Omond et al., 2017) and jellyfish (Nath et al., 2017), indicates that sleep evolved early in the lineage of animals. Whether it has persisted in all species over evolutionary time is unclear. Nonetheless, the apparent evolutionary longevity of sleep suggests that it fulfills a fundamental and inescapable need. This fundamental need is further revealed by (i) the persistence of sleep, despite the inherent vulnerability associated with this state (Lima et al., 2005); (ii) the evolution of unihemispheric SWS in marine mammals and birds (Lyamin et al., 2008; Rattenborg et al., 2000); and (iii) animals that can greatly reduce (but not eliminate) sleep when other demands favor sustained performance (Lesku et al., 2012; Rattenborg et al., 2016). It seems likely that sleep serves many functions, some of which might be evolutionarily “ancient,” present in jellyfish, flatworms, and vertebrates (Nath et al., 2017), while others might be evolutionarily new and present only in derived species (Lesku, Vyssotski, et al., 2011).

Thus, the claim that "every animal observed so far sleep" is probably correct; although REM sleep is not universal. Walker, in his reply to Alexey Guzey's comments cites some of the [same evidence](https://sleepdiplomat.wordpress.com/2019/12/19/why-we-sleep-responses-to-questions-from-readers/#species) I mention here.

Hence we can answer that "every animal sleeps" is compatible with the current evidence, although not confirmed yet; "every animal we have studied in depth sleeps"is probably right, and I add that future studies (EEG on sharks, bullfrogs, etc) will point in this direction too.

### Why we (really) sleep

This post was prompted by this [thread](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1176872077825122304) where Michael Nielsen complains we don't have a good explanation for sleep and I wondered what a good explanation would be. Here I am not going to explain why we sleep, I'll leave that for some other post, but will offer instead what an answer must meet to count.

A way to explain why we sleep would have an evolutionary level (Why the behaviour or trait evolved) and that level would talk about the benefits of sleep that make up for the downtime it requires. But also it would require underpinnings at the levels of the neuron and the synapses and the proteins and regulatory pathways therein to explain what is it that it is actually doing for us.

Evolutionary explanations can be underwhelming; it's all too easy to say that "legs evolved to move". That feels underwhelming because it ignores the possible counterfactuals; as a matter of fact we know that some animals don't have legs to move, so if "movement" is the evolutionary requirement, the phenotype of having "legs" is not the only means, thus leading to the question "Why legs and not something different?". So we would then need to complement the straight evolutionary answer with a counterfactual one, thinking about different ways the phenotype could have been acquired. Biology is a very rich domain, so there are usually multiple ways of meeting the same "design goal" so we may end up having to repeatedly think of counterfactuals.

In this article I've suggested that sleep is universal. This in turn is some evidence that sleep is a necessity for all animals that possess neurons. Now, is this true?

For example, assume that sleep is a need of systems of neurons and we need to explain why we observe sleep. Can there be systems of neurons that do not require sleep at all? There is a [debate](https://www.quantamagazine.org/comb-jelly-neurons-spark-evolution-debate-20150325/) in biology about whether or not neurons evolved more than once, but the end-result is in any case similar, we all have the same basic kind of neurons (Though we have different kinds of ion channels; [every animal have K](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/pdf/S0960-9822\(16\)30489-4.pdf) but only some have Ca and Na; the more variation the better as it shows that even different designs do not banish sleep completely). Animals have gained and lost limbs, scales, wings and other traits, yet they all share the same basic neuron design, and they all sleep. If so, the argument chain would be:

1.  Animals in a sufficiently complex environment need systems of neurons to successfully occupy their ecological niche (This is more of a premise that one has to buy. Here we stop the explanation at the fact that some animals evolved to have neurons and others do not. We could further try to explain this but not now)
2.  There is only one way to make neurons in the animal kingdom. Evidence is that it is the case so far, they all share the same basic architecture, and while as noted above there is debate about how many times they evolved, it seems such a fixed feature that they are along in the possibility-space for a system that meets what is required of a control and information processing system.
3.  Either neurons themselves, or any system of neurons requires sleep. Evidence is that every animal with neurons we have looked at so far presents some form of sleep + Some evidence I have not presented here about the molecular basis of sleep.
4.  What are the exact needs of neuronal systems that are being met with sleep? We do know that lack of sleep causes various forms of cognitive impairment, so we would have to get into the molecular biology of those to see exactly what is going on.
5.  But why do we sleep 8 hours and not 2 like elephants? I haven't looked into it yet; but it will be a combination of brain-specific architecture and environment. For a fixed brain architecture (e.g. number of neurons and synapses, overall connectomic patterns, etc) there will be an optimal amount of sleep for it to do its functions. However, other evolutionary requirements will shift this around. You could see this with the flies described above that slept 90% less; that gains was possible by paying the cost of shorter lives and effects akin to those of sleep deprivation. In any case there are h[ealthy individuals](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2214505-dna-mutation-lets-some-people-live-healthily-on-only-4-hours-sleep/) that sleep just 4 hours without any obvious drawback. The causes of individual sleep variation merit more research.

So what's next? Identifying the exact systems that are affected by sleep and how those relations vary across species. Then see what happens to them during sleep deprivation. What exactly happens to animals that die from it? If we saw them dying from, say, pathogens then that would be a clue to the immune system playing a core role. But if we observe a general dysregulation, and if malfunctioning brains are able to induce it then it is perhaps the brain alone that has that need.
