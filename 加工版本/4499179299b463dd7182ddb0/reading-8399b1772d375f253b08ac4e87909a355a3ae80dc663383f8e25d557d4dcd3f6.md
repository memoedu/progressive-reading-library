## 人类的 Energy Crisis

我们正在经历一场 human energy crisis。每三个人中就有一人 [report](https://academic.oup.com/eurpub/article/33/Supplement_2/ckad160.1278/7327262?utm_source=chatgpt.com&login=false) 感到 fatigue。每八个人中就有一人 [report](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm7245a7.htm) 在大多数日子里或每天都感到 tired。在工作中，76% 的人至少有些时候会 [feel burnout](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/288539/employee-burnout-biggest-myth.aspx)，其中近三分之一“非常频繁”或“总是”感到 burned out。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Everyday-Energyl1-1.jpg)

从某些方面看，我们的 exhaustion 是 paradoxical。作为一个社会，我们比以往任何时候都更加富裕。为了获得足以维持生存的食物，所需的 labor 比历史上几乎任何时候都少得多。我们的生活充斥着娱乐和新的休闲活动。而且，对我们大多数人来说，完成工作所需的 physical effort 已降至历史最低水平。

向几乎任何一个从历史中走来的人描述我们的生活，他们都会想象出一种轻松便利的生活：机器替我们洗衣服、洗碗；水从家中的管道流出，不必从河里或井里搬运；我们坐在办公桌前工作；食物送到家门口——拿来就能吃。

然而，有 evidence 表明，所有这些现代的奢侈享受并没有让我们拥有充沛的额外 energy。[The Psychology of Fatigue](http://www.cambridge.org/core/books/psychology-of-fatigue/026AC1D806D757CC7B4BA2FFAFA4905D) 的作者、研究者 Robert Hockey 甚至 argues，fatigue 这一 notion 本身，也就是感到 drained 的不愉快体验，是现代的发明；过去社会中的人们在工作后当然也会感到 tired，但他们并没有我们现代人所面临的 burnout 和 exhaustion 问题。

相反，我们看到的是普遍存在的 fatigue 和 burnout 趋势，depression 和 ADHD 等 energy-related mental health issues 的 incidences 不断上升，以及我们对 [coffee](https://www.ncausa.org/Newsroom/Daily-coffee-consumption-at-20-year-high-up-nearly-40) 和 [energy drinks](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/energy-drinks-market) 的 consumption 不断增加——这些都表明，我们 manage 自身 energy 的方式出了严重的问题。

## 我们为什么会 Exhausted？

这场 energy 的 crisis 有三个 root causes：

**首先，我们的现代 lifestyle 给我们的 biology 带来了沉重的负担**。Indoor lighting 和随时不停的娱乐 disrupt 我们的 sleep。Sedentary habits undermine 我们的 health 和 fitness。Poor diets 让我们同时处于 overfed 和 undernourished 的状态。Chronic psychological stress drains 我们的 bodies 和 minds。

**其次，我们的 work culture 是 unnatural 且 unhealthy 的**。在人类历史的大部分时期，work 都受 effort 和 recovery 的 natural rhythms 支配。Hunter-gatherers 必须努力工作才能生存，但他们也会在一天中 [got plenty of rest](https://faculty.washington.edu/stevehar/lee.pdf)。在有电之前，太阳落山时，工作便结束了。即使是中世纪的农民，也很可能比我们拥有 [more days off](https://udayton.edu/magazine/2025/12/peasants-holiday.php)。

**第三，social trends 已经剥夺了我们的 work 曾经拥有的大部分 meaning**。现代以前，很少有人拥有“dream” jobs，但他们也没有“[bullshit](https://www.amazon.ca/Bullshit-Jobs-Theory-David-Graeber/dp/150114331X)” jobs。Work，甚至 hard labor，都契合于人们对世界的一种理解，因此能够 fill psychological needs。脱离了这种理解，我们便在对 work 的 unhealthy obsession 与逃避现实的幻想之间 oscillate，幻想着完美的 dream jobs 或 early retirement。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Everyday-Energyl1-2.jpg)

这三股 forces 意味着，尽管生活物质充裕、看似轻松，我们中的许多人仍感到 exhausted 和 apathetic。

## Energy Management 是 Productivity 和 Well-Being 的关键

让我们 exhausted 和 drained 的 forces 远比我们自身强大。而怀念更简单的往昔，只是一场白日梦，并非解决办法。

即便如此，我们仍可以培养一些 practices，恢复过去那些更人性化的 manage 自身 energy 的方式。

**面对一个被设计成不断 drain 我们的 biological sources of energy 的世界，我们可以创造新的 fuel。**我们可以有意识地培养 sleep、exercise、consumption 和 stress-management 方面的 practices，恢复我们带着 energy 和 vigor 生活的全部 capacity。

**面对要求我们 work 更多、更久、更努力的持续压力，我们可以设计新的 flow。**我们可以构建 work 和 recovery 的 rhythms，让自己避免 exhaustion——同时实际上完成更多事情。

**面对那些脱离 meaning、令人不满意的 jobs，我们可以创造让自己 flourish 的 work。**我们可以选择具有更大 meaning 的 work，让它既不是负担，也不是 obsession；我们还可以培养一种 attitude，让已经选择的 work 变得更加丰富。

这些目标并不容易实现，整体的 cultural trends 当然也帮不上忙。但只要我们认真对待，这些目标就能够实现。

## The Human Energy Crisis

We’re living through a human energy crisis. One in three people [report](https://academic.oup.com/eurpub/article/33/Supplement_2/ckad160.1278/7327262?utm_source=chatgpt.com&login=false) feeling fatigue. One in eight [report](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/72/wr/mm7245a7.htm) feeling tired most days or every day. At our jobs, 76% of people [feel burnout](https://www.gallup.com/workplace/288539/employee-burnout-biggest-myth.aspx) at least some of the time, with nearly a third feeling burned out “very often” or “always.”

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Everyday-Energyl1-1.jpg)

In some ways, our exhaustion is paradoxical. As a society, we’re richer than we’ve ever been. The amount of labor needed to get enough food to survive is far less than at almost any point in history. We’re saturated with entertainment and new leisure activities. And, for most of us, the physical effort needed to do our jobs is at an all-time low.

Describe our lives to almost any human being plucked from history, and they would imagine a life of ease and convenience: machines that wash our clothes and dishes, water that flows from pipes in our homes rather than being hauled from rivers or wells, desk jobs, and food delivered to our doors—ready to eat.

Yet there’s evidence that all these modern luxuries haven’t left us brimming with extra energy. Researcher Robert Hockey, author of [The Psychology of Fatigue](http://www.cambridge.org/core/books/psychology-of-fatigue/026AC1D806D757CC7B4BA2FFAFA4905D), even argues that the notion of fatigue itself, the unpleasant experience of feeling drained, is an invention of the modern age; people in past societies felt tired after work, certainly, but they didn’t have our modern problems of burnout and exhaustion.

Instead, we have trends of pervasive fatigue and burnout, rising incidences of energy-related mental health issues like depression and ADHD, and increases in our consumption of [coffee](https://www.ncausa.org/Newsroom/Daily-coffee-consumption-at-20-year-high-up-nearly-40) and [energy drinks](https://www.grandviewresearch.com/industry-analysis/energy-drinks-market)—these all point to something deeply wrong with how we manage our energy.

## Why Are We Exhausted?

This crisis of energy has three root causes:

**First, our modern lifestyle puts heavy burdens on our biology**. Indoor lighting and always-on entertainment disrupt our sleep. Sedentary habits undermine our health and fitness. Poor diets leave us simultaneously overfed and undernourished. Chronic psychological stress drains our bodies and our minds.

**Second, our work culture is unnatural and unhealthy**. For most of human history, work was governed by natural rhythms of effort and recovery. Hunter-gatherers had to work hard to survive, but they also [got plenty of rest](https://faculty.washington.edu/stevehar/lee.pdf) throughout the day. Before electricity, work ended when the sun set. Even medieval peasants likely got [more days off](https://udayton.edu/magazine/2025/12/peasants-holiday.php) than we do.

**Third, social trends have robbed our work of much of the meaning it once had**. Few premodern people had “dream” jobs, but they didn’t have “[bullshit](https://www.amazon.ca/Bullshit-Jobs-Theory-David-Graeber/dp/150114331X)” jobs either. Work, even hard labor, fit into an understanding of the world that meant it could fill psychological needs. Divorced from that understanding, we oscillate between an unhealthy obsession with work and escape fantasies of perfect dream jobs or early retirement.

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Everyday-Energyl1-2.jpg)

These three forces mean that, despite lives of material abundance and apparent ease, many of us feel exhausted and apathetic.

## Energy Management is Key to Productivity and Well-Being

The forces that leave us exhausted and drained are much larger than we are. And nostalgia for a simpler time is a daydream, not a solution.

Even so, we can cultivate practices that restore the prior, more humane, ways to manage our energy.

**In the face of a world designed to drain our biological sources of energy, we can create new fuel.** We can cultivate deliberate practices of sleep, exercise, consumption and stress-management to restore our full capacity to live with energy and vigor.

**In the face of unrelenting pressure to work more, longer and harder, we can design a new flow.** We can craft rhythms of work and recovery that allow us to avoid exhaustion—while actually getting more done.

**In the face of unsatisfying jobs divorced from meaning, we can create work that allows us to flourish.** Neither a burden nor an obsession, we can choose work with greater meaning, and we can also cultivate an attitude that enriches the work we have already chosen.

These are not easy aims, and the overall cultural trends certainly do not help. But these aims are achievable, provided we take them seriously.
