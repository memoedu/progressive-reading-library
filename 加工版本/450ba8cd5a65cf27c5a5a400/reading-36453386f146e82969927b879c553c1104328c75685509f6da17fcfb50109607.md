# Zero-effort habits

---

## 正文

如果说有什么事让 self-improvement 狂热者念念不忘，那就是切实 sticking with 他们的 regimens。Life hacks 比比皆是：把你的 plan 告诉朋友，这样如果 fail，你就会觉得丢脸！务必保持 unbroken streak！如果你没有 stick to 它，就[向自己收钱](https://www.beeminder.com/)！把钱捐给[你最不喜欢的慈善机构](https://stickk.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/206833337-What-s-an-Anti-Charity-)！[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/zero/#fn:1) 先别说捐款了，试试 [electric shocks](https://pavlok.com/) 怎么样？

这些办法越来越荒唐，应该足以让你明白，这个 problem 仍是 unsolved。但在我看来，那是因为大家尝试的方式都不对。就我个人而言，我发现，只要确保自己的 productivity habits 是 _literally zero maintenance_，它们就能极其 long-lived。也就是说，到了该执行 habit 的时候，break the habit 所需的 willpower 比 keep going 更多。

literally-zero-maintenance 与并非 zero 之间的 delta 大得惊人，根本原因在于，没有人能可靠地拥有 willpower。[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/zero/#fn:2) 有时候，你需要 execute 你的 habit，却恰好 sleep-deprived，或正读一本特别好看的书读到一半，或伴侣刚刚和你分手，又或者不知为什么，你就是懒得尝试。这时候，唯一能挽救你的 habit 的情形，就是当你懒得费事时，发生的事恰好_就是_这个 habit。

Zero maintenance 很难 achieve，但一旦奏效，就非常棒。例如：

-   我一直想 track 自己的时间花在了哪里，但无论用哪种 time tracking apps 或 spreadsheets，都始终无法让自己足够勤勉地 record。后来，我发现了 [RescueTime](https://www.rescuetime.com/rp/ben)，而且确实觉得它 useful（至少有一次，它帮我 [increase my output by 50%](https://www.benkuhn.net/50pct)），因为它是 100% automatic：启动它，它就会 tracks 你使用的每一个 app 和 website，无须折腾。RescueTime 所 captures 的 data 并不能 perfectly track 我关心的事情，但每天都有完整的 data（而且是 unbiased），这一点重要得多。

-   我曾尝试用各种东西来 block distracting websites，但最后总会为了查看它们正在 blocking 的某些内容，而把它们关掉“一小会儿”，然后，呃，“忘记”再打开。后来我改用了 [Focus](http://heyfocus.com/)。Focus 通过按你选择的 schedule 来 activating and deactivating automatically（这样你就不必记得把它打开），并允许你在 active periods 内享有 limited number 的 timed “breaks”、在此期间可以使用 blocked websites，从而 solves 这个 problem。我已经用了 3+ 年，它仍能 effectively 让我免受许多 distractions 的影响，迫使我在确实想休息时做到 intentional，而且从未让我想过 disable 它。

-   我以前很难在 consistent time 醒来，于是买了会自动亮起的灯，以及一台可以设定在 pre-programmed time 开始冲煮咖啡的咖啡机。这意味着，每天早上 8:30am，default action 都是醒来，而不是继续睡觉。（严格说来，这并不是 zero maintenance，因为我晚上仍然得准备咖啡机，但它让我可以把 maintenance offload 到任何我觉得 high-willpower 的时候，而不是必须实际 execute the habit 的时候。）

-   我以前很难在 consistent time 上床睡觉，于是开始和一个会在我熬夜太晚时念叨我的人交往。[3](https://www.benkuhn.net/zero/#fn:3)

这些 systems 每个都已 lasted 2+ 年，因为到了 execution 的那一刻，它们只需要我付出 zero action 就能 sustain——default 就是我继续保持这个 habit。

如果你无法 achieve literal zero maintenance，那么，只要能把 maintenance effort _batched_ 到 high-energy periods，仍然会带来很大的不同。例如，我的 wake-up habit 要求我前一天准备好咖啡机，但那一天的任何时候都可以做。因此，我不必在醒来的时候有足够的 willpower，只需要在此前 24 小时内的_某个_时刻有足够的 willpower。如果那样还是太难，我还可以用胶囊咖啡机和一次性杯子，把 willpower 进一步 batched，只需定期订购新杯子就行。

## 原文

If there’s one thing that obsesses self-improvement junkies, it’s actually sticking with their regimens. Life hacks abound: Tell your friends about your plan so you’ll be embarrassed if you fail! Make sure you keep an unbroken streak! [Charge yourself money](https://www.beeminder.com/) if you don’t stick to it! Donate the money to [your least favorite charity](https://stickk.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/206833337-What-s-an-Anti-Charity-)![1](https://www.benkuhn.net/zero/#fn:1) Forget donations, how about [electric shocks](https://pavlok.com/)?

The escalating absurdity should tell you that this problem is unsolved. But in my opinion, that’s because everyone tries to do it wrong. Personally, I’ve found my productivity habits to be extremely long-lived when I make sure they’re _literally zero maintenance_. That is, when it’s habit time, it requires more willpower to break the habit than to keep going.

The delta between literally-zero-maintenance and not zero is staggeringly large, basically because nobody reliably has willpower.[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/zero/#fn:2) Sometimes you’ll need to execute your habit when you’re sleep-deprived, or in the middle of a really good book, or your significant other just broke up with you, or for whatever reason you just can’t be bothered to try. The only thing that can save your habit here, is if the habit _is_ what happens when you can’t be bothered.

Zero maintenance is hard to achieve, but awesome when it works. For instance:

-   I always wanted to track how I spent my time, but I could never make myself record it diligently enough in any of the time tracking apps or spreadsheets. But then I discovered [RescueTime](https://www.rescuetime.com/rp/ben), which I actually found useful (helping me [increase my output by 50%](https://www.benkuhn.net/50pct) at least once) because it’s 100% automatic: fire it up and it tracks every app and website you use with no fiddling. The data RescueTime captures doesn’t perfectly track what I care about, but it’s fully there (and unbiased) every day, which is way more important.

-   I tried to use various things to block distracting websites, but I always ended up turning them off “for a little bit” to look at something they were blocking and, uh, “forgetting” to turn them back on. Then I switched to [Focus](http://heyfocus.com/). Focus solves this problem by activating and deactivating automatically, on a schedule of your choice (so you don’t need to remember to turn it on), and allowing you a limited number of timed “breaks” during active periods where you can use blocked websites. I’ve been using it for 3+ years and it still effectively saves me from a lot of distractions, forces me to be intentional when I do want to take breaks, and has never made me want to disable it.

-   I used to have trouble waking up at a consistent time, so I bought lights that would automatically turn on and a coffee machine that could be set to start brewing coffee at a pre-programmed time. This meant that at 8:30am every morning the default action was to wake up instead of keep sleeping. (This is technically not zero maintenance since I still have to prepare the coffee machine at night, but it allows me to offload the maintenance to whenever I’m feeling high-willpower rather than when I have to actually execute the habit.)

-   I used to have trouble going to bed at a consistent time, so I started dating someone who would pester me if I stayed up too late.[3](https://www.benkuhn.net/zero/#fn:3)

Each of these systems has lasted 2+ years, because at the moment of execution they require zero action from me to sustain—the default is for me to keep having the habit.

If you can’t achieve literal zero maintenance, it still makes a big difference if the maintenance effort can be _batched_ to high-energy periods. For instance, my wake-up habit required me to prepare my coffee machine the day before, but I could do it any time that day. So instead of needing to have enough willpower when I was waking up, I just needed to have enough willpower at _some_ point in the last 24 hours. If that had been too hard, I could have used a capsule machine and disposable cups, and batched the willpower even more, to just ordering new cups periodically.
