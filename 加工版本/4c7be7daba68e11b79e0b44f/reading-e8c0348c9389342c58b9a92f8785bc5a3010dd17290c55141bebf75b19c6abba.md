# The Entrepreneurial State: The case of the iPhone (I)

## 正文

\[本文是[我的另一篇文章](https://nintil.com/2015/03/19/mazzucato-y-el-iphone-i/)的译文\]

Mazzucato 的书 The Entrepreneurial State 中最重要的 example，大概就是 iPhone 的那个：她甚至专门用了一整章来讲这个 example。

对 Apple 的 device 如此关注是有道理的：它选取了一个被视为 entrepreneurial creativity、garage entrerepeneur 的努力以及 capitalism 的胜利的典型 examples，然后将其 subverts，让我们看到 State 的 visible hand 一直在其背后，却不公正地没有从中 reapt benefits。

在这篇初步文章中，我会汇集网上谈论 Mazzucato 的 example 的文章，试着了解人们是如何理解它的。我还会纳入这本书的作者所作的 presentations，以便更精确地把握她的用词，以及她如何在自己的 explanatory framework 中 frames 这个 example。

在下一篇文章中，我会 criticise 书中与这个 example 有关的 arguments。

阅读本文前先提醒一句：本文不作 judge。请不要这样 interpret 它。它只是呈现 information。对 arguments 和 facts 的 Assesment 会放在其他文章中。如果你好奇，我并不否认这样的 claims：至少有一些人，对 State intervention 如何 shaped technological advancement 的进程所知不多；我也不否认，不谈 State 就无法理解 History of Technology。也就是说，正如我们之后会看到、但不会在本文中看到的，我所 dispute 的并非总体上的 facts -其中有些也会被 disputed-，而是从中得出的 interpretation 和 conclusions。

我们先从 Bruce Upbin 开始，他在 2013 年[在 Forbes 写道](http://www.forbes.com/sites/bruceupbin/2013/06/13/debunking-the-narrative-of-silicon-valleys-innovation-might/)

> 她用于 myth-debunking 的 case study 是 iPhone，这个 American corporate innovation 的 icon。它的每一项 core technologies–capacitive sensors、solid-state memory、click wheel、GPS、internet、cellular communications、Siri、microchips、touchscreen—都来自 U.S. government 和 military 的 research efforts 与 funding support。public 得到 iPhone dividend 了吗？并没有。Mazzucato 说，那些“求知若饥，虚心若愚”的天才带着 gains 跑了，而这家公司如今又因没有 paying 足够的 taxes，或没有在美国 creating 足够的 high-wage jobs 而受到批评。Apple 五年间的 R&D spending 占 sales 的 percentage 一直在 2% 至 3% 左右，而 Nokia 和 Samsung Electronics 等 companies 的 spend 则分别是 9% 和 8%。Steve Jobs 真正的天才之处不在于 developing new technology，而在于 integrating 在别处 invented、往往由 tax dollars backed 的 technologies。

接下来是一篇 Mazzucato 本人在 [INET](http://ineteconomics.org/new-economic-thinking/mariana-mazzucato-government-risk-and-private-sector-reward) 上的文章

> 看看 Apple 的 iPhone 和 Google 的 search engine。在这两个 cases 中，这些极受欢迎的 consumer products 都从 state intervention 中 benefitted mightily。对于 iPhone，使它及类似 devices 变得“smart”的许多 revolutionary technologies，都是由 U.S. government funded 的，例如 global positioning system（或 GPS）、touchscreen display，以及 voice-activated personal assistant Siri。而对于 Google，其 algorithm 的 creation 是由 National Science Foundation funded 的。当然，还有 Internet 的 development，这是另一个 government funded venture，它使 iPhone 能够成为有价值的 tool，也使 Google searches 成为可能。

Jeff Madrick 在 [The NY Review of Books，2014 年](http://www.nybooks.com/articles/archives/2014/apr/24/innovation-government-was-crucial-after-all/)写道

> 例如，她详细 shows，尽管 Steve Jobs 出色地 imagined 并 designed 了有吸引力的新 commercial products，但 iPod、iPhone 和 iPad 所 based 的几乎所有 scientific research，都是由欧洲和美国的 government-backed scientists 与 engineers 完成的。具体来说，如今在 Apple products 中如此常见的 touch-screen technology，是 based on 欧洲和美国 government-funded labs 在 1960s 和 1970s 开展的 research。$$…$$ 后来的这些 breakthroughs 几乎完全 dependent on government-sponsored research。“虽然这些 products 的 beautiful design 和 slick integration 应归功于 Jobs 及其庞大团队的天才，”Mazzucato 写道，但 iPod、iPhone 和 iPad 中几乎每一项 state-of-the-art technology，都是 government 和 military 的 research efforts 与 funding support 所取得、却常常被忽视和无视的 achievement。一项名为 giant magnetoresistance 的重大 government-funded discovery，为其两位欧洲 inventors 赢得了 Nobel Prize in physics，就是这种 support 的一个很能说明问题的 example。这个 process 会 enlarges computers 及较新的 electronic devices 的 storage capacity。在 Nobel 颁奖典礼的一次演讲中，Royal Swedish Academy of Science 的一位成员解释说，这项 breakthrough 使 iPod 成为可能。Mazzucato 写道，Apple 的其他重大 developments 也在 federal research 中有其“roots”，其中包括 iPhone 的 global positioning system，以及它的 voice-activated personal assistant Siri。

还是 Mazzucato，她在 2014 年的 [FT Alphaville](http://ftalphaville.ft.com/2014/07/18/1901312/mission-finance-starting-to-think-big-again/) 中写道

> iPhone 是一个很好的 example。这张 chart shows，使它如此 smart 的每一项 technology，其 funding 都可 traces back 到 US government 中某个 mission-oriented public agency；这个 government 喜欢假装自己相信 free market，实际上却一直是历史上最 interventionist 的 government 之一

这里有一张如今已经广为人知的 figure - 如果你一直关注这本书的讨论的话 - 它 summarises 了 iPhone 中 key technologies 的 origin

[![幻灯片1](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2015/08/19-1.png)](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2015/08/19-1.png)

同样是 Mazzucato，她在 2013 年的 Slate 上发表了一篇文章，标题如下：[“Entrepreneurs Drive New Technology 是一个 Myth。真正的 innovation，要感谢 state。”](http://www.slate.com/articles/health_and_science/new_scientist/2013/09/entrepreneurs_or_the_state_innovation_comes_from_public_investment.html)

> Apple 是一个绝佳的 example。在早期，这家公司通过一笔 \$500,000 的 small-business investment company grant，获得了 government cash support。而使 iPhone 成为 smartphone 的每一项 technology，其 vision 和 funding 都应归功于 state：Internet、GPS、touch-screen displays，甚至 voice-activated smartphone assistant Siri，都获得过 state cash。[U.S. Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency](http://www.newscientist.com/article/mg20427335.700-darpa-inventing-this-side-of-the-impossible.html) 为 Internet 提供了资金，而 CIA 和 military 则 funded GPS。因此，尽管 United States 被向我们宣传为通过 private enterprise 实现 progress 的典范，那里的 innovation 却 benefited from 一个非常 interventionist 的 state。

今年，这位 Sussex 的 economist [在 twitter 上](https://twitter.com/mazzucatom/status/558674794447925250)表示，她的 example shows，“使 iPhone 变得 smart 的每一项 technology 都是 publicly financed 的”

在这段来自 2014 年[一次 conference](http://speri.dept.shef.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Extract_Mariana_Mazzucato_NSSPERI_Lecture.pdf)的摘录中，她的 claims 稍微收敛了一些，

> 是什么使 iPhone 如此 smart？仅仅是 Steve Jobs 及其团队的天才，以及 risk-loving venture capitalists 提供的 visionary finance 吗？不是。在我的书 The Entrepreneurial State: Debunking Public v Private Sector Myths 中，我通过 analysing 那些使 smartphone 得以 created 的 public funds，讲述了这个故事中缺失的部分。使 internet、touch-screen displays、GPS 和 Siri voice control 成为可能的 research programmes，全都有 government backing。重点不是要贬低 Jobs 及其团队的工作，他们的工作既 essential，又 transformational。但我们必须在 Apple 及其 founders 的 historiography 中更加 balanced，其中对 Silicon Valley 背后的 collective effort 竟只字未提。问题是：今天，对 hi-tech sector 的 wealth-creation process 作出如此狭隘的 description，究竟让谁 benefits？

她在 2013 年的 [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2013/03/taxpayers-helped-apple-but-app) 中，强调了 corporations 的所作所为、它们使用的 rhetoric，以及它们为什么应当 pay 更多 taxes 来 finance Mazzucato 所谈的那些 state innovations 之间的 relation，

> 使 iPhone 及其他 products 和 services 变得“smart”的许多 revolutionary technologies，都是由 U.S. government funded 的。例如 Internet、GPS、touchscreen display，以及最新的 voice-activated personal assistant Siri。而 Apple 并非只是从 government-funded research activities 中 benefit。它还通过 U.S. government 的 [Small Business Investment Company](http://www.sba.gov/INV) program 获得了 early stage finance。Venture capitalists 只是在 government funding 已让这家公司达到关键的 proof of concept 之后才 entered。$$…$$ 这也与 Apple、Google 及其他 technology companies 为 avoid paying taxes 而采取的措施形成鲜明对比。Apple 在 Nevada 州的 Reno set up 了一家 subsidiary；这个州没有 corporate income 或 capital gains tax，Apple 将一部分 U.S. sales channeled 到那里，[据报道 saving 了 \$2.5 billion 的 taxes](http://www.nytimes.com/2012/04/29/business/apples-tax-strategy-aims-at-low-tax-states-and-nations.html)。$$…$$ 然而，没有 U.S. government，就根本无法设想这样的 capitalism；它通过 DARPA 及其他 initiatives，在 funding 真正的 innovation 方面取得了惊人出色的 track record，在世界范围内脱颖而出。其中包括 government 最近引以为傲的成绩：它对（有争议的）shale gas 和 fracking technologies 坚定不移的 financial support，这一 support [始于三十多年前](https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1144&dat=19771211&id=HOYdAAAAIBAJ&sjid=7FcEAAAAIBAJ&pg=6894,6038621)，当时是除此之外饱受诟病的 Carter Administration 执政时期。$$…$$ 对 American taxpayer 来说，一个更大的问题是，这种 support 是否 leads to 一个“parasitic”的 innovation eco-system。看看 Apple。尽管它直接从 taxpayer-funded technologies 中 benefitting，却 strategically 对过去一直直接 depended 的 tax purse 进行了“underfunded”。

在 2014 年接受西班牙报纸 [El País](http://elpais.com/elpais/2014/12/08/ciencia/1418039790_527615.html) 的一次采访中，采访标题是“Revolutionary innovations 总是来自 public sector”

> 我的 position 并不是说：一切都必须来自 State。显然，Steve Jobs 和 Apple 非常重要。但这一点我们已经知道了。我们每天都听到这样的话。我想做的是，通过讲述另一种 point of view，让 History 的叙述变得均衡。并不是说 private sector 不重要。但它得以乘上一股由 State financed 的巨大 technological wave，将 existing technology 与一种重要的 design 意识，以及一种新颖酷炫的 marketing 这个 product 的方式 cojoining 在一起。这些大家都知道。Steve Job 的传记有 80% 都在讲这些。我并不要求一个章节，至少给 public 这一面留一页就足够了。$$...$$ 并不能 guaranteed 每一笔 expenditure 都会 successful。Innovation 始终是一个 uncertain process。事实上，innovators 大多数时候都会 fail。然而，在这个 process 中，learning 会发生。必须在 government 内部 created 能够 learn 的 Organisms，同时制定 flexible corporate policies，并使其 adapted towards exploration。还要有 correct indicatos 来 value 每一项 investment。要做到这一点，需要聪明的人。我并不是说 public sector 没有这样的人。但如果 public sector 不断受到批评，就会越来越难吸引最聪明的人。除了一些例外——在 Singapore，public servants 的 paid 可高达每年 one million dollars！——仅凭金钱将很难吸引最优秀的人，必须把这件事变成一种荣誉。而只有当你有能力在自己的 field 中开创新天地时，才能做到这一点。因此，在 US，像 Steven Chu 这样的 Nobel Prize 得主接受 lead Department of Energy 的职责，是非常重要的$$...$$ causes productivity gowth 的是 R&D spending...

Martin Wolf（世界上最负盛名的 economic analysts 之一）[于 2013 年](https://www.ft.com/intl/cms/s/2/32ba9b92-efd4-11e2-a237-00144feabdc0.html#axzz3UpUxC15q)为 FT 撰文

> 一个或许更有力的 example 是 information and communications revolution。US National Science Foundation funded 了 drove [Google](https://www.ft.com/topics/organisations/Google_Inc "Google news headlines - FT.com") 的 search engine 的 algorithm。[Apple](http://markets.ft.com/tearsheets/performance.asp?s=us:AAPL) 的早期 funding 来自 US government 的 Small Business Investment Company。此外，“所有使 iPhone 变得 ‘smart’ 的 technologies 也都是 state-funded……internet、wireless networks、global positioning system、microelectronics、touchscreen displays，以及当时最新的 voice-activated SIRI personal assistant。”Apple 出色地 put this together。但它所做的是采摘 seven decades 的 state-supported innovation 的果实。$$…$$ 为什么 state 的 role 如此重要？答案在于 fundamental、science-based innovation 所伴随的巨大 uncertainties、time spans 和 costs。Private companies 不能、也不会 bear 这些 costs，部分是因为它们无法确定自己能否收获果实，部分是因为这些果实要到非常遥远的未来才会出现。$$..$$ AT&T 那个 path-breaking Bell Labs 的时代早已过去。无论如何，private sector 都不可能 created internet 或 GPS。只有 US military 拥有这么做所需的 resources。$$…$$ Mazzucato 喜欢戳破关于 risk-loving venture capital 和 risk-avoiding bureaucrats 的 myths。state 的 role 被从叙述中抹去，这有关系吗？她认为有。首先，policy makers 越来越相信 state 只是障碍这一 myth，从而 depriving innovation 所需的 support，也 depriving humanity 实现 prosperity 的最佳 prospects。事实上，对 government 的蔑视也 deprives 它承担 entrepreneurial risks 的 will 和 capacity。其次，government 也越来越接受由自己 funds risks、而 private sector reaps the rewards 的安排。那么，正在形成的并不是一个真正 symbiotic 的 innovation ecosystem，而是一个 parasitic 的 ecosystem，其中最 lossmaking 的 elements 被 socialised，而 profitmaking 的 elements 则大多被 privatised。普通 taxpayers 是否明白，他们的 taxes 正在 fund 那些 drive 其 economy 的 fundamental innovations？这本书提出了一个 controversial thesis。但它基本上是正确的。未能认识到 government 在 driving innovation 中的 role，很可能是 rising prosperity 面临的最大 threat。

Mark Buchanan 于 2013 年[进一步说，iPhone 中的每一项重要 technology](http://thebreakthrough.org/index.php/programs/economic-growth/where-the-iphone-came-from)都来自 state investments

> 即使是 Apple——通常被视为[Steve Jobs](http://topics.bloomberg.com/steve-jobs/)这位孤独天才的独一无二的创造——也在很大程度上得益于 government。Mazzucato 指出，Apple 在早期从 US government 的 small-business investment program 获得了 crucial finance。Apple 的 smart products（包括 iPhone 和 iPad）中，每一项最重要的 technologies 都是在别处 developed 的，而且在很大程度上得益于 state funding。

Mazzucato 在 2014 年的一份[提交给 OECD 的 report](http://marianamazzucato.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/MAZZUCATO-Brochure.pdf)中说

> 让我简要举一个 iPhone 背后 technology 的 example，直到不久前，iPhone 还代表着 Silicon Valley 的象征。Steve Jobs 是个天才，他所做的是以一种非常酷的方式 put together existing technology，并深知 simplicity 和 good design 有多么重要。iPhone 上所有 innovative 和 smart features——internet、GPS、touch screen display、Siri feature——都是由 government funded 的。Apple 本身在早期获得了一个 500,000 dollar 的 Small Business Investment Company (SBIC) programme。这个 iPhone table（就是我们刚才看到的那个\[这是我的话，不是 Mazzucato 的\]）shows，进入这部手机的 technology originated from 众多 government agencies，例如 CIA、DARPA 和 Department of Defense。

Andrew Warner 于 2014 年在 [The Long Now Foundation](http://blog.longnow.org/02014/03/10/mariana-mazzucato-seminar-primer/) 写道

> Mariana Mazzucato 的 research shows，构成我们 technological revolutions 支柱的许多 technologies，都是 state 开展 multi-decade research 的 direct result。看看 computers、internet 和 GPS 这些 examples——所有这些 technologies 在 entering consumer market 之前，都由 government developed 和 funded 了数十年；没有这些 technologies，就不可能想象出一部 iphone。

Greg Satell 于 2014 年在 Harvard Business Review 写道

## 原文

\[This post is translation of [this other post of mine](https://nintil.com/2015/03/19/mazzucato-y-el-iphone-i/)\]

The most important example in Mazzucato's book The Entrepreneurial State is probably that of the iPhone: She goes as far as dedicating one entire chapter only to that example.

The fixation with Apple's device is justified: It takes one of the typical examples of supposed entrepreneurial creativity, an effort of garage entrerepeneur, and a triumph of capitalism, and subverts it, making us se that the visible hand of the State has been behind it, without having, unjustly, reapt benefits from it.

In this preliminar article, I will compile articles found in the net where Mazzucato's example is talked about, to try to capture how people have understood it. I will also include presentations from the book's author to add more precision to the type of words, and the way she frames the example within her explanatory framework.

In the next article, I will criticise the arguments presented in the book related to the example.

A cautionary note before reading the article: This article does not judge. Do not interpret it as such. It just presents information. Assesment of the arguments and facts come in other posts. If you are curious about it, I'm not denying the claims that, at least among some people, there is not much knowledge of how State intervention has shaped the course of technological advancement, and I don't deny either that you can't understand the History of Technology without talking about the State. That is, what I dispute, as we will see later on but not in this post, are not the facts in general -Some of them will be disputed too-, but the interpretation and conclusions drawn from them.

Let us begin with Bruce Upbin, who in 2013 [wrote in Forbes](http://www.forbes.com/sites/bruceupbin/2013/06/13/debunking-the-narrative-of-silicon-valleys-innovation-might/)

> Her case study for myth-debunking is the iPhone, that icon of American corporate innovation. Each of its core technologies–capacitive sensors, solid-state memory, the click wheel, GPS, internet, cellular communications, Siri, microchips, touchscreen—came from research efforts and funding support of the U.S. government and military. Did the public see an iPhone dividend? Not really. The “stay foolish, stay hungry” geniuses ran away with the gains, says Mazzucato, and now the company is under fire for not paying enough taxes or creating enough high-wage jobs in the U.S. Apple’s five-year R&D spending as a percentage of sales has hovered around 2% to 3%, while companies such as Nokia and Samsung Electronics spend 9% and 8%, respectively. Steve Jobs’ real genius was not in developing new technology but integrating technologies invented somewhere else, often backed by tax dollars.

We follow with an article by Mazzucato herself in [INET](http://ineteconomics.org/new-economic-thinking/mariana-mazzucato-government-risk-and-private-sector-reward)

> Consider Apple’s iPhone and Google’s search engine. In both cases these extremely popular consumer products benefitted mightily from state intervention. For the iPhone, many of the revolutionary technologies that make it and similar devices “smart” were funded by the U.S. government, such as the global positioning system (or GPS), the touchscreen display, and the voice-activated personal assistant, Siri. And for Google, the creation of its algorithm was funded by the National Science Foundation. Plus, of course, there’s the development of the Internet, another government funded venture, which enables the iPhone to be a valuable tool and makes Google searches possible.

Jeff Madrick in [The NY Review of Books, 2014](http://www.nybooks.com/articles/archives/2014/apr/24/innovation-government-was-crucial-after-all/)

> For example, she shows in detail that, while Steve Jobs brilliantly imagined and designed attractive new commercial products, almost all the scientific research on which the iPod, iPhone, and iPad were based was done by government-backed scientists and engineers in Europe and America. The touch-screen technology, specifically, now so common to Apple products, was based on research done at government-funded labs in Europe and the US in the 1960s and 1970s. \[…\] These later breakthroughs were almost completely dependent on government-sponsored research. “While the products owe their beautiful design and slick integration to the genius of Jobs and his large team,” writes Mazzucato, nearly every state-of-the-art technology found in the iPod, iPhone and iPad is an often overlooked and ignored achievement of the research efforts and funding support of the government and military. A major government-funded discovery known as giant magnetoresistance, which won its two European inventors a Nobel Prize in physics, is a telling example of such support. The process enlarges the storage capacity of computers and more recent electronic devices. In a speech at the Nobel ceremony, a member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Science explained that this breakthrough made the iPod possible. Other major developments by Apple also had their “roots” in federal research, among them, Mazzucato writes, the global positioning system of the iPhone and Siri, its voice-activated personal assistant.

Mazzucato again, writes in 2014 in [FT Alphaville](http://ftalphaville.ft.com/2014/07/18/1901312/mission-finance-starting-to-think-big-again/)

> The iPhone is a good example. This chart shows how every technology that makes it so smart, traces its funding back to a mission-oriented public agency in the US government which likes to pretend it believes in the free market when actually it has been one of the most interventionist in history

Here we find a by now well known figure - if you have followed the discussion of the book, that is - that summarises the origin of the key technologies present in the iPhone

[![Slide1](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2015/08/19-1.png)](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2015/08/19-1.png)

Mazzucato again in Slate, 2013,  had an article running with the following title [“It’s a Myth that Entrepreneurs Drive New Technology. For real innovation, thank the state.”](http://www.slate.com/articles/health_and_science/new_scientist/2013/09/entrepreneurs_or_the_state_innovation_comes_from_public_investment.html)

> Apple is a perfect example. In its early stages, the company received government cash support via a $500,000 small-business investment company grant. And every technology that makes the iPhone a smartphone owes its vision and funding to the state: the Internet, GPS, touch-screen displays, and even the voice-activated smartphone assistant Siri all received state cash.[The U.S. Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency](http://www.newscientist.com/article/mg20427335.700-darpa-inventing-this-side-of-the-impossible.html) bankrolled the Internet, and the CIA and the military funded GPS. So, although the United States is sold to us as the model example of progress through private enterprise, innovation there has benefited from a very interventionist state.

This year, the Sussex economist said [in twitter](https://twitter.com/mazzucatom/status/558674794447925250)that her example shows that "every technology that makes the iPhone smart was publicly financed"

In this excerpt from[a conference](http://speri.dept.shef.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Extract_Mariana_Mazzucato_NSSPERI_Lecture.pdf) in 2014 she is somewhat less ambitious in her claims,

> What makes the iPhone so smart? Was it only the genius of Steve Jobs and his team and the visionary finance supplied from risk-loving venture capitalists? No. In my book The Entrepreneurial State: Debunking Public v Private Sector Myths, I tell the missing part of that story by analysing the public funds that allowed the smartphone to be created. The research programmes that made the internet, touch-screen displays, GPS and the Siri voice control possible all had government backing. The point is not to belittle the work of Jobs and his team, which was both essential and transformational. But we must be more balanced in the historiography of Apple and its founders, where not a word is mentioned of the collective effort behind Silicon Valley. The question is this: who benefits from such a narrow description of the wealth-creation process in the hi-tech sector today?

In [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2013/03/taxpayers-helped-apple-but-app), 2013, she emphasised the relation between what corporations do, the rhetoric they use, and why they should pay more taxes to finance those state innovations Mazzucato talks about,

> Many of the revolutionary technologies that make the iPhone and other products and services “smart” were funded by the U.S. government. Take, for instance, the Internet, GPS, touchscreen display, as well as the latest voice-activated personal assistant, Siri. And Apple did not just benefit from government-funded research activities. It also received its early stage finance from the U.S. government’s [Small Business Investment Company](http://www.sba.gov/INV) program. Venture capitalists entered only after government funding had gotten the company to the critical proof of concept. \[…\] This also stands in stark contrast to the steps that Apple, Google, and other technology companies take to avoid paying taxes. Consider Apple. Despite benefitting directly from taxpayer-funded technologies, it has strategically “underfunded” the tax purse on which it has in the past directly depended.

Martin Wolf (one of the world's most prestigious economic analysts) writes [in 2013](https://www.ft.com/intl/cms/s/2/32ba9b92-efd4-11e2-a237-00144feabdc0.html#axzz3UpUxC15q) for FT

> A perhaps even more potent example is the information and communications revolution. The US National Science Foundation funded the algorithm that drove [Google](https://www.ft.com/topics/organisations/Google_Inc "Google news headlines - FT.com")’s search engine. Early funding for [Apple](http://markets.ft.com/tearsheets/performance.asp?s=us:AAPL) came from the US government’s Small Business Investment Company. Moreover, “All the technologies which make the iPhone ‘smart’ are also state-funded … the internet, wireless networks, the global positioning system, microelectronics, touchscreen displays and the latest voice-activated SIRI personal assistant.” Apple put this together, brilliantly. But it was gathering the fruit of seven decades of state-supported innovation. \[…\] Why is the state’s role so important? The answer lies in the huge uncertainties, time spans and costs associated with fundamental, science-based innovation. Private companies cannot and will not bear these costs, partly because they cannot be sure to reap the fruits and partly because these fruits lie so far in the future. \[..\] The days of AT&T’s path-breaking Bell Labs are long gone. In any case, the private sector could not have created the internet or GPS. Only the US military had the resources to do so. \[…\] Mazzucato loves puncturing myths about risk-loving venture capital and risk-avoiding bureaucrats. Does it matter that the role of the state has been written out of the story? She argues that it does. First, policy makers increasingly believe the myth that the state is only an obstacle, thereby depriving innovation of support and humanity of its best prospects for prosperity. Indeed, the scorn heaped on government also deprives it of the will and capacity to take entrepreneurial risks. Second, government has also increasingly accepted that it funds the risks, while the private sector reaps the rewards. What is emerging, then, is not a truly symbiotic ecosystem of innovation, but a parasitic one, in which the most lossmaking elements are socialised, while the profitmaking ones are largely privatised. Do ordinary taxpayers understand that their taxes fund the fundamental innovations that drive their economy? This book has a controversial thesis. But it is basically right. The failure to recognise the role of the government in driving innovation may well be the greatest threat to rising prosperity.
