## 正文

> 按照大多数现有 criteria，United States 在 basic and applied research 的 performance 方面仍是 undisputed leader（National Academy of Sciences，[2007](https://www.nap.edu/read/11463/chapter/5)）

自 1969 年以来，US-based scientists 赢得的 (Science) Nobel Prizes 比其他所有 countries 加起来还要[多](https://www.statista.com/chart/19646/science-nobel-prizes-by-country-and-immigrant-share/)。虽然这个 metric 只是众多 metric 之一，而其他 metric，尤其是那些测量时间更[近](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-020-01231-w)的，并未显示出 American science 如此 overwhelming dominance，但总体情况是清楚的。

我刚读完的一本书 [Markets, Minds, and Money](https://www.amazon.com/Markets-Minds-Money-University-Research/dp/0674244230/ref=sr_1_3?dchild=1&keywords=markets+minds+money&qid=1628775579&sr=8-3) 试图解释其中的原因。简而言之，书中的 explanation 是：American academic system 具有其他 countries 所缺乏的 free-market orientation。作者声称，Europe rigidly imposes 一套自上而下的 centralized curriculum，并试图在 Europe 内部各 regions 以及各 nations 之间 distribute funds evenly；相比之下，US 让 most productive scientists 获得更多 resources，即使这会 leads to 极其 unequal funding distribution（而如果 Newton [hypothesis](https://nintil.com/newton-hypothesis) 是 true，这实际上是一件大好事）。

不过，也可以尝试指出其他能够 explain US 的 success 的 factors，作者也考虑过其中一些 alternative explanations。例如，US 之所以成为这样的 science powerhouse，或许是因为相当一部分 [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Paperclip)（WWII 之后）和 [Soviet](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2012/HSIf12/Ganguli.pdf)（USSR collapse 之后）scientists 把那里作为目的地。这当然有所贡献，但在那之前，US 就已经在走向 scientific preeminence：到 1920s，US Academia 已开始 eclipse 世界其他地方：

![image-20210812101123867](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812101123867.png)

这与 Nobel Prize winners 是 American 或 US-based foreign scientists 并不是同一回事；后者在 WWII 之前就已开始，随后又因 [attracting](https://s3.amazonaws.com/chssweb/documents/24957/original/Nobel_Brief_2017_%281%29.pdf?1496674936) top global talent 而得到了额外推动：

![image-20210812101431659](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812101431659.png)

一张类似但更详细的 figure，来自[这里](https://www.hceres.fr/sites/default/files/media/downloads/rappScien_VA_web04.pdf)：

![image-20210814134316161](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210814134316161.png)

有一张 chart 显示，相比其他 countries，[inventors](https://www.wipo.int/publications/en/details.jsp?id=3952&plang=EN) 往往 disproportionately migrate 到 US。我没能找到一张针对广义 scientists 的类似 chart（Nobel prizes 除外），但如果它看起来非常相似，我也不会感到意外：

![image-20210812095645458](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812095645458.png)

我们看到这种情况的原因，可能是 American system 的一种 intrinsic advantage，也就是书中描述的 free-market orientation（scientist 有更多机会从事自己想做的 research？），但也可能是其他 factors。例如，假设 US transitions 到一种 European-style（German-style？）scientific ecosystem。其他一切都保持不变，它仍然拥有两个可能 explain 所观察到的 patterns 的 factors：population 和 wealth。仅仅是能够 finance 数量更多、更加 expensive 的 experiments，并 pay 更高的 salaries，就可能 explain US success 的一部分。

这些 factors 中的第一个（wealth）并未在原 paper 中得到讨论；因此，Michael Nielsen 对 overall result 持怀疑态度（参见[这里](https://twitter.com/michael_nielsen/status/1416054022872645633)的推文）（这完全是应该 control for 的东西！）。我原以为这本书或许会考虑这一点。虽然他们尝试了，但我认为做得并不令人满意。

书中的 appendix 考虑了这一点，并提供了一些按 income 进行 normalized 的 numbers：

![image-20210812105749753](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812105749753.png)

不过，这里或许更应该改为 control for higher education 中的 salaries。在保留这一 caveat 的前提下，这个 pattern holds。

如果 controls for population，同样的情况也成立，只是程度较弱；

![image-20210812105852262](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812105852262.png)

UK 设法在一定程度上保持 competitive，而 France 和 Germany 则逐渐在 rankings 中下滑。即使在这里，我们想要的也不是笼统地 control by population，而是 control for 每个 country 的 scientists 的 number；不过，scientists 的 number 是 endogenous to scientific system 本身的 performance 的：success 会 attract 更多 success。在保留这一 caveat 的前提下，下面的 [image](https://www.hceres.fr/sites/default/files/media/downloads/rappScien_VA_web04.pdf) accounts for researchers，其结果基本 matches Urquiola 的说法：US 和 UK（他说 UK 具有类似的 academic setup）处于 leading 地位（暂且不考虑获颁 Nobels 的 number 很少的 countries）。这里一个有趣的 outlier 是 Israel（在 PhD level 上），但那些 Israelis 中似乎有很多人最终在 US 工作。

![image-20210814133748750](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210814133748750.png)

不过，如果同时 controlled for 两者呢？尽管这是一种 crude measure，但 economics 中通常存在一种超出 inputs 的 productivity 概念（Total Factor Productivity）。这里我们可以 define 一个 simple model，其中 scientific productivity（以 total citations，或者就以 Urquiola 自己的 mention-based metric 作为 proxied）depends on R&D spending 与 researchers 的 total number 的 product，再 times 一个 residual。这个 residual 的 evolution 就能让我们得到 productivity。虽然 US academic system 的 structured 方式似乎确实可能构成一种 advantage，但我不太确定，一旦我们 control for immigration 和更多 available funding，US 的 lead 是否仍然如此 dramatic。对于 require 更多 funds 的 fields，这一点应该更是如此。

或许也可以说，要 explaining US 的 success，以及其 rise 在 WWII 之前就已开始这一事实，需要 multiple factors。首先，可能有一个简单的 catchup 过程：US science 当时正在 developing，以 match Europe，这就 explaining 了最初的 boost；然后，我们再把 WWII 对 European science 的 effects 作为一种 one-off effect 加进去，之后再加上自那时起不断有少量 researchers immigrating 到 US 的影响。

书中对 China 的 rise 讨论不多，不过确实承认了这一点：

> 因为只有 winners 获奖后，universities 才能在他们的 biographies 中被 mentioned，所以我们的 measure 会 penalizes 那些“正在崛起”的 schools。例如，近几十年来，China 和 South Korea 等 countries 已对各自的 universities 进行了 invested。如果这些 universities 如今正在 training outstanding students，那么还需要数年，它们才会出现在 Nobel biographies 中。不过，我们针对近几十年的 re- results，与那些使用 bibliographical data 的 rankings 相当吻合，而这些 data 很可能更迅速地 reflect progress。此外，本书的重点并非近期的发展，而是 American research universities 如何 rise to dominance。

这话有道理，但如果事实证明，a) China 的 academic productivity 正在 rapidly increasing，而 b) Chinese academic system 并不是 free-market oriented，那么这本书的 thesis 就会大大 weakened。往最好说，它只能说明，_在 history 中的某个特定时期_，American university structure 是有用的。往最坏说，这就是对 null hypothesis 的一种 vindication：仅凭 money 和 minds 就能 explain scientific success 的大部分。

自 early 2000s 以来，China [granted](https://www.aip.org/fyi/2018/rapid-rise-china%E2%80%99s-stem-workforce-charted-national-science-board-report) 的 Science & Engineering (S&E) bachelor degrees 比 US 更多，doctorates 也几乎一样多。China 很快就将在 R&D 上 spending 比 US [更多](https://www.economist.com/briefing/2021/01/16/the-case-for-more-state-spending-on-r-and-d)的钱（按 absolute terms 计算）。到 late 2000s，China produces 的 scientific articles 已比 US 更多（但不比 European Union 更多）。如今，China-to-US brain drain 正在 slowing down（也许现在已经结束；不过并非每个 field 都如此，参见 [AI](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/06/09/technology/china-ai-research-education.html) 的例子），而 top Chinese talent 更[经常](https://infoproc.blogspot.com/2004/12/brain-drain-slowdown.html)留在 China，我们或许也将开始在 Nobel Prize citations 中看到 China 的 rise。根据[各种](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nickmorrison/2020/09/02/oxford-keeps-top-spot-but-china-is-the-real-winner-as-us-declines/) [rankings](https://www.economist.com/international/2018/05/19/how-global-university-rankings-are-changing-higher-education)，Chinese universities 正越来越频繁地出现在 top universities 之中。

![global university rankings 如何改变 higher education | The Economist](https://www.economist.com/sites/default/files/images/2018/05/articles/body_5)

那么，Chinese universities 是否像 US 一样，在 effectively matching money with minds？还是在 sorting outsmart students？对于后一点，我愿意大胆地说，大概是的。US 的 SAT 似乎比 China 的 Gaokao 更弱。

China 是否已经在任何 field 达到了 leading status？我们还无法从 Nobel Prizes 中看到这一点，但它比实际 research 滞后数十年。在 AI 中，答案是：正在接近（以 top [publications](https://www.theverge.com/2019/3/14/18265230/china-is-about-to-overtake-america-in-ai-research) 衡量；但请参见关于“Best Paper awards”的评论），以及还没有（以 papers 被 [accepted](https://macropolo.org/digital-projects/the-global-ai-talent-tracker/) 到 leading [conferences](https://medium.com/criteo-engineering/neurips-2020-comprehensive-analysis-of-authors-organizations-and-countries-a1b55a08132e) 的 count 衡量）。Chinese researchers 通过相互 citing 做了大量 growth hacking，但 work 本身并不好？

[这篇 paper](https://www.pnas.org/content/111/26/9437) 的 Figure 5 显示，China 确实一直在 improving 其 research 的 quality（以 highly cited work 作为 proxied），但距离 catching up with US 仍然很远；反过来看，如果把 EU 视为一个 whole，那么似乎 EU 才处于 leading 地位，如果按 GDP 进行 adjusts，就更是如此。

![image-20210812121305063](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812121305063.png)

这仍然与 US producing 最好的 work 相 compatible：例如，EU 可以 produce 最好的 10% 的 work，但如果将范围 restricting 到 top 1%，我们就会看到 US leadership，而这正是 Nobel Prizes 中呈现的情况。粗略浏览其他 Nobel Prize-type awards，就能 confirms：US-born 和 US-based researchers 获得了这些 prizes 中的 majority（von Hippel [Award](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Hippel_Award)、Charles Stark [Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Stark_Draper_Prize) Prize、Turing [Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award)、IEEE [Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Medal_of_Honor) of Honor、Millennium [Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Technology_Prize) Prize、Lasker [Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Award)，或者 Kyoto [Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Prize)）

## France（法国）

在试图 explain success 时，另一个值得考虑的有趣事实是，如果我们考虑 mathematics（它没有 Nobel Prize），那么 France 就与 [United States](https://en.everybodywiki.com/List_of_countries_by_number_of_Fields_Medalists) 不相上下；而或许是世界上 leading research center for mathematics 的 Ecole Normale Superieure（按 Fields [medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fields_Medal_winners_by_university_affiliation#Summary_of_results) 的 count 衡量），就在 France。

当然，我不是第一个[注意到](https://www.reddit.com/r/math/comments/2049h2/why_does_france_have_so_many_fields_medalists/)这一点的人。France 在 mathematics 方面的 performance 不能由 mathematics funding 来 explained（它只是 US 的一小部分 [fraction](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/31908797_Survey_analysis_of_Japanese_mathematics_research)）：

![image-20210812174126144](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812174126144.png)

甚至也不能仅由 population 来解释。US 会 publishes 更多 papers；publications 与 authors 的 number 是 directly proportional，尤其是在 mathematics 这种 single authorship 很常见的 field 中（[这里](https://www.hceres.fr/sites/default/files/media/downloads/rappScien_VA_web04.pdf)的 Table 3 confirms 了这一看法：每篇 mathematics paper 的 authors 的 average N 在各 countries 间是 similar 的），而[这份资料](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature.2016.20491)进一步 confirms，即使从历史上看，French mathematicians 也没有那么多：

![image-20210812174233679](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812174233679.png)

所以，尽管 US 在 funding 更多 mathematicians，并 publishing 更多 papers，France 在 mathematical achievement 的 highest levels 上仍然极其 competitive；这或许 implying，他们要么拥有比 US 更多的 top talent，要么能更好地 nurture 这些人才。

事情还变得更奇怪了：French 孩子在 PISA maths assessments 中的 performance [仅仅是 average](https://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/education/mathematics-performance-pisa/indicator/english_04711c74-en)，远远落后于那些惯常领先的地方（Singapore、Hong Kong 或 China）。France 在 International Mathematics Olympiad 中的表现也并不特别好，gold medals 比如说比 Bulgaria、Vietnam 或 Thailand 还少。第一个事实很容易调和：重要的不是 average，而是 best。后者或许可以用对 IMO 缺乏兴趣来 explained，也许最优秀的 French students 正在[忙于](https://www.quora.com/Why-is-Frances-performance-in-IMO-quite-mediocre)准备进入一所 Ecole Normale Superieure，而 IMO 会让他们分心。但话说回来，为什么 _China_ 在 IMO 中 lead，却在 Fields Medal 中 [underrepresented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shing-Tung_Yau) 呢？也许在 IMO 中必须 solve 的 problems 的种类，与能让你获得 Fields medal 的 problems 的种类是 different 的？

## mathematicians 的 culture

另一个 explanation 是，France 是 specialized in mathematics 的；它的 researchers 中有更高的 fraction——很可能它的 top researchers 也是如此——会 specialize in maths，而在 US，那些 top students 更有可能进入比如 biomedical research 或 social science。这似乎是真的，例如可以看看[这里](https://www.hceres.fr/sites/default/files/media/downloads/rappScien_VA_web04.pdf)的 Figure 29a，它按 discipline，对相对于 global average 的 national allocation of talent（以 publications 作为 proxied）进行了 compares：

![image-20210814135450774](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210814135450774.png)

而且，这种情况[很早](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/viewdoc/download?doi=10.1.1.550.770&rep=rep1&type=pdf)就开始了：

![image-20210814150937109](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210814150937109.png)

如果把 BScs、MScs 和 PhDs 合在一起，France 的 tertiary education 中 studying mathematics 的 students 的 proportion 是 US 的 3x。如果只考虑 PhDs，那么 France 的这一倍数会 drops 到 1.5x（参见[这里](https://stats.oecd.org/Index.aspx?DataSetCode=EAG_GRAD_ENTR_FIELD#)）。在 countries 之间 Comparing，US 看起来相对 stable，而在 France，选择 specialize in mathematics 的 PhD students 的 proportion 非但没有下降，反而在 increasing。

![image-20210816123312100](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210816123312100.png)

## 自由的 minds？

要在 mathematics 这样的 fields 中 make progress，年轻很可能会让人处于有利地位（更多内容见[这里](https://nintil.com/age-and-science/)）。在 France 的 ENS，PhD students 和 postdocs 都是 public employees（他们不用为自己的 education 付费，State 会向他们付薪；作为交换，他们须作出 10-year service commitment）。这是否意味着，他们可以 100% dedicated to research，从而 avoiding 比如 teaching responsibilities（US students 是有这些职责的）？我没能找到确切的 source 来确认是否如此，所以我去了 ENS 的 mathematics [department](http://www.math.ens.fr/-Analyse-)，查看 postdoctoral 和 doctoral students 的资料，看看他们有没有列出 teaching experience。很多 CVs 无法获取，但其中许多人确实会 teach 一些 classes

我觉得，用来 explain France 的 cultural argument（mathematics 被视为 brightest students 值得从事的追求）最为 plausible。当然，这也可能是一个 incentive 问题（France 为 mathematicians 提供 economic support，但比 US 更多吗？），这很难与 studying maths 更受到 encouraged 或 preferred 的说法 disentangle 开来。也可能与 France 的 mathematics 被 taught 的方式有关，但我没能在网上找到任何表明其与其他地方 drastically different 的资料。我起初[以为](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1426226745305276418)，也许他们非常 emphasized memorization，但这可能是 mathematics education 的 universal feature。

## Conclusion（结论）

我们能让 Urquiola 的 theory 与上述 evidence 相吻合吗？我不太确定。这个 theory 要如何 explain France，或 Chinese universities 的崛起？也许他会回应说，mathematics 是一个 special case，do math 不需要很多钱，只要 salary 和纸就行，而对于更 expensive 的工作，更多地 concentrate funding 或许是值得的？这本书试图 explain，为什么 US 成功地在 Nobel biographies 中获得了比 Europe 更多的 mentions，但读到最后，我并没有那么确信，这就是 explains US 无论现在还是过去的大部分 success 的原因。要做到这一点，就需要对 spent R&D 的 fraction 和 researchers 的 number（native 和 foreign）进行 controlling for，最好还要按 field 分别进行。

## 原文

> By most available criteria, the United States is still the undisputed leader in the performance of basic and applied research (National Academy of Sciences, [2007](https://www.nap.edu/read/11463/chapter/5))

Since 1969, US-based scientists have won [more](https://www.statista.com/chart/19646/science-nobel-prizes-by-country-and-immigrant-share/) (Science) Nobel Prizes than the rest of countries put together. While this metric is one among and others, especially those measured more [recently](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-020-01231-w), don't show such an overwhelming dominance of American science, the general story is clear.

A book I just finished reading, [Markets, Minds, and Money](https://www.amazon.com/Markets-Minds-Money-University-Research/dp/0674244230/ref=sr_1_3?dchild=1&keywords=markets+minds+money&qid=1628775579&sr=8-3) tries to explain why. The explanation from the book, briefly, is that the American academic system has a free-market orientation that other countries lack. Whereas Europe, the author claims, rigidly imposes a centralized curriculum from the top down and tries to distribute funds evenly across regions within Europe and across nations, the US lets the most productive scientists get more resources even if this leads to an extremely unequal funding distribution (Which, if the Newton [hypothesis](https://nintil.com/newton-hypothesis) is true, is actually a great thing).

One could however try to point to other factors that explain the success of the US, and the author has considered some of these alternative explanations. For example, perhaps the US is such a science powerhouse because it was the destination of a good chunk of [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Paperclip) (after WWII) and [Soviet](https://conference.nber.org/confer/2012/HSIf12/Ganguli.pdf) (after the collapse of the USSR) scientists. That surely contributed but the US was already on its way to scientific preeminence before that: US Academia began to eclipse the rest of the world by the 1920s:

![image-20210812101123867](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812101123867.png)

This is different from Nobel Prize winners being American or US-based foreign scientists, that started before WWII, and then got an additional boost by [attracting](https://s3.amazonaws.com/chssweb/documents/24957/original/Nobel_Brief_2017_%281%29.pdf?1496674936) top global talent:

![image-20210812101431659](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812101431659.png)

Similar, more detailed figure from [here](https://www.hceres.fr/sites/default/files/media/downloads/rappScien_VA_web04.pdf):

![image-20210814134316161](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210814134316161.png)

There is a chart that shows that [inventors](https://www.wipo.int/publications/en/details.jsp?id=3952&plang=EN) tend to migrate to the US disproportionately relative to other countries. I couldn't find a similar one for scientists broadly (other than Nobel prizes), but I wouldn't be surprised if it looks very similar:

![image-20210812095645458](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812095645458.png)

The reason why we see this could be an intrinsic advantage of the American system, the free-market orientation described in the book (more opportunities to do the research the scientist wants?), but it could also be other factors. Suppose for example that the US transitions to a European-style (German-style?) scientific ecosystem. Leaving everything else unchanged it would still have two factors that may explain the patterns observed: population and wealth. Being able to finance more numerous and more expensive experiments and pay higher salaries alone could explain a chunk of US success.

The first one of these factors (wealth) was not addressed in the original paper; Michael Nielsen was thus doubtful (see tweets [here](https://twitter.com/michael_nielsen/status/1416054022872645633)) about the overall result (It's something one should totally control for!). I thought that perhaps the book would have considered this. While they tried, I don't think they did satisfactorily.

The appendix of the book considers this and has some numbers normalized by income:

![image-20210812105749753](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812105749753.png)

Here one'd want to perhaps control for salaries in higher education instead though. With that caveat, the pattern holds.

The same is true, to a lesser extent, if one controls for population;

![image-20210812105852262](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812105852262.png)

The UK manages to remain competitive to some extent whereas France and Germany gradually fall in the rankings. Even here, we don't want to control by population in general, rather we'd like to control for the number of scientists in each country; however, the number of scientists is endogenous to the performance of the scientific system itself: success will attract more success. With that caveat, the [image](https://www.hceres.fr/sites/default/files/media/downloads/rappScien_VA_web04.pdf) below accounts for researchers, and it largely matches Urquiola's story, with the US and the UK (Which he says has a similar academic setup) leading (set aside the countries with a small number of Nobels awarded), the one interesting outlier here is Israel (at the PhD level) but it seems a lot of those Israelis end up working in the US.

![image-20210814133748750](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210814133748750.png)

What if one controlled for both though? Crude measure as it is, in economics in general there's a notion of productivity above and beyond inputs (Total Factor Productivity). Here we could define a simple model where scientific productivity (as proxied by total citations, or just Urquiola's own mention-based metric) depends on the product of R&D spending and the total number of researchers, times a residual. The evolution of that residual would get us productivity. Whereas it seems plausible that the way the US academic system is structured can act as an advantage, I'm not so sure that once we control for immigration and more funding available, the US' lead continues to be dramatic. This should be truer of fields that require more funds.

Perhaps, it could be also argued, explaining the success of the US and the fact that its rise starts before WWII requires multiple factors. First, there could be a simple story of catchup, where US science was developing to match Europe, explaining the initial boost, then we would add the effects of WWII on European science as a one-off effect to which we would then add the trickle of researchers immigrating to the US since then.

Something that is not discussed much in the book is the rise of China, though this is acknowledged:

> Because universities cannot be mentioned in winners’ biographies until they win, our measure penalizes “up and coming” schools. For instance, in recent decades, countries like China and South Korea have invested in their universities. To the extent that these are now training outstanding students, they will take years to show up in Nobel biographies. That said, our re- results for recent decades match up well with rankings that use bibliographical data likely to reflect progress more rapidly. In addition, this book’s focus is not on recent developments, but on the rise to dominance of American research universities.

Fair enough, but if it turns out that a) China's academic productivity is rapidly increasing and b) The Chinese academic system is not free-market oriented, then the thesis of the book is much weakened. At best, it would say that _during a particular period in history_, the American university structure was useful. At worst, it's a vindication of the null hypothesis: That money and minds alone explain the bulk of scientific success.

Since the early 2000s, China has [granted](https://www.aip.org/fyi/2018/rapid-rise-china%E2%80%99s-stem-workforce-charted-national-science-board-report) more Science & Engineering (S&E) bachelor degrees than the US and almost as many doctorates. China will soon be spending [more](https://www.economist.com/briefing/2021/01/16/the-case-for-more-state-spending-on-r-and-d) (in absolute terms) in R&D than the US. As of the late 2000s, China produces more scientific articles than the US (but not more than the European Union). Now that China-to-US brain drain is slowing down (and perhaps over by now; though not in every field, see the case of [AI](https://www.nytimes.com/2020/06/09/technology/china-ai-research-education.html)) and top Chinese talent stays in China more [often](https://infoproc.blogspot.com/2004/12/brain-drain-slowdown.html), we may start to see the rise of China among Nobel Prize citations as well. By [various](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nickmorrison/2020/09/02/oxford-keeps-top-spot-but-china-is-the-real-winner-as-us-declines/) [rankings](https://www.economist.com/international/2018/05/19/how-global-university-rankings-are-changing-higher-education), Chinese universities are appearing more often among the top universities.

![How global university rankings are changing higher education | The Economist](https://www.economist.com/sites/default/files/images/2018/05/articles/body_5)

So are Chinese universities matching money with minds effectively as in the US? Or sorting outsmart students? For that latter, I'd adventure to say that probably. The US' SAT seems weaker than China's Gaokao.

Has China reached a leading status in any field yet? We can't yet see this in Nobel Prizes, but that lags the actual research by decades. In AI the answer is: getting there (by top [publications](https://www.theverge.com/2019/3/14/18265230/china-is-about-to-overtake-america-in-ai-research); but see the remark on "Best Paper awards") and no (by the count of papers [accepted](https://macropolo.org/digital-projects/the-global-ai-talent-tracker/) to leading [conferences](https://medium.com/criteo-engineering/neurips-2020-comprehensive-analysis-of-authors-organizations-and-countries-a1b55a08132e)). Lots of growth hacking by Chinese researchers citing each other, but the work itself is not good?

Figure 5 of [this paper](https://www.pnas.org/content/111/26/9437) shows that China has indeed been improving the quality of their research (proxied by highly cited work), but it's still far from catching up with the US; conversely it looks like if one takes the EU as a whole, then the EU is leading, more so if one adjusts by GDP.

![image-20210812121305063](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812121305063.png)

This is still compatible with the US producing the very best of work, say the EU can produce the best 10% of the work, whereas restricting that to the top 1% gets us to US leadership, and that's what appears in Nobel Prizes. A quick perusal of other Nobel Prize-type awards confirms that US-born and -based researchers get the majority of those prizes (von Hippel [Award](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Hippel_Award), Charles Stark [Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Stark_Draper_Prize) Prize, Turing [Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_Award), IEEE [Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEE_Medal_of_Honor) of Honor, Millennium [Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Technology_Prize) Prize, the Lasker [Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasker_Award), or the Kyoto [Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Prize))

## France

Another interesting fact to consider when trying to explain success is that if we consider mathematics (There is no Nobel Prize for that), then France is on par with the [United States](https://en.everybodywiki.com/List_of_countries_by_number_of_Fields_Medalists); and what perhaps is the world's leading research center for mathematics (by the count of Fields [medals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Fields_Medal_winners_by_university_affiliation#Summary_of_results)), the Ecole Normale Superieure, is in France.

I'm of course not the first one [noticing](https://www.reddit.com/r/math/comments/2049h2/why_does_france_have_so_many_fields_medalists/) this. France's performance in mathematics is not explained by mathematics funding (A [fraction](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/31908797_Survey_analysis_of_Japanese_mathematics_research) of US's):

![image-20210812174126144](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812174126144.png)

or even just population. The US publishes more papers; publications are directly proportional to the number of authors, especially in a field like mathematics where single authorship is frequent (Table 3 [here](https://www.hceres.fr/sites/default/files/media/downloads/rappScien_VA_web04.pdf) confirms the idea that the average N of authors per mathematics paper is similar across countries) and [this](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature.2016.20491) further confirms that there aren't that many French mathematicians, even historically:

![image-20210812174233679](https://nintil.com/images/2021-08-12-us-science-leadership/image-20210812174233679.png)

So despite the US funding more mathematicians and publishing more papers, France continues to be extremely competitive at the highest levels of mathematical achievement; implying, perhaps that either they have more top talent than the US, or they manage to nurture it better.

It gets weirder: French kids' performance in PISA maths assessments is [merely average](https://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/education/mathematics-performance-pisa/indicator/english_04711c74-en), and way behind the usual suspects (Singapore, Hong Kong, or China). France doesn't do particularly well at the International Mathematics Olympiad, with fewer gold medals than, say, Bulgaria, Vietnam, or Thailand. The first fact is easy to reconcile: what matters is not the average but the best. The latter could be explained by a lack of interest in the IMO, perhaps the best French students are [busy](https://www.quora.com/Why-is-Frances-performance-in-IMO-quite-mediocre) preparing to join an Ecole Normale Superieure and the IMO is a distraction. But then again, why does _China_ lead the IMO but is [underrepresented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shing-Tung_Yau) in the Fields Medal?. Maybe the kinds of problems that one has to solve at the IMO are different from the kinds of problems that get you a Fields medal?

## A culture of mathematicians

Another explanation is that France is specialized in mathematics; that a higher fraction of their researchers, and plausibly their top researchers too, specialize in maths whereas in the US those top students are more likely to go into say biomedical research or social science. This seems true, see for example Figure 29a from [here](https://www.hceres.fr/sites/default/files/media/downloads/rappScien_VA_web04.pdf), which compares the national allocation of talent (proxied by publications) by discipline relative to a global average:

And this starts [early](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/viewdoc/download?doi=10.1.1.550.770&rep=rep1&type=pdf) on:

## Free minds?

It's likely that to make progress in fields like mathematics one is at an advantage if one is young (for more see [here](https://nintil.com/age-and-science/)). In France's ENS, PhD students and postdocs are public employees (they don't pay for their education, the State pays them; in exchange for a 10-year service commitment). Does this mean that they can be 100% dedicated to research, avoiding e.g. teaching responsibilities (Which US students have)? I couldn't quite find a source to see if this is the case, so I went to the mathematics [department](http://www.math.ens.fr/-Analyse-) of the ENS and looked at postdoctoral and doctoral students to see if they list any teaching experience. Many CVs are not available but many of them do teach some classes

I find the cultural argument (mathematics is seen as a worthy pursuit for the brightest students) the most plausible to explain France. To be sure, this may also be an incentive story (France has economic support for mathematicians, but more than the US?), that's hard to disentangle from saying that studying maths is more encouraged or preferred. It could also be something about the way mathematics is taught in France, but I was not able to find online anything that would suggest that it is drastically different from elsewhere. I initially [thought](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1426226745305276418) that maybe they emphasized memorization a lot, but that may be a universal feature of mathematics education.

## Conclusion

Can we square Urquiola's theory with the evidence above? Not so sure. How could the theory explain France or the rise of Chinese universities? Perhaps he may respond by saying that mathematics is a special case, one doesn't need a lot of money to do math, just a salary and paper whereas for more expensive endeavors maybe it pays to concentrate funding more? The book is trying to explain why the US succeeded at getting more mentions in Nobel biographies than Europe, but I don't end up all that convinced that this is what explains the bulk of US success, current or past. Doing that would require controlling for fraction of spent R&D and the number of researchers (native and foreign), preferably by field.
