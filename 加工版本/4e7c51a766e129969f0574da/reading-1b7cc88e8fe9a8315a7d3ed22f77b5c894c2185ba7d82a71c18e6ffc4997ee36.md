# Why Ayn Rand is not, and ought not be, taken seriously

---

## 正文

补记：请不要只读第一段！另外，请读[这篇文章](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/10/on-the-express-acceptance-and-rejection-of-beliefs/)，了解如何在没有读过某些内容的情况下 reject 它们。

除了在互联网上零星读过一些段落和摘录，我从未读过 Ayn Rand 的任何一部作品。我也从未特别重视 Objectivism，也就是她的 system of thought。

但有些人——Objectivists——认为她是有史以来最伟大的 philosopher 之一，甚至就是最伟大的那一位（他们会补上一句：还有 Aristotle）。有时，Objectivists 会让没读过 Rand 的人去读她的作品，好让他们变得 rational，并放弃自己的“non-Objectivist、mystical beliefs”。

我没有读过 Rand 的任何作品，因为我觉得这番功夫不值得：每次读 Objectivists 写的东西，我都没有被说服，相信能从 Rand 那里学到多少东西。另外，网上还有一篇 critique，足以 demolishes Objectivism。稍后我会给出 reasons，说明为什么我认为这篇 critique 是成功的。

最近，我读了 Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy 的 [Rand 条目](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand)。请注意，这个条目并不是讨厌 Rand 的人写的。Roderick T. Long 和 Neera K. Badwhar 也读过 Rand 的作品，而且从他们自己的文章来看，似乎对她颇有好感。然而，他们仍然承认：

> Rand 的 ideas 和 mode of presentation 使她受到许多 non-academics 的欢迎，但在 academics 中却带来了相反的结果。她在回应读者的 questions 时 developed 了自己的一些 views，却从未花时间 defend 它们以应对 possible objections，也没有将它们与她在 novels 中表达的 views 加以 reconcile。她的 philosophical essays 缺乏 analytic philosophy 那种 self-critical、detailed 的 style，也没有认真尝试考虑针对她的 views 的 possible objections。她的 polemical style、时常轻蔑的语气，以及她的许多追随者的 dogmatism 和 cult-like behavior，也让人觉得她的作品不值得认真对待。
> 
>  此外，understanding 她的 views 需要阅读她的 fiction，但她的 fiction 并不合所有人的口味。她常常仅凭 cursory readings，以及与少数 philosophers 和她那些年轻的 philosophy student 门徒的交谈，就 dismisses 其他 philosophers 的 views，这也于事无补。一些 contemporary philosophers 则投桃报李，仅凭道听途说便轻蔑地 dismissing 她的作品。有些确实读过她的作品的人指出，她的 arguments 太多时候并不能 support 她的 conclusions。甚至许多认为她的 conclusions，以及她对 contemporary culture、morality 和 politics 的 criticisms 是 original 且 insightful 的人，也持同样的看法。因此，在讨论有关 [virtue ethics](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/)、[egoism](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/)、[rights](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights/)、[libertarianism](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/) 或 [markets](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/markets/) 的当前 philosophical thought 的条目中，她要么只被顺带提及，要么完全没有被提及，也就不足为奇了。

关于她为什么不被认真对待，也可以看看[这篇文章](http://philosophy.stackexchange.com/questions/1668/why-is-ayn-rands-objectivism-philosophy-dismissed-by-academics)。

这里有对 Ayn Rand 的 Objectivism 的 general criticism：[为什么我不是 Objectivist](http://www.owl232.net/rand.htm)，以及[对 The Objectivist Ethics 的 Critique](http://www.owl232.net/rand5.htm)。Objectivists 说的有些东西是 true，但这并不使整套东西都成为 true。A=A、existence exists 或 consciousness perceives reality 之类的东西，与只有 serving 我们的 self-interest 才是 good、而 altruism 是 bad 这样的说法之间，存在巨大的 difference。问题也并不在于所有 conclusions：没错，reality 是 objective，capitalism 很棒，而且人应当 rational。问题在于_某些_ conclusions，以及为它们提供的 arguments。

那篇 critique 的作者是 Michael Huemer，一位读过 randian opus 中主要作品、并 rejected 它的 philosopher。从他的文章可以 inferred，他读过[这部](http://www.owl232.net/rand2.htm) Introduction to Objectivist Epistemology、[Objectivism](http://www.owl232.net/rand3.htm), the Philosophy of Ayn Rand (Peikoff, 1991)、The Virtue of Selfishness，以及 Introduction to Objectivist Epistemology。

他在 Journal of Ayn Rand studies 上发表的 articles，不是一篇，不是两篇，而是_六篇_（[Huemer, 2004a](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUEEAP)、[Huemer 2002](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUEIBE)、[Huemer 2004b](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUERTM)、[Huemer 2005](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUERTA)、[Huemer 2007](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUERTF)）。

从这些 papers 可以看出，他读过 Atlas Shrugged、Capitalism: the Unknown Ideal、The Fountainhead，以及 The Ayn Rand Lexicon。

从 Cato Unbound 关于 Rand 的 thought 的[这一期](http://www.cato-unbound.org/2010/02/02/michael-huemer/instrumentalist-egoism)，我们得知他还读过 Philosophy: Who Needs it。

他参加过一场关于 Rand 的 ethics 的 debate（见[这里](http://www.philosophyinaction.com/blog/?p=3279)），而 Objectivists 自己也称赞了 Huemer 对 Objectivism 的 understanding：

> 我们中许多人可能并不 agree 他 \[Huemer\] 说的一些东西，但他做得远比大多数尝试 analyze 和 critique Objectivism 的其他 academics 好。只要翻开五年多以前写的、关于 Rand 的 secondary literature 中几乎任意一篇，你就会明白我的意思。debate 结束后，我走上前去向他道谢。我认为其他人也应该这样做，或许可以给他发邮件，感谢他的参与。
> 
> Huemer 在整场 debate 中一次也没有 misrepresent Objectivist position，并表现出对它的 basic principles 的真正 understanding。这很少见，也值得赞赏。Huemer 的独特之处在于，他是一位认真对待 Objectivism 的 professional philosopher，尽管他并不 agree 它。……就我目前所见，我们需要_更多_像 Huemer 这样的 Objectivism 的 critics。

最后，作为一位 philosopher，他读过 Aristotle、Kant，以及你大概认为与这场 discussion 有关的大多数 philosophers 的作品。

Huemer 相信这一点，其他 philosophers 也相信这一点，而且还有一篇公开可得的 critique；这些事实构成了反对 Objectivism 的非常有力的 evidence。

此外，Jason Brennan 指出了 Rand 的几个 particular mistakes：[她提出 false claims](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2015/10/ayn-rands-philosophy-who-needs-it-is-bullshit/)，因为她在有 empirical evidence 可用时并不 relying on 它；[她并不 endorse](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/07/egoism-and-rights/)她自己的 views 的 logical conclusions；以及他 misrepresents [其他 thinkers](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/07/craig-biddles-chart-on-essential-moral-theories-objectivist-strawmen/)，尤其是 [Immanuel Kant](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/07/rand-on-kant-lets-use-this-as-a-teaching-moment/)。Huemer 在前面链接的 critique 中进一步阐述了这一点：

> Rand 严重 misrepresents 了 history of ethics。基本上，她让读者相信，ethics 中一直只有两种 alternative views：(a) moral knowledge 来自 God 的 mystical revelations；(b) moral principles 是 arbitrary conventions。无论哪一种，ethics 都被视为“irrational 的领域”。她还提到了另一个 position：Aristotle 的 position；据称，他将 ethics 建立在 noble 和 wise 的人选择做什么之上，却忽略了他们为什么选择这样做，以及他为什么认为他们是 noble 和 wise 的这些 questions。与这些 alternatives 并列比较时，Rand 的 theory 看起来几乎是 reasonable 的。
> 
> 然而，上述说法是对 history of ethics 的严重 caricature，而且 Rand 完全没有尝试用任何 citations 来 document 她的 claims。
> 
> 简而言之，Rand 通过 attacking straw men，为自己的 position 获取 plausibility。

当然，Objectivists 或许已经回应了上面链接的 critiques。以下是其中一些 responses：

-   [Objectivism subreddit 中的 Discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/Objectivism/comments/32g71a/michael_huemer_why_i_am_not_an_objectivist/)
-   [哲学思想实验（Philosophical thought experiments）](https://conjecturesandrefutations.com/2015/03/28/philosophical-thought-experiments/)
-   [就 Ethics 给 Huemer 的 Reply](http://www.noblesoul.com/orc/essays/huemer.html)
-   [关于 nonsense 和 Referents](http://www.theagon.org/blog/?p=96)
-   [对 The Objectivist Ethics 的 Metacritiques](https://necrofiles.blogspot.com/2009/10/metacritique-of-objectivist-ethics.html)

由于它们没有说服我，而且其中一些实际上相当糟糕，我仍然认为 Huemer 的 critique stands，因此，我仍然相信 Rand 不应被认真对待，即使我没有读过她写的任何东西。

不过，这个 conclusion 必须加以 qualified。我们是否应当仅仅因为其他资历出色的人说某些 views 应当被 rejected，而且没有针对他们为这些 rejections 提出的 arguments 的 critiques，就 reject 这些 views？通常来说，是的。这些因素结合起来，是一个非常好的 reason，让我们可以在没有阅读相关内容的情况下 reject 某些东西。我们在生活中通常就是这么做的：我们很少深入阅读 Physics 的 journals，才决定 believe 或 disbelieve physicists 提出的 claims。如果许多长期研究某个 subject 的人在某件事上 agree，那就是支持这件事的 evidence。它不是 ultimate evidence，但如果你想违背 consensus，burden of proof 就在你身上。

因此，那些不属于相关 experts 的 consensus 的 ideas 的 defenders，应该拿出针对这些 ideas 的最好的 critiques，并 refute 它们。如果他们还能为自己的 ideas 提供简短的介绍，以降低其他人 acquiring 关于这些 ideas 的 information 的 cost，那也很好。

我对 Objectivism 为什么受欢迎的猜测是，它提供了一套 unified body of thought，回答了许多 philosophical questions。人们喜欢 unified bodies of thought。还有其他一些 thinkers 建立了 philosophical systems，也有许多追随者，而且他们的 way of argument 有一些相似之处，在某些情况下，最终就成了不断重复几乎预先准备好的句子。

但 philosophy 不是这样运作的。去看看 Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy，看看每一个 concept 都有多少 debate、内容有多丰富。很难相信某一个人能够横空出世，在没有为 rejection 提出好的 arguments 的情况下 reject 所有这些东西，然后继续构建自己的一套 system。

Objectivists：请 Publish 一篇好的 rebuttal 来回应 Huemer 的 critique，否则就别再做 Objectivists 了。

## 原文

EDIT: Read beyond the first paragraph! Also, read [this](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/10/on-the-express-acceptance-and-rejection-of-beliefs/) to understand how to reject something without reading it.

I have never  read a piece of work from Ayn Rand, beyond some paragraphs and extracts here and there on the internet. I never gave Objectivism, her system of thought, much importance.

But there are people -Objectivists- who think she is one of the greatest, or the greatest, philosopher ever (along with Aristotle, they'll add). Sometimes, Objectivists tell people who have not read Rand to read her, so that they can become rational, and abandon their "non-Objectivist, mystical beliefs".

I haven't read anything from Rand because I consider that the effort won't be worth it, as every time I've read something from Objectivists, I have not been persuaded that there is much to be learned from Rand. And also, there exists a critique available on the net that demolishes Objectivism. I will give reasons later why I believe the critique succeeds.

Recently, I read the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy's [entry on Rand](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ayn-rand). The entry is not written by Rand haters, mind you. Roderick T. Long and Neera K. Badwhar also have read Rand's work, and in their own writings seem sympathetic to her. And yet, they admit:

> Whereas Rand’s ideas and mode of presentation make Rand popular with many non-academics, they lead to the opposite outcome with academics. She developed some of her views in response to questions from her readers, but never took the time to defend them against possible objections or to reconcile them with the views expressed in her novels. Her philosophical essays lack the self-critical, detailed style of analytic philosophy, or any serious attempt to consider possible objections to her views. Her polemical style, often contemptuous tone, and the dogmatism and cult-like behavior of many of her fans also suggest that her work is not worth taking seriously.
> 
>  Further, understanding her views requires reading her fiction, but her fiction is not to everyone’s taste. It does not help that she often dismisses other philosophers’ views on the basis of cursory readings and conversations with a few philosophers and with her young philosophy student acolytes. Some contemporary philosophers return the compliment by dismissing her work contemptuously on the basis of hearsay. Some who do read her work point out that her arguments too often do not support her conclusions. This estimate is shared even by many who find her conclusions and her criticisms of contemporary culture, morality, and politics original and insightful. It is not surprising, then, that she is either mentioned in passing, or not mentioned at all, in the entries that discuss current philosophical thought about [virtue ethics](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/), [egoism](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/egoism/), [rights](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights/), [libertarianism](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/libertarianism/), or [markets](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/markets/).

See also [this](http://philosophy.stackexchange.com/questions/1668/why-is-ayn-rands-objectivism-philosophy-dismissed-by-academics) on why she is not taken seriously.

Here's a general criticism of Ayn Rand's Objectivism: [Why I am not an Objectivist](http://www.owl232.net/rand.htm) and [Critique of The Objectivist Ethics](http://www.owl232.net/rand5.htm). There are things that Objectivists say that are true, but that does not make the whole thing true. There is a huge difference between things like A=A, existence exists, or consciousness perceives reality, and that only serving our self-interest is good, and altruism is bad. The problem isn't all of the conclusions, either: yes, reality is objective, and capitalism is great, and one should be rational. The problem is with _some_ conclusions, and with the arguments for those.

The author of that critique is Michael Huemer, a philosopher who has read the key pieces of the randian opus, and rejected it. From his writings, it can be inferred that he has read [the](http://www.owl232.net/rand2.htm) Introduction to Objectivist Epistemology, [Objectivism](http://www.owl232.net/rand3.htm), the Philosophy of Ayn Rand (Peikoff, 1991), The Virtue of Selfishness, and the Introduction to Objectivist Epistemology.

He has published not one, not two but _six_ articles in the Journal of Ayn Rand studies ([Huemer, 2004a](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUEEAP), [Huemer 2002](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUEIBE), [Huemer 2004b](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUERTM), [Huemer 2005](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUERTA), [Huemer 2007](http://philpapers.org/rec/HUERTF) ).

From these papers, it can be shown that he has read Atlas Shrugged, Capitalism: the Unknown Ideal, The Fountainhead, and The Ayn Rand Lexicon.

From [this](http://www.cato-unbound.org/2010/02/02/michael-huemer/instrumentalist-egoism) Cato Unbound issue, on Rand's thought, we learn that he has also read Philosophy: Who Needs it.

He participated in a debate about Rand's ethics ([here](http://www.philosophyinaction.com/blog/?p=3279)), and the Objectivists themselves praised Huemer's understanding of Objectivism:

> Many of us may disagree with things he \[Huemer\] said, but he did far better than most other academics who try to analyze and critique Objectivism. Just open most any of the secondary literature on Rand written more than five years ago and you’ll see what I mean. I went up after the debate and extended him my thanks. I think others should do so, as well, perhaps by sending him emails thanking him for his participation.
> 
> Huemer did not misrepresent the Objectivist position once in the entire debate, and displayed a genuine understanding of its basic principles. That is rare and commendable. Huemer is in the unique position of being a professional philosopher who takes Objectivism seriously, even though he disagrees with it. … From what I’ve seen so far, we need _more_ critics of Objectivism like Huemer.

Finally, as a philosopher, he has read works from Aristotle, Kant, and most philosophers you probably think are relevant for this discussion.

The fact that Huemer believes that, and that the rest of the philosophers believe that, and that there is a publicly available critique, is very strong evidence against Objectivism.

We then have Jason Brennan who points out a few particular mistakes of Rand: [That she makes false claims](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2015/10/ayn-rands-philosophy-who-needs-it-is-bullshit/) due to not relying on empirical evidence when it is available, that [she does not endorse](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/07/egoism-and-rights/)what are logical conclusions of her own views, and that he misrepresents [other thinkers](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/07/craig-biddles-chart-on-essential-moral-theories-objectivist-strawmen/), chiefly [Immanuel Kant](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/07/rand-on-kant-lets-use-this-as-a-teaching-moment/). Huemer expands on that in his previously linked critique:

> Rand seriously misrepresents the history of ethics. Essentially, she leads the reader to believe that there have been only two alternative views in ethics: (a) that moral knowledge comes by mystical revelations from God, and (b) that moral principles are arbitrary conventions. Either way, ethics is regarded as “the province of the irrational.” One other position is mentioned: that of Aristotle, who allegedly based ethics on what noble and wise people choose to do but ignored the questions of why they chose to do it or why he thought they were noble and wise. Next to these alternatives, Rand’s theory looks almost reasonable by comparison.
> 
> However, the above is a gross caricature of the history of ethics, and Rand makes no effort to document her claims with any citations.
> 
> In short, Rand draws plausibility for her position by attacking straw men.

Now, perhaps the critiques linked above have been answered by Objectivists. Here there are some of the responses:

-   [Discussion in the Objectivism subreddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/Objectivism/comments/32g71a/michael_huemer_why_i_am_not_an_objectivist/)
-   [Philosophical thought experiments](https://conjecturesandrefutations.com/2015/03/28/philosophical-thought-experiments/)
-   [A Reply to Huemer on Ethics](http://www.noblesoul.com/orc/essays/huemer.html)
-   [On nonsense and Referents](http://www.theagon.org/blog/?p=96)
-   [Metacritiques of The Objectivist Ethics](https://necrofiles.blogspot.com/2009/10/metacritique-of-objectivist-ethics.html)

Since they do not convince me and some of them are actually pretty bad, I maintain that Huemer's critique stands, and thus my belief that Rand should not be taken seriously remains, even when I haven't read anything she has written.

This conclusion, however, has to be qualified. Should we reject views just because others with good qualifications say they ought to be rejected, and there are no critiques of their arguments for the rejections? Usually, yes. Such combination of factors is a very good reason to reject something without reading about it. This is how we generally go about in our life: rarely we go to the depths of Physics' journals to believe or disbelieve claims that physicists make. If a lot of people who have studied a subject for a long time agree on something, that is evidence for that something. It is not ultimate evidence, but the burden of proof is on you is you want to go against the consensus.

So defenders of ideas that are out of the consensus of relevant experts should take the best critiques that have been made against them, and refute them. It would also be nice for them to provide a brief introduction to their ideas, to reduce the cost for others of acquiring information about those ideas.

My guess about the popularity of Objectivism is that it provides a unified body of thought that answers many philosophical questions. People like unified bodies of thought. There are other thinkers around who have built philosophical systems and who have lots of fans, and they have some similarities in their way of argument, which in some cases ends up in regurgitating almost pre-made sentences all the time.

But philosophy doesn't work like that. Go to the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, and see how much debate and richness you have for every single concept. It's hard to believe that a single person can come out of nowhere, and reject all of that without giving good arguments for the rejection, and then proceed to lay out a system of their own.

Objectivists: Publish a good rebuttal of Huemer's critique, or stop being Objectivists.
