-   这个 podcast 好得多得多，Weinstein 做得很出色。即使一直关注 Thiel 和 Weinstein 的人，也能在其中找到一些新想法。
-   对于 NC 演讲，我的想法则更复杂。如果考虑他提出的“为什么 Chinese money 正在 flowing out of China”这个问题，就能想到一些显而易见的 explanations：China 有很大的 savings rate 和庞大的 population（记住：在没有 adjustments 的情况下，GDP growth 与 stock market performance 之间并没有多大的 correlation）。我没怎么找，就找到了有人提出的 [explanations](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/02/why-is-capital-so-often-flowing-out-of-emerging-economies.html)。演讲中，Thiel 暗示这在某种程度上是件坏事，却没有深入讨论这是否正常，以及为什么不好。
-   他有一些不错的观点：确实，假定 free trade 必然对某个 country 是 beneficial 的，是天真的，尤其在 short term。如果一个人认为 citizen welfare 比 foreigner’s welfare 更重要（这是一种常见的 moral view，我并不认同），而 American workers 因 China shock 而 made worse off——再说一次，是在 short term——那么，如果我是 American worker，要求 protectionism 就符合我的 self-interest。即使你知道，in aggregate，20 年后 global GDP 会 higher，你眼前的选择仍可能是这样两个世界：一个世界里，你是 employed，而你的 chinese counterpart 挣的钱较少；另一个世界里，你是 unemployed，而你的 chinese counterpart 挣的钱多得多。可以理解，voters 面临这样的 tradeoff 时，会投票支持 protection。
-   但这[是真的吗？](https://www.google.com/search?q=china+shock+marginal+revolution&oq=china+shock+marginal+revolution&aqs=chrome..69i57.3535j0j4&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8) Chinese competition 正在 hurting US workers 吗？这似乎仍是一个有争议的问题。我不知道 Peter Thiel 的 claim 背后究竟有哪些 evidence，但如果他能把它们写成博客，让大家 assess，会很有帮助。
-   在 education 上，他是对的，可以从 Caplan 的 Case Against Education 中找到 empirical backing。
-   至于 political correctness，很难完全清楚地把握其背后的思路。这个 claim 有两个方面：一是现在有更多 political correctness，二是它对整个 economy 都有 negative effects，甚至 affecting innovation。
    -   如今，人们对 political correctness 的通常理解围绕着 gender 和 race 这些话题，而 Thiel 给出的例子确实也指向这个方向。这种 political correctness 似乎越来越多。但这会 lead to innovation 的 decrease 吗？我能看出它会 affecting social sciences，却看不出它会如此 affecting STEM fields；Thiel 和我都认为，当我们谈论 innovations 时，大家想到的那些 innovations，关键就在这些 fields。
    -   例如，有[一些 diseases](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/03/23/opinion/sunday/genetics-race.html) 对某些 races 的 affect 比对其他 races 更多。如果不承认 race 在 medicine 中 plays a role，那么，夸张地说，political correctness 会 kills people。但它真的如此吗？on the margin，其 effect 是什么？
    -   接着，我们也可以把 PC 理解为不 questioning consensus truths，以及存在不可触碰的禁忌。我不知道这种情况是否越来越多，但它确实是个问题。
        -   想想 Hanson 对 cutting healthcare 的呼吁
        -   Caplan 对 education 的主张也是如此
        -   还有 [Hossenfelder](https://backreaction.blogspot.com/)，以及不要再随意向 high particle physics 砸钱的主张
    -   这三个 proposals，甚至它们更温和的版本，都被一堵仿佛由受伤感情筑成的墙挡住了。但如果它们成功，而且它们是对的，就会 greatly increase 那些 areas 的 productivity（考虑到投入 healthcare 和 education 的巨额资金，society overall 也是如此），从而 freeing up resources，供其他 areas 使用。
    -   这绝对值得 investigating，也许 Thiel 一直以来就是这个意思，不过，如果能像写博客那样讲得更清楚些，就好了！
-   在 technological innovation 上，podcast 的讨论更细致（例如，他们承认，differential progress 或许只是由于各个 field of inquiry 的 relative difficulty）；
    -   至于具体的 atoms vs bits distinction，一方面，在 software 中 making things 确实比在 hardware 中更容易。Software 几乎有 zero barriers to entry，而且没有 fixed costs，你可以从小做起，再 scale。从零开始创办 airplane company 是 expensive 的。此外，hardware 受 laws of physics 的 constrained，而就 computation 而言，software 并非如此，或者至少受 constrained 的 degree 较小。例如，比较一下 doing mergesort 与在现实生活中 doing mergesort，也就是 physically rearranging objects，这能做得多 fast？或者，在 videogame 中，可以有 violate physics 的东西，比如魔法。当然，那些东西是 simulated 的，而不是 real 的；software 处理的是 abstractions 的 relations，而不是 real atoms。总之，software 受 constrained 较少，因此有更多 improve 的空间。
    -   但似乎并不是 software 在变得更 fast：有人 argued，software 正变得更 bloated、更 slower，因为我们现在有更多 computing power，让我们可以更懒。当然，新 apps 非常多，但我们也可以指出 bits 世界中的 progress：semiconductors（这是 atoms，不是 bits！）、photovoltaics、gene sequencing、cars 或 ships 的 assembly line manufacturing。即使是 [Founder Fund’s manifesto](https://foundersfund.com/the-future/) 中被当作 economic slowdown 典型的 [airplanes](https://www.theatlantic.com/business/archive/2013/02/how-airline-ticket-prices-fell-50-in-30-years-and-why-nobody-noticed/273506/)——其 speed 确实已经 stagnated——另一方面，自 80 年代以来也经历了 50% cost reduction！而且，这并不只是靠让薪酬过低的机组继续驾驶同样的老 jets；fuel efficiency 确实有了 [legitimate improvements](https://www.google.com/search?biw=1075&bih=1056&tbm=isch&sa=1&ei=g4U0XdG3Jq2NlwTvvrTADQ&q=airplane+fuel+efficiency&oq=airplane+fuel+efficiency&gs_l=img.3..0i24l3.162229.163558..163808...0.0..0.129.1450.14j2......0....1..gws-wiz-img.......35i39j0i67j0.WEL0g2TNfWA&ved=0ahUKEwiRhY_SqsbjAhWtxoUKHW8fDdgQ4dUDCAY&uact=5#imgrc=iFnwCQMoRbs_wM:)，使这些 cost reductions 成为可能。
    -   简而言之，Bits vs Atoms 作为解释 software 和 hardware 的 differential behaviour 的 key distinction，确实有道理，但在我看来，它似乎不是理解 progress 的关键。我们还必须看看其他因素。

It’s Thiel week! Thiel’s speech at the [National Conservatism](https://medium.com/@bonniekavoussi/notes-from-peter-thiels-speech-at-the-national-conservatism-conference-on-july-14-2019-6a51b26b202) conference (Thanks Bonnie Kavoussi for the notes) , and Thiel’s [podcast](https://podcastnotes.org/2019/07/17/thiel/) with Eric Weinstein.

-   The podcast is way, way better, Weinstein did a great job. Even for those who have been following Thiel and Weinstein, there are a few new ideas in there.
-   On the NC speech, I have more mixed thoughts. If one takes his “Why is Chinese money flowing out of China” point, one can think of obvious explanations: China has a large savings rate and a large population (Remember: GDP growth and stock market performance, with no adjustments, don’t have much of a correlation). I didn’t have to search much for proposed [explanations](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/02/why-is-capital-so-often-flowing-out-of-emerging-economies.html). In the speech, Thiel hints at this being bad somehow, but doesn’t go deeper on whether it’s a normal thing, and why it would be bad.
-   He has some good points: Indeed, it is naive to assume that free trade will necessarily be beneficial to a given country, especially in the short term. If one values citizen welfare more than foreigner’s welfare (A common moral view I disagree with) and if American workers are made worse off -again, in the short term- due to the China shock, if I were an American worker it would be in my self-interest to ask for protectionism. Even if you knew than in aggregate, in 20 years time, global GDP will be higher, the choice you are making might be a world where you are employed and your chinese counterpart makes less money or a world where you are unemployed and your chinese counterpart makes way more money, understandably voters, if faced with that tradeoff would vote for protection.
-   But is this [for real?](https://www.google.com/search?q=china+shock+marginal+revolution&oq=china+shock+marginal+revolution&aqs=chrome..69i57.3535j0j4&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8) Is Chinese competition hurting US workers? That seems to remain a contended question. I don’t know the exact evidence that’s behind Peter Thiel’s claim, but it would be helpful if he blogged them for everyone to assess.
-   He is right on education, one can point to Caplan’s Case Against Education for the empirical backing.
-   On political correctness, it’s hard to pin down the underlying thinking with full clarity. The claim is twofold: One that there is more political correctness now, and two that that has negative effects throughout the economy, affecting even innovation.
    -   The usual interpretation of political correctness these day is around topics of gender and race, and indeed the examples that Thiel gives point in that direction. That kind of political correctness seems to be on the rise. But does that lead to a decrease in innovation? I can see it affecting the social sciences, but not the STEM fields that Thiel and I think are the key to the innovations we all think about when we think of innovations.
    -   For example, there are [some diseases](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/03/23/opinion/sunday/genetics-race.html) that affect more some races than others. If one does not acknowledge that race plays a role in medicine, then, to put it in hyperbolic terms, political correctness kills people. But does it though? What’s the effect, on the margin?
    -   Then we can think of PC as not questioning consensus truths, and having sacred cows. This I don’t know if it’s on the rise, but it is indeed an issue.
        -   Consider Hanson’s call for cutting healthcare
        -   Ditto for Caplan and education
        -   And [Hossenfelder](https://backreaction.blogspot.com/) and not randomly throwing money at high particle physics
    -   These three proposals, and even milder versions thereof are being stopped by what seems to be a wall of hurt feelings. But if successful, and if they are right, it would greatly increase productivity in those areas (and society overall, given the large amounts of money poured into healthcare and education), freeing up resources to be used in other areas.
    -   Now, this is definitely worth investigating, and maybe this is what Thiel means all along, but it’d be nice to hear in a clearer, blog-like way!
-   On technological innovation, the podcast is more nuanced (e.g. they grant that maybe differential progress is just due to the relative difficulty of each field of inquiry);
    -   On the specific atoms vs bits distinction, on the one hand it is true that making things in software is easier than in hardware. Software has almost zero barriers to entry and no fixed costs, you can start small and scale. Starting an airplane company from scratch is expensive. Also, hardware is constrained by the laws of physics, software is not, insofar as computation is concerned, or at least to a lesser degree. e.g. compare doing mergesort vs doing mergesort in real life, physically rearranging objects, how fast can that be done? Or, in a videogame one can have things that violate physics, like magic. Of course, those things are simulated and not real, software deals with relations of abstractions, not with real atoms. In sum, software is less constrained, thus having more room to improve.
    -   But it doesn’t seem like it is software that is going faster: It has been argued that software is getting more bloated and slower because we now have more computing power, enabling us to be lazier. Sure, there is a plethora of new apps, but then we can also point at progress in the world of bits: semiconductors (This is atoms not bits!), photovoltaics, gene sequencing, assembly line manufacturing of cars or ships. Even the poster child for economic slowdown in the [Founder Fund’s manifesto](https://foundersfund.com/the-future/), [airplanes](https://www.theatlantic.com/business/archive/2013/02/how-airline-ticket-prices-fell-50-in-30-years-and-why-nobody-noticed/273506/) -whose speed, has indeed, stagnated-, has on the other hand experienced a 50% cost reduction since the 80s! And this is not merely by flying the same old jets in with an underpayed crew, there has been [legitimate improvements](https://www.google.com/search?biw=1075&bih=1056&tbm=isch&sa=1&ei=g4U0XdG3Jq2NlwTvvrTADQ&q=airplane+fuel+efficiency&oq=airplane+fuel+efficiency&gs_l=img.3..0i24l3.162229.163558..163808...0.0..0.129.1450.14j2......0....1..gws-wiz-img.......35i39j0i67j0.WEL0g2TNfWA&ved=0ahUKEwiRhY_SqsbjAhWtxoUKHW8fDdgQ4dUDCAY&uact=5#imgrc=iFnwCQMoRbs_wM:) in fuel efficiency enabling those cost reductions.
    -   Tl;dr Bits vs Atoms does make sense as a key distinction to explain the differential behaviour of software and hardware, but it does not seem to me to be the key thing to understand progress. We also have to look at other things.
