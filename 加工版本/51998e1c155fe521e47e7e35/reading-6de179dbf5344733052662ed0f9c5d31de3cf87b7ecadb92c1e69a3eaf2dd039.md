# 补充：如果 1970 年确实发生了什么呢？

---

## 正文

早在 2016 年，我写过一篇 Nintil 的经典文章，_[不存在 great technological stagnation](https://nintil.com/no-great-technological-stagnation/)_，其中我主张，就我们从 public data 中能看到的情况而言，各种 technologies 的 improvements 似乎并没有 slowing down。如果现在重写，我会指出，batteries 和 solar panels 的 trends 仍在 continued，还会加入一些新的 trends，例如 [single cell sequencing](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1334347237913022472) 的 trends（这是一个直到 2009 年才出现的全新 category）。

我在那篇 analysis 中留下了一个尚未解决的问题，

> 那些“真正 innovative”的 innovations 是否变少了？\[也就是 breakthroughs、paradigm shifts、historically significant inventions\]

因为可能出现这样的情况：每一种已有的 technologies 都在顺利 progressing，但与此同时，新的 technologies 的 number（它们不只是 chart 上的另一个 dot）却可能正在 going down。

Breakthroughs 更难 measure。比如，观察 photovoltaic cells 的 price 的 curve，就很容易指出其中的 progress。它是一个 number，number goes down，progress 就发生了。就这么简单。

![](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-1-20.png)

但“science”或“technology”的 progress，或“breakthroughs”的 rate，就更难 measure。我选择寻找所有那些 curves，并不是因为我认为这种做法能相当全面地 captures“science”或“technology”，而是因为这是现有的、可用来 quantify rates of progress 的东西。同时，我也提醒过，这样的 analysis 有其 limits。要满足什么条件，我们才能说自己正在经历 technological stagnation？

David Chapman 最近[写了一篇文章](https://metarationality.com/maps-and-territory)，批评“map 不是 territory”这句格言。map 不是 territory，难道不是显而易见的吗？但这样就没有抓住重点。相信“map 不是 territory”，意味着可能把“map”reification 为一个东西，又把“territory”reification 为另一个东西。Chapman 在那里真正想说的是，reality 比表面看上去更加 messier；不只是 map 是 territory 的 imperfect representation：map 还有很多种。而要知道该使用哪一种 map，还需要一整套 skills，这些并不是那句格言直接 implied 的。哦，而且往往根本没有 clearcut territory 可供指认。那里[并没有一个确切的“那里”](https://www.wsj.com/articles/why-gertrude-steins-no-there-there-is-everywhere-1517589198)。

我对 great stagnation 的看法也类似。我认为，“great stagnation”这个 framing 是 misguided 的，因为它似乎在引导我们寻找 single cause，并认为一切都在 stagnating（其中的“great”就是这个意思）。所有这些显示 1970 年“发生了什么”的 charts，都被用来指向一个 single event，认为它 caused 了从 R&D machine 的 slowing down 到 TFP growth 和 median GDP growth 等各种事情。然而，我们并没有看到 technological progress 的 trends 在 1970 年 slowing down；也并不是在任何一个时间点，technology 和 science 的 progress 都全面 slowed down。没错，如果仔细阅读一些讨论过这个问题的作者，就会发现 great stagnation 并不是这个意思。他们会明确谈论 TFP 和 GDP。这些都是 clean、crisply defined 的 indicators，而且没错，那里确实存在 stagnation。这个 framing 似乎也在推动我们花时间做 conceptual engineering，去理解“science”“technology”“stagnation”或“progress”是什么。我过去主张过，progress [inherently](https://nintil.com/progress-in-progress/) 就是一个 nebulous idea，其他几个也是如此。一个足够好的 definition，就足以让别人明白我们的意思。我们可以就此满足，继续往下走。

但 TFP stagnation 确实存在这一点，并不 imply 是 R&D sector 在某种意义上出了问题（虽然也可能如此）。这可能纯粹是一个 institutional problem。也可能是 [fertility](https://www.amazon.com/Fully-Grown-Stagnant-Economy-Success/dp/022666600X) 在 declining。science stagnation 这个问题，比 measuring TFP 或 GDP growth 的 stagnation 更难。Science 不像 GDP，并不是可以用一个 number 来 measured 的东西。无数次有人开玩笑说，我们正在经历 scientific golden age，因为 published 的 papers 比以往任何时候都多。我们当然知道，更多的 papers 并不意味着更多的 science。但什么才意味着更多的 science 呢？

人们可以尝试列出 [General Purpose Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_purpose_technology) 的清单，也就是在 [GPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI#GPT) 流行之前的老牌 GPTs，但如果问不同的人这些 GPTs 是什么，他们会给出不同的答案。或者，有些人会说，我们_马上就要看到下一个 GPT 了，所有 stagnation 都只是 temporary，所以没什么好担心的！_。

归根结底，我们这些研究 progress 的人关心的是同样的事情：我们希望好东西更多、坏东西更少，也希望对于给定的 output 量，使用更少的 inputs 来做到这一点。好消息是，实现这个目标并不要求我们回答是否“存在 technological 或 scientific stagnation”。这个问题或许更适合用[無](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_\(negative\)) 来回答。

所以，更好的想法是：看看 specific problems，并思考它们潜在的 solutions。并非每一个 problem 都能 solved，而某件事无法 solved 的 likelihood，加上它的 usefulness，可以用来指导 resource allocation。例如，physics 似乎不允许 faster than light travel。也许 [warp 能 works](https://nintil.com/new-physics/)？大概不能。如果能，那就意义重大。值得 funding 少数几个人来研究。但既然还有别的事情可做，就不值得把 R&D budget 中相当大的一部分押在这上面。

那么 curing cancer 呢？为什么 cancer 至今还没有 cured？对此，我们可以指出当前 oncology 领域中的一大批[原因](https://nintil.com/malignant-review/)，然后进一步深入 cancer 的 biology，或支持这些 research 的 funding ecosystem。为此所需的 knowledge 是非常 domain specific 的，与 build spaceships 或 nuclear reactors 所需的 knowledge 无关。

为什么我们还没有 [flying cars](https://rootsofprogress.org/where-is-my-flying-car)？是因为 batteries 太 heavy、太 expensive 吗？也许是，但它们最近取得了很多 progress，所以也可能不是这个原因。是缺少 regulatory framework 吗？很可能；如果如此，正确的做法就是 lobbying，并向相关 regulator 陈述支持它们的理由。或者，也许它们就是不 cost-effective。酷并不 imply usefulness。要研究 flying cars，我们需要考虑 flying cars、lithium batteries、composite materials、low-noise fans 的 fluid mechanics、short distance flights 的 economics，以及它们的 regulations。这样做，我们就能切实深入 subject matter，而远离那种非常 high level 的“great stagnation”视角。话虽如此，人们常说的 flying cars 最终还是会到来。有 [Lilium](https://lilium.com/)，有 [Kitty Hawk](https://kittyhawk.aero/)，还有 [Joby](https://www.jobyaviation.com/)。[此外](https://www.aeromobil.com/)[还存在](https://terrafugia.com/transition/)[许多](https://www.opener.aero/)[其他公司](https://www.pal-v.com/)[在做这件事](https://www.urbanaero.com/fancraft.html)。这并不是因为缺少尝试。显然，building flying cars 是 possible 的。事实上，早在 [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerocar) 年我们就已经有了 prototypes。人们很容易说，如果 flying cars 真像 Storrs Hall 所描述的那样容易，我们早就拥有它们了，所以他是错的。但这样说多少有些自满。更公允的做法，是看看实际 build flying cars 的努力为什么 stalled。[Regulations](https://www.hg.org/legal-articles/aviation-law-an-analysis-of-the-development-of-flying-cars-for-the-2020s-decade-52120)，而不是 physics、engineering 或 economics，可能是其中一部分原因。也许这个 industry 最初试图推动 personally owned flying cars，却碰上了 regulations；而现在的重点似乎是 point-to-point flying taxis，customer 对 vehicle 没有 control，这或许更合 regulators 的心意。也许这是多种 constraints 的[共同作用](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2018/jun/19/flying-cars-why-havent-they-taken-off-yet)：你可以做 horizontal takeoff winged flying cars（这样就不必用 thrust 来 lift weight），但这样就需要 runway，于是 customers 就仅限于住在 airport 附近的人。所以你需要 VTOLs。但 VTOLs 需要大量 thrust，这意味着更多 power，以及 fuel 或 batteries 带来的更多 weight。如果使用 fuel，就会有非常 hot 的 exhaust 朝向可能有人的 ground，使得在 uncontrolled 的 environment（也就是不是 airport 的地方）中 taking off 和 landing 变得 unsafe，所以你需要让它 electric，或者使用 turboshafts。如果提供的 range 太 short，customers 可能干脆就买一辆普通汽车。Batteries 一直在 getting better，所以这一 constraint 如今正在被 lifted。但 VTOLs 也可能 noisy，所以我们需要 blade geometry 的 improvements 来 reduce noise。使用多个 smaller propellers，而不是一个 big 的 propeller，也有助于处理 noise（与 aircraft turbine 的 [chevrons](https://nintil.com/2-you-didnt-invent-that-aircraft-turbine-chevrons-edition) 是同样的 principle）。也许问题只是 size：如果你的 flying car 太 big，以至于无法在当地杂货店附近 park，而你又负担不起两辆车，你可能就会决定继续使用普通汽车。

这番讨论当然还可以更长，但重点是，当你看到很多尝试花费了很长时间时，_也许_问题并不是自满，而是这个 domain 比我们这些不在 industry 中的人看来更难。也许我们会像下面这个故事里的人一样：

> 现在是 10000 年。由于 ISAs 的 improvements，以及能更好地 cool processors 的 materials，Compute power 已经 increase。但 feature size 已经在 1nm level 停留了一段时间。在 TSMC 深处的某个地方（Intel 在 2050 年破产了），有人正在挨骂。“我们已经停留在 1nm 几十年了！按照 historical rates of progress，我们现在应该已经在 building Planck scale 以下的 chips 了！你们为什么这么慢！”“先生，这已经说了第 n 次了，~我是清洁工~ fundamental physics 不允许我们 continuing XX 和 XXI 世纪的 historical trends。那做不到！我们正在专注于 producing 更好的 coprocessors，并通过其他方式 increasing power efficiency。”“为什么这么自满！继续尝试啊！”于是他们继续尝试，就像此前几千年里一样。

毫无节制的乐观，只会带来不断的失望和自我鞭挞。自满不好，并不意味着我们就应该向 builders 提出不切实际的要求，或 reifying historical trends，并要求它们在未来继续。即便是 [Boom Aerospace](https://nintil.com/building-skyscrapers-and-spending-on-major-projects/)，尽管有 entrepreneurial startup mentality 等等，要让自己的 aircraft 飞上天，花的时间也和 traditional aerospace companies 一样长。跑到 Colorado 的 Denver，对着 Blake Scholl 大喊“哥们儿，怎么这么慢！？”合理吗？当然不合理。

只有从 narratives 和 abstractions 下到 concrete 和 specific，我们才能开始考虑可以采取哪些 concrete domain-specific actions、roadmaps 或 bottleneck analysis，以便在重要的 problems 上取得更多 progress。我认为，这才是思考 stagnation 的正确方式。先把 big picture 放在一边，对为什么世界现在没有达到它应有的美好程度做一次 root cause analysis。如果在这个过程中，我们 discover 了一个 single root cause（整个社会是不是正变得 complacent？），那很好。但 _仅仅是它_ 不太可能就是每一个 field 中 less-than-maximal rates of progress 的 cause，甚至不太可能是 main cause，包括那些正在经历真正的 golden age 的 field。

## 补充：如果 1970 年确实发生了什么呢？

[1970](https://wtfhappenedin1971.com/) 年可能确实发生过什么。我并不否认这一点。为了展开论证，不妨假设这件事就是放弃了 [gold standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_shock)。或者是与 [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_energy_crisis) 有关的某件事。那么再假设，这就是整个 economy 的 TFP slowdown 的 cause（这种 slowdown 确实存在）；也就是说，可能尽管 science engine 仍在正常运行，它与 economy 其余部分之间的 transmission link 却已经断裂。或者，1970 shock 间接 induced 了 institutional quality 的 worsening，进而通过 misguided urban planning 导致了诸如 housing prices 的 rising。这会 invalidate 本文吗？

我不这么认为。上文我承认，**TFP 和 GDP 中存在 great stagnation**。我质疑的是，这种 stagnation 是否是一种 _technological stagnation across the board_；而且，与其试图弄清究竟是不是，我们更应该逐个 case 考察，看看为什么事情有快有慢。对于实际存在的 great stagnation，可以做一次 analysis，考察为什么在 economy level 上 GDP growth slowed down，也许会发现众多 cause 中的一个 single cause。这就承认，stagnation 并不是在一切都 slowing down 的意义上称得上 great（嘿，有些 technologies 表现得很好！），并把我们的注意力放在那些确实正在变糟的具体事物上；而且，或许将 accelerated sectors 与 decelerated ones 进行 contrasting，有助于弄清后者为什么会是现在这样。

[Dietz Vollrath](https://nintil.com/fully-grown/) 尝试过这样做，并找到了一系列 causes；在他看来，这些 causes 最终都很难 fix。难并不意味着不可能。如果他说问题在于 fertility rates going down，我就说，把目标定为 [one billion Americans](https://www.amazon.com/One-Billion-Americans-Thinking-Bigger-ebook/dp/B082ZR6827)；如果他说问题在于 aging population，我就说，把目标定为 [longevity research](https://nintil.com/longevity)。也许 Dietz 错了。也许 1970 年确实发生过一个 monocausal event。也许我应该写一篇 Nintil 对 1970 年究竟发生了什么的看法；那张 chart 呈现出现在的样子，想必是有原因的，而我们可以去把它找出来。

## 原文

Back in 2016 I wrote a Nintil classic, _[No great technological stagnation](https://nintil.com/no-great-technological-stagnation/)_ where I argued that, as far as we could see from public data, improvements in various technologies do not seem to be slowing down. If I were to rewrite it now I would note that trends in batteries and solar panels have continued, and I would add some new trends like for [single cell sequencing](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1334347237913022472) (A whole new category that didn't exist until 2009).

There is one problem with my analysis there that I left open,

> Are there less 'really innovative' innovations? \[That is, breakthroughs, paradigm shifts, historically significant inventions\]

Because it could be the case that each of the existing technologies was progressing just fine BUT at the same time the number of new ones (That would not just be another dot in the chart) could be going down.

Breakthroughs are harder to measure. Looking at the curve of, say, the price of photovoltaic cells is easy to point out progress there. It's a number, the number goes down, progress happens. Done.

![](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-1-20.png)

But progress in "science" or "technology" or the rate of "breakthroughs" is harder to measure. I didn't choose to go look for all those curves because I thought that such exercise fairly fully captures "science" or "technology", I did it because it's what is available to quantify rates of progress. At the same time I caveated that such an analysis has its limits. What would it take to say that we are living through a technological stagnation?

David Chapman recently [wrote an essay](https://metarationality.com/maps-and-territory) criticizing the maxim that "the map is not the territory". Isn't it obvious that the map is not the territory? Well but that misses the point. Believing that "the map is not the territory" implies a potential reification of the "map" as a thing and the "territory" as another thing. The actual point that Chapman makes there is that reality is messier than it seems, it's not just that the map is an imperfect representation of the territory: there are many maps. And there is a whole set of skills needed to know what map to use that are not directly implied by the maxim. Oh and more often than not there is no clearcut territory to point to. There is [no there there](https://www.wsj.com/articles/why-gertrude-steins-no-there-there-is-everywhere-1517589198).

My view of the great stagnation is similar. I think the framing "great stagnation" is misguided insofar as it seems to nudge us towards a single cause and towards thinking that everything is stagnating (That's the 'great' there). All these charts showing that "something happened" in 1970 have been leveraged to point to a single event that caused all sorts of things, from a slowing down of the R&D machine to TFP growth and median GDP growth. Yet we don't see trends in technological progress slowing down in 1970, it is not that case that progress in technology and science slowed down across the board at any point in time. Yes, great stagnation doesn't mean this if one reads closely some of the authors that have written on it. They will clearly talk about TFP and GDP. These are clean, crisply defined indicators, and yes there is a stagnation there. The framing also seem to push us towards spending time doing conceptual engineering to understand that "science", "technology", "stagnation", or "progress" are. I argued in the past that progress is [inherently](https://nintil.com/progress-in-progress/) a nebulous idea, so are the other ones. A good enough definition is enough to tell others what we mean. We can settle for that and move on.

But this, that there is indeed a TFP stagnation, does not imply that it is the R&D sector that is somehow at fault (Though it can also be that). It could be purely an institutional problem. It could be declining [fertility](https://www.amazon.com/Fully-Grown-Stagnant-Economy-Success/dp/022666600X). The science stagnation question is harder than measuring a stagnation in TFP or GDP growth. Science, unlike GDP, is not something that can be measured with a number. Countless are the times where someone has joked that we are living through a scientific golden age because more papers have been published than ever before. Of course we know that more papers does not mean more science. But what does?

One could try to come up with lists of [General Purpose Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_purpose_technology), the OG GPTs before [GPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI#GPT) was cool, but ask different people what those GPTs are and they will tell you different things. Or some will say that we are _just about to see the next GPT, that all the stagnation is just temporary, so nothing to worry about!_.

Ultimately those of us that study progress care about the same things: We want more of the good stuff and less of the bad stuff, and we want it to be so using less inputs for a given amount of output. The good news is that achieving that goal does not require us to answer whether "there is a technological or scientific stagnation". That question may be better answered with [無](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu_\(negative\)).

So here's a better idea: look at specific problems and think of potential solutions for them. Not every problem can be solved, and the likelihood that something cannot be solved, coupled with its usefulness, can be used to inform resource allocation. For example it seems that faster than light travel is not allowed by physics. Maybe [warp works](https://nintil.com/new-physics/)? Probably not. Huge if true. Worth funding a handful of people to work on that. But not betting a sizable chunk of the R&D budget when there are other things to do.

What about curing cancer? Why isn't cancer cured yet? Well, there we can point to a whole host of [reasons](https://nintil.com/malignant-review/) of the current field of oncology, and we could then go deeper into the biology of cancer or the funding ecosystem that supports said research. The knowledge needed to do that is very domain specific and not related to what we need to build spaceships or nuclear reactors.

Why don't we have [flying cars](https://rootsofprogress.org/where-is-my-flying-car)? Is it that the batteries were too heavy and expensive? Perhaps yes, but they have been making lots of progress lately so maybe it's not that. Is it the lack of a regulatory framework? Probably; in which case the right answer is lobbying and making the case for them to the relevant regulator. Or maybe simply they are not cost-effective. Coolness does not imply usefulness. To study flying cars we need to think about flying cars, lithium batteries, composite materials, the fluid mechanics of low-noise fans, the economics of short distance flights, and their regulations. By doing this we get close and personal with the subject matter and we get away from the very high level "great stagnation" view. This all said, the proverbial flying cars will eventually come. There's [Lilium](https://lilium.com/), there's [Kitty Hawk](https://kittyhawk.aero/), there's [Joby](https://www.jobyaviation.com/). [There](https://www.aeromobil.com/) [are](https://terrafugia.com/transition/) [many](https://www.opener.aero/) [others](https://www.pal-v.com/) [around](https://www.urbanaero.com/fancraft.html). It's not due to lack of trying. Obviously building flying cars is possible. In fact we had prototypes back in [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerocar). It is tempting to say that if flying cars were as easy as Storrs Hall make them seem we would have them already, therefore he's wrong. But that would be somewhat complacent. Instead the fair thing to do is look at why the actual efforts to build flying cars have stalled. [Regulations](https://www.hg.org/legal-articles/aviation-law-an-analysis-of-the-development-of-flying-cars-for-the-2020s-decade-52120), not physics, engineering, or economics, maybe be part of "it". Perhaps the industry tried to initially push for personally owned flying cars and that bumped against regulations; and now the emphasis seems to be on point-to-point flying taxis where the customer has no control over the vehicle, perhaps that is more to the taste of the regulators. Perhaps it's a [combination](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2018/jun/19/flying-cars-why-havent-they-taken-off-yet) of constraints: You could do horizontal takeoff winged flying cars (So you don't need to lift the weight with thrust) but then you need a runway so now you are limited to customers that live near an airport. So you need VTOLs. But VTOLs need lots of thrust, implying more power, more weight for fuel or batteries. If using fuel, you have a very hot exhaust directed towards the ground where there may be people, making it unsafe if taking off and landing in an uncontrolled (i.e. not an airport) environment, so you need to make it electric or use turboshafts. If the range provided is too short then customers may just get a regular car. Batteries have been getting better so that constraint is now being lifted. But VTOLs may be noisy as well so we needed improvements in blade geometry to reduce noise. Having multiple smaller propellers instead of a big one also helps with noise (Same principle as with aircraft turbine [chevrons](https://nintil.com/2-you-didnt-invent-that-aircraft-turbine-chevrons-edition)). Maybe a problem is just size, if you can't park near the local grocery store because your flying car is big you may decide to stick with the regular car if you can't afford to own two cars.

This discussion could of course be longer, but the point is that when you see lots of attempts taking a long time, _perhaps_ the problem is not complacency and the domain is harder than it looks like to us who are not in the industry. Perhaps we would look like this story:

> The year is 10000. Compute power has increase due to improvements to ISAs and materials to better cool processors. But feature size has been stuck at the 1nm level for a while. Somewhere deep in TSMC (Intel went broke in 2050), someone is being yelled at. "We've been on 1nm for decades now! At historical rates of progress we should be building chips under the Planck scale now! Why are you so slow!" "Sir, for the nth time, ~I'm the janitor~ fundamental physics prevents us from continuing historical trends from the XX and XXI centuries. Can't do that! We are focusing on producing better coprocessors and increasing power efficiency in other ways" "Why such complacency! Just keep trying!" And trying they kept, as they had for the previous millennia.

Rampant optimism is a recipe for constant disappointment and self-flagellation. Just because complacency is bad doesn't mean we should be making unrealistic demands of builders or reifying historical trends and demanding that they continue into future. Even [Boom Aerospace](https://nintil.com/building-skyscrapers-and-spending-on-major-projects/), entrepreneurial startup mentality and all, is taking as long as traditional aerospace companies to get their aircraft in the air. Is it reasonable to head down to Denver, Colorado and yell at Blake Scholl "Dude, why so slow!?"? Of course not.

It is only by coming down from the narratives and abstractions into the concrete and specific that we can start to think of concrete domain-specific actions, roadmaps, or bottleneck analysis that could be done to make more progress in problems that matter. This is I think the right way to think about the stagnation. Leave the big picture aside, and do a root cause analysis for why the world is not as awesome as it should be right now. If in the process of doing that we discover a single root cause (Is society as a whole getting complacent?) great. But it's unlikely that _just that_ is the cause, or even the main cause, of less-than-maximal rates of progress in every single field, including those are are living through an actual golden age.

## Addendum: What if something happened in 1970?

Something could have happened in [1970](https://wtfhappenedin1971.com/). That is not something I reject. Suppose, for the sake of the argument that this one thing was going off the [gold standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixon_shock). Or something about [oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970s_energy_crisis). I don't think so. Above I granted that **there is a great stagnation in TFP and GDP**. I questioned that such a stagnation is a _technological stagnation across the board_ and than rather to try to figure out if it is or not, we should go case by case and see why things go fast or slow. For the actual great stagnation, one could do an analysis of why, at the economy level, GDP growth slowed down, and maybe one finds a single cause of many. This grants that the stagnation is not great in the sense that everything is slowing down (Hey, some technologies are doing well!) and puts our focus on the concrete things that are going bad, and perhaps contrasting the accelerated sectors with the decelerated ones helps tease out why the latter are how they are.
