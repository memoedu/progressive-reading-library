# Year-and-a-bit in review, 2014: where I've been

---

## 正文

每隔一段时间，我都会停下来，盘点自己最近做了些什么，再想想接下来想往哪里走。（我有些想定期这样做，但总是太容易被其他博客文章分心。）这次也属于分心的情况，所以这篇回顾涵盖了自我在 January 2014 写下[上一篇回顾](https://www.benkuhn.net/review)以来的所有事情。以下是我一直在做的事：

### 离开 Harvard

我在 Harvard 的前三年过得很愉快，但到了大三结束时，我[认定再读第四年不会有多少收获](https://www.benkuhn.net/h)，于是就离开了。

我觉得这是个相当不错的决定。在 Theorem 工作让我学到了非常多的东西，而且我并不觉得自己错过了什么特定的课程或大学体验。我曾担心 Harvard Effective Altruism 会遇到困难，但事实证明这种担忧毫无根据。第一次住在 Boston 以外的地方，是一段有意思的经历。另外，今年能向 effective charities 捐出相当一笔钱，也让我很高兴。

不上学有一个被低估的好处：它让我拥有了多得多的 general agency。作为 full-time programmer，你受到的重视远远超过作为大学生时，即使两者的区别只不过是 Harvard 的 student information system 中某处有一个 bit，标示着“休学”。

### 在 Theorem 工作

促使我离开 Harvard 的一个因素，是 Theorem 提供了一份很有吸引力的工作机会。到目前为止，这件事的结果也很好。我在那里的工作依然有意思，而且我似乎做得不错。

回头看[我当初如何选定 Theorem](https://www.benkuhn.net/job)，对于自己的 priorities，我有几点想法：

-   我仍然很庆幸自己加入了 Theorem 这样一家 small company，但当时对 small size 的一些弊端认识不足。尤其是，我向同事学习的机会比较少（不是在批评他们；只是我的同事人数更少！），特别是向 senior coworkers 学习的机会。

-   多学一些 machine learning，大概是个好主意。这是一个在智力上颇具挑战性的 field，有着种类广泛的 applications。

-   Theorem 的 product（一种 investment vehicle）有几个我之前没意识到的优点。它没有 user-facing apps 所带来的许多麻烦：我们不必具备 user interface 或 24/7 uptime，而且我们能完全 control 自己的 code 所 runs 的 environment。这意味着，我花在处理典型 software-industry 烦心事上的时间少了很多。

-   我已经调整了看法，对 tech industry work 的 long-term prospects 稍微更不安了一些。各种各样的 Bootcamps 正在显著 lowering barriers to entry，而且我担心 private companies 目前的 valuations 可能已经 unsustainably high（其中的 correction 会 reduce 对 tech work 的 demand）。

### 通过 Earning to give 来挣钱捐赠

这方面目前没什么可说的。去年，我把 annual donation 的一半捐给了 GiveWell。另一半捐给哪里，我还没有决定——由于 withholding 出了岔子，我不得不等自己的 tax return。为了 transparency，我开始为自己的 donations 保留一份[记录](https://www.benkuhn.net/ea/)。

今年，我希望捐出 cash income 的 40-50%。我的 expenses 相当低，因此这样做仍能让我存下相当一笔钱。（我也计划捐出自己在 Theorem 的 equity 所带来的 proceeds，按目前的 valuation，这超过了我 salary 的一半；不过，我没有把它计入今年的 donations，因为在可预见的未来，它仍将是 illiquid。）

由于今年我要捐出相当大一笔钱，我打算从现在到六月之间，对自己的 options 做一次更认真的 evaluation；我希望在六月捐出今年 donation 的第一部分。我不确定自己能否赶上这个时间安排，因为我预计 evaluating 会花上一段时间，但这个截止期限会帮助我避免拖延做出 rigorous decision。

### 学习东西

过去一年，我学到的大部分东西都与 machine learning 有关。这非常有用，但我担心自己可能已经开始遇到 diminishing returns。具体来说，在 machine learning 的 hierarchy of skill 中，我感觉在“足以 read papers/implement new algorithms”和“足以在一个新的 domain 中 achieve breakthrough performance”之间，存在一个 regime，其中的 returns to skill 主要在于 squeezing out 一点点额外的 predictive performance，而这似乎并没有那么有用。

### 写博客

这方面比我预想的更顺利。有[两篇](https://www.benkuhn.net/nonprog)我的[文章](https://www.benkuhn.net/rha)登上了 Hacker News 首页，合计带来了将近 30,000 名访客。[第三篇](https://www.benkuhn.net/matching)则被 Paul Christiano 和 Katja Grace 的 [impact purchase](http://impactpurchase.org/) program 部分 purchased。另外，我的博客也让一些人对 effective altruism 更感兴趣，这大概是它最重要、且 easily-measurable 的 impact。

话虽如此，最近我越来越难想到自己觉得值得写的有趣素材。我觉得，与 [1.5-2 年前](https://www.google.com/search?q=site%3Abenkuhn.net+2013..2014)相比，我的文章在平均质量、实用性和趣味性上都有所下降，不过这很难确定。

最近，我把目标更新频率从每周两次降到了每周一次，以便更专注于写出更深入的文章。但我不确定，努力坚持某种固定频率，是否就是写出有趣文章的正确办法。另一方面，如果没有 [Beeminder](https://www.beeminder.com/)在后面催着，我大概也写不了这么多。

总的来说，我仍然觉得博客没有发挥出应有的潜力。我知道自己的文章有时能写得多好，但不知道怎样才能稳定地达到那个水平，也不知道与把时间花在其他事情上相比，这是否值得付出这些努力。

## 原文

Every so often, I step back, take stock of what I’ve been up to recently, and figure out where I’d like to go next. (I have some ambition of doing this on a regular schedule, but I’m too often distracted by other blog posts.) Since this was one of the times I got distracted, this review covers everything since I wrote [the last one](https://www.benkuhn.net/review) in January 2014. Here’s what I’ve been doing:

### Leaving Harvard

I enjoyed my first three years at Harvard, but by the end of my junior year I [decided I wouldn’t get much out of a fourth](https://www.benkuhn.net/h) and skipped out.

I think this was a pretty great decision. I’ve learned an incredible amount by working at Theorem, and I don’t feel like I’m missing out on any particular courses or college experiences. I had worried that Harvard Effective Altruism would struggle, but that turned out to be unfounded. It’s been interesting to live outside Boston for the first time. Plus, I’m happy that this year I can contribute a substantial amount to effective charities.

One underrated benefit of not being in school is that it gives me a lot more general agency. You get taken much more seriously as a full-time programmer than a college student, even if the difference between the two is a bit somewhere in Harvard’s student information system saying “on leave.”

### Working at Theorem

One factor that helped convince me to jump ship from Harvard was a compelling job offer from Theorem. So far, that’s been panning out as well. My work there continues to be interesting and I seem to be doing well at it.

Looking back at [how I decided on Theorem](https://www.benkuhn.net/job), I have a couple thoughts about my priorities:

-   I’m still glad that I joined a small company like Theorem, but I wasn’t as aware of some of the downsides of small size. In particular, I have less opportunity to learn from coworkers (no knock on them; I simply have fewer coworkers!), especially senior coworkers.

-   Learning more machine learning was probably a good idea. It’s an intellectually challenging field with a broad variety of applications.

-   Theorem’s product (an investment vehicle) was cool from a few angles I didn’t realize. It doesn’t come with many of the schleps that user-facing apps have: we don’t have to have a user interface or 24/7 uptime, and we have total control over the environment our code runs in. That means I spend a lot less time dealing with typical software-industry annoyances.

-   I’ve updated to be slightly more antsy about the long-term prospects for tech industry work. Bootcamps of various stripes are lowering the barriers to entry significantly, and I’m worried that current valuations of private companies might be unsustainably high (a correction in which would reduce demand for tech work).

### Earning to give

There’s little to report here so far. Last year I gave half of my annual donation to GiveWell. I haven’t decided where to give the other half yet—I had to wait for my tax return because of a withholding snafu. I started keeping a [log](https://www.benkuhn.net/ea/) of my donations, for transparency.

This year I hope to donate 40-50% of my cash income. My expenses are quite low, so this will still let me save a substantial amount. (I also plan to donate the proceeds from my equity in Theorem, which at its current valuation is more than half of my salary; but I’m not counting that towards the year’s donations since it will remain illiquid for the foreseeable future.)

Since I’m giving a large amount this year, I aim to do a more serious evaluation of my options between now and June, when I want to make the first chunk of this year’s donation. I’m not sure if I’ll be able to manage this timetable, since I expect to spend a while evaluating, but the deadline will help me avoid procrastinating on making a rigorous decision.

### Learning things

Most of the stuff I’ve learned over the past year has been about machine learning. That’s been very useful, but I’m worried that I may be hitting diminishing returns to it. Specifically, in the machine learning hierarchy of skill, it feels like there’s a regime between “good enough to read papers/implement new algorithms” and “good enough to achieve breakthrough performance in a new domain” where the returns to skill are mainly about squeezing out a little bit of extra predictive performance, which doesn’t seem that useful.

### Blogging

This went better than I expected. [Two](https://www.benkuhn.net/nonprog) of my [posts](https://www.benkuhn.net/rha) made the front page of Hacker News, netting almost 30,000 visitors combined. A [third](https://www.benkuhn.net/matching) was partially purchased by Paul Christiano and Katja Grace’s [impact purchase](http://impactpurchase.org/) program. Plus, my blog has played a role in getting a number of people more interested in effective altruism, which is probably its most important easily-measurable impact.

That said, I’ve been having more difficulty lately coming up with material that I think is interesting to write about. I think my average post quality/usefulness/interestingness has gone down somewhat since [1.5-2 years ago](https://www.google.com/search?q=site%3Abenkuhn.net+2013..2014), though it’s hard to be sure.

I recently downgraded my target frequency from twice a week to once a week so that I could focus more on writing more in-depth posts. But I’m not sure that trying to stick to any regular frequency is the right way to generate interesting posts. On the other hand, without [Beeminder](https://www.beeminder.com/) breathing down my neck, I probably wouldn’t write nearly as much.

The upshot is that I still feel like the blog is operating below potential. I know how good my posts can sometimes be, but I don’t know how to reliably get them there, and I don’t know if it’s worth the effort compared to other ways of spending my time.
