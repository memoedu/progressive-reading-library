# 那篇胡扯性别差距的文章｜That bogus gender gap article

---

## 正文

上周，Quartz 发表了一篇题为[“tech salaries 中不存在 gender gap”](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=7334659)的文章。结果，整个 internet 上出现了大量 linkbait 跟风 posts，从鲜为人知的 livejournals 到 [Smithsonian.com](http://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/female-computer-scientists-make-same-salary-their-male-counterparts-180949965/?no-ist)，无处不在。考虑到所 cited 的主要 study 只考察了恰好一年前以 B.S. graduated 的人，这些 claims 实在过于斩钉截铁，更不用说，这项 study 所作出的实际上是 opposite claim。

让我们看看这些 posts 全都 cite 的 [AAUW study](http://www.aauw.org/files/2013/02/graduating-to-a-pay-gap-the-earnings-of-women-and-men-one-year-after-college-graduation.pdf?_ga=1.7578036.722397424.1379578621)中的 evidence。

![你打算相信谁，相信我，还是相信你那双撒谎的眼睛？](https://danluu.com/images/gender-gap/actual_result.webp)

看来，在“engineering and engineering technology”中，women 的收入是 men 的 88%，而在“computer and information sciences”中，则是 men 的 77%。

这项 study 对若干 factors 进行 controls for，以尝试找出 pay gap 的 source。它发现，在对 self-reported hours worked、type of employment 和 quality of school 进行 controlling for 之后，“pay gap 中 over one-third 无法由这些 factors 中的任何一个 explained，而且似乎 attributable to gender alone”。One-third 不是 zero，12% 或 23% 的 one-third 也不是。如果这听起来很少，不妨想想 post-2008 economy 中的 average raise，以及 23% 的那个 one-third 相当于多少年的 experience。

Quartz 的文章 claims，既然整个 gap 都可以由一些 variables explained，那么这个 gap 就是 choice 的结果。事实上，这项 study 明确指出这种观点是 false，并 cite 了 [Stender v. Lucky Stores](http://scholar.google.com/scholar_case?case=2721168065773704326) 和一项[相关的 study](http://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/evoice/v4y2007i4n5.html)[1](https://danluu.com/gender-gap/#fn:B)，说道：“这个 case illustrates，当 employers mistakenly assume female employees 更喜欢传统上由 women 担任的 lower-paid positions，并且——无论是否有意——将 men 和 women place 到 different jobs 中，ensuring men 获得 higher pay、women 获得 lower pay 时，discrimination 如何能够在 pay gap 的 explained portion 中 play a role”。事实上，Women 并非就是想要 lower paying jobs；这又一次与 Quartz 文章中的 claims diametrically opposed。

请注意，这项 study 对 reduce pay gap 的 factors 进行 selectively controls for，却没有对 increase 它的 factors 这样做。例如，这项 study 指出：“Women 在 college 获得的 grades，on average，比 men 更高，因此 academic achievement 并不能帮助我们理解 gender pay gap”。对 grades 进行 Adjusting for 会 increase pay gap；对所有可能的 confounding factors 进行 adjusting for，而不只是对 reduce gap 的 factors 这样做，只会让 adjusted pay gap 更大。

AAUW study 并不是 Quartz 的 post 所 cites 的唯一 evidence。为了 support “尽管有 strong evidence suggesting gender pay equality，但仍普遍存在 women 的收入少于 men 的 perception”这一 conclusion，Quartz 作者还 cites 了另外三项 evidence。首先是 BLS 的 figure：“按 hourly 而不是 annually measured 时，men 与 women 之间的 pay gap 是 14%，而不是 23%”；14% 不是 0%。其次是[一份 BLS report](http://www.bls.gov/cps/cpswom2012.pdf?_ga=1.211369494.241714935.1393863612)，它 indicates men 的收入高于 women，作者却从中 cherry picking 了一个 women 表现优于 men 的 figure（“每周 work between 30 and 39 hours 的 women……见 table 4”）；这个 claim 是 incorrect 的[2](https://danluu.com/gender-gap/#fn:2)。第三是一项 80s 的 study，2012 年的 AAUW report 与它 directly contradicted；那项较早的 study indicates，cohort effects 是 gender gap 的原因，但 AAUW report 尽管只 studying 了 single cohort，仍然显示存在 gender gap。

Smithsonian Mag 针对其文章受到的批评发表了更正，但大多数信息有误的文章仍未更正。

很明显，Quartz 那篇文章的作者心中先有了 agenda，再挑出 supported 那个 agenda 的 evidence，然后写成了一篇 blog post。不少 bloggers 跟进了这篇 post，将它的 thesis 用作 link bait，为自己的网站吸引 hits，却没有读过任何 cited evidence。如果这就是“digitally native news”的运作方式，那我选择退出。

**如果你喜欢这篇文章，你可能也会喜欢[这篇讨论 markets 与 discrimination 之间 interaction 的 post](https://danluu.com/tech-discrimination/)，以及[这篇 post，它对为何如此多人 drop out of science and engineering 作出了很不完整的 explanation](https://danluu.com/teach-debugging/)。**

## 原文

Last week, Quartz published an article titled [“There is no gender gap in tech salaries”](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=7334659). That resulted in linkbait copycat posts all over the internet, from obscure livejournals to [Smithsonian.com](http://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/female-computer-scientists-make-same-salary-their-male-counterparts-180949965/?no-ist). The claims are awfully strong, considering that the main study cited only looked at people who graduated with a B.S. exactly one year ago, not to mention the fact that the study makes literally the opposite claim.

Let's look at the evidence from the [AAUW study](http://www.aauw.org/files/2013/02/graduating-to-a-pay-gap-the-earnings-of-women-and-men-one-year-after-college-graduation.pdf?_ga=1.7578036.722397424.1379578621) that all these posts cite.

![Who are you going to believe, me or your lying eyes?](https://danluu.com/images/gender-gap/actual_result.webp)

Looks like women make 88% of what men do in “engineering and engineering technology” and 77% of what men do in “computer and information sciences”.

The study controls for a number of factors to try to find the source of the pay gap. It finds that after controlling for self-reported hours worked, type of employment, and quality of school, “over one-third of the pay gap cannot be explained by any of these factors and appears to be attributable to gender alone”. One-third is not zero, nor is one-third of 12% or 23%. If that sounds small, consider an average raise in the post-2008 economy and how many years of experience that one-third of 23% turns into.

The Quartz article claims that, since the entire gap can be explained by some variables, the gap is by choice. In fact, the study explicitly calls out that view as being false, citing [Stender v. Lucky Stores](http://scholar.google.com/scholar_case?case=2721168065773704326) and a [related study](http://ideas.repec.org/a/bpj/evoice/v4y2007i4n5.html)[1](https://danluu.com/gender-gap/#fn:B), saying that “The case illustrates how discrimination can play a role in the explained portion of the pay gap when employers mistakenly assume that female employees prefer lower-paid positions traditionally held by women and --intentionally or not--place men and women into different jobs, ensuring higher pay for men and lower pay for women”. Women do not, in fact, just want lower paying jobs; this is, once again, diametrically opposed to the claims in the Quartz article.

Note that the study selectively controls for factors that reduce the pay gap, but not for factors that increase it. For instance, the study notes that “Women earn higher grades in college, on average, than men do, so academic achievement does not help us understand the gender pay gap”. Adjusting for grades would increase the pay gap; adjusting for all possible confounding factors, not only the factors that reduce the gap, would only make the adjusted pay gap larger.

The AAUW study isn't the only evidence the Quartz post cites. To support the conclusion that “Despite strong evidence suggesting gender pay equality, there is still a general perception that women earn less than men do”, the Quartz author cites three additional pieces of evidence. First, the BLS figure that, “when measured hourly, not annually, the pay gap between men and women is 14% not 23%”; 14% is not 0%. Second, [a BLS report](http://www.bls.gov/cps/cpswom2012.pdf?_ga=1.211369494.241714935.1393863612) that indicates that men make more than women, cherry picking a single figure where women do better than men (“women who work between 30 and 39 hours a week … see table 4”); this claim is incorrect[2](https://danluu.com/gender-gap/#fn:2). Third, a study from the 80s which is directly contradicted by the AAUW report from 2012; the older study indicates that cohort effects are responsible for the gender gap, but the AAUW report shows a gender gap despite studying only a single cohort.

The Smithsonian Mag published a correction in response to criticism about their article, but most of the mis-informed articles remain uncorrected.

It's clear that the author of the Quartz piece had an agenda in mind, picked out evidence that supported that agenda, and wrote a blog post. A number of bloggers picked up the post and used its thesis as link bait to drive hits to their sites, without reading any of the cited evidence. If this is how “digitally native news” works, I'm opting out.
