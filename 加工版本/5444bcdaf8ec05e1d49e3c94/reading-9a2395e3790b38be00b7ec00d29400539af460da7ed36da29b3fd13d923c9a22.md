## 为什么 science 中的 women 可能较少

如果 men 和 women 的 brains 是 equal，那么除了 cultural factors（其中包括 discrimination），似乎就没有任何理由预期会有 differences。但有人提出了以下几种 differences：

-   Men 的 intelligence distribution 具有 higher variance。因此，在 distribution 的 higher end（你的 STEM graduates 就来自这里），你会预期发现更多 men。low paid jobs 的情况也一样。
-   Men 比 women 更愿意 work more／更 competitive。这会导致 men 在 highly competitive fields，或者 demand 大量 work 的 fields 中出现 overrepresentation。
-   在 overall intelligence 的 level 相同的情况下，women 具有 higher verbal IQ。这意味着，与 men 相比，women 有更多可走的 paths，因此，尽管她们在 technical careers 中可以做得和 men 一样好，她们也更容易 choose 其他 non-STEM careers。
-   最后，men 和 women 在 choice of occupation 方面的 preferences 可能不同，从而 directly driving 各 career 中 genders 的 distribution。

据说，这些 traits 有 evolutionary explanation，因此，如果上述 explanations 确实是 correct，就会 predict 各 countries 都有同样的情况。technical fields 中会有更多 men，而 humanities 和 healthcare sectors 中会有更多 women。

另一方面，也可以 postulate 各种 discrimination：

-   Women 被 prohibited from attending university。这会 reduce female graduates 的 amount。
-   Women 在 culturally 层面受到 disencouraged，无法 attending university，或攻读某些 degrees。结果同上。
-   一种 toxic workplace/college culture（techbros）使 women 远离这些地方。这同样会 reduces women 在这些 fields 中的 presence。

这些 hypothesis 也可以用来作出 predictions：sexism 越少，distributions 就会越 equal。

因为

> computer science 中 women 的 share 开始 falling，大致就在 personal computers 开始大量进入 U.S. 家庭的同一时期。

> 这些早期的 personal computers 不过是玩具。你可以玩 pong 或 simple shooting games，也许还可以做一些 word processing。而这些玩具[几乎](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1CDkHs4lzUo) [完全](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rxNjx_VWJ8U)是面向 men 和 boys marketed 的。
> 
> computers 是给 boys 用的这一 idea 成了一种 narrative。它成了我们向自己讲述 computing revolution 的故事。它帮助 define 了谁是 geeks，并 created 了 techie culture。
> 
> 像 _Weird Science, Revenge of the Nerds_ 和 _War Games_ 这样的电影都在 '80s 上映。它们的剧情简介几乎可以互换：一个笨拙的 geek boy genius 运用 tech savvy 战胜逆境，赢得姑娘的芳心。
> 
> 在 1990s，researcher [Jane Margolis](http://gseis.ucla.edu/directory/jane-margolis/)interviewed 了 Carnegie Mellon University 的数百名 computer science students，该校的 programs 位居全国前列。她发现，家庭给 boys 买 computers 的可能性远大于给 girls 买——即使家里的 girls 对 computers 确实很感兴趣。
> 
> 当这些孩子上大学时，这一点影响很大。随着 personal computers 越来越普及，computer science professors 越来越倾向于 assumed，他们的 students 是从小在家玩 computers 长大的。

[这里](http://www.apa.org/action/resources/research-in-action/share.aspx)还有别人发给我的、反对 evolutionary model 的 evidence。文章说：

-   boys 和 girls 在 math performance 上没有 differences（Shibley Hyde (1990)）
-   verbal ability 上没有 major differences（Hyde (1988)）
-   cognition、communication style、social 和 personality variables、motor behaviors 以及 moral reasoning 中的 differences 是 Small 或不存在的。（Hyde, 2005）
-   math 和 science 中的 Gender differences 具有 genetic basis，并在 early childhood emerge。但总体而言，men 和 women 对 math 和 science possess equal aptitude。（Spelke, 2005）
-   Hyde、Diane Halpern 等人在 2007 发表了一份关于 science 和 math careers 中 male/female disparity 的 consensus statement。他们说，women 在 verbal abilities 上往往比 men score slighly higher，而 men 在 visuospatial skills 上具有 slight edge。但他们也说，early experience、education policies 和 culture 同样 affect math 和 science 中的 success。
-   grades 2 到 11 的 children 在 maths 上没有 gender differences（Hyde, 2008）
-   在 abilities 的 highest levels，men 在 mathematics 上比 women score higher（men 的 distribution 更 heavy tailed），但这一 gap 一直在 decreasing。研究发现，在 gender equality 更高的 countries，这一 gap 更 smaller（正如 sexism hypothesis 所 predicts 的那样）。（Hyde, 2008）

直觉上，这似乎为 sexism model 提供了不错的 case。但请注意，这些 studies 并未 address 诸如不同的 preferences（对 careers 或 amount of work 的 preferences），而且这些 studies 确实 acknowledge，women 在 verbal skills 上 score higher，而 men 则在 visuospatial skills 上 score higher。

例如，设想 men 和 women 是 cognitively equal，他们只在 preferences 上 differ。如果 preferences 的 differences 足够大，这仍会 drive 不同的 distribution。

不过，[这里](https://www.psychologytoday.com/blog/rabble-rouser/201707/why-brilliant-girls-tend-favor-non-stem-careers)是支持 evolutionary model 的 case：

-   研究发现，preferences 中的 Differences（N=500,000）是 gigantic 的（Su et al. 2009）。Women prefer working with people，men prefer working with things。这种 effects 在 monkeys 中也存在。

-   dual talented（在 maths 和 verbal skills 方面）的 girls 比 boys 多 70%；boys 具有 strong math skills、却没有 strong verbal skills 的可能性，是 girls 的两倍。作为 students 时只有 strong math skills 的人，后来更可能 working in STEM。作为 students 时同时具有 strong math 和 verbal skills 的人，后来较不可能 working in STEM。因此

-   _“Results revealed，同时具有 high verbal skills 的 mathematically capable individuals，比那些具有 high math skills 但 verbal skills 为 moderate 的 individuals 更不可能 pursue STEM careers。一个值得注意的 finding 是，具有 high math 和 high verbal ability 的 group included 的 females 多于 males……_

    _我们的 study provides evidence，causes females 去 pursue non-STEM careers 的并不是 lack of ability，而是具有 high math ability 的 females 也具有 high verbal ability 的 likelihood 更大，因此，与具有 high math ability 的 male peers 相比，她们可以考虑 wider range 的 occupations；这些 male peers 则更可能具有 moderate verbal ability。”_(Wang, Eccles, Kenny, 2013)

另外还有[这里](https://www.adamsmith.org/blog/miscellaneous/are-there-sex-and-gender-differences-in-science-and-technology)：

-   Boys 和 girls 在 math 上 on average 是 equally good 的。但 male skills 的 distribution 的 variance 更 higher。（Lindberg et. al  2010）
-   在 mental ability 的 highest levels，往往每有一名 woman，就有两名 men（Johnson et al. 2008, Deary 2007）
-   math skills 中的 Male-female differences 并非由 stereotypes 导致（Flore & Wicherts, 2015）
-   Women 希望 work less，并拥有更多 flexibility（Lubinski et al. 2014），这也 explains 了为什么在具有 constant return to hours 的 industries 中没有 ex-ante wage gap，而在 working more 会使 productivity increases more than proportionately 的 industries 中，这种 gap 很大（Goldin, 2014）
-   最后，当观察 distributions 的 tails 时，mean 的 differences 可能被 magnified。

因此，CS 中 women 较少这一 fact 在各 countries 都 holds，而且并非由更高的 gender equality 所 driven，正如我们在那些 CS 中 women 的 percentage 领先的 Arab countries 所见。Sweden 的确比 US 有更多 women，但仍远低于假如 differences 是由 sexism 导致时人们可能 expect 的 50/50 split。而且，这不仅限于 computer science：各 countries 都出现了一种 trend：topic 越 social 或 health related，women 就越多。Conversely，越 abstract、越 thing related，men 就越多。

回到我们 1985 年的 peak，reddit 上的一些人尝试提出了一些 explanations。其中一个就是 NPR 的 explanation，即 computers 被视为 male 的东西。**但这并不能 explain 为什么 male CS enrollment 也 fell**。另一种 explanation 不是 women 因为 computers 是 male 的东西而不再喜欢它们，而是 men 开始更喜欢 computers，因为它们逐渐被视为可以 hack 和 tinker 的东西，而不是无聊的 office tool。[这里提出的](http://blessingofkings.blogspot.co.uk/2014/10/women-in-computer-science.html)另一种可能的 explanation 是，men 更 financially driven，而且他们受到 CS careers 的 attracted 程度超过 women。这种 explanation 旨在 explain 为什么 time series 中出现了两个 bubbles。**computers-are-for-boys thesis 并不能 explain 这一点**。我们知道，2000s 初期有过 dotcom bubble。但 1985 年前后也有 bubble 吗？

没有 financial bubble，确实没有。但当时存在 capacity contraints ([Roberts, 2016](http://cs.stanford.edu/people/eroberts/CSCapacity.pdf))。随着 personal computing 的 boom，越来越多的人 enrolled into CS，colleges 发现自己没有足够的 capacity 来满足 growing demand。于是，它们 restricted 了向新 students 开放的 positions 的 number。让这个 process 更 competitive（即在 distribution of skills 中 sampling 的位置进一步向右移动），就会得到相对更多的 men。

> 然而，如果仔细观察 1980s 的 downturn，就很快会清楚，student enrollments 的 collapse 的原因与 student interest 完全无关。在那整个时期，学生对 computer science courses 和 degrees 的 demand 始终很高。1980s 中期的学生并不是决定不再 majoring in computer science，而是缺乏 resources 来接纳他们的 departments 禁止他们这样做。
> 
> 我认为，1980s 发生的事情最好被 described 为一次 capacity collapse：universities 和 colleges 根本无法满足不断增长的 student demand。Departments 尝试了多种 strategies 来 increase 它们的 teaching capacity，包括对来自其他 disciplines 的 faculty 进行 retraining，以及从 industry 中 hiring adjunct faculty。然而，最终 demand overwhelmed capacity，colleges 和 universities 被迫 restrict admission to the computer science major，从而引发了随后的 downturn。$$...$$
> 
> （引自一份 1981 年的 report）：
> 
> faculty 承受着巨大的 Pressures。在美国，拥有 Ph.D. 的 Computer Science faculty 从 1975 年的 805 人增长到 1979 年的 837 人——几乎没有 growth。同一时期，undergraduate student 对 Computer Science 的 demand 每年 risen 15 percent 至 20 percent。由于如此 overburdened，faculty 无法找到足够的时间来 conduct research，或 supervise graduate students in research。这种氛围构成了强烈的 incentive，促使 research-oriented faculty 到 industrial research groups 中寻找 positions。Departments 必须设法让 faculty 有更多时间与他们的 graduate students 一起 exploring new ideas，同时继续 fulfill teaching commitments。考虑到 rising enrollments 所体现的 societal need，Limiting 或 cutting back enrollments 将是 counterproductive。从长远来看，满足这一 need 的唯一办法，就是 train、hire 并 retain 新的 faculty。
> 
> $$...$$
> 
> academia 的回应：
> 
> 在一个 positions 的数量达到 applicants 数量多达 seven 倍的 labor market 中，寻找 faculty 是一项非同寻常的挑战；面对这一挑战，universities 和 colleges 被迫采用其他 strategies 来 build 它们的 teaching capacity。这些 strategies 包括 • Increasing teaching loads 和 class sizes • Hiring 更多 part-time 和 adjunct faculty • 对来自其他 disciplines 的 faculty 进行 Retraining • Limiting enrollments 以及进入该 major 的 access
> 
> $$...$$
> 
> 尽管 institutions 尝试了许多 strategies 来 expand 它们的 teaching capacity，最终仍无法应付 student demand 持续不断的 increase。虽然 Snowbird 1980 的 report 曾警告说，“考虑到 rising enrollments 所体现的 societal need，limiting 或 cutting back enrollments 将是 counterproductive”，但 **universities 和 colleges 被迫这样做了。这些 limitations 大多基于 academic performance，而且是极其 restrictive 的。例如，在 1980s 中期的 Berkeley，只有拥有 4.0 GPA 的 students 才会被 admitted 到 Electrical Engineering and Computer Science 的 major。** 那些年，我正在 Wellesley College 担任新成立的 Department of Computer Science 的负责人。尽管我们比许多 colleges 更幸运，能够在招聘时吸引少量 applicants，但真正 making actual appointments 仍几乎不可能。在 1982-83 年，Wellesley 发出了 six 份 offers，才找到一个愿意接受该 position 的人。我们的大多数 candidates 接受了其他地方提供的、salaries 更高的 competing offers，既有来自 industry 的，也有来自 academia 的。不幸的是，那一年 hiring 一个人不足以跟上学生对 computer science major 不断增长的 interest。1983 年，Wellesley 决定 restrict access to the major，只 accepting 达到 minimum GPA threshold 的 students。虽然 limiting access to the major 确实 reduce 了 class sizes，但在实现改善 faculty 的 working conditions 这一更广泛的 goal 上，它并不 effective。Enrollment limitations 不受学生——以及他们的父母——欢迎，这很自然。Imposing 这样的 restrictions 会使 faculty 与 students 的 relationship 变得 adversarial，导致 students 变得更加 competitive，而且在很多情况下感到愤怒。Teaching 变得远没有以前那么令人愉快，我最终离开了 Wellesley，去了一个 research lab。
> 
> **GPA thresholds 的 imposition，以及其他用于 reduce enrollment 的 strategies，自然而然地改变了学生对 computer science 的看法。在 1970s，学生进入这个新颖而令人兴奋的 field 时受到热烈欢迎。1984 年前后，一切都变了。Departments 不再欢迎学生，而是开始试图把他们拒之门外。学生领会了这个信息，并得出结论：这里不需要他们。在随后的几年里，computer science 是 competitive 且不欢迎人的这一观念广为流传，甚至开始影响那些并未对该 major imposed limitations 的 institutions。**

### 结论（Conclusion）

Women 与 men 是 different 的，无论在 physically、intellectually 方面，还是在他们 prefer 的事物上。这是 sexism 吗？这并不是 moral claim。这与声称 men 平均而言比 women 更 violent、更 taller、更 stronger 一样，是 true 的。这是 sexism 吗？如果是，那么现实就是 sexist 的，而我们必须接受它。但按照 sexism 的一个合理 definition，它并不是。Sexism 将意味着 discouraging women 进入某些 fields，把她们视为 morally inferior，或 banning 她们从事某些 occupations。tech-bro culture 或许可以算作这种情况。但它似乎并不是我们跨 countries、fields of study 和时间所 observe 到的现象的最大 driver：men cluster 在某些 areas，而 women 则在另一些 areas。

evolutionary hypothesis 是 holds 的：我们确实对某些 fields 有强烈的 sex based biases。除此之外，我们还会 react to incentives（同样由 personality 的 differences 所 mediated），而我们的 behaviour 也受到 culturally influences。这 three 种 explanations 可以同时存在。尝试给这些 parameters assign weights 的 literature 并不多。我们可以 rule out 100% sexism thesis：[Lippa et al.](http://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0095960)的研究 find，occupations 之间 gender 的 variance 中，有 40% 主要由 things vs people orientation 所 explained。另一部分可能源于一种巧合：highly quantitative 且 things-oriented 的 fields 是 well rewarded 的。在美国（以及 developed world 的其他地方），某个 industry 中的 [percentage of male](http://www.randalolson.com/2015/08/16/u-s-college-majors-median-yearly-earnings-vs-gender-ratio/) 与它的 rewarded 程度之间存在 correlation。

回顾一下，以下 facts 需要得到 explained：

-   在 quantitative fields 中，men 比 women 更多
-   在 highly paying jobs 中，men 比 women 更多
-   在 less gender equal countries 的 quantitative jobs 中，women 比 men 更多（参见 Saudi Arabia vs Sweden。但这一 effect 可能在更高的 levels of income 下 weaken）

我已经给出了 evidence，以 support 这些 claims：这些 facts 可以由一个关于 men 与 women 之间 different cognitive skills 和 preferences 的 baseline model 来 explained。在此基础上，economic incentives 进一步 skew 这些 ratios。Sexism 仍有发挥作用的空间。但上述 evidence suggests，即使取某个 country，挥动魔杖，让每个人都 equally 对待他人，情况充其量也不会与现在 radically different。

上述 discussion 并没有谈论应当做什么，或者是否应当做些什么。如果希望某个 field 中 women 的 share 更高，实现这一点的办法有很多。Mandating women 的 quotas 大概是最直接的办法。但我猜，人们通常想要的并不是为了 equality 而追求 equality，而是让人们不论 gender，都觉得可以自由地 choices 要 study 什么。Preferences 并不是 hard-wired 且 immutable 的。我已经表明，它们受到 nature 的 biased，但它们也能在一定程度上被 culture 所 molded。只是在一定程度上。让美国变成瑞典或许是 feasible 的。但如果没有 quasitotalitarian policies，要让各方面的 gender ratios 都达到 50 +/- 10%，则是极不可能的。

对于上述 evidence，存在一些反对的 arguments；人们可以坚持这些 arguments，继续认为 sexism 才是真正的 underlying cause。如果读完这篇文章后，你仍然认为我错了，请在评论中告诉我。我希望相信的是真实的东西。我可能是错的，我记得自己过去曾多次犯错。但也请考虑一下，你也可能是错的。

It is, and it is not sexist.

These were removed by Gizmodo when they published it.

The manifesto, I repeat, is not sexist. If one defines sexism as saying that women ought not do X because they are women, then there is nothing in the article about that. But some people consciously or subconsciously equate finding differences between groups to sexism. Some examples of people making the mistake. The quote below was classified as an example of sexism.

> eg "women relatively prefer jobs in social or artistic areas. More men may like coding because it requires systemizing and even within SWEs"

Is this sexism?

Also, think of the following:

![](https://nintil.com/why-so-few-women-in-cs-the-google-memo-is-right/07-1.jpg:large)

That says "Men prefer high status and lucrative careers more than women. Thus if it remains like that, there will be more men. If one makes some policy changes (part time work), then the proportion of woman will increase.

Well, this was read as "Women (on average) shouldn't be doing tech or be in leadership roles, although we can allow them to do part-time jobs in tech, to keep'em."

Really? This is not a case of a difference in opinion due to different context, see the article itself. It is really clear.

In this post I focus on a single claim: Are there psychologically meaningful differences between men and women that may be driving the gender distribution across fields? ## How there can be less women in science

If men and women's brains are equal, then there wouldn't seem to be any reason to expect differences except because of cultural factors (Among them, discrimination). But several differences have been proposed:

-   Men's intelligence distribution has a higher variance. Thus at the higher end of the distribution (from where you get your STEM graduates) you would expect to find more men. Same thing for low paid jobs.
-   Men's are willing to work more/are more competitive than women. This would lead to an overrepresentation of men in highly competitive fields, or fields that demand a great amount of work.
-   For the same level of overall intelligence, women have a higher verbal IQ. This means that women would have more paths open for them compared to men, and so although they could do as well as men in technical careers, they would have it more easy to choose other non-STEM careers.
-   Finally, preferences between men and women regarding choice of occupation may be different, directly driving the distribution of genders per career.

These traits, it is said, have an evolutionary explanation, and thus if it is right that the explanations above are correct, one would predict the same across countries. There will be more men in technical fields, and there will be more women in the humanities and healthcare sectors.

On the other hand, one can postulate discrimination of various sorts:

-   Women are prohibited from attending university. This would reduce the amount of female graduates.
-   Women are culturally disencouraged from attending university, or certain degrees. Same as above.
-   A toxic workplace/college culture (techbros) keep women away. This again reduces the presence of women in these fields.

These hypothesis can also be used to make predictions: the less sexism there is, the more equal the distributions will be.

One more key piece of evidence must be discussed, this:

![Media preview](https://nintil.com/why-so-few-women-in-cs-the-google-memo-is-right/07-2.jpg:large)

This seems like strong evidence against the evolutionary model, as it it a substantial variation in the percentage of women majoring in CS in the US. This cannot be due to any adaptation, thus the cause of that must be cultural. The chart comes from [this article,](http://www.npr.org/sections/money/2014/10/21/357629765/when-women-stopped-coding) that argues that

> Modern computer science is dominated by men. But it hasn't always been this way.
> 
> A lot of computing pioneers — the people who programmed the first digital computers — were women. And for decades, the number of women studying computer science was growing faster than the number of men. But in 1984, something changed. The percentage of women in computer science flattened, and then plunged, even as the [share of women in other technical and professional fields](http://www.nsf.gov/statistics/nsf13327/content.cfm?pub_id=4266&id=2) kept rising.

Because

> The share of women in computer science started falling at roughly the same moment when personal computers started showing up in U.S. homes in significant numbers.
>

>

She found that families were much more likely to buy computers for boys than for girls — even when their girls were really interested in computers.

[Here](http://www.apa.org/action/resources/research-in-action/share.aspx)'s more evidence against the evolutionary model that was sent to me. The article says:

-   No differences between boys and girls in math performance (Shibley Hyde (1990))
-   No major differences in verbal ability (Hyde (1988))
-   Small or no differences in cognition, communication style, social and personality variables, motor behaviors and moral reasoning. (Hyde, 2005)
-   Gender differences in math and science have a genetic basis and emerge in early childhood. But on the whole, men and women possess equal aptitude for math and science. (Spelke, 2005)
-   A consensus statement was published in 2007 was published by Hyde, Diane Halpern and other about the male/female disparity on science and math careers. They say, that women tend to score slighly higher than men on verbal abilities, and men have a slight edge on visuospatial skills. But early experience, education policies and culture also affect success in math and science, they say.
-   No gender differences in maths in children from grades 2 to 11 (Hyde, 2008)
-   At the highest levels of abilities, men score higher than women in mathematics (the distribution is more heavy tailed for men), but this gap has been decreasing. The gap is found to be smaller in countries with greater gender equality (as the sexism hypothesis predicts). (Hyde, 2008)

This seems, intuitively, like a good case for the sexism model. But note that these studies do not address for example different preferences (for careers, or amount of work), and these studies do acknowledge that women indeed score higher in verbal skills and men in visuospatial skills.

Imagine for example that men and women are cognitively equal, and they only differ in preferences. This would still drive a different distribution if the differences in preferences are big enough.

But [here](https://www.psychologytoday.com/blog/rabble-rouser/201707/why-brilliant-girls-tend-favor-non-stem-careers)'s the case for the evolutionary model:

-   Differences in preferences (N=500,000) are found to be gigantic (Su et al. 2009). Women prefer working with people, men prefer working with things. This effects is also present in monkeys.

-   There are 70% more dual talented (in maths and verbal skills) girls than boys, boys are twice as likely as girls to have strong math skills, but not strong verbal skills. People who only had only strong math skills as students were more likely to be working in STEM. People with both strong math and verbal skills as students were less likely to be working in STEM. Thus

-   _“Results revealed that mathematically capable individuals who also had high verbal skills were less likely to pursue STEM careers than were individuals who had high math skills but moderate verbal skills. One notable finding was that the group with high math and high verbal ability included more females than males…_

    _Our study provides evidence that it is not lack of ability that causes females to pursue non-STEM careers, but rather the greater likelihood that females with high math ability also have high verbal ability and thus can consider a wider range of occupations than their male peers with high math ability, who are more likely to have moderate verbal ability.”_(Wang, Eccles, Kenny, 2013)

-   Boys and girls are equally good at math on average. But the variance of the distribution of male skills is higher. (Lindberg et. al  2010)

Going back to out 1985 peak, some people at reddit adventure some explanations. One is the one at NPR, that computers were seen as a male thing. **But this does not explain why male CS enrollment also fell**. Another explanation is not that women stopped liking computers because they were a male thing, but men instead began liking computers more, as they came to be regarded as a thing to hack and tinker, not a boring office tool. Another possible explanation, [advanced here](http://blessingofkings.blogspot.co.uk/2014/10/women-in-computer-science.html) is that men are more financially driven, and that they were attracted more than women to CS careers. This explanation aims to explain why there were two bubbles in the time series. **The computers-are-for-boys thesis does not explain this**. We knew there was a dotcom bubble in the early 2000s. But was there a bubble around 1985?

Not a financial one, no. But there were capacity contraints ([Roberts, 2016](http://cs.stanford.edu/people/eroberts/CSCapacity.pdf)). As more and more people enrolled into CS due to the boom in personal computing, the colleges found themselves without enough capacity to attend the growing demand. So they restricted the number of positions open for new students. Making the process more competitive (i.e. sampling further to the right in the distribution of skills) will get you relatively more men.

### Conclusion

Women and men are different, both physically, intellectually, and in what they prefer. Is this sexism? This is no moral claim. This is as true as claiming that men are, on average, more violent, taller, and stronger than women. Is this sexism? If it is, reality is sexist and we have to live with it. But on a sensible definition of sexism, it isn't. Sexism would imply discouraging women from joining certain fields, regarding them as morally inferior, banning them from certain occupations. The tech-bro culture may count as this. But this does not seem to be the biggest driver of what we observe across countries, fields of study, and time: that men cluster in certain areas and women in others.

The evolutionary hypothesis holds: we do have strong sex based biases towards some fields. On top of this, we react to incentives (mediated, again, by differences in personality), and our behaviour is also culturally influences. We can have all three explanations at the same time. There isn't much literature in trying to assign weights to these parameters. We can rule out the 100% sexism thesis: [Lippa et al.](http://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0095960)find that 40% of the variance in the gender between occupations is explained mostly by things vs people orientation. Another chunk be due to the coincidence that highly quantitative and things-oriented fields are well rewarded. In the United States (and the rest of the developed world) there is a correlation between [percentage of male](http://www.randalolson.com/2015/08/16/u-s-college-majors-median-yearly-earnings-vs-gender-ratio/) in that industry and how well rewarded it is.

To recap, the following facts need to be explained:

-   There are more men than women in quantitative fields
-   There are more men than women in highly paying jobs
-   There are more women than men in quantitative jobs in less gender equal countries  (See Saudi Arabia vs Sweden. But this effect may weaken at higher levels of income)

I have given evidence to support the claims that these facts can be explained by a baseline model of different cognitive skills and preferences between men and women. On top of that, economic incentives further skew the ratios. There is room for sexism. But the evidence above suggests that at best, if one takes a given country, waves a magic wand and makes everyone treat other equally, the picture wouldn't be radically different from what it looks now.

The discussion above says nothing about what ought to be done, if anything. If one desires a higher share of women in a given field, there are many ways of doing this. Mandating quotas of women is probably the most straightforward way. But I guess that what is generally desired is not equality for the sake of equality, but that people feel free to make choices about what to study, regardless of gender. Preferences are not hard-wired and immutable. I have shown that they are biased by nature, but they can be somewhat molded by culture. Somewhat. Making the US into Sweden may be feasible. What is highly unlikely, absent quasitotalitarian policies, is to bring gender ratios across the board to 50 +/- 10%.

There are arguments against the evidence above that one can hold to to keep thinking that it is actually sexism the underlying cause. If after reading this post you still think I am wrong, please let me know in the comments. I am interested in believing what is true. I may be wrong, I can remember many times I've been wrong in the past. But consider that you might also be.

Female representation in science programs is weakest in the Netherlands and strongest in Iran, Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan, Saudi Arabia, and Oman, where science is disproportionately female. Although the Netherlands has long been considered a gender-traditional society in the European context, most people would still be intrigued to learn that women’s representation among science graduates is nearly 50 percentage points lower there than in many Muslim countries. As seen in the table on page 23, the most gender-integrated science programs are found in Malaysia, where women’s 57-percent share of science degree recipients precisely matches their share of all college and university graduates.
    > 
    > “Science” is a big, heterogeneous category, and life science, physical science, mathematics, and computing are fields with very different gender compositions. For example, women made up 60 percent of American biology graduates , but only about 19 percent of computing graduates, in 2008, according to the National Center for Educational Statistics. But even when fields are defined more precisely, countries differ in some unexpected ways. A case in point is computer science in Malaysia and the U.S. While American computer scientists are depicted as male hackers and geeks, computer science in Malaysia is deemed well-suited for women because it’s seen as theoretical (not physical) and it takes place almost exclusively in offices (thought to be woman-friendly spaces). In interviews with sociologist Vivian Lagesen, female computer science students in Malaysia reported taking up computing because they like computers and because they and their parents think the field has good job prospects. The students also referenced government efforts to promote economic development by training workers, both male and female, for the expanding information technology field. About half of Malaysian computer science degrees go to women.
    > 
    > Engineering is the most strongly and consistently male-typed field of study worldwide, but its gender composition still varies widely across countries. Female representation is generally weaker in advanced industrial societies than in developing ones. In our 2009 article in the American Journal of Sociology, Karen Bradley and I found this pattern using international data from the mid-1990s; it was confirmed by more recent statistics assembled by UNESCO. Between 2005 and 2008, countries with the most male-dominated engineering programs include the world’s leading industrial democracies (Japan, Switzerland, Germany, and the U.S.) along with some of the same oil-rich Middle Eastern countries in which women are so well-represented among science graduates (Saudi Arabia, Jordan, and the United Arab Emirates). Although women do not reach the fifty-percent mark in any country, they come very close in Indonesia, where 48 percent of engineering graduates are female (compared to a 49 percent share of all Indonesian college and university graduates). Women comprise about a third of recent engineering graduates in a diverse group of countries including Mongolia, Greece, Serbia, Panama, Denmark, Bulgaria, and Malaysia.
    > 
    > While engineering is uniformly male-typed in the West, Lagesen’s interviews suggest Malaysians draw gender distinctions among engineering subfields. One female student reported, “...In chemical engineering, most of the time you work in labs... So I think it’s quite suitable for females also. But for civil engineering... we have to go to the site and check out the constructions.”
