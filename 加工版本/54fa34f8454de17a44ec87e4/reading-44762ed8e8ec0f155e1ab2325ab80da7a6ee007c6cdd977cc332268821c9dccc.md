# General Miseducation

---

## 正文

-   第 1 周：我们选了书中的一段 central passage，画了一幅画。
-   第 4 周：我们假装根据这本书来 start a business。
-   第 6 周：我们画了一幅关于重要 concepts 的 pictorial map，并涂上颜色。
-   第 8 周：我们进行了一场 debate。为了确保每个人都发言，我们围成一圈轮流说话，每人每次说一句。

这是初中吗？不是。这是 Ethical Reasoning 18，是 Harvard 去年秋季规模最大（也最有名）的 courses 之一，_Atlantic_ 曾对此[大加赞扬地报道](http://www.theatlantic.com/education/archive/2013/10/why-are-hundreds-of-harvard-students-studying-ancient-chinese-philosophy/280356/)过（_Crimson_ 的报道则[没那么热情](http://www.thecrimson.com/article/2013/10/9/hey-atlantic-5-real-reasons-to-take-er-18/)）。对几位朋友做的一次非正式调查表明，花在 ER 18 上的 average total time 大约是 20 小时，包括 lectures、sections 和 final exam。作为对比，这大约是我花在 elective 和 concentration classes 上的时间的 10%。

先说清楚，我并不责怪我的 professor；他的 lectures 富有洞见、引人入胜，确实帮助我们理解了所读的 texts（尤其是在 final 前一晚，我用 double-speed 听这些 lectures 的时候）——我虽然在开玩笑，但这话是真心的。我也不怨我的 section leader，面对一群睡眠不足、心不在焉、连 reading 都没做的懒鬼，她已经尽力了。

不，责任完全在那些设计者身上，是他们搞出了这场名为 General Education curriculum 的灾难。作为 Harvard 的 student，我必须把 thirty-two credits 中的 eight 花在经过某个 committee 专门 vetted 的 courses 上，认定这些 courses 能够“将 student 的 liberal education——也就是以 free inquiry 的精神开展、本身便有价值的 education——与大学以后的生活 connect\[ing\] 起来”。理想也许很高尚，但结果呢？让所有参与者都白白浪费了大量时间。

原因是这样的：要通过 vetting 成为 Gen Ed course，过程很痛苦，而且 requirements 很严格。因此，这通常只留给一个 department 的 introductory courses（如果有的话）。而且 students 不能要求 exceptions：你就是必须 take 一门 vetted course。这意味着，即使你原本就对 Harvard 某个 Gen Ed categories 中的内容感兴趣，你也往往找不到教授这方面内容的 Gen Ed。例如，我本来会_非常乐意_通过 studying [metaethics](http://isites.harvard.edu/icb/icb.do?keyword=k41508) 或 [moral dilemmas](http://www.registrar.fas.harvard.edu/courses-exams/course-catalog/philosophy-15-moral-dilemmas-new-course) 来完成我的 Ethical Reasoning requirement，这两者_显然_都在 teach ethical reasoning——但荒唐的是，它们都不算。[Billy](http://billyjanitsch.com/) studied evolutionary dynamics 整整两个 semesters——其中一个还是 graduate level——但_显然_，他在“science of living systems”方面什么也没学到，无法算作 credit。我的室友 Rahul 直接跳到了 [advanced microeconomics](http://www.registrar.fas.harvard.edu/courses-exams/course-catalog/economics-1011a-microeconomic-theory)，但按照 committee 的说法，这门课没有教给他任何关于“the united states in the world”的东西，尽管 [basic](http://economics.harvard.edu/courses/economics-10-principles-economics) 的 [versions](http://www.registrar.fas.harvard.edu/courses-exams/course-catalog/economics-10b-principles-economics) 却教了。

不过，最荒唐的还是我同学 Evan 的情况。他曾[在 Intel Science Talent Search 中 won first place](http://www.maa.org/news/odorney-wins-intel-science-talent-search)；曾在 math journals 上被 [published](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00233-013-9486-7)；还曾 [dominated](http://www.imo-official.org/participant_r.aspx?id=18028) 世界上 [hardest](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow#Putnam_Fellows) 的 math competitions。他当然已经 passed out of 那些 introductory math courses，所以按照 Harvard 的说法，他_仍然对 Empirical and Mathematical Reasoning 一无所知。_

如果你的 system 宣称，一位可以说是全国最优秀的年轻 mathematician 还没有能力 doing mathematical reasoning，_那就是你的 system 出了问题。_

那么，像我、Billy、Rahul 和 Evan 这样的人会怎样？我们想要 general education，却必须先把那些规定的形式走完。于是，我们 take 自己并不感兴趣的 courses。我们成了那些心不在焉的人群中的一员：从舞台上看，Sanders Theater 是一片银色的 MacBooks 海洋；从后面看，则是一片蓝色的 Facebooks 海洋。我们草草翻过 readings，在 section 上混日子，也毁了那些真正因为想来而来的人们的体验。

到了 semester 结束时，我们又把怨气撒在 teachers 身上：如果他们布置了太多 work，我们就在 evaluations 里狠狠批评他们。于是，这成了一场 race to the bottom。谁能搞出最精彩的马戏，ratings、enrollment numbers、名声和荣耀就归谁——无论是靠[把 _Harry Potter_ 放进 syllabus](http://scholar.harvard.edu/tatar/classes/folklore-and-mythology-90i-formerly-aesthetic-and-interpretive-understanding-27-fairy-)、[每次 lecture 都带食物来](http://scholar.harvard.edu/holbrooklab/classes/oeb-52-biology-plants)、[向 students 扔糖果、在墙上写字](http://english.fas.harvard.edu/course/english-182-science-fiction/)，还是[把 class 变成一个 cult](https://cs50.harvard.edu/)。

如果 Harvard 不再强行推行这套由 paperwork、categories 和看似有选择其实没选择的东西组成的繁复 system，而是把 students 当作能够负责自身 education 的成年人，会怎样？如果 Harvard 像[许多](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University)[其他](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College)[优秀的](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University)[学校](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College)那样，取消 distribution requirements，会怎样？那些学校培养出的 students，即使不比我们更 well-rounded，也和我们一样 well-rounded。或者，哪怕只是放宽这些 requirements，让它们不那么 restrictive 呢？

我_想要_获得 general education！但 Gen Eds 却适得其反：它们鼓励 superficial 而不是 deep，鼓励 easy 而不是 challenging，鼓励胡扯而不是 learning。我的一些室友申请 four-year Master’s degrees，仅仅因为这样能让他们少 take 几门 Gen Eds。我和朋友们错过了一些精彩的 courses，因为只要它不是 Gen Ed，我们的 schedules 就没有空位。这个 program 没能实现它的理想，它在浪费所有人的时间，也在摧毁 Harvard students 获得我们想要的 education 的能力。

## 原文

-   Week 1: we picked a central passage of the book and drew a picture.
-   Week 4: we pretended to start a business based on the book.
-   Week 6: we drew and colored in a pictorial map of the important concepts.
-   Week 8: we had a debate. To ensure that everyone spoke, we went around in a circle, everyone saying one sentence at a time.

Middle school? Nope. This is Ethical Reasoning 18, one of the biggest (and biggest-name) courses at Harvard this past fall, as [gushingly covered](http://www.theatlantic.com/education/archive/2013/10/why-are-hundreds-of-harvard-students-studying-ancient-chinese-philosophy/280356/) in the _Atlantic_ (and [less gushingly](http://www.thecrimson.com/article/2013/10/9/hey-atlantic-5-real-reasons-to-take-er-18/) in the _Crimson_). An informal poll of some friends suggests that the average total time spent on ER 18 is about 20 hours, including lectures, sections, and the final exam. For perspective, that’s about 10% of what I spend on my elective and concentration classes.

To be clear, I don’t blame my professor, whose insightful and engaging lectures really did help to illuminate the texts we read (especially when I listend to them at double-speed the night before the final)—and I may joke but I mean this sincerely. Nor do I resent my section leader, who was doing the best she could with a bunch of sleep-deprived and checked-out slackers who hadn’t done the reading.

No, the blame lies squarely with the designers of the debacle known as the General Education curriculum. As a Harvard student, I’m required to spend eight out of thirty-two credits on courses especially vetted by a committee as “connect\[ing\] a student’s liberal education—that is, an education conducted in a spirit of free inquiry, rewarding in its own right—to life beyond college.” Noble ideals, maybe, but the result? A massive waste of time for everyone involved.

Here’s why: being vetted as a Gen Ed course is painful, and the requirements are strict. So it’s usually reserved for a department’s introductory courses (if any). And students can’t ask for exceptions: you simply have to take a vetted course. That means that even if you have a pre-existing interest inside one of Harvard’s Gen Ed categories, you often can’t find a Gen Ed that teaches it. For example, I would have _loved_ to spend my Ethical Reasoning requirement studying [metaethics](http://isites.harvard.edu/icb/icb.do?keyword=k41508) or [moral dilemmas](http://www.registrar.fas.harvard.edu/courses-exams/course-catalog/philosophy-15-moral-dilemmas-new-course), both of which _obviously_ teach ethical reasoning—but, absurdly, they don’t count. [Billy](http://billyjanitsch.com/) studied evolutionary dynamics for two semesters—one at the graduate level—but _apparently_ he’s learned nothing about the “science of living systems” to count for credit. My roommate Rahul skipped straight into [advanced microeconomics](http://www.registrar.fas.harvard.edu/courses-exams/course-catalog/economics-1011a-microeconomic-theory), but according to the committee it didn’t teach him anything about “the united states in the world”, even though the [basic](http://economics.harvard.edu/courses/economics-10-principles-economics) [versions](http://www.registrar.fas.harvard.edu/courses-exams/course-catalog/economics-10b-principles-economics) did.

Most ridiculous, though, is the case of my classmate Evan. He’s [won first place in the Intel Science Talent Search](http://www.maa.org/news/odorney-wins-intel-science-talent-search); he’s been [published](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00233-013-9486-7) in math journals; and he’s [dominated](http://www.imo-official.org/participant_r.aspx?id=18028) the [hardest](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putnam_Fellow#Putnam_Fellows) math competitions in the world. Of course he passed out of the introductory math courses, so according to Harvard he _still doesn’t know anything about Empirical and Mathematical Reasoning._

If your system declares arguably the country’s best young mathematician not yet capable of doing mathematical reasoning, _your system is broken._

So what happens to people like me, Billy, Rahul, and Evan? We want a general education, but we have to jump through the right hoops first. So we take courses we’re not excited about. We become part of the checked-out masses that make Sanders Theater a sea of silver MacBooks if you look from the stage, and a sea of blue Facebooks if you look from behind. We skim the readings, we slack off in section, and we ruin things for the folks who are actually there because they want to be.

And at the end of the semester, we take our resentment out on our teachers, panning them in the evaluations if they assigned too much work. So it becomes a race to the bottom. The ratings, the enrollment numbers, the fame and the glory go to whoever can throw the best circus—whether through [putting _Harry Potter_ on the syllabus](http://scholar.harvard.edu/tatar/classes/folklore-and-mythology-90i-formerly-aesthetic-and-interpretive-understanding-27-fairy-), [bringing food to every lecture](http://scholar.harvard.edu/holbrooklab/classes/oeb-52-biology-plants), [throwing candy at students and writing on the walls](http://english.fas.harvard.edu/course/english-182-science-fiction/), or [turning the class into a cult](https://cs50.harvard.edu/).

What if, instead of imposing this byzantine system of paperwork and categories and choices-but-not-really-choices, Harvard treated its students like adults who can be put in charge of their own education? What if, like [many](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_University) [other](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amherst_College) [great](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University) [schools](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_College), whose students come out just as well-rounded as ours (if not more), Harvard dropped its distribution requirements? Or even just broadened them to something less restrictive?

I _want_ to get a general education! But Gen Eds do the opposite: they encourage the superficial over the deep, the easy over the challenging, the bullshit over the learning. Some of my roommates have applied for four-year Master’s degrees solely because it lets them take fewer Gen Eds. Friends and I have missed out on amazing courses because we didn’t have space in our schedules if it wasn’t a Gen Ed. The program is failing to live up to its ideals, it’s wasting everyone’s time, and it’s destroying Harvard students’ ability to get the education we want.
