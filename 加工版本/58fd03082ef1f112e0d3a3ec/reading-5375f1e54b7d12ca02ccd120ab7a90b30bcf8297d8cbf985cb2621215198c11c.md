# Contra Hanson on Party Death

---

## 正文

Robin Hanson 最近在他的博客中发表了一篇[文章](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2016/04/is-forgotten-party-death.html)，用他的这条推文来概括最为贴切：

https://twitter.com/robinhanson/status/723905628004073473

不过，还是请读一读全文。

在这里，我将 argue，即使我们接受 Hanson 关于 personal identity 的 view，也有充分理由 reject 这个 argument。

从他的图中，我并不能_确切_明白他的意思。（那些点表示一段 amnesia 吗？还是一段 'mind jump'？）因此，我重新画了这些图，以便更好地呈现这些 cases：

[![2016-04-25 18-46-55 的屏幕截图](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-1.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/25/contra-hanson-on-party-death/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-04-25-18-46-55/#main)

所以，Case A 就是你去参加一场聚会，然后 forgetting 这件事。（因为你吃了一片药，或者因为你喝醉了，这并没有那么重要）。

在 case C 中，我们有一个 emulated person（"em"），它在所有实际意义上都与 regular person 相同，因为我们 assume，我们正在 submolecular level 上 simulating brains。于是，我们有了这个 em，然后在某个时刻，它 makes a copy of 自己（用 Hanson 的 terms 来说，就是一个 "spur"），这个 spur 做一些工作，然后 ends。我用 arrows 顶端的一个 rhombus 来 represent 这种 ending。根据你的 interpretation，这会意味着不同的事情。在我和大多数人的 interpretation 中，这个 spur dies。在 Hanson 的 interpretation 中，它只是 ends。我想，这个 spur 或许可以被看作 original em 的一部分。

现在，有趣的部分来了。目前，说在 Case A 中没有人 died，而在 Case C 中有人 died，是完全 coherent 的。不过，说这两个 cases 中都没有人 died，或者说这两个 cases 中都有人 died，也同样 coherent。

Hanson 现在 proposes 另一个 case，将这两个 cases links 起来。如果成功，这就意味着，无论你对 Case A 作出什么回答，对 Case C 都应该作出同样的回答。要么你在 case A 中 suffer from a party death，而 spur 也 dies；要么你在 case A 中并不 suffer from a party death，但也不能认为 spur 会 die。

Hanson 的 case B 看起来像 cases A 和 C 的 hybrid。但在我重新画的图中，它是什么样的呢？

嗯，他说 case B 与 A 类似，但其中的 person moves 到一个遥远的 location（case B1）。因此，

[![2016-04-25 18-45-52 的屏幕截图](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-2.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/25/contra-hanson-on-party-death/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-04-25-18-45-52/#main)

到目前为止，一切都说得通。这确实很像 case A。

现在，对于 case B2，他说它可以被呈现为类似 C 的形式，但在 spur works 时，original em 被 archived（它 goes to sleep）。

[![2016-04-25 18-50-40 的屏幕截图](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-3.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/25/contra-hanson-on-party-death/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-04-25-18-50-40/#main)

由于 Case B2 看起来与 Case B1 完全不同，它们并不 similar。因此，Cases A 和 C 是 dissimilar 的。因此，你可以说，你在 A 中不会 die，而 spur 在 C 中会 dies。

我的猜测是，既然 Hanson 相信你是一个 pattern of information（不只是某个特定 brain 中的某个特定 pattern，而仅仅是一个 pattern），那么 making copies of 你就是在 making you larger，而 destroying copies of 你就是在 making you smaller。Caplan 对此的回应见[这里](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2009/11/whats_really_wr.html)。

现在，如果我们接受这一点，就可以很容易地在脑海中对 people 进行 linear decompositions（case D）。也许 Hanson 是 subconsciously 这样做的。这样一来，在他看来，这些 cases 比没有这个 assumption 时显得更 similar。我明白这种想法从何而来，但我不认同。当然，你可以将自己 conceive 为许多个 selves，每秒一个，在 time 中向后和向前 extending。于是，你就会不断地 dying 和 appearing。或者说，每一个 self 都会如此。又或者，你可以认为，恰恰因为这种 change，并不存在 self（正如 Buddhists 所认为的那样）。但是，你的 experiences 发生于其中的 physical substrate，也就是你的 brain，在 time 中具有 relative constancy，因此，把自己看作一个与自己所制作的 copies separate 的事物，是 plausible 的。也就是说，你并不与它们 share a consciousness。

[![2016-04-25 19-44-07 的屏幕截图](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-4.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/25/contra-hanson-on-party-death/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-04-25-19-44-07/#main)

Case D 是对一个 person 的 linear decomposition，将其分为在 spur 做事时 goes to sleep 的那个 person，以及 spur 本身。基本上，这就是 case B2。如果你接受这种 equality，这个 case 就与 Case B1 一样 unproblematic（甚至更不 unproblematic，因为没有 memory loss）。

现在，至于他在那段话结尾作出的 prediction，在我看来似乎是 true 的。我认为，_现在_大多数人不会 make copies of 自己，让它们工作，然后再 end 它们。而且，我不认为这些 copies 仅仅因为自己恰好是 original person 的一个 copy，就会去做任何被分配的 task。但确实有人会这样做。在 extreme case 中，如果只有 Robin Hanson 持有这些 views，那么 em world 就会被 Robin Hansons 所 tiled。

最后，我想请 Hanson 推荐一些我应该阅读的书籍或文章，以改变我的想法。上次有人向 Hanson 索要 references 时（那个人是 Bryan Caplan，见[这里](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2009/11/a_philosophical.html)），他说，Caplan 需要有 hard science background 才能理解这些内容（相当于整个 undergrand 和 grad education 的材料量），而且还需要年轻一些，才更容易受到影响。嗯，我拥有 Mehanical Engineering 的 BSc；我十几岁时就读过 Penrose 的 The Emperor's New Mind（它基本上是对 Physics 的 summary，Roger Penrosexplains 这些内容是为了引出他的 thesis）；而且，我很快就会拥有两个 MScs（分别是 Industrial 和 Aerospace engineering）。我也还年轻。我想到至少应该读一读 Feynman's Lectures。也许 Hanson 想在这份书单中特别补充些什么。

附赠一段！如何在不 dying 的情况下将你的 mind upload 到 computer：逐步进行，将你的 brain connecting 到 computer（而不是 slice-scan-copy）。这样，你就会像 Hanson 所说的那样变得 'bigger'，然后，当你的大部分已经在 computer 中时，就可以 discard 你的 physical body。

## 原文

Robin Hanson has a [recent post in his blog](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2016/04/is-forgotten-party-death.html) that is best summarised by his tweet:

https://twitter.com/robinhanson/status/723905628004073473

Do read the full post, though.

Here, I argue that there are good reasons to reject the argument, even if we grant Hanson his view on personal identity.

From his drawing, I don't get _exactly_ what he means. (Are the dots a period of amnesia? Of 'mind jump'?) So I redrew them to present the cases better:

[![Captura de pantalla de 2016-04-25 18-46-55](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-1.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/25/contra-hanson-on-party-death/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-04-25-18-46-55/#main)

So the Case A is you going to a party, and then forgetting about it.(because you took a pill, or because you got drunk, it doesn't matter that much).

In case C we have en emulated person ("em") that for all effects is the same as a regular person, because we assume we are simulating brains at submolecular level. So we have the em, and then at some point, it makes a copy of itself (a "spur", in Hanson's terms), and the spur does some work, and then ends. I represent this ending by a rhombus at the tip of the arrows. Depending on your interpretation, this means different things. On mine, and most people's, the spur dies. On Hanson, it merely ends. The spur could be thought of as part of the original em, I guess.

Now, here's the interesting part. Right now it's perfectly coherent to say that in Case A, no one has died, while in Case C someone has. It's equally coherent to say that no one has died in both cases, or that in both cases someone has died, though.

Hanson now proposes a further case that links both cases. If succesful, it means that whatever you answer for Case A, you should answer for Case C. Either you suffer from a party death in case A and the spur also dies, or you don't suffer from a party death in case A, but the spur cannot be thought to die.

Hanson's case B looks like a hybrid of cases A and C. But how does it look on my redrawing?

Well, he says that case B is like A, but that the person moves to a distant location (case B1). So,

[![Captura de pantalla de 2016-04-25 18-45-52](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-2.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/25/contra-hanson-on-party-death/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-04-25-18-45-52/#main)

So far, so good. This really looks like case A.

Now, for case B2, he says that it can be put like C, but the original em is archived (it goes to sleep) while the spur works.

[![Captura de pantalla de 2016-04-25 18-50-40](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-3.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/25/contra-hanson-on-party-death/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-04-25-18-50-40/#main)

Since Case B2 doesn't look at all like Case B1, they are not similar. Hence, Cases A and C are dissimilar. Hence, you can say that you don't die in A, and that the spur dies in C.

My guess is that given that Hanson believes that you are a pattern of information (Not just a particular one in a particular brain, but just a pattern), then making copies of you is making you larger, and destroying copies of you is making you smaller. See [here](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2009/11/whats_really_wr.html) for Caplan's reaction to this.

Now, if we accept this, we can easily perform linear decompositions of people in our minds (case D). Perhaps Hanson did it subconsciously. By doing it, the cases looked to him more similar than they look without that assumption. I see where this comes from, but I don't buy it. Surely you can conceive of yourself as lots of selves, one per second, extending backwards and forwards in time. So you would be dying and appearing constantly. Or each one would be. Or you can think that there is no self, precisely because of this change (as Buddhists do). But the relative constancy in time of the physical substrate in which your experiences happen, your brain, makes it plausible to think of yourself as being a thing separate from the copies you make of yourself. You don't share a consciousness with them, that is.

[![Captura de pantalla de 2016-04-25 19-44-07](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/04/25-4.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/04/25/contra-hanson-on-party-death/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-04-25-19-44-07/#main)

Case D is a linear decomposition of a person in one that goes to sleep while a spur does stuff, and the spur itself. Basically, this is case B2. If you grant the equality, the case is as unproblematic (or even less unproblematic, as there is no memory loss) as Case B1.

Now, regarding the prediction he makes at the end of his paragraph, that seems to me true. I think _now_ most people wouldn't make copies of themselves to make them work, and then end them. And I don't think that the copies would work on whatever task they are assigned just because they happen to be a copy of the original person. But there are people who would do that. In the extreme case, if only Robin Hanson espoused these views, the em world would be tiled with Robin Hansons.

To conclude, I would like to ask Hanson for books or articles that I should read to change my mind.  Last time Hanson was asked for references (by Bryan Caplan, [here](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2009/11/a_philosophical.html)), he said that Caplan would need a hard science background to understand it (An entire undergrand and grad education worth of material), and that he would need to be young to be more impressionable. Well, I hold a BSc in Mehanical Engineering, I read Penrose's The Emperor's New Mind (which is basically a summary of Physics that Roger Penrosexplains to get to his thesis) when I was a teen, and soon I will have two MScs, (In Industrial and Aerospace engineering). And I am young. I suggested to myself at least Feynman's Lectures. Perhaps Hanson wants to add something in particular to the list.

Bonus paragraph! How to upload your mind to a computer without dying: Do it gradually, connecting your brain to a computer (not slice-scan-copy). That way you would get 'bigger' a la Hanson, and then when you are mostly in the computer, you can discard your physical body.
