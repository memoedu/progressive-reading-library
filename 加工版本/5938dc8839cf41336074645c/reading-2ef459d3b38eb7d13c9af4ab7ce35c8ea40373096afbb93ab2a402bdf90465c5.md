# Mazzucato y el iPhone (I)

---

## 正文

\[本文是[另一篇文章](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/06/24/mazzucato-y-el-iphone-ii-el-mito-del-estado-emprendedor/)的引言，我将在那篇文章中批评这里呈现的论点\]

Mazzucato 书中最重要的例子大概就是 iPhone：书中甚至用整整一章来专门讨论这个例子。

对 Apple 这项杰作的执着是有道理的：它取用了一个通常被视为企业创造力、车库创业者的努力以及资本主义胜利的例子，再将其颠覆，让人看到，实际上，国家这只看得见的手一直在背后发挥作用，却不公正地未能从中获得收益。

在这篇铺垫性的文章中，我将汇集网上讨论 Mazzucato 这个例子的文章，以捕捉公众对此的反应。我也会收录这位经济学家本人的论述，以明确她究竟用了哪些措辞，又如何将 iPhone 的例子纳入自己的解释框架。

在下一篇文章中，我将批评书中提出的论点。

我们先从 Bruce Upbin 开始，他在 2013 年[发表于 Forbes 的文章中写道](http://www.forbes.com/sites/bruceupbin/2013/06/13/debunking-the-narrative-of-silicon-valleys-innovation-might/)

> 她用于 myth-debunking 的 case study 是 iPhone，也就是那个 American corporate innovation 的象征。它的每一种 core technologies——capacitive sensors、solid-state memory、click wheel、GPS、internet、cellular communications、Siri、microchips、touchscreen——都来自 U.S. government 和 military 的 research efforts 与 funding support。public 得到了 iPhone dividend 吗？并没有真正得到。Mazzucato 说，那些“求知若饥，虚心若愚”的天才拿走了 gains，而如今这家 company 正因没有 paying enough taxes，或没有在 U.S. creating enough high-wage jobs 而受到批评。Apple 的 five-year R&D spending，按 percentage of sales 计算，一直徘徊在 2% 到 3% 左右，而 Nokia 和 Samsung Electronics 等 companies 的 spend 则分别为 9% 和 8%。Steve Jobs 真正的天才之处不在于 developing new technology，而在于 integrating 在别处 invented、且往往 backed by tax dollars 的 technologies。

接下来是 Mazzucato 本人在 [INET](http://ineteconomics.org/new-economic-thinking/mariana-mazzucato-government-risk-and-private-sector-reward) 上发表的一篇文章

> 请看 Apple 的 iPhone 和 Google 的 search engine。在这两个例子中，这些极受欢迎的 consumer products 都从 state intervention 中 benefitted mightily。对于 iPhone，使它及 similar devices 变得“smart”的许多 revolutionary technologies 都是由 U.S. government funded 的，例如 global positioning system（或 GPS）、touchscreen display，以及 voice-activated personal assistant Siri。而对于 Google，其 algorithm 的 creation 是由 National Science Foundation funded 的。当然，还有 Internet 的 development，这也是一项 government funded venture，它使 iPhone 能成为有价值的工具，也让 Google searches 成为可能。

Jeff Madrick 在[The NY Review of Books，2014 年](http://www.nybooks.com/articles/archives/2014/apr/24/innovation-government-was-crucial-after-all/)的文章中写道

> 例如，她详细说明，尽管 Steve Jobs brilliantly imagined 并 designed 了有吸引力的新 commercial products，但 iPod、iPhone 和 iPad 所 based 的 scientific research 几乎全部由 Europe 和 America 的 government-backed scientists 与 engineers 完成。具体而言，如今在 Apple products 中如此常见的 touch-screen technology，是 based on 1960s 和 1970s 在 Europe 与 US 的 government-funded labs 中完成的 research。这些后来的 breakthroughs 几乎 completely dependent on government-sponsored research。Mazzucato 写道：“虽然这些 products 的 beautiful design 和 slick integration 要归功于 Jobs 及其庞大团队的天才，$$...$$”
> 
> > iPod、iPhone 和 iPad 中几乎每一种 state-of-the-art technology，都是 government 与 military 的 research efforts 和 funding support 所取得、却常被忽视和无视的 achievement。
> 
> 一项被称为 giant magnetoresistance 的重大 government-funded discovery，为其两位 European inventors 赢得了 Nobel Prize in physics，就是这种 support 的一个有说服力的例子。这个 process 会 enlarges computers 及较新的 electronic devices 的 storage capacity。在 Nobel 颁奖典礼的一次讲话中，Royal Swedish Academy of Science 的一位成员解释说，正是这一 breakthrough 让 iPod 成为可能。Mazzucato 写道，Apple 的其他重大 developments 也“根植”于 federal research，其中包括 iPhone 的 global positioning system，以及它的 voice-activated personal assistant Siri。

Mazzucato 又在 2014 年[发表于 FT Alphaville 的文章中写道](http://ftalphaville.ft.com/2014/07/18/1901312/mission-finance-starting-to-think-big-again/)

> iPhone 就是一个很好的例子。这张 chart 展示了，使它如此 smart 的每一种 technology，其 funding 如何都能追溯到 US government 中的一家 mission-oriented public agency；这个 government 喜欢装作自己相信 free market，实际上却一直是历史上最 interventionist 的 government 之一。

这里有一张图，对于一直关注这本书的发展轨迹及其影响的人来说，它相当有名：iPhone 关键技术的来源：

[![幻灯片1](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2015/03/19-1.png)](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2015/03/19-1.png)

Mazzucato 又在 2013 年为 Slate 的一篇文章取了这样的标题： [“Entrepreneurs Drive New Technology 是一个 Myth。对于真正的 innovation，请感谢 state。”](http://www.slate.com/articles/health_and_science/new_scientist/2013/09/entrepreneurs_or_the_state_innovation_comes_from_public_investment.html)

> Apple 就是一个绝佳的例子。在早期，这家 company 通过一笔 \$500,000 的 small-business investment company grant，received 了 government cash support。而使 iPhone 成为 smartphone 的每一种 technology，其 vision 和 funding 都归功于 state：Internet、GPS、touch-screen displays，甚至 voice-activated smartphone assistant Siri，都 received 了 state cash。[U.S. Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency](http://www.newscientist.com/article/mg20427335.700-darpa-inventing-this-side-of-the-impossible.html) 为 Internet 提供了 bankrolled，而 CIA 和 military 则 funded 了 GPS。因此，尽管 United States 被向我们宣传为通过 private enterprise 实现 progress 的典范，那里的 innovation 却 benefited from 一个非常 interventionist 的 state。

今年，这位经济学家[在 twitter 上表示](https://twitter.com/mazzucatom/status/558674794447925250)，她的例子表明，“所有使 iPhone 变得智能的技术，都是由公共资金资助的”。

在这段 2014 年[演讲](http://speri.dept.shef.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Extract_Mariana_Mazzucato_NSSPERI_Lecture.pdf)的摘录中，她的表述稍微克制了一些，

> 是什么使 iPhone 如此 smart？仅仅是 Steve Jobs 及其团队的天才，以及 risk-loving venture capitalists 提供的 visionary finance 吗？不是。在我的书 The Entrepreneurial State: Debunking Public v Private Sector Myths 中，我通过 analysing 使 smartphone 得以 created 的 public funds，讲述了这个故事中缺失的部分。使 internet、touch-screen displays、GPS 和 Siri voice control 成为可能的 research programmes，都有 government backing。重点不是贬低 Jobs 及其团队的工作，那些工作既是 essential 的，也是 transformational 的。但我们在 Apple 及其 founders 的 historiography 上必须更加平衡，因为其中对 Silicon Valley 背后的 collective effort 只字未提。问题是：如今，谁会从对 hi-tech sector 的 wealth-creation process 如此狭隘的描述中 benefits？

在 2013 年的 [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2013/03/taxpayers-helped-apple-but-app) 上，她强调了企业所做的事、所采用的修辞，以及它们为何应当缴纳更多税款来资助 Mazzucato 所说的那些国家创新之间的关系，

> 使 iPhone 及其他 products 和 services 变得“smart”的许多 revolutionary technologies，都是由 U.S. government funded 的。例如 Internet、GPS、touchscreen display，以及最新的 voice-activated personal assistant Siri。而且，Apple 并不只是从 government-funded research activities 中 benefit。它还通过 U.S. government 的 [Small Business Investment Company](http://www.sba.gov/INV) program，received 了自己的 early stage finance。只有在 government funding 已经使这家 company 达到关键的 proof of concept 之后，Venture capitalists 才 entered。$$...$$ 这也与 Apple、Google 及其他 technology companies 为 avoid paying taxes 而采取的措施形成了鲜明对比。Apple 在 Nevada 的 Reno 设立了一家 subsidiary；这个 state 没有 corporate income 或 capital gains tax，Apple 将一部分 U.S. sales channeled 到那里，[据报道 saving 了 \$2.5 billion 的 taxes](http://www.nytimes.com/2012/04/29/business/apples-tax-strategy-aims-at-low-tax-states-and-nations.html)。$$...$$ 然而，离开 U.S. government，这种 capitalism 就无法想象；这个 government 通过 DARPA 及其他 initiatives，在 funding 真正的 innovation 方面有着惊人出色的 track record，因而在全球脱颖而出。这包括 government 最近引以为傲的事迹：对（有争议的）shale gas 和 fracking technologies 持续不懈的 financial support，这种 support [始于三十多年前](https://news.google.com/newspapers?nid=1144&dat=19771211&id=HOYdAAAAIBAJ&sjid=7FcEAAAAIBAJ&pg=6894,6038621)，也就是在其他方面备受指责的 Carter Administration 时期。$$...$$ 对 American taxpayer 来说，一个更大的问题是，这样的 support 是否 leads to 一个“parasitic”的 innovation eco-system。请看 Apple。尽管它直接从 taxpayer-funded technologies 中 benefitting，却 strategically “underfunded” 了自己过去曾直接 depended 的 tax purse。

在 [El País，2014 年](http://elpais.com/elpais/2014/12/08/ciencia/1418039790_527615.html)一篇题为“革命性创新始终来自公共部门”的访谈中

> 我的立场并不是说：一切都必须来自国家。显然，Steve Jobs 和 Apple 非常重要。但这一点我们早就知道了。我们生活中的每一天，都有人向我们重复这一点。我想做的是讲述另一个视角，让这段历史更平衡。并不是说私营部门不重要。但它成功地乘上了由国家资助的技术巨浪，将现有技术与出色的设计意识，以及一种全新的、_酷_的产品销售方式结合起来。这些都是众所周知的。Steve Jobs 传记的 80% 都在讲这些。我并不奢求一整章，只要至少有一页讲讲公共部门的贡献，我就满足了。$$...$$ 无法保证每一笔支出都会成功。创新始终是一个不确定的过程。事实上，创新者大多数时候都会失败。然而，在这个过程中，人们会学习。政府内部应当建立能够学习的机构，并采取灵活、适合探索的企业政策。还要有正确的指标，以评估每一笔投资。要做到这一点，就需要聪明的人。我不是说公共部门里没有这样的人。但如果不断批评公共部门，吸引最优秀的人才就会更加困难。除了一些特殊情况——在新加坡，公务员的年薪最高可达一百万美元！——很难靠金钱吸引最优秀的人，必须让这件事成为一种荣誉。而只有当你有能力在自己的领域开辟前景时，才做得到。因此，在美国，像 Steven Chu 这样的诺贝尔奖得主愿意执掌能源部，是非常重要的。$$...$$ 推动生产率增长的是研发支出

Martin Wolf（世界上最负盛名的经济分析师之一）[于 2013 年在 FT 上写道](https://www.ft.com/intl/cms/s/2/32ba9b92-efd4-11e2-a237-00144feabdc0.html#axzz3UpUxC15q)

> 一个或许更有力的例子是 information and communications revolution。US National Science Foundation funded 了驱动 [Google](https://www.ft.com/topics/organisations/Google_Inc "Google news headlines - FT.com") 的 search engine 的 algorithm。[Apple](http://markets.ft.com/tearsheets/performance.asp?s=us:AAPL) 的早期 funding 来自 US government 的 Small Business Investment Company。此外，“所有使 iPhone 变得 ‘smart’ 的 technologies 也都是 state-funded 的……包括 internet、wireless networks、global positioning system、microelectronics、touchscreen displays，以及最新的 voice-activated SIRI personal assistant。”Apple 出色地把这些组合在一起。但它是在收获七十年来 state-supported innovation 的果实。$$...$$ 为什么 state 的 role 如此重要？答案在于 fundamental, science-based innovation 所涉及的巨大 uncertainties、time spans 和 costs。Private companies 不能、也不愿 bear 这些 costs，部分原因是它们无法确定自己能否收获果实，部分原因是这些果实要到遥远的未来才会出现。$$..$$ AT&T 的 path-breaking Bell Labs 的时代早已过去。无论如何，private sector 都不可能 created 出 internet 或 GPS。只有 US military 才拥有这样做所需的 resources。$$...$$ Mazzucato 喜欢戳破关于 risk-loving venture capital 和 risk-avoiding bureaucrats 的 myths。state 的 role 被从这段历史中抹去，这重要吗？她认为重要。首先，policy makers 越来越相信 state 只是 obstacle 的 myth，从而使 innovation 失去 support，也使人类失去实现 prosperity 的最佳前景。事实上，对 government 的蔑视也使它失去 take entrepreneurial risks 的意愿和 capacity。其次，government 也越来越接受这样的安排：由它 funds 这些 risks，而 private sector 则 reaps the rewards。因此，正在形成的并不是真正 symbiotic 的 ecosystem of innovation，而是 parasitic 的一种，其中最 lossmaking 的 elements 被 socialised，而 profitmaking 的那些则大多被 privatised。普通 taxpayers 是否明白，他们的 taxes fund 了 drive 其 economy 的 fundamental innovations？这本书有一个 controversial thesis。但它基本上是正确的。未能 recognise government 在 driving innovation 中的 role，很可能是 rising prosperity 面临的最大 threat。

Mark Buchanan 在 2013 年[甚至说，iPhone 的所有重要 technologies 都来自 state investments](http://thebreakthrough.org/index.php/programs/economic-growth/where-the-iphone-came-from)

> 即使是通常被视为 [Steve Jobs](http://topics.bloomberg.com/steve-jobs/) 这位孤独天才的独特创造的 Apple，也在很大程度上受惠于 government。Mazzucato 指出，Apple 在早年从 US government 的 small-business investment program 获得了关键的 finance。Apple 的 smart products，包括 iPhone 和 iPad，其中每一项最重要的 technologies 都是在别处 developed 的，而且很大程度上要归功于 state funding。

Mazzucato 在 2014 年的一份[提交给 OCDE 的报告](http://marianamazzucato.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/MAZZUCATO-Brochure.pdf)中说

> 让我简要举例，谈谈 iPhone 背后的 technology，直到不久前，它还代表着 Silicon Valley 的象征。Steve Jobs 是一位天才，他所做的就是以一种非常酷的方式把已有的 technology 组合起来，并懂得 simplicity 和 good design 有多么重要。iPhone 上所有 innovative 和 smart features，包括 internet、GPS、touch screen display、Siri feature，都是由 government funded 的。Apple 本身在早期也获得了一个 500,000 dollar 的 Small Business Investment Company (SBIC) programme。这个 iPhone table（上图）表明，进入这部手机的 technology originated from 众多 government agencies，例如 CIA、DARPA 和 Department of Defense。

Andrew Warner，2014 年，发表于 [The Long Now Foundation](http://blog.longnow.org/02014/03/10/mariana-mazzucato-seminar-primer/)

> Mariana Mazzucato 的 research shows，我们的 technological revolutions 所依托的许多 technologies，都是 state 开展数十年 research 的 direct result。看看 computers、internet 和 GPS 这些例子——所有这些 technologies 在 entering consumer market 之前，都已由 government developed 并 funded 了数十年，而没有这些 technologies，就不可能想象出一部 iphone。

Greg Satell，2015 年，发表于 Harvard Business Review

> 而 Bush 的 model 显然 works。事实上，全世界都羡慕它。iPhone 是由一家 US company invented 的，这并非偶然，[它几乎所有的 basic technology 都源于某个 federal program](http://www.digitaltonto.com/2013/where-did-the-iphone-really-come-from/)。

## 原文

\[Este artículo es la introducción de [este otro](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/06/24/mazzucato-y-el-iphone-ii-el-mito-del-estado-emprendedor/), donde critico el argumentario presentado\]

El ejemplo más importante de libro de Mazzucato seguramente sea el del iPhone: se le llega a dedicar un capítulo entero sólo a ese ejemplo.

La fijación con el ingenio de Apple está justificada: Es tomar uno de los ejemplos habituales de presunta creatividad empresarial, esfuerzo de emprendedores de garaje y triunfo del capitalismo y subvertirlo, haciendo ver que realmente, la mano visible del Estado ha estado detrás del mismo, sin haber, injustamente, recogido beneficios de ello.

En este artículo preliminar recopilaré artículos de internet donde se habla del ejemplo de Mazzucato, por capturar la reacción popular de la gente ante el mismo. Incluiré también presentaciones de la propia economista, por precisar qué palabras y de qué manera enmarca el ejemplo del iPhone en su esquema explicativo.

En el próximo artículo, criticaré los argumentos expuestos en el libro.

Empezamos con Bruce Upbin, quien en 2013 [escribe en Forbes](http://www.forbes.com/sites/bruceupbin/2013/06/13/debunking-the-narrative-of-silicon-valleys-innovation-might/)

> Her case study for myth-debunking is the iPhone, that icon of American corporate innovation. Each of its core technologies–capacitive sensors, solid-state memory, the click wheel, GPS, internet, cellular communications, Siri, microchips, touchscreen—came from research efforts and funding support of the U.S. government and military. Did the public see an iPhone dividend? Not really. The “stay foolish, stay hungry” geniuses ran away with the gains, says Mazzucato, and now the company is under fire for not paying enough taxes or creating enough high-wage jobs in the U.S. Apple’s five-year R&D spending as a percentage of sales has hovered around 2% to 3%, while companies such as Nokia and Samsung Electronics spend 9% and 8%, respectively. Steve Jobs’ real genius was not in developing new technology but integrating technologies invented somewhere else, often backed by tax dollars.

Seguimos con un artículo de la propia Mazzucato en [INET](http://ineteconomics.org/new-economic-thinking/mariana-mazzucato-government-risk-and-private-sector-reward)

> Consider Apple’s iPhone and Google’s search engine. In both cases these extremely popular consumer products benefitted mightily from state intervention. For the iPhone, many of the revolutionary technologies that make it and similar devices “smart” were funded by the U.S. government, such as the global positioning system (or GPS), the touchscreen display, and the voice-activated personal assistant, Siri. And for Google, the creation of its algorithm was funded by the National Science Foundation. Plus, of course, there’s the development of the Internet, another government funded venture, which enables the iPhone to be a valuable tool and makes Google searches possible.

Jeff Madrick en[The NY Review of Books, 2014](http://www.nybooks.com/articles/archives/2014/apr/24/innovation-government-was-crucial-after-all/)

> For example, she shows in detail that, while Steve Jobs brilliantly imagined and designed attractive new commercial products, almost all the scientific research on which the iPod, iPhone, and iPad were based was done by government-backed scientists and engineers in Europe and America. The touch-screen technology, specifically, now so common to Apple products, was based on research done at government-funded labs in Europe and the US in the 1960s and 1970s. \[...\] These later breakthroughs were almost completely dependent on government-sponsored research. “While the products owe their beautiful design and slick integration to the genius of Jobs and his large team,” writes Mazzucato,
> 
> > nearly every state-of-the-art technology found in the iPod, iPhone and iPad is an often overlooked and ignored achievement of the research efforts and funding support of the government and military.
> 
> A major government-funded discovery known as giant magnetoresistance, which won its two European inventors a Nobel Prize in physics, is a telling example of such support. The process enlarges the storage capacity of computers and more recent electronic devices. In a speech at the Nobel ceremony, a member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Science explained that this breakthrough made the iPod possible. Other major developments by Apple also had their “roots” in federal research, among them, Mazzucato writes, the global positioning system of the iPhone and Siri, its voice-activated personal assistant.

Mazzucato de nuevo, en 2014, [escribe en FT Alphaville](http://ftalphaville.ft.com/2014/07/18/1901312/mission-finance-starting-to-think-big-again/)

> The iPhone is a good example. This chart shows how every technology that makes it so smart, traces its funding back to a mission-oriented public agency in the US government which likes to pretend it believes in the free market when actually it has been one of the most interventionist in history.

Aquí nos encontramos con un gráfico relativamente famoso para los que llevan siguiendo la trayectoria y repercusión del libro: el orígen de las tecnologías clave del iPhone:

Mazzucato de nuevo en Slate, 2013 titula un artículo con["It's a Myth that Entrepreneurs Drive New Technology. For real innovation, thank the state."](http://www.slate.com/articles/health_and_science/new_scientist/2013/09/entrepreneurs_or_the_state_innovation_comes_from_public_investment.html)

> Apple is a perfect example. In its early stages, the company received government cash support via a $500,000 small-business investment company grant. And every technology that makes the iPhone a smartphone owes its vision and funding to the state: the Internet, GPS, touch-screen displays, and even the voice-activated smartphone assistant Siri all received state cash. [The U.S. Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency](http://www.newscientist.com/article/mg20427335.700-darpa-inventing-this-side-of-the-impossible.html) bankrolled the Internet, and the CIA and the military funded GPS. So, although the United States is sold to us as the model example of progress through private enterprise, innovation there has benefited from a very interventionist state.

Este año decía la economista [en twitter](https://twitter.com/mazzucatom/status/558674794447925250) que su ejemplo muestra que "toda la tecnología que hace inteligente al iPhone fue financiada públicamente"

En este extracto de [una conferencia](http://speri.dept.shef.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/12/Extract_Mariana_Mazzucato_NSSPERI_Lecture.pdf), en 2014, se muestra algo más comedida en sus afirmaciones,

> What makes the iPhone so smart? Was it only the genius of Steve Jobs and his team and the visionary finance supplied from risk-loving venture capitalists? No. In my book The Entrepreneurial State: Debunking Public v Private Sector Myths, I tell the missing part of that story by analysing the public funds that allowed the smartphone to be created. The research programmes that made the internet, touch-screen displays, GPS and the Siri voice control possible all had government backing. The point is not to belittle the work of Jobs and his team, which was both essential and transformational. But we must be more balanced in the historiography of Apple and its founders, where not a word is mentioned of the collective effort behind Silicon Valley. The question is this: who benefits from such a narrow description of the wealth-creation process in the hi-tech sector today?

En [Harvard Business Review](https://hbr.org/2013/03/taxpayers-helped-apple-but-app), 2013, enfatiza la relación entre lo que hacen las empresas, la retórica que emplean y de por qué deberían pagar más impuestos para financiar esas innovaciones estatales de las que habla Mazzucato,

> Many of the revolutionary technologies that make the iPhone and other products and services “smart” were funded by the U.S. government. Martin Wolf (uno de los analistas económicos más prestigiosos del mundo) escribe [en 2013 en FT](https://www.ft.com/intl/cms/s/2/32ba9b92-efd4-11e2-a237-00144feabdc0.html#axzz3UpUxC15q)

> A perhaps even more potent example is the information and communications revolution. The US National Science Foundation funded the algorithm that drove [Google](https://www.ft.com/topics/organisations/Google_Inc "Google news headlines - FT.com")’s search engine. Early funding for [Apple](http://markets.ft.com/tearsheets/performance.asp?s=us:AAPL) came from the US government’s Small Business Investment Company. Moreover, “All the technologies which make the iPhone ‘smart’ are also state-funded ... the internet, wireless networks, the global positioning system, microelectronics, touchscreen displays and the latest voice-activated SIRI personal assistant.” Apple put this together, brilliantly. But it was gathering the fruit of seven decades of state-supported innovation. \[...\] Why is the state’s role so important? The answer lies in the huge uncertainties, time spans and costs associated with fundamental, science-based innovation. Private companies cannot and will not bear these costs, partly because they cannot be sure to reap the fruits and partly because these fruits lie so far in the future. \[..\] The days of AT&T’s path-breaking Bell Labs are long gone. In any case, the private sector could not have created the internet or GPS. Only the US military had the resources to do so. \[...\] Mazzucato loves puncturing myths about risk-loving venture capital and risk-avoiding bureaucrats. Does it matter that the role of the state has been written out of the story? She argues that it does. First, policy makers increasingly believe the myth that the state is only an obstacle, thereby depriving innovation of support and humanity of its best prospects for prosperity. Indeed, the scorn heaped on government also deprives it of the will and capacity to take entrepreneurial risks. Second, government has also increasingly accepted that it funds the risks, while the private sector reaps the rewards. What is emerging, then, is not a truly symbiotic ecosystem of innovation, but a parasitic one, in which the most lossmaking elements are socialised, while the profitmaking ones are largely privatised. Do ordinary taxpayers understand that their taxes fund the fundamental innovations that drive their economy? This book has a controversial thesis. But it is basically right. The failure to recognise the role of the government in driving innovation may well be the greatest threat to rising prosperity.
