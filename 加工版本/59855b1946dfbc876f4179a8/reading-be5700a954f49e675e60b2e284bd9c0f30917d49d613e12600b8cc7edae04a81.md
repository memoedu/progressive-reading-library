# Effective social norms: small groups

---

## 正文

在 Harvard College Effective Altruism，我们为成员举办了一系列每周聚餐。我们很快发现，大家往往会 cluster 成一个 conversation group。当参加者有十五到二十人时，这个 group 就变得很难驾驭。事实上，交谈甚至会变得_拖沓_，大概是因为大家担心在那么多不太熟悉的人面前说话。结果是，几乎没有人有机会说话，_而且_讨论也没什么意思。

Smaller groups 能让交谈更有意思。尤其是对于那些安静的人，或不太熟悉 effective altruism 的 ideas 的人，它们能让环境更加友好。好在有不少办法可以促成这样的 groups：

-   如果你正在参加 EA event，就加入一个 smaller group 吧！克制自己想和最熟悉的人，或自己觉得最酷的人 cluster 在一起的本能冲动。

-   如果你的 group 看起来越来越大，就离开它，另找一个。

-   如果你足够身手不凡，就可以通过压低声音，和身边的人开始一个 sub-topic，同时用 body language 将它与 main conversation 分开，来 induce conversation-group mitosis。（这很难。）

-   如果其他办法都不奏效，你可以明确提议将一个 group 拆开。这通常没有你想象的那么尴尬。

## 原文

At Harvard College Effective Altruism we ran a series of weekly dinners with our members. We quickly found out that people tended to cluster into a single conversation group. With fifteen or twenty attendees, that group got pretty unwieldy. In fact, the conversation actually _dragged_, probably because people were worried about talking in front of so many other that they didn’t know well. The result was that almost nobody got to talk _and_ the discussions weren’t interesting.

Smaller groups make conversations more interesting. And they especially make the environment more friendly for people who are quiet, or less familiar with the ideas of effective altruism. Fortunately, there are a number of ways to encourage them:

-   If you’re at an EA event, join a smaller group! Suppress your natural urge to cluster with the people you know best or the people you think are the coolest.

-   If it looks like your group is getting too large, break away and find another one.

-   If you’re enough of a ninja, you can induce conversation-group mitosis by starting a sub-topic with the person next to you in a lower voice, and simultaneously using body language to separate that from the main conversation. (This is hard.)

-   If all else fails, you can explicitly suggest splitting up a group. It’s usually less awkward than you’d think.
