> 当然，并非所有 scientists 都接受其他 advanced civilizations exist 这一观念。最近，一些对此有所思考的人正在问：如果 extraterrestrial intelligence 是 abundant 的，为什么我们还没有看到它的 manifestations？.. 为什么这些 beings 还没有为了自身便利而 restructured 整个 Galaxy？… 为什么它们不在这里？人们很容易据此 deduce，advanced extraterrestrial civilizations at most 只有少数几个——要么因为我们是最早 emerged 的 technical civilizations 之一，要么因为所有这样的 civilizations 都注定在进一步发展之前就 destroy themselves。
> 
> 在我看来，这样的绝望还为时过早。——[Carl Sagan（1978）](https://web.archive.org/web/20060818144558/http://www.bigear.org/vol1no2/sagan.htm)

2019年，Nick Bostrom 在 _Global Policy_ 上发表了一篇关于 [“The Vulnerable World Hypothesis”](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/vulnerable.pdf) 的重要 paper。大致而言，Vulnerable World Hypothesis（VWH）指的是这样一种观点：intelligent species discover near-ungovernable technologies，并因这些 technologies 而 wipe themselves out，这或许是 near-inevitable 的。此前已有许多作者讨论过这一观点，但据我所知，Bostrom 的 paper 是第一篇 in-depth analysis。以下是我关于这篇 paper 的工作笔记。这些笔记远非全面——它们围绕 paper 中的几个要素展开，并简要勾勒了一些受其启发的想法。笔记讨论的内容包括：(1) 将 _Friendly World Hypothesis_ 作为 VWH 的 alternative；以及对 VWH 和 FWH 的态度如何 shape 对 ASI xrisk 的态度；(2) 对 Alignment Problem 进行 reframing，将其视为让 _liberal society 的 values 和_ _institutions_ _（其中至关重要的包括 market）_ _与 differential technology development 进行 aligning 的 problem_；(3) 作为 Alignment Problem 的一种 possible solution，我勾勒了 _provably beneficial surveillance_ 这一想法；以及 (4) 勾勒一种 planetary civilization，在其中 VWH 会是 true 的，却会长期不为人所察觉。

人们很容易主要从对 Artificial Superintelligence（ASI）所带来的 risk 的担忧出发看待 VWH。这一方面是因为此类担忧如今受到广泛关注，另一方面是因为 Bostrom 在 ASI risk 方面广为人知的贡献[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn1)。然而，我认为 VWH 既具有更广泛的意义，也具有独立的意义，主要从 ASI 的角度来研究它是一个错误。相反，我们应当首先直接探讨 VWH 本身，根据它自身的价值来审视它，然后才考虑它与 ASI 的 relationship。这就是我在这些笔记中（大体上）将采用的思路。

我此前做过一些与 VWH 有关的工作，在关于 ASI 所带来的 existential risk 的笔记[2](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn2)，以及对我目前兴趣的简短介绍[3](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn3) 中，讨论过我所说的 _recipes for ruin_。我所说的 recipes for ruin，是指 simple、easily executed、immensely destructive、near ungovernable，而且可能 end humanity，或至少 wreak catastrophic world-changing damage 的 recipes。那些工作大多是在阅读 Bostrom 的 paper 之前完成的，对 Bostrom 的工作没有进行足够深入的探讨。这些笔记部分弥补了这一失误。虽然我们各自的 conceptions 有所不同，但这不是我在这里最关心的；我更关心的是 exploring VWH。

Bostrom 对 VWH 的 defines 如下：

> 如果 technological development continues，那么在某个时候，一组 capabilities 将会被 attained，使 civilization 的 devastation 变得 extremely likely，除非 civilization sufficiently exits semianarchic default condition。

他进一步将“semi-anarchic default condition”defines 为这样一个世界：其 policing 和 governance 的 capacities 非常 limited，而且存在许多具有 diverse motivations 的 actors：

> 1.  preventive policing 的 capacity 是 Limited 的。States 不具备
> 
> sufficiently reliable 的 real-time surveillance 和 interception 手段，无法使其 territory 内任何 individual 或 small group carry out illegal actions 变得 virtually impossible——尤其是那些遭到 > 99 per cent 的 population 极力反对的 actions。
> 
> 1.  global governance 的 capacity 是 Limited 的。不存在 reliable 的
> 
> mechanism 来 solving global coordination problems 和 protecting global commons——尤其是在涉及 vital national security interests 的 high-stakes situations 中。
> 
> 1.  motivations 是 Diverse 的。存在一种 wide 且可辨认为
> 
> human 的 motives distribution，由大量 actors（同时处于 individual 和 state level）体现出来——具体而言，许多 actors 在相当程度上受到 perceived self-interest（例如 money、power、status、舒适和便利）的 motivated，而另有一些 actors（‘the apocalyptic residual’）即使自己也要付出高昂代价，仍会以 destroy civilization 的方式 act。

（顺便说一句，这是一个非常有意思的 definition，因为在前两个部分中，它主要通过某些 qualities 的缺失来 defining 一类 civilization。这个 definition 也存在一些问题，因为对于与 humans 非常不同的 lifeforms，“governance”或“diverse motivations”这样的 categorizations 可能有非常不同、甚至相当不明确的含义。记住这一 caveat，我们姑且接受 Bostrom 的总体 framing，继续往下看。）

人们很容易把 VWH 当作无谓的 speculation，甚至不必要的 catastrophizing 而不屑一顾。但仅仅因为它似乎超出了我们的日常经验就将其否定，是一个错误。这让人想起第二次世界大战结束时担任美国武装部队参谋长的海军上将 William Leahy，他曾这样评价 atomic bomb：“那是我们做过的最愚蠢的事情。这个 bomb 永远不会 go off，我是以 explosives 的 expert 身份说这话的。”今天，我们会摇摇头，或许觉得 Leahy 很无知。但事实上，他具备 technical knowledge：他曾在 Annapolis 教授 physics 和 chemistry，也对海军的 weapons 有丰富的使用经验。而 Hiroshima bomb 在 _a priori_ 看来确实是 implausible 的：取两块 inert 的 material——每块都小到可以由一个人携带——然后让它们 extremely rapidly 地合到一起。如果你从未听说过 nuclear weapons，你会预期它们以 city-destroying force explode 吗？直觉上，这很荒谬。你需要对这个世界——对 relativity、particle physics 和 branching processes——有极为丰富的了解，这件事才会变得 plausible。正是那种新的、相当 subtle 的 understanding，揭示了一种 immense destruction 的 latent possibility，而仅仅几十年前，人们几乎完全没有察觉这种 possibility。同样，VWH 完全可能是 true 的，只是其 reasons 目前还不为我们 humans 所知。

## “Easy nukes”，以及一个 advanced planetary civilization 的概述：它是一个毫不知情的 Vulnerable World

作为 VWH 的 intuition pump，Bostrom 探讨了一个 thought experiment，描述了一种 alternate reality，其中存在 make nukes 的 easy way。历史上，Manhattan Project 耗资超过 \$20 billion（以 2023 dollars 计）。不过，需要如此大的投入，这一点绝非在 _a priori_ 意义上显而易见。也许存在某种其他的 physical or chemical process，我们过去（以及现在）只是不知道它，而它能够实现 nuclear weapons 的 easy construction。Bostrom 较为详细地探讨了假如这种 process 在 1930s 或 1940s 被 discovered，可能随之而来的 horrendous consequences，最后用相当克制的措辞说道：“我们很幸运，making nukes 结果是 hard 的。”我不打算在这里复述他的评论。我确实想提出三点简短的 observations：

1.  **nukes 的 energy cost 低得令人惊讶：**的确，nuclear weapon 的 intrinsic energy cost 比我在 _a priori_ 意义上所想的要低得多。1 kilotonne yield 略高于 4 trillion Joules。作为比较，1 kilowatt hour 的 electricity——wholesale price 大概在 5 cents 左右——接近 4 million Joules。如果能够 efficiently convert electricity to nuclear explosive energy，那么 cost 就会是 \$50,000 per kilotonne. Clearly, the reason for the immense cost of nuclear weapons isn't the energy input. In fact, modern nuclear weapons actually _beat_ this limit. For instance, a Brookings Institute study[4](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn4) estimated the cost of a B83 thermonuclear bomb (yield up to 1.2 megatonnes) at \$4.9 million 1996 dollars 这个量级，包括 research、development、testing 和 evaluation，但不包括 operations 和 post-deployment costs。以 2023 dollars 计，这大约是 \$8,000 per kilotonne。我觉得这个 figure 低得惊人！

2.  **通过命名来召唤这样的 technologies：**美国最杰出的 nuclear weapons designer 是 physicist Ted Taylor。作家 John McPhee 写过一本出色的书，介绍 Taylor，书名是“The Curve of Binding Energy”。这本书不时谈到的一个主题，是使用 nuclear weapons 来 devastate 美国境内的 targets。Taylor 和 McPhee 在 911 之前来到 Manhattan 的旧 World Trade Center，讨论 building a small bomb 来将其摧毁有多么容易。Taylor 说：“考虑到人们会遭遇什么，我只要详细思考这个问题，就会非常难过，完全不想再考虑它……而且，还有一个我们没有谈过的 simplicity 层次，因为谈论它超出了我的承受限度。一种 make a bomb 的 way。它如此 simple，以至于我就是不想描述它。我可以告诉你这一点：只要 make 一个具有 unpredictable yield 的 crude bomb——但 knocking this building down 的 chance 超过一半——所需要的，不过是大约 a dozen kilos 的 plutonium-oxide powder、high explosives（我不想说多少），以及几样任何人都能在五金店买到的东西。”读过上述访谈后，一位名叫 Peter Zimmerman 的 physicist 居然 carried out 了这个 design[5](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn5)。对我们而言，幸运的是，无论当时还是现在，都没有已知的 making plutonium-oxide powder 的 easy way，而且它的 distribution 受到 nuclear non-proliferation treaty 的 controlled。在民间故事里，有时只要说出恶魔的真名，就能将其召唤出来；对于 recipes for ruin，可能存在某种类似的 effect，只要知道一个真名，似乎就几乎足以将它召唤出来。的确，AI safety 似乎就发生过这样的事：对 AI dangers 的早期警告，似乎启发了几个极其追逐 power 的人，让他们创办了后来成为领先 AI companies 的企业。

3.  **directed design 的作用：**人们很容易提出一个 anthropic argument，说明为什么“easy nukes”不可能存在：“如果这真的 trivial，那么它们早就发生了，humanity 也就不会在这里了。”思考这种 argument 在什么 conditions 下是 true、又在什么 conditions 下是 false，是件有意思的事。实质上，这种 argument 曾被用来论证，像 Ice-9 这样的 destabilizing states of matter 不可能存在：如果 Ice-9 很容易成为可能，那么它现在早就已经 occurred by chance，我们也就不会在这里思考这个问题了[6](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn6)。我们存在，所以它不可能存在。这在某些方面是一个合理的 argument，但在一个方面却严重站不住脚：water molecules 的 configurations 所构成的 search space 是 exponentially large，而 random exploration 对它的 explores 极其缓慢。即便 Earth 已经存在 billions of years，也只足以 explore 一个 infinitesimal fraction。但一个深刻 understanding water 的 adversary 不会 at random 地 explore 这个 search space：相反，他们会利用这种 understanding，集中寻找 problematic configurations。我当然不是想暗示，对于 nuclear weapons 或 destabilizing states of matter，这种情况很可能发生。我想说的是，这在 generically 意义上是 true 的：dangerous new weapons 通常不会 occur by chance，而是 improved understanding 对 search 加以 directing 的结果。

> 论证 VWH 是 true，并不是这篇 paper 的 primary purpose。（我把它视为一个 open question，不过，考虑到 available evidence，在我看来，对 VWH 是 false 抱有任何程度的确信都是不合理的。）

这个处理很好：一个 question 本身要有价值，并不需要人们知道它的 answer——这里的 question 是，我们生活在一个 Vulnerable World，还是一个 Friendly World。我发现，用“Friendly World Hypothesis”这个 term 来表示 VWH 的一个（非常粗略的）converse 很有帮助。一种可能而又相当简单的 formulation 是：

> 即便 humanity 处于 semi-anarchic default state，也 extremely unlikely 会 attained 一组 capabilities，使 civilization 被 extinguished 变得 near-inevitable。

实际上，我并不清楚 FWH 的 _best_ formulation 是什么。最简单的可能做法，是把它设为 VWH 的 logical negation，但这样做会出现几个问题。这种做法的好处，是最终会得到一个真正的 dichotomy。但这只是一个小小的好处。更好的做法，是像我上面尝试的那样，选取一个本身就具有 intrinsic interest 的 alternate position，然后认为两者之间存在一个由各种 positions 构成的 continuum。我会在下文回到这一点。

关于 recipes for ruin 的三点简短 notes：

1.  **一个 advanced planetary civilization 的概述：它是一个毫不知情的 Vulnerable World：**Bostrom 的“easy nukes”存在于一种 alternate reality 中（据我们所知）。但即便在我们的 universe 中，也可以勾勒出某些 planetary civilizations，在那里，这种 scenario _确实_可能发生。举一个 example[7](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn7)，设想一个具有 CO2 atmosphere 的 planet，environment 中有 near-ambient magnesium dust grains——类似于广泛分布的 sand，只不过这些 sands 是由 magnesium 而非 silica 构成的。magnesium 本身是 reactive 的，而且相当 unstable，但假设这些 grains 覆盖着一层非常薄的 inert magnesium oxide，那是更早的时候 atmosphere 中尚有少量 oxygen 时留下的 remnant。通常，在这样一个 planet 上，你不会看到 fires——CO2 是非常好的 fire suppressant。不过，如果能够 make 少量 oxygen 和一个 intense spark，你就可以 start a fire，使 magnesium grains 即使隔着那层薄薄的 magnesium oxide coating layer 也会 ignite。Magnesium 在 burns 时温度极高，fire 可以 spread 到 adjacent magnesium sands，而 fire 的 intensity 会使 ambient CO2 被 broken down，从而提供更多 oxygen。如果 magnesium sands 分布得足够广，fire 就会 spread widely and rapidly，consuming 沿途的一切。这很可能是一种这样的 situation：(a) recipe for ruin 确实可能存在；(b) 如果不了解相关 chemistry，它在 _a priori_ 意义上会显得极其 implausible；以及 (c) catastrophic event 发生的 main barrier，会是对那种 chemistry 的 improved _understanding_。简而言之，在这样一个 planet 上，fire 基本上会是未知的，而且在大量 chemistry 被 understood 之前，这种可能性会显得 implausible。但一旦 understood，start 这样的 fire 就会是 trivial 的，而且它会比 Earth 上（known types of）的 fire devastating 得多得多。这种 scenario 应当让人停下来认真想一想。是否存在类似的 recipes，只是我们 Earth 上的人尚不知道？

2.  **这种 power 的诱惑性：**一个 skeptic 可能会回应说，speculate 关于“easy nukes”或更一般的“recipes for ruin”，说起来当然容易。但如果只是 speculation，就不是很有说服力。而且，作为一个 scientist，人很容易_想要_ prove recipes for ruin exist。这些 problems 有一种非同寻常的诱惑力。是否存在 antimatter production 的 easy method？是否存在 making nuclear weapons 的 easy method？是否存在 finding a destabilizing state of matter 的 easy method？是否存在 producing a pathogen 的 easy method？这一切都很可怕。然而，对于这些以及类似的 questions，我极其强烈地感受到——而且我怀疑许多 scientists 也感受到——一种类似于 Freeman Dyson 谈到 nuclear weapons 时所说的东西：“我自己也感受过，nuclear weapons 的光芒。如果你以 scientist 的身份接触它们，那是无法抗拒的。感到它就在你的手中。去 release 那种 fuels the stars 的 energy。让它听从你的意志。去创造这些奇迹，将 a million tons 的 rock 举向天空，这种事情会让人产生拥有 illimitable power 的错觉。我想说，它在某些方面要对我们所有的麻烦负责，这种你或许可以称为‘technical arrogance’的东西，会在人们看到自己凭借头脑能做到什么时将他们征服。”换句话说，对 scientist 而言，prove 这是 possible 的，是一个极其有趣的 puzzle。但我们应该抵制这种诱惑。

3.  **recipes for ruin 的 existence _并不_只是 laws of physics 和我们 extant tech base 的一个 property：**在最初思考时，我认为 recipes for ruin 的 existence 或 non-existence 是 laws of physics 的一个 property（尽管是 conditioned on 一个 civilization 的 existing technology level）。但这种看法过于 simplistic。事实上，它取决于这个世界的 contingent facts。一个 illustration 就足以说明这一点：“laws of biology”并不只是由 physics 来 determined，还取决于世界历史中的某些 contingent（但非常重要，而且并非 arbitrary）的偶然事件。例如，某个 molecule 或 complex 是否 acts as 一场 pandemic 的 root cause，很大程度上取决于 extant environment：其中包括 existing immune systems 能多好地 manufacture antibodies。对于其他 classes of risk，analogous statement 在更广泛的范围内也 holds。

## 使 liberal society 与 differential technology development 相 Aligning、作为 collective action problem 的 safety，以及 AI danger tax

Differential technology development (DTD) 大致是这样一种想法：优先致力于 protective technologies，而不是 risk-increasing technologies。以下是 Bostrom 对 DTD 和 VWH 的论述：

> 虽然 targeted \[technological\] regress 或许不太可能实现，但我们可以争取使 risk-increasing technologies 的 rate of advancement 相对于 protective technologies 的 rate of advancement 放缓。这就是 principle of differential technological development 所表达的想法。在其最初的 formulation 中，这个 principle 关注 existential risk；但我们也可以更广泛地 apply 它，将那些具有‘仅仅’是 devastational potential 的 technologies 也包含在内：
> 
> _Differential Technological Development 的 Principle。_ 对 dangerous and harmful technologies 的 development 进行 Retard，尤其是那些 raise the level of existential risk 的 technologies；并 accelerate beneficial technologies 的 development，尤其是那些 reduce 由 nature 或其他 technologies 所 posed 的 existential risks 的 technologies (Bostrom, 2002)。
> 
> 这个 principle of differential technological development 与 technological determinism 的 plausible forms 是 compatible 的。例如，即使所有能够 developed 的 technologies 都注定会被 developed，它们何时被 developed 仍然可能很重要。它们出现的 order 可以造成重要的 difference – \[例子\]…
> 
> 对 differential technological development 进行 Correctly implementing，显然是一项 difficult strategic task（参见 Collingridge, 1980）。尽管如此，对于一个 altruistically 关心 long-term outcomes、并且参与某种 inventive enterprise 的 actor（例如作为 researcher、funder、entrepreneur、regulator 或 legislator），仍然值得尝试。无论如何，有些 implications 似乎相当明显：例如，不要致力于 laser isotope separation，不要致力于 bioweapons，也不要 develop 那些会 empower 任意 individuals，使其能够 unilaterally 对 Earth’s climate 作出 drastic alterations 的 geoengineering forms。在 accelerating 那些会 directly facilitate 这类 ominous developments 的 enabling technologies——例如 DNA synthesis machines——之前，要三思。但应 boost 那些 predominantly protective 的 technologies；例如，能够实现对 disease outbreaks 的 more efficient monitoring，或使 detect covert WMD programs 更容易的 technologies。

Bostrom 对 DTD 的强调，与 [Vitalik Buterin 最近关于 d/acc 的 manifesto](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/27/techno_optimism.html) 之间有相当多的 overlap。Buterin 强调 market 与 DTD 之间的 alignment，但 Buterin 和 Bostrom 都没有解决如何实现这种 alignment 的 problem。我们不希望将 collective safety 仅仅交给 purely altruistic motivations：最好也能由 self-interest 来提供它。然而，safety 和 security 的许多方面都是（近似的）public goods 或 collective action problems[8](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn8)，而按照目前的构造，market 对它们的供给是 undersupplies。处理这一点的方式有很多。我支持目前正在进行的努力——包括 Buterin 的努力——即 develop 有助于处理这些 problems 的新 financial instruments。economics 中的一个 classic solution，是对任何具有 negative externalities 的 technology 征收某种形式的 Pigouvian taxes。我们已经开始看到 AI 带来的这类 negative externalities，例如：misinformation 对 public commons 的 polluting、racism 和其他形式的 bias，以及更多其他问题[9](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn9)。可以针对这类 harms 对 AI companies 进行 tax[10](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn10)，其中包括这些 companies 发布的 open source models 所造成的任何 harms[11](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn11)。情况很复杂，因为这些 models，无论是不是 open source，有时也可能具有很大的 positive spillovers，为 common good 作出贡献。对这个 problem 进行 thorough analysis 似乎非常值得。一个重要的 issue，是已经造成的 immediate harms 与 future harms 之间的 difference。在这一点上，这种情况与 carbon tax 有些相似；思考某种 AI danger tax 的可能性，是一件有意思的事。

## 作为 Xrisk 讨论中一个 Crux 的 Friendly 与 Vulnerable Worlds：Recipes for Ruin 是否 Possible？以及：Alignment Problem 的一种 More Powerful Form

取决于你认为我们生活在 Vulnerable World 还是 Friendly World，你很可能会对世界应当如何 governed 得出截然不同的 conclusions。在我生命中的大部分时间里，我都 implicitly 相信 Friendly World Hypothesis，并 explicitly 坚持这样一个 principle[12](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn12)：science 是 on net good 的，benefits humanity，而且应当被 pursued aggressively，只有对那些 obviously negative applications 才局部施加 constraints。例如，我们可以 ban leaded gasoline 或 asbestos 之类的东西，但我们当然不会 ban 那种构成背景、使 leaded gasoline 和 asbestos 这类 discoveries 得以出现的 basic research。然而，我认真看待 Vulnerable World Hypothesis，而它正使我开始怀疑，对 basic science 进行 open, aggressive exploration 的 principle 是否应当永远适用。当我开始怀疑这个 principle 的 timelessness 时，我也开始思考，应当用什么来取代它，以及何时取代。

至关重要的是，我希望任何替代方案都能让我们获得 liberal values 和 institutions 的大部分 benefits，包括 science 和 technology 的 benefits，但_同时_又不会使我们 irreversibly 暴露于 catastrophic danger。我在上文提到了 aligning market 与 differential technology development 的 problem。我怀疑，与更 conventional formulations 相比，这是一种表达 Alignment Problem 的 more powerful 方式[13](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn13)。事实上，既然“market”本身是 mutable 的，而且可以被 redesigned——它只有在真正 promotes the good 的范围内才获得 legitimacy——那么，Alignment Problem 或许最好表述为 liberal values 和 institutions（包括 market）与 differential technology development 之间的 alignment。如此表述，Alignment Problem 的一个 solution 就会 automatically entail programmable biology、ASI 和许多其他 technologies 的 safe development。我仍在苦苦思索如何做到这一点，但我喜欢这个立足点，因为它是 fundamentally optimistic 的。

回到 Vulnerable-Friendly World dichotomy，我相信，这是许多 xrisk 讨论背后的一个 fundamental crux，包括与 ASI 和 biosafety 相关的 xrisk。而且，我不认为大多数从事 advanced science 和 technology 的人认真思考过这一点[14](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn14)。事实上，许多热衷于 technology 的人根本拒绝探讨，将 Friendly World 这个根本不明显的 incumbent position 当作“显而易见”来接受。在我们缓慢走出 technologically primitive state 的过程中，采取这个 incumbent position 在历史上是合理的，因为在那种 state 中，即使 medium-scale destruction 也极其困难。但过去 100 年 science 和 technology 的发展势头，应当促使我们认真考虑：Friendly World Hypothesis 可能根本就是错的。

换一种说法，我认为，关于 technology 的一个 fundamental question 是：你是否相信 _recipes for ruin 是 possible 的_，也就是说：你是否认为，在对 science 和 technology 有 sufficient understanding 的情况下，总有一天会发现用于 building catastrophic technologies 的 inexpensive、easy-to-follow recipes？我起初以为，在这个问题上，人们会分成两个阵营：一类人本能地觉得答案是“是”，另一类人本能地觉得答案是“否”。但事实证明，还有第三个阵营：那些真的不愿意探讨这个问题的人。实际上，他们选择了 incumbent position，却省去了为它提出 good argument 的麻烦。

（我一直将这件事 framing 为 Friendly World 与 Vulnerable World 之间的 dichotomy。当然，正如前面所说，它实际上是一个 continuum。尽管如此，关于 technology 的 governance 的许多 positions，仍然 implicitly、有时也 explicitly 地采取其中一个 position。）

## 关于 (ASI) Xrisk 和 VWH 的 Intuitions 如何 Grounded 于个人的 Background Expertise

在 ASI xrisk 的讨论中，有一个相关的 challenge。并不存在真正的“ASI 方面的 experts”，原因与 18th century 没有真正的 automobiles 方面的 experts 相同。但不同 fields 的 experts 正试图运用各自（不同）的 expertises 来理解 AI 和 ASI 的 impact。Economists 将 AI 和 ASI 作为 economic problem 来 analyze：它将如何 restructure labour markets？它会 concentrate wealth 吗？Sociologists 将它作为 sociological problem 来 analyze：它会 cause unrest 吗？它会对 families 和 relationships 产生什么影响？Lawyers 则把它作为 legal problem：在 generative AI 的时代，我们应该如何看待 copyright？谁将对 abuses 负有 culpable 的责任？Biologists 将它作为 biological problem：AI 将 enable 哪些新的 biothreats 和 biomedicines？Physicists 将它作为 physical problem：是否有新的 reaction 或 phase of matter 或 material 类型等待被 discovered，并可能带来 explosive consequences？Security experts 将它作为 security problem：ASI 将 create 哪些新的 vulnerabilities？诸如此类[15](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn15)。

当然，ASI 的 problem 并不是其中任何一种；或者更准确地说，它包含所有这些，而且远不止这些。它 affects 我们 change the universe 的 ability，而这 transcends disciplines。

因此，ASI 似乎 pose 哪种 problem，在很大程度上取决于一个人此前的 expertise。比起具体细节，更重要的是，不同类型的 expert 会带来不同类型的 intuition，涉及哪些 phenomena 是 latent in nature 的、哪些是 possible（或并非如此）的，以及哪些 defenses 可以被 worked out（或不能）[16](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn16)。许多对 xrisk 不屑一顾的人缺乏这些 intuitions：例如，economist 根本不会拥有与 security expert 相同的 intuitions，反过来也一样。physicist 不会拥有 economist 的 intuitions，反过来也一样。事实上，许多人对 emergent phenomena[17](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn17) 几乎没有任何 intuition。一个有历史意识的 physicist、chemist 或 biologist，会在反复接触中逐渐理解，十分 simple 的 rules 如何可能 give rise to 令人惊讶、甚至出乎意料到令人震惊的 phenomena——从 fire 到 superfluidity，到 nuclear chain reactions，再到 public key cryptography。这使人对 latent in nature 的 power 和意外之处都产生十分健全的敬畏。我怀疑，大多数人对这种 latent power 和意外之处的感受要弱得多。此外，我预期 ASI 将会 discover 并 create 新的 levels 的 emergent phenomena，它们 governed by 尚未被想到的 rules；有些类型的 problem 实际上将完全超出我们（现在，或许也包括未来）的 comprehension，涉及尚未 discovered 的 expertise 领域。同样值得注意的是，许多最坚定相信 ASI xrisk 的人，并不是任何相关 science 的 experts，而是从 science fiction 中获取自己的 intuition。这是件好事，但也需要谨慎，因为叙事上 plausible 的事情，往往并不等同于 true 的事情。Nature 同时比我们更 constrained，也更富想象力。

我一直从 ASI xrisk 的角度 framing 这个问题，但类似的评论也适用于 Vulnerable World Hypothesis。一个人相信（或不相信）VWH 的程度，在很大程度上取决于从此前的 expertise 中获得的 intuitions。这 creates 了许多 issues，显然也不是一种令人十分满意的局面，但值得指出。

## 如何 Responding to VWH，以及 Provably Beneficial Surveillance

假设你接受 VWH，认为它有相当大的可能是 true。humanity 应该如何 respond？Bostrom investigates 并 rejects 了若干 possible solutions，认为它们不足以完成这项任务。他最终 argues，humanity 将需要强大得多的 policing 和强大得多的 global governance：

> 因此，只要我们担心 VWH 可能是 true，就必须考虑其余两种 achieving stabilization 的 possible ways：
> 
> 1.  Create 用于 extremely effective preventive policing 的 capacity。Develop 所需的 intra-state governance capacity，以 extremely high reliability 来 prevent 任何 individual 或 small group——包括那些无法被 deterred 的——carrying out 任何 highly illegal 的 action；并且
>     
> 2.  Create 用于 strong global governance 的 capacity。Develop 所需的 inter-state governance capacity，以便 reliably solve 最严重的 global commons problems，并在任何 vital security interests 受到影响的情况下，ensure states（以及其他 strong organizations）之间的 robust cooperation——即使存在非常强的 incentives，促使其 defect from agreements，或从一开始就 refuse to sign on。
>     
> 
> (1) 和 (2) 所 reflected 的两个 governance gaps，一个在 micro-scale，另一个在 macro-scale，是当代 world order 的两个 Achilles’ heels。只要它们仍然 unprotected，civilization 就仍然 vulnerable to 某个潜在的 technological black ball，后者将使 strike 能够 directed 到那里。在这样的 discovery 从 urn 中出现之前，我们很容易忽视自己有多么 exposed。
> 
> …
> 
> 要 stabilize 这样的 vulnerabilities，需要一种 extremely well-developed preventive policing capacity。States 需要具备足够密切地 monitor 其 citizens 的 ability，从而能够 intercept 任何开始 preparing 某种 act of mass destruction 的人。
> 
> 这种 surveillance 和 interception 的 feasibility 取决于 scenario 的具体情况：以 destructively 的方式 deploy black-ball technology 需要多久？涉及的 actions 有多 observable？能否将它们与我们不想 prohibit 的 behavior distinguished 开来？不过，一个将当前可用的 technologies deploy 到最大程度的 state，能够使 Type-1 vulnerability spectrum 中相当大的一部分被 stabilized，这是 plausible 的。而 surveillance technology 中预期的 advances，将极大 expand achievable protection。要想了解真正 intensive 的 surveillance level 可能是什么样子，请考虑下面的 vignette \[关于一个“High-tech Panopticon”，其中每个人都 fitted with 一个 'freedom tag'，并由 'freedom officers' monitored\]
> 
> …
> 
> 然而，ubiquitous-surveillance-powered preventive policing 与 effective global governance 结合起来，将足以 stabilize 大多数 vulnerabilities，使继续 scientific and technological development 成为 safe 的，即使 VWH 是 true。

正如 Bostrom 多次所说，这是一个 extreme solution，最好只在 VWH 极有可能是 true，而且某种 catastrophic technology 的 development 已经 imminent 时才予以考虑。任何 benefit 是否值得付出这个 cost，显然完全不清楚！“好消息是，你不必担心世界被 destroyed；坏消息是，你现在生活在 Big Brother 的统治之下。”我们确实应该极其审慎，不轻易接受这样的 regime，而且我确实怀疑还有其他出路。（我不会在这里讨论，因为虽然提出想法很容易，但我还不清楚哪一种最有希望。）不过，我确实想更深入地思考 Bostrom 的 proposal，而不止于条件反射式地否定。大部分麻烦来自 ubiquitous surveillance 的 proposal。它如今显得令人深恶痛绝，这是有充分理由的——Stasi、KGB、purges 和 Gulag 的可怕历史，以及许多其他衍生于 surveillance 的 institutions。Orwell 的“1984”是警告，不是操作手册，而“freedom officers”和“freedom tags”这样的想法，简直像直接出自 Orwell 的笔下。

尽管会有这种条件反射式的反应，我认为仍值得发挥想象力，认真思考这个问题：是否 possible 去 design 一个真正 enhances liberty 的 surveillance system？不是某种愤世嫉俗的、newspeak 式的 1984 风格，而是真正如此？我知道，有些人在 ideologically 的层面如此反对它，以至于无法认真 explore 这种 possibility，哪怕只是作为一个 hypothetical。对于这些人，我建议直接跳到最后一节。

对于其他人：我注意到，已经存在一些出人意料地 beneficial 且 unmalevolent 的 surveillance regimes。考虑到 Apple、Google、Microsoft 和 NSA 掌握着有关 individuals 活动的海量 information，它们似乎对 individuals 造成的 harm 相对如此之少，这让我颇感意外。Apple 对我的了解，远超过 Stasi 对 median citizen 的了解，甚至超过其对大多数 citizens of interest 的了解。虽然 Apple 并不掌握我的生杀大权，但它们完全可能 legally 做出一些相当可怕的事情。然而，总的来说，这似乎极其罕见。我当然不是说 abuses 不会发生。我的意思是：如果在 2000 年，你告诉我 surveillance capitalism 到了 2023 年将导致比我们已经看到的严重 orders of magnitude 的 abuses，我不会感到意外。我发现自己十分好奇，_为什么_没有出现严重得多的滑坡。

我绝不是建议将 NSA 或 surveillance capitalism 作为效仿的 model。不过，这确实引出了两个问题：(1) 为什么它们（看起来）造成的 harm 远少于 Stasi 这样的 organizations；以及 (2) 是否 possible 通过 design 来 prevent 它们造成的 harms，同时 protecting against 那些 catastrophically damaging actions？

我在上文问过，safe differential technology development 是否 possible，同时又能 aligned with 一个 liberal society 的 values 和 institutions。如果我们接受 VWH，并认真考虑 surveillance，那么我们或许可以问，是否 possible 去 develop _provably beneficial surveillance_？这是对 _provably safe AI_[18](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn18) 呼吁的回应与对照。虽然 provably safe AI 是一个激发思考的想法，但在我看来，它似乎只有在 ASIs 由 good actors created 时才会奏效。如果 bad actors 开始 producing ASIs，我们就会陷入大麻烦。

不过，假设我们转而用 provably beneficial surveillance 这一 goal 来替代那个 goal。Surveillance 比 computation 更自然地是一种 monopoly；它也更自然地是 preventative 的。当然，“Beneficial”需要被 defined，但它会 include 许多我们视为理所当然的事情：各种 act 的 freedoms 和 liberties；多种 privacy 的 maintenance；develop 大多数 science and technology 的 ability。但它也会 include detect 和 suppress catastrophic risks 的 capacity。是否有可能 develop 一种 surveillance regime，既 guaranteed 与我们今天享有的同样好、甚至更好的 liberties，同时又 suppresses catastrophic risks[19](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn19)？简而言之：为什么不转而把重点从 developing safe ASI 转向 developing provably beneficial surveillance，并让 safe ASI 成为其 consequence？

请注意，这里使用“provable”一词，并不（完全）是指 mathematical proof 意义上的 provable。事实上，我不认为那是 possible 的，因为它指向的是现实世界，而不是 mathematics 的完美世界。相反，它意在表达非常强的 guarantees——适当时使用 mathematical proofs，而在适合使用现实世界保障的情况下，则使用非常强的 real-world safeguards[20](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn20)。

如何 develop provably beneficial surveillance？这需要开展大量超出这些笔记范围的工作。值得注意的是，现有的大多数 surveillance regimes 在 developed 时，无论是在 conception 方面还是 operationally，都很少受到 external oversight。它们也很少以 decentralized 的方式，把工作 delegate 给具有不同 motives 的 actors。而且，它们往往在缺乏 effective competition 的情况下 operate。我认为这些事实极其令人鼓舞：这意味着，这里有_大量_唾手可得的机会，有一些显而易见的杠杆，可以借此 reduced 许多最恶劣的 surveillance abuses。Classic surveillance regimes 通常 prioritized 的是 regime，而不是整个人类，这意味着这里的 design space 尚未得到 explored 的程度令人惊讶。把 Signal 这样的 messaging apps 看作 overseeing authority 真正试图 prioritize users 时会发生什么的实例，是很有意思的。我以后很可能会更详细地回到这个问题。

## 杂想

1.  **Techno-capitalism 与向 technologist class 的 transfer of power：**我讨论过的几乎所有事情，都内含着这样一个想法：technology 的 solution 仍然是更多的 technology。我们可以把这称为 technological solutionism。Climate 让你沮丧？那就做些 geoengineering 或 carbon removal and sequestration。为 Biorisk 忧心忡忡？那就来些 real-time vaccines！或者 BSL-4 hardened cites[21](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn21)。Nukes 仍让你害怕？那就多做些 missile shields 方面的工作！Vulnerable World Hypothesis 让你夜不能寐？那就着手 aligning 我们的 liberal values 与 differential technology development！诸如此类。这种 technological solutionism 在同时思考 VWH 和 ASI 的各个群体中都很常见，从 startup world 到 EA、e/acc、doomer，等等。在这些观点中，_所有_观点都内含着一种 transfer of power：power 从那些不具有 agency over technology 的人手中，转移到那些具有 agency over technology 的人手中。正如 [Cory Doctorow 所强调的](https://twitter.com/michael_nielsen/status/1144445213554798593)，technology 会内在地将 power centralizes 到 designers 和 builders，以及他们周围的一圈人手中。H. G. Wells 在他由 Eloi 和 Morlocks 构成的 two-tier system 中，对这种 transfer of power 作过令人难忘的描写；Neal Stephenson 在 ["In the Beginning was the Command Line"](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs81n/command.txt) 中对此作了精彩的呈现；Neil Postman 则在 "Technopoly" 中作过富有洞见的讨论。一个 partial solution 实际上或许就在 ASI _之中_，因为它是一种工具，能够让 technology _更加_ available，并更好地 expressing 任何一个人的意愿。但问题在于，即便只是 _expressing_ technological intent，也需要相当多的 expertise。因此，ASI 是否会 closes 这个 agency over technology 方面的 gap，远不清楚；我的感觉是，它反而会 enlarge 这个 gap。无论如何，我对这种向 technological class 的 transfer of power 极为不安。

2.  **Vulnerable World Hypothesis 是哪一种 statement？**它不是一个 empirically testable statement，至少在通常意义上不是。它也不完全是一个 conventional axiomatic statement。当然，你或许会试图通过 inventing 一种能立即 wipes us out 的 cheap technology，来论证 VWH 在某种意义上是一个 empirical statement。在这个意义上，VWH（实际上是 FWH）是 falsifiable。不过，即便如此，也不能表明它是 near-inevitable。而且，VWH 的重要性恰恰在于它尚未被 empirically tested 的时候。毕竟，它涉及的不只是当前的 technology，或近期未来的 technology，而是所有 possible future technologies——这是一个远比我们有望 explore 的范围更大的 space[22](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn22)。事实上，它涉及的不只是 individual technologies，而是整个 technia[23](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn23)，因为一种 technology 的 destructive power 并不完全是 intrinsic。相反，它取决于与其他 technologies 的 relationship，以及完整的 environment：fire 本身很糟糕，但当 fire-retardant materials、firefighting technology、institutions 和 laws 广泛 available 时，它就变得容易应付得多。在这个意义上，VWH 关乎：在我们作出的 governance[24](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn24) 选择下，哪些 technia 是 accessible，以及我们是否很可能会经历其中 catastrophe 几乎 inevitable 的 technia。不过，虽然 VWH 在通常意义上并非显然 empirically testable，这并不意味着我们不能根据所获得的 knowledge，认为它具有或多或少的 _plausible_ 程度。关于 fire、nuclear weapons 和 pandemics 等事物的 Knowledge，理应让我们停下来想一想。这些事物很可能是人类 _a priori_ 未曾料想到的——在某种意义上，它们是 emergent phenomena，事先 hard-to-predict——这一事实理应让我们对 VWH 非常谨慎。

## 原文

[![](https://michaelnotebook.com/assets/home.png)](https://michaelnotebook.com/index.html)

[Astera Institute](https://astera.org/)

December 16, 2023

> Of course, not all scientists accept the notion that other advanced civilizations exist. A few who have speculated on this subject lately are asking: if extraterrestrial intelligence is abundant, why have we not already seen its manifestations?.. Why have these beings not restructured the entire Galaxy for their convenience?… Why are they not here? The temptation is to deduce that there are at most only a few advanced extraterrestrial civilizations - either because we are one of the first technical civilizations to have emerged, or because it is the fate of all such civilizations to destroy themselves before they are much further along.
> 
> It seems to me that such despair is quite premature. – [Carl Sagan (1978)](https://web.archive.org/web/20060818144558/http://www.bigear.org/vol1no2/sagan.htm)

In 2019, Nick Bostrom published an important paper on ["The Vulnerable World Hypothesis"](https://nickbostrom.com/papers/vulnerable.pdf) in _Global Policy_. Loosely speaking, the Vulnerable World Hypothesis (VWH) is the idea that it may be near-inevitable that intelligent species discover near-ungovernable technologies which cause them to wipe themselves out. This idea has been discussed by many prior authors, but to my knowledge Bostrom's paper is the first in-depth analysis. What follows are my working notes on the paper. The notes are not at all comprehensive – they riff on a few elements of the paper, and briefly sketch some ideas inspired by it. The notes discuss, among other things: (1) The _Friendly World Hypothesis_ as an alternative to the VWH; and how attitudes toward the VWH and FWH shape attitudes toward ASI xrisk; (2) A reframing of the Alignment Problem as the _problem of aligning the values and_ _institutions of a liberal society_ _(including, crucially, the market)_ _with differential technology development_; (3) As a possible solution to the Alignment Problem, I sketch the idea of _provably beneficial surveillance_; and (4) A sketch of a planetary civilization in which the VWH would be true, but would be unsuspected for a long time.

It is tempting to view the VWH mainly through the lens of concerns about risk from Artificial Superintelligence (ASI). This is due in part to the currency of such concerns, and in part due to Bostrom's well-known contributions on ASI risk[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn1). However, I believe the VWH is of both broader and independent interest, and it's a mistake to approach it primarily through the lens of ASI. Rather, one should first engage with the VWH directly, on its own merits, and only secondarily consider the relationship to ASI. That's the philosophy I shall (for the most part) use in these notes.

I've previously done some work related to the VWH, discussing what I call _recipes for ruin_, in my notes on existential risk from ASI[2](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn2), and in a brief sketch of my current interests[3](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn3). By recipes for ruin I mean simple, easily executed, immensely destructive recipes that are near ungovernable, and could end humanity, or at least wreak catastrophic world-changing damage. That work was done largely before reading Bostrom's paper, and did not engage significantly enough with Bostrom's work. These notes partially redress that error. Although there are differences in our respective conceptions, those are not my primary interest here; it's rather exploring the VWH.

Bostrom defines the VWH as follows:

> If technological development continues then a set of capabilities will at some point be attained that make the devastation of civilization extremely likely, unless civilization sufficiently exits the semianarchic default condition.

He further defines the "semi-anarchic default condition" to mean a world with very limited capacities for policing and governance, and many actors with diverse motivations:

> 1.  Limited capacity for preventive policing. States do not have
> 
> sufficiently reliable means of real-time surveillance and interception to make it virtually impossible for any individual or small group within their territory to carry out illegal actions – particularly actions that are very strongly disfavored by > 99 per cent of the population.
> 
> 1.  Limited capacity for global governance. There is no reliable
> 
> mechanism for solving global coordination problems and protecting global commons – particularly in high-stakes situations where vital national security interests are involved.
> 
> 1.  Diverse motivations. There is a wide and recognizably
> 
> human distribution of motives represented by a large population of actors (at both the individual and state level) – in particular, there are many actors motivated, to a substantial degree, by perceived self-interest (e.g. money, power, status, comfort and convenience) and there are some actors (‘the apocalyptic residual’) who would act in ways that destroy civilization even at high cost to themselves.

(Parenthetically, let me say that this is a very interesting definition, since in the first two parts it is defining a type of civilization mostly by the absence of certain qualities. It's also a somewhat problematic definition, since for lifeforms very different from humans categorizations like "governance" or "diverse motivations" might have very different and perhaps rather unclear meanings. With that caveat in mind, let us accept Bostrom's broad framing, and continue.)

It is tempting to dismiss the VWH as idle speculation, or perhaps even as needless catastrophizing. But it's a mistake to dismiss merely because it seems outside our usual experience. One is reminded of Admiral William Leahy, Chief of Staff of the US armed forces at the end of World War II, who said of the atomic bomb: "That is the biggest fool thing we have ever done. The bomb will never go off, and I speak as an expert in explosives." Today, we shake our heads, and may think of Leahy as ignorant. But in fact he was technically knowledgeable: he had taught physics and chemistry at Annapolis, and had extensive experience with the navy's weapons. And the Hiroshima bomb seems _a priori_ implausible: take two inert bodies of material – each small enough to be carried by a single human being – and bring them together extremely rapidly. If you'd never heard of nuclear weapons, would you expect them to explode with city-destroying force? Intuitively, it's absurd. You need to understand a tremendous amount about the world – about relativity, particle physics, and branching processes – before it is plausible. It was that new and rather subtle understanding that revealed a latent possibility for immense destruction, one almost entirely unsuspected just a few decades earlier. Similarly, it is entirely conceivable that the VWH is true for reasons of which we humans are presently ignorant.

## "Easy nukes", and a sketch of an advanced planetary civilization which is an unsuspecting Vulnerable World

As an intuition pump for the VWH, Bostrom explores a thought experiment describing an alternate reality in which there is an easy way to make nukes. Historically, the Manhattan Project cost more than $20 billion (in 2023 dollars). However, it is not at all _a priori_ obvious that such a large effort was required. Perhaps there is some other physical or chemical process, of which we were (and are) merely ignorant, which would enable the easy construction of nuclear weapons. Bostrom explores in some detail the horrendous consequences that would likely have ensued had such a process been discovered in the 1930s or 1940s, concluding with considerable understatement: "We were lucky that making nukes turned out to be hard". I won't recount his comments here. I do want to make three brief observations:

1.  **The energy cost of nukes is surprisingly low:** Certainly, the intrinsic energy cost of a nuclear weapon is much lower than I _a priori_ thought. A 1 kilotonne yield is a little over 4 trillion Joules. For comparison, 1 kilowatt hour of electricity – wholesale price likely in the ballpark of 5 cents – is almost 4 million Joules. If you could efficiently convert electricity to nuclear explosive energy, the cost would thus be on the order of $50,000 per kilotonne. Clearly, the reason for the immense cost of nuclear weapons isn't the energy input. In fact, modern nuclear weapons actually _beat_ this limit. For instance, a Brookings Institute study[4](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn4) estimated the cost of a B83 thermonuclear bomb (yield up to 1.2 megatonnes) at $4.9 million 1996 dollars, including research, development, testing, and evaluation, though excluding operations and post-deployment costs. That's about $8,000 per kilotonne in 2023 dollars. I find this an astonishingly low figure!

2.  **Summoning such technologies by naming them:** America's pre-eminent designer of nuclear weapons was the physicist Ted Taylor. The writer John McPhee wrote a remarkable book profiling Taylor, "The Curve of Binding Energy". An occasional theme of the book is the use of nuclear weapons to devastate targets in the US. Taylor and McPhee travel to the old World Trade Center in Manhattan, pre-911, and discuss the relative ease of building a small bomb to knock it down. Taylor says: "I can’t think in detail about this subject, considering what would happen to people, without getting very upset and not wanting to consider it at all… And there is a level of simplicity that we have not talked about, because it goes over my threshold to do so. A way to make a bomb. It is so simple that I just don't want to describe it. I will tell you this: Just to make a crude bomb with an unpredictable yield—but with a better than even chance of knocking this building down—all that is needed is about a dozen kilos of plutonium-oxide powder, high explosives (I don’t want to say how much), and a few things that anyone could buy in a hardware store." After reading the above interview, a physicist named Peter Zimmerman actually carried out the design[5](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn5). It is fortunate for us that, then and now, no easy way of making plutonium-oxide powder is known, and its distribution is controlled under the nuclear non-proliferation treaty. In folk stories demons can sometimes be summoned merely by saying their True Name; a somewhat similar effect may hold for recipes for ruin, where merely knowing a True Name seems to be almost enough to summon it. Certainly, this seems to have been true with AI safety, where early warnings of the dangers of AI seem to have inspired several very power-oriented people to start what have become the leading AI companies.

3.  **The role of directed design:** It's tempting to make an anthropic argument for why "easy nukes" are not possible: "If it were truly trivial, then they would already have happened, and humanity would not be here". It's interesting to ponder under what conditions this kind of argument is true, and under what conditions it is false. Essentially this argument has been used to justify why destabilizing states of matter like Ice-9 are not possible: if Ice-9 were easily possible, then it would have occurred by chance by now, and we wouldn't be around to ponder the issue[6](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn6). We are, ergo it's not possible. This is a reasonable argument in some respects, but it falls down badly in one respect: the search space of configurations of water molecules is exponentially large, and random exploration only explores it extremely slowly. Even billions of years of Earth's existence is only enough to explore an infinitesimal fraction. But an adversary with a deep understanding of water won't explore the search space at random: they will instead use that understanding to focus on finding the problematic configurations. I certainly don't mean to suggest this is likely for either nuclear weapons or destabilizing states of matter. Rather, I'm making the point that this is generically true: dangerous new weapons don't usually occur by chance, they are rather the result of improved understanding directing a search.

> It is not a primary purpose of this paper to argue that VWH is true. (I regard that as an open question, though it would seem to me unreasonable, given the available evidence, to be at all confident that VWH is false.)

This move is very good: one doesn't need to know the answer to a question for the question itself to have value – in this case, do we live in a Vulnerable World or a Friendly World. I've found it helpful to use the term "Friendly World Hypothesis" to mean a (very rough) converse to the VWH. One possible formulation, rather simple:

> It is extremely unlikely that a set of capabilities will be attained which make it near-inevitable that civilization will be extinguished, even with humanity in the semi-anarchic default state.

In practice it's not clear to me what the _best_ formulation for the FWH is. The simplest possibility is to make it the logical negation of the VWH, but several things go wrong with that. The benefit of such an approach is that you end up with a genuine dichotomy. But that's a minor benefit. It's better instead to pick an alternate position which is of intrinsic interest in and of itself, as I have tried to do above, and then to regard there as being a continuum of positions inbetween. I'll come back to this point below.

Three brief notes on recipes for ruin:

1.  **A sketch of an advanced planetary civilization which is an unsuspecting Vulnerable World:** Bostrom's "easy nukes" are in an alternate reality (as far as we know). But even in our universe it is possible to sketch planetary civilizations where this kind of scenario _is_ possible. Consider, as a single example[7](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn7), a planet with a CO2 atmosphere, and near-ambient magnesium dust grains in the environment – a sort of widespread sand, but where the sands are made of magnesium, rather than silica. On its own, magnesium is reactive and rather unstable, but suppose the grains are covered in a very thin layer of inert magnesium oxide, remnant of an earlier time when the atmosphere still had small amounts of oxygen. Ordinarily, on such a planet you wouldn't see fires – CO2 is a very good fire suppressant. However, if you were able to make a little oxygen and an intense spark you could start a fire that caused the magnesium grains to ignite, even through the thin coating layer of magnesium oxide. Magnesium burns very hot, and the fire could spread to adjacent magnesium sands, with the intensity of the fire causing the ambient CO2 to be broken down to provide further oxygen. If the magnesium sands were widespread enough, the fire would spread widely and rapidly, consuming everything in its path. This would plausibly be a situation where: (a) a recipe for ruin really would be possible; (b) it would seem extremely _a priori_ implausible, without knowledge of the relevant chemistry; and (c) the main barrier to the catastrophic event happening would be improved _understanding_ of that chemistry. In a nutshell: on such a planet, fire would be essentially unknown, and the possibility would seem implausible until a great deal of chemistry was understood. But once understood, such a fire would be trivial to start, and would be far, far more devastating than (known types of) fire on Earth. This kind of scenario ought to give one pause. Are there similar recipes which we on Earth are merely ignorant of?

2.  **The seductive nature of such power:** A skeptic might respond that it's all very well to speculate about "easy nukes" or more general "recipes for ruin". But it's not terribly convincing as mere speculation. And, as a scientist, it's very tempting to _want_ to prove recipes for ruin exist. There is something extraordinarily seductive about these problems. Is there an easy method of antimatter production? Of making nuclear weapons? Of finding a destabilizing state of matter? Of producing a pathogen? It's all very horrifying. And yet, in respect to these and similar questions I all-too-strongly feel – and I suspect many scientists feel – something analogous to what Freeman Dyson said of nuclear weapons: "I felt it myself, the glitter of nuclear weapons. It is irresistible if you come to them as a scientist. To feel it’s there in your hands. To release the energy that fuels the stars. To let it do your bidding. And to perform these miracles, to lift a million tons of rock into the sky, it is something that gives people an illusion of illimitable power, and it is in some ways responsible for all our troubles, I would say, this what you might call ‘technical arrogance’ that overcomes people when they see what they can do with their minds.” Put another way: as a scientist it's an immensely interesting puzzle to prove that this is possible. But we ought to resist.

3.  **The existence of recipes for ruin is _not_ just a property of the laws of physics and our extant tech base:** Early in my thinking I thought the existence or non-existence of recipes for ruin was a property of the laws of physics (albeit, conditioned on the existing technology level of a civilization). But this is too simplistic view. In fact, it depends upon contingent facts about the world. An illustration suffices to make the point: the "laws of biology" are not just determined by physics, but also upon some contingent (but very important, and not arbitrary) accidents in the history of the world. For instance, whether or not some molecule or complex acts as the root cause for a pandemic depends a great deal on the extant environment: among other things, how well existing immune systems can manufacture antibodies. The analogous statement holds much more generally about other classes of risk.

## Aligning a liberal society with differential technology development, safety as a collective action problem, and an AI danger tax

Differential technology development (DTD) is, roughly, the idea of preferring to work on protective technologies over risk-increasing technologies. Here's Bostrom on DTD and the VWH:

> While targeted \[technological\] regress might not be in the cards, we could aim to slow the rate of advancement towards risk-increasing technologies relative to the rate of advancement in protective technologies. This is the idea expressed by the principle of differential technological development. In its original formulation, the principle focuses on existential risk; but we can apply it more broadly to also encompass technologies with ‘merely’ devastational potential:
> 
> _Principle of Differential Technological Development._ Retard the development of dangerous and harmful technologies, especially ones that raise the level of existential risk; and accelerate the development of beneficial technologies, especially those that reduce the existential risks posed by nature or by other technologies (Bostrom, 2002).
> 
> The principle of differential technological development is compatible with plausible forms of technological determinism. For example, even if it were ordained that all technologies that can be developed will be developed, it can still matter when they are developed. The order in which they arrive can make an important difference – \[examples\]…
> 
> Correctly implementing differential technological development is clearly a difficult strategic task (Cf. Collingridge, 1980). Nevertheless, for an actor who cares altruistically about long-term outcomes and who is involved in some inventive enterprise (e.g. as a researcher, funder, entrepreneur, regulator, or legislator) it is worth making the attempt. Some implications, at any rate, seem fairly obvious: for instance, don’t work on laser isotope separation, don’t work on bioweapons, and don’t develop forms of geoengineering that would empower random individuals to unilaterally make drastic alterations to the Earth’s climate. Think twice before accelerating enabling technologies – such as DNA synthesis machines – that would directly facilitate such ominous developments. But boost technologies that are predominantly protective; for instance, ones that enable more efficient monitoring of disease outbreaks or that make it easier to detect covert WMD programs.

There is considerable overlap between Bostrom's emphasis on DTD and [Vitalik Buterin's recent manifesto on d/acc](https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2023/11/27/techno_optimism.html). Buterin emphasizes alignment between the market and DTD, but both Buterin and Bostrom leave unsolved the problem of how to do that alignment. We don't want to leave collective safety to purely altruistic motivations: it's best if it's provided by self-interest as well. Yet many aspects of safety and security are (approximately) public goods or collective action problems[8](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn8), and the market as currently constructed undersupplies them. There are many ways one might address this. I am a fan of ongoing efforts – including those by Buterin – to develop new financial instruments that help address such problems. A classic solution from economics is some form of Pigouvian taxes on any technology with negative externalities. We're beginning to see such negative externalities from AI, for example: misinformation polluting the public commons, racism and other forms of bias, and many more[9](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn9). It would be possible to tax AI companies for such harms[10](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn10), including any harms incurred by open source models released by those companies[11](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn11). The situation is complicated, since those models, both open source and non, may also sometimes have large positive spillovers contributing to the common good. A thorough analysis of this problem seems highly desirable. One significant issue is the difference between immediate harms incurred and future harms. In this the situation has some similarities to a carbon tax; it is interesting to ponder the possibility of some sort of AI danger tax.

## Friendly versus Vulnerable Worlds as a Crux in Discussion of Xrisk: are Recipes for Ruin Possible? And: A More Powerful Form of the Alignment Problem

Depending on whether you think we live in a Vulnerable World or a Friendly World you are likely to come to very different conclusions about the way the world should be governed. For most of my life I've implicitly believed the Friendly World Hypothesis, and explicitly held to the principle that[12](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn12) science is on net good, benefits humanity, and should be pursued aggressively, with constraints only applied locally to obviously negative applications. We may, for example, ban things like leaded gasoline or asbestos, but we certainly don't ban the kind of basic research that forms the background against which discoveries like leaded gasoline and asbestos are made. But I take the Vulnerable World Hypothesis seriously, and it's leading me to doubt the principle of open, aggressive exploration of basic science should be applied forever. And as I begin to doubt the timelessness of that principle, I wonder what it should be replaced with, and when.

Crucially, I'd like any replacement to give us most of the benefits of liberal values and institutions, including the benefits of science and technology, but _also_ without irreversibly exposing us to catastrophic danger. I mentioned above the problem of aligning the market and differential technology development. I suspect this is a more powerful way of expressing the Alignment Problem than in more conventional formulations[13](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn13). Indeed, since "the market" is itself mutable and may be redesigned – it obtains legitimacy only insofar as it genuinely promotes the good – the Alignment Problem is perhaps best phrased as alignment between liberal values and institutions, including the market, and differential technology development. Phrased this way, a solution to the Alignment Problem would automatically entail safe development of programmable biology, ASI, and many other technologies. I struggle with the question of how to do this, but I like that it is a fundamentally optimistic place to stand.

Returning to the Vulnerable-Friendly World dichotomy, I believe this is a fundamental crux underlying many discussions of xrisk, including xrisk associated to both ASI and biosafety. And I don't think most people working on advanced science and technology have seriously grappled with this[14](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn14). Indeed, many people gung ho about technology refuse to engage at all, accepting as "obvious" the not-at-all-obvious incumbent position of a Friendly World. Taking that incumbent position made historic sense as we slowly emerged out of a technologically primitive state in which even medium-scale destruction was extremely difficult. But the thrust of the last 100 years of science and technology ought to make us seriously consider that the Friendly World Hypothesis is simply wrong.

Put another way, I think a fundamental question about technology is: do you believe _recipes for ruin are possible_, that is: do you think inexpensive, easy-to-follow recipes for building catastrophic technologies will one day be found, given sufficient understanding of science and technology? I initially thought people would fall into one of two camps on this question: people who instinctively feel the answer is "yes", and people who instinctively feel the answer is "no". But it turns out there is a third camp: people who really don't want to engage the question. In effect, they're choosing the incumbent position, but without the bother of having to give a good argument for it.

(I've been framing this as a dichotomy between a Friendly World and a Vulnerable World. Of course, as mentioned earlier, it's actually a continuum. Nonetheless, many positions on the governance of technology implicitly and sometimes explicitly take one or the other position.)

## How Intuitions about (ASI) Xrisk and the VWH are Grounded in an Individual's Background Expertise

There is a related challenge in the discussion of ASI xrisk. There are no real "experts on ASI", for the same reason the 18th century had no real experts on automobiles. But experts in diverse fields are trying to use their (different) expertises to understand the impact of AI and ASI. Economists analyze AI and ASI as an economic problem: how will it restructure labour markets? Will it concentrate wealth? Sociologists analyze it as a sociological problem: will it cause unrest? What will it do to families and relationships? Lawyers as a legal problem: how should we think about copyright in the age of generative AI? Who will be culpable for abuses? Biologists as a biological problem: what new biothreats and biomedicines will AI enable? Physicists as a physical problem: are there new types of reaction or phase of matter or material waiting to be discovered, perhaps with explosive consequences? Security experts as a security problem: what new vulnerabilities will ASI create? And so on[15](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn15).

And, of course, the problem of ASI is none of these; or perhaps more accurately, it's all of these things, and much more. It affects our ability to change the universe, which transcends disciplines.

Thus, the type of problem ASI seems to pose depends in considerable part on someone's prior expertise. Even more than the details, different types of expert will bring a different type of intuition about what kinds of phenomena are latent in nature, are possible (or not), and what kinds of defenses may be worked out (or not)[16](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn16). Much dismissiveness about xrisk comes from people without those intuitions: an economist, for example, simply will not have the same intuitions as a security expert, and vice versa. A physicist won't have the intuitions of an economist, and vice versa. Indeed, many people have very little intuition for emergent phenomena[17](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn17) at all. A historically-minded physicist, chemist, or biologist comes to understand through repeated exposure the way very simple rules may give rise to surprising, even shockingly unanticipated phenomena – from fire to superfluidity to nuclear chain reactions to public key cryptography. This breeds a very healthy respect for both the power and the surprise latent in nature. I suspect most people have a much weaker sense of this latent power and surprise. Furthermore, I expect ASI will both discover and create new levels of emergent phenomena, governed by as-yet unsuspected rules; some types of problem will actually be entirely beyond our (present, and perhaps future) comprehension at all, involving as-yet undiscovered areas of expertise. It is also notable that many of the staunchest believers in ASI xrisk are not experts in any relevant science, but instead source their intuition in science fiction. This is a fine thing, but also something to be cautious of, since what is narratively plausible is often not the same as what is true. Nature is simultaneously more constrained and more imaginative than we.

I've been framing this in terms of ASI xrisk, but similar remarks apply to the Vulnerable World Hypothesis. The extent to which one believes (or disbelieves) the VWH depends in considerable part on intuitions acquired from one's prior expertise. This creates many issues, and is obviously not a terribly satisfactory situation, but it is worth noting.

## Responding to the VWH, and Provably Beneficial Surveillance

Suppose you accept the VWH as reasonably likely to be true. How should humanity respond? Bostrom investigates and rejects a number of possible solutions, as not sufficient to the task. He eventually argues that humanity would need much stronger policing and much stronger global governance:

> To the extent, therefore, that we are concerned that VWH may be true, we must consider the remaining two possible ways of achieving stabilization:
> 
> 1.  Create the capacity for extremely effective preventive policing. Develop the intra-state governance capacity needed to prevent, with extremely high reliability, any individual or small group – including ones that cannot be deterred – from carrying out any action that is highly illegal; and
>     
> 2.  Create the capacity for strong global governance. Develop the inter-state governance capacity needed to reliably solve the most serious global commons problems and ensure robust cooperation between states (and other strong organizations) wherever vital security interests are at stake – even where there are very strong incentives to defect from agreements or refuse to sign on in the first place.
>     
> 
> The two governance gaps reflected by (1) and (2), one at the micro-scale, the other at the macro-scale, are two Achilles’ heels of the contemporary world order. So long as they remain unprotected, civilization remains vulnerable to a potential technological black ball that would enable a strike to be directed there. Unless and until such a discovery emerges from the urn, it is easy to overlook how exposed we are.
> 
> …
> 
> What would be required to stabilize such vulnerabilities is an extremely well-developed preventive policing capacity. States would need the ability to monitor their citizens closely enough to allow them to intercept anybody who begins preparing an act of mass destruction.
> 
> The feasibility of such surveillance and interception depend on the specifics of the scenario: How long does it take to deploy the black-ball technology destructively? how observable are the actions involved? can they be distinguished from behavior that we don’t want to prohibit? But it is plausible that a considerable chunk of the Type-1 vulnerability spectrum could be stabilized by a state that deploys currently available technologies to the fullest extent. And expected advances in surveillance technology will greatly expand the achievable protection. For a picture of what a really intensive level of surveillance could look like, consider the following vignette \[about a "High-tech Panopticon" in which everybody is fitted with a 'freedom tag' and monitored by 'freedom officers'\]
> 
> …
> 
> In combination, however, ubiquitous-surveillance-powered preventive policing and effective global governance would be sufficient to stabilize most vulnerabilities, making it safe to continue scientific and technological development even if VWH is true.

This is, as Bostrom says several times, an extreme solution, something best considered if the VWH is highly likely to be true, and the development of some catastrophic technology is imminent. It's certainly not at all clear any benefit is worth the cost! "The good news is you don't need to worry about the world being destroyed; the bad news is you now live under Big Brother". We really ought to be extremely hesitant to accept such a regime, and, indeed, I suspect there are other ways out. (I won't discuss that here, since while it is easy to generate ideas, it's not yet clear to me which is most promising.) But I do want to think in more depth about Bostrom's proposal, and move beyond reflexive dismissal. Most of the trouble is caused by the proposal for ubiquitous surveillance. This has come to seem anathema, for good reason – the horrendous history of the Stasi, the KGB, the purges and the Gulag, and many other institutions derived from surveillance. Orwell's "1984" was a warning, not an instruction manual, and ideas like "freedom officers" and "freedom tags" seem straight out of Orwell.

Despite this reflexive response, I think it's worth engaging imaginatively with the question: is it possible to design a surveillance system which genuinely enhances liberty? Not in some cynical, newspeak 1984 style, but truly? I know some people are so ideologically opposed to this that they will be unable to seriously explore the possibility, even as a hypothetical. To those people I advise skipping ahead to the final section.

To the others: I note that that there are some surprisingly beneficial and unmalevolent surveillance regimes already in existence. I am rather surprised at how relatively little harm Apple, Google, Microsoft and the NSA seem to have done to individuals, given the extraordinary level of information they have about their activities. Apple knows far more about me than the Stasi did about the median citizen, or even most citizens of interest. And while Apple doesn't have life and death power over me, they could legally do some pretty horrible things. And yet for the most part this seems to be extremely rare. I certainly don't mean to say abuses don't happen. But I do mean: if in 2000 you'd told me surveillance capitalism would by 2023 result in orders of magnitude worse abuses than we've seen, I would not have been surprised. I find myself quite curious _why_ there hasn't been a much worse slide.

I most certainly do not propose the NSA or surveillance capitalism as a model to emulate. However, it does suggest two questions: (1) why do they (seemingly) do much less harm than organizations like the Stasi; and (2) is it possible by design to prevent the harms they do, while protecting against the catastrophically damaging actions?

I asked above whether safe differential technology development is possible while aligned with the values and institutions of a liberal society. If we accept the VWH and are considering surveillance seriously, then we might ask whether it is possible to develop _provably beneficial surveillance_? This is in response and counterpoint to calls for _provably safe AI_[18](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn18). While provably safe AI is a stimulating idea, it seems to me to only work if ASIs are created by good actors. If bad actors start producing ASIs, then we're in a lot of trouble.

But suppose instead we replaced that goal by the goal of provably beneficial surveillance. Surveillance is much more naturally a monopoly than is computation; it is also much more naturally preventative. "Beneficial" would, of course, need to be defined, but would include many of the things we take for granted: freedoms and liberties to act of various kinds; the maintenance of many types of privacy; the ability to develop most science and technology. But it would also include the capacity to detect and suppress catastrophic risks. Might it be possible to develop a surveillance regime that simultaneously guaranteed liberties as good or better than we today enjoy, and yet which simultaneously suppresses catastrophic risks[19](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn19)? Put in a nutshell: why not instead shift the focus from developing safe ASI to developing provably beneficial surveillance, with safe ASI as a consequence?

Note that the use of the term "provable" here is not (quite) meant in the sense of a mathematical proof. Indeed, I don't think that's possible, since it refers to the real world, not the perfect world of mathematics. Rather, it's to suggest very strong guarantees – mathematical proofs, when appropriate, and very strong real-world safeguards, when that is[20](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn20).

How to develop provably beneficial surveillance? It would require extensive work beyond the scope of these notes. It is worth noting that most existing surveillance regimes are developed with little external oversight, either in conception, or operationally. They also rarely delegate work to actors with different motives in a decentralized fashion. And they often operate without effective competition. I take these facts to be extremely encouraging: they mean that there is a _lot_ of low-hanging fruit to work with here, obvious levers by which many of the worst abuses of surveillance may be reduced. Classic surveillance regimes have typically prioritized the regime, not humanity at large, and that means the design space here is surprisingly unexplored. It's interesting to consider messaging apps like Signal as instances of what happens when the overseeing authority genuinely attempts to prioritize users. I am likely to return to this in more detail in the future.

## Miscellaneous thoughts

1.  **Techno-capitalism and the transfer of power to the technologist class:** Inherent in almost everything I've discussed is the idea that the solution to technology is still more technology. We may call this technological solutionism. Climate got you down? Do some geoengineering or carbon removal and sequestration. Biorisk blues? Let's have real-time vaccines! Or BSL-4 hardened cites[21](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn21). Nukes still scaring you? Let's do more work on missile shields! Vulnerable World Hypothesis means you can't sleep at night? Let's get to work aligning our liberal values and differential technology development! And so on. This kind of technological solutionism is common among the many tribes of people thinking about the VWH and ASI both, from the startup world to EA to e/acc to doomer and so on. In _all_ these views, there is an inherent transfer of power to people who have agency over technology, and away from people who don't have agency over technology. As [Cory Doctorow has emphasized](https://twitter.com/michael_nielsen/status/1144445213554798593), technology inherently centralizes power in the designers and builders, and the penumbra of humanity around them. This transfer of power was written about memorably by H. G. Wells in his two-tier system of Eloi and Morlocks; Neal Stephenson has rendered it beautifully in ["In the Beginning was the Command Line"](https://web.stanford.edu/class/cs81n/command.txt); and Neil Postman has discussed it insightfully in "Technopoly". A partial solution may actually lie _in_ ASI, insofar as it is a tool making technology _more_ available and better expressing the will of any human being. But the trouble with that is that even _expressing_ technological intent requires considerable expertise. So it is by no means clear ASI closes this gap in agency over technology; my sense is that it will, rather, enlarge it. Regardless, I am extremely uneasy about this transfer of power to the technological class.

2.  **What kind of statement is the Vulnerable World Hypothesis?** It's not an empirically testable statement, not in the usual sense. It's not quite a conventional axiomatic statement either. Now, you might try to argue that there's a sense in which the VWH is an empirical statement, by inventing a cheap technology that immediately wipes us out. In that sense the VWH (actually: the FWH) is falsifiable. Even that wouldn't show that it was near-inevitable however. Moreover, the importance of the VWH is at times at which it has not been empirically tested. It is, after all about not just current technology, or near-future technology, but about all possible future technologies – a space far greater than we can possibly hope to explore[22](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn22). In fact, it's not just individual technologies, but entire technia[23](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn23), since the destructive power of a technology is not purely intrinsic. It instead depends on the relationship to other technologies and the complete environment: fire on its own is bad, but becomes much easier to deal with when fire-retardant materials and firefighting technology and institutions and laws are widely available. In that sense, the VWH is about which technia are accessible, given the governance[24](https://michaelnotebook.com/vwh/index.html#fn24) choices we make, and whether it is likely we will pass through technia in which catastrophe is almost inevitable. But while the VWH is not obviously empirically testable in the usual sense, that doesn't mean we cannot regard it as more or less _plausible_, on the basis of knowledge acquired. Knowledge of things like fire, nuclear weapons, and pandemics ought to give us pause. These things were likely _a priori_ unsuspected by human beings – they are, in some sense, emergent phenomena, hard-to-predict in advance – a fact which ought to make us very cautious about the VWH.
