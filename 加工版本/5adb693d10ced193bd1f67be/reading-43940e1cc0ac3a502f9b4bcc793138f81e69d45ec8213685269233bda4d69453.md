# Second-order vanity

---

## 正文

虽然我通常更喜欢 David Foster Wallace 的 nonfiction，但我读过的他笔下最喜欢的几段文字之一，出自他的第一部 novel，_The Broom of the System:_

> “……一个 second-order vain person 首先是一个 vain person。他对自己的 intelligence 感到 vain，希望别人觉得他很 smart。或者是对自己的 appearance，希望别人觉得他很 attractive。再比如，对自己的 sense of humor，希望别人觉得他 amusing 而且 witty。或者是对自己的 talent，希望别人觉得他很 talented。诸如此类。你知道 vain person 是什么样的。”
> 
> “对。”
> 
> “一个 vain person 在意的是，别人不要把他 perceive 为 stupid、dull 或 ugly，诸如此类，诸如此类。”
> 
> “明白了。”
> 
> “那么，一个 second-order vain person 就是这样一种 vain person：他还会对自己显得 utter lack of vanity 而感到 vain。他极其害怕别人会把他 perceive 为 vain。一个 second-order vain person 会熬夜学笑话，好让自己显得 funny 而且 charming，但他会否认自己熬夜学笑话。或者，他甚至可能试图给人一种印象：他根本不觉得自己 funny。”
> 
> “……”
> 
> “一个 second-order vain person 在公共洗手间洗手时，会忍不住对着镜子 admire 自己、scrutinize 自己，但他这样做时，会假装自己在调整隐形眼镜，或者把什么东西从眼睛里弄出来，好让别人不会把他 perceive 为那种对着镜子 admires 自己的人，而是那种只用镜子处理 reasonable、un-vain 事务的人。”[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/second-order-vanity/#fn:1)

问题在于，如果你一开始是 vain，就不能直接跳到 non-vain。即便你觉得自己的 intentions 是 pure，在某种程度上，你想要变得 selfless 的 desire 也会是 motivated by 这样一个事实：selfless 的人更 likeable。但你_可以_从 first-order 跳到 second-order vanity，而且事实上，这通常相当有效，既能让别人更喜欢你，_也_能让自己去做 The Right Thing™（即一个 selfless 的人会做的事）。但这并不 perfect；你的 vain bias 有时难免会 seep through，而这时，perceptive 的人就会注意到，你只是 selfless to first order。

从这段引文，你能看出 DFW 的 math 背景，因为其中有一个与 Taylor series 的有趣 analogy。[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/second-order-vanity/#fn:2) 先介绍一点背景：一个 function `$f(x)$` 的 Taylor series 是关于 `$x$` 的一个“infinite-degree polynomial”（一个像 `$a_0 + a_1x + a_2x^2 + a_3x^3 + \dots$` 这样的 expression，对所有 `$n$` 一直延续下去），可以用来 approximate `$f$`。通过在 `$x^n$` term 之后 truncating 这个 series，你就 obtain 了 _`$n$`th order Taylor approximation_。随着你 increase 这个 order，这个 approximation 就会越来越接近实际的 function。

类似地，我 hypothesize，一个足够投入、想要不再 vain 的 vain person，可以 achieve third- 甚至 fourth-order vanity；也就是说，不仅 concealing 你的 vanity，还要 concealing 这样一个事实：你所谓的 selflessness 其实是 motivated by vanity。每多一层 concealment，你就能正确处理更多的“corner cases”（即在越来越多的 situations 中 act selfless），但总会有足够 unusual 的 situations 令你困惑，让你的 vanity 显露出来。

倒也不是说这样完全 selfless 的人真的存在，大概并不存在，所以，也许我们只需要努力比旁人更 higher-order？

* * *

2.  我不认为 second-order vanity 实际上是这样得名的。它更有可能是通过与 second-order logic 的 analogy 而创造出来的；也就是说，这种 logic 不仅能对 objects，还能对 logical sentences 作出 statements。那里的 parallel 更 obvious（关于 logic 的 logic；关于 vanity 的 vanity），但我觉得，Taylor series 这个 parallel 更值得玩味。 [↩︎](https://www.benkuhn.net/second-order-vanity/#fnref:2)

## 原文

Although I tend to prefer David Foster Wallace’s nonfiction, one of my favorite things I’ve read by him comes from his first novel, _The Broom of the System:_

> “…a second-order vain person is first of all a vain person. He’s vain about his intelligence, and wants people to think he’s smart. Or his appearance, and wants people to think he’s attractive. Or, say, his sense of humor, and wants people to think he’s amusing and witty. Or his talent, and wants people to think he’s talented. Et cetera. You know what a vain person is.”
> 
> “Right.”
> 
> “A vain person is concerned that people not perceive him as stupid, or dull, or ugly, et cetera et cetera.”
> 
> “Gotcha.”
> 
> “Now a second-order vain person is a vain person who’s also vain about appearing to have an utter lack of vanity. Who’s enormously afraid that other people will perceive him as vain. A second-order vain person will sit up late learning jokes in order to appear funny and charming, but will deny that he sits up late learning jokes. Or he’ll perhaps even try to give the impression that he doesn’t regard himself as funny at all.”
> 
> “…”
> 
> “A second-order vain person will be washing his hands in a public restroom, and will be unable to resist the temptation to admire himself in the mirror, to scrutinize himself, but he’ll pretend he’s fixing a contact lens or getting something out of his eye while he does so, so that people won’t perceive him as the sort of person who admires himself in mirrors, but rather as the sort of person who uses mirrors only to attend to reasonable, un-vain business.”[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/second-order-vanity/#fn:1)

The thing is, if you start off vain you can’t leap straight to being non-vain. Even if you think your intentions are pure, at some level your desire to be selfless is going to be motivated by the fact that selfless people are more likeable. But you _can_ jump from first-order to second-order vanity, and in fact this is usually quite effective in getting people to like you more _and_ getting yourself to do The Right Thing™ (i.e. what a selfless person would do). But it’s not perfect; your vain bias will inevitably seep through sometimes, at which point perceptive people will notice that you’re only selfless to first order.

You can tell DFW’s math background from this quote, because of the fun analogy to Taylor series.[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/second-order-vanity/#fn:2) For background: the Taylor series of a function `$f(x)$` is an “infinite-degree polynomial” in `$x$` (an expression like `$a_0 + a_1x + a_2x^2 + a_3x^3 + \dots$` continued for all `$n$`) that can be used to approximate `$f$`. By truncating the series after the `$x^n$` term you obtain the _`$n$`th order Taylor approximation_. As you increase the order, the approximation becomes closer and closer to the actual function.

Similarly, I hypothesize that a sufficiently dedicated vain person who wants not to be vain can achieve third- or even fourth-order vanity; that is, concealing not only your vanity, but the fact that your supposed selflessness is in fact motivated by vanity. Each additional layer of concealment causes you to get additional “corner cases” right (i.e. act selfless in more and more situations), but there will always be situations unusual enough to confuse you and make your vanity show through.

Not that such completely selfless people really exist, probably, so maybe we should just try to be higher-order than the next guy?
