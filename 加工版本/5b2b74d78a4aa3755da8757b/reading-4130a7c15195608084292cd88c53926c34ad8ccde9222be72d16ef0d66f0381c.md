# O1 visa（O1 签证）

---

## 正文

经过从远方出发、长达数小时的飞行，你抵达了 Bay Area。你觉得 California 是地球上最好的地方，也觉得自己属于这里。随后你才意识到，想留下来并不容易。

我是一名 Spanish citizen，住在 US。很长一段时间里，我一直想来这个国家，却因为这件事看起来太难而感到气馁。不过，我最终发现了一些起初并不明显的 options，于是就有了今天！

以下是我在经历 US immigration system 的过程中学到的一些事情，其他人或许也会觉得有用。这份 guide 是为那些 career 尚处于早期、与更有经验的人相比还没有一套那么明确的 achievements 的人写的。如果一个人已经有数十年的 excellent work，拿到 US visa 的确会更容易；但对于有潜力、却还没有得到那么充分证明的人来说，他们能做些什么来让 USCIS 相信自己确实是 excellent 呢？

## O1 visa（O1 签证）

这是我最了解的 visa，因为我实际持有的就是它。

一开始，拿到 O1 visa 似乎令人望而生畏，因为 [USCIS website](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-visa-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement) _似乎_暗示，你需要有 Nobel Prize 才能拿到它，但事实并非如此。拿到 O1 visa 所需的条件在[这里](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-m-chapter-4)有明确说明。你需要 argue 自己在所在 field 是 excellent。USCIS 如何 define excellent 呢？拥有 Nobel Prize 的确是一个粗略的 proxy（他们称之为 _major internationally recognized award_）。不过，他们也允许你从以下条件中选择至少三项（加粗的是我申请时使用的条件）：

> -   **beneficiary 获得 nationally or internationally recognized prizes 或 awards for excellence in the field of endeavor 的 Documentation；**
>     
> -   beneficiary 在所寻求 classification 的 field 中拥有 associations 的 membership 的 Documentation，这些 associations 要求其 members 具有 outstanding achievements，并由其 disciplines 或 fields 中的 recognized national or international experts 进行 judged；
>     
> -   professional 或 major trade publications 或 major media 中关于 beneficiary 的 Published material，内容与 beneficiary 在所寻求 classification 的 field 中的 work 有关，并且必须包含此类 published material 的 title、date 和 author，以及任何必要的 translation；
>     
> -   **beneficiary 作为 panel 的一员或以 individually 的方式，担任所寻求 classification 的同一或 allied field of specialization 中其他人的 work 的 judge 的 participation 的 Evidence；**
>     
> -   beneficiary 在该 field 中具有 major significance 的 original scientific、scholarly 或 business-related contributions 的 Evidence；
>     
> -   **beneficiary 在该 field、professional journals 或其他 major media 中具有 scholarly articles 的 authorship 的 Evidence；**
>     
> -   **证明 beneficiary 曾以 critical 或 essential capacity 被具有 distinguished reputation 的 organizations 和 establishments 所 employed 的 Evidence；或**
>     
> -   **证明 beneficiary 已经 commanded a high salary，或将会 command a high salary 或其他 remuneration for services 的 Evidence，并以 contracts 或其他 reliable evidence 作为证明**
>     

有些 lawyers 会尝试寻找 evidence 来申请所有条件，因为谁知道呢，也许 USCIS 就会 accept！我针对各项条件所作的论证是：

-   我是一名 Emergent Ventures grantee \[Evidence：来自 Emergent Ventures 的 director Tyler Cowen 的 Letter\]
-   我已经有一些 publications 发表了 \[Evidence：这些 [publications](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=MlF4TgUAAAAJ&hl=en)。不过，我不知道他们是否 accepted 了我 blog 上的那些 :)\]
-   我曾 peer-reviewed papers（2-3 次）\[Evidence：来自 journals 的 Emails\]
-   我是一家名为 Aiden.ai 的 startup 的早期 engineer，并在 setting up 它的 data engineering 和 ML capabilities 方面 played a key role \[Evidence：介绍 Aiden.ai 的 Websites，以及一位 co-founders 写的 letter \]
-   这家 startup 被 [acquired](https://marieouttier.medium.com/aiden-ai-is-now-part-of-twitter-24b2cdb88d61)，这意味着我们的 equity 被 converted to cash。我用这笔钱+我的 salary 来 fulfill "high salary" \[Evidence：Paychecks、equity statements，以及作为 London salaries 的 comparison 的 Glassdoor 的 screencap\]

我的 merits 远远比不上拥有 Nobel Prize，不过也并非 trivial。但愿这个简短的 example 能帮助更多人尝试申请这个 visa。我的 petition 中还有其他内容，我不确定它们是如何被 weighted 的：我曾在 Imperial College（London）做过一些关于 data science 的 talks，而且我用 scholar 找到了不少 citing 我的 work 的 publications。当你 compile 自己的 O1 evidence 时，越多越好；之后 lawyer 可以帮助你选择实际用于 petition 的内容。

最初，我对自己的成功机会很悲观。悲观到我拿自己会失败来打了个赌，算是给自己留点安慰：如果我第一次尝试没能拿到 visa，当时的室友 Harshita Arora 就会请我吃 100 个 tacos（她也是[另一篇](https://harshitaarora.com/2018/10/19/a-guide-on-getting-an-o-1-visa/)很棒的 O1 visa 文章的作者！）；否则，我就给全屋的人买 100 个 tacos。最后，我拿到了 visa，也给全屋的人买了 100 个 tacos。

## USCIS 可能 accept 的其他 merits

对于“judging the work of your peers”这一项，如果你以 software engineer 的身份申请，那么在 hackathon 中担任 judge 可能就符合条件。最近，USCIS 发布了[针对 STEM professions 的 guidance](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/stem-employment-pathways/nonimmigrant-pathways-for-stem-employment-in-the-united-states)，其中包含一些 merits 的 examples，说明哪些情况 qualify 于上面列出的每项 criterion。对我来说，这份更新后的 guidance 最有意思的部分是，你可以用 experts 的 letters 来 qualify for 至少一项 criteria；例如，如果你刚 published 一篇 paper（所以它还没有 citations），一位 professor 可以 testify 它的确是 meritorious work。

## process 是怎样的？

我开始自己的 application 时人在 London。我通过一位 lawyer filed for 这个 visa，最终它获得了 approved（这是 form I-797A）。获得 visa 的 Approval 并不等同于 visa 本身：visa 是放在你 passport 里的东西。为了拿到它，我不得不前往 Brussels，因为 London 的 embassy 因 covid 而关闭。要获得 appointment，需要填写 DS-160 form。

获得 visa 本身需要一次简短的 interview，其目的并不是 change USCIS officer 已经对你的 case 作出的 determination，尽管在极少数 cases 中，他们_可能_这样做。我被问到的问题包括

-   如果你正在 applying for 一个 research position，你有 PhD 吗？\[我有两个 MScs\]
-   你会 supervising 任何人吗？\[不会\]
-   这个 position 的 based 地点在哪里？\[San Francisco\]

我不记得问题的数量了，但大概是 6-7 个，感觉 process 并不很长，问题也完全不出乎意料，只是在 check 你在 petition 中 argued 的内容的 basic facts。[这里](https://www.stilt.com/blog/2019/03/o1-visa-interview/)、[这里](https://legalservicesincorporated.com/immigration/o-1-visa-interview-tips-and-questions/)，还有这里，是另外一些你可能在这次 interview 中被问到的问题列表。

interview 之后，你就有了 O1 visa，可以前往 US。一定要随身带上 I797A notice！我的 lawyer 告诉我要带上 _original copy_，但其他许多人只带 photocopy 也顺利通过了。我觉得他们 technically 可以要求你提供 originals，但在实践中，这可能很少见。对于 US immigration，多带些 documents 总是更好，以防万一。

也要带上你的整份 O1 petition。我在抵达时没有被要求出示整份 petition，但被要求出示 I797A。抵达时的 CBP agents 会问你一些常见问题，并 stamp 你的 passport。是否 allowed 由 agent determine，因此随身携带的 evidence 越多越好。

然后，你可以上网 check 自己是否有一份 [registers](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/I94/#/home) 此次 visit 的 I-94。我的并没有 automatically updated！要拿到它，你需要给抵达的 airport 发邮件或打电话。此类邮件地址的列表在[这里](https://www.cbp.gov/contact/ports/deferred-inspection-sites)。而要 apply for 一个 Social Security Number，[你需要 I-94](https://www.ssa.gov/ssnumber/ss5doc.htm)，之后 get paid 又需要这个号码。

## 持 O1 时，你被 allowed 做什么？

与持 H1B 时一样。你只能为一个 employer work。请注意，USCIS 对 "work" 有自己的 definition。如果你为别人做事并收钱，那就是 work。你 blog 中的 Patreon 可能算 work。为一家 company 进行 Volunteering，如果该 position 通常是 paid，也算 work。所以要当心！你可以通过 capital gains（selling stocks、angel investing）、renting a room、gambling 来 make money，也可以获赠 gift。如果你不因担任该职务而 get paid，也可以加入 nonprofits 的 board。

许多人可能觉得这很受限制！我为 a16z Future 写[这篇](https://future.com/why-california-burns-the-facts-behind-the-flames/)文章时，对方提出给我 payment，我却不得不拒绝。[另一篇](https://www.worksinprogress.co/issue/we-dont-know-how-to-fix-science/)也是如此。

有些人选择的一个 option 是 start 一家 US company（使用 Stripe Atlas 之类的服务 remotely 完成），再由这家 company hired。然后，这家 company 可以向其他 companies offer services，而你作为自己 company 的 employee 来 perform 这些 services。这不会 violate O1 terms，因为你仍由 single entity employed。不过，这条路线有一些 restrictions：company 必须有能力 pay 你的 salary（这可以通过 potential customers 的 letters of intent 或 financial statements 来 shown），而且重要的是，company 必须能够 fire 你，因此你在 company 中的 voting rights 必须 less than 50%。不过，这并不意味着你需要 split 自己的 profits！你可以 incorporate 一个 LLC，并[在上面 slap a board on top](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/publications/blt/2017/03/04_borden/#:%7E:text=In%20contrast%2C%20the%20%E2%80%9Cboard%E2%80%9D,a%20variety%20of%20interesting%20issues.)。这将 a) 使你成为 sole member（并获得所有 profits）b) 让 board 能够 fire 你（并 comply with O1 requirement）。这是 LLC 的 member(s)（即你）不 in control of 这个 org 的少见情况，但它完全 legal。

## 你可以拥有多个 O1 visas 吗？

很长一段时间，我都以为不可以，以为你只能拥有一个 O1，如果想为多个 employers work，就必须通过一家 company。但实际上，你可以拥有多个 O1s！只要你能 credibly show 自己确实在 working 那些 jobs。如果你有一个 60 hour workweek，就可以有一份 40hr job 和一份 20hr job，也可以有各种 advisory 或 consulting jobs。每一份都需要各自的 O1，但原则上是可行的。我的第二次 O1 visa application 与第一次的内容相同。USCIS 最初回复了一份 Request For Evidence (RFE)，要求提供我仍在 working 原来那份 job 的 proof，因此我就（通过我的 lawyer）给他们寄去了此前几个月的 paychecks 的 copies。

如果你获得第二个 visa，就不必离境再去 stamp 一次，它会 automatically 变为 valid。

## 你可以告诉 officers，你想持 O1 留在这个国家吗？

许多 US visas 属于 "non-immigrant intent"，这意味着你必须 prove，自己确实 intend 在 visa expires 后返回 home country。相关 regulation 的具体措辞见[这里](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040213.html)的 FAM 402.31-10 和 FAM 402.13-5

> noncitizen 可以 legitimately 以 O-1 或 O-3 dependent nonimmigrant 的身份来 United States 停留 temporary period，并在其 authorized stay 结束时 depart voluntarily，同时 lawfully seek 成为 United States 的 permanent resident。

所以，如果 officer 问起，你可以说，自己 intend 留在 US，直到 visa expires，但在此期间会尝试 secure 一个 extension 或一张 green card。这些都是 allowed 的。

## 我需要 letter of support 吗？

需要。你可以 submit 的一些 letters 将被用作你所 arguing for 的 criteria 的 direct evidence。有些 criteria 如果没有 letters，就很难 arguing for！如果你曾是某个 organization 的 critical employee，你不一定会获得关于自己的 press，因此你将需要一封来自 manager 或 founder 的 letter。

其他 [letters](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-visa-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement) 将成为 _written advisory opinion from a peer group_ 的一部分：

> Petitioner 必须 provide 一份来自 peer group（包括 labor organizations）或在 beneficiary’s area of ability 具有 expertise 的人的 written advisory opinion。如果 O-1 petition 是为一位在 motion picture 或 television 方面具有 extraordinary achievement 的 individual 提出的，那么 consultation 必须来自适当的 labor union 和一个在 beneficiary’s area of ability 具有 expertise 的 management organization。
> 
> 当 consultation 包含用于 confirm document 的 authenticity 的 watermark 或其他 distinctive marks 时，petitioners 应向 USCIS submit 包含该 watermark 或其他 distinctive marks 的 version。不包含适当 watermark 或其他 distinctive marks 的 documents 的 Copies，可能引起对 document 的 authenticity 的怀疑，并可能导致 processing delays。例如，USCIS 可能 request petitioner submit document 的 original version。为避免 processing delays，petitioners 应确保自己 submit 的是适当的 version，并且任何相关的 watermark 或其他 distinctive marks 都是 legible 的。

USCIS 喜欢看到来自为你撰写 advisory opinion 的 organization 或个人的 watermarks（或 letterhead）。如果你请 professor 撰写，就应使用 university letterhead；如果你请 CEO 撰写，就应使用 company letterhead。

请注意，这份 advisory opinion **并不能 substitute** 满足其中 three 项 criteria 这一要求。这些 letters 就像 petition 这块蛋糕上的樱桃，而不是蛋糕本身。即使你能让 US 的 President 写信称赞你了不起，但如果没有 specific evidence 表明你 meet 至少 three 项 criteria，你也不会获得 visa。

## 需要多长时间？

你可以在[这里](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/)查看 O1 petition（I-129 form）的 approval times。目前需要六个月。但如果你[额外付费使用 premium processing。](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/how-do-i-request-premium-processing)你就可以将其缩短到两周。当然，这是假设你已经准备好了所有 documentation。

## 你可以使用 agent 吗？

持 O1 visas 来 US 的 Artists 可能在一年里有多场 gigs（想想 music band 在 US touring 的情形）。但这并不 limited to artists，你也被[允许](https://jcklaw.com/portfolio/who-can-serve-as-an-o1-visa-agent/)由一位 agent 来 sponsors 你。如果你愿意，这位 agent 可以是你的朋友：

> 如果你希望 designate 一位熟人、朋友或 business associate，为 O-1 petition 担任你的“agent” petitioner，regulations 完全允许这样做，前提是你与你的 employer(s) authorize 此人 represent 并 act on behalf of 你以及你的 multiple employers。选择这条途径时，务必注意，有 specific evidentiary requirements 必须 met。这些要求可能包括 establishing agent-artist relationship 的 documentary evidence，以及 governing 这一 relationship 的 terms and condition，也包括 agent 与 multiple employers 之间的相应内容。不过，并不要求 agent-petitioner demonstrate 自己通常也在 O-1 petition 之外担任 agent。

这条途径能让你更容易以 1099（contracting）positions 与不同的 employers work。你还有一项额外的 requirement：你需要 show 自己已经安排了足够多的 gigs，足以让 petition 获得足够长的期限；如果你只有一份 potential contract，写着对方希望在今年 hire 你一个月，那么 visa 可能只会 granted 到那一年。

## 进一步了解 O1 process

这篇文章发表后，有人提醒我，USCIS 持有的关于你的 internal documentation 属于 Freedom of Information Act（FOIA）的适用范围，因为那是关于你的 information。这意味着你有权要求 USCIS 将那些 information 发给你。其中包括 O1 petition 是如何被 assessed 的 details。目前，要判断 petition 能否通过，唯一的办法是依靠见过足够多 cases 的 lawyers。但如果足够多的 O1 holders 聚在一起，对 USCIS 进行 FOIA'd，他们就可以汇集一份 cases 列表，为今后的 applicants 提供 guidance。

## International Entrepreneur Parole Rule（国际创业者假释规则）

在[理论](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/international-entrepreneur-parole)上，如果你 start a company，你可能被允许（“paroled”）进入 US，以 work on 这家 company。这听起来很好，因为你不必 prove 自己是 excellent。你只需要 show，这家 company 最终能够 create jobs，你 own 其中至少 10%，并且你已经从 top VCs 获得 VC money（_该 start-up entity 已从某些拥有 successful investments 的 established records 的 qualified U.S. investors 那里 received 一笔 significant investment of capital_）。

我不认识任何曾 applied for 这个项目的人，而且每当我与别人聊起 immigration，总有人告诉我，他们听说 IEPR 的 approval rates 很 low，所以连尝试都不愿意。

## research nonprofit 的 exception

如果你的 employer 恰好是 [research nonprofit 或 university](https://www.adhikarilaw.com/work-visa/h-visas/cap-exempt-h1b/)，你应该知道，它是 H1B visa cap exempt 的，因此如果你 qualify for H1B，你就会被 granted visa，而不必参加 H1B lottery！这适用于 research positions。我不知道它是否也适用于其他 roles；例如，research nonprofit 可能 employ product designers 来 create tools，而这些 tools 又会被 researchers used。

## 申请 green card

USCIS 在这里列出了许多种 [green cards](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/permanent-workers)（“EBs”）。对这里的读者而言，最值得关注的是 EB1 和 EB2。它们的 requirements 略有不同：

-   EB1 可以通过三种方式获得：在 multinational 担任 executive、成为 outstanding researcher，以及具有 extraordinary ability。最后一种相当于你申请 O1 时会据以 filed 的那些 criteria，但 USCIS 对它们会更加 strict。
-   EB2 可以通过两种方式获得：申请一份要求具有 [advanced degree](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-f-chapter-5)（如 MSc 或 PhD）或 BSc+5 years of experience 的 job。第二种同样是 meeting O1 criteria，但 reading 更为 lax。对于 EB2，你通常需要经过 labor certification（PERM），proving 你的 role 无法由 US 的任何人 fulfilled。与 EB1 相比，这可能使获得这个 visa 的时间增加数月

去年 10 月，我申请了 EB2-NIW green card。我建议继续找为你办理之前那些 visas 的同一位 lawyer，因为他们已经拥有你的全部 documentation。

关于 green cards，有一种看法认为，你应该先持另一种 visa 在 US 待一段时间，才能申请 green card。这是不对的。你可以在获得 green card 之前从未踏足 US。但这种情况很少见：大多数 green cards 都需要 sponsor，而且获得 green card 需要时间。没有多少 companies 愿意等上一两年（某些 nationalities 的人甚至要等更久）才 employ 你。

大多数 green cards（EBs）都需要 company 为你 sponsor，但如果你申请的是 EB1 或 EB2-NIW，就可以自行申请。这意味着，你甚至可以在从未踏足 US 的情况下申请 green card！

在实践中，如果你想搬到 US，而且 qualify 这些类别中的某一种，你也可能 qualify O1，因此可以 concurrently file O1 和 green card，持 O1 来到 US，然后进行到 EB 的 adjustment of status。当然，先和你的 lawyer 谈谈。

## 如果我已经申请了 green card，还能离开 US 吗？

申请 green card 需要 filing form I-140，并在它 approved 后 filing I-485。在 I-140 处于 pending 期间，理论上离开再返回是可以的。一旦你 file I-485，如果你离开 US，USCIS 就会认为你已经 abandoned 你的 application :((( 你可以通过获得 Advance Parole document 以及 Employment Authorization Document（EAD）来避免这种情况。这两个 documents 一起赋予你留在该国以及在 US work 的 right，相当于你之后获得 green card 时所得到的那些 right。

就我自己而言，自从来到 US 后，我就没有离开过，而且只有在拿到 AP/EAD combo 或 green card 后才会离开。

## 需要多长时间？

US green card process 比 O1 花费的时间更长，但也取决于 visa class。要获得 green card，steps 和[相应所需时间为](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/historic-pt)：

-   File form I-140，并获得 USCIS 的 response（10 months）
-   File form I-485，并获得 USCIS 的 response（10 month）

所以从开始到结束平均大约需要 20 months，但实际可能长得多。例如，只有在有可供你使用的 visas 时，你才被允许 file I-485。当 I-140 approved 后，你会获得一个“priority date”。然后，你需要在 [Visa Bulletin](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin/2022/visa-bulletin-for-september-2022.html)（Employment Based Preferences, Table A）中查看你的 priority date 是否为“current”。对于 EB1、EB2，甚至 EB3，它们都是 current，这意味着 I-140 一经 approved，你就可以立即 file I-485 form。不过，如果你恰好是 Indian，而且 filed EB2，那么如果你在 2014 年申请，就会在现在，也就是 2022 年，拿到你的 green card。这是因为 employment based green cards 按 country 设有 caps。Joe Biden 一直在[尝试](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2021/01/20/fact-sheet-president-biden-sends-immigration-bill-to-congress-as-part-of-his-commitment-to-modernize-our-immigration-system/) remove 这些 country-specific caps，这会让 Indian 和 Chinese nationals 更容易获得 green cards。

你可以同时 file I-140 和 I-485（Concurrent filing），参见[这里](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/concurrent-filing-of-form-i-485)。唯一的问题是，如果 I-140 因某种原因没有通过，你就会损失 I-485 的 \$1,140 fee。

## Employment Authorization Document 与 Advance Parole

当你 file I-485 时，还可以获得 Employment Authorization Document（EAD），它将允许你为任何 employer work（form I-765），这需要 4-7 months。同时，你也可以申请 Advance Parole（form I-131），这需要 7 months）。这将允许你在 green card 到来之前到 US 以外旅行。不过，你并不一定要 file I-131 或 I-765，你可以只是等待，并继续持现有的 O-1 working。你可以把 EAD+AP 看作某种意义上 equivalent to green card 的东西。

如果你确实[获得了 EAD](https://anwarilaw.com/immigration-services/green-cards/adjustments-of-status/employment-authorization-document/ead-pending-485-status/)，而 I-485 因某种原因 denied，那么使用 EAD 就意味着你已经 abandoned 之前的 O1 或 H1B status，因此你将不得不离开该国（!）。如果你能够获得 O1 的 extension，你可能会想这样做，而不是获得 EAD。

## 其他 visas

我认识的其他一些人成功获得过 J1 visa 或 TN visa，但我并不完全了解它们。关于 TN，有一个人的个别经历是，她告诉我，她凭借 1 次 internship 和 2 年的学校学习就获得了 scientific technician TN，因此这也可能比所述 regulations 给人的感觉更容易。

## Lawyers 可以提供帮助

应对 US immigration 可能很复杂；这里有各种各样的 forms，而 rules 也并非完全明确，留下了很多 interpretation 的空间。lawyers 凭借多年与这套 system 打交道的经验，掌握了大量必要的 tacit knowledge，能够理解在 USCIS 看来，什么算或不算 merit，什么算或不算 employment，等等。如果你找到了一位愿意合作的 lawyer，他们通常会提供免费的 consultation call，以 assess 你获得 visa 的 chances，甚至会建议你做一些事情，以确保你有足够的 merits；例如，他们可能建议你主动联系 hackathon organizers，成为 judge。我听说过的每位 lawyer 通常都会预先收取一定费用，而且只有在所申请的 visa 确实 granted 后，才会向你收取全部费用。

## 原文

You have arrived in the Bay Area after a multi-hour long flight from somewhere afar. You conclude California is the best place on Earth and you feel you belong here. Then you realize it's hard to stay.

I am a Spanish citizen and I live in the US. For a long time I had wanted to come to the country and was discouraged by how hard it seemed to be, but eventually I realized there were options that were not obvious at first and now here we are!

Here are some things I have learned by going through the US immigration system that others might find helpful as well. This guide was written with people earlier in their career that have a less clearly defined set of achievements than someone who is more experienced. It’s indeed easier to get a US visa if one has multiple decades of excellent work behind them, but for someone that who's promising but not as proven, what can they do to convince USCIS that they are indeed excellent?

## The O1 visa

This is what I know about the most because it's the visa I actually have.

At first getting an O1 visa seems daunting because the [USCIS website](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-visa-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement) _seems_ to imply you need a Nobel Prize to get it, but this is not true. What you need to get an O1 visa is specified [here](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-2-part-m-chapter-4). You need to argue that you are excellent in your field. How does USCIS define excellent? A rough proxy is indeed having a Nobel Prize (What they call a _major internationally recognized award_). But they also let you pick at least three of these (in bold the ones I applied for):

> -   **Documentation of the beneficiary's receipt of nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field of endeavor;**
>     
> -   Documentation of the beneficiary's membership in associations in the field for which classification is sought, which require outstanding achievements of their members, as judged by recognized national or international experts in their disciplines or fields;
>     
> -   Published material in professional or major trade publications or major media about the beneficiary, relating to the beneficiary's work in the field for which classification is sought, which must include the title, date, and author of such published material, and any necessary translation;
>     
> -   **Evidence of the beneficiary's participation on a panel, or individually, as a judge of the work of others in the same or in an allied field of specialization for which classification is sought;**
>     
> -   Evidence of the beneficiary's original scientific, scholarly, or business-related contributions of major significance in the field;
>     
> -   **Evidence of the beneficiary's authorship of scholarly articles in the field, in professional journals, or other major media;**
>     
> -   **Evidence that the beneficiary has been employed in a critical or essential capacity for organizations and establishments that have a distinguished reputation; or**
>     
> -   **Evidence that the beneficiary has either commanded a high salary or will command a high salary or other remuneration for services, as evidenced by contracts or other reliable evidence**
>     

Some lawyers will try to find evidence to apply for all of them, because who knows, maybe USCIS will accept it! What I argued in each case was:

-   I am an Emergent Ventures grantee \[Evidence: Letter from Emergent Ventures' director Tyler Cowen\]
-   I have some publications out \[Evidence: the [publications](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=MlF4TgUAAAAJ&hl=en). I don't know if they accepted the ones in my blog though :)\]
-   I have peer-reviewed papers (2-3 times) \[Evidence: Emails from the journals\]
-   I was an early engineer at a startup called Aiden.ai and played a key role in setting up its data engineering and ML capabilities \[Evidence: Websites that talk about Aiden.ai, a letter from one of the co-founders \]
-   The startup got [acquired](https://marieouttier.medium.com/aiden-ai-is-now-part-of-twitter-24b2cdb88d61), which meant our equity was converted to cash. I used that+my salary to fulfill "high salary" \[Evidence: Paychecks, equity statements, screencap of Glassdoor as a comparison for London salaries\]

My merits are not anywhere close to having a Nobel Prize, though they are not trivial either. But this brief example hopefully helps more try to apply for the visa. There were other things that were in my petition that I am not sure how they were weighted: I had done some talks at Imperial College (London) on data science, and there were a bunch of publications citing my work that I could find with scholar. When you compile your O1 evidence, the more the better; the lawyer can then help choose what to actually use in the petition.

I was initially pessimistic about my own odds. So pessimistic I made a bet against myself as a hedge of sorts: If I failed to get the visa on my first trial, I would get 100 tacos from my then housemate Harshita Arora (Author of [another](https://harshitaarora.com/2018/10/19/a-guide-on-getting-an-o-1-visa/) great O1 visa post!), otherwise I would buy 100 tacos for the house. In the end I got the visa and l also got the house 100 tacos.

## Other merits USCIS might accept

For the "judging the work of your peers", being a judge at a hackathon could work if you're applying as a software engineer. Recently USCIS issued [guidance for STEM professions](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/stem-employment-pathways/nonimmigrant-pathways-for-stem-employment-in-the-united-states) that includes some examples of merits that qualify under each criterion listed above. To me the most interesting part of that updated guidance is that one can use letters from experts to qualify for at least one of the criteria; for example if you just published a paper (so it doesn't have citations yet), a professor could testify that it is indeed meritorious work.

## What's the process like?

When I started my own application I was in London. I filed for the visa with a lawyer and it eventually got approved (This is form I-797A). Approval to get the visa is not the same as the visa itself: the visa is what goes in your passport. To get that I had to travel to Brussels because the embassy in London was closed because of covid. Getting the appointment is something that's done by filling a DS-160 form.

Getting the visa itself involves a brief interview that's not meant to change the determination the USCIS officer has made about your case, though in rare cases they _could_ do that. I was asked questions like

-   If you're applying for a research position, do you have a PhD? \[I have two MScs\]
-   Will you be supervising anyone \[No\]
-   Where is the position based \[San Francisco\]

I don't remember the number of questions but it was like 6-7, it didn't feel a very long process and the questions were not unexpected at all, just trying to check basic facts of what you argued in the petition. [Here](https://www.stilt.com/blog/2019/03/o1-visa-interview/), [here](https://legalservicesincorporated.com/immigration/o-1-visa-interview-tips-and-questions/), and here are some other lists of questions that you may be asked in this interview.

After the interview you then have the O1 visa and you can travel to the US. Be sure to bring the I797A notice with you! My lawyer told me to bring the _original copy_ but many others have had success with just a photocopy. I think they could technically ask you for originals but in practice this might be rare. With US immigration, the more documents you have, the better just in case.

Bring your entire O1 petition as well. I wasn't asked for the entire petition but I was asked for the I797A on arrival. The CBP agents on arrival will ask you the usual questions and will stamp your passport. It's up to the agent to determine if you're allowed or not, so the more evidence you have with you the better.

You can then go online to check that you have an I-94 that [registers](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov/I94/#/home) your visit. Mine was not automatically updated! To get it you then need to email or call the airport where you arrived. A list of such emails is [here](https://www.cbp.gov/contact/ports/deferred-inspection-sites). In turn [you need the I-94](https://www.ssa.gov/ssnumber/ss5doc.htm) to apply for a Social Security Number, which you'll need to get paid.

## What are you allowed to do on an O1?

The same as on an H1B. You can only work for an employer. Note that USCIS has their own definition of "work". If you do something for someone for money, that's work. A Patreon in your blog could be work. Volunteering for a company, if the position is generally paid, is also work. So watch out! You can make money from capital gains (selling stocks, angel investing), renting a room, gambling, or be given a gift. You can also be on the board of nonprofits if you don't get paid to be there.

This may feel limiting to many! When I wrote [this](https://future.com/why-california-burns-the-facts-behind-the-flames/) piece for a16z Future I was offered a payment which I had to decline. The same is true for [this](https://www.worksinprogress.co/issue/we-dont-know-how-to-fix-science/) other one.

One option some people choose is to start a US company (remotely, using something like Stripe Atlas) and be hired by the company. Then, the company could offer services to other companies which you would then perform as an employee of your company. This would not violate the O1 terms because you remain employed by a single entity. However, there are some restrictions on this route: The company must have the means to pay for your salary (This can be shown with letters of intent from potential customers, or financial statements), and importantly, the company must be able to fire you, so you must have less than 50% of the voting rights in the company. This doesn't mean you need to split your profits though! You can incorporate an LLC and [slap a board on top](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/publications/blt/2017/03/04_borden/#:%7E:text=In%20contrast%2C%20the%20%E2%80%9Cboard%E2%80%9D,a%20variety%20of%20interesting%20issues.). This will a) Make you sole member (and get all the profits) b) Make it possible for the board to fire you (and comply with the O1 requirement). It's a rare case where the member(s) of the LLC (i.e. you) are not in control of the org, but it's perfectly legal.

## Can you have multiple O1 visas?

For a long time I assumed that no, that you can only have one O1 and if you want to work for multiple employers it has to be through a company. But you can, in fact, have multiple O1s! As long as you can credibly show that you're working those jobs. If you have a 60 hour workweek you could have a 40hr job and a 20hr job, or you could have various advisory or consulting jobs. Each would require its own O1 but in principle it's doable. My second O1 visa application consisted of the same as the first one. USCIS replied back at first with a Request For Evidence (RFE) asking for proof that I was still working on my original job, so I just sent them (through my lawyer) copies of paychecks from the months prior.

If you get a second visa, you do not need to go out of the country to stamp it again, it automatically becomes valid.

## Can you tell the officers you want to stay in the country on an O1?

Many US visas are "non-immigrant intent", meaning that you have to prove that you really intend to return to your home country after the visa expires. The specific wording of the relevant regulation is in FAM 402.31-10 [here](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040213.html) and FAM 402.13-5

> The noncitizen may legitimately come to the United States for a temporary period as an O-1 or O-3 dependent nonimmigrant and depart voluntarily at the end of his or her authorized stay and, at the same time, lawfully seek to become a permanent resident of the United States.

So you can say, if an officer asks, that you intend to remain in the US until your visa expires, but that you will try to secure an extension or a green card in the meantime. That's all allowed.

## Do I need letter of support?

Yes. Some letters you can submit will be used as direct evidence for the criteria you are arguing for. Some are hard to argue for without letters! If you were a critical employee at an organization, it's not always the case that you will get press about you, so you will need a letter from a manager or a founder.

Other [letters](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-visa-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement) will be part of the _written advisory opinion from a peer group_:

> The Petitioner must provide a written advisory opinion from a peer group (including labor organizations) or a person with expertise in the beneficiary’s area of ability. If the O-1 petition is for an individual with extraordinary achievement in motion picture or television, the consultation must come from an appropriate labor union and a management organization with expertise in the beneficiary’s area of ability.
> 
> When a consultation includes a watermark or other distinctive marks to confirm the authenticity of the document, petitioners should submit to USCIS the version containing the watermark or other distinctive marks. Copies of documents that do not contain the appropriate watermark or other distinctive marks may raise doubts about the authenticity of the document and may result in processing delays. For example, USCIS may request that the petitioner submit the original version of the document. To avoid processing delays, petitioners should ensure that they submit the appropriate version and that any associated watermark or other distinctive marks are legible.

USCIS likes to see watermarks (or letterhead) from the organization or person that is writing your advisory opinion. If you ask a professor, it should be in university letterhead; if you ask a CEO it should be in company letterhead.

Note that this advisory opinion **does not substitute** for meeting three of the criteria. The letters are like a cherry on top of the petition cake, but not the cake. You could have the President of the US writing that you are amazing but if there is no specific evidence that you meet at least three criteria, you won't get the visa.

## How long does it take?

You can check approval times for the O1 petition (I-129 form) [here](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/). Currently it would be six months. But you can shorten that to two weeks if you pay [extra for premium processing.](https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/how-do-i-request-premium-processing) This is assuming you have all your documentation ready to go of course.

## Can you use an agent?

Artists coming to the US on O1 visas might have multiple gigs over a year (think a music band touring the US). But this is not limited to artists, you are [allowed](https://jcklaw.com/portfolio/who-can-serve-as-an-o1-visa-agent/) to have an agent that sponsors you. This agent could be a friend of yours if so you wish:

> if you wish to designate an acquaintance, friend or business associate to serve as your “agent” petitioner for the purpose of the O-1 petition, it is perfectly allowed by the regulations, provided that you and your employer(s) authorize this individual to represent and act on behalf of you and your multiple employers. In choosing this route, it is important to note that there are specific evidentiary requirements that must be met. These may include documentary evidence establishing the agent-artist relationship along with the terms and condition governing the relationship, as well as between the agent and multiple employers. However, it is not required that the agent-petitioner demonstrate that it normally serves as an agent outside the context of the O-1 petition.

This route would let you more easily work with different employers on 1099 (contracting) positions. An additional requirement you have is that you need to show that you have enough gigs lined up that you should get the petition for long enough; if you just have a single potential contract saying that they want to hire you for a month this year, the visa may be granted just for that year.

## Learning more about the O1 process

After the publication of this post, it was brought to my attention that the internal documentation USCIS has about you falls under the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) because it's information about you. That means you have the right to have USCIS send you that information. This includes details on how the O1 petition was assessed. Currently, the only way to get a sense of whether the petition will go through is relying on lawyers who have seen enough cases. But if enough O1 holders got together and FOIA'd USCIS, they could assemble a list of cases that could then serve as guidance for future applicants.

## The International Entrepreneur Parole Rule

In [theory](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/international-entrepreneur-parole), if you start a company you may be allowed ("paroled") into the US to work on the company. It sounds great because you don't have to prove that you are excellent. You only need to show that the company can eventually create jobs, that you own at least 10% of it, and that you have received VC money from top VCs (_The start-up entity has received a significant investment of capital from certain qualified U.S. investors with established records of successful investments_).

I don't know anyone that has applied for this and whenever I talk about immigration with others some tell me that they have heard approval rates for the IEPR are low so they don't even try.

## The research nonprofit exception

If your employer happens to be a [research nonprofit or a university](https://www.adhikarilaw.com/work-visa/h-visas/cap-exempt-h1b/), you should know it is H1B visa cap exempt, so if you qualify for an H1B you will be granted the visa without going into the H1B lottery! This works for research positions. I don’t know if it also works for other roles; eg. a research nonprofit might employ product designers to create tools that in turn are used by researchers.

## Applying for a green card

There are many kinds of [green cards](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/permanent-workers) ("EBs") that USCIS lists here. For the audience here the most interesting ones are the EB1 and the EB2. The requirements are slightly different:

-   EB1 can be achieved in three ways: Being an executive at a multinational, being an outstanding researcher, and having extraordinary ability. This latter one amounts to the same criteria you would have filed for in the O1, but USCIS will be more strict about them.
-   EB2 can be achieved in two ways: Applying a job that requires an [advanced degree](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual/volume-6-part-f-chapter-5) (Like MSc or PhD) or a BSc+5 years of experience. The second one is once again meeting the O1 criteria but with a more lax reading. With EB2 you normally need to go through labor certification (PERM), proving that your role cannot be fulfilled by anyone in the US. This can add months to this visa compared to the EB1

Last October I applied for an EB2-NIW green card. I recommend going with the same lawyer you used for your previous visas because they will already have all your documentation.

With green cards, there is a belief that you're supposed to have stayed in the US for some time under a different visa before applying for a green card. This is false. You can get a green card without ever setting foot in the US until you get it. But this is rare: Most green cards require a sponsor and getting a green card takes time. Not many companies are willing to wait a year or two (or more for some nationalities) to employ you.

Most green cards (EBs) require a company to sponsor you, but if you apply for an EB1, or EB2-NIW you can just apply yourself. This means you could apply for a green card without ever having setting foot in the US!

In practice, if you want to move to the US and you also qualify for one of these you may also qualify for an O1 so you could concurrently file for O1 and the green card, come to the US on the O1, and then do an adjustment of status to the EB. Of course talk to your lawyer first.

## Can I leave the US if I have applied for a green card?

Applying for a green card involves filing the form I-140 and once that's approved, filing the I-485. While the I-140 is pending it's fine in theory to leave and return. Once you file the I-485, if you leave the US USCIS will consider that you have abandoned your application :((( You can avoid this by getting an Advance Parole document as well as an Employment Authorization Document (EAD). These two documents together give you the right to stay in the country, and to work in the US, which equals what you get with the green card that will come later.

In my own case, I haven't left the US since I arrived here, and will only leave with an AP/EAD combo or a green card in hand.

## How long does it take?

The US green card process takes longer than the O1, but it also depends on the visa class. To get the green card, the steps and [associated times are](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/historic-pt):

-   File form I-140 and get a response from USCIS (10 months)
-   File form I-485 and get a response from USCIS (10 month)

So it's something like 20 months beginning to end on average, but it could take substantially longer. For example you are only allowed to file the I-485 if there are visas for you. When the I-140 gets approved you get a "priority date". Then you need to check in the [Visa Bulletin](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/legal/visa-law0/visa-bulletin/2022/visa-bulletin-for-september-2022.html) (Employment Based Preferences, Table A) if your priority date is "current". Foe EB1, EB2, and even EB3 they all are current, meaning that once the I-140 is approved you can immediately file the I-485 form. However, if you happen to be Indian and you have filed an EB2, you would be getting your green card now in 2022 if you applied in 2014. This is because the employment based green cards are capped by country. Joe Biden has [been trying](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2021/01/20/fact-sheet-president-biden-sends-immigration-bill-to-congress-as-part-of-his-commitment-to-modernize-our-immigration-system/) to remove these country-specific caps which would make it easier for Indian and Chinese nationals to get green cards.

You can file for I-140 and I-485 at the same time (Concurrent filing), see [here](https://www.uscis.gov/green-card/green-card-processes-and-procedures/concurrent-filing-of-form-i-485). The only issue is that if for some reason the I-140 doesn't go through then you would lose the $1,140 fee of the I-485.

## Employment Authorization Document and Advance Parole

When you file the I-485, another thing you can do is get an Employment Authorization Document (EAD) which will let you work for any employer (form I-765) which will take 4-7 months. In parallel you can also apply for a Advance Parole (form I-131) which will take 7 months). This will let you travel outside of the US until the green card arrives. You don't have to file the I-131 or I-765 though, you can just wait and continue working on your existing O-1. You can think of EAD+AP as equivalent to a green card of sorts.

If you do [get an EAD](https://anwarilaw.com/immigration-services/green-cards/adjustments-of-status/employment-authorization-document/ead-pending-485-status/) and for some reason the I-485 gets denied then by using the EAD you will have abandoned your prior O1 or H1B status, so you will have to leave the country(!). If you can secure an extension of the O1 you may want to do that instead of getting the EAD.

## Other visas

Some other people I know have had success with a J1 visa, or a TN visa, but I don't know everything about those. For the TN, anecdotally, one person told me she got a scientific technician TN with 1 internship and 2 years of school so this may also be easier than the stated regulations make it seem.

## Lawyers can help

Navigating US immigration can be complicated; there are all these forms and the rules are not perfectly clear, they leave a lot open to interpretation, and lawyers by virtue of having worked with the system for many years have a lot of that tacit knowledge required to understand what counts or doesn’t as a merit in the eyes of USCIS, what counts or doesn’t count as employment, and so forth. If you have found a lawyer you’d want to work with, usually they will offer a free consultation call to assess your chances of getting a visa, and can even suggest things to do to make sure you have enough merits; they may suggest that you proactively reach out to hackathon organizers to become a judge for example. Every lawyer I’ve heard of will usually charge some amount upfront, and only fully charge you if the visa requested is actually granted.
