# Nintil - On writing

---

## 正文

无论在 writing 还是 speech 中，同一件事都有许多种说法。这里我想试着写下一些我认为属于 good practises 的例子。请注意，我不是以英语为母语的写作者，但这里的建议原则上对任何 language 都适用。

1.  让文字 make sense 的责任，就 implicit 于 writing 这一行为本身。这就是你应该在意它的原因。你 write 时，除了 taking notes 的情况，都是写给别人 read 的。为了成功做到这一点，你需要通过 words，把脑中的 complex concepts 传入他们的脑中。因此，要做到这一点，你就必须仔细 pick 你的 words。如果你不为此付出努力，你就是在 contradicting yourself：你试图用糟糕的手段做成一件事。Writing 出你自己能理解的东西很容易。为别人 writing 就不是这样了。不要做 linguistic solipsist，也不要做 postmodern。
2.  使用 commas。commas 主要有两种用法。一种，例如，就是这一种。这里我在 commas 之间 inserted 了两个 words。这样做是为了给 main phrase 添加内容，同时不让读者难以理解。如果做得正确，你可以 suppress 那些 inserted words，而 phrase 仍然 make sense（“一种就是这一种”）。不要像我刚才这样，用很长的 inserted sentences 来做这种 operation，而这里所说的 operation，是指在 phrase 中 addition 一些 words，目的是 clarifying 某件事或为它 adding meaning，同时又不打扰读者。你会忘记 initial comma 前面说了什么，整段话便成了一根难以下咽的语言香肠。commas 的另一种用途，是让读者喘口气，就像我这里做的这样。一个能帮助你 properly place 它们的有用 heuristic，是想象自己正在大声 read 你的 text，并想想你会在哪里 introduce pauses。
3.  Parenthesis 和 brackets 可能很有用（当 parenthesis 内的 inserted words 改用 commas 来 substitute 会显得不顺时，用来 clarify 某个 concept），也可能具有破坏性（如果它们过长，尽管它们可以比 comma-sandwiched phrases 更长 \[嗯……三明治……\]）。Brackets 可以在 parenthetical insertions 本身内部 introduce 类似 parentheses 的 insertions，通过 changing enclosing signs 来让内容更容易 read。你也可以使用更多 parentheses，但这种方式无疑更清楚。另外请注意，使用 parentheses 时，你可以在其中放入包含 commas 的 whole sentences，而用 comma-sandwiching 就较难做到这一点。不管怎样，使用 two levels of parenthesising 的人并不多。
4.  不要做突然的 thematic changes。让一个 theme 自然过渡到另一个。如果你想讨论，比如 economics 和 philosophy，而某个时候你正在谈 Solow growth function，就不要在下一 paragraph 开头直接 presenting Friedman 对 methodology 的看法。这种情况下，你应该有一个（或多个）transition paragraphs，在其中提示接下来要说什么。而且务必避免在 mid-paragraph 这样做。Pinker 有一本名为 Sense of Style 的书，在其中谈到这些事情中的一些。他还有一些关于 mind、language、evolutionary psychology 以及 evolution of violence 的书。哦，他还有一头酷极了的头发。我刚才这样写，就是你不应该做的一个例子。即使我完全没有提到 Pinker 的书，那一句开头仍然显得突如其来，而且它还给我们带来了第五个问题：
5.  留意 references。当你使用像“这些”“正如我们之前所说”这样的 words 时，要记住，你自己完全清楚你指的是什么。你的读者却未必清楚。上一条建议中有一句“这些事情中的一些”。它既可能 refer to 同一 paragraph 中谈到的内容（thematic changes），也可能泛指此前的全部内容。在第二种情况下，它会更显突兀，因为你大概会认为，Pinker 谈到了本文这份 enumeration 中的大多数事情，所以，如果“这些事情”指整篇文章，这种说法就应该出现在最后。如果你不确定是否该使用 reference word，就直接使用那个 word 本身。比较一下：“当你使用这些时，你就可以讲故事”和“当你使用 words 时，你就可以讲故事”。
6.  关于 Oxford comma：要用它！“我去看了我的父母、一头牛和一匹马”和“我去看了我的父母、一头牛、和一匹马”，意思并不一样。在第一句中，comma 会与 explanatory type of comma 混淆，因此读者可能会困惑（哇，你的父母怎么会是一头牛和一匹马？？）。第二句就清楚得多。使用这个 comma 很容易：当你写到 enumeration 中最后一个“and”时，在“and”前面的那个 word 后使用一个 comma。
7.  关于 sentence length，只要尽量避免单调重复就好。不要一直使用 short sentences，也不要 constructing 长得荒唐的香肠。Shorter sentences 也可以用来 explain 事情，有时还能产生震撼效果。假设有人提出一个故作高深的 claim。你接着写道：“这个人说了如此这般，又说了这样那样，并且有这些 premises。然而，其中大部分都是 wrong。”这里，你从友善而稍长的 explanation，转为简短而直截了当的 rejection，用 short sentence punchline 否定前面呈现的内容。
8.  关于 playfulness，你可以通过 play with words 给你的 text 增添一些趣味。你可以使用 creative metaphors 或 comparisons 来做到这一点（Institutions 就像 Soylent Green。两者都是由人做成的。\[请注意，这里也运用了第 7 条建议\]）。
9.  关于 argumentation，你在 writing 时，必须在脑中清楚自己想说什么；或者至少希望在公开发表时能想清楚，因为 writing 本身也可能带来额外的 insight。你的 arguments 应该能够写成 syllogistic form，清楚 stating 你依赖哪些 premises，以及什么 follows from 什么。请注意，对于所有值得探讨的用途，你都无法找到 totally general premises，因此，你以 syllogistic form 写出的 arguments，要么 scope 比较有限，要么会出现许多“……是 plausible 的”“Given X，infer that Y 是 reasonable 的”等说法。确保你的 premises 足够 strong，使这类 deductions（实际上是额外的 premises）是 valid 的。此外，能够 identify premises 及它们之间的 links，有助于你发现一个 argument 可能哪里出了问题，而这又有助于思考 counterexamples。
10.  当你在 arguing 某件事时，要考虑可能有哪些 counterarguments。外面大概有聪明人，已经用 arguments 来 oppose 你所 arguing for 的东西。读读他们的文章，然后要么改变自己的想法，要么弄清他们为什么 wrong，并告诉你的读者原因。尽量做到 charitable，并以 strongest form 来 present 你的 counterarguments（这有时叫作 steelmaning，与 strawmaning 相对）。
11.  与你正在写的东西保持距离。不要让你的 arguments 变成你自己。不要让那些 defend 你所 argue against 的 arguments 的人成为你的敌人。按照我建议的方式 writing，每个人都是 truth-seeking enterprise 中友善的伙伴。你会遇到 trolls、bullshitters，以及各种蠢人等等。你应该忽略他们，并怜悯他们，因为他们愚蠢，也不了解自己的处境。不要憎恨他们，也不要生气。这样做的一个好处是，你能读完一篇总体上愚蠢的 argument，而不会刚看到 nonsense 就停下来，并且能够在 bullshit 之下找到一点 truth。又或许，你还能理解写下那些话的人，明白是什么驱使他们那样 argue。记住，当你强烈地 argue for or against 某件事时，那种 affirmation 或 negation 就会成为你的 self-image 的一部分，将来要摆脱它可能会更难，因此才需要与 arguments 保持 detachment。要做到这一点，就回想一下你过去可能曾 defend 过的那些 wrong 而愚蠢的东西。你仍然可以认为某人是白痴，但 writing 时要避免这样。把注意力放在 arguments 上，而不是人身上。记住，如果 writing 时开始产生什么情绪，你可以停下来，过一会儿再回来。
12.  要 Cite，也就是注明来源。你的 ideas 并不全是坐在扶手椅里思考得来的，你也从别人那里获得它们。要 Acknowledge 这一点，尤其是当你准备 paraphrase 或 quote 他们时。也尽量附上你所 citing 的 work 的 links。
13.  要 efficient。如果能用更少的 words 说清一件事，就这样做。人们的时间有限，要感谢他们拿出一部分时间来 read 你的文字，并让他们读得更轻松。也许你能写出 lengthy 而 enjoyable 的 essays，但这时你追求的还包括 crafting 一篇好读物的纯粹乐趣，而不只是 finding out 某件事的 truth。弄清楚你的每篇 writings 各自适合什么 length。
14.  在必要的地方使用——但不要滥用——charts、graphics 和 images。有时，一幅 image 确实胜过千言万语。Visual information 有助于让 point 更清楚。说“air 的 specific heat 与 temperature（above room temperature）之间存在 positive relationship，但这种 relationship 会随 pressure 而 changes”，与提供[这幅 graph](http://www.roymech.co.uk/Related/Thermos/Thermos_Specific_heat.html) 的链接并不一样；后者能 clarifies 这种 relationship、它如何 varies，以及很重要的一点：它的 magnitude。许多有趣的 relationships 都是 nonlinear 的，而没有 charts 就很难 explain 它们。即使对于 linear relations，chart 也能 conveys measurement dispersion，从而说明这种 relation 的 robustness。
15.  用 paragraphs 分隔你的 points。每个 paragraph 应该 focus 于一两个 main ideas。不要为了 break paragraphs 而 break paragraphs。这可能有些难，因为另一条建议是避免过长或过短的 paragraphs。
16.  关于 Enumeration 和 bullet points：如果你在 listing 一些东西，而且 order 并不重要，就使用 bullet points。如果 order 确实重要（因为你在 presenting 一个 argument，或者像我在这份 enumeration 中那样 cross-referencing points），那就使用 enumerations。
17.  text 本身的 composition 应该有所变化：交替使用 simple 与 complex sentences，并交替使用它们彼此 linked 的方式。
18.  要 Read，也就是阅读。这会帮助你看出什么有效、什么无效，因为你能看出哪些 forms 更 readable、哪些表达听起来有趣，等等。

暂时就这些。如果你还有更有用的建议，请给我留言。

## 原文

There are many ways of saying the same thing, both in writing and in speech. Here I will try to type down some examples of what I think are good practises. Note that I am not a native English writer, but the tips here are valid, in principle, for any language.

1.  A duty to make sense is implicit in the act of writing itself. This is why you should care about it. When you write, except when you are taking notes, you write for others to read. In order for you to be successful, you need to put complex concepts you have in mind into their heads via words. In turn, to do that you must pick your words carefully. If you don't put effort into it, you are contradicting yourself: you are trying to do something using poor means. Writing something you'd understand is trivial. Not so when writing for others. Don't be a linguistic solipsist, or a postmodern.
2.  Use commas. You can use commas in two main ways. One is, for example, this one. Here I inserted two words inside the commas. This is done to add something to the main phrase without making it difficult for the reader to understand it. If you are doing it right, you can suppress the inserted words, and the phrase still makes sense (One is this one). Avoid doing this operation, and by operation here we mean an addition of some words into a phrase with the objective of clarifying something or adding meaning to it in such a way as not to bother the reader, using long inserted sentences like I just did. You forget what came before the initial comma and this becomes a hard to digest linguistic sausage. The other use of commas is to give some breathing to the reader, like I am doing here. A useful heuristic to properly place them is to imagine yourself reading your text aloud, and figuring out where would you introduce pauses.
3.  Parenthesis and brackets can be useful (to clarify some concept when the inserted words inside the parenthesis don't sound nice to substitute using commas) or destructive (If they are overly long, although they can be longer than comma-sandwiched phrases \[Hmmm.... sandwiches...\]). Brackets can be used to introduce parentheses-like insertions inside parenthetical insertions themselves, changing the enclosing signs to make it easier to read. You can also use more parentheses, but this way is undoubtedly clearer. Also note that with parentheses you can include whole sentences with commas inside them, something that is harder to do with comma-sandwiching. Not many people use two levels of parenthesising, anyways.
4.  Don't do sudden thematic changes. Let one theme flow into the other. If you want to discuss, say, economics and philosophy and at some point you are talking about the Solow growth function, don't just begin in the next paragraph by presenting Friedman's view on methodology. In that case, you should have a (or many) transition paragraphs in which you hint what you will say in what follows. And by all means avoid doing this mid-paragraph. Pinker has a book called the Sense of Style in which he talks about some of these things. He also has some other books related to mind, language, evolutionary psychology, and the evolution of violence. Oh, and he also has a totally cool hair. What I just did here is an example of something you should not do. Even if I didn't mention anything about Pinker's books, the first sentence still feels like it appeared out of nowhere, and it also presents us with problem five:
5.  Keep track of references. When you use words like 'these','as we said before',' keep in mind that you perfectly know what you mean. Your readers may not. In the previous tip there is a 'some of these things'. That can refer either to what was commented in the same paragraph (thematic changes), or to what has come before in general. In this second case, it adds to the weirdness, because you'd guess that Pinker talks about most things in the enumeration that is this post, so saying 'these things' referring to all this post should come right at the end. If you are not sure whether to use a reference word or not, use the word itself. 'When you use these, you can tell stories' vs 'When you use words, you can tell stories'.
6.  The Oxford comma. USE IT!. It's not the same to say "I went to see my parents, a cow and a horse" than "I went to see my parents, a cow, and a horse". In the first sentence, the comma gets confused with the explanatory type of comma, and so the reader may get confused (Woah, how can your parents be a cow and a horse??). In the second sentence, this is much clearer. Using the comma is easy: when you write the final 'and' in an enumeration, use a comma in the word that goes before the 'and'.
7.  On sentence length, just try to avoid being repetitive. Avoid using short sentences all the time, and avoid constructing absurdly long sausages. Shorter sentences may also be used to explain things, sometimes in a shocking way. Suppose someone makes a highfalutin claim. You then write 'This person said such and such, and this and that, and has these premises. However, most of that is wrong.' Here you go from long-ish explanation of something in a friendly way to a short-in-your-face rejection of what was presented with the short sentence punchline.
8.  On playfulness, you can play with words to add some sense of fun to your text. You can do this with creative metaphors or comparisons (Institutions are like Soylent Green. Both are made of people. \[Note that tip 7 was also applied here\]).
9.  On argumentation, you must have what you want to say clear in your head when you write it, or hope it will be by the time it you make it public, as extra insight can occur during writing itself. Your arguments should be writeable in syllogistic form, stating clearly what premises you are relying on, and what follows from what. Note that for all interesting purposes, you won't be able to find totally general premises, and so your arguments in syllogistic form either will be modest in scope, or they will present lots of 'It is plausible that', 'Given X, it is reasonable to infer that Y' and so on. Make sure your premises are strong enough for that kind of deductions (actually, extra premises) are valid. Also, being able to identify premises and links between them help you find out what could be wrong with an argument, and this helps for thinking of counterexamples.
10.  When you are arguing something, consider what the counterarguments can be. Probably there are smart people out there who have used arguments to oppose what you are arguing for. Read them and either change your mind, or find out why they are wrong and tell your readers why. Try to be charitable and present your counterarguments in their strongest form (this is sometimes called steelmaning as opposed to strawmaning).
11.  Distance yourself from what you are writing. Don't let your arguments become yourself. Don't let the people who defend arguments you argue against become your enemies. When you write in my recommended way, everyone is a friendly partner in a truth-seeking enterprise. You will encounter trolls, bullshitters, stupid people in general and so on. You should ignore and pity them, for they are stupid and don't understand their predicament. Don't hate them or get angry. One benefit of this is that you will be able to read through a generally idiotic argument without stopping at the first sight of nonsense, and be able to find some nugget of truth under the bullshit. Or perhaps, you will also be able to understand the person who wrote that, and see what drove them to argue that way. Remember that when you argue forcefully for or against something, that affirmation or negation will become part of your self-image, and getting rid of that something in the future may be harder, hence the detachment from the arguments. To achieve this, reflect upon all the wrong and stupid things you probably used to defend in the past. You can still think someone is an idiot, but avoid it when you are writing. Focus on arguments, not people. Remember that if you start feeling anything when writing, you can stop, and come back later.
12.  Cite. You don't get all your ideas just by thinking in the armchair, you pick them from others. Acknowledge it, especially if you are going to paraphrase or quote them. Try also to include links to the work you are citing.
13.  Be efficient. If you can say something with fewer words, do it. People have limited amounts of time, be grateful they devote some to reading you and make it easier for them. Maybe you can write lengthy and enjoyable essays, but then you are also pursuing the pure pleasure of crafting a nice read, not just finding out the truth about something. Figure out what's the appropiate length for each of your writings.
14.  Use -but don't abuse- charts, graphics and images wherever necessary. Sometimes it is the case that an image is worth a thousand words. Visual information helps to make a point clearer. It's not the same to say 'there is a positive relationship between the specific heat of air and temperature (above room temperature), but that the relationship changes with pressure' than linking to [this graph](http://www.roymech.co.uk/Related/Thermos/Thermos_Specific_heat.html), which clarifies the relationship, how it varies, and importantly, the magnitude of it. Many interesting relationships are nonlinear, and these are hard to explain without charts. And even in linear relations, a chart conveys also the measurement dispersion, so as to give an account of the robustness of the relation.
15.  Separate your points in paragraphs. Each paragraph should focus in one or two main ideas. Don't break paragraphs for the sake of breaking them. This may be hard because another tip is to avoid excessively long or excessively short paragraphs.
16.  Enumeration and bullet points: If you are listing stuff, and the order does not mater, use bullet points. If the order does matter (Because you are presenting an argument, or you are cross-referencing points like I've done in this enumeration) then go with enumerations.
17.  The composition of the text itsef should be varied: alternate simple with complex sentences, and alternate they ways they are linked to each other.
18.  Read. This will help you see what works and what doesn't, as you'll be able to see what forms are more readable, what sounds fun, etc.

That's it for now. If you have more useful tips, please leave me a comment.
