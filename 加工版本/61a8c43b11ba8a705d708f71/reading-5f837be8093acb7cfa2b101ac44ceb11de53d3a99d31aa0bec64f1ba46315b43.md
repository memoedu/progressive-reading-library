## 正文

\[属于 [Soviet Union series](https://nintil.com/2016/05/14/the-soviet-union-series/) 的一篇\]

Soviet labour 'market' 是一个特殊的 labour 'market'。与我们习惯的 unemployment 普遍存在的情形不同，Soviet Union 不仅 achieved full employment，还出现了\_shortages of labour，\_尽管 population 中已有相当大的 share 在 working。在本文中，我将澄清 full employment 在 Soviet context 中意味着什么，并解释其 labour 'market' 的一些方面，这些方面并未在[我此前关于这一主题的文章](https://nintil.com/2016/04/03/the-soviet-union-working-hours/)中涉及。与往常一样，本文讨论的是 post-Stalin era。\[1\]

> official Soviet ideology 主张，socialism 不仅与 capitalism 完全不同，而且在所有方面（即 socially、economically、politically、culturally 和 morally）都 superior 于它。为了 substantiatе 这一 claim，这套 ideology 列举了一长串 achievements，其中之一便是：虽然 unemployment 是 capitalism 的 endemic feature，socialism 却会彻底且一劳永逸地 abolishes 它。
> 
> 如果 'unemployment' 这个 term 仅仅 denotes registered kind 的 open unemployment 或 dole，那么这一 assertion 就是完全 justified 的，因为在 Soviet Union，unemployment benefits 的 payment 早在 October 1930 就已停止。除此之外，多年来 Soviet regime 已成功 mobilizing 绝大多数 working age 的 able-bodied men and women，使他们 participation 于 social economy。\*\*\* (Porket, 1989)

本文主要参考 János Kornai 的 The Socialist System  (1992) 和 J.L. Porket 的 Work, Employment, and Unemployment in the Soviet Union (1989)。

我首先要提的是支持开篇段落中 claims 的 data：socialist countries 的 activity rates 远高于 West 的 activity rates

![Captur1a](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/07/30-1.png) Kornai 1992

Kornai 提醒说，economic development 会 increases participation rate，但即使将这一点考虑在内，socialist economies 在这方面仍表现突出。

> economic development 的 level 与 participation rate 之间存在一种 loose positive relationship。如果 compares 处于相同 economic development 的 level 的 socialist 和 capitalist countries 的 participation rates，就会发现，socialist countries 的 participation rates 在每个 economic development 的 level 上都是 highest。

socialist systems 与 West 之间的一个 key difference，是 socialist 在 women's activity rate 上的巨大 lead，这也 contributed to overall participation rate。粗略 Assuming women 占 population 的 half，那么在 1980，假定采用 South European 的 women's activity rate 的 levels，就 implies 61.2 percentage points 的 difference。其中 Half 大约是 30 percentage points，随后还必须将其 translated to employment/population；这一数值会更小，因为并非每个人都 works。虽然我手头没有完成这最后一步所需的 data，但 women 更高的 rate of activity 能够 explain increased overall activity rate 的很大一部分，这一判断是 plausible 的。

![Captur1a](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/07/30-2.png) Kornai 1992

我们可能会认为，这源于一种潜在的 feminist trend，即 Soviet State pursued 并 achieved gender equality。在 West，women entering the workforce 是 norms 变化的 result：这些 norms 规定 women 可以做什么，而此前某些 occupations 被视为 male-only。USSR 的 women activity rate 之所以 increased，是因为 socialist system 需要 workers，而 women 占 population 的 half。feminist interpretation 看起来似乎 plausible，例如考虑到有关 maternity leave 的 _de jure_ legislation；但它面临几个问题，比如 abortion ban（在 Stalin 之后的 1955 被 reversed）\[2\]、1936 对 divorce 设置的 legal barriers（为的是 encourage population growth）、Party 的 higher echelons 中几乎没有 women，以及 women 仍是 children 的 main carers 和 household chores 的 main doers 这一事实（[Mespoulet, 2015](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-01160379/file/mespoulet_2006_women_soviet_society.pdf)）

但为什么会有 full employment 和 labour shortages？Kornai 认为，这并不是因为存在一项明确的、旨在 ensure full employment 的 policy（尽管 work 的 right 和 _the duty_ 都被写入了 Constitution of the USSR）。full employment 的 Achievement 是 socialist system 在 pursuit of growth 过程中的 by-product。不过，这项 right to work 并非从一开始就存在，而是在 system 达到 full employment 得以 obtains 的阶段后才 granted 的。

> 一旦这一 process 已经发生，并被 official ideology 列入 system 的 fundamental achievements，它便成为 workers 的一项 "acquired right"，成为 classical system 不能也不愿 reverse 的 status quo。从此，full employment 被确立为一项 guaranteed right（在一定程度上，permanent workplace 也是如此，后文将会看到）。这是一项 actual right，而不只是 nominally proclaimed right；它不仅由 employment policy 的 principles 和 practical conventions 来 ensured，也由 classical system 的 operating mechanism，尤其是 chronic、recurrent shortage of labor 来 ensured。从若干 ultimate moral values 来看，Permanent full employment 确实是 classical system 的一项 fundamental achievement。它意义重大，而且不仅关乎 steady earnings 所带来的 direct financial advantage。它还在 inducing a sense of financial security、strengthening workers 面对其 employers 时的 resolve 和 firmness，以及帮助 bring about equal rights for women 方面发挥着重要作用。

Party 将 chronic labour shortage 的局面视为一个问题（例如在 Poland，每名 job seeker 对应超过 90 个 vacancies），因为 labour shortage 意味着 productive plans 更难 fulfill，因此 planners 往往通过 investments 于 labour saving capital goods、promoting population growth 等方式作出反应。

从 factory manager 的角度看，这种局面会促使他们 'hoard' workers：未来也许需要更多 workers，但到时候可能无法 hire 到他们，因此现在就 hire 他们。于是，在某个时间点，一家 factory 会拥有比实际需要更多的 workers。这与一些（demotivated）workers 的 low job activity 相结合，便 leads to Kornai 称为 'unemployment on the job' 的 concept。

> \*\*\*虽然 registered kind 的 open unemployment 并不存在，而且 labour force participation rate 很高，但 unregistered kind 的 open unemployment 并未消失。此外，还存在 chronic 且 general 的 overmanning，以及 voluntary 和 involuntary employment below skill level，即 employed persons 在 working time 和 educational qualifications 两方面的 underutilization。尽管 employed persons 的 underutilization 能 keeps open unemployment down，它也带来了一系列不应忽视的 adverse consequences。除此之外，它还 contributes to slack work discipline、low labour productivity、rewards 与 performance 的 divorce、low real wages、inflation，以及 consumer goods 和 services 的 shortages。因此，从 labour market 的 situation，或 labour 的 supply 与 demand 之间的 relation 来看，official Soviet economy 可以被 characterized 为 high-employment；从 employed labour force 的 utilization，或 labour input 与 economic values 的 output 之间的 relation 来看，它可以被 characterized 为 low-productivity；而从 individuals 因扮演 employed persons 的 role 而 received 的 income 来看，它可以被 characterized 为 low-incentive。(Porket, 1989)

可以这样理解：Assume 我们有 two economies，由挖沟的人们（这个世界的人们喜欢沟）及相关 activities 构成。Economy A 为这项任务 uses 人员和 capital 的 most efficient arrangement，但由于各处存在 inefficiencies，仍有一些 unemployment，因此，想要 work 的 working age people 中，只有 95% 实际在 work。

Economy B 与 Economy A 相同，但由于一项 government mandate 要求 hire 那些原本会 unemployed 的人，它具有 100% employment。人们轮班挖沟。

到头来，两个 economies 挖出的沟的 length/number 相同。

有些人会喜欢 Economy B：每个人都有 salary，而且人们 work 得更少。很棒，不是吗？但问题在于——assuming 没有 companies go bankrupt——a) Salaries 更低 b) 没有额外的 labor 可轻易用于新的 projects c) 一些人 work 和 earn 得比自己希望的更少

归根结底，这变成了 wages 和 work time 的 redistribution：有些人 win，有些人 lose。或者，也可以将其视为对 employed 征收的一种 tax，以便向 unemployed 进行 redistribute。但长期这样做的 economic consequences 是有问题的 \[3\]。

这就是 overmanning，即 hidden unemployment 的一种形式。如果 Economy B 实际 used efficient number 的 workers，那么它的 employment rate 比方说会是 80%。在这种情况下，economy 的 producing 在 aggregate 上仍会相同。

Overmanning 是 hidden unemployment 的一种类型。Porket 区分了三种：

-   想要 full-time job 时却在 part-time jobs 中 Employment
-   人们被迫在自己 overqualified 的 jobs 中 work 的 Employment
-   在既定 technological conditions 下，companies employ 超出其所需人数的 Employment（Economy B 的情况）

他 estimates，overmanning 占 total workforce 的 10-15%。

现在我们转向 post-Stalin history。

在 1957 至 1961 之间，所谓的 'antiparasite laws' 在 USSR 的大部分地区被 adopted。这些 measures 旨在强制每个人拥有一份 'socially useful work'，不遵从者会被处以 2-10 years 的 forced labour。

存在一些 open unregistered unemployment，但水平很低：

> 不出所料，这种源于缺少 vacancies 的 open unemployment 并未得到官方 quantified，因此其准确的 volume 仍只能猜测。但已知在 1959 年，working age 的 men 中有 10.0 per cent、working age 的 women 中有 27.4 per cent 既未 participated in social production，也未 studied full time（见下方 Table 5.2）。六年后，E. Manevich 断言，able-bodied population 中有 20 per cent 未 take part in social production。显然，1959 年的 figures 并非 exclusively 指那些渴望有一份 job、却因缺少 vacancies 而无法得到 job 的 jobless persons。它们也涵盖了那些无意扮演 workers 或 collective farmers 角色的 non-employed persons。不过，它们明确 indicate 至少存在一些 involuntary open unemployment，尽管是 unregistered。具体就 youth unemployment 而言，Basile Kerblay 曾报告（但未提供文献记录）称：‘根据 Gosplan statistics，1965 年 medium-sized towns 中，年龄介于 16 和 17 岁的年轻人有 25.2 per cent、年龄介于 18 和 19 岁的年轻人有 13.9 per cent 处于 unemployed 状态，相比之下，其他 age groups 为 1. 7 per cent。’
> 
> 除了承认 non-employment 和 involuntary open unemployment 都有发生之外——当然没有使用专门留给 capitalism 的术语 ‘unemployment’——Soviet sources 还承认，employed persons 经常处于 underutilized 状态，或是因为 involuntary employment below skill level，或是因为 overmanning。
> 
> 为了 remove 导致 overmanning 的这一特定 cause，E. Manevich 在 1965 年提出、并在 1969 年再次提出了一项 solution，其实质相当于 open registered unemployment。因 technological progress 而被 made redundant 的 workers，其 placement 应由 special organizations 而非 enterprises 负责。同时，在 between jobs 期间，相关 workers 应由 state 在物质上供养。不出所料，这项 proposal 并未 implemented。Open registered unemployment 和 unemployment benefits 对 regime 而言是不可接受的，因为其 ideology 主张，socialism 彻底且一劳永逸地 liquidated unemployment，而且在 socialism 下，technological progress 与 able-bodied population 的 full employment 是相伴而行的。因此，对 dismissals 的 restrictions 未被 lifted，所以 shedding surplus workers 的 enterprises 仍须负责这些 workers 的 placement。(Porket, 1989)

> 根据 (1976) constitution，citizens 享有 right to work（即 guaranteed employment，以及按照其 work 的 quantity 和 quality 获得、且不低于 state-established minimum 的 pay），其中包括根据自身 inclinations、abilities、training 和 education 选择 trade 或 profession、type of job 和 work 的 right，不过须以考虑 socieety 的 needs 为前提。另一方面，在自己选定的 socially useful occupation 中 work conscientiously，并 strictly 地 observe work discipline，是每一位 able-bodied citizen 的 duty，也是一件光荣的事。Socially useful work 及其 results determined 一个人在 society 中的 status。对 socially useful work 的 Evasion 与 socialist society 的 principles 是 incompatible 的 $$...$$
> 
> 此外，labour situation 是 contradictory 的。34 一方面，open registered unemployment 并不存在，economic activity rates 很 high，官方声称已实现 able-bodied population 的 full employment，而关于 labour-deficit areas 和 national level 的 shortage of labour 的抱怨比比皆是。另一方面，open unregistered unemployment 并非不为人知，enterprise level 的 overmanning 是 chronic 且 general 的，不少 qualified individuals 是 voluntarily 或 involuntarily employed below their skill level，labour-surplus areas 也确实存在，而且有人 evaded 在 official economy 中的 employment。这一 contradiction 反映在 Soviet 关于该国 manpower resources 的讨论中。占主导的观点断言，labour resources 是 inadequate 的，其意思是，available labour supplies lagged behind 那些已 incorporated 在 enterprise plans 中并有 funds 支持的 effective demand。相比之下，少数观点认为，labour resources 是 completely adequate 的，只是被 utilized irrationally。

总之，official unemployment rate 为 zero，几乎是 institutional definition 使然：你无法申请 unemployment benefits。Companies hoarded labour

然而，在那些已 quit their jobs 并搬到另一个地方、或刚开始 working 的人中，确实存在 non-registered unemployment。尽管在 aggregate level 上，jobs 远多于 workers，但在 micro scale 上，这些 jobs 的 geographical and skill distribution 并未与 workforce 的 location 和 skills 实现 match efficiently。尽管如此，Soviet Union 仍实现了很 high 的 employment rate，与 west 的 full employment 概念相当。

分开来看，其中一部分是 switching jobs 的人：因为 a) 没有 unemployment benefits，人们有很多 incentives 去迅速寻找另一份 job。b) 有大量 vacancies c) ‘Uninterrupted service’ 是一个会影响 social security benefits 的 legal concept。如果一名 worker 处于 without employment 的状态超过三周，就会失去该 status d) Antiparasite laws 规定，超过四个月没有一份 ‘socially useful’ job 是可以受到惩罚的。

这种 unregistered unemployment 的另一部分，是为成为母亲而 quit their jobs 的 women。

还有一部分是 youth unemployment。年轻 (<18) 人享有各种 privileges，例如 work 的 hours 更少，却能获得与 full time worker 相同的 pay。Ceteris paribus，companies 更愿意选择年长的 workers。USSR 为此对 companies imposing quotas（workforce 中必须有 0.5 到 10% 的人是 <18）。这一 group 的 (unregistered) unemployment 的 rate 为 1-5%。

此外，为了让从 school 或 university 到 work 的 transition 更轻松，State 会提前 planned 哪些 companies 将 hire 哪位 worker，这听起来似乎能解决任何问题，但是

> 实际上，这个 system 的 functions 远非 smoothly。它在 balancing supply and demand 方面存在困难。它也有 exemptions，尤其是基于 health 和 family circumstances 的 exemptions。由于自身 plans 或 needs 的 changes，enterprises 经常拒绝 hire 被 directed 到它们那里的 graduates。有些 graduates 不到 assigned posts 报到。另一些虽然到岗，却在 three-year period 届满之前离开，原因之一是他们没有得到 work

##  Conclusion（结论）

将 unregistered unemployment 考虑在内，Soviet economy 实现了 West 通常所称的 full employment（至少是指几乎每个想要 work 的人都有一份 job），其 rates 低于 5%。与处于最佳状态的 capitalist economies 相比，这一表现也毫不逊色，而且显著好于正在经历 recession 的 capitalist economies（[Gregory and Collier, 1988](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1811163?seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents), [Ellman 1979](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02371698)）

![Gregory and Collier, 1988](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/07/30-5.png) Gregory and Collier, 1988

由于需要更多 workers，women 的 Rates of activity 很 high。

然而，由于上文提到的 incentives，companies 饱受 overmanning 困扰；尽管采取了措施试图 reduce 它，它仍然持续存在。

## 原文

\[Part of the [Soviet Union series](https://nintil.com/2016/05/14/the-soviet-union-series/)\]

The Soviet labour 'market' was a peculiar one. Rather than the prevalence of unemployment, as we are used to, the Soviet Union not only achieved full employment, but also got to a situation where there were\_shortages of labour,\_even though a significant share of the population was working. In this post I clarify what does full employment mean in the Soviet context, and explain some aspects of their labour 'market' not covered in [my previous post](https://nintil.com/2016/04/03/the-soviet-union-working-hours/) on this topic. As usual, this post covers the post-Stalin era. \[1\]

> The official Soviet ideology contends that socialism is not only completely different from capitalism, but also in all respects (i.e. socially, economically, politically, culturally, and morally) superior to it. In order to substantiate this claim, the ideology cites a long list of achievements, one of them being that while unemployment is an endemic feature of capitalism, socialism abolishes it entirely and once and for all.
> 
> If the term 'unemployment' denotes exclusively open unemployment of the registered kind or the dole, then the assertion is fully justified, because in the Soviet Union the payment of unemployment benefits was stopped as early as October 1930. On top of that, over the years the Soviet regime has succeeded in mobilizing for participation in the social economy the vast majority of able-bodied men and women of working age.\*\*\* (Porket, 1989)

This post draws mainly on János Kornai's The Socialist System  (1992) and J.L. Porket's Work, Employment, and Unemployment in the Soviet Union (1989).

The first point I'll mention is the data backing up the claims in the introductory paragraph: The activity rates in socialist countries were far higher than the ones in the West

![Captur1a](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/07/30-1.png) Kornai 1992

Kornai warns that economic development increases the participation rate, but even when taking this into account, the socialist economies still shine in that sense.

> There is a loose positive relationship between the level of economic development and the participation rate. If one compares the participation rates of socialist and capitalist countries at the same level of  economic development, it turns out that the socialist countries' participation  rates are the highest on each level of economic development.

One key difference between the socialist systems and the West was a massive socialist lead in women's activity rate, which contributed to the overall participation rate. Assuming crudely that women are half of the population, in 1980, assuming South European levels of women's activity rate implies a difference of 61.2 percentage points. Half of that is roughly 30 percentage points, which then have to be translated to employment/population, which will be less, because not everyone works. While I don't have data at hand for this final step, it is plausible that the higher rate of activity of women explain a big part of this increased overall activity rate.

![Captur1a](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/07/30-2.png) Kornai 1992

We could think that this was due to an underlying feminist trend, where the Soviet State pursued and achieved gender equality. In the West, women entering the workforce was a result of changing norms of what was admissible for women to do, where previously some occupations were seen as male-only. The reason for this increased women activity rate in the USSR was that the socialist system needed workers, and women make up half of the population. The feminist interpretation, that seems plausible given _de jure_ legislation regarding maternity leave, for example, faces several issues, like the abortion ban (reversed after Stalin in 1955)\[2\] and legal barriers to divorce in 1936 (to encourage population growth), the fact that there were almost no women in the higher echelons of the Party, and that women were still the main carers of children and main doers of household chores ([Mespoulet, 2015](https://halshs.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-01160379/file/mespoulet_2006_women_soviet_society.pdf))

But why full employment and labour shortages? Kornai argues that this wasn't because of an explicit policy to ensure full employment (even though the right and _the duty_ to work were enshrined in the Constitution of the USSR). Achievement of full employment was a by-product of the socialist system in its pursuit of growth. This right to work, however, wasn't present from the beginning, but it's granted once the system has reached a point where full employment obtains.

> Once this process has occurred and been rated by the official ideology among the system's fundamental achievements, it becomes an "acquired right" of the workers, a status quo that the classical system cannot and does not wish to reverse. Thenceforth full employment is laid down as a guaranteed right (and to a degree, so is a permanent workplace, as will be seen later). This is an actual, not just a nominally proclaimed right, ensured not only by the principles and practical conventions of employment policy but by the operating mechanism of the classical system, above all the chronic, recurrent shortage of labor. Permanent full employment certainly is a fundamental achievement of the classical system, in terms of several ultimate moral values. It has vast significance, and not simply in relation to the direct financial advantage in steady earnings. It plays additionally a prominent part in inducing a sense of financial security, strengthening the workers' resolve and firmness toward their employers, and helping to bring about equal rights for women.

The situation of chronic labour shortage (In Poland, for example, there were over 90 vacancies per job seeker) is seen as problematic by the Party, as labour shortage means the productive plans are harder to fulfill, and so planners tend to react by investments in labour saving capital goods, promoting population growth, etc.

From the factory manager's point of view, this situation induces them to 'hoard' workers: perhaps more workers will be needed in the future, but maybe they will not be available for hire, so they hire them now. At a given point in time, then, a factory will have more workers than it actually needs. This, combined with low job activity from some (demotivated) workers leads to the concept that Kornai terms 'unemployment on the job'.

> \*\*\*While open unemployment of the registered kind is absent and the labour force participation rate is high, open unemployment of the unregistered kind has not disappeared. In addition, there is chronic and general overmanning as well as voluntary and involuntary employment below skill level, i.e. underutilization of employed persons in terms of both working time and educational qualifications. Although underutilization of employed persons keeps open unemployment down, it has a number of adverse consequences, which should not be overlooked. Amongst other things it contributes to slack work discipline, low labour productivity, divorce of rewards from performance, low real wages, inflation, and shortages of consumer goods and services. Thus, from the point of view of the situation in the labour market or the relation between the supply of and demand for labour the official Soviet economy may be characterized as a high-employment one; from that of the utilization of the employed labour force or the relation between labour input and the output of economic values it may be characterized as a low-productivity one; and from that of the income received by individuals for playing the role of employed persons it may be characterized as a low-incentive one. (Porket, 1989)

One way to think about this: Assume that we have two economies that consist of people digging ditches (people like ditches in this world) and related activities. Economy A uses the most efficient arrangement of people and capital for the task, but there exists some unemployment due to inefficiencies here and there, so that only 95% of working age people who want to work actually do so.

Economy B is the same as Economy A, but it has 100% employment, due to a government mandate that those who would be unemployed are to be hired. People take shifts to dig ditches.

At the end of the day, the same length/number of ditches are dug in both economies.

Some people would like Economy B: everyone has a salary, and people work less. Great, isn't it? But the problem is that -assuming no companies go bankrupt- a) Salaries are lower b) No extra labor is easily available for new projects c) Some people work and earn less than they would like to

At the end of the day, it comes to a redistribution of wages and work time: Some win and some lose. Alternatively, this could be seen as a tax on the employed to redistribute to the unemployed. But the economic consequences of doing that in the long term are problematic \[3\].

This is overmanning, a form of hidden unemployment. If Economy B actually used the efficient number of workers, they would have, say, an employment rate of 80%. In this case, the economy would be producing the same in aggregate.

Overmanning is a type of hidden unemployment. Porket distinguishes three kinds:

-   Employment in part-time jobs when a full-time job is desired
-   Employment where people are forced to work in jobs for which they are overqualified
-   Employment where companies employ more people than they need, given their technological conditions (the case of Economy B)

He estimates overmanning to be 10-15% of the total workforce.

We now jump to post-Stalin history.

Between 1957 and 1961, so called 'antiparasite laws' were adopted in most of the USSR. These measures were to force everyone to have a 'socially useful work', and noncompliers were punished with 2-10 years of forced labour.

Some open unregistered unemployment existed, but was low:

> Not surprisingly, this open unemployment stemming from a lack of vacancies was not officially quantified, so that its accurate volume remains a guess. But it is known that in 1959 10.0 per cent of men of working age and 27.4 per cent of women of working age neither participated in social production nor studied full time (see Table 5.2 below). And six years later E. Manevich asserted that 20 per cent of the able-bodied population did not take part in social production.  Obviously, the 1959 figures did not refer exclusively to jobless persons keen to have a job, yet unable to get it because of a lack of vacancies. They also covered non-employed persons uninterested in playing the roles of workers or collective farmers. Nevertheless, they clearly indicate the existence of at least some involuntary open unemployment, albeit unregistered. Concerning specifically youth unemployment, it was reported (although not documented) by Basile Kerblay that 'according to Gosplan statistics, 25.2 per cent of young people aged between 16 and 17 and 13.9 per cent of those aged between 18 and 19 were unemployed in medium-sized towns in 1965, compared with 1. 7 per cent for other age groups'.
> 
> Besides admitting the occurrence of both non-employment and involuntary open unemployment, naturally without using the term 'unemployment' which was reserved for capitalism, Soviet sources further admitted that employed persons were frequently underutilized, either because of involuntary employment below skill level, or because of overmanning.
> 
> In order to remove this particular cause of overmanning, E. Manevich proposed in 1965 and again in 1969 a solution that in fact amounted to open registered unemployment. Instead of enterprises, special organizations should be responsible for the placement of the workers made redundant in connection with technological progress. Simultaneously, while between jobs, the workers in question should be provided for materially by the state. Not surprisingly, the proposal was not implemented. Open registered unemployment and unemployment benefits were unacceptable to the regime, because its ideology contended that socialism liquidated unemployment entirely and once for all and that under it technological progress went hand in hand with full employment of the able-bodied population. Thus, restrictions on dismissals were not lifted, so that enterprises shedding surplus workers remained responsible for their placement. (Porket, 1989)

> Under the (1976) constitution, citizens had the right to work (i.e. to guaranteed employment and pay in accordance with the quantity and quality of their work, and not below the state-established minimum), including the right to choose their trade or profession, type of job and work in accorance with their inclinations, abilities, training and education, albeit subject to the caveat of taking into account the needs of socieety. On the other hand, it was the duty of and a matter of honour for every able-bodied citizen to work conscientiously in his/her chosen, socially useful occupation, and strictly to observe work discipline. Socially useful work and its results determined a person's status in society. Evasion of socially useful work was incompatible with the principles of socialist society \[...\]
> 
> Moreover, the labour situation was contradictory. 34 On the one hand, open registered unemployment was absent, economic activity rates were high, full employment of the able-bodied population was alleged, and complaints of labour-deficit areas and a shortage of labour at the national level abounded. On the other hand, open unregistered unemployment was not unknown, overmanning at the enterprise level was chronic and general, quite a few qualified individuals were voluntarily or involuntarily employed below their skill level, labour-surplus areas were to be found, and employment in the official economy was evaded. This contradiction was reflected in Soviet discussions on the country's manpower resources. The prevailing view asserted that labour resources were inadequate, meaning by it that the available labour supplies lagged behind the effective demand that was incorporated in enterprise plans and backed up with funds. In contrast, a minority view contended that labour resources were completely adequate, but were utilized irrationally.

To sum up, the official unemployment rate was zero, almost by institutional definition: You couldn't sign up for unemployment benefits. Companies hoarded labour

However, non-registered unemployment existed for people who had quit their jobs and moved to another location, or had just begun working.  While at an aggregate level, there were many more jobs than workers, in a micro scale, the geographical and skill distribution of these jobs did not match efficiently with the location and skills of the workforce. Despite this, the Soviet Union managed a very high employment rate, on par with the concept of full employment in the west.

Breaking down this by parts, a fraction were people switching jobs: since a) there were no unemployment benefits, people had lots of incentives to quickly look for another job. b) There were lots of vacancies c) 'Uninterrupted service' a legal concept that had an impact in social security benefits. If a worker was without employment for more than three weeks, they would lose that status d) Antiparasite laws that made punishable not having a 'socially useful' job for more than four months.

Other part of this unregistered unemployment were women who quit their jobs to be mothers.

Yet another part was youth unemployment. Young (<18) people were entitled to a variety of privileges, like having to work less hours for the same pay a full time worker would get. Ceteris paribus, companies preferred older workers. The USSR reacted to this by imposing quotas on companies (Between 0.5 and 10% of the workforce had to be <18). The rate of (unregistered) unemployment for this group was 1-5%.

Further, to easen up the transition from school or university to work, the State planned which companies would hire which worker in advance, which sounds like it would solve any problem, but

> In practice, the system functions far from smoothly. It has difficulties in balancing supply and demand. There are exemptions from it, particularly on grounds of health and family circumstances. Due to changes in their plans or needs, enterprises frequently refuse to hire the graduates directed to them. Some graduates fail to show up at their assigned posts. Others do turn up, but leave before the expiry of the three-year period, inter alia, because they are not given work

##  Conclusion

Taking into account unregistered unemployment the Soviet economy achieved what in the West is typically called full employment (At least in the sense that almost everyone who wanted to work had a job), with rates below 5%. This compares favourably with capitalist economies at their best, and it's significantly better than capitalist economies undergoing a recession ([Gregory and Collier, 1988](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1811163?seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents), [Ellman 1979](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF02371698))

![Gregory and Collier, 1988](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/07/30-5.png) Gregory and Collier, 1988

Rates of activity among women were high due to the need for more workers.

However, companies was plagued by overmanning due to the incentives mentioned above, and while measures were taken to try to reduce it, it still persisted.
