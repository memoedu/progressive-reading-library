# Student group notes #3: getting good speakers

---

## 正文

_这是这个 [系列](https://www.benkuhn.net/studentgroup) 的第三篇文章，讲述我在几乎从零开始创办 student group 的过程中学到的经验。_

如果说在负责 HEA 的那段时间里，有什么让我引以为傲，那就是我们的 programming。去年，我们请来了超过 10 位 speakers 为 HEA 做 talks，其中包括 Peter Singer、Jaan Tallinn，以及 [其他许多极受欢迎、日程繁忙的人](http://harvardea.org/events/#past)。我们还 raised 了数千美元，用于支付这些 speakers 的 honoraria。我们是怎么做到的？

诀窍就是像我和 John 一样人脉广泛，这样你就认识一批背景各异的名人和财力雄厚的 university organizations，可以请他们帮忙。如果你还没有这样的人脉，那就走出去，network！

……开个玩笑。事实上，在邀请我们的 speakers 来 speak 之前，其中大多数人我都不认识。我只是给他们发了一封 cold email，问他们愿不愿意为 HEA 做一场 talk（有时会由朋友或我们出色的 faculty adviser [Nir](http://medethics.med.harvard.edu/people/eyal/) 引荐）。许多邮件都石沉大海——我的 archives 里到处都是无人回复的 invitations 的尸骸——但我们也收到了许多 responses，而我们出色的 speakers 阵容，绝对值得多发那些投入虚空的 messages。

这里我还有一个重要的小建议。在给潜在的 speakers 发 emails 这件事上——乃至在日常生活中——我发现 [Boomerang](http://www.boomeranggmail.com/referral_download.html?ref=4sbhr)[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/good-speakers/#fn:1) _完全不可或缺_。我把所有已发出的 emails 都设置为：如果 recipient 没有 respond，就在一周后让它们回到我的 inbox；而且，往往在我给对方发了第二封 follow-up email 后，就会收到 response。事实上，要和某些 professors 沟通，Boomerang 几乎是必需的；我怀疑他们奉行的原则就是，email 第一次发来时一律不回复……再说一遍——**如果你靠 email 推动重要的事情，而你还没有 Boomerang，现在就开始用 Boomerang。**

从心理感受上说，发 cold emails 常常让我灰心，因为每一封邮件最终促成一场 talk 的 chance 都相当低——我常常觉得，自己的大部分工作都白费了（尽管我们的 response rate 比我原先想的高得多）。我试着通过 _重视付出的努力，而不是结果_ 来对抗这种感受——我会把自己的 goals 表述为“发出了多少 high-quality emails”，而不是“收到了多少 responses”或“安排了多少 talks”。

总体来说，我们 cold-emailed 的人中，大约有 20% 最终来 HEA 做了 talk。我不能保证其他学校的 student groups 也会有这样的效果——我们确实受益于 Harvard brand——但即使我们的 response rate 低得多，我们也可以多发一些 emails，或者开始邀请没那么忙的人。在我们 recruiting 新 members 时，这样出色的阵容带来的 credibility 和 attention 给了我们巨大的助力，所以我真的很庆幸我们做了这件事。

给出色的人发 Cold-email！这办法真的管用！

## 原文

_This is the third post in a [series](https://www.benkuhn.net/studentgroup) on lessons I learned while starting a student group almost from scratch._

If there’s one thing that I’m proud of about my tenure running HEA, it’s our programming. Last year we had over 10 speakers come to give talks to HEA, including Peter Singer, Jaan Tallinn, and [many other extremely popular and busy people](http://harvardea.org/events/#past). We also raised thousands of dollars to cover honoraria from these speakers. How?

The trick is to be well-connected like me and John so that you know a diverse pool of famous people and deep-pocketed university organizations to lean on for favors. If you don’t have such a pool already, get out there and network!

…just kidding. I didn’t actually know most of our speakers before I invited them to speak. I just sent them a cold email asking if they wanted to give a talk for HEA (sometimes with an introduction from a friend or our excellent faculty adviser, [Nir](http://medethics.med.harvard.edu/people/eyal/)). We got a lot of silence—my archives are littered with the corpses of unanswered invitations—but we also got a lot of responses, and our awesome lineup of speakers was definitely worth the additional messages cast into the void.

I also have an important sub-tip here. For emailing potential speakers—and also for life in general—I found [Boomerang](http://www.boomeranggmail.com/referral_download.html?ref=4sbhr)[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/good-speakers/#fn:1) to be _totally indispensable_. I set any sent emails to return to my inbox in a week if the recipient didn’t respond, and often if I sent them a second follow-up email I’d get a response after that. In fact, Boomerang is practically a requirement to communicate with some professors, who I suspect just have a policy of never responding to email the first time it’s sent… Again—**if you rely on email to make important things happen and you don’t have Boomerang, start using Boomerang NOW.**

Psychologically, I often found it demoralizing to send cold emails, since each one individually had a fairly low chance of turning into a talk further down the road—it often felt like most of my work was wasted (although our response rate was a lot higher than I thought it would be). I tried to fight this by _valuing effort instead of results_—I would phrase my goals in terms of “high-quality emails sent” instead of “responses received” or “talks scheduled.”

Overall, something like 20% of people we cold-emailed ended up giving a talk at HEA. I can’t promise that this will translate to student groups at other schools—we certainly benefited from the Harvard brand—but even if we had gotten a much lower response rate, we could have sent more emails or started asking less-busy people. The credibility and attention that we got from having such a great lineup was a huge boost when we were recruiting new members, so I’m really glad we did it.

Cold-email awesome people! It works!
