# The Pyramid of Economic Insight and Virtue

---

## 正文

Bryan Caplan 几年前写过一篇文章，提出了关于 macroeconomic [knowledge](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2013/05/straight_talk_a.html#) 的一种 tiered view。我想在这里提出一种类似的 tiered view。我已经思考了一段时间，最近又在 Anthony J. Evans 的书\_Markets for Managers:\_中发现了类似的想法：

> 或者，正如 [Arnold Kling 所说](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2009/07/what_makes_heal.html)，有三种 schools of thought。对 Chicago economists 而言，是‘markets work，使用 markets’。对 Keynesian economists 而言，是‘markets fail，使用 government’。但对 Austrian economists 而言，则是‘markets fail，使用 markets’。如果存在 inefficiency，那么就存在 waste。而所有 waste 都是 profit opportunity。恰恰因为 markets fail，entrepreneurs 才有 incentive 去寻找新的做生意的方式。对 friction 的 identification 并不会使人怀疑 markets 作为 coordination device 的能力。正如我的教授 Peter Boettke 常说的那样，要不是现实世界中存在 friction，我们每次试着把一只脚迈到另一只脚前面时都会摔倒。那些‘information economists’是对的——friction 是常态。但这未必是件坏事。毕竟，试着想象一个有着 perfect information 的世界。在电影《男人百分百》中，Mel Gibson 拥有了准确理解女人心中所想的能力。information asymmetry 变得更少了。但即使这样的世界有可能存在，它也未必是令人向往的。

Tier 1，即[Folk Position](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=320940)（Markets 经常 fail，government works 得更好，始终使用 government）：Markets 很糟糕，而且是 zero sum。我们需要对它们的每一个方面都 heavily regulate，甚至尝试对一些 industries 进行 nationalising，restrict free trade。在最极端的情况下，要不是存在实际困难，就 implement central planning。

Tier 2，[First Semester Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics_in_One_Lesson)（Markets work 得很好，government 经常 fails，始终使用 markets）：Markets 很棒。它们能够毫不费力地 solve 你能想到的每一个问题。Governments 如果 intervene，总会让事情变得更糟。

Tier 3，[Second Semester Economics](http://www.taxresearch.org.uk/Blog/2014/09/17/i-deal-with-reality-possibility-and-what-is-desirable-and-free-markets-fit-into-none-of-those-categories/comment-page-1/?utm_content=buffer5d0ea&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter.com&utm_campaign=buffer)（Markets 经常 fail，government 有时 works，有时使用 government）：Markets 会 fail。到处都有 externalities、public goods 和 market failures。因此，尽管 markets 很好，government 仍有一个重要的 role，就是 regulate 它们并确保它们 work properly。Fully free markets 是一种幻想。

Tier 4，[Public Choice](http://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/PublicChoice.html)（Markets 有些 fail，governments 经常 fail，使用 markets）：Governments 也会 fail。Market failures 必须与 government failures 进行 balanced against，而事实表明，在许多情况下，government interventions 的 costs 会 outweight 其 benefits，因此最好不要干预 market failures。

Tier 5，[Institutional/Cultural analysis](http://www.coordinationproblem.org/2006/05/responding_to_c.html)：（Markets work 得很好（但并非 imperfect 的反面），government 则没有 work 得那么好（它也是 imperfect 的），使用 markets）Voluntary action、institutional、cultural 和 moral rules [能够在一定程度上 solve](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/12/24/slaying-alexanders-moloch/)，而且只要给予时间，就能处理 externalities、[market failures，并 provide public goods](http://www.econlib.org/library/Columns/y2013/CardenHorwitzmarkets.html#)。人们并[非 fully self-interested](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2014/12/the_evidence_of.html)，而且能够 craft 巧妙的 [institutional arrangements](http://www.iea.org.uk/sites/default/files/publications/files/IEA%20Future%20of%20the%20Commons%20web%2029-1.10.12.pdf) 和 [contracts](https://books.google.com/books?id=0namAgAAQBAJ&pg=PA165&lpg=PA165&dq=david+schmidtz+public+goods&source=bl&ots=mU4R_xc1O-&sig=SyQRtx9xaJmFO2mf-I6hNrQRpSE&hl=es&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwik6fC8tqzLAhVC2BoKHfNGCvM4ChDoAQhOMAg#v=onepage&q=david%20schmidtz%20public%20goods&f=false)，使 cooperation 更 robust。关于 market 和 government efficiency 的 Standard economic conditions 被认为是 [sufficient，而非 necessary](http://econfaculty.gmu.edu/bcaplan/compet)。这解释了为什么 governments 比 [Public Choice](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/economics/wait-a-minute-is-the-government-self-interested-or-isnt-it/) [所会 describe 的](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/politics-government/why-we-vote-the-way-we-vote/) work 得更好，但也解释了为什么 markets 同样比某些 theories 所 suggest 的 work 得更好。

令人好奇的反转在于，Tier 5 与 Tier 2 非常相似，而后者是一种远没有那么 sophisticated 的 view；这或许又能解释 Tier 5 为什么相对不受欢迎。Tier 5ers 可能看起来像是持有‘market fundamentalist’立场的 Tier 2ers，没有意识到 Tier 3 的 insights。Bryan Caplan 在[这里](http://econfaculty.gmu.edu/bcaplan/healthstate.htm)展示了这种反转：

> 那么，为了顺着你们的担忧说下去，我先引用一下_《恐怖博士的欢唱博客》_。在这一幕中，Penny 正在向 Dr. Horrible 讲述她上一次约会的情况——丝毫没有意识到 Dr. Horrible 正暗恋着她。**[Penny](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm1260407/)**：不过，他结果真的特别贴心。有时候，人就是这样有好几层。内里与表面完全不同。**[Dr. Horrible](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000439/)**：而有时候，还会有第三层，比前面更深，而那一层又和最表面的一层一样。就像馅饼。这一幕完美地体现了我接下来要告诉你们的关于 health care 的事情。表面上看，health care 中的 free market 似乎是最好的 approach。再深入看一点，free-market approach 似乎又天真又危险。然而，当你看得更深入时，free market approach 到头来却又是最好的。

## 原文

Bryan Caplan wrote an article some years ago presenting a tiered view of macroeconomic [knowledge](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2013/05/straight_talk_a.html#). I want to present here a similar tiered view. I've been thinking on it for a while, and I recently found similar thoughts in Anthony J. Evans' book\_Markets for Managers:\_

> Or, as [Arnold Kling puts](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2009/07/what_makes_heal.html) it, there are three schools of thought. To Chicago economists ‘markets work, use markets’. To Keynesian economists ‘markets fail, use government’. But to Austrian economists ‘markets fail, use markets’. If there’s inefficiency, then there is waste. And all waste is a profit opportunity. It is precisely because markets fail that there’s an incentive for entrepreneurs to find new ways to do business. The identification of friction does not cast doubt on the ability of markets to serve as a coordination device. As my professor Peter Boettke would say, if it wasn’t for real world friction we’d fall over whenever we tried to put one foot in front of the other. The ‘information economists’ are right – friction is the norm. But this isn’t necessarily a bad thing. After all, try to imagine a world of perfect information. In the movie ‘What Women Want’, Mel Gibson had the power of understanding exactly what women were thinking. There was less of an information asymmetry. But even if such a world were possible, it wouldn’t necessarily be desirable.

Tier 1, the[Folk Position](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=320940) (Markets fail a lot, government works better, use government always): Markets are horrible and zero sum. We need to heavily regulate every single aspect of them, or even try nationalising some industries, restrict free trade. In the extreme, were if not for the practical problems, implement central planning.

Tier 2, [First Semester Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics_in_One_Lesson) (Markets work well, government fails a lot, use markets always): Markets are great. They can solve every issue you may think of effortlessly. Governments will always make things worse if they intervene.

Tier 3, [Second Semester Economics](http://www.taxresearch.org.uk/Blog/2014/09/17/i-deal-with-reality-possibility-and-what-is-desirable-and-free-markets-fit-into-none-of-those-categories/comment-page-1/?utm_content=buffer5d0ea&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter.com&utm_campaign=buffer) (Markets fail often, government works sometimes, use government sometimes): Markets fail. There are externalities, public goods, and market failures around. Therefore, while markets are very nice, there is an important role for government to regulate them and ensure they work properly. Fully free markets are a fantasy.

Tier 4, [Public Choice](http://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/PublicChoice.html) (Markets fail a bit, governments fail a lot, use markets): Governments also fail. Market failures have to be balanced against  government failures, and turns out that in many cases, the costs of government interventions outweight its benefits, therefore it's better to leave market failures alone.

Tier 5, [Institutional/Cultural analysis](http://www.coordinationproblem.org/2006/05/responding_to_c.html): (Markets work well (but are imperfect), government doesn't work as well (it is also imperfect), use markets) Voluntary action, institutional, cultural, and moral rules [can solve, to an extent](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/12/24/slaying-alexanders-moloch/), and given time, externalities, [market failures, and provide public goods](http://www.econlib.org/library/Columns/y2013/CardenHorwitzmarkets.html#). People are [not fully self-interested](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2014/12/the_evidence_of.html), and can craft clever [institutional arrangements](http://www.iea.org.uk/sites/default/files/publications/files/IEA%20Future%20of%20the%20Commons%20web%2029-1.10.12.pdf) and [contracts](https://books.google.com/books?id=0namAgAAQBAJ&pg=PA165&lpg=PA165&dq=david+schmidtz+public+goods&source=bl&ots=mU4R_xc1O-&sig=SyQRtx9xaJmFO2mf-I6hNrQRpSE&hl=es&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwik6fC8tqzLAhVC2BoKHfNGCvM4ChDoAQhOMAg#v=onepage&q=david%20schmidtz%20public%20goods&f=false) to make cooperation more robust. Standard economic conditions for market and government efficiency are seen to be [sufficient, not necessary](http://econfaculty.gmu.edu/bcaplan/compet). This explains why governments work better than what [Public Choice](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/economics/wait-a-minute-is-the-government-self-interested-or-isnt-it/) [would describe](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/politics-government/why-we-vote-the-way-we-vote/), but also why markets also work even better than what some theories suggest.

The curious reversal is that Tier 5 is very similar to Tier 2, which is a far less sophisticated view, which in turn may explain the relative impopularity of Tier 5. Tier 5ers may seem like 'market fundamentalist' Tier 2ers, unaware of the insights of Tier 3.  Bryan Caplan illustrates the reversal [here](http://econfaculty.gmu.edu/bcaplan/healthstate.htm):

> So just to play into your fears, I’ll begin by quoting _Dr. Horrible’s Sing-Along Blog_.  In this scene, Penny is telling Dr. Horrible about her last date – never realizing that Dr. Horrible is secretly in love with her. **[Penny](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm1260407/)**: But, he turned out to be totally sweet. Sometimes people are layered like that. There's something totally different underneath than what's on the surface. **[Dr. Horrible](https://www.imdb.com/name/nm0000439/)**: And sometimes there's a third, even deeper level, and that one is the same as the top surface one. Like with pie. This scene perfectly captures what I’m going to tell you about health care.  On the surface, a free market in health care seems like the best approach.  When you look a little deeper, the free-market approach seems naive and dangerous.  When you look deeper still, however, the free market approach turns out to be the best after all.
