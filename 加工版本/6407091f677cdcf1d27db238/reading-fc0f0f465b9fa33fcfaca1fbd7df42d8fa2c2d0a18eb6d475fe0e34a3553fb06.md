# Where do innovations really come from?

---

## 正文

有一篇 [Block & Keller (2011)](http://www.itif.org/files/Where_do_innovations_come_from.pdf) 的文献（“innovations 从哪里来？”），其中采用了一些非常古怪的 definitions。

Mariana Mazzucato 写道：

> 鉴于 US government 在众多 sectors 中发挥的 leading developmental role，在更 micro 的 level 上，Block and Keller (2011b) 发现，在 1971 至 2006 年间，最重要的 88 项 innovations 中有 77 项（由 R&D Magazine 的年度 awards 进行 rated）——即 88 per cent——一直 fully dependent on federal research support，尤其是在其早期 phases，但不只限于这些 phases，这也就不足为奇了——而且 R&D Magazine 的 award 还 excludes ICT innovations。

Mazzucato 说，Block & Keller 说，88 项 inventions 中有 77 项是 fully dependent on federal research support，只是它们 received 了一些 federal funding。

请注意，Mazzucato 的说法（“fully dependent”）远远超出了 Block & Keller 所说的内容。[这里](https://gowans.wordpress.com/2012/12/21/do-publicly-owned-planned-economies-work/)的 misinterpretation 程度甚至更高，而且这些 findings 被用来 support 的不是 entrepreneurial state，而是彻底的 central economic planning。

Block & Keller 将某个事物称为 private 或 public 的方式颇为奇特：

-   Private：Fortune 500 firms、其他 firms，以及 inter-firm collaboration
-   Public **或 mixed**：**近期由 government 提供 supported 的 spinoffs**、universities、federal labs，以及 **nonprofit agencies**
-   Foreign firms（外国企业）

接着，他们还这样做：

> 对于 collaborations，我们选择将它们 attribute 给单个 organization，以避免 double counting。**如果某个 university 是某项 collaboration 中的 participant，那么无论其他 participants 是谁，该 innovation 都将被 attributed 给该 university。如果没有 university，但有 government lab 参与，那么该 innovation 就被 attributed 给该 lab。如果还有其他 public 或 nonprofit participant，该 innovation 就将被 attributed 给那个 participant**。如果所有 participants 都是 private，那么它就会被 coded 到 category 3 中。

这样，他们就得到了这张 chart

[![2016-02-03 09-51-47 的屏幕截图](https://nintil.com/where-do-innovations-really-come-from/03-1.png?w=630)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/02/03/where-do-innovations-really-come-from/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-02-03-09-51-47/#main)

![截图2](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/02/03-2.png)

让我们看看 2006 年的 R&D Magazine awards，自己做一次 count：

我的 methodology 是：Private firms（包括 foreign 的），加上 private firms collaborations，再加上 nonprofits，统称为 Private。National Labs 和 national labs collaborations 统称为 Labs。Universities 和 university collaborations 统称为 Universities。Labs 与 Universities 的 sum 称为 Public（尽管部分 universities 并不是 public）。

这样做后，对于 2006 年，**我们得到的是：53 项 awards 归于 private sector，27 项归于 public sector，27 项归于两者之间的 mixes**。（items 比应有的数量多了 2 项，但 +/2 并不会改变 results）。

这里 driving 这些 results 的，是他们奇特的 attribution policy，再加上将 SBIR firms 视为 'Mixed or Public'。

SBIR firms 从 government 那里 received 了 money，但决定要 pursue 哪些 projects，以及完成相应 work 的，都是那些 companies。如果我们有 evidence 表明，没有 SBIR funding，这些 innovations 就不会发生，我可以把它们 impute 给 Public/Mixed，而这会引导我们对 SBIR 本身进行 analysis。（我还没有做过完整的 review，但可参见 [Wallsten 2000](http://www.jstor.org/stable/2601030?seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents)）

让我们对 2015 年重复这一 analysis：这次我们得到的是 **private sector 有 61 项 innovations，public sector 有 26 项，mixed innovations 有 11 项。**

正确的 conclusion 是，常识终究是对的：按照 R&D Magazine 的 measured 方式，大多数 innovations 来自 private sector。这不应令人惊讶：US 的 private sector 占 R&D spending 的 70%，以及 basic science spending 的大约一半。

这里值得探讨的 questions 是：a) SBIR 在多大程度上有助于 foster innovation？b) 这些 innovations 除了从 engineering 的角度看很有意思之外，是否也是 productive？

Block-Keller 的 take-home point 并不是像 Mazzucato 错误理解的那样，public sector dominates US 的 innovative production，而是 collaborations 正变得更加重要。支持 State-led innovation 的 case 不能依赖 SBIR，[因为这个 programme](https://sbir.nih.gov/about/three-phase-program) 并不是 \_dirigisme， \_而是试图 identify private sector 已经在做的事情，并对其进行 nourishing。

_Ricón, José Luis，“innovations 究竟从哪里来？”，Nintil（2016-02-03），可见 https://nintil.com/where-do-innovations-really-come-from/._

## 原文

There's a document around by [Block & Keller (2011)](http://www.itif.org/files/Where_do_innovations_come_from.pdf) ("Where do innovations come from?") doing some very weird definitions.

Mariana Mazzucato:

> Given the leading developmental role the US government plays in a vast number of sectors, it is no surprise that at a more micro level, Block and Keller (2011b) found that between 1971 and 2006, 77 out of the most important 88 innovations (rated by R&D Magazine’s annual awards) – or 88 per cent – have been fully dependent on federal research support, especially, but not only, in their early phases – and the R&D Magazine’s award excludes ICT innovations.

Mazzucato says that Block & Keller say that 77 out of 88 inventions were fully dependent on federal research support, just that they received some federal funding.

Note that Mazzucato goes far ("fully dependent") beyond what Block & Keller say. The level of misinterpretation is even higher [here](https://gowans.wordpress.com/2012/12/21/do-publicly-owned-planned-economies-work/), and the findings are used to support not an entrepreneurial state but outright central economic planning.

Block & Keller have a peculiar way of calling something private or public:

-   Private: Fortune 500 firms, other firms, and inter-firm collaboration
-   Public **or mixed**: **Recent spinoffs supported by government**, universities, federal labs, and **nonprofit agencies**
-   Foreign firms

Then, they also do this:

> In the case of collaborations, we have chosen to attribute them to a single organization to avoid double counting. **If a university is a participant in a collaboration, the innovation will be attributed to the university regardless of other participants. If no university is present, but a government lab is involved, then the innovation is attributed to the lab. If there is another public or nonprofit participant, the innovation will be attributed to that participant**. If all participants are private, then it will be coded in category 3.

This way, they get this chart

![Captura2](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/02/03-2.png)

Let's go to the R&D Magazine awards for 2006 and do the count ourselves:

My methodology is: Private firms (including foreign), plus private firms collaborations, plus nonprofits will be called Private. National Labs, and national labs collaborations will be called Labs. Universities and university collaborations will be called Universities. The sum of Labs and Universities will be called Public (Altough some of the universities are not public).

Doing this, for the year 2006, **we get that 53 of the awards went to the private sector, 27 to the public sector, and 27 went to mixes between the two**. (There are 2 more items than there should be, but +/2 does not change the results).

What is driving the results here is their peculiar attribution policy, plus considering SBIR firms as 'Mixed or Public'.

SBIR firms received money from the government, but the decision of the projects to pursue, and the work done, was done by those companies. I could impute these innovations to Public/Mixed if we had evidence that they would not have taken place without the SBIR funding, which would lead us to an analysis of SBIR itself. (I haven't done a full review yet, but see [Wallsten 2000](http://www.jstor.org/stable/2601030?seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents))

Let's repeat the analysis for 2015: there we get **61 innovations for the private sector, 26 for the public sector, and 11 mixed innovations.**

The correct conclusion is that, after all, common sense is right, and most of the innovations as measured by the R&D Magazine come from the private sector. This should not come as a susprise: the private sector in the US accounts for 70% of R&D spending, and roughly half of basic science spending.

The interesting questions here would be to see a) How much does SBIR help foster innovation? b) Are these innovations productive, beyond being interesting from an engineering point of view?

The take-home point from Block-Keller is not that the public sector dominates the innovative production in the US, as Mazzucato mistakenly reads, but that collaborations are becoming more important. The case for State-led innovation cannot hinge upon SBIR, [as this programme](https://sbir.nih.gov/about/three-phase-program) is not \_dirigisme, \_but attempting to identify what the private sector is already doing, and nourishing it.
