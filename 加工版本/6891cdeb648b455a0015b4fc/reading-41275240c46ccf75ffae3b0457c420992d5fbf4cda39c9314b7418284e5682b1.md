# 过去我们在电力便宜的地方建钢厂，如今我们在那里建数据中心｜We used to build steel mills near cheap power. Now that's where we build datacenters

---

## 正文

为什么如今人们如此关注 hardware power consumption？这个问题的一些常见回答是，[power 对 phones、tablets 和 laptops 至关重要](http://www.vox.com/2015/3/9/8178213/apple-macbook-all-batteries)，以及[我们能够放在 modern chip 上的 silicon 比我们能够 effectively use 的更多](ftp://ftp.cs.utexas.edu/pub/dburger/papers/ISCA11.pdf)。在 2001 年，[Patrick Gelsinger 观察到，如果 scaling 继续保持当时的 rates](http://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/login.jsp?tp=&arnumber=912412&url=http%3A%2F%2Fieeexplore.ieee.org%2Fxpls%2Fabs_all.jsp%3Farnumber%3D912412)，chips 的 power density 到 2005 年就会达到 nuclear reactor 的水平，到 2010 年达到 rocket nozzle 的水平，到 2015 年达到太阳表面的水平，这意味着 power density 不可能继续沿着当时的轨迹发展。尽管这一点在当时就已经相当明显，但如今已经是 2015 年，我们可以更加确信，power density 并没有继续以 unbounded rates grow。无论如何，portables 的重要性和 scaling limits 都是成立且重要的原因，但由于它们已经被广泛讨论，我想谈谈一个被低估的原因。

人们经常关注 portable market，因为它正在 cannibalizing desktop market，但那并不是唯一的 growth market——servers 也正在变得比 desktops 更重要，而 power 对 servers 确实非常重要。要理解为什么 power 对 servers 很重要，我们来看看 [Hennessy & Patterson](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/012383872X/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&camp=1789&creative=9325&creativeASIN=012383872X&linkCode=as2&tag=abroaview-20&linkId=Y6Z2OBCUCR72ALEB) 对 run 一个 datacenter 所需 cost 的一些 calculations。

其中一个问题是，你要为 power 多次付费。一部分 power 在 substation 处 lost，尽管我们可能不必直接为此付费。随后，在 UPS 中 storing energy 时，我们又会 lose 更多。下图给出的数值是 6%，但 scale 较小的 datacenters 很容易 lose 其两倍。之后，在把 power stepping down 到 server 能够 accept 的 voltage 时，我们又会 lose 更多 power。对于一个相当 efficient 的 setup 来说，这就已经是超过 10% 的 loss。

之后，在 server 的 power supply 中，我们还会 lose 更多 power，因为需要把 voltage stepping down 到 computer 内部有用的 levels，这通常又是约 10% 的 loss（下图没有画出）。

![来自 Hennessy & Patterson 的 Power conversion 图，该图转载自 Hamilton](https://danluu.com/images/datacenter-power/power_conversion.webp)

接着，一旦我们把 power 送入 servers，它就会 turned into waste heat。为了防止 servers melting，我们必须付费购买 power 来 cool 它们。[Barroso and Holzle](http://www.morganclaypool.com/doi/abs/10.2200/S00193ED1V01Y200905CAC006) estimated，一个 datacenter 所 drawn 的 power 中，30%-50% 用于 chillers，另外 10%-20% 用于 [CRAC](http://www.dchuddle.com/2011/crac-v-crah/)（air circulation）。这意味着，server 中每 used 一 watt 的 power，我们就要再为 1-2 watts 的 support power 付费。

而要真正获得所有这些 power，我们还必须为将 power 送入 datacenter 并输送到其各处所需的 infrastructure 付费。Hennessy & Patterson estimate，在这 $90M cost of an example datacenter (just the facilities -- not the servers), 82% is associated with power and cooling[1](https://danluu.com/datacenter-power/#fn:E). The servers in the datacenter are estimated to only cost $70M 中。直接 compare 这些 numbers 并不公平，因为 servers 需要 replaced 的频率比 datacenters 更高；一旦把 datacenter 整个 lifetime 内的 cost 考虑在内，当 servers 的 lifetime 为 3 year、infrastructure 的 lifetime 为 10-15 year 时，power 和 cooling 的 amortized cost 就是 total cost 的 33%。

如果我们查看所有 costs，其 breakdown 如下：

| category | % |
| --- | --- |
| server machines | 53 |
| power & cooling infra | 20 |
| power use | 13 |
| networking | 8 |
| other infra | 4 |
| humans | 2 |

Power use 和人员是 operating datacenter 的 cost（OPEX），而 server machines、networking、power & cooling infra 和其他 infra 则是跨 datacenter 的 lifetime 进行 amortized 的 capital expenditures（CAPEX）。

Computation uses 大量 power。[我们过去在廉价 power sources 附近建造 steel mills](http://www.datacenterknowledge.com/archives/2010/05/19/microsoft-building-new-data-center-in-quincy/)，但[如今我们在那里建造的是 datacenters](http://www.datacenterknowledge.com/archives/2010/05/19/microsoft-building-new-data-center-in-quincy/)。随着公司开始考虑 applications 的 full cost，我们看到越来越多的 power optimized solutions[2](https://danluu.com/datacenter-power/#fn:1)。遗憾的是，这确实很难。在 software 方面，除了 toy microbenchmark examples 之外，[人们对于 writing power efficient code 的 best practices 仍然没有充分理解](http://arcade.cs.columbia.edu/energy-oopsla14.pdf)。在 hardware 方面，Intel 最近发布了新一代 chips，其 performance per watt 有 significantly improved，但 absolute performance 并没有比上一代好很多。在 hardware accelerator 方面，一些大公司正在为 specific computations building dedicated power-efficient hardware。但使用现有 tools 时，hardware accelerators 的 cost 高到只有最大的公司采用 dedicated hardware 才有意义。这个问题没有简单的答案。

如果你喜欢这篇文章，你可能也会喜欢 [Hennessy & Patterson](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/012383872X/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&camp=1789&creative=9325&creativeASIN=012383872X&linkCode=as2&tag=abroaview-20&linkId=Y6Z2OBCUCR72ALEB) 的第 6 章，其中不仅详细介绍了 power 的 cost，还介绍了许多与 datacenter performance 和 cost 有关的 back of the envelope calculations。

## 原文

Why are people so concerned with hardware power consumption nowadays? Some common answers to this question are that [power is critically important for phones, tablets, and laptops](http://www.vox.com/2015/3/9/8178213/apple-macbook-all-batteries) and that [we can put more silicon on a modern chip than we can effectively use](ftp://ftp.cs.utexas.edu/pub/dburger/papers/ISCA11.pdf). In 2001 [Patrick Gelsinger observed that if scaling continued at then-current rates](http://ieeexplore.ieee.org/xpl/login.jsp?tp=&arnumber=912412&url=http%3A%2F%2Fieeexplore.ieee.org%2Fxpls%2Fabs_all.jsp%3Farnumber%3D912412), chips would have the power density of a nuclear reactor by 2005, a rocket nozzle by 2010, and the surface of the sun by 2015, implying that power density couldn't continue on its then-current path. Although this was already fairly obvious at the time, now that it's 2015, we can be extra sure that power density didn't continue to grow at unbounded rates. Anyway, the importance of portables and scaling limits are both valid and important reasons, but since they're widely discussed, I'm going to talk about an underrated reason.

People often focus on the portable market because it's cannibalizing desktop market, but that's not the only growth market -- servers are also becoming more important than desktops, and power is really important for servers. To see why power is important for servers, let's look at some calculations about how what it costs to run a datacenter from [Hennessy & Patterson](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/012383872X/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&camp=1789&creative=9325&creativeASIN=012383872X&linkCode=as2&tag=abroaview-20&linkId=Y6Z2OBCUCR72ALEB).

One of the issues is that you pay for power multiple times. Some power is lost at the substation, although we might not have to pay for that directly. Then we lose more storing energy in a UPS. This figure below states 6%, but smaller scale datacenters can easily lose twice that. After that, we lose more power stepping down the power to a voltage that a server can accept. That's over a 10% loss for a setup that's pretty efficient.

After that, we lose more power in the server's power supply, stepping down the voltage to levels that are useful inside a computer, which is often about another 10% loss (not pictured in the figure below).

![Power conversion figure from Hennessy & Patterson, which reproduced the figure from Hamilton](https://danluu.com/images/datacenter-power/power_conversion.webp)

And then once we get the power into servers, it gets turned into waste heat. To keep the servers from melting, we have to pay for power to cool them. [Barroso and Holzle](http://www.morganclaypool.com/doi/abs/10.2200/S00193ED1V01Y200905CAC006) estimated that 30%-50% of the power drawn by a datacenter is used for chillers, and that an additional 10%-20% is for the [CRAC](http://www.dchuddle.com/2011/crac-v-crah/) (air circulation). That means for every watt of power used in the server, we pay for another 1-2 watts of support power.

And to actually get all this power, we have to pay for the infrastructure required to get the power into and throughout the datacenter. Hennessy & Patterson estimate that of the $90M cost of an example datacenter (just the facilities -- not the servers), 82% is associated with power and cooling[1](https://danluu.com/datacenter-power/#fn:E). The servers in the datacenter are estimated to only cost $70M. It's not fair to compare those numbers directly since servers need to get replaced more often than datacenters; once you take into account the cost over the entire lifetime of the datacenter, the amortized cost of power and cooling comes out to be 33% of the total cost, when servers have a 3 year lifetime and infrastructure has a 10-15 year lifetime.

If we look at all the costs, the breakdown is:

| category | % |
| --- | --- |
| server machines | 53 |
| power & cooling infra | 20 |
| power use | 13 |
| networking | 8 |
| other infra | 4 |
| humans | 2 |

Power use and people are the cost of operating the datacenter (OPEX), whereas server machines, networking, power & cooling infra, and other infra are capital expenditures that are amortized across the lifetime of the datacenter (CAPEX).

Computation uses a lot of power. [We used to build steel mills near cheap sources of power](http://www.datacenterknowledge.com/archives/2010/05/19/microsoft-building-new-data-center-in-quincy/), but [now that's where we build datacenters](http://www.datacenterknowledge.com/archives/2010/05/19/microsoft-building-new-data-center-in-quincy/). As companies start considering the full cost of applications, we're seeing a lot more power optimized solutions[2](https://danluu.com/datacenter-power/#fn:1). Unfortunately, this is really hard. On the software side, with the exceptions of toy microbenchmark examples, [best practices for writing power efficient code still aren't well understood](http://arcade.cs.columbia.edu/energy-oopsla14.pdf). On the hardware side, Intel recently released a new generation of chips with significantly improved performance per watt that doesn't have much better absolute performance than the previous generation. On the hardware accelerator front, some large companies are building dedicated power-efficient hardware for specific computations. But with existing tools, hardware accelerators are costly enough that dedicated hardware only makes sense for the largest companies. There isn't an easy answer to this problem.

If you liked this post, you'd probably like chapter 6 of [Hennessy & Patterson](http://www.amazon.com/gp/product/012383872X/ref=as_li_qf_sp_asin_il_tl?ie=UTF8&camp=1789&creative=9325&creativeASIN=012383872X&linkCode=as2&tag=abroaview-20&linkId=Y6Z2OBCUCR72ALEB), which walks through not only the cost of power, but a number of related back of the envelope calculations relating to datacenter performance and cost.
