## 正文

[![](https://michaelnotebook.com/assets/home.png)](https://michaelnotebook.com/index.html)

我指的是那些 beliefs：他们同时代的大多数人都坚信这些 beliefs 完全是 misguided，而且它们始终没有获得过什么广泛认同。

如果有一份清单，列出那些曾一度遭到严厉谴责、后来却被发现是 true 的事情，那会很有意思。这样的 examples 有很多。不过，这份清单并不是那样的清单。

我指的是关于 science 的 beliefs，而不是他们对 social issues 或 policy 的 opinions，也不是他们最喜欢的早餐麦片之类的东西。

我把时间界限定在 1900 年之后。Standards 已经发生了如此大的变化，以至于很难判断该如何看待更早那些不同寻常的 beliefs。Newton 是一位 alchemist，Kepler 是一位 astrologer；不过，我不知道这在他们同时代的人看来有多奇怪，也不想为此费工夫。

我不会列入当下的热点争议话题（例如 Covid）。

我主要关注的是 extraordinary scientists。他们并非全都获得过 Nobel Prizes，但都做出过 Nobel-caliber 或接近这一水平的 work。

那些在我看来不够明确的事情，我没有列入。Freeman Dyson 有时被描述为一位“climate change denialist”，但他在 primary literature 中反复而且 unambiguously 地表示，humans 正在 warming 这个 planet。他对 climate models 的信心确实不如许多 climate scientists，对 technological solutions 也更感兴趣，但 professional climate scientists 之间同样存在很大的 variation。我认为，说他持有大多数 climate scientists 不同意、而且常常认为非常 uninformed 的 opinions，是公允的；但仅就那个主要的 scientific（而非 policy）问题——humans 是否正在 warming 这个 earth——而言，他似乎认同 consensus。

我**完全不是**在说下面列出的这些人处于同等地位。我想，和 Turing 谈论 parapsychology 应该会很有意思；我也认为，Kary Mullis 关于 HIV/AIDS 的看法显然就是错的。Turing 只是顺带提到 parapsychology；Josephson 则写过多篇关于它的 papers。

为什么要写这份清单？主要是为了提醒我自己：(a) 富有想象力的人有时也会大错特错；(b) 总体而言，这也许是一件好事，即便这些具体的 examples 是错的。

-   Turing 相当认真地看待 parapsychology，并在他那篇关于 AI 的 foundational paper 中提到了它
-   Linus Pauling 是一位 Vitamin C enthusiast——这已经是很温和的说法了
-   Kary Mullis 是一位 HIV/AIDS denialist
-   Brian Josephson 写过大量关于 parapsychology 的 papers
-   Lynn Margulis 是一位 9/11 Truther。她有时也被描述为一位 HIV/AIDS denialist，不过这一点似乎不那么明确
-   Fred Hoyle 相信，许多 pandemics 的 origins 是从 comets 抵达的 viruses 和 bacteria
-   Hannes Alfvén 的 plasma cosmology
-   Pierre 和 Marie Curie 相信某种 spirit realm，也相信 seances
-   ???

## 原文

[![](https://michaelnotebook.com/assets/home.png)](https://michaelnotebook.com/index.html)

I mean beliefs that most of their contemporaries strongly believed were completely misguided, and which never gained any significant traction.

It would be fascinating to have a list of things which came under strong censure for a time, but which were later found to be true. There are many examples of such. That's not this list, however.

I mean beliefs about science, not their opinions on social issues, or policy, favorite breakfast cereals _etc_.

I set a cutoff of after 1900. Standards have changed so much that it's hard to know what to make of earlier unusual beliefs. Newton was an alchemist, and Kepler an astrologer; I don't know how strange that seemed to their contemporaties however, and don't want to put in the work.

I'm not listing contemporary hot button topics (e.g., Covid).

I've concentrated on extraordinary scientists. Not all have Nobel Prizes, but all have done Nobel-caliber or close work.

I haven't listed things that seem to me less than clearcut. Freeman Dyson is sometimes described as a "climate change denialist", but in primary literature he repeatedly and unambiguously says humans are warming the planet. He certainly had less faith in climate models than many climate scientists, and more interest in technological solutions, but there's a lot of variation among professional climate scientists, too. I think you could fairly say he had opinions most climate scientists disagreed with, and often considered very uninformed, but on just the main scientific (as opposed to policy) point, are humans warming the earth, he seemed to agree with the consensus.

I am **not at all** saying that the people listed below are on an equal footing. I suspect it would have been very interesting to talk to Turing about parapsychology; I suspect Kary Mullis was just obviously wrong about HIV/AIDS. Turing mentions parapsychology in passing; Josephson has written multiple papers about it.

Why write this list? Mostly as a reminder to myself that: (a) imaginative people can sometimes be very wrong; (b) this may, on net, be a positive, even if these particular examples are wrong.

-   Turing took parapsychology pretty seriously, mentioning it in his foundational paper about AI
-   Linus Pauling was a Vitamin C enthusiast, to put it mildly
-   Kary Mullis was a HIV/AIDS denialist
-   Brian Josephson has written numerous papers about parapsychology
-   Lynn Margulis was a 9/11 Truther. She is sometimes described as a HIV/AIDS denialist, though it seems less clear
-   Fred Hoyle believed many pandemics had their origins in viruses and bacteria arriving from comets
-   Hannes Alfvén's plasma cosmology
-   Pierre and Marie Curie believed in some kind of spirit realm, and seances
