2013 年 11 月

1.  感谢妈妈，她教会我热爱 words。
2.  感谢爸爸，他教会我热爱 math。
3.  感谢 Jan 奶奶，她总是确保我玩得开心。
4.  感谢 Kay 奶奶，她有许多精彩的故事。
5.  感谢 Ken 爷爷，他送的礼物总是最棒的；
6.  其中包括我的 electric keyboard，它已经好几次让我免于精神崩溃。
7.  感谢 Tom 爷爷，我从未真正有机会了解他，但他写过很棒的书。
8.  感谢我出生时便拥有的、优越得不可思议的条件——无论是 wealth、health、education，还是 social status。
9.  感谢我有机会就读 [Commonwealth School](http://www.commschool.org/)，在那里，我学会了如何真正付出努力——在 academic、social 和 physical 各方面——也感谢那里所有出色的老师：
10.  比如 Barbara Grant，她做了我四年的 advisor，始终关照着我；
11.  Catherine Brewster，她教会我如何 write；
12.  Melissa Haber，一位思想深刻的 historian，也是一位温暖的朋友；
13.  Mara Dale，她第一次让我喜欢上了 English；
14.  Brent Whelan，我读过的最好的 books 和 poems，都是他教的；
15.  Jean Segaloff，在申请大学的那段时间，她的 ceramics class 让我得以从想得太多的状态中获得急需的喘息；
16.  David Hodgkins，他教会我如何 sing；
17.  Darryl Hollister，我曾与他一起为我们的 choir 演奏 Liebeslieder Waltzes，那无疑是我经历过的要求最严格、也最有收获的 musical experience；
18.  Don Conolly，他实在太棒了，连我们所有人都受到了感染；
19.  还有 [Farhad Riahi](http://www.commschool.org/page.cfm?p=847)，在所有于 regime change 后遭到 exiled、转而开始教 high school physics 的 hexalingual university Vice-Chancellor 中，他无疑是最有绅士风度、最风趣、最博览群书的一位。
20.  感谢 Commonwealth 的 Latin 学生们那次 Greece 之旅，我在那里学会了以一种真正适合自己的方式与人交往，也在一段 social isolation 之后重新融入了同年级的同学之中。
21.  感谢我在 Harvard 录取这场碰运气的赌局中胜出，也感谢我在那里遇到的令人振奋的课程和老师：
22.  比如 Physics 16 和 Math 55 这样的课程，它们不仅让我接触到了 challenging math problems，也让我认识了未来的 foommates；
23.  还有 David Benjamin 这样的老师，他精湛的 physics sections 和出色的 throat singing 令我们所有人着迷，
24.  还有 Margo Seltzer，她真正关心自己的学生，因此愿意在 flipped classroom 中，以 teaching 本该有的方式来 teach。
25.  感谢那些出色的同学和朋友，过去七年里几乎所有的时光，我都有幸与他们一起度过：
26.  比如 Ethan，他几乎是在潜移默化中教会了我 philosophy；
27.  Emma，她的聪慧和坚韧激励着我；
28.  Gautam，无论是虚拟世界还是真实世界，他都是我最喜欢的冒险伙伴；
29.  Michael，他对我的榜样作用，恐怕比他自己意识到的还要大；
30.  Billy，感谢他和我一起做那些异想天开的项目；
31.  Rahul，他总能让我保持诚实；
32.  Melanie，自从两年前相识以来，她始终微笑着、温暖地包容我的胡闹；
33.  John，他的主动和热情，大概拯救了 Harvard 的 effective altruism；
34.  Aaron，最好的 rationality 伙伴；
35.  Ruthie，她在各方面都令人赞叹；
36.  也感谢过去两年里所有与我交谈、与我同病相怜、让我向其学习、和我一起赶 p-sets，以及平日一起玩耍的人。
37.  感谢我在成长过程中住过一个 [co-op](http://www.arlingtonfriendshouse.org/)，在那里，我没有亲兄弟姐妹，却先后拥有了一群很酷的哥哥姐姐：他们年长到不会与我打架，却又没年长到不能教我东西。
38.  感谢我的室友：Chen-I，她让我重新想起 piano music 有多棒；
39.  还有 Arun，他借给我最初的几本 Dungeons and Dragons 书，让我从此踏上了超级宅人的道路；
40.  Max，他教会我如何与熊一起 waltz，也激发了我对 wordplay 的直觉；
41.  当然还有 Bree，她教会了我如何 contra dance，从而以许多美妙的方式彻底改变了我的生活。
42.  感谢我的 piano teacher [Donna Gross Javel](http://www.anythingpiano.com/)，她准确地知道什么时候该督促我，什么时候该让我偷懒玩闹。
43.  感谢 Bob Kaplan，他教 extracurricular math，似乎从来不曾讲授什么，却能引导八年级学生提出最 complex、最 insightful 的 proofs。
44.  感谢我的三年级老师 Jeanne Wall，她不知怎么就让八岁的我学会了与人相处，还让我与自己的死对头成了朋友。
45.  感谢 [Peter Singer](http://www.princeton.edu/~psinger/)，没有他，我绝不会对 effective altruism 产生兴趣；
46.  也感谢 [Julia Wise](http://www.givinggladly.com/) 和 [Jeff Kaufman](http://www.jefftk.com/index)，没有他们，我绝不会更深入地参与其中。
47.  感谢 math，它的 [unreasonable effectiveness](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unreasonable_Effectiveness_of_Mathematics_in_the_Natural_Sciences) 不断让我欣喜。
48.  感谢 computer science，它的 unreasonable usefulness 不断让世界变得更好。
49.  感谢 poetry，我完全不知道人们是怎么想出它的，但无论如何，它都很棒。
50.  感谢 piano，这是我最喜欢的放松方式。
51.  感谢 singing，以及它所创造的美妙的 group art。
52.  尤其感谢 rounds，它是最容易创造的此类 art。
53.  还要感谢 Christmas carols，对我来说，它们简直就是一个巨大的怀旧按钮。
54.  还要感谢 folk music——数百年的 memetic selection，其力量不可小觑。
55.  （也感谢一般意义上的 selection processes，即使它们像瞎眼的傻瓜一样行事，也能 produce 相当精妙的东西。）
56.  还要感谢我的 music books：[Portland Collections](http://portlandcollection.net/) 和 [Waltz Books](http://www.bfv.com/waltz/)。
57.  感谢 contra dancing，这是穿着衣服能享受到的最大乐趣，也是一个笨拙的初中生建立自信、build coordination 和结交朋友的最佳方式。
58.  感谢 folk festivals，它们让人在节奏快得要命的学期中获得理想的休息。
59.  也感谢它们必不可少的伴侣——同样能缓解压力的一大群人挤在一起拥抱。
60.  感谢高山，以及山顶壮丽的景色。
61.  感谢树木——无论哪个季节——它们都让任何风景显得更加亲切。
62.  感谢 fencing，我从中学会了如何把一件事做好，继而学会了如何在做得好时依然保持风度。
63.  感谢我的 coaches，[Elif 和 Jason](http://www.moefencingclub.com/Moe_Wen_Fencing_Club/Moe_Wen_Fencing_Club_Somerville.html)，就连我这样笨手笨脚、好胜过头、责任心不足的傻小子，他们都能培养成一个优秀的 fencer。
64.  感谢 Internet，没有它，你就不会读到这些。也感谢它所孕育的事物，包括：
65.  Wikipedia，它无需任何赞词。
66.  Library Genesis，我经常如饥似渴地读着上面的 nonfiction。
67.  Amazon，它让我省去了许多趟长途奔波。
68.  Facebook，尽管有些地方让人恼火，它确实做到了不少很酷的事情，而且你很可能就是从那里来到这里的。
69.  Google，它已经如此深入地 integrated 进我的 extended brain，以至于我都忘了没有它时自己有多么束手无策。
70.  各种形式的 video chat，尽管没有哪一种特别好用，却让我与远方所有的朋友保持亲近。
71.  [Slate Star Codex](http://slatestarcodex.com/)，我曾在一天里不知不觉读完了相当于两部长篇小说的内容。
72.  [Ribbonfarm](http://www.ribbonfarm.com/)，它是纯粹、高浓度的 [insight porn](http://theviewfromhell.blogspot.com/2012/09/trying-to-see-through-unified-theory-of.html) 的最佳来源。
73.  [Ian’s Shoelace Site](http://www.fieggen.com/shoelace/)，它凭借先进的 shoelace techniques，为我省下了_实实在在的几分钟_。
74.  当然还有 [Less Wrong](http://lesswrong.com/)，它大概是让我变聪明最多的东西。
75.  还有 CFAR，我所知道的最可靠的美好事物源泉。
76.  也感谢 Cambridge Less Wrong meetup，那里满是很酷的人和快乐的时光。
77.  感谢 _Godel, Escher, Bach_，那时的我大概太年轻，无法真正领会它，但它还是引导我走上了一条充满美好事物的道路。
78.  感谢 _The Elegance of the Hedgehog_，因为舍不得放下它，我撞上过不少电线杆。
79.  感谢 _Dubliners_，我确信它让我成为了更好的人，尽管我不太能说清究竟是怎么做到的。
80.  感谢我读过的 Tolstoy 的所有作品，它们的精彩程度，从“让人幸福得在座位上蹦起来”，一直到“指出关于 nature of humanity 的 fundamental insights”。
81.  感谢 _Braid_，尽管它是一个 video game，却同样是一件 work of art。
82.  感谢 [Crocker’s Rules](http://www.sl4.org/crocker.html)，它让我收获了许多很棒的 feedback，这或许有些出人意料。
83.  感谢 public transit，我们都爱抱怨它，但说实话，它真的相当酷。
84.  还要感谢 airplanes，它们仍然时不时让我冒出这样的念头：“这东西到底是怎么运作的？”以及“人类啊，你们也太离谱了。”
85.  更要感谢那些不那么显眼的东西——engineers 为之呕心沥血、藏在幕后的那些不可思议的东西；正因为它们把自己的工作做得太好，我们甚至都不知道它们存在。
86.  感谢 demographic transition，我还活着就是因为它。
87.  同样，也感谢所有我没有患上的、可怕至极的 diseases。
88.  感谢 modern pedagogy，我们爱抱怨它，但说真的，五十年前，人们还在大学二三年级学习 calculus，而现在我们已经在把它教给高二学生了。
89.  感谢 cities，它们让这份清单上几乎所有的事情成为可能。
90.  感谢 blogs，也感谢它们如此彻底地 improved 了围绕所有有趣事物的 discourse。
91.  感谢 open source software，从 OS kernel 往上的一切，这篇 post 正是依靠它们 written 和 served 的。
92.  感谢 board games，有了它们，连我都能举办“聚会”。
93.  感谢写这些文字时一直坐到我身上的猫咪们。
94.  还有几英尺外那只正在打呼噜的狗。
95.  感谢 gratitude lists 显然能让人更快乐这件事。
96.  也感谢其他所有出色而且 [surprisingly replicable](http://www.nature.com/news/psychologists-strike-a-blow-for-reproducibility-1.14232) 的 psychology research。
97.  感谢这个学期只剩下不到两周了。
98.  也感谢在这两周结束前，我还能学到的所有很酷的 math。
99.  感谢明天即将吃到的美味饭菜。
100.  也感谢这样的清单我还能再写 99 份，而且依然有写不完的内容。

November 2013

1.  For my mom, who taught me to love words.
2.  For my dad, who taught me to love math.
3.  For grandma Jan, who always makes sure I’m having fun.
4.  For grandma Kay, who has great stories.
5.  For grandpa Ken, who gives the best gifts;
6.  including my electric keyboard, which has saved my sanity several times over.
7.  For grandpa Tom, who I never really got to know, but he wrote awesome books.
8.  For the ridiculously privileged circumstances I was born in—both in wealth, health, education, and social status.
9.  For getting to go to [Commonwealth School](http://www.commschool.org/), where I learned how to do real work—academic, social, and physical—and for all the awesome teachers there:
10.  like Barbara Grant, my advisor of four years, who always looked out for me;
11.  Catherine Brewster, who taught me how to write;
12.  Melissa Haber, a thoughtful historian and warm friend;
13.  Mara Dale, who first got me to enjoy English;
14.  Brent Whelan, who taught the best books and poems I’ve read;
15.  Jean Segaloff, whose ceramics class provided a much-needed break from overthinking during college season;
16.  David Hodgkins, who taught me how to sing;
17.  Darryl Hollister, with whom I played the Liebeslieder Waltzes with our choir, undoubtedly the strictest and most rewarding musical experience I’ve had;
18.  Don Conolly, who was so awesome that it rubbed off on all of us;
19.  and [Farhad Riahi](http://www.commschool.org/page.cfm?p=847), unquestionably the most gentlemanly, witty, and well-read hexalingual university Vice-Chancellor ever to be exiled after a regime change and start teaching high school physics.
20.  For the Commonwealth Latin students’ trip to Greece, where I learned how to be social in a way that actually worked for me, and re-integrated into my grade after some social isolation.
21.  For winning the crap-shoot that is admission to Harvard, and the exciting classes and teachers I’ve had there:
22.  classes like Physics 16 and Math 55, which introduced me not only to challenging math problems but also to my future foommates;
23.  and teachers like David Benjamin, whose masterful physics sections and excellent throat singing captivated us all,
24.  and Margo Seltzer, who actually cares enough about her students to teach as teaching was meant to be done, in a flipped classroom.
25.  For the awesome classmates and friends that I’ve been lucky enough to spend almost all of the last seven years with:
26.  like Ethan, who practically taught me philosophy by osmosis;
27.  Emma, who inspired me with her intelligence and grit;
28.  Gautam, my favorite buddy for adventures, both virtual and real;
29.  Michael, who was more of a role model for me than he probably realized;
30.  Billy, for doing harebrained projects with me;
31.  Rahul, who always keeps me honest;
32.  Melanie, who’s put up with my antics with smiles and warmth since we met two years ago;
33.  John, whose initiative and excitement probably saved effective altruism at Harvard;
34.  Aaron, the best rationality buddy;
35.  Ruthie, who’s all-around amazing;
36.  and for everyone else I’ve talked to, commiserated with, learned from, churned out p-sets with, and generally hung out with over the past two years.
37.  For having lived in a [co-op](http://www.arlingtonfriendshouse.org/) growing up, where instead of having brothers and sisters, I had a succession of cool older siblings who were too old to fight with, but not too old to teach me stuff.
38.  For my housemate: Chen-I, who reminded me that piano music is awesome;
39.  and Arun, who lent me my first Dungeons and Dragons books, setting me on my path to uber-nerd-dom;
40.  Max, who taught me how to waltz with bears and inspired my instinct for wordplay;
41.  and of course Bree, who taught me how to contra dance, thus changing my life radically in a many awesome ways.
42.  For [Donna Gross Javel](http://www.anythingpiano.com/), my piano teacher, who knew exactly when to push me and when to let me goof off.
43.  For Bob Kaplan, who taught extracurricular math, and drew forth the most complex and insightful proofs from eighth graders without ever seeming to lecture anything.
44.  For Jeanne Wall, my third-grade teacher, who somehow managed to socialize eight-year-old me, and also got me to befriend my arch-nemesis.
45.  For [Peter Singer](http://www.princeton.edu/~psinger/), without whom I’d never have gotten interested in effective altruism;
46.  and for [Julia Wise](http://www.givinggladly.com/) and [Jeff Kaufman](http://www.jefftk.com/index), without whom I’d never have gotten more involved.
47.  For math, whose [unreasonable effectiveness](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unreasonable_Effectiveness_of_Mathematics_in_the_Natural_Sciences) continues to delight me.
48.  For computer science, whose unreasonable usefulness continues to make the world better.
49.  For poetry, which I have no idea how anyone came up with, but which is awesome anyway.
50.  For piano, my favorite way to decompress.
51.  For singing, and the wonderful group art it creates.
52.  Especially for rounds, the easiest such art to make.
53.  And for Christmas carols, which are pretty much a giant nostalgia button for me.
54.  And for folk music—hundreds of years of memetic selection is a force to be reckoned with.
55.  (For selection processes in general, which produce pretty nifty stuff, even if they’re blind idiots about it.)
56.  And for my music books, the [Portland Collections](http://portlandcollection.net/) and the [Waltz Books](http://www.bfv.com/waltz/).
57.  For contra dancing, the most fun you can have with your clothes on, and the best way for an awkward middle schooler to get confident, build coordination, and make friends.
58.  For folk festivals, the ideal break from a breakneck semester.
59.  And their inevitable companion, with similar stress-relief properties, the giant cuddle pile.
60.  For mountains, and the glorious views from the top.
61.  For trees—in any season—that make any landscape more welcoming.
62.  For fencing, from which I learned how be good at something, and then how to be good gracefully.
63.  For my coaches, [Elif and Jason](http://www.moefencingclub.com/Moe_Wen_Fencing_Club/Moe_Wen_Fencing_Club_Somerville.html), who could make a good fencer even out of a klutzy, over-competitive, under-responsible schmuck like me.
64.  For the Internet, without which you wouldn’t be reading this. And for its creations, including:
65.  Wikipedia, which needs no epithet.
66.  Library Genesis, on whose nonfiction I frequently gorge.
67.  Amazon, which has saved me many a long trip.
68.  Facebook, which despite its annoyances does some pretty cool stuff, and which probably referred you here.
69.  Google, which is so well integrated into my extended brain that I forget how hobbled I am without it.
70.  All the various forms of video chat, which (despite none working very well) keep me close to all my friends from afar.
71.  [Slate Star Codex](http://slatestarcodex.com/), from which I once read two novels’ worth in a day without realizing it.
72.  [Ribbonfarm](http://www.ribbonfarm.com/), the best source of pure freebased [insight porn](http://theviewfromhell.blogspot.com/2012/09/trying-to-see-through-unified-theory-of.html).
73.  [Ian’s Shoelace Site](http://www.fieggen.com/shoelace/), which has saved me _literally minutes_ with its advanced shoelace techniques.
74.  And of course [Less Wrong](http://lesswrong.com/), probably the thing which has made me the most smarter.
75.  And CFAR, the most reliable fountain of awesome that I know of.
76.  Also for the Cambridge Less Wrong meetup, which is full of cool people and fun times.
77.  For _Godel, Escher, Bach_, which I was probably too young to really grok, but which led me down a path of good things anyway.
78.  For _The Elegance of the Hedgehog_, for whose sake I walked into many telephone poles because I couldn’t put it down.
79.  For _Dubliners_, which I’m convinced made me a better person, though I can’t quite articulate how.
80.  For everything I’ve read by Tolstoy, which varies in awesomeness from “evokes so much happiness I’m bouncing in my seat” to “points out fundamental insights about the nature of humanity.”
81.  For _Braid_, which is just as much of a work of art, despite being a video game.
82.  For [Crocker’s Rules](http://www.sl4.org/crocker.html), which (maybe surprisingly) got me lots of awesome feedback.
83.  For public transit, which we all love to complain about, but honestly, it’s pretty darn cool.
84.  And for airplanes, which still every so often give me those “how on earth does this stuff work” and “what the hell, humans” moments.
85.  And even more for the stuff that isn’t as obvious—for all the ridculous behind-the-scenes stuff that engineers slaved over that we never even know exists, because it’s doing its job so well.
86.  For the demographic transition, which is why I’m not dead.
87.  And similarly, for all the incredibly awful diseases I don’t have.
88.  For modern pedagogy, which we love to complain about, but seriously, fifty years ago people were learning calculus in their second or third year of college and now we’re teaching it to high school juniors.
89.  For cities, which made pretty much everything on this list possible.
90.  For blogs and how radically they improved discourse on everything interesting.
91.  For open source software, on which this post was written and served, from the OS kernel on up.
92.  For board games, through which even I can host “parties.”
93.  For the cats that keep sitting on me as I write this.
94.  And the dog that’s snoring a few feet away.
95.  For the fact that gratitude lists apparently make you happier.
96.  And for all the other awesome and [surprisingly replicable](http://www.nature.com/news/psychologists-strike-a-blow-for-reproducibility-1.14232) psychology research.
97.  For the fact that there’s barely two weeks left in the semester.
98.  And for all the cool math I’ll get to learn before they’re over.
99.  For the delicious meals I’m going to eat tomorrow.
100.  And for the fact that I could write another 99 of these lists without running out.
