# Por qué el voto obligatorio es una muy mala idea

---

## 正文

不久前，Obama 发表了一些言论，流露出对强制投票提案的某种支持：所有成年公民都有投票的义务，否则可能面临罚款等处罚。

在 Bleeding Heart Libertarians 上，哲学家 Jason Brennan 去年已经[驳斥了](https://www.amazon.com/Compulsory-Voting-Against-Jason-Brennan/dp/1107613922/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&qid=1426857221&sr=8-1&keywords=compulsory+voting+for+and+against&tag=bleedheartlib-20)支持这项提案的论点，如今他又[批评了 Obama 的论证。按照 Brennan 的说法，这一论证可以表述如下：](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2015/03/the-demographic-argument-for-compulsory-voting-with-a-guest-appearance-by-the-real-reason-the-left-advocates-compulsory-voting/)

1.  事实上，在 voluntary voting regime 下，voting electorate——那些选择 vote 的人——最终呈现出的样貌与 population at large 不同。White、male、middle-aged and older、high income、educated、employed、married 或 wealthy 的人，比 non-white、female、young、low income、uneducated、unemployed、unmarried 和 poor 的人更有可能 vote。
2.  如果是这样，那么在 voluntary voting regime 下，voting public 就 disproportionately 由那些已经 advantaged 的人构成。disadvantaged 并不按照其 actual population 的 proportion 来 vote。
3.  Government 往往会给人们他们想要的东西。
4.  如果 1-3 成立，那么 government 就会倾向于 respond to 那些已经 advantaged 的人的 interests，并倾向于 ignore 甚至 undermine 那些 disadvantaged 的人的 interests。
5.  Compulsory voting 让所有人都 vote，并 ensures proportional voting。
6.  因此，compulsory voting 将会 result in fair and just government，而且是 justified 的。

这个论证有什么问题？按照 Brennan 的说法，根本问题在于，它假定人们会按照自身利益投票，然而

> 我们有来自数百项 studies 的 overwhelming empirical evidence，表明他们并不 vote their self-interest。相反，他们会 vote altruistically，为他们认为符合 national interest 的东西投票。因此，我们是否需要 protect poor voters from high income voters，并不是显而易见的，因为 high income voters 已经在尝试 vote on behalf of poor voters。

此外，事实是，较贫困的人往往对政治或经济问题了解得更少：

> 要知道该 vote for 谁，仅仅知道不同 politicians advocate 什么 political policies 是不够的。人们还需要知道，这些 politicians 是否有任何希望 implementing 那些 policies，以及那些 policies 的 likely effect 会是什么。因此，人们需要大量 social scientific knowledge。在我们的 voluntary voting regime 下，大多数 voters 缺乏这种 knowledge，但当前的 non-voters 拥有的这种 knowledge 更少。

而这种知识差异会影响投票

> 顺便说一句，这种 knowledge 会让人们 advocate 的内容大不相同。正如 Scott Althaus、Bryan Caplan 和 Martin Gilens 所 shown 的那样（他们各自 using independent sets of data），low information 和 high information 的人拥有 systematically different political beliefs，而这些 differences 并不能由他们的 demographic differences 来 explained。因此，例如，high information Democrats favor free trade，而 low information Democrats 则不 favor free trade；即使我们 correct for income 或其他对 political belief 的 demographic effects，这一点仍然 holds。

但如果我们强迫他们投票，他们难道不会更主动地了解情况吗？

> Conjecturing 确实很有趣。但遗憾的是，已经有不少关于这个问题的 empirical studies，考察了各种 natural experiments，而答案是否定的：compulsory voting 并不会 cause uninformed voters 变得更加 better informed。

Brennan 指出，左翼中有人支持这类提案，理由是这样一来，投票较少的穷人的偏好就能得到更充分的代表，但事实也并非如此：

> 但再说一次，这并不对。有办法 studying 这个问题，而结果表明，compulsory voting 几乎没有 partisan effects。在她的[对所有现有 empirical work 的 review 中，Sarah Birch](https://www.amazon.com/Full-Participation-Comparative-Compulsory-Voting/dp/9280811665?tag=bleedheartlib-20)也 finds，compulsory voting 对 partisan outcomes 几乎没有 effect，唯一可能的例外是，它会帮助 far right wing nationalist parties 在 proportional voting regimes 中获得几个 seats。

就美国而言，强制投票不会改变谁赢得选举，却会改变获胜政党所主张的内容。较贫困的民主党人掌握的信息更少，而这个社会群体，例如，曾更支持伊拉克战争、侵犯公民自由的举措、保护主义、限制堕胎权和获取避孕用品。这样一来，偏好的确会得到更充分的代表，因为所有人都表达了意见。但结果会明显更糟。

Brennan 文章中最引人注目的说法，大概就是人们投票时并不受自私动机驱使。围绕这一点，这位 Georgetown 大学的哲学家在结尾提到了自己的书，读者可以从中确认，实证证据确实支持他的观点，

## 原文

Hace poco Obama dejó caer ciertas declaraciones en las que se entreveía cierto apoyo para la propuesta del voto obligatorio: que todo ciudadano en edad adulta tenga la obligación de votar, so pena, por ejemplo, de multa.

En Bleeding Heart Libertarians, el filósofo Jason Brennan, que ya [refutó](https://www.amazon.com/Compulsory-Voting-Against-Jason-Brennan/dp/1107613922/ref=sr_1_1?ie=UTF8&qid=1426857221&sr=8-1&keywords=compulsory+voting+for+and+against&tag=bleedheartlib-20) los argumentos en defensa de esta propuesta el año pasado [critica el argumento de Obama, que puede formularse de la siguiente manera, según Brennan:](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2015/03/the-demographic-argument-for-compulsory-voting-with-a-guest-appearance-by-the-real-reason-the-left-advocates-compulsory-voting/)

1.  As a matter of fact, under a voluntary voting regime, the voting electorate–the people who choose to vote–ends up looking different from the population at large. White, male, middle-aged and older, high income, educated, employed, married, or wealthy people are much more likely to vote than non-white, female, young, low income, uneducated, unemployed, unmarried, and poor people.
2.  If so, then under a voluntary voting regime, the voting public is made up disproportionately of people who are already advantaged. The disadvantaged don’t vote in proportion to their actual population.
3.  Government tends to give people what they want.
4.  If 1-3, then government will tend to respond to the interests of the already advantaged, and tend to ignore or even undermine the interests of the disadvantaged.
5.  Compulsory voting makes everyone vote and ensures proportional voting.
6.  Therefore, compulsory voting will result in fair and just government, and is justified.

¿Qué tiene de errado este argumento? Según Brennan, básicamente que asume que la gente vota según su propio interés, pero

> we have overwhelming empirical evidence, drawn from hundreds of studies, that they don’t vote their self-interest. Instead, they vote altruistically, for what they perceive to be in the national interest. So, it’s not obvious that we need to protect poor voters from high income voters, because high income voters are already trying to vote on behalf of poor voters.

Además, resulta ser el caso que la gente más humilde suele estar peor informada en temas de política o economía:

> To know whom to vote for, it is not enough to know what political policies different politicians advocate. One also needs to know whether those politicians have any hope of implementing those policies, and what the likely effect of those policies would be. One thus needs massive amounts of social scientific knowledge. In our voluntary voting regime, most voters lack this knowledge, but current non-voters have even less of it.

Y esta diferencia de conocimiento importa a la hora de votar

> By the way, this knowledge makes a big difference in what people advocate. As Scott Althaus, Bryan Caplan, and Martin Gilens have shown (each using independent sets of data), low information and high information people have systematically different political beliefs, and these differences are not explained by their demographic differences. So, for instance, high information Democrats favor free trade, while low information Democrats don’t, and this holds even after we correct for income or other demographic effects on political belief.

¿Pero y si les obligamos a votar no se informarán mejor?

> Conjecturing sure is fun. But, alas, there are a bunch of empirical studies on this looking at various natural experiments, and the answer is no, compulsory voting doesn’t cause uninformed voters to become any better informed.

Apunta Brennan que gente en la izquierda está a favor de este tipo de propuestas basándose en el razonamiento de que así los pobres, que votan menos, podrán tener mejor representadas sus preferencias, pero tampoco:

> But, again, that’s wrong. There are ways of studying this, and it turns out that compulsory voting has few partisan effects. In her [review of all the extant empirical work, Sarah Birch](https://www.amazon.com/Full-Participation-Comparative-Compulsory-Voting/dp/9280811665?tag=bleedheartlib-20)also finds that compulsory voting has little to no effect on partisan outcomes, except, perhaps, that it helps far right wing nationalist parties get a couple seats in proportional voting regimes.

En el caso de EEUU, el voto obligatorio no cambiaría quién gana las elecciones, pero sí qué defenderían los partidos que las ganasen. Los demócratas más pobres están peor informados, y este grupo social estaban, por ejemplo, más a favor de la guerra de Irak, de invasiones de la libertad civil, el proteccionismo, la restricción de derechos de aborto y de acceso a contraceptivos. Así tendrías preferencias mejor representadas, en tanto todos habrían opinado. Pero el resultado sería notoriamente peor.

Seguramente la afirmación más llamativa del artículo de Brennan sea que la gente no vota guiada por su egoísmo, y en este sentido, el filósofo de la universidad de Georgetown cierra aludiendo a su libro, donde uno podría comprobar que efectivamente, la empiria le da la razón,
