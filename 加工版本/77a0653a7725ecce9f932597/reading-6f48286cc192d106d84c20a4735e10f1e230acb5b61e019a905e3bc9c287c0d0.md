# Russia without Stalin, Venezuela without Chávez, Cuba without Castro

---

## 正文

如果 Russia、Venezuela 和 Cuba 没有标题中提到的 leaders，会发生什么？

每个 country 都有一篇 paper 试图回答这个非常棘手的问题。先透露一下结论：这些 countries 本来会过得更好。

[Stalin 对 Russia 的 economic development 是必要的吗？](http://www.voxeu.org/article/stalin-and-soviet-industrialisation) (2013)，作者为 Anton Cheremukhin、Mikhail Golosov、Sergei Guriev 和 Aleh Tsyvinski。（[完整 paper](http://economics.mit.edu/files/8702)）

> 因此，我们对“Stalin 是 Necessary 的吗？”这一问题的回答是明确的“不”。即使我们不考虑 famine、repression 和 terror 所带来的人间悲剧，而只 focus on economic outcomes，甚至在我们所作的 assumptions 是 biased in Stalin’s favour 时，他的 economic policies 仍然 underperform 那个 counterfactual。我们认为，Stalin 的 industrialisation 不应该被当作 development economics 中的 success story，而应该被 studied 为这样一个 example：brutal reallocation resulted in lower productivity 和 lower social welfare。

[Hugo Chavez 的 economic consequences：一项 synthetic control analysis](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268116000020) (2016)，作者为 Kevin Grieg 和 Norman Maynard

> 我们 find，尽管在他担任 president 期间 average incomes 有所 rose，但它们仍然 lagged far behind 如果 Chavez 没有 taken office 时可能达到的水平。在他的 leadership 期间，life expectancy grew，而 mortality 和 poverty rates fell。然而，我们没有 find 任何 evidence 表明，这些 gains 与另一位 national leader 执政时本来会出现的 gains 有任何 different。真要说有什么不同，life expectancy 的 rose 反而比 control 所 predicted 的更慢。从有利的一面来看，2003 之后，inequality 的 fell 或许比没有 Chavez in office 时本来会出现的速度更快；但我们没有看到任何 evidence 表明，他整个 term in office 对 inequality 的 reduced 比没有他时本来会出现的程度更多。虽然这些 results 并不是在一个 complete simultaneous model 中 produced 的，但它们 suggest，所 observed 的 inequality 和 poverty 的 reductions 不应该 attributed to Chavez 的 leadership，而 Chavez 的 legacy 可能更多地与对 per capita incomes 所造成的 harm 有关。

[Cuban experiment：使用 synthetic control 来 measuring 1959 revolution 对 economic performance 的 role](http://economics.ca/2013/papers/SG0030-1.pdf) (2013)，作者为 Felipe Garcia Ribeiro、Guilherme Stein 和 Thomas H. Kang

> 我们 claimed，正如许多 scholars 已经做过的那样，由 Revolution 所 caused 的 institutional change 是 1959 以来 divergence 的 main cause。我们还 dismissed 了以下 claims：war、human capital、economic embargo，以及 Cuban government 所 pursued 的 economic policies，是 Cuban economic failure 的 main causes。后一个 hypothesis 或许可以是一种 explanation，但显然，Castro 所 undertaken 的 economic policies 是 erratic 的，因为它们全部都由于缺乏 market mechanisms 而 failed。$$...$$ 通过终结 market mechanisms 和 collectivization process 而发生的 Institutional change，很可能是这个 country 的 economic failure 的 main reason。正如 Ward 和 Devereux (2011) 所 assert 的，尽管有一些 social achievements，历史似乎仍不会 absolve 这个 Cuban regime。

是的，这是一种 speculative 判断，但它也与 economic theory 会 predict 的情况一致。如果你想相信相反的结论，就需要 arguments 来说明，为什么这些 studies，以及总体上的 economic theory，是错的。

## 原文

What would have happened if Russia, Venezuela, and Cuba hadn't had the leaders mentioned in the title?

There is one paper for each country that tries to address that really hard question. Spoiler: the countries would have been better off.

[Was Stalin necessary for Russia's economic development?](http://www.voxeu.org/article/stalin-and-soviet-industrialisation) (2013) by Anton Cheremukhin, Mikhail Golosov, Sergei Guriev, and Aleh Tsyvinski. ([Full paper](http://economics.mit.edu/files/8702))

> Therefore our answer to the ‘Was Stalin Necessary?’ question is a definite ‘no’. Even though we do not consider the human tragedy of famine, repression and terror, and focus on economic outcomes alone, and even when we make assumptions that are biased in Stalin’s favour, his economic policies underperform the counterfactual. We believe Stalin’s industrialisation should not be used as a success story in development economics, and should instead be studied as an example where brutal reallocation resulted in lower productivity and lower social welfare.

[The economic consequences of Hugo Chavez: a synthetic control analysis](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268116000020) (2016) by  Kevin Grieg and Norman Maynard

> We find that although average incomes rose somewhat during his time as president, they lagged far behind where they might have been if Chavez had not taken office. During his leadership, life expectancy grew while mortality and poverty rates fell. Yet we find no evidence that these gains are any different than they would have been with another national leader. If anything life expectancy rose slower than what the control predicted. On the positive side of the ledger, it may be the case that inequality fell faster than it would have without Chavez in office after 2003; but we see no evidence that his term in office as a whole reduced inequality more than it would have without him. While these results are not produced in a complete simultaneous model, they suggest that the observed reductions in inequality and poverty should not be attributed to Chavez's leadership, and that Chavez's legacy may have more to do with the harm caused to per capita incomes.

[The Cuban experiment: measuring the role of the 1959 revolution on economic performance using synthetic control](http://economics.ca/2013/papers/SG0030-1.pdf) (2013) by Felipe Garcia Ribeiro, Guilherme Stein, and Thomas H. Kang

> We claimed, as many scholars have already done, that institutional change caused by the Revolution was the main cause of the divergence from 1959 onwards. We also dismissed the claims that war, human capital, economic embargo, and economic policies pursued by the Cuban government were the main causes of Cuban economic failure. The latter hypothesis could be an explanation, but clearly the economic policies undertaken by Castro were erratic because all of them failed due to lack of market mechanisms. \[...\] Institutional change through the end of market mechanisms and the collectivization process is probably the main reason of the country's economic failure. As Ward and Devereux (2011) assert, despite some social achievements, it does not seem that history will absolve the Cuban regime.

Yes, this is speculative, but it is also in line with what economic theory would predict. If you want to believe the opposite, you would need arguments to show why these studies, and economic theory in general, are wrong.
