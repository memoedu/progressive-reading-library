# 关于 Cruise 行人事故的笔记｜Notes on Cruise's pedestrian accident

---

## 正文

这是一组关于 Quinn Emanuel 就 Cruise 如何处理 2023-10-02 accident 所作 report 的笔记。在那次 accident 中，一辆 Cruise autonomous vehicle (AV) hit 了一名 pedestrian，stopped，随后又 started moving，而 pedestrian stuck 在 AV 的 bottom 下，车辆 dragging 了这名 pedestrian 20 feet。看到一些关于这份 report 的评论后，我读了五篇关于它的报道，随后略读了 report；我的感觉是，其中四篇报道的作者很可能没有读过 report，而发表评论的人通常读的是那些似乎没有阅读 source material 的记者所写的报道，因此这些评论大多偏离事实。[正如我们之前讨论过的，summaries 错得离谱是很常见的，即使它们 summarizing 的只是一篇外行也能轻松读懂的短 paper](https://danluu.com/dunning-kruger/)，所以，一份 200-page report 的 summaries 即使往好里说，也很可能是 misleading 的。

读完整份 report 后，我会说，与我看到的文章相比，Cruise 既显得更好，也显得更坏；这与我们查看 [Twitter v. Musk 中的 Exhibits H and J](https://danluu.com/elon-twitter-texts/)、[United States v. Microsoft Corp. 的 docs](https://danluu.com/us-v-ms/) 等实际 source 时看到的情形相同。有些记者似乎支持或反对 Elon Musk、支持或反对 Microsoft，愿意推动 inaccurate narrative，以尽可能地贬损他们，或尽可能地为他们 exonerate；在 Cruise 这里，我们也看到了同样的事情。而且，正如我们在那些案例中看到的，尽管有些文章似乎试图把 Cruise 描绘得尽可能好或尽可能坏，report 本身包含的材料，却比最正面或最负面的报道所展示的还要更正面，也更负面。

除了纠正关于这份 report 的 misleading 观点之外，我觉得它很有意思，因为很少能看到对 tech 中出了什么问题进行如此详尽的 investigation，更不用说公开的 investigation 了。我们经常在 safety critical systems 中看到这类 investigation，有时也会在 sports 和 historical events 中看到，但 tech events 通常不会得到这样的报道。当然，公司会对 incidents 做 post-mortems，但你一般不会看到针对单个 incident 的 200-page report，post-mortems 的重点通常也不会是这里的重点。过去，[我们提到过，查看关于 safety-critical systems 的 literature 和 incident reports 能学到很多东西](https://danluu.com/wat/)，这里当然也是如此；这里的 safety-critical system 比我们之前看过的那些更接近 tech。

这里 report 的篇幅和深度，反映了 safety-critical systems 与“tech”之间的文化差异。report 中被描述为不可接受的行为，在 tech 中不仅很正常，而且很可能比你在多数大型 tech companies 中看到的做法更加 transparent、更加光明正大；我也觉得 tech 与 safety-critical systems 之间的文化冲突很有意思。我尽可能少地把自己的观点掺入这份 report 的笔记中，即使在某些地方，基于对 tech companies 或 engineering 的了解，我个人本来会写出不同的内容。更多观点，[见末尾的部分](https://danluu.com/cruise-report/#back-to-danluu-com)。

## I. Introduction（引言）

### A. Overview（概述）

-   **2023-10-24**：California DMV suspended 了 Cruise 的 driverless license
-   **2023-10-02**：由人驾驶的 Nissan hit 了一名 pedestrian，使 pedestrian 进入一辆 Cruise autonomous vehicle (AV) 的 path，后者随后 dragged 了 pedestrian `20 feet`，之后才 stopping
-   DMV 声称
    -   Cruise 未能 disclose，AV 在 initial impact 后又 moved forward
    -   Cruise 播放的 video 只展示了 accident 的一部分，没有展示 pedestrian dragging
    -   DMV 只是从另一个 government agency 才得知 dragging 的情况，这“impeding 了它的 oversight”
-   NHTSA 和 CPUC 也对 Cruise took action，并提出了类似的 claims
-   Media outlets 也抱怨它们被 Cruise misled
-   与 regulators 交谈的 Cruise leadership 和 Cruise employees 承认，他们没有解释 dragging，但表示他们播放了完整的 video clip；不过，除一次 meeting 外，所有 meetings 中的 internet issues 都可能使 regulators 无法看到完整的 accident
-   Cruise employees 声称，NHTSA 在 **10-03** meeting 后立即收到了完整的 video，而 CPUC 拒绝了提供完整 video 的提议
-   Cruise employees 指出，他们在 **10-03** 向 SF MTA、SFPD 和 SFFD 播放了完整的 video，没有出现 internet issues，并与这些 agencies 进行了充分讨论
-   Cruise leadership 承认，他们从未告知 media，但 leadership 认为，Cruise 对 media 的 obligations 不同于其对 regulators 的 obligations

### C. Review Plan 的 Methodology 和 Limitations

-   205k 份“documents”，包括“包括 e-mails、texts、Slack communications 和 Cruise 内部 documents”
-   Interviewed 了 88 名现任及前任 employees 和 contractors
-   Reviewed 了 3rd party firm Exponent Inc. 的一份 report
-   仅限 Internal review；没有 interview regulators 和 public officials

-   Report 不涉及 mandate 之外更广泛的问题，“例如 Cruise AVs 或其 operations 的 safety 或 safety processes，这些更适合由具有 engineering 和 technical safety expertise 的人 evaluated”

### D. Principal Findings 和 Conclusions 的 Summary

-   到 **10-03** 早晨，leadership 和 100+ 名 employees 已经知道，Cruise AV 在 came to a stop 之后的 secondary movement 中，将 pedestrian dragged 了 ~20ft.。
-   计划是通过播放完整的 video 来 disclose 这件事，“让‘video 自己说明一切’。”
    -   Cruise 假定 regulators 和 government officials 会提问，而 Cruise 会提供更多信息
-   “Weight of the evidence”表明，Cruise 曾尝试播放完整的 video，但在 3 次 meetings 中，internet issues 使其未能做到，而 Cruise 也没有指出发生了 pedestrian dragging
-   在 **10-02** 和 **10-03**，“Cruise leadership 一心只想 correcting 那种 inaccurate media narrative”，即 Cruise 的 AV caused 了这次 accident
    -   这使 Cruise 向“media、regulators 和其他 government officials”传达了关于 Nissan 的信息，却 omit 了关于 accident 的“其他重要信息”
-   “Cruise 此次 failings 的原因有很多：poor leadership、mistakes in judgment、缺乏 coordination、面对 regulators 时抱持‘我们对抗他们’的心态，以及从根本上 misapprehension 了 Cruise 对 government 和 public 所负有的 accountability 和 transparency obligations。Cruise 必须采取果断措施来解决这些问题，以恢复 trust 和 credibility。”
-   “DMV Suspension Order 是某些 Cruise senior leadership 和 employees 自作自受的直接结果；他们似乎并未充分理解，一个 regulated business 应当如何与其 regulators interact ... 无论是 Cruise 还是其他任何 business，若认为一段造成 serious injury 的 accident video 就向 regulators 提供了所有必要信息，并因此免除了自己主动、全面地向这些 regulators 告知所有 relevant facts 的必要性，这种做法从根本上就是 flawed。正如一名 Cruise employee 在就此事发给另一名 employee 的 text message 中所说，我们的‘领导辜负了我们。’”

## II. 关于 OCTOBER 2 ACCIDENT 的 FACTS

### A. 关于 Cruise 的 Business Operations 的 Background

-   Cruise 于 2013 年 founded，于 2016 年被 GM acquired（GM owns 79%）
-   Cruise 声明的 goal："responsibly deploy 世界上最 advanced 的 driverless vehicle service"
-   "Cruise 声明的 mission 是让 transportation 更 cleaner、safer 且更 accessible"
-   Driverless ride-hail operation 于 2021-09 在 SF 开始
-   于 2022-06 开始 charging
-   已 expanded 到其他地区，包括海外
-   **10-02** accident 是在 > `5M mi` 的 driving 中首次发生的 pedestrian injury

### B. 关于 Accident 的 Key Facts

-   **10-02, _9:29pm_**：human-driven Nissan Sentra 在 SF 的 5th & Market 的 4-way intersection 的 crosswalk 内 strikes pedestrian
-   Pedestrian 在违反 red light 和 "Do Not Walk" signal 的情况下 entered crosswalk，随后在 Nissan 的 lane 内 paused。Police report 因 code violations 而 cited driver 和 pedestrian 双方，并 concluded driver 是 "most at fault"
-   这次 impact 将 pedestrian launched 到 Crusie AV 的 path 中
-   Cruise AV braked，但仍然 hit pedestrian
-   在 coming to a complete stop 后，AV moved 以寻找 safe place 来 stop，这被称为 "'minimal risk condition' pullover maneuver (pullover maneuver) 或 'secondary movement.'"
-   AV 以最高 `7.7mph` drove 了 `20 feet`，同时 dragging pedestrian
-   Nissan driver fled the scene（hit and run）

### C. Key Events 的 Timeline

-   **10-02, _9:29pm_**：accident occurs，Nissan driver flees；AV 向 Cruise Remote Assistance Center transits 确认 collison 的 low-res 3-second video（Offload 1）
-   **_9:32pm_**：AV transmits 关于 collision 的 medium-res 14-second video（Offload 2），但其中没有 pullover maneuver 和 pedestrian dragging
-   **_9:33pm_**：emergency responders 于 9:33pm 至 9:38pm 之间到达
-   **_9:40pm_**：SFFD 使用 heavy rescue tools 将 pedestrian 从 AV 下方 remove
-   **_9:49pm_**：Cruise Incident Response team 将 accident labels 为 "Sev-1"，该级别适用于 minor collisions。Team 在 Google Meet 上 created 了 virtual "war room"，并 created 了 dedicated slack channel（war room slack channel），其中有 ~20 名 employees
-   **_10:17pm_**：Cruise contractors 到达 accident scene。一名 contractor 拍摄了 > 100 张 photos 和 videos，并注意到地上的 blood 和 skin patches，表明 AV 从 point-of-impact moved 到了 final stopping place
    -   另一名 contractor 经 Cruise 的 authorization，向 SFPD 提供了显示 Nissan 的 14-second video
-   **_11:31pm_**：Cruise 将 incident raises 至 "Sev-0"，适用于 "major vehicle incident，其中任何一方遭受 moderate to major injury 或 fatality"。可能又有 200 名 employees 被 paged 到 war room

-   **_3:21am_**：应 Cruise govt. Affairs 的请求，Director of Systems Integrity Matt Wood creates 了一段 12s accident video，显示 Nissan hitting pedestrian，以及 pedestrian landing 在 Cruise AV 前方。Video 在 AV hits pedestrian 之前结束

### E. 关于 October 2 Accident，Cruise 知道哪些 Facts，以及何时知道

#### 1\. Cruise 在 October 2 晚上得知的 Facts

##### a. Accident Scene（事故现场）

-   Driverless Support Specialists (DSS) 于 9:39pm 至 9:44pm arrive 在 scene
-   另一支 2-person DSS team 与 operations team 的一名成员及 Safety Escalation team (SET) 的一名成员一同 arrive，时间为 10:00-10:30pm
-   至少一名 contractor 拍摄了 > 100 张 photos 和 video，并表示自己了解 pedestrian dragging 的情况
    -   Contractor 注意到了 blood 和 skin pieces，拍摄了 trail of blood 的 long shots，这些表明 impact 后发生了 traveled；contractor 被要求将手机带到 Cruise，而不是将 video uploading 到通常使用的 Slack channel；contractor 认为这是为了保护 injured pedestrian 的 privacy
    -   Photos 和 video 于 2:23am uploaded 到“RINO”database，从 **10-03, _5:11am_** 开始被 > 100 名 employees accessed；DB 不会 show 具体哪些 employees reviewed 了具体哪些 photos 和 videos
-   scene 的另一个人否认知道 dragging 的情况
-   在 Quinn Emanuel 介入之前，Cruise 的 internal review 中，一名 Remote Assistance operator (RA) 说自己看到“ped 被 flung 到 AV 的 hood 上。你能看到并听到那次 bump”，另一名则看到 AV“已经在 pulling over to the side”。Quinn Emanuel 直到 12-14 的 RIF 之后才得知这些情况。在联系他们后，一人拒绝了 interview，另一人没有回复

##### b. Virtual "Sev-0 War Room"（虚拟“Sev-0 作战室”）

-   最初有 20 人在 war room 中

## Appendix：一件关于 physical hardware 的好奇事

我向上文提到的 exec 问过一个问题，虽然与这个案例无关，但我已经好奇了一段时间：为什么我看到在路上行驶的 AVs 没有 upgraded tires 和 brakes。对于并不特别 heavy 的 cars，通过 upgrading 它们的 tires 和 brakes，可以获得短得多的 stopping distances，但我见过的 AVs 都没有这样做。

在这个案例中，我们无法对 upgraded vehicle 与 base vehicle 做 exact comparison，因为 appendix 的 section 3.3.3、table 9 和 figure 40 中的 vehicle dynamics data 被 redacted 了，但众所周知，你能对一辆 car 做的最简单的 safety upgrade，就是 upgrading tires（以及在适用时 upgrading brakes）。有人可能认为，这不值得付出额外的 running cost 或精力（对于我经常看到被 converted into AVs 的 low-performance cars，要获得与 sporty vehicle equivalent 的 stopping distances，通常需要 modifying wheel well，以免 wider tires 发生 rub），但作为一个外行，我很想知道 shorter stopping distances 的 cost benefit trade-off 是怎样的。

这位 exec 此前没有考虑过这个问题，但认为更好的 tires 和 brake 会在很多其他案例中产生作用，并 prevent accients，还这样解释为什么没有进行这项 upgrade：

> 我认为，如果你同时抱着“我们想把 AV base 在 commodity cars 上”和“我是个 algorithms 人”这两种心态，你就不会去研究这辆 car 应当是什么样的。

而且，要明确的是，upgraded tires 和 brakes 并不会 changed 这个案例的 outcome。Exponent report 中的 timeline 是

-   **\-2.9s**：Nissan 与 Pedestrian 之间发生 contact
-   **\-2s**：Pedestrian track 被 dropped
-   **\-1.17s**：Pedestrian beings separating，自 Nissan 分离
-   **\-0.81s**：\[已 redacted\]
-   **\-0.78s**：Pedestrian lands 在 AV 的 travel lane 中
-   **\-0.41s**：Collision checker predicts collision（预测碰撞）
-   **\-0.25s**：AV 开始 sending braking and steering commands（`19.1 mph`）
-   **0s**：collision（碰撞）（`18.6 mph`）

查看一辆配有 upgraded tires 和 brakes 的 car 的 actual accelerometer data，这辆 car 从 `19.1mph` 开始的 stopping time 约为 `0.8s`，因此在这个案例中，这不会产生多大区别。如果 brakes 在尝试 brake 前没有 pre-charged，那么最初 braking 时就会有显著的 latency，以至于 `0.25s` 几乎不足以让任何 braking 发生；这一点可以从这个案例中 speed 仅 slower 了 `0.5mph` 看出来。

## 原文

This is a set of notes on the Quinn Emanuel report on Cruise's handling of the 2023-10-02 accident where a Cruise autonomous vehicle (AV) hit a pedestrian, stopped, and then started moving again with the pedestrian stuck under the bottom of the AV, dragging the pedestrian 20 feet. After seeing some comments about this report, I read five stories on this report and then skimmed the report and my feeling is that the authors of four of the stories probably didn't read the report, and that people who were commenting had generally read stories by journalists who did not appear to read the source material, so the comments were generally way off base. [As we previously discussed, it's common for summaries to be wildly wrong, even when they're summarizing a short paper that's easily read by laypeople](https://danluu.com/dunning-kruger/), so of course summaries of a 200-page report are likely to be misleading at best.

On reading the entire report, I'd say that Cruise both looks better and worse than in the articles I saw, which is the same pattern we saw when we looked at the actual source for [Exhibits H and J from Twitter v. Musk](https://danluu.com/elon-twitter-texts/), the [United States v. Microsoft Corp. docs](https://danluu.com/us-v-ms/), etc.; just as some journalists seem to be pro/anti-Elon Musk and pro/anti-Microsoft, willing to push an inaccurate narrative to dunk on them to the maximum extent possible or exonerate them to the maximum extent possible, we see the same thing here with Cruise. And as we saw in those cases, despite some articles seemingly trying to paint Cruise in the best or worst light possible, the report itself has material that is more positive and more negative than we see in the most positive or negative stories.

Aside from correcting misleading opinions on the report, I find the report interesting because it's rare to see any kind of investigation over what went wrong in tech in this level of detail, let alone a public one. We often see this kind of investigation in safety critical systems and sometimes see in sports as well as for historical events, but tech events are usually not covered like this. Of course companies do post-mortems of incidents, but you generally won't see a 200-page report on a single incident, nor will the focus of post-mortems be what the focus was here. In the past, [we've noted that a lot can be learned by looking at the literature and incident reports on safety-critical systems](https://danluu.com/wat/), so of course this is true here as well, where we see a safety-critical system that's more tech adjacent than the ones we've looked at previously.

The length and depth of the report here reflects a difference in culture between safety-critical systems and "tech". The behavior that's described as unconscionable in the report is not only normal in tech, but probably more transparent and above board than you'd see at most major tech companies; I find the culture clash between tech and safety-critical systems interesting as well. I attempted to inject as little of my opinion as possible into the report as possible, even in cases where knowledge of tech companies or engineering meant that I would've personally written something different. For more opinions, [see the section at the end](https://danluu.com/cruise-report/#back-to-danluu-com).

## II. THE FACTS REGARDING THE OCTOBER 2 ACCIDENT

### A. Background Regarding Cruise’s Business Operations

-   Cruise founded in 2013, acquired by GM in 2016 (GM owns 79%)
-   Cruise's stated goal: "responsibly deploy the world’s most advanced driverless vehicle service"
-   "Cruise’s stated mission is to make transportation cleaner, safer and more accessible"
-   Driverless ride-hail operation started 2021-09 in SF
-   Started charging in 2022-06
-   Has expanded to other areas, including overseas
-   **10-02** accident was first pedestrian injury in > `5M mi` of driving

### B. Key Facts Regarding the Accident

-   **10-02, _9:29pm_**: human-driven Nissan Sentra strikes pedestrian in crosswalk of 4-way intersection at 5th & Market in SF
-   Pedestrian entered crosswalk against a red light and "Do Not Walk" signal and then paused in Nissan's lane. Police report cited both driver and pedestrian for code violations and concluded that the driver was "most at fault"
-   The impact launched the pedestrian into the path of the Crusie AV
-   Cruise AV braked but still hit pedestrian
-   After coming to a complete stop, the AV moved to find a safe place to stop, known as a "'minimal risk condition' pullover maneuver (pullover maneuver) or 'secondary movement.'"
-   AV drove up to `7.7mph` for `20 feet`, dragging pedestrian with it
-   Nissan driver fled the scene (hit and run)

## Appendix: a physical hardware curiosity

One question I had for the exec mentioned above, which wasn't relevant to this case, but is something I've wondered about for a while, is why the AVs that I see driving don't have upgraded tires and brakes. You can get much shorter stopping distances from cars that aren't super heavy by upgrading their tires and brakes, but the AVs I've seen have not had this done.

In this case, we can't do the exact comparison from an upgraded vehicle to the base vehicle because the vehicle dynamics data was redacted from section 3.3.3, table 9, and figure 40 of the appendix, but it's common knowledge that the simplest safety upgrade you can make on a car is upgrading the tires (and, if relevant, the brakes). One could argue that this isn't worth the extra running cost, or the effort (for the low-performance cars that I tend to see converted into AVs, getting stopping distances equivalent to a sporty vehicle would generally require modifying the wheel well so that wider tires don't rub) but, as an outsider, I'd be curious to know what the cost benefit trade-off on shorter stopping distances is.

They hadn't considered it before, but thought that better tires and brake would make a difference in a lot of other cases and prevent accients and explained the lack of this upgrade by:

> I think if you have a combination of "we want to base AV on commodity cars" and "I am an algorithms guy" mindset you will not go look at what the car should be.

And, to be clear, upgraded tires and brakes would not have changed the outcome in this case. The timeline from the Exponent report has

-   **\-2.9s**: contact between Nissan and Pedestrian
-   **\-2s**: Pedestrian track dropped
-   **\-1.17s**: Pedestrian beings separating from Nissan
-   **\-0.81s**: \[redacted\]
-   **\-0.78s**: Pedestrian lands in AV's travel lane
-   **\-0.41s**: Collision checker predicts collision
-   **\-0.25s**: AV starts sending braking and steering commands (`19.1 mph`)
-   **0s**: collision (`18.6 mph`)

Looking at actual accelerometer data from a car with upgraded tires and brakes, stopping time from `19.1mph` for that car was around `0.8s`, so this wouldn't have made much difference in this case. If brakes aren't pre-charged before attempting to brake, there's significant latency when initially braking, such that `0.25s` isn't enough for almost any braking to have occurred, which we can see from the speed only being `0.5mph` slower in this case.

Another comment from the exec is that, while a human might react to the collision at `-2.9s` and slow down or stop, "scene understanding" as a human might do it is non-existent in most or perhaps all AVs, so it's unsurprising that the AV doesn't react until the pedestrian is in the AV's path, whereas a human, if they noticed the accident in the adjacent lane, would likely drastically slow down or stop (the exec guessed that most humans would come to a complete stop, whereas I guessed that most humans would slow down). The exec was also not surprised by the `530ms` latency between the pedestrian landing in the AV's path and the AV starting to attempt to apply the brakes although, as a lay person, I found `530ms` surprising.

On the advantage of AVs and ADAS, as implemented today, compared to a human who's looking in the right place, paying attention, etc., the exec said

> They mainly never get tired or drink and hopefully also run in that terrible driver's car in the next lane. For \[current systems\], it's reliability and not peak performance that makes it useful. Peak performance is definitely not superhuman but subhuman
