# Yet another half-baked theory of the political spectrum

---

## 正文

大多数 political theorising 和 philosophical discourse 都发生在 anglosphere。而按人数计算，anglosphere 主要就是 United States。因此，解释 political blocs 的尝试以某种方式带有 USAcentric 特征，也就不足为奇了。

即使在 academic literature 中，也能找到诸如「economic conservatism」这样的说法，意思是「赞成 government 对 economy 进行更少的 intervention、更低的 taxation 等」。这些东西根本就不是 conservative（按 preserving the status quo 这个意义而言）！但之所以这样称呼，是因为 US 的 political conservatives endorse 这些 policies。

要弄清这些「left and right」究竟是什么，我们应当先思考这里所谓的 right 和 left 的 essence 或 core，如果它们确实存在的话。但在像前人那样把世界 classifying 为两类之前，我们必须问：为什么是两类？

确实，对一些人来说，从 left 和 right 的角度看世界可能很容易。对于「你认为自己属于 right 还是 left？」这个问题，许多人早有答案。我心里却从未有过一个让我感到自在的答案。这就像问我「你喜欢咖啡还是酒？」（碰巧我两者都不喜欢）。这些问题要么 assume 必须是其中一个，要么忘记了可以有更多 categories。

但究竟是什么 categories 呢？我们稍后再谈。

此前解释 political spectrum 的尝试，都试图把 left 和 right 之间的 differences 与 wealth 联系起来。Scott Alexander 的 [thrive/survive theory](http://slatestarcodex.com/2013/03/04/a-thrivesurvive-theory-of-the-political-spectrum/)，或 Robin Hanson 所倡导的类似 theory，都指出 wealth 发挥着重要作用：一个 society 越 wealthier，就越是 [thrive/left/hunter](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2017/08/forager-v-farmer-elaborated.html)。

但这个 theory 有一个问题：那些 Oil rich、high GDP 的 arab countries。即使我们只看这些 countries 内部的 rich，也看不到像 Sweden 那么多的「leftism」。同样，人们随着变得 rich，理应变得更加 leftist，但在 US，income 对 ideology 的 affect 并没有那么大。

这些 theories 看起来有效，是因为如果 cross-nationally 地考察，确实能看到 wealth-values correlation。

Bryan Caplan 有另一种说法：[Left 是 anti-market，而 Right 是 anti-left。](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2015/10/my_simplistic_t.html) 他并不描述什么能够 affect values，而只是描述，

> 有许多显著的候选解释，比如：
> 
> 1\. Leftists 更关心 equality，rightists 更关心 efficiency。2. Leftists 更关心 poor，rightists 更关心 rich。3. Leftists 更 secular，rightists 更 religious。
> 
> 不过，在我看来，所有这些 theories 都在 overintellectualize Left 和 Right。两种 ideology 都不是 deduction from first principles。甚至远远谈不上。把 Leftists 与其他 Leftists、Rightists 与其他 Rightists binds 在一起的，不是 logic，而是 [_psycho-logic_](http://www.mtoomey.com/psychologic.html)。是 Feelings，而不是 theories。
> 
> 我的替代解释是什么？就是这个：
> 
> 1\. Leftists 是 anti-market。在 emotional level 上，他们对 market outcomes 持 critical 态度。不管 market outcomes 有多好，他们都无法忍受说出：「Markets 已经做得很棒了，谁还能要求更多呢？」
> 
> 2\. Rightists 是 anti-leftist。在 emotional level 上，他们对 leftists 持 critical 态度。不管他们在某个 issue 上有多 agree with leftists，他们都无法忍受说出：「left 完全正确，criticize 他们就太刻薄了。」

他还用这个 theory 批评了 [Scott Alexander 的 theory，](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2017/09/whats_wrong_wit_23.html)主张 society 并没有 drifting leftward（Soviet Union 解体了，等等），radical left parties 在更 poorer 的 countries 中更突出，而且并不特别 socially liberal，还有一些 issues 不 match 这个 theory（比如 abortion）。他还指出，我们的 theory 的解释力可能取决于我们把什么称为 left 和 right

> 我的 Simplistic Theory 是 \_intentionally 地\_无法 predict 许多 details。为什么？因为 Scott 视为 quintessentially leftist 的许多 features——比如「freedom of religion、democratic-republican governments、weak gender norms、minimal family values，以及对 education 和 abstract ideas 的 high emphasis」——都曾被历史上最 influential、最 dyed-in-the-wool 的 leftists 所 neglected 或 rejected。Scott 亲自认识的 leftists 或许珍视他清单上的这些东西。不过，坦率地说，Scott 亲自认识的 leftists，只不过是一个 diverse 而 enduring 的 movement 中时髦的 subset。

### 对 Left 和 Right 进行 Defining

对 Scott Alexander 来说，这个 theory 应当 fit 哪些 facts？

> **1)** 为什么这两种 ideologies 都会 combine 看似 unrelated 的 political ideas？例如，为什么想要 laissez-faire free trade 的人，empirically 也 prefer strong military，并 oppose gay marriage？为什么想要帮助 environment 的人，也 support feminism，并 dislike school vouchers？
> 
> **2)** 为什么这两种 ideologies 在不同的时代和 cultures 中似乎 _broadly_ 保持 stable，以至于我们相对容易指出 Tories 比 Whigs 更 right，或者古代 Athens 比古代 Sparta 更 left？说到这里，为什么它们似乎还 correspond to brain 中某些 neural patterns，以至于 [neurologists 仅通过 examining brain structure，就能以 83% accuracy 来 determine 你的 political beliefs？](http://blogs.smithsonianmag.com/science/2013/02/study-predicts-political-beliefs-with-83-percent-accuracy/)
> 
> **3)** 为什么这些基本上属于 political 的 ideas，会与 moral、aesthetic 和 religious preferences 如此紧密地 correlate？
> 
> **4)** 最初的问题：为什么只要给予足够时间，并任其自行发展，[leftism 似乎通常都会 win out over rightism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_history)，把 Overton window 向前 pushing 一点，直到出现新的 leftism 和 rightism？

我对此的异议是，1) 并不完全成立。存在 libertarians、Vox leftists、SJW leftists、conservatives、alt-righters、fascists 和 communists。这些 groups 彼此相当不同，看起来并不会在一条 1D axis 上 neatly align，除非一个人深陷 1D political world，以至于这条 1D axis swampts 了其他 differences。

第 2) 点可能有些道理，但也可能是 availability bias。我们印象中的 Athenians 是 enlightened philosophers，Spartans 是 skilled warriors，但这两个 societies 都存在大量 slavery，而且在 Sparta，[women 显然拥有更多 freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_ancient_Sparta)，超过其他地方。此外，Spartans 对 commerce 和 trade 显然也不如 Athenians 那么 open。

Sparta 看起来确实像是一个为 survival 和 expansion 而 configured 的 society，而 Athens 则不然。但这并不会直接 map 到现代 left 和 right 的 categories。如果要把 right define 为 survival，把 left define 为 thriving，就不能保留现代的 axis：一边是 socially conservative 和 economically liberal（按 classic 意义而言）的 policies，另一边是 socially liberal 和 economically progressive 的 policies。因为如果这样 define，那么 Sparta 相对的 economic leftism 就会使它落在以 US 为基础的 right 定义之外。

第 3) 点似乎很难 evaluate——而且文中也没怎么讨论它？。拿 [socialist realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_realism) 与 [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_the_Third_Reich) 偏好的 style 作 compare，你会注意到一些 similarities。

第 4) 点，如上所述，这根本不成立。真要说的话，获胜的是 liberalism，这更接近 Scott 链接的 Whig theory，而不是 leftism。（除非 liberalism 就是 leftism！）

所讨论的具体 issues 是：

-   担心 society 可能 collapse（right），与之相对的是一切都好（left）
-   Pro gun（right），与之相对的是 anti gun（left）
-   规模 Big 且 powerful 的 police 和 military（right），与之相对的是规模 smaller（left）
-   更多 religion（right），与之相对的是更少 religion（left）
-   对 outsiders 的 Distrust（right），与之相对的是 trust（left）
-   对 hierarchy 和 conformity 的 Preference（right），与之相对的是相反的偏好（left）
-   对 rules 的 Harsh enforcement（right），与之相对的是较不 strict（left）
-   Wealth maximisation（right），与之相对的是其他关切（left）
-   Purity ethics（right），与之相对的是没有这种 ethics（left）
-   Practical skills（right），与之相对的是 bookish skills（left）
-   Black and white thinking（right），与之相对的是 shades of grey（left）

这可能让我们觉得「对，全都对得上……」，但当我们把 communists 或 libertarians 纳入考虑时，似乎就对不上了。在 USSR 和 Nazi Germany 的年代，Central planning 曾经盛行一时。它也受到 [West 的 progressive elites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiberal_Reformers) 的欢迎，而这些人当时还 endorsed eugenics，以及 biased against women、immigrants、disabled 和 blacks 的 policies。这些人[也都是 religious 的](https://newrepublic.com/article/128144/dark-history-liberal-reform)。我们也需要解释上个世纪的 Progressivism。

这里先给出一个不成熟的解释尝试，我自己并不完全赞同：leftists 的共同点是 utilitarianism。[Eric Hobsbawm](https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=rQTgHvsUhyMC&pg=PT186&lpg=PT186&dq=Ignatieff:+What+that+comes+down+to+is+saying+that+had+the+radiant+tomorrow+actually+been+created+%5Bin+the+USSR%5D,+the+loss+of+fifteen,+twenty+million+people+might+have+been+justified?+Hobsbawm:+Yes.&source=bl&ots=nbI7BXDA3S&sig=ZHR02VYnUczdOPBrT2-7qzSb_j0&hl=en&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwiT1ZT7mcHWAhWGI8AKHU8pD60Q6AEIKDAA#v=onepage&q=Ignatieff%3A%20What%20that%20comes%20down%20to%20is%20saying%20that%20had%20the%20radiant%20tomorrow%20actually%20been%20created%20%5Bin%20the%20USSR%5D%2C%20the%20loss%20of%20fifteen%2C%20twenty%20million%20people%20might%20have%20been%20justified%3F%20Hobsbawm%3A%20Yes.&f=false)——一位 marxist historian——曾说过一句臭名昭著的话：对 utopian society 的许诺，justified 两千万或一千五百万人的 death。同样，（可以说）把过去的 Progressives 和今天的 Vox wonk 联系起来的，是这样一种 belief：无论他们相信什么，那都是在 serves the greater good。因此，过去的 eugenicists 与今天的 wonks 之间的 difference，更多在于 empirics，而非 values。那么，是什么把 rightists 联系在一起呢？

对 nation 的关切？这条线索似乎在 Progressives 中也很常见（在现代 rational leftists 中则不尽然）

Free marketism？（Trump 或 Theresa May 并非如此。在 markets 方面，rightists 似乎接受 conservatism：保留现有的东西。随后他们在 margins 上提出的某些主张，会或多或少地 pro-free market）。Nazis 确实保留了 private property，并且[相对不干预 markets，同时 crushed unions](https://pseudoerasmus.com/2015/05/03/fascism-left-or-right/)。这不是 free market，而是 pro-business（或者归根结底，只要能持续生产 Panzer IVs，什么都行）

又或者，rightists 也是 utilitarians！（再说一次，我并不真这么认为）。但这未免太牵强。确实，utilitarianism [可能 commands](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2015/08/an-singerian-argument-against-abortion/) 一些可能被视为「rightist」的事情，例如 increasing the fertility rate（也许是 banning abortions，或者使 contraception 更难获得），而且 social conservatism 确实有过它的 [utilitarian defenders](https://webcache.googleusercontent.com/search?q=cache:KRdafq89X1MJ:econlog.econlib.org/archives/2017/09/does_prosperity_1.html&num=1&hl=en&gl=uk&strip=1&vwsrc=0)）。Utilitarianism 恐怕不是我们要找的东西。

这里还有另一个不成熟的解释，仅仅基于 semantics：right wing 意味着 maintaining the status quo，而 left wing 意味着 changing 它。这就会使 western countries 从 communism 到 capitalism 的 transition 成为 left wing，使 Stalin 成为一个极端的 rightist，还会使 Sweden 的 libertarians 成为 left wing，而 social democrats 成为 right wing。

* * *

我有自己的 theory，也有自己的 facts 要去 fit。

需要 fit 的 facts 包括 Scott Alexander 已经 mapped 的那些，适用于 Group A（US republican-types）和 Group B（US democrat-types）时就是如此。但除此之外还有：

-   有些人认为事情正在 getting better，另一些人则认为正在 getting worse，或 stagnating
-   有些人比其他人更愿意 use force，将一套 more extensive 的 values impose 于 society
-   有些人想要的 society 只是人们各做各的事情并 cooperating，另一些人则更喜欢由 common purpose 联结起来的 society
-   Communism 和 fascism 必须在至少一个 axis 上是 similar 的
-   Libertarianism 和 Vox leftism 必须在至少一个 axis 上是 similar 的

既然如此，我的 theory 是这样的：有三个 axes 会 define political ideology。

1.  第一个 Axis 是 liberalism（Ideology）

Liberalism 大致意味着 recognizing 人们为 moral equals，并具有 strong anti-coercion 和 anti-violence principles。Rule of law、civil rights、free speech，诸如此类。 Liberals 往往考虑 individuals，而不是 nations，而 liberal utopia 是这样一种 society：每个人都可以在不 harming 他人的情况下随心所欲，彼此 freely associating。

Illiberalism 并不将所有人视为 moral equals，而且往往更加 group 或 nation focused。Rights 不被视为由 individuals posessed、由 states recognised 的东西，而是由 states created 和 granted 的东西，或许是为了 society as a whole 的 benefit。Society 应当具有某种 common goal 意识，而人们不应当只是做自己想做的事，还应当超越自身，考虑某种被认为值得投身的事业。

2\. Axis 2 是 conservatism-progressivism（Mood）

这里的 Conservatism 意味着担心 society 可能 collapse、cooperation 很 difficult、trust 很难 gain 却容易 lose，牢记 institutions 是由人构成的，而且无论 expected outcome 如何，都 preferring 对 status quo 作 small marginal changes，而非 major changes。

Progressives 看到了 change 和 improvement 的机会。Cooperation 是 easy 的，每个人都可以被 trusted。如果有可能让事情变得更好的 big sweeping changes 可以做，为什么不去做呢。事情一直进展顺利，而且会继续如此，这就是 progressive 的 belief。

3\. Axis 3 是 voluntarism-involuntarism（Compulsion）

Voluntarists 只会让人们做自己想做的事，而不 forcing 他们去做，不管他们应不应该这样做。例如，一个 voluntarist conservative 可能认为 homosexual marriage 是 wrong 的，但他不会 oppose banning 它。一个 involuntarist liberal 会更愿意接受 coercing the rich to give to the poor。

有了这三个 axis，我们就能 account for 大多数 political subgroupings。

以 nazis 和 communists 为例。他们的 compulsion 都是 high 的，但在 ideological orientation 上 differ。两者都偏向 illiberal 一侧（一个 biased against 其他 nationalities，另一个 biased against the bourgeoisie，而 fascism 比 communism 更 illiberal。而且两者都是 progressive ideologies：他们希望 radically transform 世界，并对能够 achieve 那种 transformation 抱有 optimistic 的态度。

现在以 social democrats 和 libertarians 为例。两者都持有 liberal ideas，但 social democrats 更愿意 use force 去 enforce 它们。两个 groups 内部都有 conservative 和 progressive camps。Conservative libertarians（我会把 David Friedman、~~Bryan Caplan ~~（编辑说明：Bryan 不是处于 conservative pole 的好例子），或 public-choice libertarians 包括在这里）相对而言更多谈论 compulsion 的 negative effects，而 progressive libertarians（Matt Zwolinski 或 Jason Brennan）则更多谈论 voluntarism 的 good aspects。只有在 progressive libertarian camp 中，我们才能找到在 ideal theory 的 frame 下对 libertarianism 的 discussions（Brenann 的 Why not capitalism）。Progressive social democrats 往往 endorse UBI 之类的东西，而 conservative social democrats 则 endorse 对 existing system 的 minor tweaks，通常 emphasising 我们走到今天这一步有多么不容易。多年前，Conservative social democrats 会因为 UBI defenders 的 utopian aspirations 而嘲笑他们，而 progressive social democrats 则会热衷于对此进行 open discussiong。

Neoreaction 和 nazis 是 different 的，但究竟在哪种意义上呢？这里的答案是 Compulsion。他们具有共同的 nationalist（Illiberal）bias，以及对世界的 progressive outlook。Neoreaction 想要 change 事情，使其恢复从前的样子，而这需要 massive changes。但 nazis 想要 by force 做到这一点，而 alt-righters 则想通过 application of the right of exclusion，更 voluntarily 地做到这一点，至少他们是这样说的。

Scott Alexander 和我都相当 liberal，而且我们在 compulsion scale 上也都 relatively low，但他比我更偏向 conservative direction。他写了如今已成为经典的 Meditations on Moloch，那是一篇文笔优美的、关于 coordination problems 的 explanation；而我写了（远没有那么知名、文笔也没有那么优美的）Slaying Alexander's Moloch，讨论为什么那些 problems 并没有那么糟糕！

这个 theory 将大多数人 places 在中间的某个位置。就 liberalism、enforce 其 ideas 的意愿，以及对未来有多么 sanguine 而言，US republicans 和 democrats 都处于中间的某个位置，自然，conservatives 更偏向 conservative 一侧，而 democrats 更偏向 progressive 一侧。

的确，他们可能 equally liberal，也 equally 愿意 use compulsion，而他们更大的 difference 或许只是 mood 上的，是他们对 politicians 有多么 trust，或者他们在多大程度上认为 social problems 能被 government solved。

这与其他关于 spectrum 的 theories 形成 contrast，后者具有一个从一个 extreme 延伸到另一个 extreme 的 defined axis。thrive/survive theory（或者说 Robin Hanson 的 theory 也是如此）在面对 communism 和 fascism 时会很难处理，我的则不会。

但有人可能会说：那些 theories 至少尝试寻找一种 common explanation，甚至 make predictions。你的却像 Caplan 的一样：只是 describes 已经存在的东西。是的，这样说很公允。

我们能给这个 model 增加一些实质性的 predictive 内容吗？我认为可以。

-   （Ideology）Liberalism 可由 IQ predicted，wealth 的作用则没有那么大。
-   （Mood）Conservatism/Progressivism 可由 personality factors predicted（我能想到 openness to experience）
-   （Compulsion）关于这一项，坦白说我毫无头绪。

纵观历史，人们尝试新事物的意愿总有高低，这一点大概也会保持不变：于是就有了我们的 progressives 和 conservatives，我们永恒的两大 social confrontation blocks。但由于 Flynn effect，人们正变得越来越 liberal。我说 Compulsion 是棘手的一项，因为如果看看聪明人怎么想，确实往往能发现 liberalism，既有 conservative 也有 progressive fractions，同时也能发现愿意／不愿意 use force 去 implement liberalism 的人。也就是说，关键的 political conflict 过去存在于 illiberal 和 liberal values 之间。如今如果说有什么变化的话，那就是我们正在迁往一个 compulsion vs less compulsion 的世界。

这个 theory 还指出这样一个 fact：你所有聪明的朋友都 equally liberal，但他们在其他 dimensions 上各不相同：elite political spectrum 是 bi-dimensional 的（illiberal quarter 是 empty 的），其中有 Vox leftists（progressive，compulsion 为 high）、left libertarians（progressive，compulsion 为 low）、right libertarians（conservative，compulsion 为 low），以及那少数 advocate Singapore 式 state paternalism 的人（conservative，compulsion 为 high）

这个 theory 还不成熟，而且有很多漏洞（例如，Poland 是一个 high IQ、highly traditional 的 society），但我相信，与上面讨论的其他 theories 相比，它更好地 approximates reality 及其 change。

## 原文

Most political theorising and philosophical discourse happens in the anglosphere. And the anglosphere is mostly, by numbers, the United States. It is no surprise that attempts at explaining political blocs are in one way or another USAcentric.

Even in the academic literature, one can find such things as "economic conservatism", meaning "being in favour of less intervention of the government in the economy, lower taxation, etc". Those things are not conservative (in the sense of preserving the status quo) at all! But it is called like that because political conservatives in the US endorse those policies.

To see what are these "left and right" things, we should think first about the essence or core of what here is called right and left, if any. But before classifying the world into two, as others have done before, we must ask: Why two?

Indeed, for some it may come easily to see the world in terms of left and right. Many people have an answer ready to the question "Do you consider yourself to be on the right or the left?". I've never had an internal answer to that question that feels comfortable. It's like asking me "Do you like coffee or alcohol?" (It happens to be neither). Those questions either assume that it has to be one or the other, or forget that there can be more categories.

But what categories. We'll come back to that.

Previous attempts at explaining the political spectrum have tried to tie differences between left and right to wealth. The[thrive/survive theory](http://slatestarcodex.com/2013/03/04/a-thrivesurvive-theory-of-the-political-spectrum/) that Scott Alexander has or the similar theory that Robin Hanson espouses point to a strong role for wealth: the wealthier the more [thrive/left/hunter](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2017/08/forager-v-farmer-elaborated.html) a society is.

But this theory has an issue: Oil rich, high GDP, arab countries. Even when we limit ourselves to the rich within those countries, we don't see as much "leftism" as we would see in Sweden. Equally, as people get rich they should get more leftist, yet in the US, income does not affect ideology that much.

These theories seem to work because if one looks cross-nationally, one does see a wealth-values correlation.

Bryan Caplan has a different story:[the Left is anti-market and the Right is anti-left.](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2015/10/my_simplistic_t.html) Instead of describing what can affect values, he merely describes,

> There are many prominent candidates, like:
> 
> 1\. Leftists care more about equality, rightists care more about efficiency. 2. Leftists care more about the poor, rightists care more about the rich. 3. Leftists are more secular, rightists are more religious.
> 
> To my mind, though, all these theories overintellectualize Left and Right.  Neither ideology is a deduction from first principles.  Not even close.  What binds Leftists with fellow Leftists, Rightists with fellow Rightists, is not logic, but [_psycho-logic_](http://www.mtoomey.com/psychologic.html).  Feelings, not theories.
> 
> What's my alternative?  This:
> 
> 1\. Leftists are anti-market.  On an emotional level, they're critical of market outcomes.  No matter how good market outcomes are, they can't bear to say, "Markets have done a great job, who could ask for more?"
> 
> 2\. Rightists are anti-leftist.  On an emotional level, they're critical of leftists.  No matter how much they agree with leftists on an issue, they can't bear to say, "The left is totally right, it would be churlish to criticize them."

And with this theory, he criticised [Scott Alexander's,](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2017/09/whats_wrong_wit_23.html) arguing that society is not drifting leftward (the Soviet Union fell, etc), radical left parties were more prominent in poorer countries, and were not particularly socially liberal, and a few issues don't match the theory (abortion, for one). He also points out that the power of our theory may hinge on what we call left and right

> My Simplistic Theory \_intentionally \_fails to predict lots of details.  Why?  Because many of the features that Scott sees as quintessentially leftist - such as "freedom of religion, democratic-republican governments, weak gender norms, minimal family values, and a high emphasis on education and abstract ideas" - have been neglected or rejected by history's most influential, dyed-in-the-wool leftists.  The leftists Scott personally knows may cherish the items on his list.  To be frank, though, the leftists Scott personally knows are just a trendy subset of a diverse and enduring movement.

### Defining Left and Right

What facts should the theory fit, for Scott Alexander?

> **1)** Why do both ideologies combine seemingly unrelated political ideas? For example, why do people who want laissez-faire free trade empirically also prefer a strong military and oppose gay marriage? Why do people who want to help the environment also support feminism and dislike school vouchers?
> 
> **2)** Why do the two ideologies seem _broadly_ stable across different times and cultures, such that it’s relatively easy to point out the Tories as further right than the Whigs, or ancient Athens as further left than ancient Sparta? For that matter, why do they seem to correspond to certain neural patterns in the brain, such that [neurologists can determine your political beliefs with 83% accuracy by examining brain structure alone?](http://blogs.smithsonianmag.com/science/2013/02/study-predicts-political-beliefs-with-83-percent-accuracy/)
> 
> **3)** Why do these basically political ideas correlate so well with moral, aesthetic, and religious preferences?
> 
> **4)** The original question: how come, given enough time and left to itself, [leftism seems to usually win out over rightism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_history), pushing the Overton window a bit forward until there’s a new leftism and rightism?

My issue with this is that 1) is not quite true. You have libertarians, Vox leftists, SJW leftists, conservatives, alt-righters, fascists, and communists. These groups are quite different from each other, and they do not seem to neatly align in a 1D axis unless one is so enmeshed in the 1D political world that the 1D axis swampts the other differences.

Point 2) may have some truth to it, but it may also be availability bias. We have this image of Athenians as enlightened philosophers and Spartans as skilled warriors, but both societies featured large amounts of slavery, in Sparta [women apparently had more freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_ancient_Sparta) than elsewhere. And Spartans were apparently less open than the Athenians to commerce and trade.

Sparta does seem like what you would get when a society is configured for survival and expansion, and Athens not so. But that doesn't immediately map into modern categories of left and right. If one wants to define right as survival and left as thriving, one cannot keep the modern axis where you have socially conservative and economically liberal (in the classic sense) policies at one side, and socially liberal and economically progressive policies at the other, for if we define it like that, then Sparta's relative economic leftism would put it outside of a US-based version of what the right is.

Point 3) seems hard to evaluate - and it is not much discussed in the post? . Take [socialist realism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_realism) and compare it to the preferred style of the [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_the_Third_Reich) and you will note some similarities.

Point 4) As mentioned above, this is just not true. If anything, liberalism wins, which is closer to the Whig theory linked by Scott than leftism. (Unless liberalism is leftism!)

-   Fear that society might collapse (right) vs everything is okay (left)
-   Pro gun (right) vs anti gun (left)
-   Big and powerful police and military (right) vs smaller (left)
-   More religion (right) vs less religion (left)
-   Distrust of outsiders (right) vs trust (left)
-   Preference for hierarchy and conformity (right) vs the opposite (left)
-   Harsh enforcement of rules (right) vs less strict (left)
-   Wealth maximisation (right) vs other concerns (left)
-   Purity ethics (right) vs not having it (left)
-   Practical skills (right) vs bookish skills (left)
-   Black and white thinking (right) vs shades of grey (left)

This may make us think "Yeah, it all fits..." but when we bring into scene communists, or libertarians, it doesn't seem to fit. Central planning was a big thing back in the days of the USSR and Nazi Germany. It was also popular among [the progressive elites in the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illiberal_Reformers), which at the time also endorsed eugenics and policies that were biased against women, immigrants, the disabled and blacks. These guys [were also religious](https://newrepublic.com/article/128144/dark-history-liberal-reform). We need to explain last-century Progressivism too.

Similarly, (one could say) what unites past-day Progressives and the modern day Vox wonk is the belief that whatever they believe in is what serves the greater good. Thus the difference between the eugenicists of the past and the wonks of the present is more of empirics than of values. What then unites rightists?

Concerns about the nation? That thread also seems common among Progressives (not quite so with modern rational leftists)

Free marketism? (Not with Trump, or Theresa May. In what respects to markets, rightists seem to accept conservatism: keep what is on the table. Some of what they will then propose on the margins will be more or less pro-free market). Nazis certainly did keep private property and [markets relatively alone and crushed unions](https://pseudoerasmus.com/2015/05/03/fascism-left-or-right/). This is not free market, but pro-business (Or ultimately, whatever worked to keep churning Panzer IVs)

Or maybe rightists are also utilitarians! (Again, I don't really believe this). But this would be far fetched. It is true that utilitarianism[could commands](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2015/08/an-singerian-argument-against-abortion/) things that could be "rightist", such as increasing the fertility rate (perhaps banning abortions, or making contraception less available), and it is true that social conservatism has had its [utilitarian defenders](https://webcache.googleusercontent.com/search?q=cache:KRdafq89X1MJ:econlog.econlib.org/archives/2017/09/does_prosperity_1.html&num=1&hl=en&gl=uk&strip=1&vwsrc=0)). Utilitarianism is not probably what we are looking for.

I have my own theory, and my own facts to fit.

The facts to fit include what Scott Alexander has already mapped, when applied to Group A (US republican-types) and Group B (US democrat-types). But in addition:

-   Some people see things as getting better, others as getting worse, or stagnating
-   Some are more willing than others to use force to impose a more extensive set of values on society
-   Some desire a society that is just people doing their stuff and cooperating, others prefer a society tied together by a common purpose
-   Communism and fascism must be similar in at least one axis
-   Libertarianism and Vox leftism must be similar in at least one axis

Given that, my theory is this: There are three axes that define political ideology.

1.  Axis one is liberalism (Ideology)

Liberalism is something around the lines of recognizing people as moral equals, and having strong anti-coercion and anti-violence principles. Rule of law, civil rights, free speech, all that.  Liberals tends to think of individuals, not nations, and the liberal utopia is a society where everyone can do as they please without harming others, freely associating with each other.

Illiberalism does not see everyone as moral equals, and tends to be more group or nation focused. Rights are not seen as being posessed by individuals and recognised by states, but as being created and granted by states, perhaps for the benefit of society as a whole. Society should have some sense of common goal, and people should not just do what they want, they should think beyond themselves in some cause deemed worthy.

2\. Axis 2 is conservatism-progressivism (Mood)

Conservatism here means worrying that society might collapse, that cooperation is difficult, that trust is hard to gain and easy to lose, keeping in mind that institutions are made of people, and preferring small marginal changes to the status quo versus major changes, regardless of the expected outcome.

Progressives see opportunities for change and improvement. Cooperation is easy, everyone can be trusted. If there are big sweeping changes to be made that might make things better off, why not go for it. Things have been going well and they will continue to do so, is the belief of the progressive.

3\. Axis 3 is voluntarism-involuntarism (Compulsion)

Voluntarists would just leave people to do what they wish, without forcing them to do it, regardless of whether they should or not. For example, a voluntarist conservative may think that homosexual marriage is wrong, but he won't oppose banning it. An involuntarist liberal will be more open to coercing the rich to give to the poor.

With these three axis, we account for most political subgroupings.

Take nazis and communists. They both are high in compulsion, but they differ in their ideological orientation. Both are towards the illiberal side (one is biased against other nationalities, the other is biased against the bourgeoisie, with fascism being more illiberal than communism. And both are progressive ideologies: they look to radically transform the world, and are optimistic about being able to achieve that transformation.

Take now social democrats and libertarians. Both share liberal ideas, but social democrats are more willing to use force to enforce them. Within both groups there are conservative and progressive camps. Conservative libertarians (I would include here David Friedman, ~~Bryan Caplan ~~(Edit: Bryan is not a good example of being on the conservative pole), or public-choice libertarians) talk - comparatively - more about the negative effects of compulsion, and progressive libertarians (Matt Zwolinski or Jason Brennan) talk more about the good aspects of voluntarism. It is only in the progressive libertarian camp where we can find discussions of libertarianism in the frame of ideal theory (Brenann's Why not capitalism). Progressive social democrats tend to endorse things like UBI, and conservative social democrats minor tweaks to the existing system, usually emphasising how hard it was to get where we are. Conservative social democrats would be sneering at UBI defenders years ago for their utopian aspirations, while progressive social democrats would be keen to have open discussiong about it.

Neoreaction and nazis are different, but in what sense? Compulsion is the answer here. They share a nationalist (Illiberal) bias, and a progressive outlook of the world. Neoreaction wants to change things to make them as they used to be, and this requires massive changes. But nazis want to do it by force, while alt-righters want to do it more voluntarily through the application of the right of exclusion, or so they say.

Scott Alexander and myself are quite liberal, and we are also relatively low on compulsion scale, but he is somewhat more on the conservative direction as I am. He is the one who wrote the now classic Meditations on Moloch, a beautifully written explanation of coordination problems, and I was the one who wrote the (far less known, and less beautifully written) Slaying Alexander's Moloch, on why those problems are not that bad!

This theory places most people somewhere in the middle. US republicans and democrats are both somewhere in the middle in terms of liberalism, willingness to enforce their ideas, and how sanguine their are about the future, with conservatives being, naturally, more on the conservative side, and democrats being more on the progressive side.

Indeed, they are probably equally liberal, and equally willing to use compulsion, and their greater difference may be just one of mood, of how much they trust politicians, or on how much they think social problems can be solved by the government.

This is in contrast to other theories of the spectrum, where we have a defined axis that goes from one extreme to the other. The thrive/survive theory (Or Robin Hanson's, for that matter) would have a hard time with communism and fascism, mine doesn't.

But one may argue: Those theories at least try to find a common explanation, or even make predictions. Yours is like Caplan's: it merely describes what there is. Yes, that's fair.

Can we add some predictive meat to this model? I think we can.

-   (Ideology) Liberalism is predicted by IQ, not so much wealth.
-   (Mood) Conservatism/Progressivism is predicted by personality factors ( I can think of openness to experience)
-   (Compulsion) For this one I honestly have no idea.

Throughout history, there have been people more or less willing to try new things, and this probably will stay the same: there we have our progressives and conservatives, our eternal two blocks of social confrontation. But thanks to the Flynn effect, people are getting more and more liberal. Compulsion I said is a tricky one because if one looks at what clever people think, one indeed tends to find liberalism, both conservative and progressive fractions, and also people who are willing/not willing to use force to implement liberalism. This is, the key political conflict used to be one between illiberal and liberal values. Now we are migrating to a compulsion vs less compulsion world, if anything.

This theory also points at the fact that all your clever friends are equally liberal, but then they vary across the other dimensions: the elite political spectrum is bi-dimensional (The illiberal quarter is empty), with Vox leftists (progressive, high in compulsion), left libertarians (progressive, low in compulsion), right libertarians (conservative, low in compulsion), and  those few who advocate state paternalism a la Singapore (conservative, high in compulsion)

This theory is half-baked, and has lots of holes (e.g. Poland is a high IQ highly traditional society), but I believe this approximates reality and its change better than the other theories discussed above.
