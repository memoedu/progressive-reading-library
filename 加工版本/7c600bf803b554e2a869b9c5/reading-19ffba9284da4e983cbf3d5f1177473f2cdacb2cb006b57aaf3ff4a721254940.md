# 如何不再觉得自己是个冒牌货｜How to Stop Feeling Like an Imposter

---

## 正文

[Imposter syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impostor_syndrome) 是这样一种 feeling：你的 achievements 并不重要，你只是靠运气勉强过关，或者蒙骗了别人，让他们相信你。你对自己的 work 和 accomplishments 感到深深的 insecurity，总是 anxious，担心自己会被揭穿是个 fraud。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/fake.png)

虽然 arrogance 并不好，但我认为 impostor syndrome 的 prevalent 程度远超我们大多数人的认识。这是因为，对被“识破”的 fear 使那些受其困扰的人在沉默中苦苦煎熬。他们不愿承认自己的 insecurities，生怕这会让自己“真正”的 worthlessness 暴露在所有人面前。

Impostor syndrome 不只会 strike 那些 high-achieving 的人，即便 success 比较 modest 的人也可能受到它的影响。

它似乎也以比 men 更高的 rates 对 women 产生 impact；如果你觉得自己不符合所在 field 中“successful person”的 stereotypical image，这就不难理解。

## 我自己与 Insecurity 的 Struggles

收听这篇文章

写这篇文章多少有些讽刺，因为我并不是一个满怀 self-confidence 的人；恰恰相反，这确实是我每天都在挣扎应对的一种 feeling。事实上，写到这件事时，我觉得很不自在，仿佛承认自己的 insecurities，就会以某种方式在别人眼中进一步坐实自己的 inadequacy。

不过，如果退后一步审视，我能注意到自己生活中的一种 pattern。我会先看到某个 achieved 了某些事情的人，然后把自己与这样的人 compare，并想象他们是 successful 的，而我不是。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/before-achievement.png)

后来，如果我恰好 reach 了同样的 benchmark，自己做过的事此时又会显得 trivial；要想 qualify 为 accomplished 了什么，我还得 reach 一个新的 standard。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/after-achievement.png)

在公开场合，我已经学会在一定程度上 suppress 这种 instinct，但我发现，它 dominates 了我私下的许多 thoughts 和 feelings。

language learning 就是一个 example。当我[刚开始 learning French](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2009/06/09/update-on-my-goal-to-learn-french/) 时，我对 [Benny Lewis](https://www.fluentin3months.com/) 以及他 speak several languages 的 ability 敬佩不已。后来，[当我自己也做到了这一点](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/the-year-without-english-2/)，这项 achievement 却不再让我觉得 special，而只是 trivial。

现在，我的 feeling 是一种 anxiety，担心自己的 level 可能还不够好。过去，我本会认为能够 holding extended conversations 就已经“足够好”，现在却觉得，与 complete fluency 相比，这仍然是 inadequate 的——我应该能够 understand everything perfectly，并且 speak without mistakes。

这种 pattern——发现某件事很 special，reaching 它，然后立刻 discounting 它，认为它算不上“真正”的 accomplishment——在我自己的生活中 pervasive 地存在着。也许你也注意到了自己身上的这种 pattern。

setting harder goals 本身没有什么问题。impostor syndrome 的问题在于，你通过 changing 自己的 standards，把过去的 progress 放到你所认为的 successful 的标准“之下”，从而 erase 了自己过去的 progress。

## 为什么 Successful 的人常常觉得自己是 Failures？

要 understand impostor syndrome，我们需要看看什么可能 cause 它。这并不是一份 exhaustive list，不过，下面这两个重要的 factors 会 distort 你对自己的 thinking

### Factor #1：Achievements 是 Objective 的，但感觉却是 Relative 的

我们先从第一个 factor 开始：relative status。

Intuitively，跳出自身，以 birds-eye view 审视任何 achievement 的 field，并不困难。从这个角度，你可以根据人们的 overall level of success 对他们进行 rank。在 company 内，有 entry level、manager、vice president、C-level executive。Academia 中有 masters、PhDs、professors 和 prize-winning researchers。Athletics 中有 regional、national、Olympic 和 world-record performers。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/status-ladder.png)

然而，实际上，相比于这个 absolute scale of excellence，我们对自己在其中的 relative position 要 sensitive 得多。因此，对于我们上方和下方的 rungs，我们看到的 detail 更多，conscious awareness 也更强；而对于那些距离很远的 rungs，则不是如此。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/status-ladder-relative.png)

对我来说，所有远高于我的 achievement 的 rungs 都足够遥远，基本上可以忽略。那些 world-famous 的 Authors。那些已经 mastered 40 languages 的 Linguists。那些因在许多 subjects 中展现 brilliance 而获得 recognized 的 Polymaths。这些 accomplishments 远远超出了我的经历，因此完全不会给我带来 anxiety。

### Insecurity 来自对恰好位于你上方的 Levels 的 Magnifying

然而，我更容易注意到那些在 rank 中略高于我的人。因此，谈到 topic expertise，我会把自己与那些在 grad school 中 studied subjects deeply 的人 compare，并觉得自己不如他们。我会把自己与那些 run 着拥有几十名 employees 的“真正”的 businesses 的人 compare。我会把自己与那些能够把我会的某种 language 说得 fluently 的人 compare，而我的程度只是 intermediate level。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/progress-perception.png)

问题在于，一旦你上升了一个 rank，更上方的 rank 就会突然进入视野。当我从 solo business 发展为 team 时，这项新的 accomplishment 立刻成了 baseline，我的 comparison 范围也随之扩大。如果我在某个领域获得 PhD，我就会立刻意识到自己与 tenured professors 或 recognized experts 之间的 difference。

简而言之，我们的 relative status perception 意味着，在我们上方和下方，总有一个 clearly-defined 的 rung。**因此，Insecurity 可能来自对你上方那个 rung 的 over-emphasis，而不是 recognizing 你已经 climbed 的那些 rungs**，以及你已经 surpassed 的那些 successes。

imposter syndrome 与此不同的地方在于，它往往让你觉得，就连当前的 rung 也似乎是 untenable 的。你所 reached 的 position，是靠运气、circumstance，或者因为别人 incorrectly perceived 你做了什么 impressive 的事才得到的。因此，你始终怀有一种 fear，担心自己会滑回更早的 rungs，或者彻底跌下 ladder，摔得一败涂地。

### Factor #2：Insecurity 的 Invisibility

imposter syndrome 的另一个 contributing factor 是，你可能没有意识到其他人也在经历它。别人看起来如此 confident、secure，而且充满 self-esteem，这可能会 reinforce 你的 belief，让你觉得自己的 doubts 就是自己 illegitimacy 的 proof。

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/invisible-insecurity-1.png)

当被问到自己的 peers 中有多少人有过性行为，或他们[喝多少酒](https://consumer.healthday.com/general-health-information-16/alcohol-abuse-news-12/college-students-overestimate-peers-drinking-528146.html)时，大学生会严重地 [overestimate 其 promiscuity](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/students-today-hook-up-no-more-than-parents-college/)，也会 overestimate 他们的聚会狂欢程度。似乎其他人都玩得热闹尽兴，只有你独自一人，过得无聊。

这种 overestimation 似乎源于一种 [availability heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Availability_heuristic)。那些看起来玩得很疯的人非常显眼，而待在家里学习的人则不然。因此，我们会 overestimate 这些情况的 occurrence。

这也可能 lead to 一些 depression，尤其是当你认为玩乐和频繁约会是学生时代的一个重要 goal 时。你会觉得只有自己是这样，于是你的 insecurities 会 deepen。如果你能看到实际情况（大多数人的性生活和聚会狂欢并没有你想象的那么多），你或许就会对自己感到更 secure。

同样，如果你能走进许多 successful 人士的私人生活，看到他们如何与自己的 insecurities 挣扎，或担心自己达到的 levels of success 已经 exceeding 了自身的 merit，你或许就会安心，相信自己的 feelings 是 normal 的。然而，confident 的人非常显眼，而那些与 doubts 挣扎的人却闭口不谈。

## 克服你的 Imposter Syndrome

我认为，克服自身 insecurities 的第一步，是意识到它们实际上有多么 normal。人们表现出的许多看似 confidence 和 bravado 的东西，其实只是薄薄地掩盖着他们对自己的 doubts。

一旦你不再把 self-doubt 和 insecurity 看作自己独有的 disorder，而是把它们看作会 impacts 许多人的东西，就会更容易坦然接受这些 feelings。

另一个对我有用的建议，是阅读 successful 人士的传记。原因在于，你会惊讶地发现，有那么多极其 successful 的人，一生都在与强烈的 self-doubt 和 insecurities 挣扎。著名诗人 [Maya Angelou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Angelou) 曾写道：

> “我已经写了 11 本书，但每一次我都会想：‘哎呀，这下他们要发现了。我把所有人都骗了，他们就要识破我了。’”

接下来，要 consciously adjust 你的 fixation，不再紧盯 achievement ladder 上就在你面前的那一级。你或许还不习惯这样做，但请把注意力放在你已经攀登过的那些级，以及随之而来的困难上。这样可以避免你忍不住把过去的 achievements 贬低为 trivial，却把眼前尚未达到的 achievements 当作真正重要的东西。

最后，我建议在 evaluating 别人的 works 和 accomplishments 时，采取更温和的看法。不断 Attack 和 criticize 别人，最终你也会 attacking 自己。当你开始 exaggerating 别人的 flaws 时，你自己的 flaws 看起来也会被 magnified。

我不知道有什么万无一失的 cure，能立刻让你对自己充满持久的 confidence。我也不确定，即使存在这样的 cure，过于频繁地使用它就一定是个好主意。不过，moderating 自己 discount 自身 achievements 的 tendency，并更坦然地接受自己已经达到的 position，是我们都可以努力的方向。

## 原文

[Imposter syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impostor_syndrome) is the feeling that your achievements don’t matter. That you only scraped by because of luck or by fooling others into believing in you. You feel deep insecurity about your work and accomplishments, always anxious that you’ll be exposed as a fraud.

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/fake.png)

While arrogance isn’t good, I think impostor syndrome is far more prevalent than most of us realize. This is because the fear of being “found out” causes those who suffer from it to languish in silence. They don’t want to admit their insecurities for fear that this may trigger the revelation of their “true” worthlessness for all to see.

Impostor syndrome doesn’t just strike those who are high-achieving, but can hit even people who have more modest success.

It also seems to impact women more at higher rates than men, which makes sense if you don’t see yourself as fitting the stereotypical image of what a “successful person” looks like in your field.

## My Own Struggles with Insecurity

Listen to this article  

There’s some irony in writing this post because rather than being brimming with self-confidence, this is definitely a feeling I struggle with daily. Indeed, I feel awkward writing about it, as if admitting my own insecurities might somehow bolster proof of my own inadequacy in the eyes of others.

However, if I step back, I can notice a pattern in my own life. I start by looking at someone else who has achieved something, I will compare myself to such a person and imagine that they are successful, while I am not.

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/before-achievement.png)

Later, if I happen to reach the same benchmark, what I did now feels trivial, and there’s a new standard that needs to be reached if I can qualify as having accomplished something.

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/after-achievement.png)

Publicly, I’ve learned to suppress this instinct somewhat, but I find it dominates a lot of my private thoughts and feelings.

An example would be language learning. When I [first started learning French](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2009/06/09/update-on-my-goal-to-learn-french/), I was in awe of [Benny Lewis](https://www.fluentin3months.com/) and his ability to speak several languages. Later, [when I did so for myself](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/the-year-without-english-2/), the achievement no longer felt special, but simply trivial.

Now, my feeling is an anxiety that my level might not be good enough. While I would have previously considered holding extended conversations to be “good enough,” now I felt like it was inadequate compared to complete fluency—I should be able to understand everything perfectly and speak without mistakes.

This pattern—finding something special, reaching it, and then immediately discounting that as not being a “true” accomplishment—is something I’ve found pervasive in my own life. Perhaps it is a pattern you notice in yourself as well.

There’s nothing wrong with setting harder goals. The problem with impostor syndrome is that you erase your own past progress by changing your standards to put it “below” what you consider successful.

## Why Do Successful People Often Feel Like Failures?

To understand impostor syndrome, we need to look at what can cause it. This isn’t an exhaustive list, but here are two big factors that distort your thinking about yourself

### Factor #1: Achievements are Objective Yet Feel Relative

Let’s start with the first factor: relative status.

Intuitively, it’s easy to step outside and take a birds-eye view of any field of achievement. From there, you can rank people on their overall level of success. Within a company, there is entry level, manager, vice president, C-level executive. Academia has masters, PhDs, professors and prize-winning researchers. Athletics has regional, national, Olympic and world-record performers.

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/status-ladder.png)

However, in reality, we’re much less sensitive to this absolute scale of excellence than we are to our relative position within it. As such, we see the rungs above us and below us in much greater detail and conscious awareness than those which are quite distant.

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/status-ladder-relative.png)

For me, all the rungs of achievement much above me are far enough away that I can basically ignore them. Authors who are world-famous. Linguists who’ve mastered 40 languages. Polymaths who were recognized for their brilliance in many subjects. These accomplishments are so beyond what I’ve experienced, that they don’t cause me any anxiety at all.

### Insecurity Comes from Magnifying the Levels Just Above You

However, I am more aware of people who are slightly above me in the rank. When it comes to topic expertise, therefore, I compare myself unfavorably to those who have studied subjects deeply in grad school. I compare myself to people who run “real” businesses with dozens of employees. I compare myself to people who speak a language I know fluently, instead of my intermediate level.

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/progress-perception.png)

The problem is that once you go up one rank, the rank above that suddenly snaps into view. When I went from a solo business to a team, immediately that new accomplishment became the baseline and my comparison grew. If I were to get a PhD in something, I’d become immediately aware of the difference I had with tenured professors or recognized experts.

In short, our relative status perception means that there’s always a clearly-defined rung above us and below. **Insecurity therefore, can come from an over-emphasis on the rung above you, rather than recognizing the rungs you’ve already climbed** and the successes you’ve already surpassed.

Where imposter syndrome differs from this, is that it tends to make even the current rung seem untenable. The position you’ve reached was through luck, circumstance or because others incorrectly perceived you to have done something impressive. Therefore, there’s a constant fear that you’ll slide back to earlier rungs or fall off the ladder altogether in a spectacular crash.

### Factor #2: Invisibility of Insecurity

Another contributing factor to imposter syndrome is that you may not be aware that other people are experiencing it. The fact that others seem so confident, secure and full of self-esteem may reinforce your belief that your own doubts are proof of your illegitimacy.

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/invisible-insecurity-1.png)

When asked how many of their peers have had sex or [how much alcohol they drink](https://consumer.healthday.com/general-health-information-16/alcohol-abuse-news-12/college-students-overestimate-peers-drinking-528146.html), college students grossly [overestimate the promiscuity](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/students-today-hook-up-no-more-than-parents-college/) and partying of their peers. Everyone else, it seems, is having a raucous time and it’s just you who is alone and boring.

This overestimation seems to be from an [availability heuristic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Availability_heuristic). The people who seem to be having wild fun are highly visible, whereas those staying at home studying are not. Therefore, we overestimate their occurrence.

This may also lead to some depression, especially if you think having fun and going on lots of dates is an important goal in your student days. It feels like you’re the only one, and thus your insecurities deepen. If you could see the reality (most people aren’t getting laid and partying as much as you think) then you’d probably feel more secure in yourself.

Similarly, if you could enter into the private lives of many successful people, and see how they struggle with their insecurities or worries that they may have reached levels of success exceeding their merit, you’d probably be reassured that your own feelings are normal. Yet confident people are highly visible, those who struggle with doubts keep their mouths shut.

## Overcoming Your Imposter Syndrome

I think the first step to overcoming your own insecurities is to realize how normal they actually are. That much of the seeming confidence and bravado people display is really thinly covering up their own doubts about themselves.

Once you see self-doubt and insecurity, not as a unique disorder you exhibit, but something that impacts many people, it’s a lot easier to get comfortable with those feelings.

Another useful tip from me has been to read successful people’s biographies. The reason is that you’d be surprised how many extremely successful people spent their whole lives grappling with intense self-doubt and insecurities. Famed poet, [Maya Angelou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Angelou) once wrote:

> “I have written 11 books, but each time I think, ‘Uh oh, they’re going to find out now. I’ve run a game on everybody, and they’re going to find me out.’”

Next is to consciously adjust your fixation away from that rung of the achievement ladder immediately in front of you. It may not be habitual, but focus on the rungs you’ve already climbed and the difficulties that came with them. This can avoid the temptation to write down your past achievements as being trivial and the ones in front of you as being those that truly matter.

Finally, I would recommend taking a softer view when evaluating the works and accomplishments of others. Attack and criticize others constantly, and you’ll end up attacking yourself. When you start exaggerating the flaws of others, your own will seem to be magnified as well.

I don’t know of any bulletproof cure that will immediately fill you with lasting confidence in yourself. I’m not sure if one existed it would necessarily be a good idea to use it too often. But, moderating your own tendency to discount your achievements and feel more comfortable in the position you’ve reached is something we can all aim for.
