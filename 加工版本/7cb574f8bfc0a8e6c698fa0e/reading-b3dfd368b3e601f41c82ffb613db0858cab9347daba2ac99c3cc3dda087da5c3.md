## 正文

你可能以为自己了解这件事，但其实并不了解，我也是直到最近才有所了解，所以这里又有一篇 Nintil 文章，能让你有所认识。

这篇文章可能会引起争议。欢迎在评论区留言。如果你认为有我尚未考虑的 evidence，或者有我未曾恰当 weighted 的 evidence，请告诉我。

我们先看两段引文：

> Industrial farming 对 animals 是 abusive 的。先说 Pigs。在美国，每十只 pregnant sows 中有九只生活在“gestation crates”中。这些 pens 如此 small，以至于 pigs 几乎无法 move。sows 刚被 crated 时，会四处 flail，仿佛正试图逃出 crate。但很快它们就放弃了。pigs 经常 show signs of depression：它们会 engage in meaningless, repetitive behavior，例如 chewing the air 或 biting the bars of the stall。这些 animals 在这样的 conditions 中生活四个月。（Vegetarianism，James Rachels）

> 人们试图通过改变 purchasing habits 来有所作为的另一个领域，是 meat eating 和 vegetarianism。正如我前面提到的，cutting out meat（尤其是 beef）是 reduce 你的 carbon emissions 的一种 effective way。不过，我们也已经看到，通过向 Cool Earth 捐款，只需约五美元就能 offset one metric ton of carbon emissions。如果你宁愿付五美元，也不愿 go vegetarian，那么支持 vegetarianism 的 environmental argument 就相当 weak。
> 
> 支持 vegetarianism 的 animal welfare argument 则 comparatively stronger。绝大多数 farmed animals 都是在 factory farms 中 raised 的，这些 factory farms 仅仅为了稍微 cheaper 的 produce，就对这些 animals inflict severe and unnecessary suffering。factory farm animals 的 living conditions 已经在书籍、杂志和纪录片中得到了广泛记录，所以这里就不向你罗列那些令人不忍卒读的细节了。（MacAskill，Doing Good Better）

Rachels 的文章接着描述了 animal farming industry 所采用的各种 practises。由于它们 cause 大量 suffering，而 ~生命是~ suffering 是 bad 的，这篇文章要求 ban factory farming animals。如果你更喜欢影像版本，可以观看最近有人向我推荐的纪录片 Earthlings，但它并没有比 Rachels 的文章多提供多少内容，除非你有情绪之类的东西。

请注意，MacAskill 的书中提供了对 animal welfare 的 estimate，将 cattle 和 cows 的 lives 评为 worth living，而将 chicken、pigs 和 hens 的 lives 评为不 worth living。（**在美国**）。

那么，我们先列出上面提到的 practises：

-   Pigs（猪）
    -   Gestation crates（妊娠限位栏）
    -   Tail docking（断尾）

    -   Crowding（拥挤）
-   Cows（牛）
    -   Hot-iron branding 和 castration（热铁烙印和阉割）

James Rachels 谈的是**美国的**情况。我的 hypothesis 是，大多数人对 animal welfare 的印象都基于上述内容，因此也就基于 US data。于是我去了 [Animal Charity Evaluators](https://animalcharityevaluators.org/donation-advice/top-charities/)，并 recursively check 他们的 links，看来确实如此。有一些值得关注的视频，不过我稍后再谈。

首先，我会回顾英国 e[xisting animal welfare laws](https://www.gov.uk/topic/keeping-farmed-animals/animal-welfare) 的要点。

> ### animal welfare 的 five freedoms
> 
> Animal welfare legislation 和 Farm Animal Welfare Council (FAWC)——一个 government advisory body——规定，你始终有 duty of care，须 ensure 你的 animals 享有以下 freedom：
> 
> -   免于 hunger 和 thirst——animals 必须能够获得 fresh water，以及能 maintain health and vigour 的 diet
> -   免于 discomfort——应提供 appropriate environment，包括 shelter 和 comfortable resting area
> -   免于 pain、injury 或 disease——你必须 ensure 对 illnesses 的 prevention，或 rapid diagnosis and treatment
> -   能够 express normal behaviour——应提供 sufficient space、proper facilities，以及 animal’s own kind 的 company
> -   免于 fear 和 distress——你必须提供能 avoid mental suffering 的 conditions 和 treatment

这项 law 的 goal 是 ensure animals 拥有 life worth living（这不同于 good life，下文会进一步讨论）

总体而言，适用的 laws 似乎是 Animal Welfare Act 2006、Welfare of Farmed Animals Regulations 2007、Welfare of Farmed Animals Regulations 2010，以及 Mutilations Regulations 2007 和 2010。

这些宽泛的 five freedoms 听起来足够美好，但在实践中，它们是否 ban 了 Rachels 所列举的 practices？

我们不妨看看 [pigs](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-recommendations-for-the-welfare-of-livestock-pigs)。Gestation crates 被 explicitly banned。英国使用的是 Farrowing crates（larger）。这些 crates 应当允许 sow rise up 和 lie down (93-97)。Tail docking 不受鼓励，被列为 last resort procedure；如果 pig 超过 7 天大，进行时须使用 anaesthesia。Routine teeth clipping 是 illegal 的，仅允许作为 exceptional measure 使用。Crowding 也受到 regulated，不过对于所允许的 space 是否足够好，我不是专家。

对于 [chicken](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/poultry-on-farm-welfare)，beak trimming [是 allowed 的](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2010/9780111503553/regulation/2)，但 limited 于 beak 的三分之一，而且只允许——但不鼓励——对不足 10 天大的 birds 进行，并且须使用 infra-red debeaking；这似乎是一个 quick 且 relatively painless 的 process（相较于欧洲大部分地区和美国使用的 hot blade trimming）。一个 action group 最近[对这项 policy 进行了 reviewed](https://www.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/480111/Beak-Trimming-Action-Group-Review.pdf)，以确定它应被 banned，还是 allowed to continue。2011 年，曾有人寻求对 beak trimming 实施 ban，但最终没有 imposed，因为那样就无法 averting birds pecking at each other，因此 enacted 了 Mutilations Regulations 2010，其中 imposes 了本段开头详述的 conditions。每 metre 的 chicken 的 Density 也受到 regulated (59-64)。所有 birds 每天都必须能够获得 feed (11)，应采取 measures 来 minimise leg health issues (33)，而发现有此类 issues 的 birds 会被 humanely killed (32)。Battery cages 也是 illegal 的（与美国大部分地区不同）。

对于 [cows](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-recommendations-for-the-welfare-of-livestock-cattle)、calves 等，禁止对超过 2 个月大的 animals 在不使用 anesthetic 的情况下进行 castration。这项 law 推荐三种 castration 的 methods，例如 restriction of scrotal blood vessels（仅限不足 7 天大的 animal）、bloodless castration（crushing the spermetic cords），最后是老办法，即使用 anesthesia 进行 castration。Dehorning 不得作为 routine procedure 进行；进行时应使用 anesthesia，并在之后提供 painkillers。Hot iron branding 是 illegal 的。Ear tagging 是 allowed 的，但操作方式应 avoid piercing main blood vessels。不必说，temperature control 和 sanitation regulations 也已到位。Tail docking 是[不](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2007/1100/schedule/1/made) allowed 的。Diet 也受到 regulated，并 mandates 其中须含有一定量的 roughage（如 hay）。如果[这份资料](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-industry/farmingfood/animalfeed/what-farm-animals-eat)可信，实际的 diet 由 forage crops 构成。Calves 在 birth 后须与其 dam 留在一起至少 12 小时——最好是 24 小时——而且 law 推荐 calf 在最初三天从其 mother 处获得 colostrum。

此外，关于英国 slaughterhouses，一个鲜为人知的事实是，其中许多都配备了 CCTV systems。虽然这并非 legally mandatory，但 retailers 要求其 suppliers 配备这些 systems，一些 certification schemes 也有此要求。其中包括英国大多数 supermarkets。到 2013 年，所有 slaughterhouses 中有 40-60% 配备了 CCTV。不过，大多数 slaughterhouses 都是 small installations。如果我们改为按 processed 的 animals 的 number 进行 weigh，这些 percentages 就会升至 80-98%。

最后，还有其他 voluntary standards，producers 可以选择 adhere to，例如 [RSPCA Freedom Food](https://www.berspcaassured.org.uk/rspca-welfare-standards/) scheme。这些 standards 要求 better conditions。

但它们真的这样做了吗？

我正是在这个博客上写过[苏联在其 Constitution 中承诺一套，实际做的却是另一套](https://nintil.com/2016/05/14/the-soviet-union-series/)的人。farms 真的 comply with 上述 regulations 吗？我会先提供一些相反的 evidence，然后再作回应。

我们的第一个例子是 [Viva! 的 Cruel Britannia：英国 factory farms 中的 Life 和 death](http://www.viva.org.uk/what-we-do/cruel-britannia-life-and-death-uk-factory-farms) (2013)，它展示了几个 law breaches 的例子。

接着，[Animal Aid](http://www.animalaid.org.uk/h/n/CAMPAIGNS/factory//3047//) 也有几个展示 noncompliance 的视频，时间从 2007 年到 2016 年不等。（见附录 I）

然后，government sources 的 official count 显示，过去两年共有 [4000 起 animal welfare laws 的 major breaches](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2016/aug/28/fsa-4000-breaches-animal-welfare-laws-uk-abattoirs-two-years)（每起 breach 涉及一只或多只 animal）。其中包括我们可能在 animal welfare 纪录片中看到的事情，例如 cows 被猛撞到墙上、chickens 被 boiled alive 等。假定每起 breach affects 50 只 animals（一起 breach 可能涉及 1 只到数百只 animals），那么按每年每只 slaughtered 的 animal 计算，incidences 的 proportion 约为总数的 1%。

我们还有来自 AnimalAid 的[这串信息](http://animalaid.org.uk/h/n/NEWS/pr_factory//2053//)，他们在其中声称，一份 2008 年的 EFSA report 发现 tail docking ratio 为 75-80%，这肯定 implies 了被 forbidden 的 routine tail docking。EFSA report 在[这里](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2007.611/abstract)。接着我们翻到第 23 页，那里指向一项 2006 年的 BPHS (British Pig Health Scheme) study，但事实证明，根本找不到它。因此，我们改用向 British Parliament 提交的[这份 report](https://www.publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm200809/cmselect/cmenvfru/96/96.pdf)作为 supporting evidence，相信编写这份 report 的人确实查阅了那份 report。[FAWC](https://www.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/325042/FAWC_opinion_on_pig_mutilations_and_environmental_enrichment.pdf) (2011) 也相信这个 number。有意思的是，他们在同一份 report 中 assert：

> 85\. **我们认为，一只 piglet 即使经历了这里所讨论的一种、乃至几种 mutilations，也不会因此被 prevented from having “a life worth living”**。不过，这会使它无法拥有“a good life”，而 FAWC 也曾建议，我们应当努力为 animals 提供这样的生活14。在这方面，提供能让 pig express biologically relevant behaviours 的 appropriate environmental enrichment，是一项重要 consideration，而不取决于它在 preventing harmful behaviours（如 tail biting）方面是否有任何 benefit。

在 European Parliament 的 study [Routine Tail-Docking of Pigs](http://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/STUD/2014/509997/IPOL_STU\(2014\)509997_EN.pdf) 中，他们提到了另一次 survey，时间是 2014，也包括 UK；在那次 survey 中，UK 被发现 compliant 于 European 针对 routine tail docking 的 regulations。data 本身汇编在[这里](http://stud.epsilon.slu.se/7178/)。那里链接了两份 Food and Veterinary Office 的 reports，较晚的一份来自 2009，涉及实地走访总共……两家 pig farms。这种 sample size 看起来太小，不足以 judge compliance，不是吗？

那么看来，law 确实有一些回旋余地。Tail docking 在有需要时是 allowed 的，但如果是 routine process，就不 allowed。但如果它作为 routine process 是必需的呢？

undercover videos 的问题在于，我们无法确定 video 中所展示情况的 prevalence。我们需要类似每年对 factory farms 进行 evaluations 的资料，看看 noncompliance 占总数的 percentage 是多少。幸运的是，UK Government 有这样的 data：

## 官方 data

在 UK government 内部，animal welfare 是 Department for Environment, Food, and Rural Affairs (Defra) 的职责；在该部门内部，则由[Farm Animal Welfare Committee (FAWC)](https://www.gov.uk/government/groups/farm-animal-welfare-committee-fawc#our-members) 负责。[这里](https://www.food.gov.uk/sites/default/files/fsa150605.pdf)可以找到他们最近一次 board meeting minutes，其中讨论的事项包括一次 unannounced inspection programme 的 results，该 programme 覆盖了 317 家 slaughterhouses 中的 306 家。results 是：87% 的 slaughterhouses 达到了 standards，12% 需要 improvement，只有一家 facility 需要“urgent improvement”。注意，这并没有按 processed 的 pigs 的 number 进行 weighted。较大的 slaughterhouses 更加 comply 于 regulations，是很有可能的。

[另一份 report](https://www.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/319292/Farm_Animal_Welfare_in_Great_Britain_-_Past__Present_and_Future.pdf)来自 2009，提供了关于 factory farms 的 welfare 自 1965 以来 improvement 的 evidence。report 表示，这些 laws 的 aim 并不是给予 farmed animals 一种 good life，而是给予它们每一个一种 life worth living。report 还说，compliance levels 很 high。

[这份 report](http://webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk/20130402151656/http://archive.defra.gov.uk/corporate/about/who/cvo/documents/2009report.pdf)也来自 2009，**根据不同 farms 的 9963 次 welfare inspections，确定 random inspections 的 level of compliance 为 97%。对于按 risk model 在 ex-ante 被预计较少 comply 的 farms，compliance rates 较低，为 77%。在所研究的全部情况中，大多数 cases 的“unnecesary pain, suffering, or distress”似乎都 below 1%**（涉及不同 animals 和不同 welfare critaria），这恰好接近我在本文前面所作的 ballpark estimate。

我已向 UK 的 Chief Veterinary Office 发了一封电子邮件，请求提供更新后的 figures。本文发表一个月后，我应该就能收到这些 figures，而本文届时也应该已经据此更新。

[World Animal Protection](http://api.worldanimalprotection.org/compare) 对 UK 在 animal welfare protection 方面的整体努力给予 A，对 United States 给予 D；对于 farmed animals 的评级也一样。

## 结论

根据已经呈现的 evidence，我认为，UK factory farms 中的大多数 animals

-   并不 show meaningful levels 的 animal suffering（也就是说，仅从 hedonic standpoint 来看，UK 的 factory farmed animals 的 aggregate 是 net positive）
-   确实 show 对 strong animal rights 的 violations（但要考虑到，任何 farm、zoo 或 keeping pets 这一行为，很可能都 implies 这一点）
-   具有与 UK law 相 in compliance 的 welfare level（存在一些 non-conformances）
-   自 1965 以来，welfare 的 level 一直在 increasing
-   并不 necessarily 在与其他国家 factory farmed animals 相同的 conditions 中 live

大体上，inference chain 是这样的

1.  UK 的 Animal welfare laws 会 set standards，使得如果存在 compliance，animal lives 就会是 worth living 的，也不会 incur 很多 suffering（我将此视为一个 premise。我尚未 assessed，从 biological 或 veterinarian 的角度看，它们是否足够好）
2.  Farms 对 UK 的 animal welfare laws 的 comply，至少达到了 animal suffering 不会太 severe 的程度（支持这一点的是 government inspections。反对这一点的是 undercover videos。考虑到 data samples 的 size，以及 videos 中大多数 animals 的 suffering 的 severity 并不是很 high，我认为 official data 大体正确。

## 原文

You'd think you know about it, but you don't, and I didn't either until recently, so here's yet another Nintil post that will enlighten you.

This post might be controversial. Comments are welcome in the comments section. If you think there is evidence that I have not considered, or evidence that I haven't properly weighted, please let me know.

We begin with a pair of quotes:

> Industrial farming is abusive to animals. Pigs. In America, nine out of ten of pregnant sows live in “gestation crates.” These pens are so small that the pigs can hardly move. When the sows are first crated, they flail around, as if they’re trying to escape from the crate. But soon they give up. The pigs often show signs of depression: they engage in meaningless, repetitive behavior, like chewing the air or biting the bars of the stall. The animals live in these conditions for four months. (Vegetarianism, James Rachels)

> Another area where people try to change their purchasing habits in order to make a difference is meat eating and vegetarianism. As I mentioned earlier, cutting out meat (especially beef) is one effective way to reduce your carbon emissions. However, we’ve also seen that by donating to Cool Earth you can offset one metric ton of carbon emissions for about five dollars. If you’d rather pay five dollars than go vegetarian, then the environmental argument for vegetarianism is rather weak.
> 
> The animal welfare argument for vegetarianism is comparatively stronger. The vast majority of farmed animals are raised in factory farms, which inflict severe and unnecessary suffering on those animals merely for the sake of slightly cheaper produce. The living conditions of factory farm animals have been extensively documented in books, magazines, and documentaries, so I will spare you the grim details here. (MacAskill, Doing Good Better)

Rachels' essay continues with descriptions of a variety of practises the animal farming industry engages in. Since they cause much suffering, and ~life is~ suffering is bad, the article asks for a ban on factory farming animals. If you prefer a graphical version, you could watch the documentary Earthlings, which was recently recommended to me, but it doesn't add much to Rachels' essay, unless you have emotions and all of that.

Note than in MacAskill's book, an estimate of animal welfare is provided, rating the lives of cattle and cows as worth living, and those of chicken, pigs, and hens as not worth living. (**In the US**).

So, let's first list the above mentioned practises:

-   Pigs
    -   Gestation crates
    -   Tail docking
    -   Needle teeth clipping and ear marking without anaesthesia
    -   Crowding
-   Cows
    -   Hot-iron branding and castration
    -   Tail docking
    -   Dehorning (through sensitive tissue)
    -   Ear cutting for identification
    -   Separation of cow and calf shortly after birth
    -   Exposure to extreme temperatures (cold, hot weather)
    -   Poor sanitation
    -   Grain based diet, which leads to a variety of medical conditions
    -   Transporting by road without food and water
-   Chickens
    -   Beak mutilation without pain relief
    -   Extreme crowding
    -   Chronic pain from bone disease
    -   Hen underfeeding
    -   Small wire cages

James Rachels talks about the situation **in the US**. It is my hypothesis that the image of animal welfare that most people have is based on the above, and thus on US data. So to [Animal Charity Evaluators](https://animalcharityevaluators.org/donation-advice/top-charities/) I go, and recursively check their links, and so it seems to be the case. There are some videos of interest, but I'll come to that later.

First, I'll review the main points of the e[xisting animal welfare laws](https://www.gov.uk/topic/keeping-farmed-animals/animal-welfare) in the UK.

> ### The five freedoms of animal welfare
> 
> Animal welfare legislation and the Farm Animal Welfare Council (FAWC) - a government advisory body - state that, at all times, you have a duty of care to ensure your animals have freedom:
> 
> -   from hunger and thirst - animals must have access to fresh water and a diet which will maintain health and vigour
> -   from discomfort - an appropriate environment should be provided, including shelter and a comfortable resting area
> -   from pain, injury or disease - you must ensure the prevention of illnesses, or rapid diagnosis and treatment
> -   to express normal behaviour - sufficient space, proper facilities and company of the animal’s own kind should be provided
> -   from fear and distress - you must provide conditions and treatment which avoid mental suffering

The law has as a goal to ensure that animals have a life worth living (Which is different from a good life, more on that below)

Overall, the laws applicable seem to be the Animal Welfare Act 2006, the Welfare of Farmed Animals Regulations 2007, the Welfare of Farmed Animals Regulations 2010, and the Mutilations Regulations 2007 and 2010.

The broad five freedoms sound nice enough, but in practise, do they ban the practices that Rachels enumerates?

Let's look at, say, [pigs](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-recommendations-for-the-welfare-of-livestock-pigs). Gestation crates are explicitly banned.  Farrowing crates (larger) are used in the UK. These crates should allow the sow to rise up and lie down (93-97). Tail docking is discouraged, and is mentioned as a last resort procedure, to be carried out with anaesthesia if the pig is older than 7 days. Routine teeth clipping is illegal, and is only permitted as a exceptional measure. Crowding is also regulated, though I'm not an expert on whether the space allowed is good enough.

For [chicken](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/poultry-on-farm-welfare), beak trimming [is allowed](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2010/9780111503553/regulation/2), but limited to one third of the beak, and is only allowed - but discouraged- for birds younger than 10 days, and using infra-red debeaking, which seems to be quick and relatively painless process (compared to hot blade trimming, used in most of Europe and the US). An action group recently [reviewed the policy](https://www.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/480111/Beak-Trimming-Action-Group-Review.pdf) to see if it should be banned or allowed to continue In 2011, a ban was sought on beak trimming, but it wasn't imposed because there would be no way of averting birds pecking at each other, so the Mutilations Regulations 2010 was enacted, which imposes the conditions detailed at the beginning of this paragraph. Density of chicken per metre is also regulated (59-64). All birds must have daily access to feed (11), measures should be taken to minimise leg health issues (33), and birds found to have such issues are humanely killed (32). Battery cages are also illegal (unlike in most of the US).

For [cows](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/code-of-recommendations-for-the-welfare-of-livestock-cattle), calves, etc, castration without anesthetic of  animals over 2 months old is forbidden. The law recommends three methods of castration, such as restriction of scrotal blood vessels (only if the animal is less than 7 days old), bloodless castration (crushing the spermetic cords), and finally, plain old castration with anesthesia. Dehorning is not allowed as a routine procedure, and when practised, it should be done under anesthesia, with painkillers provided afterwards. Hot iron branding is illegal. Ear tagging is allowed, but it should be done in such a way as to avoid piercing main blood vessels. Needless to say, temperature control and sanitation regulations are also in place. Tail docking is [not](http://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2007/1100/schedule/1/made) allowed. Diet is also regulated, and mandates some presence of roughage (like hay). If [this](https://www.food.gov.uk/business-industry/farmingfood/animalfeed/what-farm-animals-eat) is to be believed, in practise the diet consists of forage crops. Calves are to be left with their dam for at least 12 hours - preferably 24 - after birth, and the law recommends that the calf should receive colostrum from its mother for the first three days.

In addition, a little known fact about UK slaughterhouses is that many are equipped with CCTV systems. Altough not legally mandatory, retailers demand their suppliers to have them, and some certification schemes also demand them. This includes most of the supermarkets in the UK. By 2013, 40-60% of all slaughterhouses had CCTV. However, most of slaughterhouses are small installations. If we instead weigh by the number of animals processed, the percentages raise to 80-98%.

Finally, there are other, voluntary, standards, that producers can adhere to, such as the [RSPCA Freedom Food](https://www.berspcaassured.org.uk/rspca-welfare-standards/) scheme. These require better conditions.

But do they really do that?

I am the one who wrote in this blog that the [Soviet Union promised one thing in their Constitution, and delivered another](https://nintil.com/2016/05/14/the-soviet-union-series/). Is it the case that farms do comply with the regulations above? I will present some evidence to the contrary, then a rejoinder.

Our first example is[Viva!'s Cruel Britannia: Life and death on UK factory farms](http://www.viva.org.uk/what-we-do/cruel-britannia-life-and-death-uk-factory-farms) (2013) shows a few examples of law breaches.

[Animal Aid](http://www.animalaid.org.uk/h/n/CAMPAIGNS/factory//3047//) then has a few videos that also show noncompliance, ranging from 2007 to 2016. (See Appendix I)

Then, from government sources, we have an official count of [4000 major breaches of animal welfare laws](https://www.theguardian.com/environment/2016/aug/28/fsa-4000-breaches-animal-welfare-laws-uk-abattoirs-two-years) (each breach involves one or more than one animal) in the last two years. These include things we might see in animal welfare documentaries, like cows being slammed against a wall, chickens being boiled alive, etc. Assuming that each breach affects 50 animals (A breach can be between 1 and hundreds of animals), say, the proportion of incidences per animal slaughtered per year is 1% of the total.

We also have [this thread](http://animalaid.org.uk/h/n/NEWS/pr_factory//2053//) of information from AnimalAid, where they claim that a 2008 EFSA report found a tail docking ratio of 75-80%, which surely implies routine tail docking, which is forbidden. The EFSA report is [here](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.2903/j.efsa.2007.611/abstract). We then go to pg. 23 which refers us to a 2006 BPHS (British Pig Health Scheme) study, but finding it has proven impossible. Instead, we use [this report](https://www.publications.parliament.uk/pa/cm200809/cmselect/cmenvfru/96/96.pdf) for the British Parliament as supporting evidence, trusting that whoever made the report did check the report. [FAWC](https://www.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/325042/FAWC_opinion_on_pig_mutilations_and_environmental_enrichment.pdf) (2011) also trusts the number. Interestingly, in that same report they assert that

> 85\. **We suggest that a piglet having undergone one or even several of the mutilations considered here is not thereby prevented from having “a life worth living”** . However, this would prevent it from having “a good life”, which FAWC has also suggested that we should aspire to provide for animals14 . In this respect, provision of appropriate environmental enrichment that allows the pig to express biologically relevant behaviours is an important consideration, independent of any benefit in preventing harmful behaviours such as tail biting

In the European Parliament study [Routine Tail-Docking of Pigs](http://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/STUD/2014/509997/IPOL_STU\(2014\)509997_EN.pdf), they mention another survey, in 2014, that also included the UK, and in that one, they were found to be compliant with European regulations against routine tail docking. The data itself is compiled [here](http://stud.epsilon.slu.se/7178/). There, they link to  two Food and Veterinary Office reports, the later of which is from 2009, and involved a visit to a grand total of... two pig farms. It seems this is too small of a sample size to judge compliance, isn't it?

Seemingly, then, the law does have some wiggle room. Tail docking is allowed if needed, but not allowed if is a routine process. But what if it's needed as a routine process?

The trouble with the undercover videos is that we cannot be sure of the prevalence of what is shown in the video. We would need something like yearly evaluations of factory farms, and see what percentage of noncompliance over the total there is. Fortunately, the UK Government has such data:

## Official data

Within the UK government, animal welfare is a responsibility of the Department for Environment, Food, and Rural Affairs (Defra), and withing that, of the[Farm Animal Welfare Committee (FAWC)](https://www.gov.uk/government/groups/farm-animal-welfare-committee-fawc#our-members). [Here](https://www.food.gov.uk/sites/default/files/fsa150605.pdf) you can find their last board meeting minutes, discussing among other things the results from an unannounced inspection programme, totalling 306 out of 317 slaughterhouses. The results were that 87% of the slaughterhouses were up to standards, 12% needed improvement, and one single facility required 'urgent improvement'. Note that this is not weighted by number of pigs processed. It is highly plausible that larger slaughterhouses do comply more with regulations.

[This other report](https://www.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/319292/Farm_Animal_Welfare_in_Great_Britain_-_Past__Present_and_Future.pdf), from 2009, provides evidence regarding improvement in welfare in factory farms (since 1965). The aim of the laws, say the report, has not been to give farmed animals a good life, but to give each of them a life worth living. The report also says that compliance levels are high.

[This report](http://webarchive.nationalarchives.gov.uk/20130402151656/http://archive.defra.gov.uk/corporate/about/who/cvo/documents/2009report.pdf), also from 2009, **based on 9963 welfare inspections at different farms establishes a level of compliance of 97% for random inspections. On farms that were ex-ante expected to comply less as per a risk model, compliance rates were lower, 77%. In all cases, 'unnecesary pain, suffering, or distress' seems to be below 1% in most of the cases** (different animals, and different welfare critaria) studied, which is conveniently close to my ballpark estimate above in this post.

I've sent an email to the Chief Veterinary Office of the UK asking for updated figures. One month after the publication of this post, I should have them, and this should have been updated accordingly.

[World Animal Protection](http://api.worldanimalprotection.org/compare) grants an A to the UK, and a D to the United States overall for their efforts in animal welfare protection, and the same for farmed animals.

## Conclusion

On the basis of the presented evidence, I submit that most animals in UK factory farms

-   Do not show meaningful levels of animal suffering (That is, the aggregate of factory farmed animals in the UK is net positive, from a mere hedonic standpoint)
-   Do show violations of strong animal rights (But take into account that any farm, zoo, or the act of keeping pets probably implies this)
-   Have a welfare level in compliance with UK law (with some non-conformances)
-   Have had an increasing level of welfare since 1965
-   Do not necessarily live in the same conditions as factory farmed animals in other countries

Broadly, the inference chain is that

1.  Animal welfare laws in the UK set standards such that if compliance exists, animal lives will be worth living, and won't incur in much suffering (I take this as a premise. I haven't assessed whether they are, from a biological or veterinarian point of view, good enough)
2.  Farms comply with animal welfare laws in the UK at least to the point where animal suffering is not too severe (For this, government inspections. Against this, undercover videos. Given the size of the data samples, and that the severity of suffering in the videos is not very high for most animals, I think the official data is mostly right.
