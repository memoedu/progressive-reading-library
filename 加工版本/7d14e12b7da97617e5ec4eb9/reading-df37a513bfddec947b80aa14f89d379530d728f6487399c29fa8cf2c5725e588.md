# Attention is all we have

## 阅读前置卡

- 价值判断：高。以数学家的亲身经验切入认知不平等这一根本问题，提出六个可讨论的猜想并给出可证伪预测，兼具理论野心与实践指向，对理解天赋、练习与元认知有较高参考价值。
- 信息密度判断：高。全文围绕认知差异的生物学与元认知解释层层推进，概念密集，含猜想、可证伪预测、fMRI 证据与历史引文，几乎没有冗余铺陈。
- 是否继续阅读：3 全文。论证链条完整且猜想间有递进关系，值得通读全文以把握框架；若只关心结论可读概览，但细节论证是其主要价值所在。

## 核心洞察

一、认知差异的物理基础仍空白：关于认知不平等起源的争论激烈，但其本质几乎无人追问：极聪明者大脑中究竟发生什么。高斯大脑被长期错标、爱因斯坦大脑切片研究缺乏说服力，说明在坚实科学层面所知甚少；而这一空白并未阻止人们继续使用有缺陷的大脑隐喻与可疑的生物学假设。
[[#^pr-e38a53c78477|正文跳转]]

二、基因只造成同架构内的微调：基因编码蛋白质、蛋白质组成细胞与组织；种内遗传变异主要是相同蛋白质在数量与构象上的微小变化，而非新蛋白带来全新架构。个体差异在胚胎发育中累积成可观的生理差别，但所有人共享同一功能蓝图——更像同款芯片的不同型号，而非手机处理器与 GPU 之别。
[[#^pr-29471335ecaf|正文跳转]]

三、天才自述指向态度而非天赋：笛卡尔、牛顿、爱因斯坦、费曼、格罗滕迪克等人都拒绝天生异禀的说法，转而强调直觉的清晰、耐心思考、好奇与注意力质量。这些声明未能撼动大众的遗传决定论外壳，部分原因是他们未能说清“勤奋”“好奇”究竟指何种心理操作。
[[#^pr-dde4e06cd69c|正文跳转]]

四、数学才能分布呈肥尾而非高斯：高度可遗传的多基因性状通常呈高斯分布（如身高集中在均值附近窄带），而数学才能的分布尾部远更肥厚，类似帕累托分布。作者据此提出可证伪预测：在百万分之一的顶尖成就者群体中，其家族内多基因评分中位数不会落入总人群的前百分位。
[[#^pr-1e9556c3c181|正文跳转]]

五、认知差异主要落在突触连接组：猜想二主张，任一时点上个体间的认知差异主要由突触连接组差异解释，这与顶叶-额叶整合理论一致；连接组的精细结构会随刺激持续被修改，突触可塑性是学习与记忆的普遍机制。该猜想中较具原创性的部分是强调测量：IQ 测验主要测的是连接组差异。
[[#^pr-4c626546829a|正文跳转]]

六、数学直觉是可训练的知觉：作者看瑞文推理题时几乎不需思考，直接获得三个叠加置换矩阵的直观知觉，认知负荷等同于计算 132+37。这种能力并非天生：他十八岁才接触该概念，经本硕阶段练习与原创研究逐步养成，说明数学结构可以被内化为类似视觉节奏的直觉。
[[#^pr-90f3cf63be3e|正文跳转]]

七、心智习惯差异可能极其悬殊：猜想三认为人们的心理习惯与元认知方式差异巨大。清醒状态有许多风味，而我们缺乏文化脚手架去辨识各自的“大脑模式”。若统计不同人在数周数月内花在各模式上的时间，差异可能令人震惊——如同有人日行十万步，有人终日躺在沙发上刷屏。
[[#^pr-f65eb153c874|正文跳转]]

八、数学家调用非语言脑区：2016 年 Amalric 与 Dehaene 的 fMRI 研究显示，职业数学家在处理复杂数学陈述时调用语言处理通常不动员的颞下与顶叶区域，且不限于本人研究领域；同等学术地位的非数学家对照组则激活预期的语言区。这与作者主动切换到“数学模式”去“看”和“感觉”对象的主观经验吻合。
[[#^pr-6d45e0803ed2|正文跳转]]

九、双重标准低估了练习差距：人们听说数学家大脑有特定激活模式便觉得遗传信念得到印证，却接受健美者肱二头肌或酗酒者肝脏有非遗传成因。由于认知活动发生在不可见的帷幕之下，人们系统性低估了练习差距的规模。
[[#^pr-ef1e9ec74355|正文跳转]]

十、次级刺激塑造高级认知：猜想五及其强化版主张：大脑连接组不仅响应外部原始感官信号而重构，也响应我们不断生成的内心意象之流。遗传设定上限，但极少有人接近上限，观察到的认知方差很大部分归因于次级刺激的强度与质量——这也解释了教学难以规模化的原因。
[[#^pr-d8926b67d403|正文跳转]]

十一、极端才能源于长期有效的元认知：猜想五之三提出，极端认知才能是异常有效的元认知方法长期持续、产生异常强烈且有益的次级刺激的例外结果。它为极端才能提供非魔法的解释框架：这类脑活动有可测量的生理实在，机器学习视角下预期影响巨大，也解释了认知不平等为何有“凭空而来”的错觉。
[[#^pr-c92d40b8a735|正文跳转]]

十二、认知抑制是对不可靠意象的防护：猜想六认为，认知抑制是对“从不可靠心理意象中学习”的适应性保护，必须被克服才能解锁创造与精通；它部分受社会反馈调节，形成随年龄固化的自我强化认知分层。这呼应瑟斯顿所述：证明重要定理后地位跃升者往往变得远更高效。
[[#^pr-0eab6a316900|正文跳转]]

十三、没有奇迹之人，但有奇迹轨迹：认知层级在短时间尺度上显得僵化，但三十年 hindsight 显示同辈群体的数学认知层级并非静止，存在令人印象深刻的上升与下降轨迹。应像看待财富那样看待智力：它是非确定性资本化过程的持续结果；真正属于自己的注意力与好奇心焦点，可能比想象中更重要。

## 正文

有些人比其他人更聪明——而有些人则聪明得惊人。我们可能在许多事情上意见不一，但至少能否在这点上达成一致？

cognitive inequality 的 origin 是一个热门且引发分歧的话题。cognitive inequality 的 existence 几乎毫无争议。我们确实难以给出一个令人满意的 intelligence 定义，尤其是在其最具创造性、最具社会价值的形式上，但大多数人同意，像 Leonardo da Vinci、William Shakespeare、Nikola Tesla 或 John von Neumann 这样的人是 extraordinarily intelligent。

这与我在 pure math academia 中的经历高度吻合：在那里，careers 乃至 personal identities 都由一种共同感知所塑造——即数学家之间存在着一道令人着迷般宽阔的 cognitive gap。这是他们讨论中的一个 central theme，也是恐惧与钦佩的共同来源。

然而，尽管围绕 cognitive inequality 的 origins 存在种种 culture wars，关于其 very nature 却鲜有争论。

当一个人 unusually smart 时，其 brain 中究竟在发生什么？这与“_cognitive inequality 从何而来？_”是不同的问题，也与“_我们真的能测量它吗？_”（另一个有争议的话题）不同。

这是一个 basic、foundational 的问题，它源于两个被广泛接受的事实——有些人比其他人更聪明，以及 intelligence 是 brain activity 的一种 manifestation——并追问自然的后续：究竟在发生什么？一个 super smart 的人的 brain 与一个 normal 人的 brain 之间有哪些 physical differences？

尽管它与科学中一些最具争议的问题相邻，这个话题却一直相对安静。这也许正是因为这种邻近性。

又或者，这个问题太过简单、太过自然。五岁的孩子喜欢问这样残酷的问题，但成年人通常更为犹豫，因为他们不想显得不够 sophisticated。

又或许，这是因为历史上有过先例：人们去寻找答案，却空手而归。Carl Friedrich Gauss 的 brain 被装在一个罐子里，放在 Göttingen 大学某处的一个 storage shelf 上，它如此平平无奇，以至于[它在一个多世纪里一直贴着错误的标签](https://www.mpg.de/7589532/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss_brain)，却无人察觉。Albert Einstein 于 1955 年去世后，[那位 pathologist 未经家人许可取走了他的 brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_of_Albert_Einstein)，将其保存在 formaldehyde 中，并解剖成数百个 slices——一些研究人员声称在样本中观察到了 peculiarities，但这些 findings 普遍被认为缺乏说服力。

^pr-e38a53c78477

如果我们坚持 solid science，那么在这个 subject 上我们所知甚少，这一点相当显著。

但问题就在这里。当这个最简单、最自然的问题仍未得到解答时，人们却继续依赖同样有缺陷的 brain metaphors 和同样可疑的 biological assumptions。这只会加剧围绕 IQ 和 intelligence 的 heritability 的混乱。

例如，人们惊叹于 von Neumann 的 brain 的“speed”和“working memory”，仿佛它是一块拥有独特 clock speed 和 L1 cache 的 CPU。但从 biochemical perspective 来看，这究竟能意味着什么？

Genes 编码 proteins，proteins 组装成 cells，cells 组装成 tissues。Within-species genetic variability 并不是关于新的 genes 编码具有新 functional roles 的新 proteins、从而为全新的 architectures 打开可能性——而是关于同样的 proteins 在 quantity 和 conformation 上的微小 variations，这些 proteins 保持着相同的 overall structures 和 functional roles。

确实，这些微小的 variations 在 embryogenesis 和生命过程中不断 compound，导致个体之间产生可观的 physical 和 physiological differences。然而，同样确实的是，所有人类都以相同的 architecture 和相同的 functional blueprint 被构建。并不是说有些人拥有 normal hearts，而另一些人拥有 500 horsepower 的 turbopumps。也不是说有些人的 brain 里装着 [Snapdragon 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Qualcomm_Snapdragon_systems_on_chips) smartphone processors，而另一些人装着 [NVIDIA H200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopper_\(microarchitecture\)) Tensor Core GPUs。

^pr-29471335ecaf

沿用这个糟糕的 CPU metaphor，情况更像是有些人拥有 Snapdragon 7，而另一些人拥有 Snapdragon 8——performance gap 是显而易见的，但每个人运行的都是同样的 apps。

对此有一个 caveat。Genetic anomalies 可能使 brain development 偏离正轨，并妨碍其 normal functioning。一块 thermal management probe 失效的 Snapdragon 8 将无法正常运作。Phenylketonuria，又称 PKU，就是一个 recessive genetic condition 的例子，若不加以治疗，可能导致 microcephaly 和严重的 learning disabilities。

相比之下，Snapdragon 8 上不存在任何偶然的变化能神奇地把它变成 NVIDIA H200。这或许解释了为什么我们从未发现任何能导致 cognitive ability 急剧提升的 specific mutation。

然而，仍然，一个 super smart 的人的 brain 与一个 normal 人的 brain 之间**必定**存在 physical difference。若非如此，cognitive difference 又从何而来？

在我作为一名 pure mathematician 的整个旅程中，我常常感到，progress 不仅关乎 mathematics 本身，也关乎 metacognition 和 emotion control。

像许多 math students 一样，我一开始带着强烈的 hereditarian prior，因为对于我周围那令人震惊的 inequality pattern，我想不出任何替代的 model——而这一 pattern 显然无法用 social determinism 来解释。

后来，随着时间推移，我意识到 genetic determinism 同样问题重重，尤其是因为它无法解释我自己的 progress trajectory。

再后来，我发现许多杰出的 mathematicians 都曾试图阐明这样一个想法：他们的 talent 首先是一种 cognitive _attitude_。一个著名的例子是 Descartes，他在其 _Discourse on Method_ 的开篇几行中坚称自己并非特别有天赋：

> 就我自己而言，我从未认为自己的 mind 在任何方面比常人的 mind 更为出众。

相反，他把自己的成功归因于他偶然发现的一种神奇的_“method”_，就一切实际事务而言，这是一套 metacognitive techniques，旨在提高一个人 intuition 的 clarity 和 reliability。如果你为他最喜爱的词生成一个 tag cloud，_intuition_、_clear_ 和 _distinct_ 会以巨大的字体凸显出来。同样的主题在 Poincaré、Hadamard、Thurston、Grothendieck 以及许多其他人的 writings 中反复出现。

^pr-dde4e06cd69c

这属于一个更广泛的 pattern 的一部分：科学界一些最响亮的名字都拒绝接受自己天生具有独特 abilities 的观念，转而强调自己的 curiosity 和 stubbornness：

-   Isaac Newton：_“但如果我以这种方式为公众做了一些服务，那也仅仅归功于 industry 与 a patient thought。”_

-   Albert Einstein：_“我没有 special talent。我只是 passionately curious。”_

-   Richard Feynman：_“我是一个努力学习的 ordinary person。不存在 miracle people。只是碰巧他们对这件事产生了兴趣，然后他们学会了所有这些 stuff。”_

-   Alexander Grothendieck：_“这种 power 绝不是某种 extraordinary gift——比如一种不寻常的 cerebral strength，（我们不妨这么说）……这样的 gifts 固然珍贵，值得那些（像我这样）生来未曾‘无可估量地’被赐予它们的人羡慕。”_

不用说，这些大胆的 declarations 几乎未能在普通大众的 hereditarian carapace 上留下任何凹痕。

需要说明的是，我完全理解为什么大多数人觉得这些 statements 荒谬而虚伪。我记得十五岁时听到 Einstein 那句_“no special talent”_的 quote，立刻就很讨厌它。说真的，谁愿意听 supermodels 给我们讲 inner beauty 的重要性呢？后来我成了一名 professional mathematician，Einstein 的 quote 渐渐变得不那么令人恼火了。而在我跨过职业生涯中的某个 threshold 之后，它甚至显得深刻起来。

如果所有这些杰出的人都未能传达他们的观点，那也许是因为他们对自己所说的 _industry_、_patient thought_ 或 _curiosity_ 究竟指什么，表达得不够明确。

这是我那本书 _[Mathematica, a Secret World of Intuition and Curiosity](https://www.amazon.com/Mathematica-Secret-World-Intuition-Curiosity/dp/0300283288)_ 的一个 central theme，该书以 Einstein 的_“no special talent”_ quote 开篇，并试图补全那些缺失的细节——do math 意味着什么，我们在头脑中执行的那些看不见的 actions，我们 intuition 缓慢而渐进的 build-up。

我们没有好的 words 来讨论这些事情。从来没有人向我们解释过 think、meditate、imagine、reason 或 dream 意味着什么。

这些 activities 的 neurological basis 是什么？它们有什么共同之处？是什么将它们区分开来？它们对我们 brains 的 long-term effects 是什么？会不会有些人以错误的方式练习它们，而另一些人则偶然发现了极其有效的 techniques，从而彻底改变了他们的 capabilities？

我是一名 mathematician，不是 neuroscientist，我并非通过 theoretical considerations 抵达这些问题。相反，我走的是 practical path：努力在 math 上变得更好，在自己身上做实验，摆弄我的 mental imagery，并在最有效的地方加倍投入。

表面上，这只是一个关于 mathematics 的故事——而这一部分相当有共识。

大多数 mathematicians 都认同“doing math”的 phenomenology，并且他们常常觉得[关于 mathematics 的 traditional discourses 遗漏或歪曲了他们 inner experience 中某些 fundamental 的东西](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/weve-been-wrong-about-math-for-2300)。

在讨论 consequences 时，这种 consensus 就瓦解了。令人惊讶的是，[一些人同意 math talent 是通过一种学校从未真正解释过的 specific mental practice 发展起来的，却仍坚持认为它主要是 innate](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/i-am-struggling-to-think-who-would)。

正是在这里，关于 extreme mathematical talent 这个相对小众的故事，与 cognitive inequality 这个远为广泛的文化与科学议题发生了碰撞。

Mathematics 被广泛认为是 intelligence 最纯粹、最[“g-loaded”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_factor_\(psychometrics\))的形式——关于百万分之一的 outlier mathematicians 实际 thought processes 的争论，其核心揭示的是一场关于 human cognition 之 fundamentals 的争论。

_John von Neumann，math genius myth 的 poster child_

这让我们 mathematicians 处于一个尴尬的境地，因为我们的 autobiographical knowledge 常常与流行的 hereditarian myths 相冲突。

我以前写过这个（关于 [Turkheimer’s Third Law](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/beyond-nature-and-nurture)，关于 [the catastrophic science of Twins Reared Apart](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/twins-reared-apart-do-not-exist)），并将继续这个系列。但 debunking bad science 是一回事，解释 math talent 则是另一回事。

由于我在核心 neuroscience 上没有专业知识，我一直尽可能回避这个话题。

然而，在看到同样糟糕的 brain metaphors 一次又一次地出现在我的社交媒体时间线上之后，我逐渐得出结论：hereditarian myths 与有缺陷的 human cognition models 如此紧密地交织在一起，以至于不修正后者就不可能 debunk 前者。

每个人都需要一个 theory，尤其是当 established science 尚未提供 one 的时候。如果我能够摆脱自己过去的 hereditarian beliefs，那是因为我拼凑出了自己的一套关于 cognitive inequality 的 alternate model，它受到我的 life experience 以及我对 modern neuroscience 的（不完美的）理解的启发。

在我分享这个私人的、conjectural theory 之前，在所有人涌向评论区指出我的鲁莽之前，让我先澄清它的 epistemic status：

第一，它不过是一个 _theory_——也就是说，一组连贯的 unproven hypotheses。我没有 proof，没有 empirical evidence，在这个 subject 上也没有 legitimacy。

第二，这个 theory 显然是 simplistic 且 incomplete 的。它有 gaps、blindspots、ambiguities，其中一些是有意为之（这是一篇 Substack post，而不是 peer-reviewed publication），另一些则源于我的 ignorance 和 naivety。

这不过是我私人的一次尝试，试图把我自己在 math 上变得更好的 experience，与 neuroscience 的这条 fundamental lesson 调和起来：_the brain is a learning device, not a computing device._ 我分享它，主要是因为它作为 mental model 的价值。

这个 theory 并不完美，但可以说，它在生物学上没有 _"von Neumann was a mutant with a crazy fast CPU."_ 那么荒谬。

```
Conjecture 1. Some differences in the structure, volume, speed, and efficiency of neural tissue result from genetic variability, but they cannot account for the magnitude of the observed cognitive inequality.
```

IQ 的一个已知问题是，它人为地压缩了 distribution 的上端以拟合 bell curve。主观上，math（以及许多其他 domains）中的 cognitive gap 感觉要宽得多。以下是我在书中对此的表述：

> Math is so unequal that it’s as if some people could run the one-hundred-meter dash in under a second, while the majority wouldn’t make it in a week. While it’s conceivable that some people may genetically be endowed with a neuronal metabolism that is more efficient and powerful, making them, let’s say, _twice_ as capable in math, or why not _ten times_ as capable in math, it’s hard to believe that genes alone could explain such an absurd level of inequality.

[正如在早先的一篇 post 中详述的那样](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/why-genes-cant-explain-genius)，这个 heuristic 是：高度 heritable 的 polygenic traits 通常是 Gaussian 的——canonical example 是 height，人们聚集在均值周围一个狭窄的 ±25% band 内——而 math talent 的 distribution 则有一个肥得多的 tail，就像 Pareto distribution 一样。

这个 conjecture 的措辞是有意 informal 的，但它可以被转化为一个量化的、falsifiable prediction：

考虑 population P，它由最具 cognitively demanding 的 fields 中 one-in-a-million 的 overachievers 组成。你可以选择 active chess grandmasters、International Math Olympiads gold medallists、Fields medallists & invited speakers at the International Congress of Mathematics、Nobel laureates in physics，或者你喜欢的任何变体。这些选择带有一些 subjectivity，但我怀疑大多数人会觉得它们是 relevant 的。我的 prediction 是：_we will never find a within-family polygenic score S such that the median value of S on the population P lies in the top centile for S with respect to the general population._

^pr-1e9556c3c181

如果 P 最终被证明与 top decile 无法区分，我实际上并不会感到惊讶，但最稳妥的 top centile prediction 已经足够重大：要从 _top centile_ polygenic scores 走到 _one-in-a-million_ outcomes，会有四个数量级无法由 genetics 解释。

这个 falsifiable version 有两个不幸的问题：1/ 它依赖 achievement 作为 talent 的 proxy，2/ 它是 elitist 的。这是因为这个 conjecture 从根本上关乎 cognitive inequality 的 non-Gaussian aspects，而这些方面在 distribution 的 tail 部分更容易显现（在那个区域，不存在经过良好校准的 cognitive talent 测量）。

一个不那么明确可检验、但更 relatable 的版本会是：_if you’re genetically average, you still have a non-trivial chance of landing in the top 1% for any meaningful dimension of cognitive achievement_。

下一个 conjecture 更难转化为一个量化的、falsifiable statement。但它是 Conjecture 1 的自然对应物，因为[我们大脑的 interconnected structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connectome) 是它唯一可能可信地展现出极端的、Pareto-like variability 的方面。

```
Conjecture 2. At any given moment, the measured cognitive differences between individuals are primarily explained by differences in their synaptic connectomes.
```

^pr-4c626546829a

这与 conventional view 一致，即 human intelligence 源自 neurons 的 interconnected network。[Parietal-Frontal Integration Theory (P-FIT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parieto-frontal_integration_theory)——一个 classical biological model——通过大脑不同区域之间 integration 的相对质量来解释 cognitive inequality，强调 connectome 的重要性。

当然，human brain 有一些固定的、heritable features，其 morphogenesis 由 regulatory genes 控制，而这些 genes 因人而异。但同样，这感觉更像是 Snapdragon 7 vs Snapdragon 8 的细微差别。

这里重要的是，fine interconnected structure 会响应 stimuli 而被持续修改。事实上，这种 plasticity 是 learning 和 memory formation 的普遍解释，正如[the Synaptic Plasticity and Memory Hypothesis](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.neuro.23.1.649) 所概括的那样：

> Activity-dependent synaptic plasticity is induced at appropriate synapses during memory formation and is both necessary and sufficient for the information storage underlying the type of memory mediated by the brain area in which that plasticity is observed.

Conjecture 2 中唯一可能具有原创性的部分，是它对 measurement 的强调。它特别声称，IQ tests 主要测量的是 connectome 中的差异。

让我通过分享我个人对 [Raven’s Progressive Matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raven%27s_Progressive_Matrices) 的反应来阐明这一点，它是最常见的 IQ tests 之一，据称也是 [one of the most g-loaded ones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_factor_\(psychometrics\)。下面是一个典型的问题：

_这八个选项中，哪一个最适合填入右下角的空白处？_

我不知道“normal”的人是如何处理这样一道问题的，但对于像我这样的 mathematician 来说，很难把它当作对“working memory”、“processing speed”或“logical thinking”的令人满意的 test——无论这些表达是什么意思。

主观上，感觉通过把 mathematical structure 投射到图上，我能够减少对“working memory”的需求。

现在你可能会反驳说，我天生就有一个特殊的大脑，配备了 permutation matrices 的 eidetic perception。但我确知事实并非如此。我直到十八岁才了解到这个概念，并在接下来的几十年里持续发展对它的 intuition，先是练习 undergraduate linear algebra，然后是 graduate-level group theory，再后来是对 generalize permutations 的 structures 做原创研究。多年来，这些 patterns 对我来说已经变得像 whole numbers 对普通人那样自然和具体。我感觉它们就像 visual rhythms。

虽然我的 algebraic synesthesia 程度可能很极端，但我怀疑许多 software engineers——以及其他经常接触 tabular structures 的专业人士——也发展出了类似的感觉。tabular structures 在我们的 cognitive environments 中日益渗透，这或许实际上解释了为什么 Raven’s Progressive Matrices _["has demonstrated a rate of IQ gain of 7 points per decade, more than double the rate of the Flynn effect as manifested on WAIS, SB, and other multifactorial intellectual tests."](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4152423)_

Flynn effect 适用于所有 mathematical structures，包括那些对“normal”的人来说显而易见的结构。两千年前，在 Ancient Rome，我那个作为对比的“trivial”问题（132 + 37 = 169）会构成一道更具挑战性的 IQ test question（CXXXII + XXXVII = CLXIX）。

我们的 number sense 在过去几千年里已经大幅演化。我们生活在一个（几乎）人人都懂 Hindu-Arabic numerals、（几乎）人人都懂 zero、（几乎）人人都懂 negative numbers 的世界里——这三个概念曾长期超出普通人的理解范围。这场大规模的 anthropological transformation 无法用我们 genomes 的变化来解释，它直接归因于我们 cultural and technological environment 的变化。

请注意，Conjecture 2 本身与 hereditarian worldview 完全兼容。确实，可能有些人天生就拥有好得多的 learning mechanisms，随着时间推移产生出远为优越的 connectome。例如，我发展出对 mathematical structures 的 eidetic perceptions 的不寻常能力，完全可能由 genetic factors 解释。

然而，鉴于接下来的三个 conjectures，这将变得不那么可信。

```
Conjecture 3. People have vastly different mental habits and metacognitive approaches.
```

这一个非常具体，尽管其语言不幸地偏软。这使它成为最容易 testable 的一个。

语言偏软的原因我们已经讨论过——我们没有生物学上有意义的词语来表达我们在思考、冥想、想象、推理、做白日梦时在头脑中能够执行的那些特定的 unseen actions……

每当 neuroscientists 用 EEG 或 MRI 观察 brain activity 时，他们都会注意到截然不同的 activation patterns。事实上，这正是他们研究 brain activity 的原因：看看它如何响应某些 stimuli 而变化，或如何因人而异。然而，不知何故，我们缺乏 cultural scaffolding 来注意到我们各自不同的“brain modes”，并训练自己有策略地使用它们。

我们知道 wakefulness 和 sleep，在 sleep 之中我们知道 paradoxical sleep——但 wakefulness 有许多种风味。_Mental habits_ 和 _metacognitive approaches_ 是我为弥补这一缺失的 vocabulary 所做的尝试。

这个 conjecture 预测：如果我们做出合理的努力，对这些“brain modes”以及人们在完成某些 tasks 时可以从一种切换到另一种的不同 strategies 进行分类，并且如果我们统计不同人在数周和数月内花在不同“brain modes”上的时间，我们会发现 _shocking_ 的差异。

^pr-f65eb153c874

打个比方，我的 prediction 是：有些人每天走 100,000 步，而另一些人则一直在沙发上 doomscrolling。

一些现有的 empirical evidence 在方向上与这个 conjecture 一致。在 [a fascinating 2016 fMRI study](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1603205113) 中，[Marie Amalric](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=varwPM0AAAAJ) 和 [Stanislas Dehaene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislas_Dehaene) 观察到，professional mathematicians 在处理 complex mathematical statements 时，会调用那些在 language processing 中通常不会被动员的 brain areas，具体来说是 inferior temporal and parietal regions。无论这些 statements 是否属于他们自己的 specific domain of research，情况都是如此。相比之下，具有同等 academic standing 的 non-mathematicians 组成的 control population 在接触同样的 mathematical statements 时，激活的是预期的 language-processing regions。

^pr-6d45e0803ed2

这与我的 subjective experience 高度吻合。当面对一个对我来说并非一眼就能看出的 mathematical statement 时，我会自觉地切换到一个特殊的 “math mode”，在其中我试着以某种方式去 “see” 事物，去 “feel” 它们的存在，并像用想象中的身体部位那样与它们 “interact”。

这种引导我注意力的奇特方式是在时间中逐渐发展出来的。起初它相当费力，后来它成了 second nature。当它涉及我熟悉的 mathematical objects 时，它可以毫不费力。

Amalric-Dehaene study 只涉及在被 mathematical statement 提示时的 _reactive_ brain activity。但 [mathematicians preemptively practice all day long, through peculiar rumination techniques that slowly enhance their intuitive comprehension](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/thinking-fast-slow-and-super-slow)。

当 Einstein 坚称他只是 _“passionately curious,”_，当 Grothendieck 宣称 _“the quality of the inventiveness and the imagination of a researcher comes from the quality of his attention, listening to the voice of things,”_ 时，我相信他们谈论的正是这种性质的活动。

引人注目的是，Descartes 的整个 approach 都可以用这些说法重新解读。他 _Rules for the Direction of the Mind_ 中的这段话清楚地表明，这个方法真正关乎的是 attention control：

> The whole method consists entirely in the ordering and arranging of the objects on which we must concentrate our mind’s eye if we are to discover some truth.

Conjecture 3 同样与 hereditarian worldview 相容。从纸面上看，这种 access these special brain modes 并以足够的 intensity 和 duration 维持它们的能力，完全可能是 innate 的。

有趣的是，当人们面对 brains 之间可测量的 biological differences 时，许多人会自发地默认这种 genetic interpretation。

这暴露出一种 double standard。当听说 professional mathematicians 的 brains 具有特定的 activation patterns 时，人们觉得自己的 hereditarian beliefs 得到了印证。然而，他们却接受 bodybuilder 的 biceps 或 alcoholic 的 liver 有非遗传的成因。因为 cognitive activity 发生在 invisibility 的帷幕之下，人们低估了 practice gap 的 magnitude。

^pr-ef1e9ec74355

```
Conjecture 4. Most of these differences in mental habits and metacognitive approaches are acquired.
```

一个 essential caveat：_acquired_ 并不等同于 _nurtured_。许多 acquired 的东西极难、甚至不可能被有意地 nurture 或 self-develop。所谓 _acquired_，我只是指这些 differences 中的大多数不能归因于 genetic variance，最好被理解为 _developmental outcomes_——这种 divergence 在从 egg’s fertilization 开始、一直延续到 adulthood 的漫长而混乱的过程中出现并逐渐扩大。（这并不意味着 genetic variability 没有影响——它很可能有 substantial influence，但并非 predominant。）

这个 conjecture 被有意地表述得含糊且难以检验，更应被视为一个 generic pattern，用来构建更 modest、falsifiable 的 predictions。

一个具体的做法是制作 Amalric-Dehaene study 的 longitudinal versions，尤其是覆盖 early infancy 的 peak neuroplasticity period 的那些。我的直觉是，neurodevelopment pathways 有多条，并且 [each child follows a singular trajectory shaped by a unique combination of genetic, cultural, and emotional factors, and also freak events—with long-term consequences on their cognitive style](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/beyond-nature-and-nurture)。

但这些 early childhood studies 会带来许多 ethical issues，也会带来 practical ones。

一个更简单的版本是，追踪年龄更大的学生穿过他们 progression 中可预测的 decisive stages。French preparatory classes system 就是一个好例子。它接收 “gifted” 的 17-18 year-olds，让他们在两年里接受一套极其 ambitious 的 boot-camp-style math curriculum，这是一段 make-or-break 的经历，既产生 remarkable outcomes，也产生 disastrous ones（以不到世界人口 1% 的规模，[France has received nearly 20% of all Fields medals ever awarded](https://www.voronoiapp.com/maps/-Fields-Medal-for-Mathematics-by-Nations-1936-2022-1856)；但许多学生被留下 trauma。）

从最深刻的意义上说，这些年对我是 transformative years。我的 attention mechanisms、imagination techniques 以及 suppress intellectual fear 的能力都发生了某种 radical 的变化。我能想到的唯一说法是 _cognitive transmogrification._

我的 prediction 是，individual students 在这两年里的 progress trajectories，会与特定的 Amalric-Dehaene “math brain” activation patterns 的 intensification 和/或更频繁的使用显著相关。

```
Conjecture 5. Differences in mental habits and metacognitive approaches compound, over time, into measurable differences in cognitive ability. 
```

这一条基本上是说 Einstein 是对的，而且这确实关乎培养正确的那种 _curiosity_。

一个 fair objection 是 Conjecture 5 太含糊。另一个 fair objection 是它是 circular 的，除非人们找到一种可靠的方式来 disentangle the causality。人们变聪明，是因为他们发现了秘密的 _curiosity mode_，还是因为他们本来就比较聪明，所以才发现了它？（没有理由期待一个 binary answer，因为 cognitive development 是一个持续的过程，带有明显的 feedback loops。）

尽管它含糊且 speculative，我个人却觉得它有 clarifying 的作用。

从 machine-learning perspective 看，这个 conjecture 可以被视为先前那些 conjecture 的自然结果。确实，我们的 synaptic connectome 会重新配置，不仅响应 _primary stimuli_——我们从世界接收到的 raw sensory signal——也响应 _secondary stimuli_，即我们不断 elaborating 的 mental imagery 之流。

当你读一本书时，primary stimulus 是页面上的 ink。如果某些书让你变得聪明得多，原因不仅在于页面上的 ink，也在于相关的 secondary stimuli，即由这本书触发、并持续数分钟、数小时、数天、数年的 drifting thoughts and mental images。

这就是 teaching 如此困难、以及成功的 educational interventions 很少能 scale well 的根本原因。虽然可以 deterministically control the primary stimuli，但它们对 secondary stimuli 的影响总是 indirect 的，并且取决于许多 uncontrolled factors，其中最突出的是学生现有的 connectome 和 emotional state。

这提示了 Conjecture 5 的更大胆、更强的版本：

```
Conjecture 5 bis. In humans, advanced cognitive development is primarily mediated by secondary stimuli. Genetics do define a ceiling that varies from one person to another, but very few people ever approach their ceiling and, in practice, much of the observed cognitive variance is attributable to differences in the intensity and quality of secondary stimuli.
```

^pr-d8926b67d403

```
Conjecture 5 ter. Extreme cognitive talent is the exceptional outcome of unusually effective metacognitive approaches sustained over long periods of time, producing unusually intense and beneficial secondary stimuli.
```

^pr-c92d40b8a735

这是整个 framework 背后的 fundamental intuition。我们为什么会 dream？我们为什么不断地 imagine things？我们为什么会经历人们称之为 _stream of consciousness_ 的这种 bizarre thing？这些 capacities 在代谢上是昂贵的，而且从表面上看完全是 reckless——我们不断地用自己 hallucinatory slop 重新训练自己的 brains。

如果我们的祖先不是把这些 singular behaviors 当作 cognitive reinforcement mechanisms，他们为什么会进化出它们？在核心上，这是一个 machine-learning question。如果我们把这么多 synthetic data 喂进我们的 cortex，那一定有很好的理由。

Conjecture 5 ter 可能过于 ambitious，但它为 extreme talent 提供了一个 non-magical explanatory framework，并且在多个层面上是 consistent 的：

1.  The brain activity that produces these secondary stimuli is a physiological reality with concrete, measurable manifestations.

2.  From a theoretical machine-learning perspective, secondary stimuli are expected to have a massive impact on cognitive fitness.

3.  According to Conjecture 3, there should exist _massive_ measurable differences in how these secondary stimuli streams are produced from one individual to another—so cognitive inequality would at last have a physiological explanation based on something where people would exhibit _massive_ physical variability.

4.  As secondary stimuli aren’t part of the shared experience, and their effects compound over decades, it would also explain why cognitive inequality has this magical feel of “coming from nowhere” (hence the common hereditarian misinterpretation.)

5.  It would align with the accounts of historical “geniuses” who insisted their abilities stemmed from practice and attention (Einstein, Descartes, Grothendieck)—and it would explain why they struggled so much to find the right words.

回到坚实的地面上，自然的问题是：假设这个 framework 在方向上是正确的，那么 practical consequences 是什么？

如果你不喜欢 hereditarianism，是因为它没有给 human agency 留下足够的空间，那么我的回答可能不会让你完全满意。

The human brain 是一个极其复杂、non-linear 的 device，带有多层 feedback loops，这使它的 behavior 在本质上难以控制。

有充分的 evidence 表明，cognitive inequality 的关键方面，在儿童获得足以理解 metacognitive guidance 的 language capability 时就已经 crystallized 了。无论是 genetic lottery、socioeconomic lottery，还是 idiosyncratic neurodevelopment lottery，结论基本上都一样：你无法把自己的孩子训练成下一个 Einstein。

然而，有一个 crucial nuance。Mental habits 和 metacognitive approaches 无法直接从一个 brain 转移到另一个 brain，但可以讨论它们——或者至少 _try_ to——而且每个人都有 meaningful margin for improvement。

这就是那只 20% full glass，无论从远处看多么令人沮丧，它都值得你投入时间和精力。

我熟悉这种 bizarre、看似 contradictory 的感觉。像许多 math students 一样，我曾被 talent gap 的 immensity 和表面上的 immutability 所震惊。我在 math cognition hierarchy 中的位置，似乎被一次性确定在一个相对 boring 的地方，远高于 “normal people”，又远低于 “the geniuses”。而且，在某种意义上，我一直就停留在那里——远高于 “normal people”，又远低于 “the geniuses”。

但 talent gap 如此之宽，以至于这并不是一个 well-defined spot。以三十年的 hindsight 来看，同样清楚的是，我 peer group 的 math cognition hierarchy 并非真正 static。事实上，曾出现过一些令人印象深刻的 upward and downward trajectories。

对我来说，最难的部分不是识别出正确的 mental habits——我从 20 岁出头起就有足够清晰的想法——而是拼凑起勇气和 resilience，以更多的 discipline 去 practice 它们。

任何 cognitive inequality 的 theory，若没有对 fear 的讨论，都不会完整。我们为什么会进化出 cognitive inhibition，这种对 intellectual difficulty 的 bizarre response，这种 visceral panic，这种 brain fog，它阻止我们——除其他事情之外——甚至去 _engaging_ with mathematical abstractions？

有趣的是，Conjecture 5 bis 包含一个初步的解释。从我们自己的 imaginary stimuli 中学习是一件有风险的事，除非满足正确的条件，否则它本不该奏效。它可以改善我们的 cognitive fitness，否则我们早就灭绝了。但我们也知道许多情形下它会产生相反的效果（superstitions、cults、paranoid delusions……）

这最后一个 conjecture 可能过于雄心勃勃，而且无论如何，它都很难被 validate 或 falsify：

```
Conjecture 6. Cognitive inhibition is an adaptive protection against learning from unreliable mental imagery, which must be overcome to unlock creative thinking and mastery. It is partially regulated by social feedback, resulting in a self-reinforcing cognitive stratification that crystallizes with age.
```

^pr-0eab6a316900

它映照出许多 mathematicians 共同拥有的一种主观体验，他们坚持 self-confidence 的突出作用。以下是 [Bill Thurston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Thurston) 那篇著名的文章 _[On Proof and Progress in Mathematics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9404236)_ 中一段引人注目的文字：

> 经常发生的情况是，某个原本处于一群人中间的人 prove 了一个被广泛认可为重要的 theorem。他们在 community 中的地位——他们的 pecking order——立刻且戏剧性地上升。当这种情况发生时，他们通常会变得高效得多，成为一个 ideas 的中心和 theorems 的来源。为什么？首先，self-esteem 大幅提升，随之而来的是 productivity 的提升。其次，当他们的 status 上升时，人们会更处于 ideas 网络的中心——其他人会更认真地对待他们。最后，也许最重要的是，一个 mathematical breakthrough 通常代表一种新的 thinking 方式，而有效的 thinking 方式通常可以应用于不止一种情境。

这是对 20% full glass 的完美例证。我们确实感知到相当僵化的 cognitive hierarchies，而且在某种程度上，这种感知是正确的。然而，当我们从长时间跨度上观察 individual trajectories 时，这些 hierarchies 就远没有那么僵化了，而这才是思考我们未来的正确视角。Progress 涉及强化 self-confidence、找到合适的 sparring partners 和 ecosystems，以及发展新的 thinking 方式。

如果 intelligence 主要 embodied 于我们的 connectomes 之中（Conjecture 2），那么我们就应该像思考 wealth 那样思考它，把它看作一个 non-deterministic capitalization process 的持续结果。

80% empty glass 是：生活是不公平的，我们无法重放过去。没有 miracle people，但有 miracle trajectories。

^pr-aaed7caf7ebf

20% full glass 是：那唯一真正属于我们的东西——我们的 attention、我们 curiosity 的焦点、我们如何 navigate 自己的 stream of consciousness——可能比我们曾经梦想的更重要。

## 原文

Some people are smarter than others—and some are fabulously smart. We may disagree on many things, but can we at least agree on that?

The origin of cognitive inequality is a hot, divisive topic. The existence of cognitive inequality is hardly controversial. We do struggle to come up with a satisfactory definition of intelligence, especially in its most creative and socially valuable forms, but most agree that people like Leonardo da Vinci, William Shakespeare, Nikola Tesla, or John von Neumann were extraordinarily intelligent.

This aligns well with my experience in pure math academia, where careers and even personal identities are shaped by the shared perception of a mesmerizingly wide cognitive gap between mathematicians. This is a central theme in their discussions and a source of both fear and admiration.

Yet for all the culture wars on the origins of cognitive inequality, there is little debate on its very nature.

When someone is unusually smart, what is going on in their brain? This is a different question from “_where does cognitive inequality come from?”,_ and also from _“can we really measure it?”_ (another controversial topic).

It is a basic, foundational question that stems from two widely accepted facts—that some people are smarter than others, and that intelligence is a manifestation of brain activity—and asks for the natural follow-up: what is really going on? What are the physical differences between the brain of a super smart person and the brain of a normal person?

Despite its adjacency to some of the most contentious issues in science, this topic has remained relatively quiet. It might be because of this very proximity.

Or maybe the question is too simple and natural. Five-year olds love to ask brutal questions like this one, but grown-ups are usually more hesitant, as they don’t want to come across as unsophisticated.

Or perhaps this is because there were historical precedents where people went looking for answers and came back empty-handed. The brain of Carl Friedrich Gauss sits in a jar on a storage shelf somewhere at the University of Göttingen, and is so unremarkable that [it remained mislabeled for over a century](https://www.mpg.de/7589532/Carl_Friedrich_Gauss_brain) without anyone noticing. After Albert Einstein died in 1955, [the pathologist removed his brain without the permission of the family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_of_Albert_Einstein), preserved it in formaldehyde, and dissected it into hundreds of slices—some researchers claimed to have observed peculiarities in the samples, but these findings are generally regarded as unconvincing.

If we stick to solid science, there is remarkably little we know on the subject.

But here’s the catch. While the simplest and most natural question remains unaddressed, people continue to rely on the same flawed brain metaphors and the same dubious biological assumptions. This only adds to the confusion around IQ and the heritability of intelligence.

For example, people marvel at the “speed” and “working memory” of von Neumann’s brain, as if it were a CPU with a unique clock speed and L1 cache. But from a biochemical perspective, what could this even mean?

Genes code for proteins, which assemble into cells, which assemble into tissues. Within-species genetic variability isn’t about new genes coding for new proteins with new functional roles that open the possibility for radically new architectures—it is about minute variations in the quantity and conformation of the same proteins, which retain the same overall structures and functional roles.

It is true that these minute variations compound over embryogenesis and life, resulting in sizable physical and physiological differences between individuals. Yet it is also true that all human beings are built with the same architecture and the same functional blueprint. It’s not like some people have normal hearts while others have 500 horsepower turbopumps. And it’s not like some have [Snapdragon 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Qualcomm_Snapdragon_systems_on_chips) smartphone processors in their brains while others have [NVIDIA H200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopper_\(microarchitecture\)) Tensor Core GPUs.

To stick with this terrible CPU metaphor, it’s more like some people have a Snapdragon 7 while others have a Snapdragon 8—the performance gap is noticeable, but everybody runs the same apps.

There is one caveat to that. Genetic anomalies can derail brain development and hamper its normal functioning. A Snapdragon 8 with a defunct thermal management probe will not operate properly. Phenylketonuria, also known as PKU, is an example of a recessive genetic condition that, if untreated, can lead to microcephaly and severe learning disabilities.

By contrast, there is no accidental change to a Snapdragon 8 that magically turns it into an NVIDIA H200. This probably explains why we have never found any specific mutation that causes a drastic increase in cognitive ability.

Yet, still, there **MUST** be a physical difference between the brain of a super smart person and that of a normal person. If not, where would the cognitive difference come from?

Throughout my journey as a pure mathematician, it often struck me that progress wasn’t only about mathematics itself, but also metacognition and emotion control.

Like many math students, I started out with a strong hereditarian prior, because I couldn’t think of an alternate model for the shocking inequality pattern around me—which, clearly, couldn’t be explained by social determinism.

Then, over time, I realized that genetic determinism was equally problematic, not least because it couldn’t account for my own progress trajectory.

Later on, I found out that many prominent mathematicians had tried to articulate the idea that their talent was first and foremost a cognitive _attitude_. A famous example is that of Descartes who, in the opening lines of his _Discourse on Method_, insisted that he wasn’t particularly gifted:

> For myself, I have never presumed my mind to be any way more accomplished than that of the common man.

Instead, he attributed his successes to his chance discovery of a miraculous _“method”_ that, for all practical matters, is an assortment of metacognitive techniques aimed at improving the clarity and reliability of one’s intuition. If you were to generate a tag cloud of his favorite words, _intuition_, _clear_ and _distinct_ would stand out with massive fonts. The same themes can be found over and over again in the writings of Poincaré, Hadamard, Thurston, Grothendieck, and many others.

This is part of a broader pattern where some of the biggest names in science have rejected the notion that they were born with unique abilities, insisting instead on their curiosity and stubbornness:

-   Isaac Newton: _“But if I have done the public any service this way it is due to nothing but industry & a patient thought.”_

-   Albert Einstein: _“I have no special talent. I am only passionately curious.”_

-   Richard Feynman: _“I was an ordinary person who studied hard. There are no miracle people. It just happens they got interested in this thing, and they learnt all this stuff.”_

-   Alexander Grothendieck: _“This power is in no way some extraordinary gift—like an uncommon cerebral strength, (shall we say). . . . Such gifts are certainly precious, worthy of the envy of people (like me) who haven’t been blessed with them at birth, ‘beyond measure.’”_

Needless to say, these bold declarations have barely made a dent in the hereditarian carapace of the general public.

To be clear, I completely get why most people find these statements absurd and hypocritical. I remember hearing Einstein’s _“no special talent”_ quote when I was fifteen, and instantly hating it. Seriously, who wants to hear supermodels lecturing us on the importance of inner beauty? Then I became a professional mathematician and Einstein’s quote gradually felt less and less irritating. And, after I crossed a certain threshold in my career, it even appeared profound.

If all these brilliant people failed to communicate their point, it might have been because they weren’t explicit enough about what they meant by _industry_, _patient thought_, or _curiosity_.

This is a central theme of my book _[Mathematica, a Secret World of Intuition and Curiosity](https://www.amazon.com/Mathematica-Secret-World-Intuition-Curiosity/dp/0300283288)_, which opens with Einstein’s _“no special talent”_ quote and tries to fill in the missing details—what it means to do math, the unseen actions that we perform in our heads, the slow and gradual build-up of our intuition.

We have no good words to discuss these things. No one ever explained to us what it means to think, meditate, imagine, reason, or dream.

What is the neurological basis of these activities? What do they have in common? What differentiates them? What are their long-term effects on our brains? Could it be that some people practice them the wrong way, while others have stumbled upon remarkably effective techniques that radically transformed their capabilities?

I am a mathematician, not a neuroscientist, and I didn’t get to these questions through theoretical considerations. Rather, I took the practical path of trying to become better at math, experimenting with myself, tinkering with my mental imagery, and doubling down on what worked best.

On the surface, this is just a story about mathematics—and this part is fairly consensual.

Most mathematicians agree on the phenomenology of “doing math”, and they often feel that [traditional discourses about mathematics miss or misrepresent something fundamental about their inner experience](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/weve-been-wrong-about-math-for-2300).

The consensus breaks down when discussing the consequences. Surprisingly, [some agree that math talent develops through a specific mental practice that school never really explains, yet stick to the idea that it is primarily innate](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/i-am-struggling-to-think-who-would).

This is where the relatively niche story of extreme mathematical talent collides with the much broader cultural and scientific issue of cognitive inequality.

Mathematics is widely considered to be the purest and most [“g-loaded”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_factor_\(psychometrics\)) form of intelligence—the dispute about the actual thought processes of one-in-a-million outlier mathematicians reveals, at its core, a dispute about the fundamentals of human cognition.

_John von Neumann, the poster child of the math genius myth_

This puts us mathematicians in an awkward spot, as our autobiographical knowledge often conflicts with popular hereditarian myths.

I have written on this before (on [Turkheimer’s Third Law](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/beyond-nature-and-nurture), on [the catastrophic science of Twins Reared Apart](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/twins-reared-apart-do-not-exist)) and will continue the series. But debunking bad science is one thing, and explaining math talent is another.

Since I have no expertise on the core neuroscience, I have avoided this topic as much as possible.

However, after seeing the same bad brain metaphors popping up again and again in my social media timelines, I am coming to the conclusion that hereditarian myths are so tightly intertwined with flawed models of human cognition that it is impossible to debunk the former without fixing the latter.

Everyone needs a theory, especially when established science hasn’t yet provided one. If I could move away from my past hereditarian beliefs, it was because I cobbled together my own alternate model of cognitive inequality, informed by my life experience and my (imperfect) understanding of modern neuroscience.

Before I share this private, conjectural theory, and before everyone jumps to the comment section to call out my recklessness, let me clarify its epistemic status:

First, it is nothing but a _theory_—that is, a coherent set of unproven hypotheses. I have no proof, no empirical evidence, and no legitimacy on the subject.

Second, the theory is clearly simplistic and incomplete. It has gaps, blindspots, ambiguities, some of which are intentional (this is a Substack post, not a peer-reviewed publication) and others result from my ignorance and naivety.

This is just my private attempt to reconcile my own experience of becoming better at math with this fundamental lesson from neuroscience: _the brain is a learning device, not a computing device._ I am sharing it chiefly because of its value as a mental model.

This theory isn’t perfect, but it is arguably less biologically absurd than _“von Neumann was a mutant with a crazy fast CPU.”_

```
Conjecture 1. Some differences in the structure, volume, speed, and efficiency of neural tissue result from genetic variability, but they cannot account for the magnitude of the observed cognitive inequality.
```

One known issue with IQ is that it artificially compresses the upper end of the distribution to fit a bell curve. Subjectively, the cognitive gap in math (and many other domains) feels much wider than that. Here is how I put it in my book:

> Math is so unequal that it’s as if some people could run the one-hundred-meter dash in under a second, while the majority wouldn’t make it in a week. While it’s conceivable that some people may genetically be endowed with a neuronal metabolism that is more efficient and powerful, making them, let’s say, _twice_ as capable in math, or why not _ten times_ as capable in math, it’s hard to believe that genes alone could explain such an absurd level of inequality.

[As detailed in an earlier post](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/why-genes-cant-explain-genius), the heuristic is that highly heritable polygenic traits are typically Gaussian—the canonical example being height, where people cluster in a narrow ±25% band around the mean—while the distribution of math talent has a much fatter tail, like a Pareto distribution.

The phrasing of the conjecture is intentionally informal, but it can be turned into a quantified, falsifiable prediction:

Consider the population P of one-in-a-million overachievers in the most cognitively demanding fields. You could pick active chess grandmasters, International Math Olympiads gold medallists, Fields medallists & invited speakers at the International Congress of Mathematics, Nobel laureates in physics, or whichever variation you like. There is some subjectivity in these choices, but I suspect most people will find them relevant. My prediction is that _we will never find a within-family polygenic score S such that the median value of S on the population P lies in the top centile for S with respect to the general population._

I wouldn’t actually be surprised if P turned out to be indistinguishable from the top decile, but the safest top centile prediction is already substantial: to go from _top centile_ polygenic scores to _one-in-a-million_ outcomes, there would be four orders of magnitude unexplained by genetics.

This falsifiable version has two unfortunate issues: 1/ it relies on achievement as a proxy for talent, 2/ it is elitist. This is because the conjecture is fundamentally about the non-Gaussian aspects of cognitive inequality, which are more readily visible in the tail part of the distribution (a region where there exists no well-calibrated measurement of cognitive talent).

A less explicitly testable but more relatable version would be: _if you’re genetically average, you still have a non-trivial chance of landing in the top 1% for any meaningful dimension of cognitive achievement_.

The next conjecture is harder to turn into a quantified, falsifiable statement. But it is a natural counterpart to Conjecture 1, since [the interconnected structure of our brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connectome) is its one aspect that could credibly exhibit extreme, Pareto-like variability.

```
Conjecture 2. At any given moment, the measured cognitive differences between individuals are primarily explained by differences in their synaptic connectomes.
```

This aligns with the conventional view that human intelligence emerges from the interconnected network of neurons. The [Parietal-Frontal Integration Theory (P-FIT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parieto-frontal_integration_theory)—a classical biological model—explains cognitive inequality by the relative quality of the integration between different regions of the brain, emphasizing the importance of the connectome.

Of course, the human brain has some fixed, heritable features, and its morphogenesis is controlled by regulatory genes that vary from one person to another. But, again, this feels more like Snapdragon 7 vs Snapdragon 8 nuances.

What matters here is that the fine interconnected structure is continuously modified in response to stimuli. In fact, this plasticity is the prevalent explanation for learning and memory formation, as captured by [the Synaptic Plasticity and Memory Hypothesis](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev.neuro.23.1.649):

> Activity-dependent synaptic plasticity is induced at appropriate synapses during memory formation and is both necessary and sufficient for the information storage underlying the type of memory mediated by the brain area in which that plasticity is observed.

The only bit of Conjecture 2 that might be original is its emphasis on measurement. It claims, in particular, that IQ tests primarily measure differences in the connectome.

Let me motivate this by sharing my personal reaction to [Raven’s Progressive Matrices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raven%27s_Progressive_Matrices), one of the most common IQ tests, and supposedly [one of the most g-loaded ones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_factor_\(psychometrics\)). Here’s a typical question:

_Which of these eight options fits best in the bottom-right white space?_

I have no idea how “normal” people approach a question like this one, but it is very hard for a mathematician like me to take it as a satisfactory test of “working memory,” “processing speed,” or “logical thinking”—whatever these expressions mean.

I hardly have to think to get the right answer. When looking at the picture, I get the eidetic perception of three superimposed permutation matrices (one for the background geometric shape, one for the color of the foreground rectangle, one for the angle of the foreground rectangle.) And, thanks to this eidetic perception, I perceive this question as requiring the same cognitive load as computing 132 + 37.

Subjectively, it feels that by projecting mathematical structure onto the picture, I am able to reduce the need for “working memory”.

Now you could object that I was born with a special brain equipped with eidetic perception of permutation matrices. But I know for a fact that this wasn’t the case. I only learnt about this notion when I was eighteen, and I continued to develop my intuition for it over the following decades, practicing undergraduate linear algebra, then graduate-level group theory, then producing original research on structures that generalize permutations. Over the years, these patterns have become as natural and concrete to me as whole numbers are to laypeople. I feel them like visual rhythms.

While my degree of algebraic synesthesia might be extreme, I suspect that many software engineers—and other professionals who are regularly exposed to tabular structures—develop a similar feel. The increasing penetration of tabular structures in our cognitive environments might actually explain why Raven’s Progressive Matrices _[“has demonstrated a rate of IQ gain of 7 points per decade, more than double the rate of the Flynn effect as manifested on WAIS, SB, and other multifactorial intellectual tests.”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4152423)_

The Flynn effect applies to all mathematical structures, including the ones that feel obvious to “normal” people. Two thousand years ago, in Ancient Rome, my comparison “trivial” problem (132 + 37 = 169) would have constituted a more challenging IQ test question (CXXXII + XXXVII = CLXIX.)

Our number sense has substantially evolved over the past millennia. We live in a world where (almost) everybody understands Hindu-Arabic numerals, (almost) everybody understands zero, and (almost) everybody understands negative numbers—three notions that had long been out of reach of ordinary people. This large-scale anthropological transformation, which cannot be explained by changes in our genomes, is directly attributable to changes in our cultural and technological environment.

Note that Conjecture 2, in itself, is entirely compatible with the hereditarian worldview. Indeed, it could be that some people are born with substantially better learning mechanisms, producing over time a vastly superior connectome. It could be, for example, that my unusual ability to develop eidetic perceptions of mathematical structures is entirely explained by genetic factors.

However, this will become less credible in light of the next three conjectures.

```
Conjecture 3. People have vastly different mental habits and metacognitive approaches.
```

This one is very concrete, despite the unfortunate softness of its language. This makes it the most easily testable.

The reason for the soft language is one we already discussed—we have no biologically meaningful words to express the specific unseen actions that we can perform in our heads when we think, meditate, imagine, reason, daydream…

Whenever neuroscientists observe brain activity with EEG or MRI, they notice wildly different activation patterns. This is in fact the very reason why they study brain activity: to see how it varies in reaction to certain stimuli, or from one person to another. Yet, somehow, we lack the cultural scaffolding to take notice of our distinct “brain modes” and train ourselves to use them strategically.

We know about wakefulness and sleep, and within sleep we know about paradoxical sleep—but there are many flavors of wakefulness. _Mental habits_ and _metacognitive approaches_ are my attempts to make up for this missing vocabulary.

The conjecture predicts that, if we make a reasonable effort to categorize these “brain modes” and the distinct strategies one could employ to switch from one to another when accomplishing certain tasks, and if we produce statistics of how much time different people spend on distinct “brain modes” over weeks and months, we will find _shocking_ differences.

Metaphorically, my prediction is that some are walking 100,000 steps a day while others are constantly doomscrolling on their sofas.

Some existing empirical evidence is directionally aligned with the conjecture. In [a fascinating 2016 fMRI study](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1603205113), [Marie Amalric](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=varwPM0AAAAJ) and [Stanislas Dehaene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislas_Dehaene) observed that professional mathematicians process complex mathematical statements by recruiting brain areas that aren’t typically mobilized in language processing, specifically in the inferior temporal and parietal regions. This happens regardless of whether the statements belong to their specific domain of research. By contrast, the control population of non-mathematicians with equal academic standing activate the expected language-processing regions when exposed to the same mathematical statements.

](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Z-ei!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fe7d483d6-c34d-4471-a99c-566be2590677_1280x1025.jpeg)

This aligns well with my subjective experience. When confronted with a mathematical statement that isn’t immediately obvious to me, I consciously switch to a special “math mode” where I try to “see” things in a certain way, to “feel” their presence and “interact” with them as if with imaginary body parts.

This peculiar way of channeling my attention developed over time. At first it was quite demanding, then it became second nature. When it involves mathematical objects that are familiar to me, it can be effortless.

The Amalric-Dehaene study only concerns _reactive_ brain activity, when prompted with a mathematical statement. But [mathematicians preemptively practice all day long, through peculiar rumination techniques that slowly enhance their intuitive comprehension](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/thinking-fast-slow-and-super-slow).

When Einstein insists that he is only _“passionately curious,”_ when Grothendieck declares that _“the quality of the inventiveness and the imagination of a researcher comes from the quality of his attention, listening to the voice of things,”_ I believe that they are talking about activities of this very nature.

Strikingly, Descartes’s entire approach can be reinterpreted in those terms. This passage from his _Rules for the Direction of the Mind_ makes it clear that the method was really about attention control:

> The whole method consists entirely in the ordering and arranging of the objects on which we must concentrate our mind’s eye if we are to discover some truth.

Conjecture 3 is again compatible with the hereditarian worldview. On paper, this ability to access these special brain modes and maintain them with sufficient intensity and duration could very well be innate.

Interestingly, many people spontaneously default to this genetic interpretation when they are presented with measurable biological differences between brains.

This reveals a double standard. When hearing that the brains of professional mathematicians have specific activation patterns, people feel vindicated in their hereditarian beliefs. Yet they accept non-genetic causes for the biceps of a bodybuilder or the liver of an alcoholic. Because cognitive activity takes place under a veil of invisibility, people underestimate the magnitude of the practice gap.

```
Conjecture 4. Most of these differences in mental habits and metacognitive approaches are acquired.
```

An essential caveat: _acquired_ isn’t equivalent to _nurtured_. Many things are acquired that are extremely hard, if not impossible, to intentionally nurture or self-develop. By _acquired_, I simply mean that most of these differences cannot be attributed to genetic variance and are best understood as _developmental outcomes_—the divergence emerges and gradually widens throughout the long and messy process that starts with the egg’s fertilization and continues into adulthood. (Which doesn’t mean that genetic variability has no influence—it is very likely to have a substantial influence, yet not predominant.)

The conjecture is intentionally vague and hard to test, and should rather be viewed as a generic pattern for building more modest, falsifiable predictions.

A concrete approach would be to create longitudinal versions of the Amalric-Dehaene study, especially ones covering the peak neuroplasticity period of early infancy. My hunch is that there are multiple neurodevelopment pathways, and [each child follows a singular trajectory shaped by a unique combination of genetic, cultural, and emotional factors, and also freak events—with long-term consequences on their cognitive style](https://davidbessis.substack.com/p/beyond-nature-and-nurture).

But these early childhood studies would come with many ethical issues, as well as practical ones.

A simpler version would be to follow older students through predictably decisive stages of their progression. The French preparatory classes system is a good example. It takes “gifted” 17-18 year-olds and submits them for two years to an incredibly ambitious boot-camp-style math curriculum, a make-or-break experience that produces remarkable outcomes as well as disastrous ones (with less than 1% of the world population, [France has received nearly 20% of all Fields medals ever awarded](https://www.voronoiapp.com/maps/-Fields-Medal-for-Mathematics-by-Nations-1936-2022-1856); but many students are left traumatized.)

These were transformative years for me, in the most profound sense. Something radical changed in my attention mechanisms, imagination techniques, and ability to suppress intellectual fear. The only expression that I can think of is _cognitive transmogrification._

My prediction is that the progress trajectories of individual students throughout these two years would be significantly correlated with the intensification and/or more frequent use of the specific Amalric-Dehaene “math brain” activation patterns.

```
Conjecture 5. Differences in mental habits and metacognitive approaches compound, over time, into measurable differences in cognitive ability. 
```

This one basically says that Einstein was right, and that this really is about developing the right kind of _curiosity_.

A fair objection is that Conjecture 5 is too vague. Another fair objection is that it is circular, unless one finds a reliable way of untangling the causality. Do people become smarter because they’ve discovered the secret _curiosity mode_, or do they discover it because they’re already smarter? (There is no reason to expect a binary answer, as cognitive development is an ongoing process with obvious feedback loops.)

While it is vague and speculative, I personally find it clarifying.

From a machine-learning perspective, the conjecture can be viewed as a natural consequence of the prior ones. Indeed, our synaptic connectome reconfigures in response to not just _primary stimuli_—the raw sensory signal we receive from the world—but also _secondary stimuli_, the stream of mental imagery that we continuously elaborate.

When you read a book, the primary stimulus is the ink on the page. If some books make you much smarter, the cause lies not just in the ink on the page, but also in the associated secondary stimuli, the drifting thoughts and mental images triggered by the book and lingering for minutes, hours, days, years.

This is the fundamental reason why teaching is so difficult, and why successful educational interventions rarely scale well. While it is possible to deterministically control the primary stimuli, their impact on the secondary stimuli is always indirect and contingent on many uncontrolled factors, most notably the existing connectome and emotional state of the student.

This suggests bolder, reinforced versions of Conjecture 5:

```
Conjecture 5 bis. In humans, advanced cognitive development is primarily mediated by secondary stimuli. Genetics do define a ceiling that varies from one person to another, but very few people ever approach their ceiling and, in practice, much of the observed cognitive variance is attributable to differences in the intensity and quality of secondary stimuli.
```

```
Conjecture 5 ter. Extreme cognitive talent is the exceptional outcome of unusually effective metacognitive approaches sustained over long periods of time, producing unusually intense and beneficial secondary stimuli.
```

This is the fundamental intuition behind the entire framework. Why do we dream? Why do we constantly imagine things? Why do we experience this bizarre thing that people call a _stream of consciousness_? These capacities are metabolically expensive and, on the face of it, entirely reckless—we are constantly retraining our brains on our own hallucinatory slop.

Why did our ancestors evolve these singular behaviors, if not as cognitive reinforcement mechanisms? At the core, this is a machine-learning question. If we’re feeding this much synthetic data into our cortex, then there must be a good reason for that.

Conjecture 5 ter might be overambitious, but it provides a non-magical explanatory framework for extreme talent, consistent on many levels:

1.  The brain activity that produces these secondary stimuli is a physiological reality with concrete, measurable manifestations.

2.  From a theoretical machine-learning perspective, secondary stimuli are expected to have a massive impact on cognitive fitness.

3.  According to Conjecture 3, there should exist _massive_ measurable differences in how these secondary stimuli streams are produced from one individual to another—so cognitive inequality would at last have a physiological explanation based on something where people would exhibit _massive_ physical variability.

4.  As secondary stimuli aren’t part of the shared experience, and their effects compound over decades, it would also explain why cognitive inequality has this magical feel of “coming from nowhere” (hence the common hereditarian misinterpretation.)

5.  It would align with the accounts of historical “geniuses” who insisted their abilities stemmed from practice and attention (Einstein, Descartes, Grothendieck)—and it would explain why they struggled so much to find the right words.

Returning to solid ground, the natural question is this: assuming that the framework is directionally correct, what are the practical consequences?

If you dislike hereditarianism because it doesn’t leave enough room for human agency, then my answer might not leave you entirely happy.

The human brain is a fabulously complex, non-linear device with multiple layers of feedback loops, which makes its behavior inherently hard to control.

There is ample evidence that key aspects of cognitive inequality have already crystallized by the time children acquire the language capability to make sense of metacognitive guidance. Whether it’s the genetic lottery, the socioeconomic lottery, or the idiosyncratic neurodevelopment lottery, the conclusion is basically the same: you cannot train your kid to become the next Einstein.

There is, however, a crucial nuance. Mental habits and metacognitive approaches cannot be directly transferred from one brain to another, but it is possible to discuss them—or at least _try_ to—and everyone has a meaningful margin for improvement.

This is the 20% full glass and, however depressing it may look from afar, it is worth your time and effort.

I am familiar with this bizarre, seemingly contradictory feeling. Like many math students, I was shocked by the immensity and apparent immutability of the talent gap. My standing in the math cognition hierarchy seemed determined once for all in a relatively boring place, far above “normal people” and far below “the geniuses”. And, in a sense, this is where I have always remained—far above “normal people” and far below “the geniuses”.

But the talent gap is so wide that this isn’t a well-defined spot. With a three-decades hindsight, it is also clear that the math cognition hierarchy of my peer group wasn’t truly static. In fact, there have been some impressive upward and downward trajectories.

For me, the hardest part wasn’t to identify the right mental habits—I had a clear enough idea from my early 20s—but to cobble together the courage and resilience to practice them with more discipline.

No theory of cognitive inequality would be complete without a discussion of fear. Why did we evolve cognitive inhibition, this bizarre response to intellectual difficulty, this visceral panic, this brain fog that prevents us—among other things—from even _engaging_ with mathematical abstractions?

Interestingly, Conjecture 5 bis contains a tentative explanation. Learning from our own imaginary stimuli is a risky business that isn’t supposed to work unless the right conditions are met. It can improve our cognitive fitness, otherwise we would have gone extinct. But we know of many circumstances where it has the opposite effect (superstitions, cults, paranoid delusions…)

This final conjecture might be overambitious and, in any case, it is hard to validate or falsify:

```
Conjecture 6. Cognitive inhibition is an adaptive protection against learning from unreliable mental imagery, which must be overcome to unlock creative thinking and mastery. It is partially regulated by social feedback, resulting in a self-reinforcing cognitive stratification that crystallizes with age.
```

It mirrors a subjective experience shared by many mathematicians, who insist on the prominent role of self-confidence. Here is a striking passage from [Bill Thurston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Thurston)’s celebrated essay _[On Proof and Progress in Mathematics](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9404236)_:

> It regularly happens that someone who was in the middle of a pack proves a theorem that receives wide recognition as being significant. Their status in the community—their pecking order—rises immediately and dramatically. When this happens, they usually become much more productive as a center of ideas and a source of theorems. Why? First, there is a large increase in self-esteem, and an accompanying increase in productivity. Second, when their status increases, people are more in the center of the network of ideas—others take them more seriously. Finally and perhaps most importantly, a mathematical breakthrough usually represents a new way of thinking, and effective ways of thinking can usually be applied in more than one situation.

This is a perfect illustration of the 20% full glass. We do perceive fairly rigid cognitive hierarchies and, to a certain extent, this perception is correct. Yet these hierarchies are far less rigid when we look at individual trajectories over long periods of time, which is the right perspective for thinking about our future. Progress involves reinforcing self-confidence, finding the right sparring partners and ecosystems, and developing new ways of thinking.

If intelligence is primarily embodied in our connectomes (Conjecture 2), then we should think about it in the same way we think about wealth, as the continuing outcome of a non-deterministic capitalization process.

The 80% empty glass is that life is unfair and we cannot replay the past. There are no miracle people, but there are miracle trajectories.

The 20% full glass is that the one thing that is truly ours—our attention, the focus of our curiosity, how we navigate our stream of consciousness—may matter more than we ever dreamed.
