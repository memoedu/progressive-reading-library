# 人工智能不必非常出色就能取代人类｜AI doesn't have to be very good to displace humans

---

## 正文

关于 "AI" 究竟会不会好到足以 displace humans，以及如果会，何时会发生，一直存在争论。在这场争论中，乐观者往往关注 AI 正在 improving 多少，而悲观者则指出 AI 不如理想人类的种种方面。我认为，这忽略了两个非常重要的 factors。

第一，那些可能遭到裁撤的 jobs，例如 first-line customer service、面向 low margin 或不关心 customer 的 industries 的 customer service 等，往往由 poorly designed system 中漠不关心的人类担任，而且[人类甚至不太擅长 simple tasks](https://danluu.com/p95-skill/)，即使他们[十分在意这些 tasks](https://danluu.com/bad-decisions/)。当我们漠不关心时，我们的表现简直糟透了；要做到至少有些相近的表现，并不需要 nearly-omniscient sci-fi level AI。

第二，companies 将会在许多 roles 中用 AI 来 replace humans，即使 AI 明显更差，只要 AI 便宜得多。其中一个已经发生这种情况的地方就是 phone trees（尽管这种 software 也许过于 basic，算不上 AI）。与它们所 replaced 的人类相比，Phone trees 简直糟透了，但它们也便宜了 orders of magnitude。虽然有些 high-margin high-touch companies 不会让你经过 phone tree，但在大多数 companies，对于寻求 customer service 的 customer 而言，大量 work hours 已经被 phone trees 所 replaced，随后又被带有糟糕 AI voice recognition 的 phone trees 再次 replaced；在我看来，这些比老式 touch pad phone trees 更差。这种体验并不好，而当 AI automates 更多 process 时，体验可能会糟糕得多。

但另一方面，下面是我上周经历的一次并不算罕见的 customer service interaction，对方是个人类，却明显不如一个平庸的 AI。我为 MRI 安排了一次 appointment。这次 MRI 是为了检查一个让我说话时会痛的 jaw problem。我本来希望 scheduling 会很容易，这样就不必花很多时间打电话说话。但正如与 bureaucracy 打交道时常有的情况一样，事情并不容易。

以下是这件事所经历的 steps。

1.  出现 jaw pain。
2.  去看 dentist。当 dentist determines 这很可能是 joint problem 时，拿到 MRI 的 referral。
3.  Dentist 从 UW Health 取得 referral form，按照 form 上的 instructions 将它 faxes 给他们，并通过 emails 给我一份 referral 的副本。
4.  给 UW Health 打电话。
5.  UW Health 试图为我 schedule 一次 pituitary 的 MRI。
6.  请他们确认没有 error。
7.  UW Health 又看了一遍，发现那是别的事情的 referral。他们找不到任何与我有关的东西。
8.  请 UW Health 给 dentist 打电话，把事情解决。UW Health 声称他们不能打电话。
9.  再次与 dentist 交谈。请 dentist 再次 fax 那份 form。
10.  再次给 UW Health 打电话。请他们再次检查。
11.  UW Health 说 form 的填写是 illegally 的。
12.  再次请他们给 dentist 打电话，把事情解决。
13.  UW Health 说这不可能。
14.  问为什么。
15.  UW Health 说：“出于 legal reasons”。
16.  意识到这大概是对 HIPAA regulations 一种含糊而毫无根据的恐惧。换一种说法，再次客气地请他们给我的 dentist 打电话。
17.  UW Health 同意给 dentist 打电话。挂断电话。
18.  看了 referral，意识到按照 form 上的 instructions，UW Health 以外的人（比如我的 dentist）实际上不可能 legally 填写这份 form。
19.  再次与 dentist 交谈。
20.  Dentist 同意这份 form 不可能填写，与 UW Health 交谈，弄清该怎么办。
21.  给 UW Health 打电话，看看他们是否收到了 form。
22.  UW Health 确认已收到 valid referral。
23.  请求 schedule 最早可行的 appointment。
24.  UW Health 不确定他们是否能 accept 来自 dentists 的 referrals。去查一查。
25.  UW Health determines 可以 accept 来自 dentist 的 referral。
26.  UW Health 提议了 2/17 的一个时间。
27.  我指出，自己大概没法赴约，因为有一个 conflicting appointment，也是在 UW Health；我之所以知道，是因为 log into UW Health online system 时能在我的 profile 上看到它。
28.  UW Health 提议了 2/18 的一个时间。
29.  我指出了 UW Health system 中另一个 conflict。
30.  UW Health 开始寻找更晚日期的时间。
31.  我问 2/17 是否还有其他可用时间。
32.  UW Health 注意到 2/17 还有其他可用时间，于是将我 schedules 在 2/17 更晚的时候。

我讲这件事，不是因为这是个糟糕的 case，而是因为它是个 representative 的 case[1](https://danluu.com/customer-service/#fn:R)。在这个 case 中，我的 dentist 的诊所很乐意做任何必要的事情来解决问题，但如果不是反复提醒 UW Health，与我的 dentist 交谈会是解决问题最简单的办法，他们就不肯与诊所交谈。即便如此，我也不确定这有多大帮助。这甚至还不算特别糟糕，因为我成功说服了固执的一方配合。糟糕的 cases 是双方都拒绝彼此交谈，而且都声称只有另一方联系自己，情况才能得到解决，结果造成 deadlock。好的 cases 则是双方都愿意彼此交谈，解决所有需要解决的问题。一个提供 simple scheduling 的 non-AI phone tree 或 web app，会远胜于这里的 human customer service 体验。一个只是对 web app 所用 API 做了 light wrapper 的 AI chatbot，虽然不如能直接使用普通 website，但仍然比 human customer service 更好。一个不只是 light wrapper 的 AI chatbot，则会彻底胜过 UW Health 做这项工作的人类。

反对使用 computers 而不是人类的理由是，computers 不擅长 handling error conditions，不能 adapt 于 unusual situations，而且按照 mechanical rules 行事，这往往会 generate 荒谬的 outcomes；但这恰恰就是我们眼下与人类打交道时的处境。感觉已经像是在与 computer program 打交道。不是现代的 computer program，而是 80 年代的 compiler：它告诉你至少有一个 error，却不给任何其他 diagnostic information。

UW Health 给我的 dentist 发了一份 instructions 根本无法遵照执行的 form。这不太好，但可以理解；人总会犯错。然而，当他们收到退回的 form，发现它没有被 correctly 填写时，他们没有联系我的 dentist，而是直接把它扔掉了。就像 80 年代的 compiler 一样。Error（错误）！第二次，他们告诉我 form 填写得 incorrectly。Error（错误）！另一端明明有个人类，本可以注意到这份 form 根本无法填写。但就像 80 年代的 compiler 一样，他们在第一个 error 处就停了下来，没有再多想。这件事最终得到了解决，但过程中我收到的 error messages，远比我对现代 program 的预期糟糕。Clang（甚至 gcc）给我的 error messages 都比这里收到的好得多。

当然，正如我们在 healthcare.gov 上看到的，将 interaction outsourcing 给 computers 并不能保证好的 results。[有些人声称 market solutions 会 automatically fix 任何 problem](https://danluu.com/tech-discrimination/)，但这些[说法](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=9051049)[并不](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=5523992)[总能](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=5208988)[得到](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=453507)[印证](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=4216898)。

![寻找负责 AdSense 的 Googler。需要 AdSense 方面的帮助。我的 emails 始终无人回复。你会是那位愿意帮忙的特别的 Googler 吗？](https://danluu.com/images/customer-service/seeking_googler.webp)

这是有人为了找到 Google 的一个真人来帮忙，在 Facebook 上投放了几个月的广告，因为他们能用的所有常规办法都失败了。Google 也许拥有世界上最 advanced 的 ML，他们与其他任何 public company 一样是 market driven 的，而且他们大体上一直试图通过 automate 来消除 first-level support 这样的 service jobs，因为 support 无法 scale。因此，在 Google 获得 support 最可靠的 methods 是

1.  足够出名，让一篇 blog post 或一条 tweet 能得到足够的关注，从而获得回复。
2.  在 Google 工作，或者认识在 Google 工作的人，而且对方不仅愿意 file 一个 internal bug，还愿意推动它，确保它得到处理。

如果你无法直接采用这些 methods 中的任何一种，投放广告其实是个相当合理的 solution。(1) 和 (2) 并非总是有效，但它们比不出名却指望一篇 blog post 能登上 HN，或者[成为 paying customer](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=526688)，更为有效。这里的重点不是数落 Google，而是 automated customer service solutions 并非万无一失，即使你拥有能 beat 世界上最强 go player 的 AI，以及坐满了多栋楼、正在将同样的 technology applying 于 practical problems 的人们，也是如此。

虽然用 computers 来 replacing humans 并不总能带来很好的体验，但用于 scheduling 和 referrals 等事情的优秀 computer based systems，现在就已经可以远胜于 bureaucratic institution 中的普通人类[2](https://danluu.com/customer-service/#fn:L)。有了合适的 setup，computer-based system 可以比 human-based system 更善于将棘手 problems escalating 给有能力 solving 它们的人。而 computers 在这方面只会越来越好。会有 bugs。也会有糟糕的 systems。但 human systems 中已经存在 bugs。也已经存在糟糕的 human systems。

我不确定在自己的有生之年，technology 是否会 advance 到让 computers 能够媲美 well designed system 中乐于助人的人类的程度。但我们已经到了这样一个地步：computers 可以与 poorly designed system 中漠不关心的人类一样有帮助，而相当一部分 service jobs 恰恰就是如此。

### 2023 年更新

当 ChatGPT 于 2022 年 released 时，上文所述的 2015 年那场争论又发生了一遍，双方的 arguments 仍然相同。人们又一次说，AI（这一次是 ChatGPT 和 LLMs）不能 replace humans，因为出色的人比 ChatGPT 更好。他们经常挑出几个 examples，展示 ChatGPT 说出极其愚蠢的话，也就是在 "hallucinating"，但如果你[让一个人 explain 某件事，即便是 world-class expert，他们也经常会 hallucinate 一个完全虚假的 explanation](https://mastodon.social/@danluu/109458271351706514)。

悲观一方的许多人 argued，LLMs 还需要几十年才能 replace humans，他们给出的 reasons，恰恰就是我们在 2015 年已指出是 false 的那些。所有人都是在[多个 industries 已经因为 pre-LLM "AI" automation 而 massive cuts 了所需 employ 的人数之后](https://mastodon.social/@danluu/109579276227658596)才提出这个 argument 的，其中许多人甚至在 companies 已经 laid people off，并用 LLMs replaced people 之后，还在提出这个 argument。[我当时对此发表了评论，用的 reasoning 与我在这篇 2015 年文章中使用的相同](https://mastodon.social/@danluu/109458264830891897)，然后才意识到，[我早在 2015 年就已经写下了这套 reasoning](https://mastodon.social/@danluu/109458901726543386)。不过，请对我宽容一点；我只是个人，不是 computer，所以我的 memory 会出错。

如今距离 ChatGPT released 已过了一年，一轮又一轮的 layoff 中，customer service orgs 被 cut to the bone，并且大部分被 AI replaced，这进一步证明了那些认为 LLMs 要很久才会 displace human jobs 的 AI 悲观者错得有多离谱。AI customer service 看起来相当糟糕，就像 human customer service 一样。但 human customer service 没有在 improving，而 AI customer service 正在 improving。例如，最近我把车送去 get the oil changed、rotate the tires，以及做第三件事（说来话长），由此经历了下面这些 customer service interactions。

1.  我给本地的 tire shop 和 oil change place[3](https://danluu.com/customer-service/#fn:O)打电话，问他们能不能为我的 car 做我想做的那三件事
2.  他们说可以
3.  我问能不能直接过去，还是需要 make an appointment
4.  他们说可以，我可以直接过去把事情办完
5.  我问能不能直接和 service manager 谈谈，多了解一些情况
6.  被转接给 service manager 后，我再次说明自己的需求，并问什么时候可以过去
7.  他们说，那会花很长时间，我需要 make an appointment。他们下周可以安排我过去。如果听了第一个人的话，我就会完全白跑一趟，往返开车一个小时，因为他们实际上不能在我直接上门时完成我想做的 work
8.  一周后，我把 car 送过去，和前台的一个人交谈，他问我需要做什么
9.  我说明了自己的需求，注意到他只记下了我说的大约 1/3，于是我接着追问，并且
10.  我问他们准备使用什么 oil
11.  那个人说："我们会用 right oil"
12.  我告诉他，我要 0W-20 synthetic，因为我的 car 有一份 service bulletin，说明这是 recommended 的，与 car 上的 label 不同，所以能否请他们记下来。
13.  那个人重复道："我们会用 right oil"。
14.  再次重复 (12)，措辞略有不同
15.  再次重复 (13)，措辞略有不同
16.  再次重复 (12)，措辞略有不同
17.  那个人说："都在 computer 里，computer 里有 right oil"。
18.  我问他 computer 说要用什么 oil
19.  那个人不耐烦地走到 computer 前，pull up 我的 car，告诉我，我的 car 应该用 5W-30
20.  我告诉他，由于那份 service bulletin，这不适合我的 vehicle，我要 0W-20 synthetic
21.  那个人看起来很震惊，说了声"哦"，然后看着 computer 说："哦，上面说我们也可以用 0W-20"
22.  那个人在我 car 的 sheet 上记下了 0W-20
23.  我离开了，心想自己要求的第三件事应该不会被做，或者不会被完全做完，因为它实际上没有被记下来
24.  第二天，我去取 car，他们果然完全没做第三件事。

总体而言，LLM 相比之下如何？它大概显著优于这个人；那个人表现得像个典型的瘾君子，既不想待在那里，也不想做任何事，而 LLM 也会 cheaper。不过，LLM 会不如一个 web interface，后者能让我 book 我确切想做的 work，并给做这项 work 的 tech 写一条 note。不管这是好事还是坏事，我觉得本地这家 tire / oil change place 不会很快给我提供一个好用的 web interface，让我 book 我确切想做的 work，因此，这个人将被 LLM replaced，而不是被一个 simple web app replaced。

## 原文

There's an ongoing debate over whether "AI" will ever be good enough to displace humans and, if so, when it will happen. In this debate, the optimists tend to focus on how much AI is improving and the pessimists point to all the ways AI isn't as good as an ideal human being. I think this misses two very important factors.

One, is that jobs that are on the potential chopping block, such as first-line customer service, customer service for industries that are either low margin or don't care about the customer, etc., tend to be filled by apathetic humans in a poorly designed system, and [humans aren't even very good at simple tasks](https://danluu.com/p95-skill/) they [care a lot about](https://danluu.com/bad-decisions/). When we're apathetic, we're absolutely terrible; it's not going to take a nearly-omniscient sci-fi level AI to perform at least somewhat comparably.

Two, companies are going to replace humans with AI in many roles even if AI is significantly worse as if the AI is much cheaper. One place this has already happened (though perhaps this software is too basic to be considered an AI) is with phone trees. Phone trees are absolutely terrible compared to the humans they replaced, but they're also orders of magnitude cheaper. Although there are high-margin high-touch companies that won't put you through a phone tree, at most companies, for a customer looking for customer service, a huge number of work hours have been replaced by phone trees, and were then replaced again by phone trees with poor AI voice recognition that I find worse than old school touch pad phone trees. It's not a great experience, and may get much worse when AI automates even more of the process.

But on the other hand, here's a not-too-atypical customer service interaction I had last week with a human who was significantly worse than a mediocre AI. I scheduled an appointment for an MRI. The MRI is for a jaw problem which makes it painful to talk. I was hoping that the scheduling would be easy, so I wouldn't have to spend a lot of time talking on the phone. But, as is often the case when dealing with bureaucracy, it wasn't easy.

Here are the steps it took.

1.  Have jaw pain.
2.  See dentist. Get referral for MRI when dentist determines that it's likely to be a joint problem.
3.  Dentist gets referral form from UW Health, faxes it to them according to the instructions on the form, and emails me a copy of the referral.
4.  Call UW Health.
5.  UW Health tries to schedule me for an MRI of my pituitary.
6.  Ask them to make sure there isn't an error.
7.  UW Health looks again and realizes that's a referral for something else. They can't find anything for me.
8.  Ask UW Health to call dentist to work it out. UW Health claims they cannot make phone calls.
9.  Talk to dentist again. Ask dentist to fax form again.
10.  Call UW Health again. Ask them to check again.
11.  UW Health says form is illegally filled out.
12.  Ask them to call dentist to work it out, again.
13.  UW Health says that's impossible.
14.  Ask why.
15.  UW Health says, “for legal reasons”.
16.  Realize that's probably a vague and unfounded fear of HIPAA regulations. Try asking again nicely for them to call my dentist, using different phrasing.
17.  UW Health agrees to call dentist. Hangs up.
18.  Look at referral, realize that it's actually impossible for someone outside of UW Health (like my dentist) to fill out the form legally given the instructions on the form.
19.  Talk to dentist again.
20.  Dentist agrees form is impossible, talks to UW Health to figure things out.
21.  Call UW Health to see if they got the form.
22.  UW Health acknowledges receipt of valid referral.
23.  Ask to schedule earliest possible appointment.
24.  UW Health isn't sure they can accept referrals from dentists. Goes to check.
25.  UW Health determines it is possible to accept a referral from a dentist.
26.  UW Health suggests a time on 2/17.
27.  I point out that I probably can't make it because of a conflicting appointment, also with UW Health, which I know about because I can see it on my profile with I log into the UW Health online system.
28.  UW Health suggests a time on 2/18.
29.  I point out another conflict that is in the UW Health system.
30.  UW Health starts looking for times on later dates.
31.  I ask if there are any other times available on 2/17.
32.  UW Health notices that there are other times available on 2/17 and schedules me later on 2/17.

I present this not because it's a bad case, but because it's a representative one[1](https://danluu.com/customer-service/#fn:R). In this case, my dentist's office was happy to do whatever was necessary to resolve things, but UW Health refused to talk to them without repeated suggestions that talking to my dentist would be the easiest way to resolve things. Even then, I'm not sure it helped much. This isn't even all that bad, since I was able to convince the intransigent party to cooperate. The bad cases are when both parties refuse to talk to each other and both claim that the situation can only be resolved when the other party contacts them, resulting in a deadlock. The good cases are when both parties are willing to talk to each other and work out whatever problems are necessary. Having a non-AI phone tree or web app that exposes simple scheduling would be far superior to the human customer service experience here. An AI chatbot that's a light wrapper around the API a web app would use would be worse than being able to use a normal website, but still better than human customer service. An AI chatbot that's more than a just a light wrapper would blow away the humans who do this job for UW Health.

The case against using computers instead of humans is that computers are bad at handling error conditions, can't adapt to unusual situations, and behave according to mechanical rules, which can often generate ridiculous outcomes, but that's precisely the situation we're in right now with humans. It already feels like dealing with a computer program. Not a modern computer program, but a compiler from the 80s that tells you that there's at least one error, with no other diagnostic information.

UW Health sent a form with impossible instructions to my dentist. That's not great, but it's understandable; mistakes happen. However, when they got the form back and it wasn't correctly filled out, instead of contacting my dentist they just threw it away. Just like an 80s compiler. Error! The second time around, they told me that the form was incorrectly filled out. Error! There was a human on the other end who could have noted that the form was impossible to fill out. But like an 80s compiler, they stopped at the first error and gave it no further thought. This eventually got resolved, but the error messages I got along the way were much worse than I'd expect from a modern program. Clang (and even gcc) give me much better error messages than I got here.

Of course, as we saw with healthcare.gov, outsourcing interaction to computers doesn't guarantee good results. [There are some claims that market solutions will automatically fix any problem](https://danluu.com/tech-discrimination/), but those [claims](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=9051049) [don't](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=5523992) [always](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=5208988) [work](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=453507) [out](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=4216898).

![Seeking AdSense Googler. Need AdSense help. My emails remain unanswered. Are you the special Googler who will help?](https://danluu.com/images/customer-service/seeking_googler.webp)

That's an ad someone was running for a few months on Facebook in order to try to find a human at Google to help them because every conventional technique they had at their disposal failed. Google has perhaps the most advanced ML in the world, they're as market driven as any other public company, and they've mostly tried to automate away service jobs like first-level support because support doesn't scale. As a result, the most reliable methods of getting support at Google are

1.  Be famous enough that a blog post or tweet will get enough attention to garner a response.
2.  Work at Google or know someone who works at Google and is willing to not only file an internal bug, but to drive it to make sure it gets handled.

If you don't have direct access to one of these methods, running an ad is actually a pretty reasonable solution. (1) and (2) don't always work, but they're more effective than not being famous and hoping a blog post will hit HN, or [being a paying customer](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=526688). The point here isn't to rag on Google, it's just that automated customer service solutions aren't infallible, even when you've got an AI that can beat the strongest go player in the world and multiple buildings full of people applying that same technology to practical problems.

While replacing humans with computers doesn't always create a great experience, good computer based systems for things like scheduling and referrals can already be much better than the average human at a bureaucratic institution[2](https://danluu.com/customer-service/#fn:L). With the right setup, a computer-based system can be better at escalating thorny problems to someone who's capable of solving them than a human-based system. And computers will only get better at this. There will be bugs. And there will be bad systems. But there are already bugs in human systems. And there are already bad human systems.

I'm not sure if, in my lifetime, technology will advance to the point where computers can be as good as helpful humans in a well designed system. But we're already at the point where computers can be as helpful as apathetic humans in a poorly designed system, which describes a significant fraction of service jobs.

### 2023 update

When ChatGPT was released in 2022, the debate described above in 2015 happened again, with the same arguments on both sides. People are once again saying that AI (this time, ChatGPT and LLMs) can't replace humans because a great human is better than ChatGPT. They'll often pick a couple examples of ChatGPT saying something extremely silly, "hallucinating", but if you [ask a human to explain something, even a world-class expert, they often hallucinate a totally fake explanation as well](https://mastodon.social/@danluu/109458271351706514).

Many people on the pessimist side argued that it would be decades before LLMs can replace humans for the exact reasons we noted were false in 2015. Everyone made this argument [after multiple industries had massive cuts in the number of humans they need to employ due to pre-LLM "AI" automation](https://mastodon.social/@danluu/109579276227658596) and many of these people even made this argument after companies had already laid people off and replaced people with LLMs. [I commented on this at the time, using the same reasoning I used in this 2015 post](https://mastodon.social/@danluu/109458264830891897) before realizing that [I'd already written down this line of reasoning in 2015](https://mastodon.social/@danluu/109458901726543386). But, cut me some slack; I'm just a human, not a computer, so I have a fallible memory.

Now that it's been a year ChatGPT was released, the AI pessimists who argued that LLMs would displace human jobs for a very long time have been proven even more wrong by layoff after layoff where customer service orgs were cut to the bone and mostly replaced by AI, AI customer service seems quite poor, just like human customer service. But human customer service isn't improving, while AI customer service is. For example, here are some recent customer service interactions I had as a result of bringing my car in to get the oil changed, rotate the tires, and do a third thing (long story).

1.  I call my local tire shop and oil change place[3](https://danluu.com/customer-service/#fn:O) and ask if they can do the three things I want with my car
2.  They say yes
3.  I ask if I can just drop by or if I need to make an appointment
4.  They say yes, I can just drop by to get the world done
5.  I ask if I can talk to the service manager directly to get some more info
6.  After being transferred to the service manager, I describe what I want again and ask when I can come in
7.  They say that will take a lot of time and I'll need to make an appointment. They can get me in next week. If I listened to the first guy, I would've had a completely pointless one-hour round trip drive since they couldn't, in fact, do the work I wanted as a drop-in
8.  A week later, I bring the car in and talk to someone at the desk, who asks me what I need done
9.  I describe what I need and notice that he only writes down about 1/3 of what I said, so I follow up and
10.  I ask what oil they're going to use
11.  The guy says "we'll use the right oil"
12.  I tell him that I want 0W-20 synthetic because my car has a service bulletin indicating that this is recommended, which is different from the label on the car, so could they please note this.
13.  The guy repeats "we'll use the right oil".
14.  (12) again, with slightly different phrasing
15.  (13) again, with slightly different phrasing
16.  (12) again, with slightly different phrasing
17.  The guy says, "it's all in the computer, the computer has the right oil".
18.  I ask him what oil the computer says to use
19.  Annoyed, the guy walks over to the computer and pull up my car, telling me that my car should use 5W-30
20.  I tell him that's not right for my vehicle due to the service bulletin and I want 0W-20 synthetic
21.  The guy, looking shocked, says "Oh", and then looks at the computer and says "oh, it says we can also use 0W-20"
22.  The guy writes down 0W-20 on the sheet for my car
23.  I leave, expecting that the third thing I asked for won't be done or won't completely be done since it wasn't really written down
24.  The next day, I pick up my car and they fully didn't do the third thing.

Overall, how does an LLM compare? It's probably significantly better than this guy, who acted like an archetypical stoner who doesn't want to be there and doesn't want to do anything, and the LLM will be cheaper as well. However, the LLM will be worse than a web interface that lets me book the exact work I want and write a note to the tech who's doing the work. For better or for worse, I don't think my local tire / oil change place is going to give me a nice web interface that lets me book the exact work I want any time soon, so this guy is going to be replaced by an LLM and not a simple web app.
