# 可疑的不连续性｜Suspicious discontinuities

---

## 正文

如果你在去年年底读过一些 personal finance forums，那么你很可能遇到过这样的问题：有人正拼命想在年底之前 lose money。做到这一点有好几种办法；一种经常有人建议的方案是 buy [预计会 expire worthless 的 put options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Put_option)，让 buyer 能够（很可能）take a loss。

人们寻找 lose money 的办法，其中一个原因是，在 U.S.，health insurance subsidy 存在一个 [hard income cutoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Protection_and_Affordable_Care_Act#Subsidy_Cliff_at_400%_FPL)，为 \$48,560 for individuals (higher for larger households; $100,400，适用于四口之家）。有许多 factors 可能使具体情况有所不同（age、location、household size、type of plan），但在各种 circumstances 下，一个人从 cut-off 的一侧跨到另一侧后，health insurance cost increase 大约 $7200/yr. That means if an individual buying ACA insurance was going to earn $55k 的情况并不罕见，他们通过 reducing 自己的 income，幅度为 \$6440 and getting under the \$48,560 subsidy ceiling，会比 earning $55k 时过得更好。

尽管这是一个严重得不同寻常的例子，[U.S. tax policy 中充满了 disincentivize increasing earnings 的 discontinuities，而且在某些情况下，它们实际上还会 incentivize decreasing earnings](http://www.cbo.gov/sites/default/files/cbofiles/attachments/11-15-2012-MarginalTaxRates.pdf)。其他一些 discontinuities 包括 [TANF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Assistance_for_Needy_Families) income limit、[Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid) income limit、适用于 free coverage 的 [CHIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Health_Insurance_Program) income limit，以及适用于 reduced-cost coverage 的 CHIP income limit。它们随 location 和 circumstance 而有所不同；TANF 和 Medicaid income limits 落在通常被认为是“low income”的 ranges 内，而 CHIP limits 则落在通常被认为是“middle class”的 ranges 内。这些 subsidy discontinuities 与 ACA subsidy discontinuity 有相同的 impact -- 在某些 income levels，人们会被 incentivized 去 lose money。

> 任何人都可以安排自己的事务，让自己的 taxes 尽可能 low；他没有义务选择最有利于 treasury 收款的安排。甚至不存在 increase 自己的 taxes 这样的爱国义务。Courts 一再表示，通过安排事务让 taxes 尽可能 low，并没有什么不可告人之处。每个人都这样做，无论贫富，而且这样做都没有错，因为任何人都没有公共义务，去 pay 超过 law 所 demands 的数额。

如果你赞同 [Learned Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Hand) 的这段名言，那么，为了 reduce effective tax rate、increasing disposable income 而 losing money，在 individual level 上就是完全 legitimate 的 behavior。然而，一个鼓励人们 lose money 的 tax system，似乎是 sub-optimal 的；人们或许会通过 buying put options，把钱 funneling 给（on average）比自己 wealthier 得多的 options traders。

对上述问题，一个简单的解决办法是采用 slow phase-outs，而不是 sharp thresholds。有些 subsidies 实际上已经采用 Slow phase-outs，尽管这也可能带来问题，但通常比在 tax/subsidy policy 中 introducing sharp discontinuity 的问题更小。

在这篇文章中，我们会看看各种 discontinuities。

### Hardware 或 software queues（硬件或软件队列）

一个 naive queue 具有 discontinuous behavior。如果 queue 是 full，新的 entries 就会被 dropped。如果 queue 不是 full，新的 entries 就不会被 dropped。取决于你的目标，这往往会带来 non-ideal 的 impacts。例如，在 networking 中，一个 naive queue 可能会被认为对 overall bandwidth utilization 为 low 的 bursty workloads 是“unfair”的，因为 bandwidth utilization 为 low 的 workloads，“不应该”比那些 bursty 程度较低、但 use 更多 bandwidth 的 workloads 遭遇更多 drops（取决于你的目标是什么，也可以说这并非 unfair）。

这类问题的一类 solutions 是 [random early drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_early_detection) 及其 variants，它让 incoming items 具有一个被 dropped 的 probability，这个 probability 可以由 queue fullness（也可能包括其他 factors）来 determined，从而 smoothing out 这个 discontinuity，并 mitigating 由 queue drops 的 discontinuous probability 所 caused 的问题。

[这篇关于 link aggregators 中 voting 的文章](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/)，本质上也是同样的想法，尽管从某种意义上说，polarity 是 reversed 的。某个内容获得多少 traffic，取决于它是否在 front page 上，而这里存在一个非常 sharp 的 discontinuity。你可以把它看作：一个 link 如果只 receives N-1 votes，就会从 queue 中被 dropped；如果 receives N votes，就不会被 dropped。

### [College admissions 与 Pell Grant recipients（大学录取与佩尔助学金领取者）](https://www.insidehighered.com/admissions/article/2019/01/28/study-pressure-enroll-more-pell-eligible-students-has-skewed-colleges)

[Pell Grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pell_Grant) 开始被用作一个 proxy，用来衡量 schools 对 helping/admitting low-income students 有多认真。其 first order impact 是：在 Pell Grant threshold 之上的 students，被 admitted 的 probability 显著 reduced，而在 Pell Grant threshold 之下的 students，被 admitted 的 chance 则显著 higher。这样说来，似乎一切都在按预期运作。

然而，当我们看看每个 group 内部发生了什么，就会发现，如果目标是 benefit 来自 low income families 的 students，那么这些 outcomes 与我们想要的正好相反。在不 qualify for Pell Grant 的人中，income 为 lowest 的那些人受到的 impacted 最为 severely，admission 的 probability 也 reduced 得最为 severely。在确实 qualify 的人中，最有可能 benefit 的却是 income 为 highest 的那些人；如果你的目标是 benefit 来自 low income families 的 students，这同样与你大概想要的结果相反。

我们可以在下面的 graphs 中看到这些情况，它们是两所 universities 的 students 在 2008（第一张 graph）和 2016（第二张 graph）的 parental income histograms，其中 red line 表示 Pell Grant threshold。

![两所 universities 在 2008 的 students income distribution 的 Histogram；relative to general population，high incomes 被高度 overrepresented，但 distribution 是 smooth 的](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/pell-2008.webp)

![两所 universities 在 2016 的 students income distribution 的 Histogram；high incomes 仍被高度 overrepresented，在 Pell grant threshold 处还存在一个 sharp discontinuity；plot 看起来大致是两个 upwards sloping piecewise linear functions，在 Pell grant threshold 处的 discontinuity 位置 drop 回接近 0](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/pell-2016.webp)

universities 针对 Pell Grant recipients 进行 optimizing 所带来的一个 second order effect 是，精明的家长也可以像一些人那样，在最后一刻 cut 自己的 taxable income。有人可能会把钱放进 traditional IRA，而不是 Roth IRA；如果他们已经达到 IRA contribution limit，就可以尝试在 options 上 lose money，实际上是把钱 transferring 给很可能比自己 wealthier 的 options traders，以便让自己的 income 降到 Pell Grant threshold 以下，increasing 自己的孩子被 selective school admitted 的 probability。

### [p-values（p 值）](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-value)

psychology papers 的 Authors 受到 incentivized，去 produce 其 [p values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-value) below 某个 threshold 的 papers，这个 threshold 通常是 0.05，但有时是 0.1 或 0.01。[Masicampo et al. 对三本 psychology journals 中 published 的 papers 的 p values 进行了 plotted](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/22853650)，并发现 p values 恰好 below 0.05 的 papers 数量高得令人好奇。

![published p-values 的 Histogram；在 p=0.05 处出现 spike](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/p-value.webp)

p = 0.05 处的 spike 与若干不太妙的 hypothesis 相符，例如：

-   Authors 在 fudging results，以得到 p = 0.05
-   与 p = 0.055 的情况相比，Journals 更有可能 accept 一篇 p = 0.05 的 paper
-   与 p = 0.05 的情况相比，如果 p = 0.055，Authors 就远不那么可能 submit results

[Head et al. (2015)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4359000/) 对若干 fields 的 evidence 进行了 surveys。

多年来，Andrew Gelman 等人一直在倡导摒弃 statistical significance 和 p-value thresholds 这一观念，[关于其中的原因，请参见这篇 paper 中的简短 summary](https://stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/research/unpublished/abandon.pdf)。这不仅会 reduce authors 在 p values 上 cheat 的 incentive，而且还有其他理由让我们不希望用一条 bright-line rule 来 determine 某件事是否是“significant”。

### [High school exit exam scores（高中毕业考试分数）](https://danluu.com/matura-2013.pdf)

这是 Polish language exam 的 high school exit exam scores 的 histogram。我们可以看到，score 为 30 或恰好 above thirty 的 students 数量高得令人好奇，而 score 在 23-29 之间的 students 数量却低得令人好奇。这是 2013 年的情况；我看过的其他年份（2010-2012）也显示出类似的 discontinuity。

在我考察过的年份（2010-2013）中，Math exit exam scores 没有 exhibit 任何不寻常的 discontinuities。

![](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/matura-polish-2013.webp)

[一位匿名 reddit 评论者对此作了解释](https://www.reddit.com/r/dataisbeautiful/comments/1bqf9r/unusual_distributions_of_scores_on_final/c994zxt/)：

> 当 teacher 在 grading matura（final HS exam）时，他／她不知道这是谁的 test。唯一知道的是：student 的 number（code），以及 matura 来自哪个 district（通常来自 Poland 完全不同的地区）。这个 system 的设计是为了 prevent 任何形式的 manipulation，例如，teachers supervisor 会时不时过来 check test 是否被 graded correctly。我不想过多谈论 system flaws（以及 advantages），这些在世界上每一个设有 final tests 的 education system 中都广为人知，不过你必须记住，有一份 key，teachers 在 grading 时会非常严格地 follow 它。
> 
> 所以，当 test 的 score below 30% 时，exam 就是 failed。不过，在 protocol 中作出最后的 statement 之前，一个由 3 人组成的 commision（我不记得确切人数了）会再次 checking test。就在这时，humanities 与 math 之间的 difference 显现出来：teachers 经常试着 find 一个（或几个）missing points，好让 test 不至于被 failed，因为这对这个人及其 school 来说是一场悲剧，也会给 grading team 带来一些麻烦。当你在 grading writing 或 open questions 时，Finding 一个“missing” point 并没有那么难，polish language 就是这种情况，但在 math 中几乎不可能。所以，这就是 distribution of scores 如此不同的原因。

与 p values 一样，存在 bright-line threshold 会导致令人好奇的 behavior。在这个例子中，任何 subject 的 scoring below 30（每个 subject 都要求 30 或 above）以及 failing the exam，都会给人带来 arbitrary negative effects，因此，如果有容易做到的办法，teachers 通常会尽力 prevent 人们 failing，但这个问题更深层的根源在于这样一种观念：必须 produce 一份 certification，而它是对 continuous score 的 discretization。

### Birth month 与 sports（出生月份与体育运动）

这些是 [UEFA Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Youth_League) 中 football (soccer) players 的 scatterplots。这两个 plots 的 x-axis 都是 players 的年龄 modulo 年，即他们的 birth month 从 0 到 1 进行 normalized 后的值。

左边的 graph 是一个 histogram，它显示，一个人的 birth 在一年内的位置与其进入 UEFA Youth League (U19) level 的 club 的 odds 之间，存在非常 strong relationship。右边的 graph 则声称显示，birth time 与在 field 上提供的 actual value 仅有 weakly correlated 的关系。authors 使用 playing time 作为 value 的 proxy，想必是因为它容易 measure。这并不是一个很好的 measure，但他们发现的 result（在 making the U19 league 这一条件下，younger-within-the-year players 具有 higher value）与其他关于 sports 和 discrimination 的 studies 是 consistent with 的；例如，这些 studies 发现，[在 baseball 的 desegregation 之后数十年间，black baseball players 都 significantly better than white baseball players，French-Canadian defensemen 也 better than average（人们对 French-Canadians 的刻板印象是他们害怕 fight、不够努力，而且过于 focused on offense）](https://danluu.com/tech-discrimination/)。

上面的 graphs 没有直接 shown 这个 discontinuity，因为这些 graphs 只 show 一年的 birth date。如果我们跨多个年份，按 cohort 来 plot birth date，我们预计会在 player 进入 UEFA youth league 的 probability 中看到 sawtooth pattern，其中在 threshold 前一天与后一天出生的人之间，会有 10x difference。

![](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/u19-age.webp)

这种 phenomenon，即 birth day 或 month 是 higher-level youth sports 以及 pro sports 的 participation 的良好 predictor，已经在多种 sports 中得到 studied。

人们普遍认为，这是由 youth sports 中的一个 discontinuity 所 caused 的：

1.  孩子们按以年计的 age 被 bucketed 到 groups 中，并与同一年的人 compete
2.  在给定的一年内，年龄较大的孩子更 stronger、faster 等，而且 perform better
3.  这 causes older-within-year 的孩子 outcompete 年龄较小的孩子，随后又因多种原因 results in older-within-year 的孩子具有 higher levels of participation

这可以说是 youth sports 运作方式中的一个“bug”。但是，[正如我们在 baseball 中所见](https://danluu.com/bad-decisions/)，[以及一项对多种 sports 的 survey 所显示的那样](https://danluu.com/tech-discrimination/)，即使人们公开讨论这些 decisions 有多糟糕，那些显然糟糕、让单个 teams 损失数千万乃至数亿美元的 decision making，仍然可以持续数十年。在这里，youth sports teams 并不是 pro teams 的 feeder teams，因此它们没有 financial incentive 去 select 相对于其 age 而言 skilled 的 players（而不是仅仅因为年龄稍大就 taller 和 faster 的 players），所以这种 system-wide non-optimal 状况，比 pro sports teams 作出完全处于其自身控制之下的 locally non-optimal decisions 更难 fix。

### [Procurement auctions（采购拍卖）](http://www.keikawai.com/Full_0804.pdf)

Kawai et al. 考察了 Japanese government procurement，以寻找类似于 [Porter et al. (1993)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4013) 中所描述的 suspicious pattern of bids；后者考察了 Long Island（位于美国 New York）的 procurement auctions 中的 collusion。其中给出的一个例子是：

> 1983 年 2 月，New York State Department of Transportation (DoT) 为 0.8 miles 的 road 的 resurfacing 举行了一场 pro- curement auction。这场 auction 中的 lowest bid 是 \$4 million，而 DoT 决定不 award 这份 contract，因为相对于它自己的 cost estimates，这个 bid 被认为过高。该 project 于 1983 年 5 月再次进行 reauction，initial auction 中的所有 bidders 都 participated。这次 reauction 中的 lowest bid 比 initial auction 中的高 20%，由此前的 low bidder submitted。这份 contract 再次没有被 awarded。DoT 于 1984 年 2 月举行了第三次 auction，bidders 与 initial auction 中相同。第三次 auction 中的 lowest bid 比第二次高 10%，仍由同一个 bidder submitted。DoT 显然认为这很可疑：“值得注意的是，同一家 firm 在每次 auctions 中都 submitted 了 low bid。由于这些 unusual bidding patterns，直到 1987 年，这份 contract 都没有被 awarded。”

可以认为，这是意料之中的，因为不同的 firms 具有不同的 cost structures，因此，在某个特定 project 的 auction 中的 lowest bidder，也理应是同一 project 后续 auctions 中的 lowest bidder。为了 distinguish firms 之间的 collusion 与真实的 structural cost differences，Kawai et al. (2015) 考察了 first 和 second place firms 之间的 bid 的 difference 非常小的 auctions，使得 winner 实际上是 random 的。

在所 studied 的 auction structure 中，bidders submit 一个 secret bid。如果这个 secret bid 是 above 一个 secret minimum 的，那么 lowest bidder 就 wins 这场 auction，并获得 contract。否则，lowest bid 会被 revealed 给所有 bidders，然后进行另一 round 的 bidding。Kawai et al. 发现，在约 97% 的 auctions 中，第一 round submitted 了 lowest bid 的 bidder，在第二 round 也 submitted 了 lowest bid（second lowest bidder 仍为 second lowest 的 probability 是 26%）。

下面是一个 histogram，展示 first-lowest 与 second-lowest bidders（left column）以及 second-lowest 与 third-lowest bidders（right column）之间，在第一和第二 round bids 中的 difference。每一 row 都有不同的 filtering criteria，规定 auction 必须有多 close 才会被 included。在最上面的 row 中，所有进入第三 round 的 auctions 都被 included；在第二和第三 rows 中，first 和 second biders 之间的 normalized delta 分别是 less than 0.05 和 0.01；在最后一 row 中，first 和 third bidder 之间的 normalized delta 是 less than 0.03。所有 numbers 都经过 normalized，因为 auctions 的 absolute size 可能不同。

![](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/jp-procurement-bidding.webp)

我们可以看到，在 comparing second 和 third lowest bidders 时，第一与第二 round 之间的 deltas 的 distributions 大致是 symmetrical 的。但在 comparing first 和 second lowest bidders 时，zero 处存在一个 sharp discontinuity，indicating second-lowest bidder 几乎从不以超过 first-lower bidder 的幅度 lowers 自己的 bid。如果你阅读这篇 paper，就会看到，同样的 structure 在进入第三 round 的 auctions 中仍然 persists。

我并不是想专门挑 Japanese procurement auctions 的毛病。关于 procurement auctions，已有大量 literature 在许多 cases 中发现了 collusion，往往比上面呈现的 case 明目张胆得多（例如，有几家 firms，它们在各场 auctions 之间以 round-robin 的方式决定谁 wins；或者，有少数几家 firms，除了 winner 以外，每家 firm 都提交相同的 losing bid）。

### 备注

这篇文章其实没有什么目标或主旨，只是收集了一些我觉得有趣的 discontinuities。

也许值得一提的是，在我的职业生涯中，无论是对 discontinuities 保持怀疑并弄清它们的来源，还是 applying standard techniques 来 smooth out discontinuities，都让我受益良多。

要 finding discontinuities，像“drawing 一个 scatterplot”、“[drawing 一个 histogram](https://danluu.com/perf-tracing/#histogram)”、“drawing 这个 [CDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulative_distribution_function)”这样的 basic tools 往往很有用。其他加入 temporality 的 visualizations，例如 [flamescope](https://github.com/Netflix/flamescope)，也会很有用。

我们[在上文提到，queues 会 create 一种 discontinuity，在某些情况下应当将它 smoothed out](https://danluu.com/discontinuities/#hardware-or-software-queues)。我们还提到，其他种类的 thresholds 也会出现类似的 behavior，而且 randomization 也可以成为 smooth out thresholds 中的 discontinuities 的有用 tool。Randomization 还可以用于在使用 ML 以及其他 applications 进行 reducing precision 时，帮助 reducing quantization error。

## 原文

If you read any personal finance forums late last year, there's a decent chance you ran across a question from someone who was desperately trying to lose money before the end of the year. There are a number of ways someone could do this; one commonly suggested scheme was to buy [put options that were expected to expire worthless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Put_option), allowing the buyer to (probably) take a loss.

One reason people were looking for ways to lose money was that, in the U.S., there's [a hard income cutoff for a health insurance subsidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_Protection_and_Affordable_Care_Act#Subsidy_Cliff_at_400%_FPL) at $48,560 for individuals (higher for larger households; $100,400 for a family of four). There are a number of factors that can cause the details to vary (age, location, household size, type of plan), but across all circumstances, it wouldn't have been uncommon for an individual going from one side of the cut-off to the other to have their health insurance cost increase by roughly $7200/yr. That means if an individual buying ACA insurance was going to earn $55k, they'd be better off reducing their income by $6440 and getting under the $48,560 subsidy ceiling than they are earning $55k.

Although that's an unusually severe example, [U.S. tax policy is full of discontinuities that disincentivize increasing earnings and, in some cases, actually incentivize decreasing earnings](http://www.cbo.gov/sites/default/files/cbofiles/attachments/11-15-2012-MarginalTaxRates.pdf). Some other discontinuities are the [TANF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Assistance_for_Needy_Families) income limit, the [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid) income limit, the [CHIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children%27s_Health_Insurance_Program) income limit for free coverage, and the CHIP income limit for reduced-cost coverage. These vary by location and circumstance; the TANF and Medicaid income limits fall into ranges generally considered to be "low income" and the CHIP limits fall into ranges generally considered to be "middle class". These subsidy discontinuities have the same impact as the ACA subsidy discontinuity -- at certain income levels, people are incentivized to lose money.

> Anyone may arrange his affairs so that his taxes shall be as low as possible; he is not bound to choose that pattern which best pays the treasury. There is not even a patriotic duty to increase one's taxes. Over and over again the Courts have said that there is nothing sinister in so arranging affairs as to keep taxes as low as possible. Everyone does it, rich and poor alike and all do right, for nobody owes any public duty to pay more than the law demands.

If you agree with the famous [Learned Hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Learned_Hand) quote then losing money in order to reduce effective tax rate, increasing disposable income, is completely legitimate behavior at the individual level. However, a tax system that encourages people to lose money, perhaps by funneling it to (on average) much wealthier options traders by buying put options, seems sub-optimal.

A simple fix for the problems mentioned above would be to have slow phase-outs instead of sharp thresholds. Slow phase-outs are actually done for some subsidies and, while that can also have problems, they are typically less problematic than introducing a sharp discontinuity in tax/subsidy policy.

In this post, we'll look at a variety of discontinuities.

### Hardware or software queues

A naive queue has discontinuous behavior. If the queue is full, new entries are dropped. If the queue isn't full, new entries are not dropped. Depending on your goals, this can often have impacts that are non-ideal. For example, in networking, a naive queue might be considered "unfair" to bursty workloads that have low overall bandwidth utilization because workloads that have low bandwidth utilization "shouldn't" suffer more drops than workloads that are less bursty but use more bandwidth (this is also arguably not unfair, depending on what your goals are).

A class of solutions to this problem are [random early drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_early_detection) and its variants, which gives incoming items a probability of being dropped which can be determined by queue fullness (and possibly other factors), smoothing out the discontinuity and mitigating issues caused by having a discontinuous probability of queue drops.

[This post on voting in link aggregators](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/) is fundamentally the same idea although, in some sense, the polarity is reversed. There's a very sharp discontinuity in how much traffic something gets based on whether or not it's on the front page. You could view this as a link getting dropped from a queue if it only receives N-1 votes and not getting dropped if it receives N votes.

### [College admissions and Pell Grant recipients](https://www.insidehighered.com/admissions/article/2019/01/28/study-pressure-enroll-more-pell-eligible-students-has-skewed-colleges)

[Pell Grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pell_Grant) started getting used as a proxy for how serious schools are about helping/admitting low-income students. The first order impact is that students above the Pell Grant threshold had a significantly reduced probability of being admitted while students below the Pell Grant threshold had a significantly higher chance of being admitted. Phrased that way, it sounds like things are working as intended.

However, when we look at what happens within each group, we see outcomes that are the opposite of what we'd want if the goal is to benefit students from low income families. Among people who don't qualify for a Pell Grant, it's those with the lowest income who are the most severely impacted and have the most severely reduced probability of admission. Among people who do qualify, it's those with the highest income who are mostly likely to benefit, again the opposite of what you'd probably want if your goal is to benefit students from low income families.

We can see these in the graphs below, which are histograms of parental income among students at two universities in 2008 (first graph) and 2016 (second graph), where the red line indicates the Pell Grant threshold.

![Histogram of income distribution of students at two universities in 2008; high incomes are highly overrepresented relative to the general population, but the distribution is smooth](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/pell-2008.webp)

![Histogram of income distribution of students at two universities in 2016; high incomes are still highly overrepresented, there's also a sharp discontinuity at the Pell grant threshold; plot looks roughly two upwards sloping piecewise linear functions, with a drop back to nearly 0 at the discontinuity at the Pell grant threshold](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/pell-2016.webp)

A second order effect of universities optimizing for Pell Grant recipients is that savvy parents can do the same thing that some people do to cut their taxable income at the last minute. Someone might put money into a traditional IRA instead of a Roth IRA and, if they're at their IRA contribution limit, they can try to lose money on options, effectively transferring money to options traders who are likely to be wealthier than them, in order to bring their income below the Pell Grant threshold, increasing the probability that their children will be admitted to a selective school.

### [p-values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-value)

Authors of psychology papers are incentivized to produce papers with [p values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-value) below some threshold, usually 0.05, but sometimes 0.1 or 0.01. [Masicampo et al. plotted p values from papers published in three psychology journals](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/22853650) and found a curiously high number of papers with p values just below 0.05.

![Histogram of published p-values; spike at p=0.05](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/p-value.webp)

The spike at p = 0.05 consistent with a number of hypothesis that aren't great, such as:

-   Authors are fudging results to get p = 0.05
-   Journals are much more likely to accept a paper with p = 0.05 than if p = 0.055
-   Authors are much less likely to submit results if p = 0.055 than if p = 0.05

[Head et al. (2015)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4359000/) surveys the evidence across a number of fields.

Andrew Gelman and others have been campaigning to get rid of the idea of statistical significance and p-value thresholds for years, [see this paper for a short summary of why](https://stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/research/unpublished/abandon.pdf). Not only would this reduce the incentive for authors to cheat on p values, there are other reasons to not want a bright-line rule to determine if something is "significant" or not.

[An anonymous reddit commenter explains this](https://www.reddit.com/r/dataisbeautiful/comments/1bqf9r/unusual_distributions_of_scores_on_final/c994zxt/):

> When a teacher is grading matura (final HS exam), he/she doesn't know whose test it is. The only things that are known are: the number (code) of the student and the district which matura comes from (it is usually from completely different part of Poland). The system is made to prevent any kind of manipulation, for example from time to time teachers supervisor will come to check if test are graded correctly. I don't wanna talk much about system flaws (and advantages), it is well known in every education system in the world where final tests are made, but you have to keep in mind that there is a key, which teachers follow very strictly when grading.
> 
> So, when a score of the test is below 30%, exam is failed. However, before making final statement in protocol, a commision of 3 (I don't remember exact number) is checking test again. ### Birth month and sports

These are scatterplots of football (soccer) players in the [UEFA Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Youth_League). The x-axis on both of these plots is how old players are modulo the year, i.e., their birth month normalized from 0 to 1.

The graph on the left is a histogram, which shows that there is a very strong relationship between where a person's birth falls within the year and their odds of making a club at the UEFA Youth League (U19) level. The graph on the right purports to show that birth time is only weakly correlated with actual value provided on the field. The authors use playing time as a proxy for value, presumably because it's easy to measure. That's not a great measure, but the result they find (younger-within-the-year players have higher value, conditional on making the U19 league) is consistent with other studies on sports and discrimination, which ind (for example) that [black baseball players were significantly better than white baseball players for decades after desegregation in baseball, French-Canadian defensemen are also better than average (French-Canadians are stereotypically afraid to fight, don't work hard enough, and are too focused on offense)](https://danluu.com/tech-discrimination/).

The discontinuity isn't directly shown in the graphs above because the graphs only show birth date for one year. If we were to plot birth date by cohort across multiple years, we'd expect to see a sawtooth pattern in the probability that a player makes it into the UEFA youth league with a 10x difference between someone born one day before vs. after the threshold.

![](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/u19-age.webp)

This phenomenon, that birth day or month is a good predictor of participation in higher-level youth sports as well as pro sports, has been studied across a variety of sports.

It's generally believed that this is caused by a discontinuity in youth sports:

1.  Kids are bucketed into groups by age in years and compete against people in the same year
2.  Within a given year, older kids are stronger, faster, etc., and perform better
3.  This causes older-within-year kids to outcompete younger kids, which later results in older-within-year kids having higher levels of participation for a variety of reasons

This is arguably a "bug" in how youth sports works. But [as we've seen in baseball](https://danluu.com/bad-decisions/) [as well as a survey of multiple sports](https://danluu.com/tech-discrimination/), obviously bad decision making that costs individual teams tens or even hundreds of millions of dollars can persist for decades in the face of people pubicly discussing how bad the decisions are. In this case, the youth sports teams aren't feeder teams to pro teams, so they don't have a financial incentive to select players who are skilled for their age (as opposed to just taller and faster because they're slightly older) so this system-wide non-optimal even more difficult to fix than pro sports teams making locally non-optimal decisions that are completely under their control.

### [Procurement auctions](http://www.keikawai.com/Full_0804.pdf)

Kawai et al. looked at Japanese government procurement, in order to find suspicious pattern of bids like the ones described in [Porter et al. (1993)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4013), which looked at collusion in procurement auctions on Long Island (in New York in the United States). One example that's given is:

> In February 1983, the New York State Department of Transportation (DoT) held a pro- curement auction for resurfacing 0.8 miles of road. The lowest bid in the auction was $4 million, and the DoT decided not to award the contract because the bid was deemed too high relative to its own cost estimates. The project was put up for a reauction in May 1983 in which all the bidders from the initial auction participated. The lowest bid in the reauction was 20% higher than in the initial auction, submitted by the previous low bidder. Again, the contract was not awarded. The DoT held a third auction in February 1984, with the same set of bidders as in the initial auction. The lowest bid in the third auction was 10% higher than the second time, again submitted by the same bidder. The DoT apparently thought this was suspicious: “It is notable that the same firm submitted the low bid in each of the auctions. Because of the unusual bidding patterns, the contract was not awarded through 1987.”

It could be argued that this is expected because different firms have different cost structures, so the lowest bidder in an auction for one particular project should be expected to be the lowest bidder in subsequent auctions for the same project. In order to distinguish between collusion and real structural cost differences between firms, Kawai et al. (2015) looked at auctions where the difference in bid between the first and second place firms was very small, making the winner effectively random.

In the auction structure studied, bidders submit a secret bid. If the secret bid is above a secret minimum, then the lowest bidder wins the auction and gets the contract. If not, the lowest bid is revealed to all bidders and another round of bidding is done. Kawai et al. found that, in about 97% of auctions, the bidder who submitted the lowest bid in the first round also submitted the lowest bid in the second round (the probability that the second lowest bidder remains second lowest was 26%).

Below, is a histogram of the difference in first and second round bids between the first-lowest and second-lowest bidders (left column) and the second-lowest and third-lowest bidders (right column). Each row has a different filtering criteria for how close the auction has to be in order to be included. In the top row, all auctions that reached the third round were included; in second, and third rows, the normalized delta between the first and second biders was less than 0.05 and 0.01, respectively; in the last row, the normalized delta between the first and the third bidder was less than 0.03. All numbers are normalized because the absolute size of auctions can vary.

![](https://danluu.com/images/discontinuities/jp-procurement-bidding.webp)

We can see that the distributions of deltas between the first and second round are roughly symmetrical when comparing second and third lowest bidders. But when comparing first and second lowest bidders, there's a sharp discontinuity at zero, indicating that second-lowest bidder almost never lowers their bid by more than the first-lower bidder did. If you read the paper, you can see that the same structure persists into auctions that go into a third round.

I don't mean to pick on Japanese procurement auctions in particular. There's an extensive literature on procurement auctions that's found collusion in many cases, often much more blatant than the case presented above (e.g., there are a few firms and they round-robin who wins across auctions, or there are a handful of firms and every firm except for the winner puts in the same losing bid).

### Notes

This post doesn't really have a goal or a point, it's just a collection of discontinuities that I find fun.

One thing that's maybe worth noting is that I've gotten a lot of mileage out in my career both out of being suspicious of discontinuities and figuring out where they come from and also out of applying standard techniques to smooth out discontinuities.

For finding discontinuities, basic tools like "drawing a scatterplot", "[drawing a histogram](https://danluu.com/perf-tracing/#histogram)", "drawing the [CDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumulative_distribution_function)" often come in handy. Other kinds of visualizations that add temporality, like [flamescope](https://github.com/Netflix/flamescope), can also come in handy.

We [noted above that queues create a kind of discontinuity that, in some circumstances, should be smoothed out](https://danluu.com/discontinuities/#hardware-or-software-queues). We also noted that we see similar behavior for other kinds of thresholds and that randomization can be a useful tool to smooth out discontinuities in thresholds as well. Randomization can also be used to allow for reducing quantization error when reducing precision with ML and in other applications.
