但这些 [FAO data](http://faostat3.fao.org/mes/methodology_list/E) 是 right 吗？我做了两件事，试着弄清这一点。首先，我 checked 了相关的 [methodology](ftp://ftp.fao.org/docrep/fao/011/x9892e/x9892e00.pdf)，也就是他们当时[在使用的](http://www.fao.org/economic/the-statistics-division-ess/methodology/methodology-systems/food-balance-sheets-and-the-food-consumption-survey-a-comparison-of-methodologies-and-results/en/)那套。其次，我与其他 data 进行了 compared。

这些 FAO data 来自 Food Balances，represent 的是 food supply：production plus imports minus exports，再 plus 上一年的 stocks。这与 household surveys 形成 contrasts；在后者中，人们会被问到自己实际上 eating 多少。而这些 data 又来自 official statistics。

在我的第二张 chart 中，我汇集了许多不同的 sources。

“我的名字是 Ozymandias，charts 中的 chart：诸位强者啊，看看我的作品，然后绝望吧！”

这确实令人绝望。这些 data 存在巨大的 spread。下面我会解释每个 data series 来自哪里。FAO data 来自，嗯，FAO 的 food balances。

-   CIA data 来自一份 1984 年的 report，于 1999 年公开。[The Nutrient Content of the Soviet Food Supply](http://www.foia.cia.gov/sites/default/files/document_conversions/89801/DOC_0000498133.pdf)。与 FAO 的一样，这些 figures 针对的是 food supply，而不是 ingestion；后者由于 losses 而更低。这份 report 表示，Soviet food supply 的 nutrient content 与 US 的 matches。Carbohydrate intake 是 higher，而 fat intake 是 lower，但 gaps 当时在 narrowing。CIA analysts 还 estimate，从 production 到 consumption 的 losses 比 US 的 higher。他们也 indicate，从 public health perspective 来看，high caloric consumption 是 problematic。SU 各 regions 的 Food availability 存在差异。例如，Russian SFSR 的 fruit consumption per capita 是每人 40 kg，而 Siberian region 仅 consumed 12 kg。Estonia 的 Meat consumption 是 80 kg，但 Uzbekistan 仅为 31 kg。（1975-1981 年的 Data）
-   EXPORT data 来自 Vladimir G. Treml 于 1986 年为 US Bureau of the Census 撰写的一份 report，[Soviet Foreign Trade in Foodstuffs: A measure](https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=DO5aSpp_YyIC&pg=PA24&lpg=PA24&dq=ussr%20%20exports%20food%203000%20calories%20soviet&source=bl&ots=QeA88v49bu&sig=ppe7M3c_8417Y1uekEN8VRKw0us&hl=es&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwie-dr39vrLAhVM1xQKHUoaB70Q6AEIKjAC#v=onepage&q=united%20states&f=false)。他提到的事情之一，是 1970 至 1981 年间 food imports（尤其是 grain）的巨大 increase。据他说，当时 published 的 FAO figures（1975-77 年为每天 3400 cal）overstated 了 actual intake，主要因为对 Soviet foodstuff 的 nutritional composition 进行了 wrongly calculating（与 official Soviet data 相比）。他 references 的 CIA data 比 official Soviet data higher，但比 FAO 的 lower。他讨论了一些有趣的事情，例如 Soviet news agency Novosti 在 reported SU 的 daily caloric consumption 时，给出的竟是 CIA 在 1980 年的 figure。Treml 因 FAO data 过高而 rejects 它，并给出了 1970 年（2900-3100）和 1981 年（3000-3300）的 range of estimates。他还表示，Soviets 和 Western analysts 都可能在 overestimating Soviet nutrient quality。
-   BIRMAN 来自 Igor Birman 的著作 Personal consumption in the USSR and the USA (1981)。稍后我会解释为什么 Birman 值得在这里占有一席之地。他说，SU 的 caloric consumption 比 US 略少，尽管它本应 higher，因为其 population 是 younger，而且在 colder environment 中 performs 更多的 extenous work。Birman 给出的 1976 年 figure 是 3330（US 则为 3380）。他的 data 来自 _Narodnoye khozyaystvo SSR v 19-9odu: statisticheskiy yezhegodnik_（Soviet Union 出版的一本 yearbook。）
-   AvailRus 来自 [Nutritional Risk Factors in the Former Soviet Union](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK233395/pdf/Bookshelf_NBK233395.pdf#page=327)，这是 US 的 National Research Council 一本书中的 chapter。他们 reference 了 Goskomstat 开展的一项 consumer survey 的 data；Goskomstat 是 SU 的 official statistics agency，（据作者所说）这项 survey 的问题在于没有使用 representative sample（并不是说 sample 小，他们覆盖了 49.000 人）。
-   Goskomstat 这一项是来自 official Soviet sources 的 food balance data，也取自前面提到的 sources。
-   Russia (official) 指的是以 kg 计的 food consumption 的 official statistics，来自[这本书](https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=JSwstF6SEAkC&pg=PA509&lpg=PA509&dq=soviet+food+losses&source=bl&ots=V9IuVXFeeX&sig=RCXMqH4ojSKFbJEhKCfTpKO-Sv4&hl=es&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwjVmpvP0fzLAhUBaxQKHbcIC-wQ6AEISDAG#v=onepage&q=calories&f=false)，其中 references 了 [Torgovlya SSSR](http://istmat.info/files/uploads/17076/vneshnie_ekonomicheskie_svyazi_sssr_v_1989_g.pdf)（另一本 statistics yearbook），并使用 US Dept. of Agriculture 制定的 coefficients 将其 converted 为 calories
-   USDA adjusted 和 unadjusted 是来自 US department of agriculture 的 data。Unadjusted 可以理解为“food supply”，而 adjusted 则 accounting for food losses，试图 reflect 人们实际上 eating 多少。参见[这里](http://www.usda.gov/factbook/chapter2.pdf)的 figure 2.1。你可以从 USDA 的 website 获取这些 series。
-   ALLEN 是来自 Robert Allen 的 From Farm to Factory 的 data。他从 FAO 获取 raw data，并 calculates 自己的 data series，明确 accounting for food losses。最终 result 与 official FAO data 是 identical。他还 takes 其他 researchers（Wheatcroft、Jasny）into consideration，所以他的 estimate 意在做到 all-things-considered。

这里的问题在于，试图做同样事情的 sources（USDA 和 FAO）得到了不同的 results。FAO 的 series 看起来像 USDA unadjusted series。但 FAO 的 series 看起来也像 Allen 的，而 Allen 的本应 take losses into account。所以有人在某处犯了错误。我们或许可以相信 _official_ Soviet data，而不是 FAO 的。但[据 FAO 所说](http://www.fao.org/docrep/007/y5069e/y5069e05.htm)，Goskomstat surveys 有两个问题：一是它 oversamples 了 lower income households，另一个是，用来将 food 的 kg convert 为 calories 的 coefficients 比 FAO 的低 15-20%。他们 conclude：“存在如此大的 differences，很难有把握地对 caloric consumption 的 level 作出多少判断。”而且，即使采用那些据说已经 accounting for losses 的 official consumption data，它们仍然与 USDA 提供的 US intake 的同类 figure 相去甚远，所以 methodology 必定存在 differences。

因此，我的 conclusion 是，根据上述 data，**Soviet caloric intake 当时已经 high enough**，可以说 Soviet citizens 得到了相当充足的 fed，_就 calories 而言_。

[这里](http://www.fao.org/docrep/007/y5069e/y5069e05.htm#bm05)展示了使用不同 coefficients 将 food 的 kg convert 为 calories 时，情况会如何变化：

> Dronin and Bellinger (2005, 310) 指出，**per capita consumption figures 很可能 overstate 了 actually available amounts，因为 Soviet Union 的 inadequate transportation 和 storage infrastructure 导致 stores 中 frequent shortages，以及 foodstuffs 和 raw products 因 spoilage 而发生 significant loss**。顺带一提，这个问题一直持续到今天：根据最近的 estimates，equipment 和 infrastructure deficiencies 每年给 Russia 造成 grain 的 loss 达 15–20 million tons、meat 的 loss 达 1 million tons、milk 的 loss 达 7 million tons（“Russia’s Machines Cost 20 Million Tons of Grain, Institute Says”, by Marina Sysoyeva. Bloomberg, October 13, 2011）。

> 例如，Dronin and Bellinger (2005) 在发现 official government data 所 reported 的 per capita consumption increases，与 local Soviet press 对 food shortages 和 rationing 的广泛提及之间存在 discrepancy 后，对 Soviet food consumption statistics 的 reliability 提出了质疑。

> foreign observers 和 Soviet press 都反复指出，official production statistics 与 stores 中 food 的 actual availability 之间存在 discrepancies。**1988 年，在 perestroika 的高峰期，人们发现 Soviet authorities 一直在 inflating meat consumption statistics；此外还发现，meat consumption 中存在 considerable inequalities，其中 poorest socioeconomic strata 的 intake 自 1970 年以来实际上 declining 了 over 30 percent**（Dronin and Bellinger 2005）。

> 在整个 Soviet era，各种似乎持续存在的问题不断出现，困扰着该国的 food system。**Government experts estimated，如果 elimination 了 food 的 production、storage 和 distribution 中的 waste 和 spoilage，grain 的 availability 本可 increased 25 percent，fruits 和 vegetables 的 availability 本可 increased 40 percent，meat products 的 availability 本可 increased 15 percent**（Library of Congress 1991）。此外，正如 Gray (Gray 1990, 94) 所 estimated 的，“Soviet milk 中只有 60 percent 的 protein \[被\] humans consumed，而那些 \[被\] fed to animals 或直接 wasted 的 milk，\[所含\] protein 比 Soviet citizens \[consumed\] 的 meat 的一半还多。”

### (III) Soviet nutrition 的 Estimates

首先是 James R. Millar 的 _Politics, work, and daily life in the USSR (1987)_，它以 Soviet Interview Project 的 interviews（N=922）为基础。询问许多人实际经历了什么，是 official statistics 的一个很好的 alternative。他们发现了什么？

Citizens reported，每天都要 queuing，goods 的 quality 较 inferior，而且有时 lack of supply。大多数 respondents 表示，meat supply 存在 deficits。然而，几乎每个人每周都吃几次 meat（以及 cheese、kefir、milk 和 eggs），大多数人每天都吃。为什么既有 queuing 和 shortages，人们却又几乎每天都在吃 meat？一个 explanation 是，当时存在 legal private market（_rynok_）

> 现行的 food distribution system 显然是几乎所有 income classes 不满的一个主要来源，即使其中最富裕、最享有特权的人也不例外。

最后是 Igor Birman 的 _Personal Consumption in the USSR and the USA (1989)_。[Birman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Birman) 于 1928 年出生在 Moscow，获得了 Economics 的 PhD，曾在三家 factories 担任 Director of Planning，并于 1974 年移民 US，在那里担任 Soviet economy 的 consultant。他 criticised 了 CIA 的 estimates，而且看来，在其他所有人还说这种事几乎不可能发生时，他已经看到了 Soviet economy 的 downfall（Wilhelm 2003）。他的 analytical approach，也就是所谓的 _anecdotical economics_，依靠 facts、logic 和 intuition（Superforecaster approach？）。相比之下，CIA approach 倾向于更多地信任 official statistics，然后再对其 applied corrections。

> 在认真看待通过 models 得出的 calculations 的 results 之前，我们应该先看看所用的 data。遗憾的是，models 往往比 data 好得多。另一方面，不应该因为 ideas 和 assertions 没有得到 models 的支持就将其 dismissed。我在那个国家生活了 45 年，又在国外 studied 它的 economy 11 年，因此我对自己的 intuition 的信任不亚于对 models 的信任。我不是说所有 models 都不好，或者不应该使用，而是认为，被称为 anecdotal economics 的 reasoning、simple logic 等不应被 dismissed。 $$...$$
> 
> 考虑到已经发生的事情以及我们如今知道的情况，Birman 显然确实判断对了。….. 对 Soviet economy 的 studies 使用了一些最为“advanced”的 techniques….. 但在准确把握 Soviet economic situation 方面，这些 techniques 的表现显然不如 Birman 的“anecdotal economics”。…..然而，如果 scholarship 的过程要避免成为 Birman 所遭遇的那种.. self-perpetuating 且 closed 的 review 与 citation 的 system，就必须有一个比 refereed、scholarly journal 更好的仲裁者。我愿称之为 reality test。(Wilhelm 2003)

>

> Table 6.3 presents 了我自己的一些 calculations，而我必须立即说明，其中远非所有内容都是 trustworthy。关于 USSR 的 data，问题不仅在于，由于没有其他 data，我把 vegetables 的 total consumption 按 type，依照所有 types 的 farms（不包括 private plots）的 production 进行了 proportionately 的 distributed。这只是小事，尤其是因为（根据 official statistics！）private plots 的 production 只占 total production 的略多于 one-fourth。问题也不在于，我依照 Lokshin 得到了一个大得惊人的 melons 数量，而主要在于我们的 totals 肯定被 greatly overstated 了。当然，最主要的是 losses。如果把所报告的 per capitas consumption of vegetables（86 kg）multiplied by production，再把 resulting figure 从 total production（25 m tons）中 subtracted，我们发现 losses 只有 11.7 per cent。但 Soviet literature 中到处都在说 losses 要 significantly greater，至少应当是这个数的 two or three times。我们也不应忘记，只有未 sold 给 consumer 的部分才被视为 losses；consumer 把它们带回家后，还会扔掉 rotten 和 spoiled 的部分，这不低于 amount purchased 的 10 per cent（Soviet literature 经常提到这一点）。换句话说，读者在 examining Table 6.3 中 USSR 的 figures 时，绝不能相信自己的眼睛；无论是 consumption 的 total amount，还是各个 vegetables 的 consumption，都至少被 overstated 了约 20 per cent。

> 我必须非常明确地说，不应过分相信这个 estimate。我再次提醒读者，虽然我一直在 criticising CIA figures，但我不得不在很大程度上以它们作为 calculations 的 basis；尤其是，我并没有自己 determine price ratios 和 parities。由于 CIA report 没有给出所有 details，许多事情无法 checked。此外，我的 corrections 的 extent 是 arbitrary 的：它们最多只能让人对所需 corrections 的 direction 和 scale 有个大致印象。当然，我的 critique 远未结束。正如读者将在 Chapter 12 中看到的，还有一整系列 comments 直接 relate to food 的 calculations。

> **作为 Summarising，我要重申我的 basic conclusion：Soviet living standard，也就是 Soviet consumption，比 American 至少低一个 factor of 4.5**。这个 conclusion 可以作为一种 rough estimate。7 我绝不想、事实上也无法 idealise 我们的 American standard of living。我并没有对许多可以说不那么美好的事情视而不见。New Y ork 的 slums 很可怕。我在 New York 和 Washington 见过乞丐，也知道在一堆旧报纸上过夜并不是 Soviet 记者凭空编造的。虽然 America 已经远远走在 USSR 前面，但仍有一些房屋没有电话，甚至没有自来水、空调或电视。我走访了半数的州，见过的房屋远非每一栋都如梦中家园。在 West Virginia 一个偏远的山谷里，电视只能收到一个频道。并非每一种 good 或 service 都必然具有最高的 quality。火车会晚点。一场降雪就会让首都的生活陷入瘫痪。Europe（Western！）的 fresh fruits 和 vegetables 更好吃。Unemployment 当然不是什么礼物，就像靠发给穷人的微薄 benefits 度过余生也不是什么馈赠一样。America 这些“不那么美好”的事情还可以轻易列举下去；但我想说的是，逐一指出它们，只会更加凸显我的 conclusions 的 significance：尽管 American standard of living 的某些方面或许不那么好，尽管我们存在不足，Soviet Union 的生活、Soviet standard of living，仍要差 4.5 times。

### (III) 综合（A synthesis）

那么，我们应该怎么看呢？

**Soviet  Union 到处都有 queues 吗？**

是的。当时没有 rationing，人们可以去 supermarkets，购买自己想要的东西。但他们想要的东西往往没有供应。legal private market 帮助人们获得了一些缺少的 goods。

**Soviets 挨饿吗？发生过 famines 吗？**

1947 年之后没有。Holodomor 发生于 1932-33 年

**Soviet caloric intake 是 sufficiently high 吗？**

是的

**Soviet caloric intake 比 US 的 higher 吗？**

不是。我这样说，就是说 FAO 错了，而 Robert Allen 以 FAO data 为依据进行 calculations（并使用了他们的 multipliers），却没有注意到这一点。为了这样说，我不得不做了一次完整的 literature review，才形成这个看法。在读我的文章之前，你完全有理由相信 caloric intake 是 higher 的。现在不再如此了。除非某位 FAO 官员告诉我们，他们为什么使用那些似乎与 Sovietological literature 相悖的 coefficients。

**Soviet food quality 比 American 的 worse 吗？**

总体而言，是的。

**USSR 的 food consumption 与 US 的相比如何？**

请看上面 Birman 的 Table 7.1。这个 estimate 已针对 quality 和 quantity 进行 adjusted。Food consumption 在 quality 方面比 US 的 lower，而且在许多情况下，quantity 也如此。总体而言，除了 alcoholic beverages，它都比 US 的 lower。

**你怎么确定 FAO 错了？**

他们的 calories figure 来自使用错误的 coefficients。上面提到的 Analysts 指出了这一点。FAO 知道这一点，但他们仍保留自己的 coefficients，没有确切说明为什么 official Soviet coefficients 是错的。无论如何，[他们自己也怀疑](http://www.fao.org/docrep/007/y5069e/y5069e05.htm)能否从 Soviet data 得到 reliable estimates，

> 然而，存在如此大的 differences，就很难有把握地对 caloric consumption 的 level 多说些什么。

**对于 currencies、economic systems、preferences、climates 等不同的两个国家，你如何 compare 它们的 nutrition……？**

谨慎地进行。Igor Birman 在他的书中第 2 章解释了自己的 methodology

**food consumption 存在 inequalities 吗？**

当然存在！住在 Moscow 与住在 Magnitogorsk 并不一样，与 Party 关系密切和身为 kolkhoz farmer 也不一样。以后的一篇文章将讨论 Soviet inequality 这个完整的主题。

**我们不应该相信 official Soviet data 吗？**

CIA 先采用 official data 并对其进行 correcting，以此开始他们的 analysis。然后 Birman 再 corrects CIA data。Soviets 自己承认，部分 data 是 fabricated 的，但你可以认为 post-glasnost data 是 relatively accurate 的。据我所知，没有人因为 Birman 或 CIA 的 estimates 太 low 而批评它们。而且，如果我们依据 official data，使用 official coefficients，得到的就不是 FAO numbers，而是 Goskomstat 的那些 numbers；因此，与 CIA 的相比，final results 对 USSR 来说会显得更好，但还没有_好到足以\_得到 FAO 的结果_。\_

**要更好地了解这件事，我应该读你的哪些 references？**

Igor Birman 的书，以及他在开头就 references 的 CIA report。如果你发现任何与这里呈现的整体图景相矛盾的内容，请告诉我。

**这一切有多 reliable？Soviets 实际吃了多少 potatoes？**

不太 reliable，而且我们确实不知道！凭借 publicly available sources，我们能做到的最好程度就是这样。这就是不得不处理可能 fabricated 的 data、不得不 compare 不同 methodologies 等等所带来的问题。但更 reliable 的是这样一个总体判断：如果你出生在 West，你吃得会比在 Soviet Union _好得多_。

## 原文

\[Part of the [Soviet Union series](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/05/14/the-soviet-union-series/)\]

In this post, I cover food and nutrition in the Soviet Union. What were the Soviets eating?

... It's complicated

### (I) The Paradox

I begin with one chart:

[![compar](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/05/11-1.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/05/11/the-soviet-union-food/compar-2/#main)

(This post deals with the post-1960 period)

So the Soviets were _seemingly_ eating more calories than the Americans right almost until the end of the Soviet Union. Given that some people thought of the Soviet Union as a place stricken with famines and widespread queuing, this may look surprising. (Actually, the [last famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droughts_and_famines_in_Russia_and_the_Soviet_Union#Post-1900_droughts_and_famines) in the SU happeend in 1947)

Here there are other countries, including Finland (so high Soviet intake was not due (just) to a colder environment) and Japan (Because you can have lower caloric intake and do fine too!)

[![12439436\_10154042774894641\_2950067066892173612\_n](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/05/11-2.jpg)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/05/11/the-soviet-union-food/12439436_10154042774894641_2950067066892173612_n/#main)

But is the [FAO data](http://faostat3.fao.org/mes/methodology_list/E) right? I did two things to try to see if it is. First, I checked the [methodology](ftp://ftp.fao.org/docrep/fao/011/x9892e/x9892e00.pdf) they [were using](http://www.fao.org/economic/the-statistics-division-ess/methodology/methodology-systems/food-balance-sheets-and-the-food-consumption-survey-a-comparison-of-methodologies-and-results/en/). Second, I compared with other data.

The FAO data comes from the Food Balances, and represents the food supply: production plus imports minus exports, plus stocks from the previous year. This contrasts with household surveys, in which people are asked how much are they actually eating. In turn, the data comes from official statistics.

In my second chart, I gathered many different sources.

[![food](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/05/11-3.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/05/11/the-soviet-union-food/food-2/#main)

'My name is Ozymandias, chart of charts: Look on my works, ye Mighty, and despair!'

This is certainly despairworthy. There is a huge spread in the data. Below I explain where each data series comes from. FAO data comes from, well, FAO's food balances.

-   CIA data comes from a report from 1984, released in 1999. [The Nutrient Content of the Soviet Food Supply](http://www.foia.cia.gov/sites/default/files/document_conversions/89801/DOC_0000498133.pdf).  Like FAO's, these figures are for food supply, not ingestion, which is lower because of losses. The report says that the nutrient content of Soviet food supply matches the US's. Carbohydrate intake is higher, and fat intake is lower, but gaps were narrowing. The CIA analysts also estimate that losses from production to consumption are higher than in the US. They also indicate that the high caloric consumption are problematic from a public health perspective. Food availability differed between regions in the SU. For example, fruit consumption per capita in the Russian SFSR was 40 kg per person, while the Siberian region just consumed 12 kg. Meat consumption in Estonia was 80 kg, but just 31 kg in Uzbekistan. (Data for 1975-1981)
-   EXPORT data comes from a report written in 1986, [Soviet Foreign Trade in Foodstuffs: A measure](https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=DO5aSpp_YyIC&pg=PA24&lpg=PA24&dq=ussr%20%20exports%20food%203000%20calories%20soviet&source=bl&ots=QeA88v49bu&sig=ppe7M3c_8417Y1uekEN8VRKw0us&hl=es&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwie-dr39vrLAhVM1xQKHUoaB70Q6AEIKjAC#v=onepage&q=united%20states&f=false), by Vladimir G. Treml, written for the US Bureau of the Census. Among other things, he notes the enormous increase in food imports (especially grain) that happened between 1970 and 1981. According to him, the FAO figures published then (3400 cal per day for 1975-77) overstated actual intake mainly due to wrongly calculating the nutritional composition of Soviet foodstuff (Compared to official Soviet data). CIA data he references is higher than official Soviet data, but lower than FAO's. He discusses some funny incidents, like Novosti, the Soviet news agency, reported daily caloric consumption in the SU by giving the CIA figure from 1980. Treml rejects FAO data as too high, and gives a range of estimates for 1970 (2900-3100) and 1981 (3000-3300). He also says that both Soviets and Western analysts are probably overestimating Soviet nutrient quality.
-   BIRMAN comes from Igor Birman's book Personal consumption in the USSR and the USA (1981). I'll explain later why Birman deserves a place here. He says caloric consumption in the SU is a little less than in the US, even though it should be higher due to a younger population that performs more extenous work in a colder environment. Birman gives a figure of 3330 for 1976 (vs 3380 in the US). His data comes from _Narodnoye khozyaystvo SSR v 19-9odu: statisticheskiy yezhegodnik_(a yearbook published by the Soviet Union.)
-   AvailRus comes from [Nutritional Risk Factors in the Former Soviet Union](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK233395/pdf/Bookshelf_NBK233395.pdf#page=327), a chapter in a book by the US's National Research Council. They reference data from a consumer survey made by Goskomstat, the official statistics agency in the SU that (according to the authors) had the problem of not using a representative sample (Not that it was small, they covered 49.000 people).
-   The Goskomstat item is food balance data from official Soviet sources, also from the previous sources.
-   Russia (official) refers to official statistics of food consumption in kg coming from [this book](https://books.google.co.uk/books?id=JSwstF6SEAkC&pg=PA509&lpg=PA509&dq=soviet+food+losses&source=bl&ots=V9IuVXFeeX&sig=RCXMqH4ojSKFbJEhKCfTpKO-Sv4&hl=es&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwjVmpvP0fzLAhUBaxQKHbcIC-wQ6AEISDAG#v=onepage&q=calories&f=false) that references [Torgovlya SSSR](http://istmat.info/files/uploads/17076/vneshnie_ekonomicheskie_svyazi_sssr_v_1989_g.pdf) (another statistics yearbook), and converted to calories used coefficients developed by the US Dept. of Agriculture
-   USDA adjusted and unadjusted is data from the US department of agriculture. Unadjusted can be read as 'food supply', and adjusted is accounting for food losses, trying to reflect how much people are actually eating. See figure 2.1 [here](http://www.usda.gov/factbook/chapter2.pdf). You can get the series from USDA's website.
-   ALLEN is data from Robert Allen's From Farm to Factory. He takes raw data from FAO and calculates his own data series, explicitly accounting for food losses. The end result is identical to official FAO data. He takes other researchers (Wheatcroft, Jasny)into consideration, so his estimate is meant to be an all-things-considered one.

The problem with this is that sources who are trying to do the same (USDA and FAO) get different results. FAO's series looks like USDA unadjusted series. But FAO's series also look like Allen's, and Allen's are supposed to take losses into account. So someone is making a mistake somewhere. We could perhaps believe the _official_ Soviet data rather than FAO's. But [according to FAO](http://www.fao.org/docrep/007/y5069e/y5069e05.htm), the Goskomstat surveys have two problems: one is that it oversamples lower income households, and that the coefficients used to convert food kg into calories are 15-20% lower than FAO's. They conclude that "with such large differences, it is difficult to say much about the level of caloric consumption with any confidence". And even if we took the official consumption data, that supposedly would be accounting for losses, it is still far from the similar figure for US intake from USDA, so differences in methodology must be present.

My conclusion is then that, based on the data above, **Soviet caloric intake was high enough** to say Soviet citizens were reasonably well fed, _in terms of calories_.

[Here](http://www.fao.org/docrep/007/y5069e/y5069e05.htm#bm05) is how the picture changes when using different coefficients to convert kg of food into calories:

![](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/05/11-4.jpg)

The lines trending down are official Goskomstat data, consistently lower than FAO's estimates.

### (III) Estimates of Soviet nutrition

The first one is James R. Millar's _Politics, work, and daily life in the USSR (1987)_, built from the Soviet Interview Project's interviews (N=922). Asking lots of people what they were actually experiencing is a good alternative to official statistics. What did they find?

Citizens reported daily queuing, inferior quality goods, and lack of supply sometimes. Most respondents said that there were deficits in meat supply. However, almost everyone had meat (and cheese, kefir, milk, and eggs) several times per week, and the majority had them daily. One explanation of why there was queuing and shortages while at the same time they were eating meat almost daily is that there existed a legal private market (_rynok_)

> The prevailing system of food distribution is clearly a major source of dissatisfaction for essentially all income classes, even the best off and even the most privileged of these.

[![Captura de pantalla de 2016-05-11 18-06-31](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/05/11-8.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/05/11/the-soviet-union-food/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-05-11-18-06-31/#main)

This doesn't tell us anything about the quality of these goods: Schroeder, the CIA analyst reported that Soviet meat, or lettuces weren't as good as Western's.

An additional problem of this survey is that

### (III) A synthesis

So what should we think, then?

**Were there queues everywhere in the Soviet  Union?**

Yes. There wasn't rationing, and people were able to go to supermarkets and buy what they wanted. But often, what they wanted wasn't available. The legal private market helped people obtain some of the missing goods.

**Were Soviets hungry? Were there famines?**

Not after 1947. Holodomor happened in 1932-33

**Was Soviet caloric intake sufficiently high?**

Yes

**Was Soviet caloric intake higher than the US'?**

No. In saying this, I'm saying the FAO is wrong, and that Robert Allen, who based his calculations in FAO data (and used their multipliers), didn't notice. To say this, I had to go through a full literature review, and I come to this opinion. Before reading my post, you were totally justified in believing that caloric intake was higher. Not anymore. Unless some FAO official tells us why did they used their coefficients, that seem to go against the Sovietological literature.

**Was Soviet food quality worse than the American's?**

In general, yes.

**How does food consumption in the USSR compare to that of the US?**

See Birman's Table 7.1 above. This estimate is adjusted for quality and quantity. Food consumption was lower than in the US in quality, and in many cases, in quantity. Overall, it was lower than in the US, except for alcoholic beverages.

**How are you sure the FAO is wrong?**

Their figure for calories comes from using wrong coefficients. Analysts referenced above pointed this out. FAO is aware of this, but they keep their coefficients without exactly saying why the official Soviet coefficients are wrong. Anyway, [they themselves doubt](http://www.fao.org/docrep/007/y5069e/y5069e05.htm) you can get reliable estimates for Soviet data,

> However, with such large differences, it is difficult to say much about the level of caloric consumption with any confidence.

**How do you compare nutrition in two countries with different currencies, economic systems, preferences, climates, etc...?**

Carefully. Igor Birman explains his methodology in chapter 2 of his book

**Were there inequalities in food consumption?**

Surely there were! Living in Moscow is not the same as living in Magnitogorsk, or being close to the Party is not the same as being a kolkhoz farmer. A future post will deal with the whole topic of Soviet inequality.

**Shouldn't we trust the official Soviet data?**

The CIA begins their analysis by taking the official data and correcting it. Birman then corrects CIA data. The Soviets themselves admitted that some of the data was fabricated, but you could take post-glasnost data as relatively accurate. As far as I know, no one has criticised Birman or the CIA's estimates for being too low. And if we go by official data, using the official coefficients, we don't get the FAO numbers, but the Goskomstat ones, so the final results will look better for the USSR if we compare them to the CIA's, but not _good enough\_to get FAO's_.\_

**Which of your references should I read to get a better idea?**

Igor Birman's book, and the CIA report he references right at the beginning. If you find anything contradicting the broad picture presented here, please let me know.

**How reliable is all of this? How much potatoes were Soviets actually eating?**

Not much, and we don't really know! This is the best we can do with publicly available sources. That's the problem of having to deal with potentially fabricated data, having to compare different methodologies, and so on. But what is more reliable is the broad idea that if you were born in the West, you would be eating _far_ better than in the Soviet Union.
