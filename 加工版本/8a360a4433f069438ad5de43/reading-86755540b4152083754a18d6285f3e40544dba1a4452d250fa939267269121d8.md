# Nintil - Darwin-level insights

## 正文

Laura Deming 最近就这篇文章的话题发了推文，我回复道：

> 我会说，没有人。  
> 但我也会说，science 的 progress 往往是从 high level 和 vague 走向 low level 和 concrete（以及 useful）。Evolution 并不 cure disease，但过去 20 年 bio 中的 discoveries 确实会。
> 
> — José Luis Ricón Fernández de la Puente (@ArtirKel) [2020 年 8 月 13 日](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1293996922550857735?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw)

为了进一步展开我的推文，我想在这里思考一下，某个东西要成为一个“Darwin-level insight”，意味着什么。

我最初的想法是，这样一个 insight，除了是 correct 的，还应该：

-   能够 applicable 于一个 large domain（Darwin 的 insight applies 于所有 living matter）
-   Overturn 一个广为接受的 prior conception，就像一次 [paradigm shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm_shift) 那样（Creationism，或 Lamarckism）

从这个角度看，其他类似的 insights 可能包括 heliocentrism（geocentrism）、Newtonian mechanics（Aristotelian physics）、modern chemistry（phlogiston theory、alchemy）、QM&GR（classical mechanics）、general computation（即 complex thought 不能被 reduced to simple mechanical operations 这一 idea）。

这些都发生在一段时间以前。近来有没有这样的“Darwin-level insights”？如果没有，那可能是因为我们也许已经习惯了自己的 scientific conceptions 被 changed，因此，那种会让我们将某个东西 classify 为 revolutionary 的震撼可能减弱了：revolutions 正在发生，但没有人为它们感到那么兴奋。也可能是因为我们已经足够充分地弄清了 Nature 的 nature，穷尽了这类可能的 insights：transition 到一个完全不同的 theory，与 tweak 一个已有的 theory，是不一样的。

[Physics](https://nintil.com/new-physics/) 几十年来一直都在完善我们已经拥有的图景，因此我们不会在那里找到近期的 worldview changes。

Biology 会是一个更好的候选，因为它是一门 complex 得多的 subject，不像 physics 那样适合 first principles thinking 和 simple clean models，但如果看看近几十年 biology 的任何一份成就清单，可能会看到 CRISPR 或 cancer immunotherapy，或 mapping the human genome，或 single cell sequencing。这些都是 useful discoveries，但它们似乎并不是 Darwin-level 的。这里最接近的可能是 immunotherapy 的 fundamental underpinnings：immune 实际上能够 fight cancer 这一 idea（而以前人们认为它不能，因为那是我们自己的 cells），确实 overturn 了相反的 idea，尽管它所 overturns 的是关于 immune system 如何 works 的 knowledge，而 immune system 只是那些具有 immune systems、具体而言是 adaptive immune systems 的 organisms 的一个 subsystem。

再往更早的过去看，另一个候选显然是对 DNA 的 structure 和 function 的 discovery。还要注意，虽然我们会倾向于同意这一点，但对于一个类似、同样 highly functionally relevant 的 discovery，我们可能就不那么倾向于同意了：那就是 epigenetics 的 discovery。这或许是因为我们认为它与 DNA 的情况足够相似；在这里打个比方，这就像去 Mars，既然我们已经去过 Moon，那么这项成就就没有那么引人注目了。

## 原文

Laura Deming recently tweeted on the matter of this post, and I replied:

> Would say that no one.  
> But would also say that progress of science tends to be from the high level and vague to the low level and concrete (and useful). Evolution doesn't cure disease, but the lats 20 years of discoveries in bio do.
> 
> — José Luis Ricón Fernández de la Puente (@ArtirKel) [August 13, 2020](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1293996922550857735?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw)

To expand on my tweet, I wanted to think here about what would it mean for something to be a "Darwin-level insight".

My first thought was that such an insight, besides being correct, would:

-   Be applicable to a large domain (Darwin's applies to all living matter)
-   Overturn a widely held prior conception, as in a [paradigm shift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradigm_shift) (Creationism, or Lamarckism)

In that regard, other similar insights might be heliocentrism (geocentrism), Newtonian mechanics (Aristotelian physics), modern chemistry (phlogiston theory, alchemy), QM&GR (classical mechanics), general computation (the idea that complex thought cannot be reduced to simple mechanical operations).

All of these happened a while ago. Are there any recent such "Darwin-level insights" in recent times? If there are not, that may be because maybe we have gotten used to have our scientific conceptions changed, so the shock that would cause us to classify something as revolutionary might be lessened: the revolutions are happening but no one is getting all that excited about them. Or it could also be that we have figured out sufficiently well the nature of Nature, exhausting the repertoire of possibe such insights: it is different to transition to a completely different theory than to tweak an existing one.

[Physics](https://nintil.com/new-physics/) has been all about completing the picture we already had for decades, so we won't find any recent worldview changes there.

Biology would be a better candidate, as it is a far more complex subject, not as amenable to first principles thinking and simple clean models as physics is, but if one looks at any list of achievements in biology in recent decades one might find CRISPR or cancer immunotherapy, or mapping the human genome, or single cell sequencing. These are all useful discoveries, but they do not seem Darwin-level. The closest here might be the fundamental underpinnings of immunotherapy: the idea that the immune can actually fight cancer (Whereas it was previously thought that it couldn't, because it's our own cells) does overturn the opposite idea, although what it overturns is knowledge of how the immune system works, which is a subsystem of only those organisms that have immune systems, and specifically adaptive immune systems.

Going further to the past, another contender is clearly the discovery of the structure and function of DNA. Note also that while we would be inclined to agree with this, we may be less inclined to agree with a similar and also highly functionally relevant discovery: that of epigenetics, perhaps because we see it as similar enough to the case of DNA, to make a simile here it's like going to Mars, given that we already went to the Moon, that achievement is not as striking.
