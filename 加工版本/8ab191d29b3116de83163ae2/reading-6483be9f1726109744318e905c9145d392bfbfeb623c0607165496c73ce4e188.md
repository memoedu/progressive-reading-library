## 结论（Conclusion）

USSR 在 Stalin 时期确实 grow 得相当 fast。那么，问题就是要解释：它为何 grow 得如此 fast，为何会 stagnate，以及为何 Cheremukhin、Golosov 等人 find 这并不是因为 Stalin，而 Allen 却 finds 相反的结果（我会再写一篇文章解释这一点）。然而，如果我们考察从 Stalin 时期开始、到 1989 年结束的 Soviet Union，其 performance 就没有那么 good 了。此外，如果我们将 Soviet Union 的 post-WWII performance 与其他 countries 相比，它就表现出了 stagnation 的迹象。因此，我们可以说，Soviet Union 那令人瞩目的 growth 是一次 one-off event，由 Stalin 的 industrial policies 所 produced。然而，USSR 未能 sustain 这一 growth rate。

此外，我们还必须 examine 其他实行 central planning 的 countries，看看 Soviet model 是否得到了 replicated，或者 Soviet economy 的 performance 是否也源于 Russia 存在、但其他地方不存在的 specific factors。还要注意，Soviet Union 将其 economy 中相当大的 share（15-17%）用于 military spending（相比之下，US 在 80 年代为 5%），以及 investment（而非 consumption），因此 GDP growth 并不会在每个 country 都同等地 translate into population welfare。这可以被理解为：如果 USSR 在 military 上 spent 得更少，它本可以做得更好。或者，反过来说，如果让一个以 produce T-72s 和 MiG-29s 为导向的 economy 实现 grow，比让一个以 consumer goods 为导向的 economy 实现 grow 更容易，那么它的 GDP 恰恰就是因此才表现得那么 good。正如你可以在[我的其他文章](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/05/14/the-soviet-union-series/)中看到的，healthcare、food consumption 和 working conditions 并没有这个 GDP comparison 可能暗示的那么 good。

**(Edit4)**

The analysis above may need some further reconsideration. The charts presented until now are controlling just for initial GDP. But Easterly & Fischer (1995) run a regression for the 1960-1989 period, controlling for variables such as initial GDP, share of investment, and secondary school enrollment. GDP growth for the Soviet Union in that period was 2.36% vs 2.00% for the West. But when controlling for those variables, the predicted growth for the Soviet Union jumps to 4.7%. Meaning that the economic system itself reduced growth in -2.34 points. (The value for the West does not change). In the 1950s, however, Easterly & Fischer do find that the Soviet system performed well in terms of growth. They ultimately explain this by what the dub the \_extensive growth hypothesis: \_Growth fueled by increasing the amount of available factors (Labour and Capital) rather than increasing productivity. That, combined with a low elasticity of capital-labour substitution (Using a CES production function, they get 0.4) meant that diminishing returns to capital would ultimately stall growth when the capital share of the economy began to reduce as the economy shifted to consumer goods.

## Conclusion

The USSR did grow quite fast under Stalin. The question would then be to explain why did it grow so fast, why did it stagnate, and why Cheremukhin, Golosov et al. find that it wasn't because of Stalin, while Allen finds the opposite (I'll write a followup post explaining that). However, if we consider the Soviet Union starting with Stalin and ending in 1989, its performance was not as good. Furthermore, if we consider the post-WWII performance of the Soviet Union in comparison to other countries, it shows signs of stagnation. We can then say that the impressive growth of the Soviet Union was a one-off event, produced by Stalin's industrial policies. The USSR, however, wasn't able to sustain this growth rate.

Furthermore we would also have to examine other countries with central planning to see if the Soviet model was replicated, or if the performance of the Soviet economy was also due to specific factors present in Russia but not elsewhere. Note also that the Soviet Union spent a sizable share of its economy (15-17%) on military spending (vs 5% in the US in the 80s) and on investment (rather than consumption), so GDP growth does not translate into population welfare equally in every country. This could be read to mean that the USSR would have done even better had it spent less on the military. Or, conversely, that its GDP did that good because of it, if it's easier to grow an economy oriented to produce T-72s and MiG-29s than consumer goods. As you can see in [my other posts](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/05/14/the-soviet-union-series/), healthcare, food consumption, and working conditions were not as good as this GDP comparison might suggest.
