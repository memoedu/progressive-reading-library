## 正文

> 只要对 man 的 definition 做足 manipulation，就能让 freedom 意味着 manipulator 想让它意味的任何东西（Matt Zwolinski）

![ea6c1736db7a167d4f13e3cefbe6ee28d1abf1d4d731112c1c52ff670ae4d5e9](https://nintil.com/rise-and-fall-of-rawlsianism/06-1.jpg?w=293)

John Rawls (1921 - 2002) 是 modern political philosophy 的 History 中最重要的 philosophers 之一。Robert Nozick 被普遍认为是 Rawls 的 intellectual rival，他甚至写道：“political philosophers 必须在 Rawls 的 conceptual framework 内开展工作，或者解释他们为什么不这样做。”Rawls 还终结了 utilitarianism 作为 academia 中最流行的 moral framework 的统治，将 deontology 重新引入 intellectual discourse——而且这还是直接 draws from Kant 的 deontology。

在这篇文章中，我将提出 Rawls 的 philosophical project 所存在的几个问题。这些问题足够严重，足以让 Rawls（或者至少是早期的 Rawls）置身于 liberalism 本身之外。

前些时候，我用西班牙语写了四篇关于 Rawls 的文章，你可以在文章栏目中查看。我在这里收集了其中最好的内容，补充了一些其他内容，并将它们翻译了出来。

本文的安排如下：首先，我将介绍 street Rawlsianism，这是 Rawls 的 Theory of Justice 的 simplified version，让大家了解这里究竟在讨论什么。然后，我将解释它是如何形成的，其中还会补充 Rawls 为其 theory 提供的 justification 的一些细节。这段经历本身，也就是 Rawls 自己的 philosophical views 的 development，就已经是对他最初的 theory 足够有力的 criticism，不过我还会在最后加上我所知道的、在我看来最有力的 critiques。

#### 通俗版 Rawlsianism（Street Rawlsianism）

简而言之，Rawls 的想法是这样的：一个 society 应该 abide by 什么 principles of justice？思考这个问题时，人应该努力避免 biased。我们都承认，ethics 不应该对我们 partial；一种宣称“每个人都应该是 X 的 servant”的 ethics，其中 X 是某个给定的 individual，在 morally 看来是 suspicious 的。“the weak 必须 obey the strong”等说法也是如此。为了避免这种 bias，Rawls 提议我们使用一种 philosophical tool：veil of ignorance 下的 original position。这意味着，想象自己处于这样一种情境：我们已经失去了关于自己将处于 society 中什么位置的 knowledge。我们不知道自己的家庭、朋友、工作、wealth、income 等情况。我们只知道所有相关的 scientific knowledge。Rawls 还告诉我们，在这些 conditions 下，要尝试与其他 equally situated 的人 negotiate，以 decide principles of justice。在 veil 后面，我们应该 relatively self-interested，但也要 reasonable，而且不 envious。必要时，我们应该愿意 compromise。在 deliberation 结束后，你可以想象自己将获得属于自己的人生，而你必须 abide by 自己决定的 rules。正如他所解释的，

> _这里的想法，只是让我们自己清楚地看到那些似乎可以 reasonable 地 impose 于支持 principles of justice 的 arguments、从而也 impose 于这些 principles 本身的 restrictions。因此，在 choice of principles 时，没有人应该因为 natural fortune 或 social circumstances 而 advantaged 或 disadvantaged，这似乎是 reasonable 且 generally acceptable 的。人们似乎也普遍同意，不应该有可能根据自身情况来 tailor principles。我们还应该确保，特定的 inclinations 和 aspirations，以及 persons 对其 good 的 conceptions，不会 affect 所 adopted 的 principles。……可以说，我们随时都能 enter original position，只需遵循某种 procedure，也就是依照这些 restrictions，为 principles of justice 提出 arguments。_
> 
> _人们自然会问：如果从来没有人实际达成过这一 agreement，我们为什么还应该关心这些 principles？……答案是，original position 的 description 中所体现的 conditions，正是我们事实上 accept 的那些 conditions。或者，如果我们不 accept，那么也许可以通过 philosophical reflection 说服我们去 accept。_（A Theory of Justice）

接下来这一步就是说：你肯定不想最终陷入 poor、一无所有，并且余生都过得悲惨的境地。因为每个人都会这么想，所以每个人都会同意做点什么，帮助 society 中的 poorest。Rawls 主张，在这些 conditions 下，我们会 choose maximin principle of justice：choose 那种能够 maximises poorest 的 holdings 的 morally arbitrary goods 的 distribution。这里的想法是，大体而言，一个人并不 deserve 自己的 income 或 assets，因为它们来自 genetics、成长环境或运气。它们是 unmerited，因此可以在没有任何 moral 问题的情况下被 rearranged。

这就是 Rawls 的 second principle of justice，即 difference principle。第一个是 liberty principle，它 ranks lexically above second principle。

> **First Principle**：每个 person 都拥有相同的 indefeasible claim，要求获得一个 fully adequate 的 equal basic liberties 的 scheme，而这一 scheme 与所有人拥有相同的 liberties 的 scheme 是 compatible 的；**Second Principle**：Social and economic inequalities 必须 satisfy 两个 conditions：
> 
> 1.  它们必须 attached 于在 _fair equality of opportunity_ 的 conditions 下向所有人 open 的 offices 和 positions；
> 2.  它们必须为 society 中 least-advantaged members 带来 greatest benefit（即 _difference principle_）。（_JF_, 42–43）

也就是说，存在一组不能被 equality 方面的考虑 overturned 的 basic liberties。其中包括 political liberty（right to vote、hold office 等）、freedom of speech、freedom of conscience and thought、freedom from physical and psychological harm、freedom of posession of personal property，以及 freedom from arbitraty detention（habeas corpus）。expansive 意义上的 Private property 是 forbidden 的，这里不包括 means of production。即使 violating 这些 rights 会让 poor 变得 better off，也不能 curtailed 这些 rights。正是这个（看似）strong 的 principle of liberty，让 Rawls 稳稳地处于 liberal 阵营之中。

总而言之，Rawls 走了一步巧棋。他是在对你说：你是否同意，在 deciding principles of justice 时，你应该 unbiased、rational 且 reasonable？在这些 conditions 下，你就会同意 Rawlsianism。如果你不 accept Rawlsianism，那么你要么是 evil 的——\_按照你自己的 moral values 来看， \_要么你 reject 一种不作 arbitrary discriminations 的 universal ethics 的想法；又或者，如果你想 rationally believe 自己不是一个坏人，并将 universalism 保留为自身 ethics 的一项 principle，那么你就必须指出 Rawls 的 argument 有什么问题。

重要的是，Rawls 将自己的 Theory of Justice 视为一种 ideal theory。也就是说，它讨论的是人们会为 ideal world 所 chosen 的 principles of justice。这引来了许多 criticisms，例如 [Brennan (2007)](https://philpapers.org/rec/BRERP) 就认为，Rawls 对 explicitly maximising poorest 的 economic position 的坚持，是 economically self-effacing 的。下文中，我只考虑 ideal case，因此我们 assume away 战争、crime，以及一般意义上的坏事。我们只处理 rational、reasonable 的 individuals 的关切，而他们有时可能会有 disagreements。

我们的 exposition 到此结束。现在，让我们把历史倒回几十年，看看我们是如何走到这一步的。

### 伟大的 Rawlsian project 的瓦解

这是对 Vallier (2013)、Gaus (2014) 和 Gaus & Thrasher (2016) 的简要概述，其中很好地概述了 Rawls 如何走到后来那一步，我在这里将它进一步缩短：

Rawlsianism 可以追溯到 1951，随着 \_Outline of a Decision Procedure for Ethics. \_的发表而出现。与他之前和之后的许多人一样，Rawls 提出了这个问题：

> 是否存在一种 reasonable decision procedure，它足够 strong … 能够 determine 应当以何种方式 adjudicated competing interests，以及在 conflict 的情况下，给予某个 interest 相对于另一个的 preferences；进一步说，能否通过 rational methods of inquiry 来 established 这个 procedure 的 existence 及其 reasonableness？

于是，这个问题，即 justifying 应当 favour 哪个 interest，被 converted 到 rational choice 的 framework 中：应当被 favoured 的 interest，就是在 veil of ignorance 后通过 agreement 达成的 principles 所 designated 的那些。由于 Rawls 施加的 constraints，principles of justice 不能 based on 单纯的 balance of power。Rawls 是 Kantian contractualist，而不是 Hobessian contractarian。目标不是如何 devise principles 来 avoid 社会规模的 violence，而是如何 instantiate Justice。

然而，在 veil of ignorance 后，你甚至不知道自己的 conception of the good。或者用如今的说法，你不知道自己的 utility function。你应当带上什么来 maximise 呢？Rawls 的解决办法是说，每个人都会 share 一种 thin theory of the good。每个人都会拥有 "primary goods"，而且他们会想要 maximise 这些。这些东西是

> rational individuals 所欲求的东西，“无论他们还想要什么，他们都会 desire 这些东西，作为 carrying out 其 plans of life 的 prerequisites” (TJ, 348)。这些是 liberties、opportunity、wealth、income 和 social bases of self-respect (TJ, 54) (Gauss, 2014)

（social bases of self-respect 的意思是“social institutions 给予的 recognition，它赋予 citizens 一种 sense of self-worth，以及 carry out 其 plans 的 confidence”）

接下来出现的一个问题是，一旦这些 principles 确立并开始 ordering 一个 society，它们就必须是 stable。它们不能让 society 无法 comply with 它们。这就是为什么支持 utilitarianism 的 Ken Binmore 最终站到了一个更温和的 Rawlsian version 一边。他说，我们还没有好到足以实行 utilitarianism，这呼应了 G.A. Cohen 对 communism 也说过的话。\[1\]。

在 Rawls 的 framework 中，人们有 conceptions of the good，大致包括他们长远而言想怎样度过自己的一生、他们的 personal moral codes 等等。他起初 assumed，respecting Rawlsian idea of justice 会是每个人的 goods 的一部分：一种 intrinsically motivating 的东西。他 assumed，如果人们生活在这样的 society 中，就自然会 develop 出 comply with 这些 principles 的 dispositions 和 inclinations。

> 这使 well ordered society 面临 Rawls 所谓的“generalized prisoner’s dilemma 的 hazards”——每个人都看得到 acting on 这些 principles 的 collective rationality，但当她的 self-interested point of view 建议她这么做时，她就会受到诱惑，在自己的情形中 defect (TJ, 505, 435, 296; Weithman, 2010, 48)。Weithman 认为，为了克服这个 hazard，Rawls 试图在 Theory 中 show，在 well-ordered society 中，justice as fairness constitutes 一个 Nash equilibrium：“W\[ell\] O\[ordered\] S\[ociety\] 的每个 member 都从 thin theory of the good 内部 judges，当其他人的 plans 也 similarly regulated 时，她的 balance of reasons 倾向于支持在自己的 rational plans 中 maintaining 她从 principles of justice 出发 act 的 desire，并将其作为 highest-order regulative desire” (Weithman, 2010, 64，强调为原文所有)。那么，Acting justly 就会是对其他人 acting justly 的 best reply。
> 
> 因此，Rawls 的 stability argument 还有第二个阶段：在已经 shown，在由 justice as fairness regulated 的 well-ordered society 中，sense of justice 往往会产生之后，还必须 shown，这个 society 中的 conceptions of the rational good 会使人们通常不会 alienated from 自己的 sense of justice。**对于一个 based on justice as fairness 的 well-ordered society 的 typical member 而言，best life 会包括：当其他人也这样做时，devotion to 并 acting upon principles of justice (TJ, 382-3)**。如果一个 society (1) 不 encourage strong sense of justice，或者 (2) encourages 会诱使人们偏离其 sense of justice 的 conceptions of the good，那么从 original position 来看，这个 society 就无法通过 stability test (TJ, 398)
> 
> (Gauss, 2014)

但多年后，他 rejected 了这个 assumption。他 rejected 这个 assumption，不仅因为他允许人们不把 justice as fairness incorporate 到自己的 goods 中，还因为他逐渐意识到，他的 framework 并不会 uniquely generates 他的 principles of justice。

第一个问题的一个例子是，假如有人从自己的 self-interested point of view 出发 reasons，会发生什么：这会 undermine 他们 follow 这些 principles 的 willingness，使他们将 justice as fairness 视为一种 onerous imposition，并对此心生怨恨。

因此，首先，他接下来的书 Political Liberalism and Public Reason 处理了 moral ideas 中 reasonable pluralism 的问题。他试图 show，可以从许多不同的 perspectives 来 defended justice as fairness，但最终没有成功。这样，即便人们对于 justice 为何如此存在分歧，他们仍然可以就 Rawlsian framework 中宽泛而统摄全局的 principles 达成一致。因此，justice as fairness 变成了一种“political，而非 metaphysical”的 theory of justice。这意味着，一个人可以是 christian、marxist、socialist、libertarian、feminist、buddhist 等等，同时仍然 endorse justice as fairness，只是基于不同的 arguments。

> 除了相互 conflicting 的 comprehensive doctrines，PL 也确实承认，在任何实际的 political society 中，若干不同的 liberal political conceptions of justice 会在 society 的 political debate 中相互 compete…。这就引出了 PL 的另一个目标：说明在不仅存在 reasonable pluralism \[涉及 comprehensive conceptions\]，而且存在一组 reasonable liberal conceptions of justice 的情况下，应当如何 formulated 一个 well-ordered liberal society (PL, xlviii)。(Gauss, 2014)

其次，Rawls accepted，veil of ignorance 并不会把人引向他的 principles of justice。随后他 argued，人们仍然会 converge on 一系列 liberal positions，它们必须 a) pick 一组 basic liberties b) assign 它们一种特殊的 priority（甚至不必是 lexical）以及 c) provide 能够 enjoy 它们的 means。

这就是 Rawls 不认为 libertarianism 是 liberal、甚至不认为它是 reasonable doctrine 的原因，因为它不满足他的 condition c)。然而，许多主要 libertarian philosophers 所 espoused 的 sufficientarian libertarianism 会通过他的筛选。

你在哪里看到 difference principle 了吗？我没有。因为到最后，Rawls 不得不舍弃它：他意识到，并非每一个处于 veil of ignorance 后的 rational 且 reasonable agent 都会 choose 它。因此，Rawlsianism 转而成为 Public Reason Liberalism：处理我们应当如何就 political matters 彼此交谈，以及哪些 reasons 能够 valid 地 justify political actions。

这一瓦解过程在 Rawls 最晚的书 The Law of Peoples 中继续了下去。

在这里，Rawls 考虑了应当 regulate nations 之间 relations 的 rules。读过上面的讨论后，人们或许会以为，Rawls 可能会 endorse 类似 world-state 的东西，或者要求 developed countries 对最贫穷的国家承担十分严格的 redistributive duties。但并没有。如果你感到惊讶，那很正常。Rawls 的追随者 (Pogge, 2004) 也同样惊讶。Rawls 最后反而 endorsing 了 international libertarianism：countries 不应当相互 attack，应当 keep 其 treaties、respect human rights，并 respect laws of war。此外，还有一项 weak duty，即帮助遭受 famine 或其他 catastrophe 的较贫穷 countries。

为什么会这样？因为在 A Theory of Justice 的另一处，当 Rawls 解释为什么 the rich 会愿意 submit themselves 于一种以 maximise the welfare of the poor 为其目的的 social order 时，他说道

> _natural distribution 既不是 just，也不是 unjust；persons 出生在 society 的某个特定 position，也不是 unjust。这些不过是 natural facts。just 和 unjust 的，是 institutions 处理这些 facts 的方式。. . . social system 并不是一种超出 human control 的 unchangeable order，而是一种 pattern of human action。**在 justice as fairness 中，人们同意彼此分担命运。** (TJ, 102)_

有意思的是，正如 Loren Lomasky 所讨论的，Rawls 在这本书后来的 editions 中删掉了这一段。Lomasky 继续说道：

> 对此，恰当的回应是：正是如此！在 Rawlsian conception 中，uniting citizens 的 Bonds 显得格外 tight。**I\*\*\*\*t 所说的 agree to share one’s fate with another，绝非小事。这类 undertaking 会出现在 family 之内，出现在决心一起 pursue the good life 的朋友或恋人之间，出现在一项影响深远的事业的伙伴之间，出现在彼此掩护的一个排的士兵之间，出现在共同礼拜的教众之间，或出现在共同热爱某种 good 的 devotees 之间。在这些情境中，一个人的 success 在很大程度上 constitutes 所有人的 success；而一个不准备 sacrifice 自己那份 good 中的一部分、甚至不准备慷慨 sacrifice 的 individual，在这种 pursuit 中便是一个 anomalous partner。** $$…$$
> 
> 确实如此。Rawls 对 justice as fairness regime 下 social bonds 的 intensity 有了新的疑虑。**在 A Theory of Justice 的修订版中，那段关于 individuals sharing each other’s fate 的醒目文字消失了**。很可能，在两个版本之间，Rawls 开始觉得自己此前的措辞过于强烈，不够妥当。也许有些友善的批评者问过他，是否真想给 A Theory of Justice 的 theoretical structure 挂上 social solidarity 这样一辆沉重的车。无论修订的动因是什么，第二版的文字都变得平淡无害。Citizens 仍要 share in society’s assets，却不再 share 彼此的 fates。很难说，这种通过删略进行的修订是否令人满意。读者可能会怀疑，它只是 camouflages 而非 eliminates 这个 project 的一个 fundamental problem $$…$$
> 
> **尽管 arguments ad hominem 通常带有几分令人不快的意味，但在这里，这种意味或许有所减轻，因为 arguing against Rawls’s understanding 的正是 Rawls 自己**。这个 argument 始于 Political Liberalism (1993)：他在其中并未 abandon 对 difference principle 的支持，却 concedes，它只是 reasonably distributing social cooperation 的 benefits and burdens 的数种方式之一。但在 The Law of Peoples (1999) 中，Rawls 不顾自己弟子的敦促，16 否认 difference principle 是世界各 peoples 之间 interaction 的 suitable basis，17 从而与它进一步拉开距离。Rawls 在后一本书中给出的 reasoning 和 illustrative examples 有些晦涩，但其要旨是，并不存在从世界上 wealthier countries 向 less well-off countries 进行 economic redistribution 的 general requirement。只有对于陷入严重困境的 peoples，即 Rawls 所称的 “burdened societies”（其含义基本等同于更常见的说法 “failed states”），才存在 duty of positive assistance，而且也只到“一个 people 的 basic needs（以 primary goods 来 estimated）得到 fulfilled，且这个 people 能够自立的 point”为止 (LP, 119)。**这是因为，世界各 peoples 首先有 reason 去 value 自己的 autonomy 和 preferred national culture，而不是 subscription to an egalitarian cosmopolitanism**。除了为应对一时困境而作出的 contingent temporary provision 之外，wealthy 对 less well-off 所负的 duties 都是 noninterference 的 modes（例如 observance of treaties、nonintervention，以及 adherence to human rights and rules of war；见 LP, 37），而对方也以同样方式回报：**令人印象深刻的是，equal liberty principle 在 international level 上的 sweepingness，以及对 egalitarianism 的 rejection，因为它与每个 society 将精力投入自身 distinctive interests 的 overriding interest 是 inconsistent 的。**
> 
> (Lomasky, 2005)

基本上，Rawlsianism 对一个 family 来说似乎说得通。Families 至少会尽量确保没有 member 因 mistfortunes 而遭受严重痛苦。但不妨设想，你把 fellow citizens 看作朋友或熟人。一个朋友在 morally 的意义上，可以向另一个朋友要求多少 redistribution？Rawls 的 justice as fairness 似乎需要这样一个 requirement：我们所有人都把彼此看作甚至比朋友更亲近的人。而事实并非如此，将来也不会如此，而且我敢再加一句，也不应该如此。

Rawls 在讨论 justice 之后，最终 endorsing 的是什么 system？许多人的回答是：liberal democracy 和 welfare state。但并不是。对 Rawls 来说，这还不够。Welfare states 的 redistribute 不够，也不够 egalitarian。有些人听到 [Matt Bruenig](http://mattbruenig.com/2012/11/25/the-radical-rawls/) 关于把大多数或全部 productive enterprises 进行 socialising、纳入一个庞大的 sovereign wealth fund 的 views 时，会感到震惊。但他不是 marxist，只是一个 orthodox Rawlsian。

Rawls 至少起初 accepted 两种与其 theory of justice 是 compatible 的 systems：property owning democracy 和 liberal socialism。关于 POD 的一篇长篇 critical discussion 可以在[这里](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/11/property-owning-democracy/)找到。POD 基本上是：

> (1) 享有 equal public education 的 right。(2) 享有 minimum income 和/或用于 supporting oneself and one’s family、达到 minimal level of social acceptability 的 means 的 right。(3) 一套 campaign financing 的 public system，以及对 corporate political activity 的 explicit limitations。(4) individuals 享有 society’s productive capital 和/或 wealth 的一份 share 的 right。(5) 享有足够的 productive capital、以 sustain local level 上 viable democratic communities 的 collective right。

第 (4) 和 (5) 点，正是超出 welfare state 的部分。

简而言之，Liberal socialism 就是一个拥有 civil rights 的 Soviet Union。state 不必 centrally plan economy，但最终会 own 全部 productive assets。比如说。

Rawls 通过在 ideal theory 内 reasoning（没有人是 corrupt 的，也没有 crime，等等），并给予 economic freedoms 零 weight，得出了这个结论。人们不禁要问，他为什么 reject economic freedom 和 extensive private property？为什么 veil 后面的 agents 必须 choose political rights，却连一点点 own productive assets 的 right 都不 choose？当然，Rawls 最终不再接受他们不会 choose 那些 rights 的说法，所以到了他学术生涯的末期，他就不得不 accept 带有 minimal safety net 的 capitalism 也是可以的。他最初为 disregard economic freedoms 所给出的 reason，可以追溯到 J.S. Mill：economic activity 不是 liberty 的 expression，而且一个人在 pursue 自己的 conception of the good 时并不需要它，

> liberal tradition 中的一个 crucial junction point，出现在 Mill 对 economic liberty 的 treatment 上。(Freeman 2011) \*\*Mill 认为，对 economic liberties 的 treated 应当不同于 freedom of thought, association and religion。\*\***人们就 labor 和 ownership 所作的 Decisions，与 Mill 意义上的 liberty 没有 intrinsic connection。Economic activity 不是 liberty 的 expression，因此 ownership 和 labor 的 rights and powers 应当主要由 utility 的 requirements 来 defined。** (Mill 2004, 209-223; Jacobson 2000, 293-295)
> 
> **John Rawls 同样 adopts 一种 economic exceptionalism 的 platform。他将 economic liberties 单独置于较低的 level of protection。他声称，wide economic rights 并非“necessary for the development and exercise of the moral powers”。**对于 freedom of labor，Rawls 仅 recognizes right to occupational choice，却不 recognizes 从事 self-organized economic activity 和/或为 productive purposes 而 hire others 的 freedom。对于 ownership，Rawls 仅 recognizes 一项 basic right to personal property，将任何 protecting ownership of productive property（family farms、businesses，以及其他 “means of production”）的 rights 都 severing off。**对于 private productive property 和 contract 的 Rights 并非 basic，因此任何 recognizing 这些 rights 的 laws 都应当为 realize distributive justice 而 crafted。** (Brennan & Tomasi, 2011)

因此，Rawlsianism 为可以 chosen 什么 principles of justice 设置了 contraints，却最终没有 picking 究竟是哪些。所以在历史上，人们曾尝试从 rawlsian framework 内 derive 出许多东西，从 utilitarianism (Harsanyi, 1975) \[2\] 到 libertarianism (Tomasi, 2013)。

本文开头的引语——“只要对 man 的 definition 进行足够的 manipulation，freedom 就可以被赋予 manipulator 想要的任何含义”——道出了这些作者之间 discrepancy 的 reason。如果 man 只是承载 happiness 的容器，而 happiness 是唯一重要的东西，那么得出的就是 utilitarianism。如果你认为 persons 是 disembodied kantian souls，那么 Rawlsianism 便 follows。而如果你认为 persons 拥有在 morally 的意义上重要的 personal projects，并且 property 在 extended sense 上是 oneself 的一部分，那么就会得出更 classically liberal 的 positions。Rawls 明确说，他想要 respect 这个 fact：people 是 separate 的；但由于他对 kantian conception of the person 的 commitment，他最终得到的 framework 会 entails 一些出人意料的东西，我将在下一节展示。关于这个 topic 的更多讨论，见 Zwolinski (2008)，我在下面长篇引用其中相关的一段：

> Rawls endorses 这样的 claim：theory of justice 必须 respect separateness of persons，但他又以 holds 如下观点而闻名：justice permits，甚至 requires，从 rich 向 poor 进行 largescale transfers of wealth。什么能够 explains 这些 differing perspectives？
> 
> 其 explanation 并不在于 Rawls 和 Nozick 以 fundamentally different 的方式 understand respect for the separateness of persons 这一 basic moral requirement。他们两人似乎都 endorse 这样的 idea：respect for the separateness of persons prohibits 那些将他人 use 为 mere means 的 treating others 方式。相反，difference 在于他们对 person 是什么的 understanding，或者更准确地说，在于他们对 person 的 normatively significant aspects 是什么的 understanding。
> 
> 对 Nozick 而言，从 Alan 向 Betty coercively redistributing wealth，就是将 Alan using 为 mere means 的一种情形，因为 Alan 的 wealth 在某种重要意义上是 Alan 的一部分。最按字面理解这一 idea 的方式，是借助 property appropriation via labor mixing 的 account，这是 Nozick 从 John Locke 那里继承的。按照 Nozick 的说法，一个人通过将自己的 labor 与某物 mixing，便会 own 此前 unowned 的东西。由于一个人 owns 自己以及自己的 labor，因此他便会 own 那些与自己的 labor 相 mixes 的 external goods。External goods 之所以被 owned，是因为它们在某种意义上 contain 一个人的 self 的一部分。因此，deprive 一个人的 external goods，就是夺走其 self 的某个部分，或者将这个人的 self use 为实现他人 goal 的 means。
> 
> 但我们不必以如此按字面而又 simplistic 的方式 understand Nozick 的 position。一个人不必 endorse 那个 problematic 的 labor mixing 故事，也可以承认：一个人的 external goods 在某种意义上是这个人的一部分。要得出基本相同的 conclusion，一种争议小得多的方式，是注意 persons 与他们的 aims 之间的 strong connection，然后再注意 external goods 的 property 以哪些多样而 significant 的方式 is connected to 一个人的 aims。23 我们作为 persons 的 identity，我们如何看待自己以及他人如何看待我们，在很大程度上都是我们所 pursue 的 projects 的 function。我们的人生随着时间推移，逐渐 develop 为一组 embedded aims，其中一些非常 general 和 abstract，例如过上 virtuous 或 experience-rich 的生活这一 aim；另一些则更 focused，例如养育孩子或成为教授这一 aim。通过 identifying with 某些 projects 而不是其他 projects，我们赋予自己的人生以形状和意义。但如果没有 plan 以及依靠 external goods 的 use 的 ability，就无法在任何相当长的时期内 pursued aims。例如，一个人的家，随着时间推移，会成为其养育家庭这一 aim 的 essential part。一个人的 business 的 financial holdings，是经营 successful firm 这一 aim 的重要部分。因此，一个人的 external property 不仅部分地是其 aims、physical work 或 mental energies 的 product，也部分地是它们的 constitutive 要素。因此，为了他人的 benefit 而拿走一个人的 property，便是在非常切实的意义上将这个人 use 为 means。
> 
> 相比之下，对 Rawls 而言，将一个人 using 为 means 与将一个人的 property using 为 means 之间，存在重要的 difference。persons 与 persons 的 endowments 之间存在 difference，而当 persons 无法为其 endowments claim 任何 moral credit 时，这一 difference 尤其 significant。如果一个人的 wealth、intelligence、merit 等等都是 ‘‘arbitrary from a moral perspective,’’ 那么，一个让我们将这些 endowments 的 legitimacy 视为理所当然的 moral system，也同样会是 arbitrary 的
> 
> Nozicks 对这一 reasoning 的 response 是指出：它要求我们以一种异乎寻常地 abstract 而陌生的方式 conceive of persons，极力强调 persons 与那些 individuate 他们的 characteristics 之间的 distinction。25 在 Rawls 看来是 arbitrary 的东西，在 Nozick 看来却是 essential 的。更准确地说，在 Rawlss 对该词的意义上是 arbitrary 的东西，不一定在 Nozicks 关于 respect for persons 的 account 所关心的意义上也是 arbitrary 的。Natural talents 和 ownership of external goods，可能在 ultimately undeserved 的意义上是 arbitrary 的。但这与承认以下观点是 compatible 的，正如 Loren Lomasky 所说：‘‘contingencies 一旦与 persons 实际过着的人生 intimately attached，就会被赋予 moral weight……最初 absurd 的东西，一旦进入一个 person 的 projects，就会充满 moral significance。’’26 从 morality 的 impartial third-person point of view 看来似乎 arbitrary 的、关于一个人的 facts，从 first person perspective 看来，却与这个人的 identity 和 aims essentially bound up。因此，认真对待 separateness of persons，部分地就是在 clarifying persons 之间的 boundaries 时认真对待这一 perspective，让它帮助我们 understanding 什么 belong to 一个人，什么不 belong to 一个人。

关于 economic liberties 是否应当是 fundamental 的，有一场有趣的 debate。我无意在这里讨论它，只是在你想进一步阅读时，为你指出一些 sources：Platz (2014)、Wells (2016)、Layman (2015)、Tomasi (2014) 和 Tomasi (2015)。沿着同一思路、但并不直接属于 Rawlsian tradition 的，还有一方的 Brennan (2004) \[3\]、Brennan (2014)、Huemer (2014)，以及另一方的 Liam & Nagel (2002) 和 Holmes and Sunstein (2000)。

无论这些 arguments 如何，下文提出的那些我认为对 Rawlsianism 构成最有力 condemnation 的 arguments，都不 depend on 这场 debate 最终如何 resolved。如果有人想知道我本人的 opinion，那就是：对 Rawlsian freedom 的 reasonable interpretation，确实需要一种关于 economic freedom 的 "thick" conception。

### Rawls 的黑暗面

现在，Doctor Who 式的恐怖情节来了。如果我告诉你，Rawlsianism 可以 justify "thought engineering"（即 surgically editing 人们的 thoughts、beliefs 或 values）以及 "organ redistribution"，你会怎么想？liberty principle 肯定会 forbid 这些，对吧？它明明写着 "freedom of conscience" 和 "freedom from harm"。而且 Rawls 从未 advocated 这些。

但如果仔细想想，我们已经看到，Rawls asserted，我们周围所见的许多东西，包括我们的 talents 或 willingness to make an effort，都是 morally arbitrary 的：

> natural capacities 的 develop 和 reach fruition 的 extent，受到各种 social conditions 和 class attitudes 的 affected。甚至 willingness to make an effort、去 try，从而在通常意义上成为 deserving，本身也 dependent upon 幸福的 family 和 social circumstances \[我还要补充，还有 genetics\]

这一做法的 issue 在于，它也使一切都成为 morally arbitrary 的。organs 的 distribution，或者我们 hold 某种 conception of the good 和 values、而不是其他 conception 和 values 这一事实，在 Rawlsian framework 中也都是 morally arbitrary 的。毕竟，你又不是靠努力才得到一对 healthy kidneys 之类的东西。

Loren Lomasky 在 Reasons and Persons and the Moral Community 的 p.  135-141 中，对这一 question，以及 Rawls 为何不能 use liberty principle 来保护自己免受这一 critique，作了详细讨论，

> 关于依照一个 socially derived criterion，即 difference principle，来 distributing economic goods 的 moral permisibility，Rawlsian argument 并不以这样的 claim 为基础：property 存在某种 peculiar 的 characteristic，使得它且只有它 falls under the public aegis；相反，**morally arbitrary 的整个 realm 都 subject to collective determination**。**然而，这包括了所有 individuates one person from another 的东西：physical attributes、character、intelligence，以及 relations to property**。事实上，甚至 persons 所具有的 ends 也将被视为 arbitrary。A 最终 devote himself to end E1，而 B 最终 devote himself to E2，这将是无数 causal factors 的 product；按照 Rawlsian account，这些 factors 没有 inherent moral significante。**只有被 stripped of 所有 attachments 以及所有使它 distinct from other egos 的东西的 bare deontological ego，仍然是 nonarbitrary**。如果任何沾有 moral arbitrariness 污点的东西都不能被允许进入 Rawlsian veil 背后，那么 persons 能带入 contractual process 的就只有他们作为 persons 的 existence。$$…$$
> 
> 有些自称 liberal 的 theories 并不认为，affirmation 所有 dispositions of property 最终都 reside 于 collectivity 有什么问题。**然而，Rawls’ informal argument 的 implications extend far beyond 通常所理解的 property。所有事实上 individuates one person from another 的东西都 becomes common property：“于是我们看到，difference principle 实际上 represents 一项 agreement，将 distribution of natural talents 视为 common asset**。” (p. 101 ATOJ)
> 
> Rawls 并没有 explicitly 将 personal conceptions of the good 也列为 common assets，但这正是他的 argument 的明确 implication。如果 A 作为 pianist 的 virtuosity 是一种 talent，却并不在 morally privileged sense 上 belong to A；如果它可以 properly 被 harnessed for the good of everyone，那么 A 对 pursue a life in music 的 commitment 同样也是 social asset。那个 end 只有在它恰好 characterise A's volitional makeup 这一 restricted sense 上才是 A 的，而不是说 A 对某个 moral space 拥有 claim，并能在其中 free to act in the pursuit of that end。
> 
> $$…$$ 在追求 elimination 那些 morally arbitrary 的东西时，Rawls 不幸 undercut 了 viable liberalism 必须依靠的 supporting structures。A 具有他所具有的 abilities、character 和 projects，这是 arbitrary 的；因此，A 有 reason to pursue 属于他自己的那些 ends，而不是属于 B 的那些 end，这也是 arbitrary 的。**Rawls 会 preclude A from demanding 一种 liberty，来 serve 那些 distinctively his own 的 ends，因为 Rawls 不愿承认 contingent facts 所 carry 的 moral weight 是 prior to and determinate of social arrangements 中的 justice。** $$…$$ 在 social unit adjudicates 那些 ends 和 liberties 应当是什么之前，A 没有任何 rightfully 属于他的 ends，A 也不 deserves 任何 liberty。$$…$$
> 
> **诚然，Rawls 并不 advocate 为了 collective interest 而进行 brainwashing 和 corrective neurosurgery。** Persons 将获准保留他们已有的 ends，以及他们的 distinguishing abilities 和 character：“这并不 follow 出人们应当 eliminate 这些 distinctions。还有另一种处理它们的方式。basic structure 可以被 arranged，使这些 contingencies work for the good of the lesat fortunate。” (p. 102 ATOJ).
> 
> **“这并不 follow 出人们应当 eliminate 这些 disctinctions”——但同样也不 follow 出人们不应当这样做。** 任何大规模 altering men’s minds and bodies、shaping ends and abilities 以 fit a collective design 的 social program，很可能都会是 counterproductive。least well off 的 condition 将会 worsened，而不是 made better。基于 efficiency，或许最好让 ends 保持原样。但是，如果 totalitarian psychoengineering 未能通过 cost-benefit analysis，那也是一种 fortuitous accident，而且很大程度上是 available technology 的 function。Rawls 无法从它的 practical unfeasability 中获得多少安慰。I**t 若是最坚持 rejection of appeal to 那些 morally arbitrary 的东西的 philosopher，却不得不将一项要求 noninterference 于 project pursuers 的 integrity 所 central 的东西的 claim，建立在（暂时）abscence 便于 mold men’s souls 的 means 之上，那将是一种奇怪的讽刺。**
> 
> 有人或许会提出，Rawls 在这方面并不 vulnerable，因为 maximum equal liberty principle rules out 任何这种对 personality 的 interference。$$…$$
> 
> 这个 objection fails。**它没有 explain，为什么 societal meddling 于某个人恰好具有的 character 就 counts as a violation of liberty，而对某个人恰好具有的 property holdings 行使 ultimate control 却不算**。更关键的是，它没有注意到，在这个语境中，任何 appeal to 那些 formally derived principles of justice 都是 logically inadmissible。现在所考察的是 Rawls’ informal argument，其意图是使如下 claim 令人信服：无论 veil 背后产生什么，都是我们 intuitive sense of justice 的 adequate representation。在此时引入一个其 moral accepatbility 正在受到审视的 principle，会是 blatantly circular 的。**为什么我们应当在 wide reflective equilibrium 中 subscribe 于 difference principle？这个 informal argument 使 principles of maximum equal liberty 陷入危险。如果 contingencies 可以被 socially controlled，以 work for the benefit of the least well off，那么 persons 所 contingently 具有的 ends 也必须被放入 social assets 这口锅中。在试图使他的两个 principles 之一可信时，Rawls 无意间抽掉了另一个的根基。**

实质上，如果某一组东西可以被 redistributed 的 reason 是它们的 moral arbitrariness，那么那个 argument 也 allow 去 redistribute 那些受到 first principle 保护的东西。我们不能 appeal to principles of justice，因为此时正在被 examined 的正是它们的 justification 本身。如果没有对 bodies 和 thoughts 的 ownership 的 assignment，liberty principle 就是 empty 的：取走一个健康男人的 kidney 并不会 violate 他的 liberty，也不会对他构成 harm，因为那个 kidney 原本就不 belong to 他。

当然，人们可以 stipulate，persons 确实对某些 morally arbitrary items（例如他们的 body 或 conception of the good）具有 ownership rights，以此让这个问题消失，然后说 second principle 并不 apply to 那些受到 first principle 保护的 morally arbitrary items。但这样一来，为 second principle 提供的 argument 就 loses its force，因为人们可以随心所欲地将任意多的 moral irrelevancy 移入 first principle，让 second principle 变得 empty。因此，再一次，真正 determining 所 chosen 的 principles 是什么的，是我们的 conception of man。

> Rawls 未能 show，social control over property 比 social control over personality 在任何方面更加 justifiable。这一失败并不令人意外。他关于 property 是 social asset 的 argument，explicitly based on 如下 proposition：natural talents——以及其他一切 morally arbitrary 的东西——都是 social asset。由此 follows，不存在任何 domain，让 individual 在其中享有 privileged moral position，使得他且只有他 entitled to control 任何东西——包括他自己的 activities。除了 personhood 之外，所有 pertaining to persons 的东西都被 thoroughly socialized 了；而 personhood 在被 abstracted from ends、character、abilities 和 relations to material posessions 之后，究竟还能是什么，也未可知。Moral space 已经 shrunk to zero。Persons 所享有的任何 liberty rights 之所以属于他们，并不是因为他们作为 project pursuers 而 existence，有自己的人生要过，而是因为 social decision process 已经 created and bestowed 那些 rights。这个 conclusion 是 thoroughly illiberal 的。Individuals，只要他们被 recognized 为不只是 bare egos，就是 society 的 creation，而非它的 creators。这一 result 显然不是 Rawls 想要的，也不是他认为自己已经 achieved 的，但它依然纠缠着 A Theory of Justice。

## 结论（Conclusion）

Rawls 的 Theory of Justice 存在他自己也 recognised 的 issues。此后，他转而研究 public reason liberalism，并研究一个 community 中的人如何相互 give reasons，以 justify 他们所遵循的 rules。

不过，他的努力并未白费，因为他的 reflective equilibrium 方法和 veil of ignorance 一直延续至今。Rawls 要求能够向所有人 justified 的 impartial rules，这一点是对的；而后，在尝试由此走向一种 theory of justice 的过程中，他让我们注意到，在这一转换过程中，我们对 person 和 good life 的 conception，对于 determine 我们 ought to do 什么以及 society ought to be structured 成什么样，具有极其重要的意义。我们究竟在意什么？我们应该在意什么？（我们甚至应该在意吗？）不过，那是另一个问题，留待下次讨论[。](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Neverending_Story)

## 原文

> Enough manipulation with the definition of man, and freedom can be made to mean whatever the manipulator wishes (Matt Zwolinski)

![ea6c1736db7a167d4f13e3cefbe6ee28d1abf1d4d731112c1c52ff670ae4d5e9](https://nintil.com/rise-and-fall-of-rawlsianism/06-1.jpg?w=293)

John Rawls (1921 - 2002) was one of the most important philosophers in the History of modern political philosophy. Robert Nozick, who is broadly considered an intellectual rival of Rawls even wrote that "political philosophers must work within Rawls's conceptual framework or explain why they don't". Rawls also ended the reign of utilitarianism as the most popular moral framework in academia, reintroducing deontology - one that directly draws from Kant, no less - into intellectual discourse.

In this article, I will put forward a few problems that Rawls's philosophical project had. The problems are severe enough to put Rawls (or, at least the early Rawls) outside of liberalism itself.

I wrote four articles about Rawls a while ago in Spanish, which you can view in the Articles section. Here I've collected the best of them and added some extra bits, and translated them.

The plan for the post is as follows: first, I will introduce street Rawlsianism, a simplified version of Rawls's Theory of Justice to get an idea of what this is all about. Then, I will explain how that came to be, including some extra details about Rawls's justification for his theory. This story itself, the development of Rawls's own philosophical views, is a good enough criticism of his original theory, but I will add at the end what I think are the strongest critiques I know.

#### Street Rawlsianism

Rawls in a nutshell is this: What principles of justice should a society abide by? When thinking about this one ought make an effort not to be biased. We all recognise that ethics shouldn't be partial to us, than an ethics that says "Everyone should be X's servant", where X is a given individual, is morally suspicious. Similarly for "the weak must obey the strong", and so forth. To avoid this bias, Rawls proposes we use a philosophical tool: the original position under the veil of ignorance. This means imagining us in a situation where we have lost our knowledge of where we will be in society. We don't know about or family, friends, job, wealth, income, etc. We only know all the relevant scientific knowledge. Rawls also tells us to, in this conditions, try to negotiate with other equally situated people in order to decide the principles of justice. Behind the veil, we should be relatively self-interested, but reasonable and not envious. We should be willing to compromise if needed. After the deliberation finishes, you can imagine you will be given a life of your own, and you will have to abide by the rules you decided. As he explains,

> _The idea here is simply to make vivid to ourselves the restrictions that it seems reasonable to impose on arguments for principles of justice, and therefore on these principles themselves. Thus it seems reasonable and generally acceptable that no one should be advantaged or disadvantaged by natural fortune or social circumstances in the choice of principles. It also seems widely agreed that it should be impossible to tailor principles to the circumstances of one’s own case. We should insure further that particular inclinations and aspirations, and persons’ conceptions of their good do not affect the principles adopted. … At any time we can enter the original position, so to speak, simply by following a certain procedure, namely, by arguing for principles of justice in accordance with these restrictions._
> 
> _It is natural to ask why, if this agreement is never actually entered into, we should take any interest in these principles. … The answer is that the conditions embodied in the description of the original position are ones that we do in fact accept. Or if we do not, then perhaps we can be persuaded to do so by philosophical reflection_(A Theory of Justice)

The move then there is to say: surely you wouldn't want to end up poor and having nothing and being miserable the rest of your life. And because everyone would think the same, everyone would agree on doing something to help the poorest in a society. Rawls claims that in these conditions we would choose the maximin principle of justice: choose the distribution of morally arbitrary goods that maximises the holdings of the poorest. The idea here is that one does not deserve one's income or assets broadly because they are due to genetics, upbringing or luck. They are unmerited, and so they can be rearranged without any moral trouble.

This is Rawls's second principle of justice, the difference principle. The first one is the liberty principle, which ranks lexically above the second principle.

> **First Principle**: Each person has the same indefeasible claim to a fully adequate scheme of equal basic liberties, which scheme is compatible with the same scheme of liberties for all; **Second Principle**: Social and economic inequalities are to satisfy two conditions:
> 
> 1.  They are to be attached to offices and positions open to all under conditions of _fair equality of opportunity_;
> 2.  They are to be to the greatest benefit of the least-advantaged members of society (the _difference principle_). (_JF_, 42–43)

This is, there is a set of basic liberties that cannot be overturned by equality concerns. This includes, political liberty (right to vote, hold office, etc), freedom of speech, freedom of conscience and thought, freedom from physical and psychological harm, freedom of posession of personal property, and freedom from arbitraty detention (habeas corpus). Private property in an expansive sense is forbidden, it excludes means of production. These rights cannot be curtailed even if violating them makes the poor better off. And it is this (seemingly) strong principle of liberty that puts Rawls firmily in the liberal camp.

To summarise, Rawls is making a clever move. He is saying to you: Do you agree that you should be unbiased and rational and reasonable when deciding the principles of justice? In those conditions, you would agree to Rawlsianism. If you do not accept Rawlsianism they you are either evil \_by your own moral values, \_or you reject the idea of an universal ethics that does not make arbitrary discriminations, or if you want to rationally believe you are not a bad person, and retain universalism as a principle of your own ethics, then you have to show what's wrong with Rawls's argument.

Rawls, importantly, thought of his Theory of Justice as an ideal theory. This is: principles of justice that would be chosen for an ideal world. This has been the source of many criticisms, for example [Brennan (2007)](https://philpapers.org/rec/BRERP)who argues that Rawls's commitment to explicitly maximising the economic position of the poorest is economically self-effacing. In what follows I consider only the ideal case, so we assume away wars, crime, and bad things in general. We just deal with the concerns of rational, reasonable, individuals, who sometimes may have disagreements.

Thus finishes our exposition. Now, let's rewind history a few decades and see how we got to this.

### The unraveling of the great Rawlsian project

This is an abridged summary of Vallier (2013), Gaus (2014), and Gaus & Thrasher (2016) and has a nice summary of how Rawls got to where he got, which I make it even shorter:

Rawlsianism can be traced back to 1951, with the publication of \_Outline of a Decision Procedure for Ethics. \_Like many people before and after him, Rawls posed the question:

> Does there exist a reasonable decision procedure which is sufficiently strong … to determine the manner in which competing interests should be adjudicated, and, in instances of conflict, one interest given preferences over another; and, further, can the existence of this procedure, as well as its reasonableness, be established by rational methods of inquiry?

This problem, justifying which interest to favour is then converted into the framework of rational choice: The interest that ought to be favoured are those designated by the principles reached under agreement behind the veil of ignorance. Because of the constraints Rawls imposes, the principles of justice cannot be based on a mere balance of power. Rawls is a Kantian contractualist, not a Hobessian contractarian. The goal is not how to devise principles to avoid violence at a social scale, but how to instantiate Justice.

Behind the veil of ignorance, however, you don't even know your conception of the good. Or in modern parlance, you don't know your utility function. What should you bring to the table to maximise? Rawls fixes this by saying that everyone will share a thin theory of the good. Everyone will have "primary goods" and they will want to maximise these. These things are

> things that rational individuals, “whatever else they want, desire as prerequisites for carrying out their plans of life” (TJ, 348). These are liberties, opportunity, wealth, income and the social bases of self-respect (TJ, 54) (Gauss, 2014)

(The social bases of self-respect means "the recognition by social institutions that gives citizens a sense of self-worth and the confidence to carry out their plans")

One issue that arises then is that once the principles are in place, ordering a society, they have to be stable. They cannot be such that a society is unable to comply with them. This is the reason why Ken Binmore, who favours utilitarianism, ends up siding with a softer, Rawlsian, version. We are not good enough for utilitarianism, he says, echoing what G.A. Cohen also said about communism. \[1\].

In Rawls's framework, people have conceptions of the good, sort of what they want to do with their lives in the long term, their personal moral codes, etc. He initially assumed that respecting the Rawlsian idea of justice would be part of everyone's goods: something intrinsically motivating. He assumed that people would simply develop dispositions and inclinations to comply with the principles if they lived in such a society.

> This confronts a well ordered society with what Rawls called the “hazards of the generalized prisoner’s dilemma” — each sees the collective rationality of acting on the principles but is tempted to defect in her own case when recommended by her self-interested point of view (TJ, 505, 435, 296; Weithman, 2010, 48). To overcome this hazard, Weithman argues, Rawls sought to show in Theory that in a well-ordered society justice as fairness constitutes a Nash equilibrium: “Each member of the W\[ell\] O\[ordered\] S\[ociety\] judges, from within the thin theory of the good, that her balance of reasons tilts in favor of maintaining her desire to act from the principles of justice as a highest-order regulative desire in her rational plans, when the plans of others are similarly regulated” (Weithman, 2010, 64, emphasis in original). Acting justly would then be the best reply to others acting justly.
> 
> Rawls’s stability argument thus has a second stage: having shown a sense of justice would tend to arise in a well-ordered society regulated by justice as fairness, it must also be shown that the conceptions of the rational good in such a society would be such that people typically are not alienated from their sense of justice. **The best life for a typical member of a well-ordered society based on justice as fairness would include a devotion to, and acting upon, the principles of justice when others do so as well (TJ, 382-3)**. If a society (1) does not encourage a strong sense of justice or (2) encourages conceptions of the good that tempt people away from their sense of justice, the society will fail the stability test as viewed from the original position (TJ, 398)
> 
> (Gauss, 2014)

But years later, he rejected this assumption. And he rejected the assumption not only because he allowed people not to incorporate justice as fairness into their goods, but because he realised over time that his framework does not uniquely generates his principles of justice.

One example of the first issue is what would happen if someone reasons from their own self-interested point of view: that would undermine their willingness to follow the principles, leading them to resent justice as fairness as an onerous imposition.

Thus, first, his next book, Political Liberalism and Public Reason deals with the issue of reasonable pluralism in moral ideas. He tried, unsuccessfully in the end, to show that justice as fairness could be defended from many different perspectives. This way, even if people disagreed about why justice is what is is, they could still agree in the broad overarching principles of the Rawlsian framework. Thus justice as fairness becomes a "political, not metaphysical" theory of justice. This means that one could be a christian, a marxist, a socialist, a libertarian, a feminist, a buddhist, etc, and still endorse justice as fairness, but from different arguments.

> In addition to conflicting comprehensive doctrines, PL does recognize that in any actual political society a number of differing liberal political conceptions of justice compete with one another in society’s political debate…. This leads to another aim of PL: saying how a well-ordered liberal society is to be formulated given not only reasonable pluralism \[of comprehensive conceptions\] but a family of reasonable liberal conceptions of justice (PL, xlviii). (Gauss, 2014)

Second, Rawls accepted that the veil of ignorance does not lead one to his principles of justice. He then argued that people would still converge on a range of liberal positions that must a) pick a set of basic liberties b) assign them a special (not even lexical) priority and c) provide means to enjoy them.

This reason is why Rawls didn't consider libertarianism a liberal or even reasonable doctrine, as it doesn't meet his condition c). However, a sufficientarian libertarianism, as espoused by many of the leading libertarian philosophers, would pass his filter.

Do you see the difference principle somewhere? I don't. Because in the end, Rawls had to get rid of it: he realised not every rational and reasonable agent behind a veil of ignorance would choose it. So Rawlsianism became instead Public Reason Liberalism: dealing with the way we should talk to one another about political matters, and which reasons are valid to justify political actions.

The unraveling continued in Rawls's latest book, The Law of Peoples.

Here Rawls considered the rules that ought to regulate the relations between nations. One might think, after having read the discussion above, that Rawls might endorse something like a world-state, or very stringent redistributive duties on developed countries towards the poorest. But no. If you are surprised, that's normal. Rawls's followers (Pogge, 2004) also were. Rawls ended up instead endorsing international libertarianism: countries ought not attack each other, they should keep their treaties, respect human rights, and respect the laws of war. There is also a weak duty of helping poorer countries that are stricken by famine or some other catastrophe.

Why did this happen? Well, because elsewhere in A Theory of Justice, when Rawls explain why would the rich be willing to submit themselves a social order where their purpose is to maximise the welfare of the poor, he says

> _The natural distribution is neither just nor unjust; nor is it unjust that persons are born into society at some particular position. These are simply natural facts. What is just and unjust is the way that institutions deal with these facts. . . . The social system is not an unchangeable order beyond human control but a pattern of human action. **In justice as fairness men agree to share one another’s fate.** (TJ, 102)_

Interestingly, as discussed by Loren Lomasky, Rawls removed this paragraph in later editions of the book. Lomasky continues:

> To this the indicated response is: Exactly! Bonds uniting citizens in the Rawlsian conception reveal themselves to be extraordinarily tight. **I\*\*\*\*t is no small thing to agree to share one’s fate with another. That is the sort of undertaking embarked on within a family, by friends or lovers determined to pursue the good life together, partners in a far-reaching enterprise, a platoon’s soldiers guarding each other’s backs, congregants joined in common worship, or devotees of a mutually adored good. In such settings, the success of one constitutes in no small measure the success of all, and an individual who is not prepared to sacrifice some of her own quota of the good, indeed sacrifice liberally, is an anomalous partner in the pursuit.** \[…\]
> 
> And so it does. Rawls has second thoughts concerning the intensity of social bonds under a justice as fairness regime. **In the revised edition of A Theory of Justice, the striking passage about individuals sharing each other’s fate disappears**. Most likely, during the interval between the two editions Rawls had come to believe that he had expressed himself more forcefully than was felicitous. Perhaps some friendly critics had asked him whether he really wished to hitch to the theoretical structure of A Theory of Justice so weighty a wagon of social solidarity. Whatever the impetus behind the revision, the text of the second edition has become anodyne. Citizens are still to share in society’s assets but not each other’s fates. It is not clear that such revision by omission is satisfactory. The reader may suspect that it camouflages rather than eliminates a fundamental problem with the project \[…\]
> 
> **Although arguments ad hominem normally carry a somewhat unsavory flavor, in this case that may be mitigated by the fact that the one arguing against Rawls’s understanding is Rawls himself**. That argument is initiated in Political Liberalism (1993), where he does not abandon his support of the difference principle but concedes that it is one among several ways of reasonably distributing the benefits and burdens of social cooperation. But in The Law of Peoples (1999), Rawls distances himself further from the difference principle by denying, against the urging of his own disciples,16 that it is a suitable basis for interaction among the world’s peoples.17 The reasoning and illustrative examples Rawls provides in the latter book are somewhat opaque, but the upshot is that there is no general requirement of economic redistribution from the world’s wealthier countries to those less well-off. Only for peoples in considerable distress, what Rawls calls “burdened societies” (meaning thereby essentially the same as the more familiar locution “failed states”), is there a duty of positive assistance, and then only to the “point at which a people’s basic needs (estimated in primary goods) are fulfilled and a people can stand on its own” (LP, 119). **This is because the world’s peoples have reason primarily to value their own autonomy and preferred national culture rather than subscription to an egalitarian cosmopolitanism**. Beyond contingent temporary provision to address episodes of distress, duties owed by the wealthy to the less well-off are modes of noninterference (e.g., observance of treaties, nonintervention, and adherence to human rights and rules of war; see LP, 37), and they are returned in kind: **one is struck by the sweepingness on the international level of an equal liberty principle, and by the rejection of egalitarianism as inconsistent with each society’s overriding interest in devoting its energies to its own distinctive interests.**
> 
> (Lomasky, 2005)

Basically, Rawlsianism seems to make sense for a family. Families, at a minimum, try to make sure no member suffers greatly from mistfortunes. But imagine instead if you regard your fellow citizens as friends or acquaintances. How much redistribution can a friend morally ask of another friend? What Rawls seems to need for justice as fairness is the requirement that we all see each other as even more than friends. And that is not, will not, and I dare to add, should not be the case.

What system did Rawls end up endorsing after his discussion of justice? The answer for many is: liberal democracy and a welfare state. But no. For Rawls that is not enough. Welfare states do not redistribute enough, are not egalitarian enough. Some people are startled when they hear [Matt Bruenig](http://mattbruenig.com/2012/11/25/the-radical-rawls/)'s views on socialising most or all productive enterprises into a huge sovereign wealth fund. But he is no marxist, just an orthodox Rawlsian.

Rawls accepted two systems as compatible with his theory of justice, at least initially: property owning democracy, and liberal socialism. A lengthy critical discussion of POD can be found [here](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/11/property-owning-democracy/). POD basically is:

> (1) A right to equal public education. (2) A right to minimum income and/or the means for supporting oneself and one’s family at a minimal level of social acceptability. (3) A public system of campaign financing and explicit limitations on corporate political activity. (4) A right of individuals to a share of society’s productive capital and/or wealth. (5) A collective right to sufficient productive capital to sustain viable democratic communities at the local level.

Points (4) and (5) is what goes beyond the welfare state.

Liberal socialism is, in a nutshell, a Soviet Union with civil rights. The state need not centrally plan the economy, but ultimately would own all productive assets. For example.

Rawls gets there by reasoning within ideal theory (no one is corrupt, there is no crime, etc), and giving zero weight to economic freedoms. Why, one may wonder, does he reject economic freedom and extensive private property? Why must the agents behind the veil choose political rights but not even a tiny right to own productive assets? Of course, in the end Rawls ended up rejecting that they wouldn't choose that, and so by the end of his career, he would have to accept capitalism with a minimal safety net as okay. The reason he initially gave to disregard economic freedoms is one that can be traced back to J.S. Mill: that economic activity is not an expression of liberty, and that one doesn't need it to pursue one's conception of the good,

> A crucial junction point within the liberal tradition occurs with Mill’s treatment of economic liberty. (Freeman 2011) \*\*Mill suggests that economic liberties should be treated differently from freedom of thought, association and religion.\*\***Decisions people make regarding labor and ownership have no intrinsic connection to liberty in Mill’s sense. Economic activity is not an expression of liberty, and so rights and powers of ownership and labor should mainly be defined by the requirements of utility.** (Mill 2004, 209-223; Jacobson 2000, 293-295)
> 
> **John Rawls likewise adopts a platform of economic exceptionalism. He singles out the economic liberties to a lower level of protection. He claims wide economic rights are not “necessary for the development and exercise of the moral powers.”** Regarding freedom of labor, Rawls recognizes only the right to occupational choice, but not the freedom to engage in self-organized economic activity and/or to hire others for productive purposes. Regarding ownership, Rawls recognizes only a basic right to personal property, severing off any rights protecting the ownership of productive property (family farms, businesses, and other “means of production”). **Rights to private productive property and contract are not basic, and so any laws recognizing such rights should be crafted to realize distributive justice.** (Brennan & Tomasi, 2011)

Thus, Rawlsianism sets contraints for what principles of justice may be chosen, but it doesn't end up picking which ones. And so historically, there have been attempts to derive many things from within the rawlsian framework, from utilitarianism (Harsanyi, 1975) \[2\] to libertarianism (Tomasi, 2013).

The quote at the beginning of this post, "Enough manipulation with the definition of man, and freedom can be made to mean whatever the manipulator wishes" is the reason of the discrepancy between these authors. If man is a mere vessel for happiness, and happiness is the only thing that matters, then you get utilitarianism. If you think that persons are disembodied kantian souls, then Rawlsianism follows. And if you think that persons have personal projects that matter morally, and that property is in an extended sense part of oneself, then one gets to more classically liberal positions. Rawls explicitly says that he wants to respect that fact: that people are separate, but because of his commitment to the kantian conception of the person, he ends up with a framework that entails unexpected things, as I'll show in the next section. For more on this topic, see Zwolinski (2008), I quote at length a relevant bit:

> Rawls endorses the claim that a theory of justice must respect the separateness of persons, but famously holds that justice permits, indeed requires, largescale transfers of wealth from the rich to the poor. What explains these differing perspectives?
> 
> The explanation does not lie in the fact that Rawls and Nozick understand the basic moral requirement of respect for the separateness of persons in fundamentally different ways. Both of them appear to endorse the idea that respect for the separateness of persons prohibits ways of treating others which use them as mere means. The difference, instead, lies in their understanding of what a person is, or, more precisely, what the normatively significant aspects of a person are.
> 
> For Nozick, coercively redistributing wealth from Alan to Betty is a case of using Alan as a mere means because Alan's wealth is, in some important sense, a part of Alan. The most literal way of understanding this idea is through the account of property appropriation via labor mixing, which Nozick takes on from John Locke. One comes to own that which was previously unowned, according to Nozick, by mixing one's labor with it. Since one owns oneself and one's labor, one therefore comes to own the external goods with which one mixes one's labor. External goods are owned because they contain, in some sense, a part of one's self. To deprive a person of their external goods is thus to take away some part of their self or to use the person's self as a means to somebody else's goal.
> 
> But we need not understand Nozick's position in such a literal and simplistic way. One need not endorse the problematic story of labor mixing in order to grant that there is some way in which a person's external goods are a part of the person. A much less contentious way of getting at essentially the same conclusion would be to note the strong connection between persons and their aims, and then to note the various and significant ways in which property in external goods is connected to a person's aims.23 Our identity as persons, how we see ourselves and how others see us, is largely a function of the projects we pursue. Our lives develop over time as a set of embedded aims, some very general and abstract, such as the aim of living a virtuous or experience-rich life, others somewhat more focused such as the aim of raising a child or being a professor. By identifying with some projects rather than others, we give shape and meaning to our lives. But aims cannot be pursued over any significant period of time without the ability to plan and rely on the use of external goods. One's home, for instance, becomes over time an essential part of one's aim of raising a family. The financial holdings of one's business are an important part of the aim of running a successful firm. One's external property is thus not only partially the product of one's aims, one's physical work or mental energies, it is partially constitutive of them as well. To take a person's property for the benefit of someone else is thus in a very real way to use that person as a means.
> 
> For Rawls, in contrast, there is an important difference between using a person as a means and using a person's property as a means. There is a difference between persons and the endowments of persons, and this difference is especially significant when persons can claim no moral credit for their endowments. If ones wealth, intelligence, merit and so forth are ‘‘arbitrary from a moral perspective,’’ then a moral system which would have us take the legitimacy of such endowments for granted would be equally arbitrary
> 
> Nozicks response to this line of reasoning is to point out that it requires us to conceive of persons in an oddly abstract and foreign way which pushes very hard on the distinction between persons and the characteristics that individuate them.25 What is arbitrary to Rawls is essential to Nozick. To put the point more accurately, what is arbitrary in Rawlss sense of the term need not be arbitrary in the sense that matters for Nozicks account of respect for persons. Natural talents and ownership of external goods might be arbitrary in the sense of being ultimately undeserved. But this is compatible with recognizing, as Loren Lomasky puts the point, that ‘‘contingencies become imbued with moral weight once they are intimately attached to the lives that persons actually live…. That which is initially absurd becomes suffused with moral significance once it enters the projects of a person.’’26 The facts about a person which seem arbitrary from the impartial third-person point of view of morality are essentially bound up with a person's identity and aims from a first person perspective. To take seriously the separateness of persons is thus in part to take seriously this perspective in clarifying the boundaries between persons, informing our understanding of what does and does not belong to a person.

There is an interesting debate about whether economic liberties should be fundamental. It is not my purpose to address that here, just point you to to some sources if you want to read more: Platz (2014), Wells (2016), Layman (2015), Tomasi (2014), and Tomasi (2015). In the same line, but not directly in the Rawlsian tradition, Brennan (2004) \[3\], Brennan (2014), Huemer (2014), on the one side, and Liam & Nagel (2002) and Holmes and Sunstein (2000) on the other.

Regardless of those arguments, the ones advanced below, the ones that I think are the strongest condemnation of Rawlsianism, do not depend on how this debate ends up being resolved. My own opinion, if anyone wonders, is that a "thick" conception of economic freedom is indeed needed by a reasonable interpretation of the Rawlsian freedom.

### The dark side of Rawls

Now here it comes the Doctor Who horror bit. What if I told you Rawlsianism can justify "thought engineering" (i.e. surgically editing people's thoughts, beliefs, or values) and "organ redistribution"? Surely the liberty principle would forbid that, right? It literally says "freedom of conscience" and "freedom from harm". And Rawls never advocated for that.

But if we think about it, we have seen that Rawls asserted that much of what we see around, including our talents, or willingness to make an effort are morally arbitrary:

> The extent to which natural capacities develop and reach fruition is affected by all kinds of social conditions and class attitudes. Even the willingness to make an effort, to try, and so to be deserving in the ordinary sense is itself dependent upon happy family and social circumstances \[And genetics, I would add\]

The issue with this move is that this also makes everything morally arbitrary. The distribution of organs, or the fact that we hold some conception of the good and values and not others are also morally arbitrary in the Rawlsian framework. It's not like you worked hard to get a pair of healthy kidneys or something.

Loren Lomasky, in Reasons and Persons and the Moral Community p.  135-141, includes a lengthy discussion of this question, and why Rawls cannot use the liberty principle to shield himself from this critique,

> The Rawlsian argument for the moral permisibility of distributing economic goods according to a socially derived criterion, the difference principle, does not rest on a claim that there is some characteristic peculiar to property such that it and it alone falls under the public aegis; rather, **the entire realm of the morally arbitrary is subject to collective determination**. **That, however, includes everything that individuates one person from another: physical attributes, character, intelligence, and relations to property**. Indeed, even the ends that persons have will be regarded as arbitrary. That A comes to devote himself to end E1 and B to E2 will be the product of a myriad of causal factors that have, on the Rawlsian account, no inherent moral significante. **Only the bare deontological ego stripped of all its attachments and everything that sets it off as distinct from other egos remains as nonarbitrary**. If nothing that is tainted by the stain of moral arbitrariness can be allowed behind the Rawlsian veil, then persons can bring to the contractual process only their existence as persons. \[…\]
> 
> Some theories that profess to be liberal perceive no problem with affirmation that all dispositions of property reside ultimately in the collectivity. **Yet the implications of Rawls’ informal argument extend far beyond property, as commonly conceived. Everything that in fact individuates one person from another becomes common property: “We see then that the difference principle represents, in effect, an agreement to regard the distribution of natural talents as a common asset**” (p. 101 ATOJ)
> 
> Rawls does not explicitly list personal conceptions of the good as also being common assets, but that is the clear implication of his argument. If A’s virtuosity as a pianist is a talent that does not, in a morally privileged sense, belong to A, if it can properly be harnessed for the good of everyone, then A’s commitment to pursue a life in music is equally a social asset. That end is A's only in the restricted sense that it happens to characterise A's volitional makeup, but not in the sense that A has a claim to moral space within which he is free to act in the pursuit of that end.
> 
> \[…\] In pursuit of the elimination of what is morally arbitrary, Rawls has, unfortunately, undercut the supporting structures on which a viable liberalism must rest. It is arbitrary that A has the abilities, character and projects that he does, and thus it is arbitrary that A has reason to pursue those ends which are his rather than those end which are B’s. **Rawls would preclude A from demanding a liberty to serve those ends which are distinctively his own because Rawls will not grant that the contingent facts carry moral weight that is prior to and determinate of justice in social arrangements.** \[…\] A has no ends that rightfully are his, and A deserves no liberty, until the social unit adjudicates what those ends and liberties shall be. \[…\]
> 
> **To be sure, Rawls does not advocate brainwashing and corrective neurosurgery in the collective interest.** Persons will be allowed to keep the ends that they have along with their distinguishing abilities and character: “It does not follow that one should eliminate these distinctions. There is another way to deal with them. The basic structure can be arranged so that these contingencies work for the good of the lesat fortunate.” (p. 102 ATOJ).
> 
> **“It does not follow that one should eliminate these disctinctions” – but neither does it follow that one should not.** It is probably the case that any vast social program of altering men’s minds and bodies, shaping ends and abilities to fit a collective design, will be counterproductive. The least well off will have their condition worsened that made better. On grounds of efficiency it might be better to leave ends where they may lie. But if totalitarian psychoengineering flunks a cost-benefit analysis, that is a fortuitous accident and one that is largely a function of available technology. Rawls can take little comfort from its practical unfeasability. I**t would be a strange irony if the philosopher most insistent on the rejection of appeal to which is morally arbitrary had to rest a claim for noninterference with that which is central to the integrity of project pursuers on the (temporary) abscence of handy means to mold men’s souls.**
> 
> It might be argued that Rawls is not vulnerable in this respect because the maximum equal liberty principle rules out any such interference with personality. \[…\]
> 
> The objection fails. **It does not explain why societal meddling with the character a person happens to have counts as a violation of liberty while the exercise of ultimate control over property holdings a person happens to have does not**. More crucial, it fails to note that any appeal to the formally derived principles of justice is logically inadmissible in this context. What is now under consideration is Rawls’ informal argument, intended to make convincing the claim that whatever emerges from behind the veil is an adequate representation of our intuitive sense of justice. It would be blatantly circular to introduce at this point a principle whose moral accepatbility is being scrutinized. **Why should we subscribe in wide reflective equilibrium to the difference principle? The informal argument places the principles of maximum equal liberty in jeopardy. If contingencies can be socially controlled to work for the benefit of the least well off, then the ends that persons contingently have must also be placed in the pot of social assets. In the attempt to render credible one of his two principles, Rawls inadvertently pulls the rug out under the other.**

Essentially, if the reason that a certain set of things can be redistributed is their moral arbitrariness, then that argument also allow to redistribute things that are protected by the first principle. We cannot appeal to the principles of justice because their justification itself is what is being examined at that point. Without the assignment of the ownership of bodies and thoughts, the liberty principle is empty: taking a healthy man's kidney wouldn't violate his liberty, it wouldn't be a harm to him, as that kidney doesn't belong to him in the first place.

One can of course stipulate that persons do have ownership rights over some morally arbitrary items (as their body or their conception of the good) to make this problem go away, and then say that the second principle doesn't apply to the morally arbitrary items protected by the first principle. But then the argument given for the second principle loses its force, as one can just move as much moral irrelevancy into the first principle as one wants and leave the second one empty. Thus, again, our conception of man is doing the work in determining what the principles chosen are.

> Rawls has failed to show that social control over property is in any way more justifiable than social control over personality. That failure is not surprising. His argument that property is a social asset is explicitly based on the proposition that natural talents - and all else that is morally arbitrary - are a social asset. It follows that there is no domain within which an individual enjoys a privileged moral position such that he and he alone is entitled to control anything - including his own activities -. Everything pertaining to persons except their personhood, whatever that could be when abstracted from ends, character, abilities, and relations to material posessions, is thoroughly socialized. Moral space has shrunk to zero. Any liberty rights that persons enjoy are theirs not in virtue of their existence as project pursuers with their own lives to lead, but because the social decision process has created and bestowed those rights. The conclusion is thoroughly illiberal. Individuals, insofar as they are recognized as more than bare egos, are the creation of society rather than its creators. The result is clearly not one that Rawls wants or believes that he has achieved, but it dogs A Theory of Justice nonetheless.

## Conclusion

Rawls's Theory of Justice has issues that he himself recognised. He moved on to study public reason liberalism, and to study how can a community of people give reasons to one another in order to justify the rules they live by.

His undertaking, however, was not in vain, as his method of reflective equilibrium and the veil of ignorance have lived on to our days. Rawls was right to ask for impartial rules that can be justified to all, and then in the process of trying to get from there to a theory of justice, he made us notice that in the process of translation, our conception of person and the good life is supremely important to determine what we ought to do and how a society ought to be structured. What do we really care about? What should we care about? (Should we even care?) But that's another issue and shall be discussed another time[.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Neverending_Story)

\[1\] Cohen argued that Rawls claims to be doing ideal theory, but he is inconsistent. Rawls says that the difference principle allows inequalities because in doing so, this will motivate some people to work harder. But if we are doing ideal theory, we cannot have in our though experiment lazy people who are unjust. In ideal theory, everyone conforms to the principles of justice. Thus Rawlsian ideal theory should really be egalitarian, as Cohen claims. It is only when one introduces real world considerations when one ends up with Rawlsianism.

In addition, he argued that even on Rawls's terms, beings in the original position wouldn't choose the maximim principle, but instead they would choose to maximise their expected own utility. Because they are symmetrically placed, this implies that average utilitarianism is the chosen principle of justice, or so argues Harsanyi. Rawls says he rejects using probabilities in the original position, not because of risk aversion, but because he says it is the rational thing to do in a situation of such a high uncertainty. Apparently, it once was a popular idea. But by the time Harsanyi wrote his reply, it wasn't, and as of today, plain expected utility maximisation is regarded as the rational thing to do in choice theory.

\[3\] Brennan argues that Rawls's theory - and by extension what has come to be known as "high liberalism" is illiberal. In a magnificent move, Jason Brennan (2004) does this and lets Benito Mussolini, Adolf Hitler, Marx, and G.A. Cohen define what liberalism is. Liberalism is what they do not defend, or they do criticise. By this criteria, Rawls is illiberal.
