# Are ideas getting harder to find?

---

## 正文

Nicholas Bloom、Chad Jones、John van Reenen 和 Michael Webb 撰写的 **_ideas 是否越来越难以 find？(2020)_**，反复出现在关于 technological progress、the great stagnation 及相关话题的讨论中。他们采用了不同于通常做法的视角：不是争论一系列 metrics（如 TFP、Moore's law、crop yields）中是否存在 stagnation，而是选择乐观的看法（在他们的一些 models 中，这些 metrics 仍像往常一样 advancing），转而 calculate 通过各种方式（如 R&D expenditures）达到这一水平需要多少 cost。结果是：US 正在 spending 更多，才能获得相同水平的 yearly progress，因此 ideas 确实越来越难以 find。而且不只是 US，后来的一项 followup（[Böeing and Hünermund, 2020](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/222264/1/1703784650.pdf)）采用相同的 methodology，考察了 Germany 和 China 的一个 panel of firms，也发现了同样的情况。

有一次参加晚宴，席间有人问我是否存在 great stagnation，我回答说，这个问题问错了。ideas 是否越来越难以 find，也属于同一类问题。它过于 high level，无法在 operationally 的意义上发挥作用。既然目标是 accelerating progress，就必须逐一考察每个 field，看看该 field 面临哪些 roadblocks，remove 它们需要多少 cost，又能带来什么 return。无论我们认为一个 field 是 stagnant、accelerating，还是处于 business as usual 阶段，这项 assessment 都一样。

那么，这篇 paper 做了什么？他们 assume，$$\frac{\dot{A_t}}{A_t}=\alpha S_t$$

其中，$\alpha$ 是 research productivity 的一个 measure，而 $S_t$ 是 researchers 的 number（经过一些 adjustments），$\frac{\dot{A_t}}{A_t}$ 则是 TFP 的 growth，它与新的 ideas 有关。你可能正在想各种问题：

1.  这个 relation 不是 linear，或许应该 introduce 一个 correction term，将 duplication of research 导致的 reduced productivity 考虑进去。他们想过这一点：这会 weakens 这个 result，但它仍然会出现）
2.  他们应该 measure 什么 R&D？问得好！作者承认这很棘手，这也是他们没有选择 human genome project 或 light（Nordhaus 的一个经典 example）这类更棘手的 case studies 的原因。但即便如此，仍然可能存在问题。后面我会说明，无论你选择什么 R&D metric，result 都一样。
3.  这个 equation assumes exponential growth！确实如此，因此他们承认，这篇 paper 完全可以叫作“exponential growth 是否越来越难以 achieve？”。在许多 sectors 中，你看到的就是这种情况，我也不反对为 Moore's law 使用 exponential model。但正如我在[别处](https://nintil.com/no-great-technological-stagnation/)提到的，一些 technologies 会以 superexponentially 的方式 improve（至少在某些时期如此），另一些则以 linearly 的方式 improve。无论如何，由于我将着重讨论 Moore's law，就不在这一点上过多计较了。我认为，在给定的 paradigm 内，exponential，或者更准确地说 logistic，在大多数情况下都是 growth 的合理 model。
4.  作者还 consider 了这样一种情况：某个 field 中的 productivity 停滞不前，但新的 fields 被 invented，使 aggregate productivity 得以继续 improve（我在[这里](https://nintil.com/on-the-constancy-of-the-rate-of-gdp-growth/)提出过的 model：exploiting 一个 paradigm 就像采矿，由于每座矿的 knowledge 都是 finite，就必须不断开新矿）。这就是他们考察整个 US 的原因。这是合理的。
5.  你也可能认为，researchers 的 number 被人为 inflated 了，因为 academic institutions 不断涌入低薪苦力，他们并没有真正作出 contribute。对于 aggregate economy，可以提出这种说法，但对于 Moore's law 的 case 则不行，因为他们只 consider semiconductor _industry_。
6.  他们的另一个选择是按 prevailing wage rate 对 R&D expenditures 进行 deflate。在 semiconductor 领域，许多 R&D 很可能是 capital expenditures，但他们也在“lab equipment 这一 section”中讨论了这一点。后面我会用 CPI 作为 deflator，而 result 再一次没有变化。
7.  或者，你可能会想到他们的这个 assumption：“如果 expanding research 涉及 employing 才能较低的人，那么这会由 R&D spending 来 properly measured。”理由是，更好的 researchers 薪酬更高。这是一个有意思的 assumption！在 Academia 中，这未必成立：靠惯性混进 Academia、只想混下去的投机者，拿到的薪酬与正在 inventing 下一代 batteries 的聪明人一样多（而且[并不多](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-020-03191-7?fbclid=IwAR0uWnjdlCGQrU-ak_fttTYb4pNdQ6UVYmlAVbaHOciCWVaxGL7FXhAses4)）。当然，在最顶端，顶尖 PIs 可能通过 patents、board seats 或自己的 startups 获得额外收入。但是！请记住，他们考察的是 industry，这项 critique 适用于他们的 aggregate numbers，而不是 Moore's law 的 numbers。industry 总该对 semiconductor companies 施加一些 discipline，迫使 pay 与 productivity 更一致吧？

Moore's law 是他们在 paper 中讨论的一个好 example。它有充分的记录，semiconductor 领域也没有那么大，因此可以知道谁在做什么，而且他们在 paper 中 consider 了这个 law 最乐观的 case：transistor count。如果你还记得我关于 [Moore's law](https://nintil.com/progress-semicon/) 的文章，文末的 diagram 表明，transistor count 确实似乎在 rising exponentially，而其他一切都在 slowing down（但仍在 growing！）。因此，如果他们能够表明，即便以 transistor count 来看，research productivity 也在 going down，那么 a fortiori，以其他方式 interpreting Moore's law（如 single thread performance 或 computations per dollar）时，也会出现 slowdown。他们使用的所有 data 都可以在 online appendix 中找到，所以我们可以查看它（我在[这里](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1RcODXzatE5t8BLbFq6ZfPR7XHWHhz2pBLjXc8zt8OxQ/edit?usp=sharing)放了一份副本，Sheet 1 中有我自己的一些 calculations）。semiconductor companies，以及像 [ASML 或 Trumpf](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NHSR6AHNiDs) 这样向它们 supplying tooling 的 companies，其 R&D expenditure 是什么样的？在所有 semiconductor companies 中，你是否应该把所有公司都 include 在 analysis 中？作者尝试了一些 variations（Table 1），包括只考虑 Intel，或者 Intel+AMD。将 Intel 纳入会 dominates 这些 results，大幅 driving down productivity growth。

![image-20201120164716972](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201120164716972.png)

他们在最后的 table 中没有使用这个 number，而是使用了 baseline result，它大致位于其他 estimates 的中间。但这立刻显得很有意思：会不会正是 Intel 在 driving down 这些 results？如果不是 ideas 越来越难以 find，而是某些 companies 在这方面越来越差呢？这未必是一种 general explanation。在 physics 中，我确实认为 low hanging fruit theory 很好地 fits 了 data。但这里不是。expenditures 如下所示（Numbers 没有进行 adjusted for inflation，但可用于 relative comparisons）

![image-20201120165302596](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201120165302596.png)

semiconductor industry 中的 R&D expenditures。Data 来自《Ideas 是否越来越难以 find》的 supplement

确实，Intel 在 R&D expenditures 的版图中遥遥领先。下一条 line 是所有 equipment companies 的 sum。然后是 Qualcomm，再然后是 Analog Devices、Broadcom，最后是 TSMC。把所有这些 companies 归在一起似乎值得质疑。一家 foundry 和 Broadcom？Broadcom 曾经有 chip 登上过 Moore's law chart 吗？Analog Devices 呢？）AMD 在哪里？NVIDIA 以及它源源不断的 state of the art GPUs 又在哪里？为什么 ASML 被放在 semiconductor category 中，而不是 semiconductor equipment 那一类？AMD 今年刚 launched 了目前最好的 desktop processors（还有 Apple 的 M1）。我们也刚迎来了 RTX 3090。还有 Apple 那款[出色得令人惊叹的](https://daringfireball.net/2020/11/the_m1_macs) M1，但要从 Apple 的其他 R&D 中分离出 semiconductor R&D 更难，所以这里我就不探究它了。

![image-20201120172012436](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201120172012436.png)

从 1996 到 2015，Intel 的 R&D increased 到原来的 6.7 倍。AMD 则是 2.36 倍。AMD 不只是 AMD，它们现在在 TSMC 制造自己的 chips，而 Intel 是一家 integrated fab+design 企业。那么我们这样做：取 ASML+这些 semiconductor equipment companies，将其同时 add 到 AMD 和 Intel，再只给 AMD add 上 TSMC，然后 compare 这两个 numbers。我们得到：

![image-20201120174008620](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201120174008620.png)

所以，看起来 Intel 在最近这段时间 spending 了很多，而 AMD 并非如此。会不会 AMD 在这之后才开始 spending，于是我们才有了 Zen3？但并不是，AMD 自身的 R&D 一直保持 [flat](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/stock-comparison?s=research-development-expenses&axis=single&comp=INTC:AMD)。举个例子，按我最喜欢的 metric——performance（为方便起见，采用 geekbench 的结果）——来看，AMD 已经从 2016 年 FX-4330 的 587 points 提升到了最新 Ryzens 的 3562，increase 为 6x。Intel 在同一时期 achieved 了 1.5x improvement。当然，有人可能会说，Intel 直到现在都处于 frontier of technology，而 AMD 或许得以 benefit from Intel 的 knowledge。这话有道理；往后 AMD R&D 是否会 rise 到 Intel 的 level 甚至更高，还有待观察。但目前的事实仍然是：与 Intel 相比，AMD 每 $ 能让 chips 获得显著更多的 improve。你也可以从[这里](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2020/11/a-history-of-intel-vs-amd-desktop-performance-with-cpu-charts-galore/)采用不同 tests 的一些 charts 中看到同样的总体情况。我这里考察的不是 transistors，而是 single thread performance，因为这个 figure 更容易获得，也更忠实地 reflects 了 industry 正在 optimizing 的目标（除了 thermal performance 之外）。

有人可能会反对我的 analysis，说我只是把其他所有公司都扔掉了，难道其他 companies 投入 semiconductors 的所有资金都毫无作用吗？不是，它们带来了各自的 chips 和 designs，但在 bleeding edge of design，其他公司一直在做的事情确实可能是 irrelevant。事实上，如果我们只谈把更多 components 塞进更少的 space，而不考虑 design，那么我们考察的就只有 TSMC 和 supplier companies（或 Intel 加 suppliers）；在这种情况下，从 1995（TSMC 的 time series 开始之时）到 2015，它们的 expenditure increased 了 15x，这个 figure 更接近 AMD 的 cost increase，而不是 Intel 的 increase。Moore's law implies，transistor count 每年有 35% increase；因此在那段时期，transistors 增至了 404x。

不过，以上所做的并不完全是这篇 paper 的做法；此外，我们还必须取 expenditure，再 divide by highly skilled labor 的 wages，以 account for inflation。在做我上面那样的 comparisons 时，denominator 并不重要（因此，Intel 看起来存在的 inefficiency，以及它对 industry 的 research productivity 的 driving down，仍然存在）。但重新进行 research efficiency 的 absolute changes 的 calculation 时，它就重要了。对应的 file 是 RNDSalaries.mat（使用 US wage data 来 deflate Taiwan 和 Europe 的 companies 是否合理，留给读者作为 exercise），我们由此得到，effective researchers 的 change 为 8.11，这让我们落在 paper 的 narrow estimate 范围内：他们的 result 仍然成立。

要满足什么条件，ideas 才不会越来越难以 find？首先，在一个 simplified model 中，如果唯一的 R&D costs 是 researchers 的 wages，那么 researchers 的 number 就必须是 constant！在一个更接近该 paper 的 model 中，我们要求 $$\tilde{S_t}=\frac{RD_t}{Wage_t}=\tilde{S_{t'}}=\frac{RD_{t'}}{Wage_{t'}}$$

$$\tilde{S_{1995}}=\frac{81.16}{48450}=\tilde{S_{2015}}=\frac{RD_{1995}}{92898}$$

所以，2015 年的 R&D expenditure 就必须是 155M$ instead of 1263M$，即减少到约 1/~8.11。要么是 8.11 倍之少的钱，要么是相同的 researchers，这就是这个 model 的 conclusion。而这还是在一个更 optimistic 的 case 下（其中我只 counting TSMC 和 manufacturers）。如果我们改为按 inflation 而非 reserachers wages 来 deflate R&D，这些 numbers 也不会消失。回想一下，TSMC et al. 的 expenditure increase 从 1995 年到 2015 年是 15x。在此期间，cumulative rate of inflation [是](https://www.usinflationcalculator.com/) 55.5%，意味着 \$1 in 1995 is ~ $现在为 1.8。因此，actual expenditure increase 就会是 15/1.8=8.33，与我们使用 wages 得到的 figure 大致相同。

也有人可能反驳说：哦，但 chip companies 还关心 power efficiency 和其他事情。是的，但那是一个 design issue：我选择的是不 design semiconductors、只 make 它们的 companies（TSMC et al.），以便将 cramming transistors 与 designing 它们如何 arranged 区分开来。

所以，回顾一下：无论如何 deflates 这些 numbers，或选取哪些 firms 来进行 calculations，这些 results 都 hold。考虑到这篇 paper 所依据的 broad class of models，我认为没有任何 plausible 的 slicing of the data 能 yields constant productivity。因此，与 [Bem's psychic ability meta-analysis](https://slatestarcodex.com/2014/04/28/the-control-group-is-out-of-control/) 一样，我们可以不按这篇 paper 的表面意思接受 research productivity 已经 gone down 的说法；相反，我们可以 conclude，**从历史来看，我们做得不错，错的是这些 models**。想一想：这并不是说 science 在 1970 年发生了变化，或 science 在 1930s 做得更好；就 aggregate TFP 和 researchers calculations 而言，这些 trends 看起来是 relatively constant 的。**Science 一直都是这样**。至少这篇 paper 中的 data 看起来如此，我并不是说一切都很好！：

![image-20201120174312357](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201121085946158.png)

[Scott Alexander](https://slatestarcodex.com/2018/11/26/is-science-slowing-down-2/) 也指出了这一点：

> science 中响应 inputs 的 exponential increases 而出现的 constant progress 应当是我们的 null hypothesis，而且几乎无法想象情况还能不是这样。

这是他为 observed data 列出的 potential explanations 清单中的 alternative 3：

1.  只有 top researchers 才 produce productivity-raising ideas；如果是这样，将 researcher count increasing 到超出这些 elites 的范围就是 inefficient 的。
2.  当前的 academic system 在 producing useful research 方面，比过去的那个差一个 order of magnitude
3.  我们已经 picked 了 low-hanging fruit，而要继续 picking 它就变得 progressively harder。（Scott 更倾向于的 explanation；[Matt Clancy](https://mattsclancy.substack.com/p/are-ideas-getting-harder-to-find) 指出了它的一个 sub-variant，即这些新的 ideas 需要 harder work，具体形式是比以前 learning 更多）

GDP growth 是 exponential 的，population growth 也是，market capitalization 也一样（对于成功的 companies 而言）。R&D expenditures 会 increase exponentially 并不令人意外。值得担忧的是 R&D expenditures 是否会成为 GDP 的 large share，但眼下这似乎是一个非常遥远的可能性。

人们可能忍不住直接说，正确的 model 应当类似于作者们所提出的那种：随着我们已经有大量的 "ideas" in stock，它们的 growth 确实会 goes down，也就是一个如下所示的 semi-endogenous model：

$$\frac{\dot{A_t}}{A_t}=(\alpha {A_t^{-\beta}})\cdot S_t$$

但为什么会这样呢：是的，下一步在 absolute terms 上是 harder 的：CERN 比 Curie's lab 更 complex。但我们现在的 tools 和 technologies 也让 build 更 complex 的 tools、attempt 更 complex 的 projects 变得更容易：我们并不是用 Curies 当年可用的相同手段来 attempting to build colliders，现在我们有 computers 和更好的 materials。

[为什么](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1188102372561752065) projects 的 difficulty 就必须比我们用来 attempt 它们的 tools 的 capacity grow faster 呢？我还没有一个好的 explanation！

但无论如何，我认为更 practical 的做法是 study 每个 field，看看其中正在发生什么。尽管听起来很难，我认为，要回答某个 science 或 engineering 的 field “有多 promising 或 healthy”，比回答那些关于 progress curves 必须 shaped 成某种样子的 abstract theoretical questions 更容易，也更 practical。知道 US TFP 需要越来越多的 resources 才能维持下去，并不会立即 lead to 一个 solution，但知道是什么 preventing airplanes from flying faster，[确实](https://boomsupersonic.com/)会 [lead](https://www.reactionengines.co.uk/) 到 [solutions](https://www.hermeus.com/)。这对任何 Progress Studies researcher 来说，都是一项值得做的任务！

_Ricón, José Luis, “Are ideas getting harder to find?”, Nintil (2020-11-23), available at https://nintil.com/ideas-harder-find/._

## 原文

**_Are ideas getting harder to find? (2020)_** by Nicholas Bloom, Chad Jones, John van Reenen and Michael Webb repeatedly pops up in discussions of technological progress, the great stagnation and related topics. They take a different angle from the usual, rather than debating whether there is stagnation or not in a range of metrics (Like TFP, Moore's law, crop yields), they instead opt for taking the optimistic view (That those metrics are advancing as usual, in some of their models) and calculate instead how much does it cost to get there by various means (like R&D expenditures). The result: The US is spending more to get the same level of yearly progress, hence ideas are indeed getting harder to find. And not just the US, a later followup ([Böeing and Hünermund, 2020](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/222264/1/1703784650.pdf)) applying the same methodology found the same thing in Germany and China looking at a panel of firms.

I was in a dinner once where at some point I was asked if there's a great stagnation, I answered that that's the wrong question to ask. Whether ideas are getting harder to find is in the came category. It's too high level to be operationally useful. Given the goal of accelerating progress, one has to look at each field and see what the roadblocks facing the field are, and how much would it cost to remove them, for what return. This assessment is the same regardless of whether we regard a field as stagnant, accelerating, or in a business as usual stage.

So what does the paper do? They assume that $$\frac{\dot{A_t}}{A_t}=\alpha S_t$$

where $\alpha$ is a measure of research productivity, while $S_t$ is the number of researchers (with some adjustments) and $\frac{\dot{A_t}}{A_t}$ is growth in TFP, which relates to new ideas. You are probably thinking various things:

1.  The relation is not linear, maybe one should introduce a correction term that accounts for reduced productivity due to duplication of research. They thought about that: that weakens the result but it still shows up)
2.  What R&D should they measure. Good point! The authors admits that this is tricky, and the reason why they didn't chose trickier case studies as the human genome project or light (A classic example from Nordhaus). But even then there may be issues. Later I show that no matter what R&D metric you choose, the result is the same.
3.  The equation assumes exponential growth! Well they do, hence they concede that the paper may well be "Is exponential growth getting harder to achieve?". In many sectors this is what you see and I won't object to using an exponential model for Moore's law. But as I remarked [elsewhere](https://nintil.com/no-great-technological-stagnation/), some technologies improve (at least for some times) superexponentially, and others linearly. In any case as I will be focusing on Moore's law I won't quibble much with this. I think exponential, or rather logistic, is a reasonable model for growth in most cases within a given paradigm.
4.  The authors also consider the case where productivity in a given field gets stuck yet new fields get invented that allow aggregate productivity to continue to improve (The model I had [here](https://nintil.com/on-the-constancy-of-the-rate-of-gdp-growth/) where exploiting a paradigm is like mining, you need to keep opening new mines as each mine has finite knowledge). This is why they look at the US as a whole. This is reasonable.
5.  You may also think that the number of researchers is artificially inflated because academic institutions keep getting flooded by lowly paid grunts that do not really contribute. One could make this case for the aggregate economy but not for the Moore's law case as they consider exclusively the semiconductor _industry_.
6.  Another choice they make is to deflate R&D expenditures by the prevailing wage rate. In the semiconductor world a lot of R&D plausibly is capital expenditures, but they also address this in the "lab equipment section". Later I use CPI as a deflator and the result, once again, is unchanged.
7.  Or you may think that their assumption that "if expanding research involves employing people of lower talent, this will be properly measured by R&D spending." because better researchers are paid more. This is an interesting assumption! In Academia this may not be true, the grifter that got into Academia by inertia and just wants to stay afloat gets paid as much (And [not much](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-020-03191-7?fbclid=IwAR0uWnjdlCGQrU-ak_fttTYb4pNdQ6UVYmlAVbaHOciCWVaxGL7FXhAses4)) as the bright one who is inventing the next iteration of batteries. At the very top sure, top PIs may get extra money from patents, board seats, or their own startups. But! Remember, they looked at industry, this critique applies for their aggregate numbers, not the Moore's law one. Surely industry imposes some discipline on semiconductor companies, forcing pay to go more in line with productivity?

Moore's law is a good example they discuss in the paper. It's well documented, the semiconductor world is not that big so it's possible to know who is up to what, and in the paper they consider the most optimistic case of the law: transistor count. If you remember from my post on [Moore's law](https://nintil.com/progress-semicon/), the diagram at the end shows that indeed transistor count seems to be rising exponentially, with everything else slowing down (But still growing!). So if they can show that even with transistor count research productivity is going down, then a fortiori for other ways of interpreting Moore's law (Like single thread performance or computations per dollar), those will also show a slowdown. All the data they used is available in the online appendix so we can look at it (I got a copy [here](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1RcODXzatE5t8BLbFq6ZfPR7XHWHhz2pBLjXc8zt8OxQ/edit?usp=sharing) with some of my own calculations in Sheet 1). What does R&D expenditure look like, both for semiconductor companies and companies supplying tooling to said companies like [ASML or Trumpf](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NHSR6AHNiDs). Out of all the semiconductor companies, should you include everything in your analysis? The authors try some variations (Table 1) including just Intel, or Intel+AMD. Including Intel dominates the results, drastically driving down productivity growth.

![image-20201120164716972](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201120164716972.png)

They don't use this number in the final table, they use their baseline result which is around the middle of the other estimates. But this immediately seems interesting: Could it be that it is Intel that is driving down the results? What if it's not that ideas are getting harder to find, but that some companies are getting worse at it? This may not be a general explanation. In physics, I do think the low hanging fruit theory fits well the data. But not here. This is what expenditures look like (Numbers are not adjusted for inflation, but serve for relative comparisons)

![image-20201120165302596](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201120165302596.png)

R&D expenditures in the semiconductor industry. Data from the supplement to Are Ideas Getting Harder to find

Indeed Intel by far dominates the R&D expenditures landscape. The next line is the sum of all the equipment companies. We then have Qualcomm and then Analog Devices, then Broadcom, and then finally TSMC. Bunching together all these companies seem questionable. A foundry and Broadcom? Has Broadcom ever put a chip on the Moore's law chart? Has Analog Devices?) Where's AMD? Where's NVIDIA and their stream of state of the art GPUs? Why is ASML in the semiconductor category instead of in the semiconductor equipment one? AMD just launched this year the best desktop processors there are (Along with Apple's M1). We just got the RTX 3090. There's also Apple's [astonishingly great](https://daringfireball.net/2020/11/the_m1_macs) M1 but teasing out Apple's semiconductor R&D from other R&D is harder so I won't be looking into that here.

![image-20201120172012436](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201120172012436.png)

From 1996 to 2015, Intel increased their R&D by a factor of 6.7. AMD did so by 2.36. AMD is not just AMD, they now make their chips at TSMC while Intel is an integrated fab+design shop. So let's do the following: Let's take ASML+the semiconductor equipment companies, add that to both AMD and Intel, then add TSMC to just AMD, and compare those two numbers. We get this:

![image-20201120174008620](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201120174008620.png)

So it looks like Intel has been spending a lot quite recently, and not so for AMD. Maybe AMD started spending after this and that's where we got Zen3 from? But no, AMD's own R&D has stayed [flat](https://www.macrotrends.net/stocks/stock-comparison?s=research-development-expenses&axis=single&comp=INTC:AMD). Anecdotically, going by my favorite metric, performance (According to geekbench for convenience), AMD has been able to go from the 587 points of the FX-4330 in 2016 to the 3562 of the latest Ryzens, an increase of 6x. Intel achieved a 1.5x improvement in the same period. Granted, one may say that Intel was a the frontier of technology until now and maybe AMD has been able to benefit from Intel's knowledge. That's fair; going forward it will remain to be seem if AMD R&D will rise to Intel's level and beyond. But for now the fact remains: AMD has been able to improve their chips substantially more per $ than Intel. You can see the same broader point with some charts [here](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2020/11/a-history-of-intel-vs-amd-desktop-performance-with-cpu-charts-galore/) using different tests. I am not doing here transistors but single thread performance as it's an easier figure to come by, and reflects more faithfully what the industry is optimizing for (Besides thermal performance).

One may object to my analysis that I'm just throwing away everyone else, has all the money that others companies put into semiconductors done nothing? No, it has led to their own chips and designs, but it's plausible that at the bleeding edge of design indeed that what the others have been doing is irrelevant. In fact if we just talk about cramming more components into less space and forget about design, then we are looking just at TSMC and the supplier companies (Or Intel plus suppliers), in which case their expenditure increased 15x since 1995 (When the TSMC time series begins) to 2015, a figure closer to AMD's cost increase than the Intel's increase. Moore's law implies a 35% increase every year in transistor count; so in that period that's 404x more transistors.

This all is, however, not exactly what the paper does; additionally we have to take the expenditure and divide by the wages of highly skilled labor to account for inflation. When doing comparisons as I have done the denominator does not matter (So the seeming inefficiency of Intel which drives down the industry's research productivity is still there). But when redoing the calculation of absolute changes in research efficiency it does. The corresponding file is RNDSalaries.mat (The legitimacy of using US wage data to deflate companies in Taiwan and Europe is left as an exercise to the reader), and from there we get that the change in effective researchers was of 8.11 which puts us in the narrow estimate from the paper: Their result stands.

What would it take for ideas not to be getting harder to find? Well, for one in a simplified model where the only R&D costs is researchers' wages, then the number of researchers must be constant!. In a model closer to the paper where we require that $$\tilde{S_t}=\frac{RD_t}{Wage_t}=\tilde{S_{t'}}=\frac{RD_{t'}}{Wage_{t'}}$$

$$\tilde{S_{1995}}=\frac{81.16}{48450}=\tilde{S_{2015}}=\frac{RD_{1995}}{92898}$$

So the 2015 R&D expenditure would have to be 155M$ instead of 1263M$, ~8.11 times less. Either 8.11 less money, or the same researchers, that's the conclusion from the model. And this is in a more optimistic case (Where I am only counting TSMC and manufacturers). The numbers do not go away if we instead deflate R&D by inflation instead of by reserachers wages. Recall, the TSMC et al. expenditure increase was 15x since 1995 to 2015. In that period, the cumulative rate of inflation [was](https://www.usinflationcalculator.com/) 55.5%, meaning that $1 in 1995 is ~ $1.8 now. So the actual expenditure increase would have been 15/1.8=8.33, roughly the same figure we get if we use wages.

One may also retort with: Oh but chip companies are worried about power efficiency and other things. Yes, but that's a design issue: I have chosen companies that do not design semiconductors, just make them (TSMC et al.) to separate between cramming transistors and designing how they will be arranged.

So to recap: Regardless of how one deflates the numbers, or which firms one takes for the calculations the results hold. Given the broad class of models that underlie the paper, I think there is no plausible slicing of the data that yields constant productivity. So in the same way as [Bem's psychic ability meta-analysis](https://slatestarcodex.com/2014/04/28/the-control-group-is-out-of-control/) the paper could be taken not at face value implying that research productivity has gone down; rather one could conclude that **we are doing fine historically and it's the models that are wrong**. Think about it: This is not about science changing in 1970 or science doing better in the 1930s, the trends look relatively constant for the aggregate TFP and researchers calculations. **Science has always been like this**. Or so it seems from the data in the paper, I'm not saying it's all fine!:

![image-20201120174312357](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-20-ideas-harder-find/image-20201121085946158.png)

[Scott Alexander](https://slatestarcodex.com/2018/11/26/is-science-slowing-down-2/) pointed out as much:

> constant progress in science in response to exponential increases in inputs ought to be our null hypothesis, and that it’s almost inconceivable that it could ever be otherwise.

This is alternative 3 from this list he gives of potential explanations for the observed data:

1.  Only top researchers produce productivity-raising ideas; if so increasing researcher count beyond those elites is inefficient.
2.  The current academic system is an order of magnitude worse than the one from times past at producing useful research
3.  We've picked the low-hanging fruit and it gets progressively harder to keep picking it. (Scott's preferred explanation; [Matt Clancy](https://mattsclancy.substack.com/p/are-ideas-getting-harder-to-find) points to a sub-variant of this, that these new ideas require harder work in the form of learning more than before)

GDP growth is exponential, as is population growth, as well as (for successful companies) market capitalization. That R&D expenditures increase exponentially is no surprise. The worry would be if R&D expenditures become a large share of GDP but that seems a very remote possibility right now.

One may be tempted to just go ahead and say that the right model should be something like what the authors suggest, where indeed the growth of "ideas" goes down as we already have lots of them in stock, a semi-endogenous model that looks like:

$$\frac{\dot{A_t}}{A_t}=(\alpha {A_t^{-\beta}})\cdot S_t$$

but why would that be: Yes, the next step is harder in absolute terms: CERN is more complex than the Curie's lab. But our tools and technologies now make it easier to build more complex tools and attempt more complex projects: We are not attempting to build colliders with the same means available to the Curies, now we have computers and better materials.

[Why](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1188102372561752065) would it be the case that the difficulty of the projects has to grow faster than the capacity of the tools we have to attempt them? I don't have a good explanation yet!

But in any case I think it's more practical to study each field and see what's going on with it. As hard as it sounds, I think "How promising or healthy" a particular field of science or engineering is easier and more practical to answer than abstract theoretical questions about the necessity of progress curves to be shaped in a certain way. Knowing that US TFP requires more and more resources to keep going does not immediately lead to a solution, but knowing what is preventing airplanes from flying faster [does](https://boomsupersonic.com/) [lead](https://www.reactionengines.co.uk/) to [solutions](https://www.hermeus.com/). A worthy task for any Progress Studies researcher!
