# 在飞机上睡觉

---

## 正文

我经常因工作而旅行。[Wave](https://wave.com/) 是一家 distributed company，我们每两个月举办一次为期一周的 company retreats，地点经常在 Africa。此外，我还花了很多时间在 Ethiopia 实地工作。去年我的飞行次数居然足以获得 United 的 frequent flyer status，不过只是勉强达到，而且还没到能享受特别好 perks 的 level。

能到世界各地看看实在太棒了，而更棒的是，我找到了很多办法，让自己的旅行尽可能轻松。下面是我会做的一些关键事情：

## 在飞机上睡觉

对于 long-distance travel，我以前总是避开 redeye flights，因为我总会睡不好，第二天精疲力竭。但后来我意识到，如果把一整天都花在飞机上，我还是会因为 jetlag 而睡不好，第二天照样精疲力竭——这样就会损失两天，而坐 redeye 只会损失一天。

事实上，乘坐 redeye 并一路睡过去，通常能让我完全摆脱 jetlag。2017 年 1 月，我每周都跨越相差八小时的 timezones，却几乎完全没有受到 jetlag 的困扰。（当然，这肯定有一部分是因为我年轻，但安排 flights 的时间，让自己总能在睡了很久之后于早晨抵达，确实起到了很大的作用。）

幸运的是，行驶中的交通工具很容易让我睡着，不过我还会做下面这些事，让入睡更容易：

-   我总是选 window seat，这样就能把头靠在机舱壁上。

-   我货比三家，找到了一副舒服到戴着也能睡觉的 in-ear headphones。[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/travel/#fn:1) 想睡觉时，我就用它们播放 [brown noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownian_noise)，屏蔽附近可能出现的婴儿尖叫声。

-   在 international flights 上，所有免费饮品我都会要。视时间安排而定，我还会在出发当天让自己进入 caffeine withdrawal。

-   几乎所有自称“sleep masks”的东西都会勒疼我的耳后，而且一动头就掉。但我终于发现，[一段 tube of wool](http://www.buffwear.com/buff-adult-headwear/wool-buff) 用来做这个非常好用，而且还有额外好处：它也很适合当帽子、小毛巾、吃东西时的垫布、bandage，或用来做其他任何需要一块布的事情。

-   我有一个 memory-foam 的 [Cabeau Evolution](https://www.cabeau.com/products/evolution-travel-neck-pillow/) pillow，[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/travel/#fn:2) 它比 standard bean-filled neck pillows 更 supportive，也更 compressible。我通常会把它和航空公司提供的 head pillows 搭配使用（再加上前面提到的 window seat），让自己充分舒服起来。

-   出发前，我会把手机时钟设为新的 timezone（注意：千万别因此把自己的 departure time 搞混了！）。我会尽量在接近新 timezone 的就寝时间时服用 melatonin，并在适当的时间吃饭，以 reset 我的 circadian rhythm。

## 对一切都保持 robust：遇到什么都能应付

旅行时，各种小状况层出不穷，所以我带的很多东西都是为了让小意外尽可能 recoverable。如果你主要在 developed world 旅行，大概不必做到我这么彻底，不过这些办法可能仍然有用：

-   我会带 odor resistant、容易 hand wash 的衣服，再加上一条小型 travel clothesline 和一条毛巾。我洗澡时会顺便洗 base layers，然后让它们在夜里晾干。这意味着，除了 running water，我不依赖任何 hygiene amenities，而且能轻装旅行，因为我不需要带多件厚重的 pants/overshirts，其他东西也都只需带几件，除非行程真的很长。

-   我很依赖 electronics，所以我会确保所有小件都有 backups：两个 universal adapters，每种 cable（MicroUSB 和 Lightning）各两条，等等。我还会带一个容量足够大的 spare battery，能给 laptop 提供相当可观的 extra charge。

-   有了 offline maps app，我几乎可以完全避免迷路。我更喜欢 OpenStreetMap client（我目前用的是 [Galileo](https://galileo-app.com/)），因为 Google Maps 在 Africa 的 coverage 往往不好，而且 OSM clients 的 interface 更方便为重要 locations 添加 pins。

-   我会带少量的多种 medicine（ibuprofen、Nyquil、antacids 和 zinc；我大概还应该加点应对 nausea 的药）；还会带很多 protein bars，以防被困在附近找不到食物的地方。

-   我的 Kindle 里存着 Terry Pratchett 的全集，这意味着，在意料之外的漫长等待中，我基本上从不会无聊。假设你在 ebooks 还没流行起来之前买过一堆 Terry Pratchett 的实体书，我觉得你就有合理的 moral case，可以通过某种方式 pirating 那些 electronic copies，这样就不至于贵得让人负担不起。

-   而这些基本上就是我带的_全部_东西了，这意味着我从来不需要 checked bag。这很重要，因为只要我 check a bag，差不多有一半的时候它都会莫名其妙地 delayed。（在这方面，我可能比大多数人更容易碰上意外。）不带 checked bag 还能省下不少钱，以及在 baggage claim 等待的大量时间。

## 尽量减少压力、匆忙和麻烦

如果我信奉 junk psychology，我就会告诉你，因为 [peak/end rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak%E2%80%93end_rule)，让 return flight 轻松愉快很重要。那个结论并不能 replicate，但我确实觉得，旅行中压力最大的几个环节——收拾行李和坐飞机——对我如何记住这趟旅行有着不成比例的影响。因此，下面是几个让这些环节更轻松的办法：

-   对我来说，最重要的是好用的 luggage。我（还有多得令人意外的一群朋友！）用的是[这个 Osprey backpack](https://www.ospreypacks.com/us/en/product/farpoint-55-FARPNT55.html)，拉开拉链就能把它分成一个 carry-on-sized main suitcase 和一个 personal-item-sized day pack。它也很 maneuverable，上下楼梯时容易携带，不像 roller backs，而且还能兼作 hiking backpack。

-   Osprey pack 里面的 internal divisions 不多，这意味着你需要一些 packing cubes 来 organizing。在自己买了一些之前，我_实在_低估了它们有多好用。现在，我不必记住自己带的每一件东西，只需要做两件容易得多的事：(a) 确保所有 packing cubes 都在；(b) 把所有东西放在正确的 packing cube 里。它也让 pack 和 unpack 快得多，因为可以把东西装在 cubes 里一起移动。

-   我确保总把重要 documents（主要是 passport 和各种 tickets）放在完全相同的位置——途中放在 overshirt breast pocket 里，其他时候放在 backpack 的一个固定口袋里。这意味着，我只需检查一个口袋，就知道有没有丢失它们；也意味着，我不必花那么多时间心里没底地盼着东西都带齐了，而能有更多时间踏踏实实地安心。

-   我把所有听着最开心的音乐整理成一个 playlist，只要在途中而又不打算睡觉，就会反复听。它对我心情的改善之大，出乎意料。

## 原文

I travel a lot for work. [Wave](https://wave.com/) is a distributed company, and we have week-long company retreats once every two months, often in Africa. On top of that, I’ve spent a lot of time working on the ground in Ethiopia. I actually flew enough last year to qualify for frequent flyer status on United, though only barely and not at a level with particularly good perks.

Getting to see the world is super awesome, but it’s even more awesome because I’ve found a lot of ways to make traveling as easy for myself as possible. Here’s some of the key stuff I do:

## Sleep on the plane

For long-distance travel, I used to avoid redeye flights because I’d always sleep badly and be exhausted the next day. But then I realized that if I spent the whole day on the plane, I’d sleep badly because of jetlag and be exhausted the next day anyway—so I’d lose two days, compared to one for a redeye.

In fact, I can usually get over jetlag completely by taking a redeye and sleeping for all of it. In January 2017 I changed timezones by eight hours every week and barely suffered from jetlag at all. (Part of this is definitely because I’m young, of course, but timing my flights so I always arrived in the morning after lots of sleep made a huge difference.)

I’m fortunate that moving vehicles put me to sleep really easily, but here are some things I do to make it even easier:

-   I always get a window seat so I can rest my head against the plane wall.

-   I shopped around for a pair of in-ear headphones comfortable enough to sleep in.[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/travel/#fn:1) When I’m trying to sleep I play [brown noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownian_noise) through them, to screen out the sound of any screaming babies that might be nearby.

-   I always take all the free drinks on international flights. Depending on the timing, I’ll also give myself caffeine withdrawal on the day I leave.

-   Almost all things that market themselves as “sleep masks” hurt me behind the ears and fall off when I move my head. But I finally discovered that [a tube of wool](http://www.buffwear.com/buff-adult-headwear/wool-buff) works great for this and, as a bonus, also works great as a hat, mini-towel, eating surface, bandage, or anything else you might want a piece of cloth for.

-   I have a memory-foam [Cabeau Evolution](https://www.cabeau.com/products/evolution-travel-neck-pillow/) pillow,[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/travel/#fn:2) which is both more supportive and more compressible than standard bean-filled neck pillows. I usually combine it with the airline-provided head pillows (and the aforesaid window seat) for full comfort.

-   I set my phone clock to the new timezone before I leave (note: be sure you don’t confuse yourself about your departure time this way!). I try to take melatonin close to my bedtime in the new timezone, and eat meals at the appropriate time, to reset my circadian rhythm.

## Be robust to everything

Lots of minor things go wrong when I’m traveling, so a lot of the things I take with me are geared towards making small mishaps as recoverable as possible. If you’re traveling mostly in the developed world, you probably don’t need to be as hardcore about this, but it may still be useful:

-   I take clothes that are odor resistant and easy to hand wash, plus a small travel clothesline and towel. I wash my base layers in the shower and dry them overnight. This means I don’t depend on any hygiene amenities except running water, and I can travel much lighter since I don’t need multiples of bulky pants/overshirts, or more than a few of anything else unless the trip will be really long.

-   I depend a lot on electronics, so I make sure I have backups of everything small: two universal adapters, two of each cable (MicroUSB and Lightning), and so on. I also have a spare battery big enough to give my laptop a fair amount of extra charge.

-   I can almost completely avoid getting lost with an offline maps app. I prefer an OpenStreetMap client (I currently use [Galileo](https://galileo-app.com/)) because Google Maps often has poor coverage in Africa and the OSM clients have an easier interface for adding pins for important locations.

-   I carry small amounts of a lot of different kinds of medicine (ibuprofen, Nyquil, antacids, and zinc; I should probably add something for nausea); also lots of protein bars in case I’m stuck somewhere without food nearby.

-   I have the complete works of Terry Pratchett on my Kindle, which means I’m pretty much never bored during long unexpected waits. Hypothetically, if you were to purchase a bunch of physical Terry Pratchett books before ebooks became a thing, I think you would have a reasonable moral case for pirating the electronic copies somehow, which would make this not prohibitively expensive.

-   And that’s basically _all_ I carry, which means I don’t ever need a checked bag. That’s important because about half the time I do check a bag, it somehow gets delayed. (I’m probably more accident-prone than most people here.) Skipping the checked bag also saves a bunch of money and lots of time at baggage claim.

## Minimize stress/rushing/hassle

If I were into junk psychology, I would tell you that making your return flight relaxing is important because of the [peak/end rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak%E2%80%93end_rule). That doesn’t replicate, but I do feel like the few most stressful parts of my trips—packing and flights—have an outsized impact on how I remember them. So here are a few ways to make that easier:

-   The most important thing for me is good luggage. I (and a surprising number of friends!) use [this Osprey backpack](https://www.ospreypacks.com/us/en/product/farpoint-55-FARPNT55.html), which zips apart into a carry-on-sized main suitcase and personal-item-sized day pack. It’s also maneuverable and easy to carry up or down stairs, unlike roller backs, and doubles as a hiking backpack.

-   The Osprey pack doesn’t have very many internal divisions, which means you’ll need some packing cubes for organizing. I _seriously_ underestimated how nice these were until I bought some. Instead of keeping track of everything I brought with me, I now have two much easier tasks: (a) make sure I have all my packing cubes; (b) keep everything inside the right packing cube. It also makes it much faster to pack and unpack, because you can move things around in the cubes.

-   I make sure always to put my important documents (mainly my passport and any tickets) in exactly the same place—inside an overshirt breast pocket while I’m in transit, and inside a particular backpack pocket otherwise. This means I only have to check one pocket to know whether I’ve lost them, which means I spend less time vaguely hoping I have everything with me, and more time with my mind securely at rest.

-   I put together a playlist of all the music that makes me happiest to listen to, and I listen to it on repeat whenever I’m in transit and not trying to sleep. This made a surprising amount of difference to my mood level.
