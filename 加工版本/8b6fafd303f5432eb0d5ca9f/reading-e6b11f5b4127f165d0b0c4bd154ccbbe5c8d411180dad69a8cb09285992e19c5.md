## 引言

_关于 adhan，也就是 Islamic call to prayer 的一些个人感想。这些笔记非常业余——我并不是_ _religious scholar，对 Islam 也非常无知_ _（对另外两个高度相关的话题——Arabic language 和 Christianity——也同样如此。）_ _不过，要理解，总得迈出第一步。_ _欢迎比我更有知识的人纠正其中的 misconceptions！_ _我也努力以一种_ _尊重的方式来写——如果我有什么粗鲁失礼之处，请告诉我，好让我改正。_

我最近第一次去了 Istanbul。这是我去过的第一个 majority-Muslim 城市——超过 90% 的人口 identifies as Muslim，约有一半是 practicing。这也是我第一次亲身体验 adhan，也就是每天会被 recited 五次的 Islamic call to prayer。

如果你从未亲身体验过 adhan，那么你的第一次体验很可能是这样的：你正走在街上，空气中突然充满了美妙的 singing，从听得到的每一座 Mosque 的 minarets 传来。“God 是 greatest！God 是 greatest！我 testify，除了 God，没有任何事物 worthy of worship。”它持续数分钟，以美妙的 musicality 和 devotion 唱出。当然，recordings 无法完整呈现这种体验，但能让人略窥一二[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/adhan/index.html#fn1)：

听这段 recording 时，请想象它从 Istanbul 超过 2,000 座 mosques 中传出，在整座城市回响，充满大街小巷；这献给 God 的 music，是对 transcendental 的共同 celebration 和 affirmation。我在其他 cultures 中还从未有过类似的体验。它令人难忘。

令我震撼的，部分是 adhan 在整座城市、乃至同一 timezone 中 creates 的 shared beauty 体验。但这些 words 还 created 了一种 shared intellectual experience。Islam 的不同 branches 中，adhan 的 words 存在一些 variation，不过粗略 translation 成 English 如下：

```

God is greatest
God is greatest
God is greatest
God is greatest
I testify there is nothing worthy of worship except God
I testify there is nothing worthy of worship except God
I testify Muhammad is the messenger of God
I testify Muhammad is the messenger of God
Come to prayer
Come to prayer
Come to success
Come to success
God is greatest
God is greatest
There is nothing worthy of worship except God
```

被一种 emotion 打动是一回事；被一种与这种 emotion 相契合的 idea 或一组 ideas 打动，则是另一回事，而且归根结底要有力得多。adhan 有力地 expresses 了 ideas 和 emotion。

我写这些笔记，是为了加深自己对 adhan 的理解。

我曾 baptized 为 Catholic，在 7 岁时成为 atheist，此后一直是 atheist。虽然我“believe in \[其价值的\] belief”，但正如 Alan Watts 所指出的，这与真正 believing 完全不是一回事。我只在 Christian-dominated cultures 中生活过，分别是在 Australia、Canada 和 US。尽管我是 atheist，但从某种意义上说，我在 culturally 仍是 Christian。我对 Islam 极其无知，不会说 Arabic，只能依靠 third-party sources 和体谅我的 Muslim 朋友来纠正 misapprehensions。我知道，人们通常会说“笔记中任何剩余的 errors 都由我负责”，但这一次，我对这一点的认识格外深刻！

## adhan 的各个 lines

我将讨论的 adhan variation，据 Wikipedia 所述，是 Hanifa Sunni School 使用的版本。我相信，这是 Sunni schools 中最大的一个，也是现代 Turkey 最普遍的一个。不过，Islam 的不同 branches 中，adhan 存在 variations。这些 variations 很值得研究。但为了限制范围，我只 focused on 一个 version。另外，我考虑的是 English language translation，这显然也是一大 limitation。先迈小步！

**“God 是 greatest”：**这个非同寻常的 statement 将 God places 于当地大人物之上——无论是你的 Sultan、King、Lord 还是 Master。更重要的是，它在整个 community 中 creates 了 God 高于这些地方权势的 common knowledge！这是极其有力的 act。它也将 God places 于当地 Imam 之上。它将 God places 于 self 之上。高于你自己的 success。高于 family。高于朋友。高于 Earth。高于 Universe。高于 wealth。高于 status。高于 fame。高于 love。高于 sex。高于 pleasure。而且，再说一次，和所有 lines 一样，它正在使这些 facts 成为整个 community 的 common knowledge。

**Repetition（重复）：**“God 是 greatest”这个 line 被 repeated 两次。其他许多 lines 也被 repeated，通常是两次，有时是四次。单是 repetition 这个事实就极其有趣。在普通 writing 中，repetition 往往是思考懒惰的表现，好的 writers（和 speakers）会努力 eliminate repetition。但伟大的 writers 和 speakers 会把 repetition，尤其是非常明显的 repetition，当作强调重要内容的工具。想想 Martin Luther King 一次又一次声如洪钟地喊出“我有一个梦想！”这个梦想也开始成为我们的梦想。adhan 中的 repetition 告诉我们，没有任何 inconsequential lines。它几乎具有 hypnotic 的性质，或者说——这是很不一样的另一回事——具有 meditative 的性质。它如此明显，如此刻意，以至于我发现，自己不得不与 repetition 这个事实形成某种关系。

当然，这些 local repetitions 本身又处于更大的 repetition 之中。adhan 每天都会被 recited 五次，日日如此。一年就是超过 1,800 次。到 10 岁生日时，一位 Muslim 已经听过超过 18,000 次 adhan！据 Wikipedia 所述，adhan 常常是对 newborn baby 说出的第一段话。它也可能在其他重要的人生时刻被 recited。简而言之，adhan 是生活中扎根极深的一部分，就像太阳在天空中升起一样。

这或许会带来一些问题——我想知道，这些 words 会在多大程度上变得习以为常：听见了，却没有真正倾听，或许也从未真正消化。我不知道。我很想听听在 Islamic cultures 中生活过的人怎么说：他们觉得，与这些 words 保持有意义的联系有多容易？在 Christianity 中，最接近的 analogue 或许是 Lord's Prayer。我的印象是，许多 Christians 确实会将 Lord's Prayer 背下来，却很少停下来深入思索它究竟在说什么。要对经常 repeated 的事物始终保持鲜活的投入，是一项挑战，就像 mantra meditation 一样。

**“我 testify，除了 God，没有任何事物 worthy of worship”：**这与第一个 line 相似，却又有新的 overtones。它 establishes 的是：God 不仅 greater than 其他一切，其他任何事物甚至都无法与之接近。你不应 worship Muhammad 或 Sultan，当然更不应 worship money、你的 self 或你自己的 success。不，God 高于其他一切。

**“我 testify，Muhammad 是 God 的 messenger”：**有趣的是这个 line 没有说什么。它没有说 Muhammad 是 God 唯一的 messenger（不过，也许这是 implied 的？）；它也没有将 Muhammad 与 Quran associate 起来；事实上，adhan 中并未提到 Quran。不过，它确实通过 Muhammad 这个人，将 God 与世间某个十分具体的存在 tie 在了一起。否则，adhan 几乎可以被理解为适用于任何 monotheistic religion。

**“来 prayer”：**这是 call-to-action，也是 adhan 当下的 functional purpose。下一个 line 有着相同的 form，却不是 call-to-action：

**“来 success”：**我很欣赏其中微妙的 implication：success 是前来 prayer 的 consequence。这个 line 本身的 ambiguous 很有意思——我真希望自己懂 Arabic，能够充分理解其中的 connotations，尽管我怀疑 ambiguity 仍然存在。在 English 中，这个 line 可能 imply 许多不同的事情。我认识一些人，他们用“success”来表示“make money”、“achieve status and recognition”或“make an impact on the world”[2](https://michaelnotebook.com/adhan/index.html#fn2)，而另一些人的 success 观念可能 encompass 他们的 community、其他 living things，或与 God 的 alignment。不过，无论确切 meaning 是什么，这个 line 都是一个 focusing element，尤其当“success”的 meaning 并非人们原本就会 instinctually 持有的 meaning 时。

**“God 是 greatest”和“除了 God，没有任何事物 worthy of worship”：**回过头来 reiterate 开头提出的 points，这是通过 repetition 进行 emphasis 的另一种方式，尽管此时 context 已经改变。

## 讨论

**与 Christ 的 greatest commandment 的 Comparison：**我在上文提到过它与 Lord's Prayer 的 similarity。它与 Jesus 所说的 first and greatest commandment 也有一些 similarity：“用你全部的 heart、全部的 soul，以及全部的 mind 来 Love 这个 Lord God……Love God above all else。”这条 commandment 用“love”替换了 adhan 中的“worship”，并额外要求以三种不同的方式来 love（用你全部的 heart、全部的 soul，以及全部的 mind）。我觉得用“love”替换“worship”这一 replacement[3](https://michaelnotebook.com/adhan/index.html#fn3) 很耐人寻味；后者或许是一种更 equal 的 relationship。“Love God above all else”也与“God 是 greatest”颇为 similar，不过，其中的 God 被呈现为本身就 intrinsically worthy of 那种 love，而不是因为其具有任何 property，比如是“greatest”。

**“浪费”的时间，以及 disengagement 的挑战：**我听过 non-Muslims 有时出声疑惑，人们怎么能每天 praying 五次，这样“浪费”时间。也可以把这个问题反过来，想想提问者会不会只是不理解 prayer（或者不太擅长 prayer）。有个老笑话，讲的是一位 master：当被问及时间紧张时是否会调整每天一小时的 meditation practice，他回答：“会的，时间紧张时，我必须每天 meditate 两小时，才能把所有事情都安排进去。”我怀疑 prayer 也有类似的道理。不过，正如上文提到的，我想知道怎样才能避免 adhan 和 prayers 变得 rote？相比之下，Christian 的 prayer 观念往往更 personal，也更像是在与 God 进行 conversational 交流[4](https://michaelnotebook.com/adhan/index.html#fn4)，以这种方式保持新鲜感或许更容易。另一方面，我怀疑，必须付出努力才能保持新鲜感，本身确实也有益处。

**adhan 的 structure：**它很简单：提醒人与 God 的 relationship（前两句）；Muhammad 作为 messenger 的 role；call to prayer；call to success；再次出现人与 God 的 relationship。更简单地说：adhan 提醒你自己与 God 的 relationship，提醒你 Muhammad 是 God 的 messenger，并立即发出 call to action。

**Variations（变体）：**下面是关于 adhan 的 hypothetical variations 的四个想法——我不是在具体提议采用它们，而是在考虑 speculative variations，以帮助加深我对现有 form 的理解。我想，这听起来或许有些 sacreligious，但它源于深深的欣赏，以及想要更好地理解的愿望。理解某个事物的最佳方式，往往就是考虑它在发生 varied 后会是什么样子。

1.  _用“love”来 Replace“worship”：_例如，把第二句 replace 为类似这样的话：“我 love God above all else”。我不确定其中哪一句显然更好，但它们确实暗示了与 God 的不同种类的 relationship。我在文化上属于 Christian 的头脑或许更偏爱“love”这种 formulation，但我不会对此作过多解读；这可能只是因为我更熟悉它。
2.  _从 assertion 转向 call-to-action：_例如，不说“我 testify，除了 God，没有任何事物 worthy of worship”，而改成：“Worship God，而不 worship 任何其他事物”。或者，在“love”的 formulation 中，“我 love God above all else”可以改成“Love God above all else”。不过，虽然我通常赞成尽可能采用 active phrasings，但这种做法的一个缺点是，它会分散整体的 call-to-action，从而使之不那么 clear。
3.  _timing 上的 Variation：_我想，是否可以通过某些 timing 方式，帮助人们以一种新鲜的方式重新投入 adhan？显然有很多做法；这里只举一个想法：或许每年可以有一个月，用另一种 form 的 call to prayer 来替换 adhan，让人们重新接触它时带着新鲜的心态，以不同的方式聆听它？
4.  _“来做 good deeds”：_据我了解，在一些 versions 中，这一句被 inserted 在“来获得 success”之后。我喜欢它：它迫使我们考虑，我们所说的 good deeds 是什么意思，以及它们可能如何与 prayer、success、God 和 Muhammad related。

这些笔记就写到这里。当然，还可以说很多。我还没有讨论 history、variation、musical elements、muezzin、recordings 和 amplification 在现代的 role，等等。或许留待以后吧！

## Introduction

_A few personal reflections on the adhan, the Islamic call to prayer. The notes are very amateur – I am not_ _a religious scholar, and am very ignorant of Islam_ _(and also of two other highly relevant topics, the Arabic language and Christianity.)_ _Still, in understanding one must take first steps._ _Corrections of misconceptions by those more knowledgeable than I are welcome!_ _I've also attempted to write in a way which is_ _respectful – if I've made any boorish missteps, let me know so I can fix them._

I recently visited Istanbul for the first time. It's the first majority-Muslim city I've visited – more than 90% of the population identifies as Muslim, and roughly half are practicing. It's also the first time I have experienced the adhan, the Islamic call to prayer, which is recited five times a day.

If you have never experienced the adhan, then your first experience may well go something like this: you will be walking down the street, when the air is suddenly filled with glorious singing, ringing out from the minarets of any Mosque within earshot. "God is greatest! God is greatest! I testify there is nothing worthy of worship except God." It continues on, lasting several minutes, sung with glorious musicality and devotion. Of course, the experience isn't captured by recordings, but they provide a hint[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/adhan/index.html#fn1):

As you listen to the recording, imagine it echoing across the entire city, from the more than 2,000 mosques in Istanbul, filling the streets, a shared celebration and affirmation of the transcendental, this music for God. There is nothing like it that I have yet experienced in other cultures. It is unforgettable.

I was struck in part by the experience of shared beauty the adhan creates, across the entire city, and indeed across the same timezone. But there is also the shared intellectual experience created by the words. There is some variation in the words of the adhan in different branches of Islam, but a rough translation into English is:

```

God is greatest
God is greatest
God is greatest
God is greatest
I testify there is nothing worthy of worship except God
I testify there is nothing worthy of worship except God
I testify Muhammad is the messenger of God
I testify Muhammad is the messenger of God
Come to prayer
Come to prayer
Come to success
Come to success
God is greatest
God is greatest
There is nothing worthy of worship except God
```

It is one thing to be captured by an emotion; it is another, and ultimately much more powerful, to be captured by an idea or set of ideas that are in concert with the emotion. The adhan expresses both ideas and emotion powerfully.

I wrote these notes to help deepen my understanding of the adhan.

I was baptized Catholic, became an atheist at age 7, and have remained an atheist ever since. While I "believe in \[the value of\] belief", as Alan Watts has pointed out that is not at all the same as actually believing. I have only ever lived in Christian-dominated cultures, in Australia, Canada, and the US. And although I am an atheist, there is of course a sense in which I am culturally a Christian. I am extremely ignorant of Islam, cannot speak Arabic, and am reliant on third-party sources and understanding Muslim friends to correct misapprehensions. I know it's conventional to say "Any remaining errors in the notes are my own", but in this case I'm unusually aware of the fact!

## The lines of the adhan

I will discuss the adhan in the variation that, according to Wikipedia, is used in the Hanifa Sunni School. This is, I believe, the largest of the Sunni schools, and is also the one most widespread in modern Turkey. However, there are variations in the adhan in different branches of Islam. Those variations would make a good study. But to limit my scope I have focused on a single version. It is also obviously a great limitation that I am considering an English language translation. Small steps!

**"God is greatest":** This remarkable statement places God above the local big shot – your Sultan or King or Lord or Master. What's more, it creates common knowledge among the entire community that God is above those local powers! That's an extremely powerful act. It also places God above the local Imam. It places God above self. Above your own success. Above family. Above friends. Above the Earth. Above the Universe. Above wealth. Above status. Above fame. Above love. Above sex. Above pleasure. And, again, as with all the lines, it is making these facts common knowledge among the entire community.

**Repetition:** The line "God is greatest" is repeated twice. Many of the other lines are also repeated, often twice, sometimes four times. The mere fact of the repetition is extremely interesting. In ordinary writing, repetition is often a sign of lazy thinking, and good writers (and speakers) work hard to eliminate repetition. But great writers and speakers will use repetition, especially very obvious repetition, as a tool to emphasize what is important. Consider Martin Luther King thundering over and over "I have a dream!" It starts to become our dream, too. The repetition in the adhan tells us that there are no inconsequential lines. It is almost hypnotic or – a very different thing – meditative in nature. It is so obvious, so deliberate, that I find myself forced into some relationship with the fact of the repetition.

Of course, those local repetitions are themselves within a larger repetition. The adhan is recited five times a day, every day. That's more than 1,800 times a year. By their 10th birthday, a Muslim will have heard the adhan more than 18,000 times! The adhan is, according to Wikipedia, often the first thing spoken to a newborn baby. And it may be recited at other important life moments. In short, the adhan is an incredibly deeply ingrained part of life, like the sun rising in the sky.

This perhaps creates some issues – I wonder to what extent the words become taken for granted, heard, but not truly listened to, perhaps never truly metabolized. I do not know. I'd love to hear from people who've lived in Islamic cultures how easy they find it to remain meaningfully connected to the words. In Christianity, the closest analogue is perhaps the Lord's Prayer, and my impression is that many Christians do learn the Lord's Prayer by heart, but rarely stop to deeply ponder what it is saying. To remain consistently, freshly engaged with something repeated often is challenging, like mantra meditation.

**"I testify there is nothing worthy of worship except God":** This is similar to the first line, but has novel overtones. It establishes that not only is God greater than everything else, nothing else even comes close. You aren't to worship Muhammad, or the Sultan, and certainly not money, or your self, or your own success. No, God is above all else.

**"I testify Muhammad is the messenger of God":** Interesting what is not in this line. It doesn't say Muhammad is the sole messenger of God (though perhaps it is implied?); nor does it associate Muhammad with the Quran; indeed, the Quran is not mentioned in the adhan. Still, it does tie God to something very specific in the world, in the person of Muhammad. The adhan otherwise could be taken to apply to almost any monotheistic religion.

**"Come to prayer":** This is the call-to-action, the immediate functional purpose of the adhan. The next line has the same form, but is not a call-to-action:

**"Come to success":** I appreciate the subtle implication that success is a consequence of coming to prayer. The line itself is interestingly ambiguous – I wish I knew Arabic and understood the connotations well, though I suspect the ambiguity persists. In English the line could imply many different things. I know people who use "success" to mean "make money" or "achieve status and recognition" or "make an impact on the world"[2](https://michaelnotebook.com/adhan/index.html#fn2), while others' notion of success may encompass their community, or other living things, or alignment with God. Still, no matter the exact meaning, this line is a focusing element, especially insofar as the meaning of "success" is a meaning that would otherwise not be held instinctually.

**"God is greatest" and "There is nothing worthy of worship except God":** Returning to reiterate the points made at the beginning, another type of emphasis through repetition, albeit now in a changed context.

## Discussion

**Comparison to Christ's greatest commandment:** I noted above the similarity to the Lord's Prayer. There is also some similarity to what Jesus described as the first and greatest commandment: "Love the Lord God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your mind… Love God above all else." This commandment replaces the "worship" of the adhan with "love", and makes additional injunctions to love in three separate ways (with all your heart, all your soul, and all your mind). I find the replacement[3](https://michaelnotebook.com/adhan/index.html#fn3) of "worship" with "love" fascinating; the latter is, perhaps, a more equal relationship. "Love God above all else" is also quite similar to "God is greatest", although God is presented as simply being intrinsically worthy of that love, not because of any property they have, like being "greatest".

**"Wasted" time, and the challenge of disengagement:** I've heard non-Muslims sometimes wonder aloud how people can "waste" their time praying five times a day. One may invert this, and wonder if perhaps the questioner merely doesn't understand (or isn't very good at) prayer. There is an old joke about the master who, when asked if he modified his hour-a-day meditation practice when under time pressure, replies: "yes, when under time pressure I must meditate two hours a day in order to fit everything in". I suspect something similar is true of prayer. Still, as mentioned above, I wonder how to avoid the adhan and the prayers becoming rote? By comparison, the Christian notion of prayer tends to be more personal and conversational with God[4](https://michaelnotebook.com/adhan/index.html#fn4), and it's perhaps easier to maintain freshness in this way. On the other hand, I suspect there are real benefits to having to exert effort to keep things fresh.

**The structure of the adhan:** It's very simple: reminder of relationship to God (first two lines); Muhammad's role as messenger; call to prayer; call to success; recurrence of relationship to God. Even more simply: the adhan reminds you of your relationship to God, that Muhammad is God's messenger, and makes an immediate call to action.

**Variations:** Here's four ideas for hypothetical variations on the adhan – I'm not concretely proposing these, but rather considering speculative variations to help improve my understanding of the current form. I suppose this perhaps sounds sacreligious, but it is coming out of a place of deep appreciation, and a desire to better understand. Often the best way to understand something is to consider how it would be if varied.

1.  _Replace "worship" by "love":_ E.g., something replace the second line by something like: "I love God above all else". I'm not sure either is obviously a better line, but they do suggest a different kind of relationship to God. My culturally Christian mind perhaps prefers the "love" formulation, but I wouldn't read much into that; it may be just my familiarity.
2.  _Move from assertion to call-to-action:_ For example, instead of "I testify there is nothing worthy of worship except God", make it: "Worship God and nothing else". Or in the "love" formulation, "I love God above all else" could be "Love God above all else". Still, while I'm generally in favour of active phrasings when possible, a disadvantage of this approach that it diffuses the overall call-to-action, and thus makes it less clear.
3.  _Variation in timing:_ I wonder if there are ways timing can be used to help people re-engage in a fresh way with the adhan? Obviously there's many ways this could be done; as just one idea, perhaps for one month each year the adhan could be replaced by an alternate form of the call to prayer, so that people come back to it with fresh minds, and listen to it in a different way?
4.  _"Come to good deeds":_ In some versions I understand that this line is inserted after "Come to success". I like it: it forces us to consider what we mean by good deeds, and how those might be related to prayer, to success, to God, and to Muhammad.

I'll leave the notes there. There is, of course, much more one could say. I have not discussed the history, variation, musical elements, the muezzin, the modern role of recordings and amplification, and so on. Perhaps for another day!
