### India（印度） (II)

我在上一段中犯了错误。我在挑选 sources 时不够仔细，做 simple math 时也不够仔细。结果发现，我选的还是 total female enrolled population，而不是 graduates 的 number。我有时会犯错。但我不会让错误留着不改。

CS 有 1914 female bachelor graduates（[2013-14 年的 data](https://nces.ed.gov/programs/digest/d15/tables/dt15_318.30.asp?current=yes)）。而根据同一份 data，有 11306 males。这意味着 CS graduates 中的 46% 是 female。考虑到其他所有人所报告的 US 的 gap，这很奇怪。这里的 gap 是 14%。如果改为考虑整个“Computer and information sciences and support services”category，我们得到的 gap 是 18%。总计有 1.068 million females graduated。如果按 narrowly defined 来看 computer science，0.18% 的 women picked 了它。如果按 broadly defined 来看 computer science（包括 support services 那部分），这个 figure 是 0.93%。

现在，对于 India，我们得去看上面所链接文档的 table 35，便能得到各种听起来像 computer sciencey- 的 categories 的 figures。这里的 denominator 是 3.2 million women

-   IT & Computer：2.64%（84660 名 female students）
-   Computer Engineering：2.47%（79236 名 female students）
-   Information Technology：0.81%（26143 名 female students）

为求完整，我们也来看一下 postgraduates，因为 Indian data 中，除了 postgraduate studies 之外，没有叫作 CS 的 item。

-   11948 名 female students completed 了 CS 的 postgraduate students，相比之下 male 的有 6231 名。因此，CS Masters 中有 65% 是 women。在上面所列的 degrees 中，ratio 更 equal。
-   在 India 获得 postgrad 的 739150 名 female students 中，11948 名 picked CS。这意味着 1.61%。

这并不是 naming conventions 的问题：与我的预期相反，India 的 women 确实以比 US 更大的 proportion choose computer science studies。

为什么？一个 hypothesis 当然是 sexism，或者在 US，CS 被认为是 male industry，而相比之下被认为是被认为是 neutral。对 sexism hypothesis 进行 Testing 很困难。（你会怎么做呢？）

另一个 hypothesis 是，male-female 在 things vs people oriented 方面的 preferences 往往是 similar 的，但正如我之前提到的，这些 fields 内的 distribution 是 different 的，也许是由于 different earnings，或者，也许确实是因为 relative sexism。换句话说：CS 的这种 higher choice，是因为 women 更少 choose 其他 STEM subjects，还是因为她们更少 choose education、nursing 或 psychology 之类的 fields？relative sexism hypothesis，即在 India，CS 比例如 mechanical engineering 更不 sexist，会 predict：CS 被 chosen 得更多，mechanical engineering 被 chosen 得更少，而且在 aggregate 上，women 会 choose 更多 CS。我们已经 confirmed，women 事实上确实 choose 更多 CS。

men 更少 choose CS 吗？在 US，所有 men 中有 1.41% chose narrowly defined 的 CS，而 broadly defined 时是 5.66%。在 India，broadly defined measure 对应的是 3.38%。对于 narrow measure，我们必须 compare MScs：India 是 0.93%，US 是 1.7%。因此，India 的 men 也更少 choose CS。既然已经说到这里，我们还可以看看 mechanical engineering 的 numbers：2.65%（US）和 5.1%（India）。India 几乎是两倍！

women 更少 choose mechanical engineering 吗？在 US，所有 women 中的 0.30% chose mechanical engineering，而 India 是 0.284%，足够 similar。

回顾一下到目前为止这番数字的舞动：

-   Women 比 US 的 women 更多地 pick CS
-   Women 以与 US equal 的 rates pick mechanical engineering
-   Men 比 US 的 men 更少 pick CS

如果我们 aggregate US 和 India 的 data，建立 STEM（India 的 Science、Engineering 和 IT&Compute，以及 Engineering、math/stats、physical sciences、biosciences 和 computational science），另一边则是 Education 和 health professions，我们会得到：

-   在 US，57% 的 students 是 women。在 India，是 50%
-   在 US，India 的 STEM students 中有 35% 是 women。在 India，是 41.75%
-   在 US，18% 的 engineering students 是 women。在 India，是 32%
-   在 US，84.3% 的 Education 和 Health students 是 women。在 India，是 63.72%
-   在 US，10.46% 的 women chose STEM，而 25.89% 的 men chose STEM
-   在 India，24.80% 的 women chose STEM，而 35.43% 的 men chose STEM

“choice gap”（men 与 women 之间 (gender in STEM/total students of that gender)x(male,female) 的 Difference，对于 STEM，在 India 是 10.63pp，在 US 是 15.43pp，以及 6.34pp（India）和 24.49pp（US）

1.  根据 data，US 的 STEM gap 比 India 大 6 points。
2.  在 India，Women 更少 choose education 和 health degrees，而更多 choose STEM

所以，我必须承认，上面的 argument 是错的。India 更低的 gap 仍然需要一个 explanation。这个 explanation 最好能 account for 这样一个事实：developing countries 的 STEM gap 更低。考虑到这些国家整体上更 sexist，我认为它们的 STEM 中存在更少 sexism 的可能性极低，因此我确信一定有另一个 explanation。另一个反对 sexism 的 argument 是，如果只看 Indian Institutes of Technology，也就是那些只做 STEM、并且在 national exam 中只 accept top 5% students 的 universities，再看 gender ratios，就会发现 gender shares [为 8.8-9.2%。](http://www.hindustantimes.com/india-news/improving-sex-ratio-in-iits-more-women-take-admission-in-2017/story-2EKeUwqbqG6ibB0Tf5EftL.html)如果 STEM 被视为更不 male-typed，这就不该发生。但 CS 呢？我找不到 IITs 的 CS data，所以我访问了其中几所，查看了 staff 的 gender ratios。在 [IIT Kharagpur](http://iitkgp.ac.in/department/CS/faculties)、[IIT Delhi](http://www.cse.iitd.ernet.in/index.php/2011-12-29-23-14-30/faculty) 或 [IIT Bombay](https://www.cse.iitb.ac.in/page14)，同样几乎所有人都是 male。对于 students，必须手动点击 student profiles，所以我在每所里都随机点了一些（我的时间有限！），同样，most 都是 male。

## Conclusion（结论）

有人反对这样的 view：是 preferences（或其他跨越 cultures 的 factors，例如 intelligence 具有这一 fact ）drives women 在 STEM 中的 distribution；他们提出的 arguments 并不成立。还可以提出更多 arguments，但我对它们能成功不抱太大希望。有人可以指出 Malaysia 之类的地方，或者不断指出任何一个 ratio 超过 45% 的 country，要求我解释，但我的时间不是无限的。不过，我愿意打赌，所有这些 cases 都能找到 explanation。

environment 和 culture 确实有影响，economic incentives 也是如此。但它们怎样产生影响？首先，如果 women 被 institutionally forbidden 在 university 中 study，这就有影响。其次，如果 women 明明是 apt 的，却被灌输自己并不 apt 在 university 中 study 的想法，这也有影响。哪些 majors 在未来更有可能带来 higher salaries，或者 country 中存在哪类 industry，同样有影响。如果 majors 的 choice 是 limited 的，而且人们难以 choose psychology 或 English literature，这也有影响。如果你的家人逼你 study 一项你可能并不想从事、但社会却期待你从事的 career，这也有影响。诸如此类。

无论如何，有一点很清楚：如果你逐步移除这一切，让人们 naturally express 自己的 preferences，那么你看到的就是实际结果：university degrees 中 skewed gender proportion。

这不是说那些 forces 在 Western developed countries 中不存在，而是说，它们在那里的程度比 developed countries 更小。另外，这也不意味着你只靠 diversity courses，或者更强调 outreach，让更多 women 进入 STEM，就能扭转局面。这会在 margin 上改变情况。这样做并非毫无意义；我们的 preferences 中有一些 biases，这无所谓好坏，事实就是如此。它们并非必须维持原样。Young men 也比 young women 在 on average 意义上更 violent，而这是一种我们都认为其 intensity 应当 reduced 的 preference（针对 aggression）。

对于 education，仍然可以论证应当对那些 preferences 施加压力，但这并不那么容易：随着时间推移，每个人都会逐渐接受 violence 是不好的，并认为那些 teenage fights 不过是 testosterone 捣鬼的结果。但没有人认为 choosing medicine over CS 是 immoral 的。

在 culture 和 institutions 方面，要 achieve equality，需要做些什么？这里有一些我并不赞同、但有助于实现这一目的的 policy proposals：

-   对 men 从事某些 degrees 进行 Banning 或 restricting
-   按 degree 为 women 进行 Setting quotas
-   对 men 进行 Brainwashing，让他们认为自己并不 apt 上 university，就像几十年前 women 认为自己并不 apt 一样
-   在 men 更多的那些 sector of employment 中 Increasing taxes

你大概也不赞同这些。（我希望如此。）但如果你想通过 culture 和 institutions 来 close STEM 中的 gap，这就是所需的做法。我认为 Reducing sexism 和 encouraging women 进入 STEM 是好事（谁不这么认为呢？），但人不应自欺欺人，以为只要周围仍有 discrimination 的残余，移除它们就能在 university degrees 的 distribution 中 achieve equality。也许存在令人信服的 quantitative evidence，表明 discrimination 具有巨大的 relevance。但我没有见过。因为，它很可能根本不存在。

Why? One hypothesis is of course sexism, or perceptions of CS being a male industry in the US, vs being perceived as being perceived as neutral. Testing the sexism hypothesis is difficult. (How would you do it?)

Another hypothesis is that male-female preferences in things vs people oriented tend to be similar, but that, as I mentioned earlier, the distribution is different within those fields, perhaps due to different earnings, or perhaps, yes, because of relative sexism. In other words: Is this higher choice of CS because women choose other STEM subjects less, or because they choose fields like education, nursing, or psychology less?  The relative sexism hypothesis, that CS is less sexist, than, say mechanical engineering in India, would predict that CS is chosen more, mechanical engineering is chosen less, and also that on the aggregate, that women would choose more CS. We have confirmed that women do in fact choose more CS.

Do men choose CS less? In the US, 1.41% of all men chose CS narrowly defined, and 5.66% broadly defined. In India, it was 3.38% for the broadly defined measure. For the narrow measure we have to compare MScs: 0.93% for India and 1.7% for the US. Thus men also choose less CS in India too. Now that we are at it, we can get the numbers for mechanical engineering: 2.65% (US) and 5.1% (India). Almost twice as many in India!

Do women choose less mechanical engineering? In the US, 0.30% of all women chose mechanical engineering, while in India it was 0.284%, similar enough.

-   Women pick mechanical engineering at equal rates vs the US
-   Men pick CS less than in the US

### Carnegie Mellon University

According to the data just shown, CMU had a gender ratio of 18.7% in 2014 and 23% in 2015. Total student population for CS was 256 and and 274, respectively.  Carnegie Mellon has data for _first year_ students [here](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2016/september/images/cmu_factsheet.pdf). Last year people wouldn't have been able to use the CMU argument, so this is not very solid.

What would be a good critique of this argument? To show that the strength of prevalence of diversity programmes is correlated with an increase in the future number of female students. This would still leave open the question of whether it is possible to increase levels of female students in STEM, especially CS, at an aggregate level.

A better critique would be to show that diversity encouragement policies works in the largest institutions, or to show that there is a correlation between having more women friendly policies and an institution being smaller. Doing that is left as an exercise for the reader.

## Conclusion

The arguments put forward against the view that it is preferences (or other factors that cut across cultures such the fact that intelligence has ) that drives the distribution of women in STEM fails. One could present more arguments, but I don't gave high hopes for them succeeding. One could point to Malaysia or something, or keep pointing to any single country where the ratio is over 45%  and ask me to explain it, but my time is not unlimited. But I am willing to bet that it is possible to find an explanation for all those cases.

The environment and culture matters, as do economic incentives. But how do they matter? First, it matters if it is institutionally forbidden for women to study in university. Second, it matters if women have been made to think that they are not apt to study in university, when they are obviously apt. It also matters what majors are more likely to lead to higher salaries in the future, or the sort of industry that exists in the country. It also matters if the choice of majors is limited and one has it difficult to choose psychology or English literature. It also matters if your family pushes you to study a career that you may not want, but that you are socially expected to have. And so on.

Regardless, one thing is clear: If you progressively remove all of that and let people express their preferences naturally, what you see is what you get: a skewed gender proportion in university degrees.

This is not to say that those forces are absent in Western developed countries, this is to say that they are there to a lesser extent than in developed countries. Also, this doesn't mean that you can undo the situation just diversity courses, or a stronger emphasis on outreach to get more women in STEM. That will change things on the margin. It is not pointless to do; we have some biases in preferences, that is not good or bad, it is what is is. They need not be left as they are. Young men are also more violent on average than young women, and that is a preference (for aggression) that we all think should be reduced in intensity.

For education one can still make a case for exerting pressure on those preferences, but it is not as easy: Everyone comes to accept violence as bad over time and to think that those teenage fights were just fruit of testosterone playing funny tricks. But no one thinks choosing medicine over CS is immoral.

What would it take in terms of culture and institutions to achieve equality? Here you have some policy proposals that I don't endorse, but that are useful for that end:

-   Banning or restricting men from certain degrees
-   Setting quotas for women by degree
-   Brainwashing men into thinking that they are not apt for university, in the same way that women thought they were not apt back decades ago
-   Increasing taxes in those sector of employment with more men

You probably don't endorse this either. (I hope). But this is what it takes to close the gap in STEM if you want to play with culture and institutions. Reducing sexism and encouraging women to join STEM I see as a good thing (Who wouldn't?), but one shouldn't delude oneself that just because there are still vestiges of discrimination around, removing then will achieve equality in the distribution of university degrees. Maybe there is convincing quantitative evidence for the huge relevance of discrimination. But I haven't seen it. Because, most likely, it doesn't exist.
