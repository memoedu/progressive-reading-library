## Science funding：diversity 与 randomization（科研资助：多样性与随机化）

写那篇文章时，我已经承认了一些显而易见的 critiques：当然，measuring science 中什么 works 很难，这不会像有 crisply defined endpoint 的 clinical trial，而是会有 _varied range 的 metrics 来 measure success_。与此同时，我也承认，我们不需要对所有事情都进行 RCT（那_就会陷入同一种 flawed thinking，正是这种思维导致了“[没有 evidence 表明 masks work](https://twitter.com/EricTopol/status/1248255130132033537)_”的说法）。

不过，我觉得自己没有充分关注 hypothetical funder 在 implementing 这些 policies 时会面对的 costs and benefits，也没有充分关注实际 run 这些 comparisons 有多难。就 costs and benefits 而言，以任何一种既定方式 allocating science funding 的 funder，都在 implicitly asserting 自己的 method does better than chance，但他们这样做的依据并不是过去的 RCTs，而是各种 knowledge 的综合，其他人可能会对这些 knowledge 赋予不同的权重。例如，我得出的 conclusion 是，top science 中 disproportional amounts 的工作由一个 [small elite](https://nintil.com/newton-hypothesis) 完成，而且这很可能不是 Matthew effect 所致。反过来，NIH 内部有一种思潮，希望更多地 [spread funding](https://nintil.com/funding-lotteries)，因为他们认为 concentrating funding 于 elite labs 不会带来更多或更好的 science。还有一种立场认为，一切都应该采用 funding lottery（因为 radical uncertainty 之类的理由，不过[在这里](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs11138-012-0193-5)，我们不相信这种说法）。但在这种情况下，作为 funder，你将面临这样的选择：funding 你认为最好的东西，或者 running 一个 RCT，让 funding 被 allocate 给你并不十分相信的 funding mechanisn。说得更具体些，最近成立的 [Arc Institute](https://arcinstitute.org/) 正在押注一种非常特定的 funding 和 doing science 的 model（HHMI-inspired）。funders 是否本该改为创办两个 institutes，让第二个做完全不同的事情？这里的问题是，a) 要知道这个 second-best 是否真的值得，需要几十年；b) 要 compare 这两个 institutes，并说其中一个 did substantially better than 另一个，会非常困难。这两种选择都有合理的理由！具体的 managerial talent 可能比为每个 entity 设定发展路线的 nominal charter 更重要，使得公平地 compare 这两种 models 更加困难。

Randomization 是一种 gain information 的方式，但人们需要有 ideas 才能向其 allocate funds。创办 novel institutions 也是一种 generate information 的方式。如果没有 HHMI 及其 Janelia campus，或 Broad Institute，Arc 就不会成为现在的样子。当然，[并没有 super incontrovertible](https://nintil.com/hhmi-and-nih/) econometric evidence 表明，特别是 HHMI，相对于 counterfactual 会带来更好的 research；但如果把各方面的 evidence 放在一起（这需要 [judgement](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1463573243449593861)，而不是在 meta-analysis 中对 results 进行 mechanical aggregation），那么这个 conclusion 就开始更说得通了。我曾为一个 hypothetical funding institution 提出草案，那个 three pronged design（可以在[这篇文章](https://nintil.com/funding-lotteries)底部找到）反映了我的一些 beliefs：funding “people not projects” 很重要（Parts A and B）；focused、engineering-heavy efforts 也是必需的（Part C）；一些 randomization 是好的（Part A）；让一个 small intellectual elite 腾出精力去 pursue very long term projects，不必担心 grant renewals，也不必极其详细地说明自己想做什么，可能是件好事（Part B）。我没有花很多时间思考这个 structure，如果明天再做，也未必会这样做；不过，它可以说明 beliefs 如何 influence institutional design，也就是说，我对 science funding 中的一些事情已经足够确定，因而能够 propose 一个 concrete structure，而不是 randomizing 一切，再试着从中学习。

_Works in Progress_ 的那篇文章指出了 randomizing 的一个 benefit：它会引入更多 diversity

> 还有一个 argument 支持通过这种 experimental approach 尝试更多事情：它会增加_任何给定时刻_可用的 funding mechanisms 的 diversity。按大多数 measures 来看，US innovation ecosystem 都是全世界 technical and scientific progress 的首要引擎。这种成功的一部分可能源于 funding 的 [diversity](https://books.google.co.uk/books?hl=en&lr=&id=YFDGjgxc2CYC&oi=fnd&pg=PR7&ots=Oq8trj1vVS&sig=skMQdBGu2DKgm0qRglb0OL6RQZc#v=onepage&q=pluralism&f=false)：US 历来没有 centrally coordinating 或 planning 全国的 scientific investments，而是让形形色色的 entities 蓬勃发展，从 philanthropies、[privately-run federally funded research](https://www.mitre.org/sites/default/files/publications/ffrdc-primer-april-2015.pdf) centers，到 university 和 industrial labs。这让一个 researcher 更容易找到适合她和她的 ideas 的 research home。还可以进一步 pursued Diversity：像 NIH 这样的大型 agency，或者其 member institutes 之一，例如 National Cancer Institute，都可以在内部 split 成两个或更多 funding mechanisms，并且每隔几年 assessed 一次它们的 performance。

不过，要得到这一点，其实并不需要 randomize，直接去创办 institutions 就可以了。这些 institutions 不应该仅仅为了 diversity 而力求 maximally diverse：我认为，一些 competition 是好的。

## Tools for thought（思考工具）

我写了一篇 essay，探讨更好的 [Google Scholar](https://nintil.com/better-google-scholar) 会是什么样子。在这个过程中，我发现了大量为 scientists building tools 的尝试（在该 essay 的语境中，指 search engines 和 social annotation），但它们都失败了。在[其他情境](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1466532004254470150)中，许多看起来 well funded 的工作也未能转化成 useful tools，尽管，[确实](https://twitter.com/michael_nielsen/status/1474976399043686404)，正如 Michael Nielsen 指出的，有些东西确实留存了下来。什么会留存下来，什么不会？Nielsen 和 Andy Matuschak 自己的 [Quantum Country](https://quantum.country/) 是一个设计精美的 website，也是一项 experiment，探索通过在 essay 正文中 embedding 精心 curated 的 SRS prompts 来 teach 的新方法。然而，这样的 essays 仍然很少见（实际出现的两个例子是[我自己的 essay](https://nintil.com/age-and-science/)，探讨 scientists 的 performance 是否随 age 而 drops，以及 David Chapman 的 [_Maps, the territory, and meta-rationality_](https://metarationality.com/maps-and-territory)）。现有的 tools for thought 有哪些种类（显然，note taking app、SRS prompts、[hypothes.is](https://web.hypothes.is/)，以及 retweeting 这个想法，都值得拥有各自的 subcategory！）。_Thought_ 包括 memory，但也包括 idea generation、improved information acquisition、richer understanding，等等。

## Learning：对 brain 如何 learns 进行 reverse engineering，还是 leveraging 它

前些时候，我写了一篇关于 Bloom's two sigma problem 的 [review](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/)：其 finding 是 one on one tutoring 极其 effective，而 problem 在于如何 at scale achieve 这一效果。人们一直尝试采用的办法，是对 tutors 进行 reverse engineer。通过 building models 来描述 students 如何 learn，以及如何最好地给予他们 cues，帮助他们 overcome 可能遇到的 specific problems（“Intelligent Tutoring Systems”，ITS），人们希望能 solve 这个 problem，但目前它仍然是 unsolved。

我在那里 reviewed 的 literature，有相当大一部分 focuses on educating children，其中又特别关注有 learning disabilities 的 children。我在那里讨论的最有希望的 case study 是 DARPA Digital Tutor，但同样，目前它仍然只是一次性的奇闻。原因在于，要 build effective ITSs，就需要找到 domain experts，并 distill 他们的 knowledge，而这出了名地困难。Experts 往往不知道自己知道多少，因此他们可能会跳过或一带而过某些 obvious details，而这些细节对 beginners 来说并没有那么 obvious。有时，他们甚至无法 explain 自己的 actions！一个例子是这位 fire commander，他[说](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1466534290104020998)：“我不 make decisions。”他对 firefighting 这个 domain 的 interiorized 程度如此之深，以至于只需 acting on well honed instinct 就够了。ITS approach 会是坐下来和这位 fire commanded 交流，并 extract 上述 knowledge，而这在一定程度上确实有效。

但还有另一种办法，就是我在 [Scaling tacit knowledge essay](https://nintil.com/scaling-tacit-knowledge) 中描述的：通过接触一个庞大的 library of examples（例如，多个小时的 real world firefighting operations 录像），一个人可能不必先听人 explained 这个 domain，就能被 taught。当然，理想情况下，explanation 和“show, don't tell” videos 两者都应具备。我以及像我这样 STEM-brained 的人，在得到一个 problem 时，会从 finding solutions 中获得一些乐趣，却没有像我们本应做的那样，经常停下来问问这个 problem 是否是恰当的。与其问如何 model knowledge acquisition，再用它来 build 一个 tutor，不如让 brain 自己去 make sense of 这个 library of examples：从 examples 中进行 Knowledge distillation，_是我们的 brain 自然而然就会 solves 的一个 problem_！

我已经[开始](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1471581125277536256)录下自己通读 papers 的过程；在那篇 essay 中，我也指出了一个 test，用来检验这种 learning 视角的 usefulness：只需 build 那个 library of examples，然后看看 students 表现如何。在 chess 和 language learning 中，这似乎已经有效了！

## Science funding: diversity and randomization

When I wrote it I already acknowledged some obvious critiques: Sure, measuring what works in science is hard, this wouldn't be like a clinical trial where there is a crisply defined endpoint, rather _a varied range of metrics to measure success_. At the same time, I acknowledged that we don't need to RCT all the things (That _would be engaging in the same flawed thinking that led to the claim that “[there is no evidence that masks work](https://twitter.com/EricTopol/status/1248255130132033537)_").

I think however that I didn't pay enough attention to the costs and benefits that a hypothetical funder would face when implementing these policies, nor to how hard it is actually run these comparisons. Regarding costs and benefits, a funder that is allocating science funding in any one given way is implicitly asserting that their method does better than chance, but they are not doing this on the basis of past RCTs, rather on a mix of all sorts of pieces of knowledge that someone else may weigh differently. For example, I came to the conclusion that top science is done by a [small elite](https://nintil.com/newton-hypothesis) in disproportional amounts and that is is probably not due to a Matthew effect. Conversely there is a current of thought within NIH that wants to [spread funding](https://nintil.com/funding-lotteries) more because they think that concentrating funding in elite labs won't lead to more or better science. There is also the position that everything should be a funding lottery (because radical uncertainty or something, but [here](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs11138-012-0193-5) we don't believe in that). But in that case as a funder you would be facing the choice between funding what you think is best, or running an RCT that would allocate funding to a funding mechanisn you don't quite believe in. To be more concrete, the recently launched [Arc Institute](https://arcinstitute.org/) is making a bet on a very specific (HHMI-inspired) model of funding and doing science. Should the funders have instead started two institutes, with the second one doing something different altogether? The problem here is that a) Knowing if this second-best was actually worth it would take decades b) It would be very hard to compare both institutes and say that one did substantially better than the other. There is a reasonable case to be made for both! The particular managerial talent may matter more than the nominal charter that sets off the courses of each entity, making it even harder to compare both models fairly.

Randomization is a way to gain information, but one needs ideas to allocate funds to. Starting novel institutions is also a way to generate information. Arc wouldn't have taken the form it took without HHMI and its Janelia campus or the Broad Institute. Sure there [isn't super incontrovertible](https://nintil.com/hhmi-and-nih/) econometric evidence that in particular HHMI leads to better research relative to a counterfactual, but if one puts together various strands of evidence (Something that requires [judgement](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1463573243449593861), not mechanical aggregation of results in a meta-analysis) then that conclusion starts to make more sense. When I proposed a draft for a hypothetical funding institution, that three pronged design (May be found at the bottom of [this](https://nintil.com/funding-lotteries)) reflects some beliefs about the importance of funding "people not projects" (Parts A and B) that focused, engineering-heavy efforts are also required (Part C), that some randomization is good (Part A), and that it may be good to free up a small intellectual elite to pursue very long term projects without worrying about grant renewals, and without having to be extremely detailed about what they want to do (Part B). I didn't spend a lot of time thinking about this structure and I wouldn't necessarily do it this way if I were to do it tomorrow, but it serves as an illustration of how beliefs influence institutional design, i.e. I am certain enough of some things in science funding to propose a concrete structure vs randomizing everything and trying to learn from that.

The _Works in Progress_ essay points out to a benefit of randomizing: That it would introduce more diversity

> There is one more argument in favor of trying more things out through this experimental approach: it will increase the diversity of funding mechanisms available _at any given time_. By most measures, the US innovation ecosystem is the world’s leading engine of technical and scientific progress. Part of this success may be due to the [diversity](https://books.google.co.uk/books?hl=en&lr=&id=YFDGjgxc2CYC&oi=fnd&pg=PR7&ots=Oq8trj1vVS&sig=skMQdBGu2DKgm0qRglb0OL6RQZc#v=onepage&q=pluralism&f=false) of funding: rather than coordinating or planning the entire nation’s scientific investments centrally, the US historically has enabled a menagerie of entities to thrive, from philanthropies, [privately-run federally funded research](https://www.mitre.org/sites/default/files/publications/ffrdc-primer-april-2015.pdf) centers, to university and industrial labs. This makes it easier for a given researcher to find a research home that suits her and her ideas. Diversity could be further pursued: a large agency like NIH or one of its member institutes like the National Cancer Institute could be split into two or more funding mechanisms internally, and their performance could be assessed every few years.

But one does not really need to randomize to get this, one can just go and start institutions. These shouldn't strive to be maximally diverse just for the sake of diversity: I think some competition is good.

## Learning: reverse engineering how the brain learns vs leveraging it

A while back I wrote a [review](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/) of Bloom's two sigma problem: the finding that one on one tutoring is extremely effective and the problem being how to achieve that at scale. The way this has been approaches is to try to reverse engineer tutors. By building models of how students learn, and how best to give them cues to help them overcome specific problems that they may have ("Intelligent Tutoring Systems", ITS) the hope is to solve this problem, but for now it remains unsolved.

A substantial chunk of the literature I reviewed there focuses on educating children, with some particular focus on children with learning disabilities. The most promising case study I discuss there is the DARPA Digital Tutor, but again for now it remains a one-off curiosity. The reason why is that to build effective ITSs one needs to find domain experts and distill their knowledge, and this is notoriously hard. Experts often don't know how much they know, so they may skip or gloss over obvious details that are not so obvious to beginners. Sometimes they won't even be able to explain their actions! An example is this fire commander who [says](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1466534290104020998) that "I don't make decisions". He has interiorized the domain of firefighting so much that acting on well honed instinct is all one needs. The ITS approach would be to sit down with the fire commanded and extract said knowledge, and this work to some extent.

But there is another way, that described in my [Scaling tacit knowledge essay](https://nintil.com/scaling-tacit-knowledge): By being exposed to a large library of examples (e.g. multiple hours of filmed real world firefighting operations), one may be able to be taught without having to be explained the domain. Ideally one would of course have both explanation and "show, don't tell" videos. Me and people like me who are STEM-brained when given a problem take some joy in finding solutions to it without stopping as much as we ought to ask if the problem is the right one. Rather than asking how to model knowledge acquisition and use that to build a tutor, leave it up to the brain to make sense of the library of examples: Knowledge distillation from examples _is a problem our brain naturally solves_!

I've [started](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1471581125277536256) recording myself reading through papers, and in the essay itself I point at a test of the usefulness of this perspective on learning: One could just build that library of examples and see how students fare. In chess and language learning this already seems to work!
