# 第 49 篇 Verse：Trust 与 Power

---

## 正文

[![](https://michaelnotebook.com/assets/home.png)](https://michaelnotebook.com/index.html)

在这些笔记中，我对 Ursula K. Le Guin 于 1997 年翻译的 Tao Te Ching 中的五篇 verses 进行 reflect[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn1)。虽然此前我曾粗读过 Tao Te Ching，却从未真正产生共鸣。我之前的阅读很肤浅：Tao Te Ching 不是一本可以快速、linearly 阅读的 novel；它更像 mathematics，需要投入和下苦功。而且，像 mathematics 一样，它的深度会逐渐显现，每次重读都会带来更深的 insights 和更丰富的 meaning。

虽然写笔记有助于集中思绪，但我还没有准备好承诺为 Tao Te Ching 的全部 81 篇 verses 作 annotating。作为初步尝试，我选了五篇 verses 来 reflection。我有哪些地方不理解？我能弄明白吗？我在哪些方面不赞同？这篇 verse 激发了什么新想法？我反复重读这些 verses 和自己的笔记，也重读其他提供 contextualizing 的材料。因为 Tao Te Ching 涉及 profound themes，我把每篇 verse 的笔记限制在几百字；要写得多得多并不难。因此，虽然这些笔记让我在理解文本方面有了一个立足点，但仍只是最初的几步，熟悉 Daoism 的人恐怕不会感兴趣。不过，对于同样希望 deeply process 类似文本的人来说，它们或许有 methodological 方面的意义。

## 第 49 篇 Verse：Trust 与 Power

>       这些 wise 没有自己的 mind，
>       而是在 minds 中 finding 它，
>       那些 minds 属于 ordinary people。
> 
>       他们对 good people 是 good 的，
>       对 bad people 也是 good 的。
>       Power 就是 goodness。
>       他们 trust 那些有 good faith 的 people，
>       也 trust 那些有 bad faith 的 people。
>       Power 就是 trust。
> 
>       他们将自己的 life 与 world 相 mingle，
>       他们将自己的 mind 与 world 相 mix up。
>       Ordinary people 照料他们。
>       Wise souls 是 children。
>       

1.  _“这些 wise 没有自己的 mind，而是在 ordinary people 的 minds 中 finding 它”_：按字面理解，这不可能是真的。但或许它的意思是，一个 wise 的人明白自己并没有那么特别？大多数 wisdom 都在 world 中，往往出现在意想不到的地方，而不在我们自己的 minds 中，无论我们自己（或别人）认为我们多么聪明。因此，这些 wise 会认真而深入地倾听许多人，而不只是倾听那些被他们认定为 wise 的人。当然，他们不会沉溺于 self-absorption。又或许，这句话表达的是对 everyday will 的 deference。这可能显得愚蠢，但作为一种 governance mechanism（democracy），它的实际效果好得出奇[2](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn2)。
2.  _“他们对 good people 是 good 的，对 bad people 也是 good 的。”_ 这个想法出现在许多 traditions 中：being good 本身就值得，与 beneficiary 无关。其 justification 可能是 selfish 的（你会因 being good 而受益），也可能是广义上的 altruistic（world 会受益）。我很好奇，这个想法最早出现在哪里？它与 iterated prisoner's dilemma 中的 tit-for-tat strategy 表面上存在 contradiction。我赞同这个想法：tit-for-tat presumes 这个 game 会继续。可能的话，更好的回应通常是停止玩下去，转而寻找 good people 与之玩 games。话虽如此，我敬佩那些有意帮助做过坏事的人的人。那种程度的 _hope_ 极其动人。我有一位熟人，过去曾作为 therapist，为 abused 自己妻子的男人工作。这是一件了不起的事，既体现在她的选择上——这项工作不可能轻松，而她还有许多其他选择——也说明支持这项工作的 society 有可取之处。这或许说明了那句话的用意：她对做过坏事的人 being good[3](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn3)，但这并不意味着她会忍受他们的恶劣对待。值得注意的是，实现 safety 需要相当多的 institutional support。
3.  _“Power 就是 goodness”_：Empirically，这往往是 false 的！他们这么说可能是什么意思？Hammurabi 曾以 defense of the powerless 作为自己 power 的 justification，这一点广为人知（也有一定道理）。你也可以把 Hammurabi 的 declarations（以及一直延续到现代的类似 declarations）理解为 cynical propaganda。在实践中，我相信真正的 underlying intent 存在很大差异。作为一种 aspiration，这个想法_确实_令人钦佩：那样的 world 会是什么样子？我们怎样才能 design institutions，使 power 与 good 相 align？你可以把 democracy、markets、invisible hand 和 separation of powers 之类的想法视为朝这个方向所作的尝试（取得了部分成功）。在实践中，power 往往使人免受自己 actions 的 consequences，并导致他们的 corruption。怎样才能确保 power 就是 goodness？
4.  _“他们 trust 那些有 good faith 的 people，也 trust 那些有 bad faith 的 people。Power 就是 trust。”_ 这些 powerful 因为有能力 retaliate，可能得到 preferential treatment。不过，也可能有更多 untrustworthy individuals 试图 exploit 他们。另一个 perspective 是，采取 default trust 的立场会带来巨大的 benefit。我认识的那些生活过得最好的人中，有许多人极其 trusting，有时也曾被人占便宜。然而，总体而言，他们仍从 default trust 的立场中受益。reward 的 level 取决于他们 environment 中 goodness 的 ambient level：如果它很高，default trust 就会带来回报；如果它很低，default trust 就可能导致可怕的事情。religions 最有意思的一点，或许就在于它们常常立志（而且有时成功了？[4](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn4)）改变这个 ambient level。
5.  “_他们将自己的 life 与 world 相 mingle，他们将自己的 mind 与 world 相 mix up_”：这同样符合对自身 understanding 所持的 humility，即意识到，无论自己知道多少，world 的其他地方都有多得多的 wisdom。这让我想起 Emerson 的那句名言：“在世间，按照世人的 opinion 生活很容易；独处时，按照自己的 opinion 生活也很容易；但伟大的人，是那个身处人群之中，仍能以十足的温和保持 solitude 的 independence 的人。”不过，Emerson 对他人 wisdom 的 deference 还不够充分。但他理解这种 tension，也明白学会应对它是值得的。我自己也可以学着把这件事做得更好！
6.  _“Ordinary people 照料他们。”_ 我不理解它的用意。这是说，一个 wise 的人是 dependent 的，要靠别人来照料吗？还是在提醒我们，所有人都在彼此照料？division of labour 的一个显著 consequence 是，它既使我们与 overall purpose 相 alienates，_又_产生了大量 abundance，以及彼此照料的 capacity。
7.  _“Wise souls 是 children。”_ 这句话最明显地接近 conventional wisdom，甚至接近一句 platitude[5](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn5)。platitudes 的一个问题是，人们通常不把它们当真：人们会点点头，然后做出与那个想法相反的行为。这篇 verse 有一点很有帮助：前面有些句子描述了 child-like behaviour，并促使你思考，那样做是否正确。因此，它创造了一种 tension，迫使你更加认真地思考最后一句。这就创造了一个很好的 context。
8.  **大多是错误的想法：** 其中大部分在我看来都不对。然而，稍微深入地读一读，仍然很有意思，也很有启发。与此密切相关的是：**粗浅阅读时，我其实一点也没有理解这些内容。** 即使我以为自己理解了，一开始提问，就意识到自己并没有理解。我当时是在一种茫然的状态下阅读，只是在摄入文字，没有进行任何 deep processing。直到写下这些笔记，我才有所进展。

## Verse 37：统摄一切（Over all）

>       这个 Way 从不做任何事，
>       而一切都得以完成。
>       如果那些掌握 power 的人能够 hold to 这个 Way，
>       这 ten thousand things
>       便会自行照料自己。
>       如果即便如此，它们仍试图 act，
>       我便会用 nameless 使它们 quiet，
>       用 natural。
> 
>       在 unnamed 之中，在 unshapen 之中，
>       便是 not wanting。
>       在 not wanting 之中便是 stillness。
>       在 stillness 之中，all under heaven 都 rests。
>       

1.  _“这个 Way 从不做任何事，而一切都得以完成”：_我不知道这是什么意思。这或许是在尝试让读者的 mind 产生 confusion，使这个反复出现、被称为 Way 的 entity 更添 mystery。
2.  _“如果那些掌握 power 的人能够 hold to 这个 Way，这 ten thousand things 便会自行照料自己”：_Tao Te Ching 中有许多给 powerful 的建议。它对 power 往往是 deferential，甚至是 sycophantic。Power 被预设为 _static_，意思是有些人拥有它，而另一些人没有。不过，这个 viewpoint 本身——向 power 提供建议——就是 subversive 的。我很好奇，Tao Te Ching 的作者（或作者们及编者们）会对它给 world affairs 带来的 impact 感到多么惊讶？他们的 impact 远远超过成书时任何一位“powerful”[6](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn6)。
3.  “_在 unnamed 之中，在 unshapen 之中，便是 not wanting。在 not wanting 之中便是 stillness。_”颇具讽刺意味的是，这确实在 describing 一种人能够 cultivate 的 genuine state of mind。它与 Iris Murdoch 关于 unselfing 的 idea 并不相同，但据我的经验，unselfing——与我们周围的 universe 进行 connecting more deeply，并失去一部分 sense of self——往往是进入 not wanting 的良好准备。反过来也许同样如此：当你停止 wanting 时，这能帮助你更好地与周围环境 commune。
4.  _“在 stillness 之中，all under heaven 都 rests”：_这似乎是随手写下的一句，主要是在重新表达前面的观点。不过，它的 image 很美——也许这就是用意所在！
5.  **Wu Wei（无为）：**这一 verse 讨论的是 _wu wei_，这是 Tao Te Ching 中反复出现的一个 idea，意为 non-action 或 effortless action。这完全足以成为一篇长 essay（乃至一生）的 subject。我只谈几点 observations。
    1.  **Harmony 与我们的 animal nature：**一种解读是，wu wei 意味着处于 passive 状态，过着 quiet contemplation 的生活，与 wold 保持 harmony。“在 not wanting 之中便是 stillness。在 stillness 之中，all under heaven 都 rests”。但 human beings（部分而言）是 pan African plains apes。我们是 _animals_。我们都有 ambitions、desires 和追求 change 的 drive；在我们一些人身上，这种 drive 非常强烈。否认这一点，本身就是在与我们的 nature 处于 disharmony 的状态下生活。我不禁怀疑，wu wei 究竟在多大程度上算得上一个 coherent notion[7](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn7)？
    2.  **与 flow 的 Connection：**这一 verse 与 modern psychology 中“flow”的 notion closely connected。在 flow 的 state 中，我们以与 environment 几乎处于 effortless harmony 的方式 act。
        1.  **反对 flow：**Flow 常常受到 idolized。我认为这是个错误。它最适合 tennis 之类的 activities：你希望一场 match 的大部分时间都处于 flow，与 goal 或 environment 之间没有 conflicted 状态。又或者是在舞台上 acting，此时 actors 理想情况下会 identify with 自己的 character。但在 practicing 时，你往往会处于 flow 之外，learning 去做那些起初 awkward 且 uncomfortable 的事情[8](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn8)。往往，如果你处于 flow 的时间太多，就意味着你 learning 得很少。即使对于 relatively well-defined tasks，比如 playing tennis，也是如此。对于 highly creative tasks 就更是如此了：这些 tasks 要求你习惯 failure，习惯那些（所谓的）“unproductivity”与 frustration 的时期，在这些时期，efficiency 这个 notion 本身就是错误的。这并不意味着你不该希望花一些时间处于 flow。但把 flow 当作 aspiration 来 idolize，是个错误。更好的问题是：flow 与 uncomfortable work 之间恰当的 balance 是什么，我们又如何更接近这种 balance？
        2.  **Higher flow（更高层次的心流）：**话虽如此，还有一个 notion，我将其视为 _higher flow_：在其中，你能坦然面对 persistence、gumption、embracing mistakes，不因自己“不擅长”某些事情而羞愧，在世间 living equably，并乐于处于 creative uncertainty 的 state。这些也是 skills！而且是你可以不断提高的 skills。Children 起初往往很擅长这些。随着他们更加意识到自己是 social beings，并开始 internalize social judgements，他们便失去了这种能力。“我做得对吗？这样够好吗？我的朋友还会喜欢我吗？”然后，他们（有时）会在成长中跨越这些。（我无意声称自己已经跨越了：在某些方面，我确实如此，但在另一些方面，我仍在非常努力地摸索！）
    3.  **E. O. Wilson：**许多 stress 源于我们的 individual selfish desires 与 group needs 之间的 tension。Wilson 指出，individual 与 group 之间的这种 tension founded in evolutionary history，而且出现在所有 eusocial species 中，并不只存在于 human beings。举例来说：许多 human systems（当然包括我们的 economic system）serve group 的程度，超过它们 serve 大多数 individuals 的程度。想想有多少人难以找到一份 intrinsically satisfying job。即使许多“好”工作也是 alienating 且枯燥的，并不适合 individuals；它们之所以存在，是因为它们 serve 一种 economically valuable collective purpose。这种 tension 也有许多较为温和的版本。当我们不情愿地觉得“我应该做某某事”\[也许是去参加办公室的圣诞聚会\]时，这往往就是这种 conflict 的结果。我的看法是，这类 conflicts 不应遭到 rejected，也不应被视为坏事。相反，它们处于 human life 的核心；learning 去 understand them deeply，有时甚至 embrace 它们，是好好生活的一部分。
    4.  **Conflicted nature（充满冲突的天性）：**更一般地说，我把 wu wei 视为对 conflicted nature 的 critique。但只有当 conflicted nature 是你始终唯一的体验时，它才是坏事。它是一种需要 embrace 和 understand 的 condition，同时也要认识到，有些时候处于 unconflicted 状态是有价值的。有一种 viewpoint 认为，人必须在 Will to Power 与 submission to nature 之间作出选择。而另一种更健康的 viewpoint 则认为，它们实际上可以共同发挥作用。理想情况下，wu wei 讨论的是后者。
6.  **一个现代版本：**对于许多事情，我们今天已有的理解，使 Tao Te Ching 显得过于 simplistic。上面我们就看到了一个例子：它没有理解 humans 是 animals，具有 animal drives。思考如何重写 Tao Te Ching 来纠正这些错误，是件有意思的事[9](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn9)。与此相关的，还有为 Tao Te Ching（或其他类似作品）创作 discovery fiction 的 idea。

## 第 1 章：Taoing

>       你能 go 的 way
>       并不是 real way。
>       你能 say 的 name
>       并不是 real name。
> 
>       Heaven 与 earth
>       在 unnamed 中 begin：
>       name 是
>       ten thousand things 的 mother。
> 
>       因此，unwanting soul
>       sees 那些 hidden 的东西，
>       而 ever-wanting soul
>       只 sees 它 wants 的东西。
> 
>       两个 things，同一个 origin，
>       却在 name 上 different，
>       它们的 identity 是 mystery。
>       一切 mysteries 中的 Mystery！
>       通往 hidden 的 door。
>       

1.  _“你能 go 的 way 并不是 real way。你能 say 的 name 并不是 real name。Heaven 与 earth 在 unnamed 中 begin”：_Tao Te Ching 一开篇就发出了警告！Words 与 internal models——我们自己的 minds——也许是我们的 reality。但它们并不是 reality！接下来的文本不是 reality。这有点棘手，因为我们（以及 words）是 reality 的一部分。但它们只是极小的一部分，我们不应把 words 与整个 reality 混为一谈[10](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn10)。
2.  _“因此，unwanting soul sees 那些 hidden 的东西，而 ever-wanting soul 只 sees 它 wants 的东西”：_从字面上看是 false，却指向某种 real 的东西。要留意自己 want 什么（以及 want 多少），因为它会改变你 see 什么。这可能是一种非常有用的 pattern，也可能极具 destructive 性。我怀疑 unwanting soul 是否 sees 那些 hidden 的东西。我认为 unwanting soul 往往 sees 得很少。但它可以成为一种良好的 transition state，为 see 做好准备。
3.  _“两个 things，同一个 origin，却在 name 上 different，它们的 identity 是 mystery。一切 mysteries 中的 Mystery！通往 hidden 的 door。”_就我的理解而言，这里说的大概是，Way（Tao）与 named 的 origin 都在 Way 之中。那个 Way 从根本上说是个 mystery，却又是 all things 的 source。这相当接近某种形式的 pantheism，也接近 Korzybski 所说的“map 不是 territory”。不过，它更像是“territory 是 map 与 territory 二者的 source，但不要把前者与后者混为一谈”，还附带一个限制：我们无法 direct access 那个 territory。这也有点像我们写下的 mathematics 与其 underlying Platonic reality 之间的 relationship。我喜欢的一点是，它提醒我们，这_确实_是个 mystery。

## 第 48 章：Unlearning

>       每天 Studying 与 learning，你便 grow larger。
>       每天 Following 这个 Way，你便 shrink。
>       你变得 smaller and smaller。
>       因此，你抵达 not doing。
>       你 do nothing，却没有什么没有 done。
> 
>       要 run things，
>       就不要对它们 fuss。
>       任何 fusses 的人
>       都不适合 run things。
>       

1.  _“每天 Studying 与 learning，你便 grow larger。每天 Following 这个 Way，你便 shrink。你变得 smaller and smaller。_ _因此，你抵达 not doing。你 do nothing，却没有什么没有 done”：_这里又像其他章节一样，设置了一个 puzzle。studying、learning 和 growing larger 当然是 good！那么，它怎么会与 Way 构成 contradiction 呢？为什么 following 这个 Way 会让你 shrink？这会让 following 这个 Way 成为 bad 吗？还是说 studying 与 learning 是 bad？我怀疑这里仅仅是有意设置一个 false dichotomy。我再次想到 Iris Murdoch 的 unselfing 概念：与 universe 更加 connected，同时更少 self-involved。Einstein 的说法很有意思，他把自己的一生描述为一场“从 I 和 We 飞向 It”的旅程；他还说过：“我们能 experience 的最 beautiful 的东西，就是 mysterious。”我得说，比起 Tao Te Ching 过于 abstract 的 notion，我更喜欢 Einstein 对 unself 的 concrete engagement！当你深入阅读 Einstein 的著作和生平时，会惊讶于他 explored 这个 universe（事实上也包括 mysterium tremendum）时的深度，以及其中 concrete detail 的丰富程度。不过，Einstein 有一个优势：在现代，我们_能够_与 unself 建立好得多的 connected 关系。举个例子，诸多例子之一：在 Hubble discovery 了 expanding universe 之后，他 abandonment 了 cosmological constant！我也钦佩 Feynman 对“doubt、uncertainty 与 not knowing”的 primacy 所怀有的热切坚持。同样，他也是在极其详尽的细节中付诸实践。我扯远了！回到 Tao Te Ching 的这句话：也许只应把它理解为，将自己的 studying 与 learning 聚焦于和某种更宏大之物的 connection[11](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn11)。
2.  _“要 run things，就不要对它们 fuss。任何 fusses 的人都不适合 run things。”_Le Guin 指出，translators 认为她 translates 为“fuss”的那个词存在问题，另一种 translation 是“diplomatic”。于是便成了：“要 run things，就要 undiplomatic。没有 diplomat 适合 run things。”我觉得这种 translation 更容易理解！Diplomacy 常被理解为不把 hard truths 说出来。（我怀疑，Good diplomacy 往往恰好相反。）这会使 run things well 更加困难，因为它使得将 truth 确立为 common knowledge 更加困难。不过，这与本章的前半部分有什么关系呢？也许是说，只要我们面对的是 truth，它就比我们更宏大；在某种意义上，我们占据的空间便更少。而我们的 lies、misconceptions 与 misunderstandings 则仅仅存在于我们的 self 之中。

## 第 35 章：Humane power

>       坚守那个 great thought，
>       整个 world 就会来到你身边，
>       是 harmless、peaceable、serene 的。
> 
>       四处行走时，我们停下脚步，
>       为了音乐，为了食物。
>       但如果你 taste 这个 Way，
>       它是 flat、insipid 的。
>       它看起来没什么，
>       听起来也没什么。
>       然而，你却怎么也无法满足。
>       

1.  _“坚守那个 great thought，整个 world 就会来到你身边，是 harmless、peaceable、serene 的。”_我喜欢这句话！它常常是 true 的，却很难记住，所以这种提醒很有价值。变得 impatient 更容易。我喜欢 Tao Te Ching 的一点，是它对 dualities 的强调。“更加 patient”和“更加 impatient”都是极好的建议。事实上，有时它们**在完全相同的情境下**都是极好的建议。这有点像开车穿过一个即将发生事故的路口：大幅加速或减速可能是极好的主意，但保持原速可能是个错误[12](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn12)。
2.  _“如果你 taste 这个 Way，它是 flat、insipid 的。它看起来没什么，听起来也没什么。_ _然而，你却怎么也无法满足。”_我不理解这句话。在我目前的理解阶段，似乎主要值得把它作为一个 assertion 记下来，作为我弄清文本背后意图的过程的一部分。一种有意思的 metaphorical meaning 是：假设我们把 Way 与 underlying laws，也就是 all things 的 source，视为一体。这样的 laws 事实上相当 abstract、inhuman，而且很难与之建立联系；insipid 并非完全不准确。Steven Weinberg 的观察也许与此有关：“universe 越是 comprehensible，它也就越显得 pointless。”

## 总体观察

1.  **“5,000 字的 classic”：**我很喜欢 Tao Te Ching 的这个别名。它让人想到一项每个人都应该尝试的任务：你个人的 5,000 词 classic 会是什么？你能在 5,000 个词中 distill 多少 wisdom？
2.  **100 词的 classics？**想想更加 condensed 的 versions 也很有趣。我想到了几个：
    1.  Jesus 的两条 great commandments：(1) “用你全部的 heart、全部的 soul 和全部的 mind 去 Love 你的 Lord、你的 God”；以及 (2) “像爱自己一样 Love 你的 neighbour”。
    2.  Keanu Reeves 对“你觉得我们死后会发生什么？”的即兴回答：“我知道，爱我们的人会想念我们。”
    3.  Feynman 那段著名的 observation：“如果在某场灾难中，所有 scientific knowledge 都被毁灭，只有一个 sentence 能传给后世的 creatures，那么什么 statement 能用最少的 words 包含最多的 information？我相信是 atomic hypothesis（或者叫 atomic fact，随你怎么称呼），即所有 things 都是由 atoms 构成的——这些 little particles 以 perpetual motion 四处 move around，彼此相隔一点 distance 时会相互 attracting，但在被 squeezed into one another 时又会 repelling。你会发现，只要运用一点 imagination 和 thinking，这一个 sentence 就包含了有关这个世界的大量 information。”
3.  **以 Puzzles 促使人们认真 engagement 于看似 simple 而 familiar 的事物**
    1.  我曾出于相当自以为是的理由，抵触对 Tao Te Ching 进行 deep reading：“Tao Te Ching 里的 ideas 非常 simple，没有必要 deep reading。”确实，总体而言，这些 ideas 比做 difficult mathematics[13](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn13) 要 simpler 得多。然而，关键在于：_一个 idea 可以是 simple 而 important 的，却也可能很难_ _internalize，也很难 understand 它的_ _consequences、据此 act，以及 understand 它的 limits_。Tao Te Ching 讨论了许多这样的 ideas。如果把它们写得明明白白，我们就会点点头，心想“这显而易见”，随后在日常生活中继续做出与之相反的行为。Tao Te Ching 以更加 puzzling 的 form 呈现 ideas，迫使人们进行更深入的 engagement。
    2.  举个例子，只为说明这一点：“善待他人。”当然，我们会说，听起来没错，随后就去做别的事了。但在实践中，善待他人充满了 tensions，因而并不容易。你没睡够。你的脖子疼。孩子们让你头疼。那个人让你想起了前任。大多数 verses 都人为 create tension，让人难以接受它们的 conclusions。我相信，它们这样做，是为了让你静下心来面对这些 ideas，以慢得多的节奏、花长得多的时间去 engagement 于它们。因此，其基本的 media form 是：**每个 verse 都是一个单独的 puzzle，****通常针对一个主要的 idea，这个 idea 往往****是 conventional 的，却值得更深入的 engagement 和 reflection。**
    3.  **另一种 approach（fiction 与 history）：**你也可以通过其他方式 create 这种 tension。在 fiction 中，narrative 迫使 characters 思考这些“simple”的 problems，但它所用的 contexts 会具体展示其中可能涉及的 tensions。这是另一种 problematizing the familiar 的 approach。
    4.  顺带一提，Paul Dirac 曾抱怨：“在 science 中，人们试图以人人都能理解的方式，告诉大家一些此前从未有人知道的东西。但在 poetry 中，情况恰好相反。”Tao Te Ching 说明了这为什么有价值：正如我所说，它迫使我们 question 那些自以为已经 understand 的 ideas，并更深入地 engagement 于它们。然而，这并不意味着，所有 problematizing the familiar 的方式都同样有价值！
4.  **写完这些笔记后，我更喜欢读其他 verses 了！**我仍然经常受阻，或无法理解。但理解起来已经更容易了。我猜，再为几个 verses 写些笔记（也许也为 structure 写一些，见下文），会帮助我从剩余 verses 中获得更多。事实上，我猜一旦跨过某个理解的 threshold，阅读 Tao Te Ching 就会成为一件愉快而且非常有意义的事(?)
5.  **关于 structure 的笔记：**我猜，为 Tao Te Ching 的 overall structure 写笔记会让我学到很多，也可能帮助我更好地理解 local structure。顺带一提，沿着这个思路，[请 ChatGPT](https://x.com/michael_nielsen/status/1836806935678763329) 对这篇 text“进行（metaphorical 的）Fourier transform”很有收获。
6.  **只要读得足够宽厚，几乎任何作品都能变得值得一读：**在 fandoms 中，你常会看到 fans 为理解一本书或一部剧投入越来越多。即便原本的 source material 并不很好，这也能让体验变得十分丰富。Tao Te Ching 的一个优点是，它为 deep reflection 提供了丰富的着眼点。
7.  **认真对待，但不要崇拜。目的是 metabolize：**有些人，尤其是 fans，会崇拜 classic texts，把它们奉上神坛。我认为这是个错误。不是因为我们比作者更聪明，而是因为 culture 在进步，有时我们熟悉的 ideas 已经 supersede 了更早的 ideas。当你认定一篇 text 值得认真对待时，就应该 attack 其中的 ideas，尝试 digest 它们，真正 metabolize 它们。
8.  **作为一种 communion：**有件事曾让我困惑：为什么有些人即便不做 deep reading，也会喜欢 Tao Te Ching？Le Guin 指出，text 的 beauty 起着 essential role。它不“仅仅”是 style，它也是 content。在许多 contexts 中——art、music、sculpture，以及其他许多地方——我都会对 beauty 产生非常强烈的反应，但在 text 中却不太如此。这也许就是我此前如此难以产生共鸣的原因。
9.  **我会从中带走什么？**
    1.  我学到了许多关于 writing 的 ideas，对我来说既新鲜又令人兴奋。尤其让我兴奋的是 problematizing the familiar 这种 approach。我一直困惑，为什么讨论“big issues”的有趣 modern writing 如此之少。出色的书有很多，但往往讨论的是非常 narrow 的 subjects。我想，这是因为要对 big issues 说出新东西并不容易。
    2.  至于能否从这些 ideas 本身带走那么多东西，我就不太确定了。这部分是由于它选择的 form：其中很少有 Hemingway 所说的 true sentences。我不确定是否会有任何 sentences 留在我的脑海中。某种程度上，也是因为 Tao Te Ching 受到了成功的诅咒：它的许多 ideas 如今已经以 improved forms 存在于我们的 culture 中。
    3.  我想我不会马上重读。但我会好奇地观察，这些 ideas 究竟能在脑海中留下多少。未来我也可能再读。

## 原文

[![](https://michaelnotebook.com/assets/home.png)](https://michaelnotebook.com/index.html)

In these notes I reflect on five verses of the Tao Te Ching, as translated by Ursula K. Le Guin in 1997[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn1). Although I had previously skimmed the Tao Te Ching, I never truly connected. My earlier readings were superficial: the Tao Te Ching is not a novel to be read quickly and linearly; it is more like mathematics, demanding engagement and hard work. And, like mathematics, it reveals its depth gradually, with each new reading offering deeper insights and richer meaning.

While writing notes helps focus my thinking, I wasn't ready to commit to annotating all 81 verses of the Tao Te Ching. As an initial commitment, I selected five verses for reflection. What did I not understand? Could I make sense of it? How did I disagree? What new thoughts did the verse inspire? I repeatedly revisited both the verses and my notes, as well as other contextualizing material. Because the Tao Te Ching addresses profound themes, I restricted myself to a few hundred words per verse; it would be easy to write far more. Thus, although these notes have given me a foothold in understanding the text, they remain early steps, not likely of interest to anyone versed in Daoism. But perhaps they are of methodological interest to other people aiming to deeply process similar texts.

## Verse 49: Trust and Power

>       The wise have no mind of their own,
>       finding it in the minds
>       of ordinary people.
> 
>       They're good to good people
>       and they're good to bad people.
>       Power is goodness.
>       They trust people of good faith
>       and they trust people of bad faith.
>       Power is trust.
> 
>       They mingle their life with the world,
>       they mix their mind up with the world.
>       Ordinary people look after them.
>       Wise souls are children.
>       

1.  _"The wise have no mind of their own, finding it in the minds of ordinary people"_: this can't literally be true. But perhaps it means a wise person understands they are not that special? Most wisdom is found in the world, often in surprising places, not in our own minds, no matter how brilliant we (or others) may think we are. So the wise listen well and deeply to many people, not just listening to others they judge wise. Certainly, they don't wallow in self-absorption. Or perhaps the line represents a deference to everyday will. Which might seem silly, but as a governance mechanism (democracy) it has worked surprisingly well[2](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn2).
2.  _"They're good to good people and they're good to bad people."_ An idea that appears in many traditions: that being good is worthwhile in itself, independent of the beneficiary. The justification may be selfish (you benefit by being good), or broadly altruistic (the world benefits). I wonder where this idea first appeared? It's in superficial contradiction to the tit-for-tat strategy in the iterated prisoner's dilemma. I'm sympathetic: tit-for-tat presumes the game continues. When possible, the better response is usually to stop playing and instead find good people to play games with. That said: I admire people who deliberately work to help people who have done bad things. The degree of _hope_ is tremendously moving. An acquaintance used to work as a therapist for men who had abused their wives. That's a marvellous act, both her choice – it could not have been easy work, and she had many other options – and also speaks well of the society that supports it. It may illustrate the intent of the line: she was being good to people who had done bad[3](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn3), but that didn't mean she would put up with bad treatment from them. It's interesting that achieving safety required considerable institutional support.
3.  _"Power is goodness"_: Empirically this is often false! What could they mean by this? Hammurabi famously (and somewhat reasonably) used the defense of the powerless as the justification for his own power. You can also read Hammurabi's (and similar, right up to the modern day) declarations as cynical propaganda. In practice I believe there's much variation in true underlying intent. The idea _is_ admirable as an aspiration: what would such a world be like? How can we design institutions that align power with good? You can view ideas like democracy and markets and the invisible hand and separation of powers as (partially successful) attempts in this direction. In practice, power often insulates people from the consequences of their actions, and leads to their corruption. How to ensure power is goodness?
4.  _"They trust people of good faith and they trust people of bad faith. Power is trust."_ The powerful, being in a position to retaliate, may receive preferential treatment. Though they may also have a larger number of untrustworthy individuals attempt to exploit them. Another perspective is the enormous benefit in a position of default trust. Many of the people I know with the best lives are extremely trusting, and sometimes have been taken advantage of. And yet they benefit overall from their position of default trust. The level of reward depends on the ambient level of goodness in their environment: if it's high, default trust is rewarding; if it's low, default trust may lead to horrors. Perhaps one of the most interesting things about religions is that they often aspire to (and sometimes succeed?[4](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn4)) change that ambient level.
5.  "_They mingle their life with the world, they mix their mind up with the world_": This is consonant, again, with humility about their understanding, realizing that no matter how much they know, there is far more wisdom elsewhere in the world. I'm reminded of Emerson's famous comment: "It is easy in the world to live after the world's opinion; it is easy in solitude to live after our own; but the great man is he who in the midst of the crowd keeps with perfect sweetness the independence of solitude." Though Emerson isn't paying sufficient deference to the wisdom in others. But he understands the tension, and appreciates that it is worth learning to navigate. Something I could learn to do better myself!
6.  _"Ordinary people look after them."_ I don't understand the intent. Does it mean a wise person is dependent, relying on others to look after them? Or is this a reminder that we all look after each other? One remarkable consequence of the division of labour is that it both alienates us from overall purpose _and_ produces much abundance and capacity to care for one another.
7.  _"Wise souls are children."_ This is the line most obviously close to conventional wisdom, indeed, to a platitude[5](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn5). One problem with platitudes is that they're usually not taken seriously: people will nod and then behave contrary to the idea. One helpful thing about this verse is that some of the earlier lines describe child-like behaviour, and challenge you to consider whether it is right to behave in that way. So it creates tension that forces you to engage much more seriously with the final line. This is a good context to have created.
8.  **Wrong ideas mainly:** Most of this seemed wrong to me. And yet it was interesting and stimulating to read in some depth. Closely related: **I didn't really understand any of this, upon a superficial read.** Even when I thought I did, as soon as I began asking questions I realized I hadn't understood. I was reading in a glazed state, ingesting words but not doing any deep processing. It took writing these notes to get anywhere.

## Verse 37: Over all

>       The Way never does anything,
>       and everything gets done.
>       If those in power could hold to the Way,
>       the ten thousand things
>       would look after themselves.
>       If even so they tried to act,
>       I'd quiet them with the nameless,
>       the natural.
> 
>       In the unnamed, in the unshapen,
>       is not wanting.
>       In not wanting is stillness.
>       In stillness all under heaven rests.
>       

1.  _"The Way never does anything, and everything gets done":_ I don't know what this means. It is perhaps an attempt to create confusion in the mind of the reader, creating more mystery around the recurring entity referred to as the Way.
2.  _"If those in power could hold to the Way, the ten thousand things would look after themselves":_ There's a lot of advice to the powerful in the Tao Te Ching. It's often deferential or even sycophantic to power. Power is presumed to be _static_ in the sense that it's something some people have and others do not. Still, the very viewpoint – offering advice to power – is subversive. I wonder how surprised the author (or authors and editors) of the Tao Te Ching would be at the impact on world affairs? They've had far more impact than any of the "powerful" at the time of composition[6](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn6).
3.  "_In the unnamed, in the unshapen, is not wanting. In not wanting is stillness._" This is, ironically, describing a genuine state of mind that one can cultivate. It's not the same as Iris Murdoch's idea of unselfing, but in my experience unselfing – connecting more deeply to the universe around us, and losing something of our sense of self – is often a good preparation for not wanting. And perhaps the reverse as well: when you cease wanting it can help you better commune with your surroundings.
4.  _"In stillness all under heaven rests":_ This seems like a throwaway line, mostly re-expressing the earlier points. It's a lovely image, though – maybe that is the point!
5.  **Wu Wei:** This verse is concerned with _wu wei_, a repeated idea in the Tao Te Ching, meaning non-action or effortless action. This could easily be the subject of a long essay (or lifetime). I will make just a few observations.
    1.  **Harmony and our animal nature:** One read is that wu wei means being passive, living a life of quiet contemplation in harmony with the wold. "In not wanting is stillness. In stillness all under heaven rests". But human beings are (in part) pan African plains apes. We are _animals_. We all have ambitions and desires and a drive for change; in some of us this is very strong. To deny that is itself to live in disharmony with our nature. I wonder to what extent wu wei is a coherent notion at all[7](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn7)?
    2.  **Connection to flow:** The verse is closely connected to the notion of "flow" in modern psychology. In a state of flow we act in near-effortless harmony with our environment.
        1.  **Against flow:** Flow is often idolized. I think that's a mistake. It's best adapted to activities like tennis, where you want to spend most of a match in flow, unconflicted with goal or environment. Or acting on stage, where actors ideally identify with their character. But while practicing you'll often be out of flow, learning to do things which at first are awkward and uncomfortable[8](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn8). Often, if you're in flow too much it means you're learning little. This is true even for relatively well-defined tasks, like playing tennis. And it's even more true for highly creative tasks, which require that you get used to failure, to periods of (so-called) "unproductivity" and frustration, where the very notion of efficiency is a mistake. This doesn't mean you don't want to spend some time in flow. But it's a mistake to idolize flow as an aspiration. A better question: what's the right balance between flow and uncomfortable work, and how can we move closer to that balance?
        2.  **Higher flow:** That said, there is also a notion that I think of as _higher flow_, in which you are comfortable with persistence, gumption, embracing mistakes, not being ashamed to be "bad" at things, living equably in the world, being happy to be in a state of creative uncertainty. These are skills too! And skills you can get better at. Children often start out very good at them. They lose that as they become more conscious of themselves as social beings, and begin to internalize social judgements. "Am I doing the right thing? Is this good enough? Will my friends still like me?" And then, they (sometimes) grow through that. (No intent to claim I've grown through it: I have in some ways, but I'm still very much working on others!)
    3.  **E. O. Wilson:** Much stress is caused by tension between our individual selfish desires and group needs. Wilson points out this tension between individual and group is founded in evolutionary history, and appears in all eusocial species, not only human beings. As an example: many human systems (certainly, our economic system) serve the group more than they serve most individuals. Think of how many people have trouble finding an intrinsically satisfying job. Even many "good" jobs are alienating and dull, poorly suited for individuals, existing because they serve an economically valuable collective purpose. There's also many softer versions of this tension. When we reluctantly feel "I should do such-and-such" \[perhaps go to the office Christmas party\] it's often the result of such a conflict. My view is that such conflicts aren't to be rejected or regarded as bad. Rather, they're at the heart of human life, and learning to understand them deeply, sometimes even embrace them, is part of living well.
    4.  **Conflicted nature:** More generally, I take wu wei to be a critique of conflicted nature. But conflicted nature is only bad when it's all you ever experience. It is a condition to embrace and understand, while realizing that being unconflicted some of the time is valuable. There is some viewpoint in which one must choose between the Will to Power and submission to nature. And another healthier viewpoint in which they actually work together. Ideally, wu wei is about this latter.
6.  **A modern version:** We understand many things today where the Tao Te Ching is too simplistic. We saw above an example: the lack of understanding that humans are animals, with animal drives. It's interesting to ponder rewriting the Tao Te Ching to correct those errors[9](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn9). Related: the idea of writing discovery fiction for the Tao Te Ching (or other similar works).

## Verse 1: Taoing

>       The way you can go
>       isn't the real way.
>       The name you can say
>       isn't the real name.
> 
>       Heaven and earth
>       begin in the unnamed:
>       name's the mother
>       of the ten thousand things.
> 
>       So the unwanting soul
>       sees what's hidden,
>       and the ever-wanting soul
>       sees only what it wants.
> 
>       Two things, one origin,
>       but different in name,
>       whose identity is mystery.
>       Mystery of all mysteries!
>       The door to the hidden.
>       

1.  _"The way you can go isn't the real way. The name you can say isn't the real name. Heaven and earth begin in the unnamed":_ The Tao Te Ching begins with a warning! Words and internal models – our own minds – might be our reality. But they are not reality! The text which is to follow isn't reality. It's a little tricky, since we (and the words) are part of reality. But they are only a tiny part, and we shouldn't confuse words with the whole reality[10](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn10).
2.  _"So the unwanting soul sees what's hidden, and the ever-wanting soul sees only what it wants":_ Literally false, but pointing at something real. Be careful what (and how much) you want, for it will change what you see. This can be a very useful pattern. It can also be immensely destructive. I am skeptical the unwanting soul sees what's hidden. I think the unwanting soul often sees little. But it can be a good transition state and preparation to see.
3.  _"Two things, one origin, but different in name, whose identity is mystery. Mystery of all mysteries! The door to the hidden."_ As nearly as I understand, what's going on is that both the Way (the Tao) and the named have their origin in the Way. That Way is fundamentally a mystery, but is the source of all things. It's pretty close to a form of pantheism. Also to Korzybski's "the map is not the territory". Though it's more like "the territory is the source of both map and territory, but don't confuse the former for the latter", and with the caveat that we don't have direct access to the territory. It's also somewhat like the relationship between our written mathematics and the underlying Platonic reality. Something I like about this is the reminder that it _is_ a mystery.

## Verse 48: Unlearning

>       Studying and learning daily you grow larger.
>       Following the Way daily you shrink.
>       You get smaller and smaller.
>       So you arrive at not doing.
>       You do nothing and nothing's not done.
> 
>       To run things,
>       don't fuss with them.
>       Nobody who fusses
>       is fit to run things.
>       

1.  _"Studying and learning daily you grow larger. Following the Way daily you shrink. You get smaller and smaller._ _So you arrive at not doing. You do nothing and nothing's not done"_: Again, as in the others, a puzzle is set up. Surely studying and learning and growing larger is good! So how is it in contradiction to the Way? Why does following the Way make you shrink? Does that make following the Way bad? Or does it mean studying and learning is bad? I suspect it's simply meant as a false dichotomy. Iris Murdoch's notion of unselfing comes to mind again: getting more connected to the universe, and less self-involved. Einstein put it interestingly, describing his life as a flight "from the I and the We to the It"; also his comment that "The most beautiful thing we can experience is the mysterious". I must say, I prefer Einstein's concrete engagement with the unself to the Tao Te Ching's too-abstract notion! When you read deeply into Einstein's writing and his life, it's astounding just how deeply and with how much concrete detail he explored the universe (and, indeed, the mysterium tremendum). But Einstein had the advantage that in modern times we _can_ be far better connected to the unself. An example, one of many: his abandonment of the cosmological constant after Hubble's discovery of the expanding universe! I also admire Feynman's passionate commitment to the primacy of "doubt and uncertainty and not knowing". Again: carried out in great detail. I'm far afield here! To refocus on the line from the Tao Te Ching: perhaps it should simply be taken to mean focusing one's studying and learning on connection to something larger[11](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn11).
2.  _"To run things, don't fuss with them. Nobody who fusses is fit to run things."_ Le Guin notes that the word she translates as "fuss" is considered problematic by translators, and that another translation is "diplomatic". The result is: "To run things, be undiplomatic. No diplomat is fit to run things". I find this translation easier to understand! Diplomacy is often taken to mean leaving hard truths unstated. (Good diplomacy I suspect is often the opposite.) And that makes it harder to run things well, since it makes it harder to establish the truth as common knowledge. That said: what does this have to do with the first half of the verse? Maybe it's that insofar as we deal with truth, it is larger than us; we occupy less space, in some sense. Whereas our lies and misconceptions and misunderstandings are only in our self.

## Verse 35: Humane power

>       Hold fast to the great thought
>       and all the world will come to you,
>       harmless, peaceable, serene.
> 
>       Walking around, we stop
>       for music, for food.
>       But if you taste the Way
>       it's flat, insipid.
>       It looks like nothing much,
>       it sounds like nothing much.
>       And yet you can't get enough of it.
>       

1.  _"Hold fast to the great thought and all the world will come to you, harmless, peaceable, serene."_ I like it! It's so often true. And hard to remember, so the reminder is valuable. It's easier to be impatient. Something I enjoy in the Tao Te Ching is the emphasis on dualities. "Be more patient" and "be more impatient" are both excellent pieces of advice. Indeed, sometimes they're both excellent pieces of advice **in the exact same situation**. It can be a bit like driving a car through an intersection where an accident is about to happen: it may be an excellent idea to greatly speed up or slow down, but staying the same speed may be a mistake[12](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn12).
2.  _"if you taste the Way, it's flat, insipid. It looks like nothing much, it sounds like nothing much._ _And yet you can't get enough of it."_ I don't understand this. At this stage of my understanding, it seems mostly worth noting as an assertion, part of my process of figuring out the intent behind the text. One interesting metaphorical meaning: suppose one identifies the Way with the underlying laws, the source of all things. Such laws are, in fact, rather abstract and inhuman and hard to connect with; insipid isn't entirely inaccurate. Steven Weinberg's observation "The more the universe is comprehensible the more it also seems pointless" may be related.

## General observations

1.  **"The 5,000 character classic":** I love this alternate title for the Tao Te Ching. It suggests a task everyone should attempt: what is your personal 5,000 word classic? How much wisdom can you distill into 5,000 words?
2.  **100 word classics?** It's enjoyable to think about even more condensed versions. A few that come to mind:
    1.  Jesus's two great commandments: (1) "Love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind"; and (2) "Love your neighbour as yourself".
    2.  Keanu Reeves' spontaneous response to "What do you think happens when we die?": "I know that the ones who love us will miss us."
    3.  Feynman's famous observation: "If, in some cataclysm, all of scientific knowledge were to be destroyed, and only one sentence passed on to the next generations of creatures, what statement would contain the most information in the fewest words? I believe it is the atomic hypothesis (or the atomic fact, or whatever you wish to call it) that all things are made of atoms – little particles that move around in perpetual motion, attracting each other when they are a little distance apart, but repelling upon being squeezed into one another. In that one sentence, you will see, there is an enormous amount of information about the world, if just a little imagination and thinking are applied."
3.  **Puzzles as a way to cause serious engagement with what seems simple and familiar**
    1.  I resisted the idea of a deep reading of the Tao Te Ching for rather snotty reasons: "the ideas in the Tao Te Ching are very simple, there's no need for deep reading". It's true that the ideas are on the whole much simpler than doing difficult mathematics[13](https://michaelnotebook.com/tao/index.html#fn13). However, crucially: _an idea can be simple and important, but also hard_ _to internalize and understand the_ _consequences of, to act on, and to understand the limits of_. The Tao Te Ching is concerned with many such ideas. If they were written out plainly, we'd nod our heads, think "that's obvious", and then continue acting contrary to them in our everyday life. By presenting ideas in a more puzzling form, the Tao Te Ching forces deeper engagement.
    2.  An example, just to illustrate the point: "Be kind to others". Sure, we say, sounds right, and move on to other things. But in practice being kind is fraught with tensions which make it hard. You didn't get enough sleep. Your neck hurts. Your kids are giving you a hard time. The person reminds you of your ex. Most of the verses artificially create tension which make it hard to accept their conclusions. I believe they're doing that to get you to sit with the ideas and engage with them much more slowly and at length. The basic media form is then: **Each verse is a single puzzle,** **usually aimed at one main idea, often an idea that** **is conventional, but bears deeper engagement and reflection.**
    3.  **A different approach (fiction and history):** You can create this kind of tension in other ways. In fiction, the narrative forces characters to consider such "simple" problems, but in contexts which concretely illustrate the tensions which may be involved. It's a different approach to problematizing the familiar.
    4.  Incidentally, Paul Dirac once complained: "In science one tries to tell people, in such a way as to be understood by everyone, something that no one ever knew before. But in poetry, it's the exact opposite." The Tao Te Ching illustrates why this is valuable: as I said, it forces us to question and more deeply engage with ideas we already think we understand. That does not mean, however, that every way of problematizing the familiar is equally valuable!
4.  **After writing these notes, I'm enjoying the other verses more!** I'm still often stymied or uncomprehending. But I understand more easily. And I suspect writing notes on a few more verses (and perhaps on structure, see below) would help me get more out of the remaining verses. Indeed, I suspect that once I've passed some threshold of understanding, reading the Tao Te Ching will be a pleasure and highly meaningful(?)
5.  **Notes on structure:** I suspect I'd learn a lot by writing notes on the overall structure of the Tao Te Ching, and perhaps it would help me understand the local structure better. Incidentally, in this vein it was [rewarding to ask ChatGPT](https://x.com/michael_nielsen/status/1836806935678763329) to "take the (metaphorical) Fourier transform" of the text.
6.  **A generous enough read will make almost anything worthwhile:** In fandoms, you often see fans put more and more into their understanding of a book or show. It can make the experience very rich, even when the underlying source material isn't very good. A benefit of the Tao Te Ching is that it is a rich focus for deep reflection.
7.  **Take seriously, but don't venerate. The aim is to metabolize:** Some people, especially fans, venerate classic texts, and place them on a pedestal. I think that's a mistake. Not because we're smarter than the author. But culture advances, and sometimes ideas familiar to us supersede older ideas. When you've decided a text is worth taking seriously, you should attack the ideas, try to digest them, truly metabolize them.
8.  **As an act of communion:** Something that puzzled me is why some people enjoy the Tao Te Ching even without deep reading. Le Guin points out that the beauty of the text plays an essential role. It is not "merely" style, it is content. I respond very strongly to beauty in many contexts – in art, music, sculpture, and many other places – but not so much in text. And that is perhaps why I had so much trouble connecting.
9.  **What will I take away?**
    1.  I've learned many ideas about writing that are new and exciting to me. I'm especially excited about the approach to problematizing the familiar. I've puzzled why there is so little interesting modern writing on "big issues". Many terrific books are written, but often on very narrow subjects. And I think it's because it can be hard to say something fresh about big issues.
    2.  I'm less sure I'll take away that much from the ideas themselves. This is partly due to the chosen form: there are few of Hemingway's true sentences. I'm not sure any of the sentences will stick in my mind. And to some extent it's that the Tao Te Ching suffers the curse of success: many of its ideas are now carried by our culture in improved forms.
    3.  I don't think I'll immediately return. But I will be curious to observe how much the ideas stay in mind. And I may return in future.
