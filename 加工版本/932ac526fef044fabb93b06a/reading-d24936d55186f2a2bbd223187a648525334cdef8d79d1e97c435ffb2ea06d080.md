# On inclusivity in Less Wrong: a response to Scott Alexander

---

## 正文

_感谢 Ruthie Byers 启发了这篇文章中的许多观点，并对草稿提供了很棒的反馈。_

[最近](http://apophemi.wordpress.com/2014/01/04/why-im-not-on-the-rationalist-masterlist/)，Less Wrong community 围绕对 minorities 的 inclusivity 发生了[一场](http://lesswrong.com/r/discussion/lw/jfr/link_why_im_not_on_the_rationalist_masterlist/)[~轩然大波~ 小风波](http://slatestarcodex.com/2014/01/12/a-response-to-apophemi-on-triggers/)[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/inclusivity/#fn:1)。一位名叫 Apophemi 的人写了一篇博客文章，说明 ze[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/inclusivity/#fn:2) 为什么不 participate in Less Wrong，并指出，对某些议题的 dispassionate discussion 让 ze 感到 threatened：

> 我……要求任何与我交谈的人 adherence to 这些 ideas，或者至少不对它们提出 explicit challenges，然后我才能以 good faith 来 entertain 他们的 arguments。

这惹恼了一些 Less Wrong 的人，包括 Scott Alexander；那篇文章显然主要是针对他的。于是 Scott 写了一篇出色而详尽的 response，解释说 safe spaces 很好，但也需要供 dispassionate discussion 使用的 safe spaces：

> 这让我想起了 safe spaces 这个 idea。
> 
> Safe spaces 是 disadvantaged groups 的 members 可以去的地方，他们通常受到保护，不受其他 groups 中那些容易 trigger 他们的人的影响，并且可以在不受 ridicule 或 attack 的情况下 discuss 与该 group relevant 的事情。我知道有许多这样的地方是为 women 设置的，一些是为 gays 设置的，而且我最近听说一所大学为 atheists 开设了一个。它们似乎是不错的 ideas。
> 
> 我将 Apophemi 的 proposal 理解为：rationalist community 应当努力成为 women、minorities 和其他 disadvantaged groups 的 safe space。
> 
> safe spaces 的一个重要 feature 是，它们无法总是在同一时间对两个 groups 都是 safe。Jews 是受到 discriminated-against 的 minority，需要一个 safe space。Muslims 是受到 discriminated-against 的 minority，也需要一个 safe space。但 Jews 的 safe space 应当离 Muslims 的 safe space 非常远，否则两个 space 对任何人都不是 safe。
> 
> rationalist community 是这样一些人的 safe space：即使这样做是 socially unacceptable，他们仍然 obsessively focus on reason and argument。
> 
> 我不认为，说这些人需要一个 safe space 是不公平的。我因为说了些 rational 的话而被叫作“nerd”“dork”或“autistic”的次数，多得我根本数不过来。就在最近，Marginal Revolution 上的评论者——那里可不是什么以 intellect-hating jocks 聚集而出名的地方——找到了我的一篇旧文章，除了许多其他称呼外，还叫我“aspie”“娘娘腔”“retarded”和“omega”（一个 PUA term，指 socially inept 到永远不会与任何人 date 的 man）。

一如既往，Scott 的文章读来令人愉快，而且我认为他的大体观点是对的：Less Wrong 不应在成为 Apophemi 想要的那种 safe space 的方向上走得太远。正如几位评论者在 LW 对 Apophemi 文章的讨论中指出的，inclusiveness 与 freedom of speech 之间存在 trade-off，而这个 trade-off 两侧的 communities 都有各自的位置。

不过，我担心 Apophemi 的 extreme position 引发了一篇过于 extreme 的 rebuttal，而整场 debate 正在让人忽略一个更广泛、也更有价值的观点。也就是说，inclusivity 与 free speech 之间存在 tradeoff，但 _Less Wrong 在 tradeoff-space 中并不处于 [Pareto optimum](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_optimality)_。我们几乎肯定可以在 inclusivity 这个 dimension 上 improve，而_不必_ sacrificing free discourse。

也就是说，Scott 太急于 dismiss“Less Wrong 应当变得更加 inclusive”这一 suggestion，因为 Apophemi 的文章将讨论 anchored 到了一些成为 inclusive 的方式上，而这些方式也会 radically reduce freedom of discussion。这带来的结果，是让人很容易彻底 dismiss Less Wrong 在 inclusivity 上的 problems。这似乎正是促使 Scott 将 Less Wrong 与 yoga class 相比较的原因：

> 我上过几次 yoga classes。最近一次参加的课上大约有三十位 women，再加上我（那是在 Ireland；我不知道 American yoga 是否有不同的 gender balance）。
> 
> 我们为这个显然 significant 的 result 提出两个不同的 explanations。
> 
> 首先，这些 yoga classes 不知怎么在 driving men away。也许他们说了一些关于 men 的刻薄话（也许并非有意！我们不是说他们是 intentionally misandrist！），或者他们谈论议题的方式对 male viewpoints 是 exclusionary。yoga class 应当邀请一些 men’s rights activists 来，向参加者讲解他们可以怎样让 men 感到舒适，也许还应当在每堂课中花一些时间，讨论对 men 极为重要的议题，比如 Truckasaurus。
> 
> 其次，men 就是没有 women 那么喜欢 yoga。人们可以为此提出一个大概会很滑稽的 evolutionary genetic explanation（比如，women 在 ancestral environment 中是 gatherers，因此她们需要很强的 flexibility，才能弯腰采摘小植物，这个说法怎么样？），但更有可能的只是，men 与 women 以许多微妙的不同方式被 socialized，而他们最终形成的 interests 和 values，在 women 身上更为 pro-yoga，在 men 身上更为 anti-yoga。在这种情况下，yoga class 仍然可以通过极其明确地表示欢迎 men，并去除几件可能让 men 不自在的事情而受益，但除非彻底 re-ordering society，否则他们能为实现 equal gender balance 做的事情并不多，也不应因为他们没有做到而责怪他们。
> 
> 对我参加的 yoga class 来说，第二个 explanation 似乎要 plausible 得多；说实话，对 rationalist community 来说，它似乎也要 plausible 得多。

所以……women 不会被泛滥的 evo-bio-determinism 劝退？不会被关于 pickup artistry 的 discussions 劝退？不会被 comment threads 中普遍令人作呕的东西劝退？因为这些东西确实存在。人们觉得 Less Wrong 是 exclusionary。我_问过_他们，而他们_就是这么说的_；就他们是否因此不愿 contributing 而言，事情到这里就已经说清楚了。[3](https://www.benkuhn.net/inclusivity/#fn:3)

我的感觉是，这个网站正在变好。但有些 meetups 的氛围（在那里，不必担心自己的言行会被永久留在互联网上）仍然相当糟糕。当需求已经大到足以让 Bay Area mailing list 开始建立一个 women’s-only group 时，_我们就有 problem 了_，不管我们多么努力地争辩、找借口，试图把它说成不存在。

Scott 接着试图说明，Less Wrong 的 demographics 实际上根本不能作为它是 exclusionary 的 evidence：

> 大多数 rationalists 来自 computer science community，其中大约 80% 是 male。少数来自 math 和 physics 这样的 hard science fields，这两个领域都有 80 – 90% 是 male。完全没有必要援引“没有 limitations 的 discourse”，来 explanation 为什么 rationalist community 如此 heavily male-dominated……

还有 economics（30% 是 female）和 psychology _（seventy percent 是 female）_。Rationalists 中大约 [10% 是 female](http://lesswrong.com/lw/jj0/2013_survey_results/)。而且 computer science _已经_受到 unfriendly discourse 的困扰。

> 你提到那些可能被 driven away 的 minorities groups，我们实际上并不缺少。事实上，在许多情况下，我们拥有的人数远远超过 by chance 所 expected 的人数。例如，我们这里 transgender people 的 rate 大约是 general population 的五倍（1.5% vs. 0.3%），而 gays/lesbians/bisexuals 的 rate 大约是 general population 的三倍（15% vs. 4%）。Jewish by descent 的人数是 national average 的四倍（8% vs. 2%），有 mental disorders 的人的比例则要么与 general population 大致 equal，要么高得多得多，这取决于如何 interprets 我收集得一塌糊涂的 data（抱歉）。我们这里以 English as a second language 的人，比我知道的几乎任何其他 online community 都多（rationalists per capita 最多的国家仍然是 Finland），而且还有来自 Kenya、Pakistan、Egypt 和 Indonesia 的 members。

如果上面我们是在与 computer science 的 base rate comparing，为什么这里却与 general population compare？在 [people of Jewish descent](http://www.jinfo.org/Computer_Info_Science.html) 方面，我们显著不如 computer science；[foreigners](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_born_scientists_and_engineers_in_the_United_States) 也是如此（例如，grad schools 中占 30%），因此 ESL speakers 很可能也是如此；甚至 Asian people 可能也是如此，尽管我找不到任何 statistics。你完全可以随心所欲地 cherry-pick 自己的 indicators 和 reference classes。

而且，即使与 general population 相比，我们在 Hispanic 和 Black Less Wrongers 方面也低于 average，Scott 却省略了这一点——这个 argument 实在没有呈现一个 balanced case。

> 更糟的是，这个 hypothesis 在另一个方向上也 fails。有很多 groups 对 minorites 极其 offensive，却仍然能拥有非常多这样的 members。在几乎每一个 denomination 中，去 church 的 women 都远多于 men——如果你去参加一场 Catholic Mass，就会看到长椅上坐满了女士，人数之多是 atheist community 做梦都想达到的。atheist community 是如此 feminist，以至于已经出现了一场认真的 movement，要用 excludes 所有 non-feminists 的“Atheism Plus”取代它；Catholic Church 则是如此 regressive，以至于不允许 women 成为 priests，并认为她们被创造出来就是为了给 man 当“helpmeet”。然而，women 在 aggregate 上却喜爱后者，厌恶前者。

Scott 在这里很可能是对的，但在这个具体 example 中，他进行了一些创造性的 reinterpretation。

据我了解，atheism 的[名声](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevatorgate#Elevator_incident)恰恰来自它存在 gender [issues](http://freethoughtblogs.com/lousycanuck/2013/08/12/sexual-harassment-accusations-in-the-skeptical-and-secular-communities-a-timeline-of-major-events/)。之所以出现这个 splinter movement，是因为 atheists _实在太不善于做到 inclusive_，以至于 atheist feminists 决定她们需要重新开始。所以，我认为“如此 feminist，以至于……”是一个有些 misleading 的说法。Atheism Plus 看起来大致 analogous to 比如 Catholic feminist theology，而后者完全是存在的（虽然没有到“exclude 所有 non-feminists！”的程度，但它确实存在）。

> 我们总可以看看，邀请一些 feminists 来，不加反驳地听她们说话，并同意照她们所说的一切去做，是否会有帮助……

我认为，可以借这句话来总结我对这篇文章的不满。

基本上，对 Apophemi 的 rebuttal 没有问题。我同意，没有 limitations 的 discourse 是 Less Wrong 的重要组成部分，不应消失，这没问题。但这并不能成为 overreaching 和 ignoring middle ground 的借口。Less Wrong _确实_存在 discourse marginalizes 人们的 problems。我们至少需要注意到，我们正在这里做出一个 trade-off，并在可能的时候，寻找沿两个 axes 都能 improve 的方式。

## 原文

_Thanks to Ruthie Byers for inspiring many of the points in this post and providing awesome feedback on a draft._

There was [recently](http://apophemi.wordpress.com/2014/01/04/why-im-not-on-the-rationalist-masterlist/) [something](http://lesswrong.com/r/discussion/lw/jfr/link_why_im_not_on_the_rationalist_masterlist/) of a [~brouhaha~ kerfuffle](http://slatestarcodex.com/2014/01/12/a-response-to-apophemi-on-triggers/)[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/inclusivity/#fn:1) in the Less Wrong community over inclusivity to minorities. A person known as Apophemi wrote a blog post about why ze[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/inclusivity/#fn:2) didn’t participate in Less Wrong, noting that ze felt threatened by dispassionate discussion of certain issues:

> I… require adherence to these ideas or at least a lack of explicit challenges to them on the part of anyone speaking to me before I can entertain their arguments in good faith.

This raised the hackles of some Less Wrong folk, including Scott Alexander, at whom the essay was apparently primarily directed. So Scott wrote an excellent and detailed response, explaining that safe spaces were great, but there needed to be safe spaces for dispassionate discussion too:

> This reminds me of the idea of safe spaces.
> 
> Safe spaces are places where members of disadvantaged groups can go, usually protected against people in other groups who tend to trigger them, and discuss things relevant to that group free from ridicule or attack. I know there are many for women, some for gays, and I recently heard of a college opening one up for atheists. They seem like good ideas.
> 
> I interpret Apophemi’s proposal to say that the rationalist community should endeavor to be a safe space for women, minorities, and other disadvantaged groups.
> 
> One important feature of safe spaces is that they can’t always be safe for two groups at the same time. Jews are a discriminated-against minority who need a safe space. Muslims are a discriminated-against minority who need a safe space. But the safe space for Jews should be very far way from the safe space for Muslims, or else neither space is safe for anybody.
> 
> The rationalist community is a safe space for people who obsessively focus on reason and argument even when it is socially unacceptable to do so.
> 
> I don’t think it’s unfair to say that these people need a safe space. I can’t even count the number of times I’ve been called “a nerd” or “a dork” or “autistic” for saying something rational is too high to count. Just recently commenters on Marginal Revolution – not exactly known for being a haunt for intellect-hating jocks – found an old post of mine and called me among many other things “aspie”, “a pansy”, “retarded”, and an “omega” (a PUA term for a man who’s so socially inept he will never date anyone).

As always, Scott’s post was a pleasure to read, and I think his broad point is right: Less Wrong shouldn’t go too far in the direction of becoming the kind of safe space that Apophemi wants it to be. As several commenters on the LW discussion of Apophemi’s post pointed out, there’s a trade-off between inclusiveness and freedom of speech, and there are places for communities on both sides of the trade-off.

However, I’m afraid that Apophemi’s extreme position inspired a too-extreme rebuttal, and the whole debate is distracting from a broader and more valuable point. Namely, there’s a tradeoff between inclusivity and free speech, but _Less Wrong is not at a [Pareto optimum](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_optimality) in tradeoff-space_. We can almost certainly improve on the dimension of inclusivity _without_ sacrificing free discourse.

That is, Scott is too hasty to dismiss the suggestion “Less Wrong should become more inclusive” because Apophemi’s post anchored the discussion toward ways of becoming inclusive that also radically reduce freedom of discussion. The result of that is a temptation is to dismiss Less Wrong’s problems with inclusivity entirely. Which seems to be what led to Scott comparing Less Wrong to a yoga class:

> I have been to several yoga classes. The last one I attended consisted of about thirty women, plus me (this was in Ireland; I don’t know if American yoga has a different gender balance).
> 
> We propose two different explanations for this obviously significant result.
> 
> First, these yoga classes are somehow driving men away. Maybe they say mean things about men (maybe without intending it! we’re not saying they’re intentionally misandrist!) or they talk about issues in a way exclusionary to male viewpoints. The yoga class should invite some men’s rights activists in to lecture the participants on what they can do to make men feel comfortable, and maybe spend some of every class discussing issues that matter deeply to men, like Truckasaurus.
> 
> Second, men just don’t like yoga as much as women. One could propose a probably hilarious evolutionary genetic explanation for this (how about women being gatherers in the ancestral environment, so they needed lots of flexibility so they could bend down and pick small plants?) but much more likely is just that men and women are socialized differently in a bunch of subtle ways and the interests and values they end up with are more pro-yoga in women and more anti-yoga in men. In this case a yoga class might still benefit by making it super-clear that men are welcome and removing a couple of things that might make men uncomfortable, but short of completely re-ordering society there’s not much they can do to get equal gender balance and it shouldn’t be held against them that they don’t.
> 
> The second explanation seems much more plausible for my yoga class, and honestly it seems much more plausible for the rationalist community as well.

So… women aren’t turned away by the rampant evo-bio-determinism? The discussions of pickup artistry? The generally ugh in the comment threads? Because it’s totally there. People find Less Wrong exclusionary. I’ve _asked_ them and they _said so_, and as far as being discouraged from contributing goes, that’s the end of the story.[3](https://www.benkuhn.net/inclusivity/#fn:3)

My sense is that the site is getting better. But the atmosphere at some of the meetups (where there’s no danger of being immortalized on the internet) is still pretty dire. At the point at which there’s enough demand for the Bay Area mailing list to start a women’s-only group, _we have a problem_, no matter how hard we try to argue and excuse it away.

Scott goes on to try to show that Less Wrong’s demographics aren’t actually evidence that it’s exclusionary at all:

> Most rationalists come from the computer science community, which is something like 80% male. A few come from hard science fields like math and physics, both of which are 80 – 90% male. There is zero need to invoke “discourse without limitations” as an explanation for why the rationalist community is heavily male-dominated…

Also economics (30% female) and psychology _(seventy percent female)_. Rationalists are about [10% female](http://lesswrong.com/lw/jj0/2013_survey_results/). And computer science _already_ suffers from unfriendly discourse.

> We’re not actually missing all those groups you mention as minorities who might be driven away. In fact, in many cases, we have far more of them than would be expected by chance. For example, we contain transgender people at about five times the rate in the general population (1.5% vs. 0.3%), and gays/lesbians/bisexuals at about three times the rate in the general population (15% vs. 4%). People who Jewish by descent are four times the national average (8% vs. 2%), and people with mental disorders are either around equal to the general population or much much higher, depending on how one interprets the data I did a terrible job collecting (sorry). We have more people with English as a second language than almost any other online community I know (the country with most rationalists per capita continues to be Finland) and members from Kenya, Pakistan, Egypt, and Indonesia.

If we’re comparing to the base rate for computer science above, why compare to the general population here? We’re significantly worse than computer science on [people of Jewish descent](http://www.jinfo.org/Computer_Info_Science.html); [foreigners](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_born_scientists_and_engineers_in_the_United_States) (30% in grad schools, e.g.) and hence probably ESL speakers also; and probably even Asian people, though I can’t find any statistics. You can cherry-pick your indicators and reference classes any way you like.

And even against the general population, we’re worse than average for Hispanic and Black Less Wrongers, which Scott omitted—this argument doesn’t really present a balanced case.

> Worse, the hypothesis fails in the other direction as well. There are lots of groups that are horribly offensive towards minorites yet nevertheless manage to have very many of them. Across nearly every denomination, far more women than men go to church – if you go to a Catholic Mass, you will see pews full of ladies at levels the atheist community can only dream of. The atheist community is so feminist that there has been a serious movement to replace it with “Atheism Plus” that excludes all non-feminists; the Catholic Church is so regressive that it won’t let women become priests and thinks they were created as a “helpmeet” for man. And yet women, in aggregate, love the one and hate the other.

It seems likely that Scott is right here, but with this specific example there’s some creative reinterpretation going on.

My understanding is that atheism is [famous](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elevatorgate#Elevator_incident) for having gender [issues](http://freethoughtblogs.com/lousycanuck/2013/08/12/sexual-harassment-accusations-in-the-skeptical-and-secular-communities-a-timeline-of-major-events/). The splinter movement is because atheists were _so bad at being inclusive_ that atheist feminists decided they needed to start over. So I think “so feminist that…” is something of a misleading phrase. Atheism Plus seems roughly analogous to, e.g., Catholic feminist theology, which totally exists (not to the level of “exclude all non-feminists!” but it’s there).

> We could always see whether it might help to inviting some feminists in, listen to them without protest, and agree to do whatever they say…

I think this line is a good place to sum up my issues with this post.

Basically, the rebuttal of Apophemi is fine. I agree, discourse without limitations is an important part of Less Wrong and shouldn’t go away, fine. But that’s no excuse for overreaching and ignoring the middle ground. Less Wrong _does_ have problems with discourse that marginalizes people. We need to at least notice that we’re making a trade-off here, and look for ways to improve along both axes when we can.
