# 随机浏览 HN｜Randomize HN

---

## 正文

你有没有注意到，在 HN 这样的 sites 上，要进入 front page 存在一个有趣的 threshold？确切的 threshold 会随 traffic 的多少而 varies，不过，对于并非极其 popular 的 articles，总会有一个时刻，article 处于 N-1 votes。也许有 60% 的 chance，那个 vote 会到来，article 随之被推上 front page，并在那里收到大量 votes。也许有 40% 的 chance，它永远也得不到那个将它推上 front page 的 vote，从此永远默默无闻。

一篇 [in expectation](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/probability/random-variables-topic/random_variables_prob_dist/v/expected-value--e-x) 会收到 50 votes 的 article，却有 60% 的 chance 获得 100+ votes，还有 40% 的 chance 只获得 2 votes，这是 non-optimal 的。理想情况下，每篇 article 都应始终获得其 expected number of votes，并在 front page 停留 expected number of time，让 readers 对 article 的 exposure 与其 popularity 成比例。实际却是，由于 random happenstance，许多有趣的 content 从未进入 front page，结果，那些确实进入的 content 获得了高于 optimal 水平的 exposure。

在那些一经 posted 就把东西推上 front page 的 low traffic sites 上，例如 [lobste.rs](https://lobste.rs/) 和[规模较小的 sub-reddits](http://www.reddit.com/r/programming/)，你也会看到同样的问题，只是 sign bit flipped：它们把几乎无人在意的 links 放上 front page，挤掉大多数人会感兴趣的 links，仅仅是为了让人们确实在意的少数东西获得足够的 exposure，从而得到 upvoted。在 reddit 上，users 通过大量 downvoting 大多数 submissions 来“修复”这个问题，把它们挤出 front page，结果造成了一个与 HN 面临的问题根本相同的问题。

sites 没有 implementing 某种简单且易于 optimize 的东西，而是不断堆叠 ad hoc rules。Reddit [implemented 了 rising page](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6499144)，但它并未解决问题。在 [r/programming](http://www.reddit.com/r/programming/rising) 这样的 low-traffic subreddits 上，threshold 高得让这个页面几乎总是空的。在 high-traffic sub-reddits 上，凡是获得足够 upvoted、能够进入 rising page 的东西，都已经非常成功了，而一篇 article 能否成功，很大程度上取决于最初几个 voters 是否恰好会 upvote 这个 post，而不是 downvoting 它；也就是说，进入 rising page 的问题，与通常登上榜首的问题并无不同。

HN 试图通过 manually penalizing 某些 domains 和 keywords 来解决问题。这并没有解决那 95% 未被 penalized 的 posts 所面临的问题。对于未能进入 front page 的 posts，显而易见的 workaround 是[如果你的 post 第一次未能进入 front page，就 delete 并 re-submit 它](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6861735)，但如今这已经是[足以受到 ban 的违规行为](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6861740)。当然，人们正在绕过这个限制，而 HN 又有针对这个 workaround 的 workaround，如此往复。没完没了。这就是“简单”的 ad hoc solutions 的问题。

有一个容易的 fix，不过它是 counter-intuitive 的。通过向一篇 article 的 rank 中 adding 少量 random noise，我们可以 smooth out 进入 front page 与默默无闻之间的 discontinuity。math 很简单，而 intuition 更简单[1](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:A)。想象一个极度 oversimplified model，其中，对于每篇 article，每位 reader 都以 fixed probability 进行 upvotes，而且 front page 得到的关注远多于 new page。由此便能得出 result。如果你愿意，可以用一个更 realistic model 来完成这个 exercise，但 result 是一样的[2](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:1)。

当你可以接受 approximate result 时，Adding noise 来 smooth out 一个 discontinuity 是一种常见技巧。我最近就用它绕过了经典的 floating point problem：向一个 large number 中 adding 一个 tiny number，结果却没有变化；当向一些 large numbers 中 adding 许多 small numbers 时，这就成了问题[3](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:2)。作为应用这一技巧的简单 example，考虑维护一个 reduced precision counter，它使用 `loglog(n)` bits 来 store 其 value。令 `countVal(x) = 2^x` 和 `inc(x) = if (rand(2^x) == 0) x++`[4](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:3)。就像理解何时 apply Taylor series 一样，这是一个简单的技巧，没见过它的人往往会对此印象深刻[5](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:4)。

**更新**：HN 试过这个办法！Dan Gackle 告诉我，它的效果不太好（它导致许多 low quality 垃圾短暂登上 front page，然后又消失。我认为，这或许可以通过 tweaking 一些 parameters 来修复，不过 HN 最终选定的 solution——由一个人（或多个人）把被认为不错或有趣的 submissions 放入一个会将 submission boosts 到 front page 的“second chance queue”——比没有 direct human input 的简单 randomized algorithm 无论经过多少 parameter tweaking 所能达到的效果都更好。我认为 moderation 也是如此：“新的” dang/sctb moderation regime 带来了 comment quality 的显著提升，可能比今天使用 automated ML-based solution 所能做到的任何效果都更好——Google 和 FB 拥有世界上一些最先进的 automated systems，但其结果的 quality 远不如我们在 HN 上看到的。

另外，在撰写这篇 post 时（2013），进入 front page 的 threshold 往往是 2-3 votes，这使得一个恰好喜欢某个 submission 的随机过路人在查看 new page 时，能产生非常大的 marginal impact。如今（2019），即使在 off peak times，threshold 似乎也高得多，从而减少了 randomness。此外，HN 的 popularity 上升，使得滞留在 new page 上的 low quality content 总量大幅 increased；如果某个“不错的” submisison 是 new page 上将被 randomly boosted 到 front page 的 30 个 items 之一，这也会 reduce 它能获得的 exposure。这并不意味着当前 system 仍然没有问题：大多数人似乎是依据 article 的 title，而非 content 来 upvote 和 comment 的（要验证这一点，可以在有人指出某篇 post 的问题之前，读一读那些 mistitled 的 articles 下的 comments——通常很明显，大多数 commenters 甚至没有略读过 article，更不用说仔细读过了），不过那是另一篇 post 的话题。

## 原文

You ever notice that there's this funny threshold for getting to the front page on sites like HN? The exact threshold varies depending on how much traffic there is, but, for articles that aren't wildly popular, there's this moment when the article is at N-1 votes. There is, perhaps, a 60% chance that the vote will come and the article will get pushed to the front page, where it will receive a slew of votes. There is, maybe, a 40% chance it will never get the vote that pushes it to the front page, causing it to languish in obscurity forever.

It's non-optimal that an article that will receive 50 votes [in expectation](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/probability/random-variables-topic/random_variables_prob_dist/v/expected-value--e-x) has a 60% chance of getting 100+ votes, and a 40% chance of getting 2 votes. Ideally, each article would always get its expected number of votes and stay on the front page for the expected number of time, giving readers exposure to the article in proportion to its popularity. Instead, by random happenstance, plenty of interesting content never makes it the front page, and as a result, the content that does make it gets a higher than optimal level of exposure.

You also see the same problem, with the sign bit flipped, on low traffic sites that push things to the front page the moment they're posted, like [lobste.rs](https://lobste.rs/) and [the smaller sub-reddits](http://www.reddit.com/r/programming/): they displace links that most people would be interested in by putting links that almost no one cares about on the front page just so that the few things people do care about get enough exposure to be upvoted. On reddit, users "fix" this problem by heavily downvoting most submissions, pushing them off the front page, resulting in a problem that's fundamentally the same as the problem HN has.

Instead of implementing some simple and easy to optimize, sites pile on ad hoc rules. Reddit [implemented the rising page](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6499144), but it fails to solve the problem. On low-traffic subreddits, like [r/programming](http://www.reddit.com/r/programming/rising) the threshold is so high that it's almost always empty. On high-traffic sub-reddits, anything that's upvoted enough to make it to the rising page is already wildly successful, and whether or not an article becomes successful is heavily dependent on whether or not the first couple voters happen to be people who upvote the post instead of downvoting it, i.e., the problem of getting onto the rising page is no different than the problem of getting to the top normally.

HN tries to solve the problem by manually penalizing certain domains and keywords. That doesn't solve the problem for the 95% of posts that aren't penalized. For posts that don't make it to the front page, the obvious workaround is to [delete and re-submit your post if it doesn't make the front page the first time around](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6861735), but that's now [a ban worthy offense](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=6861740). Of course, people are working around that, and HN has a workaround for the workaround, and so on. It's endless. That's the problem with "simple" ad hoc solutions.

There's an easy fix, but it's counter-intuitive. By adding a small amount of random noise to the rank of an article, we can smooth out the discontinuity between making it onto the front page and languishing in obscurity. The math is simple, but the intuition is even simpler[1](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:A). Imagine a vastly oversimplified model where, for each article, every reader upvotes with a fixed probability and the front page gets many more eyeballs than the new page. The result follows. If you like, you can work through the exercise with a more realistic model, but the result is the same[2](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:1).

Adding noise to smooth out a discontinuity is a common trick when you can settle for an approximate result. I recently employed it to work around the classic floating point problem, where adding a tiny number to a large number results in no change, which is problem when adding many small numbers to some large numbers[3](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:2). For a simple example of applying this, consider keeping a reduced precision counter that uses `loglog(n)` bits to store the value. Let `countVal(x) = 2^x` and `inc(x) = if (rand(2^x) == 0) x++`[4](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:3). Like understanding when to apply Taylor series, this is a simple trick that people are often impressed by if they haven't seen it before[5](https://danluu.com/randomize-hn/#fn:4).

**Update**: HN tried this! Dan Gackle tells me that it didn't work very well (it resulted in a lot of low quality junk briefly hitting the front page and then disappearing. I think that might be fixable by tweaking some parameters, but the solution that HN settled on, having a human (or multiple humans) put submissions that are deemed to be good or interesting into a "second chance queue" that boosts the submission onto the front page, works better than an a simple randomized algorithm with no direct human input could with any amount of parameter tweaking. I think this is also true of moderation, where the "new" dang/sctb moderation regime has resulted in a marked increase in comment quality, probably better than anything that could be done with an automated ML-based solution today — Google and FB have some of the most advanced automated systems in the world, and the quality of the result is much worse than what we see on HN.

Also, at the time this post was written (2013), the threshold to get onto the front page was often 2-3 votes, making the marginal impact of a random passerby who happens to like a submission checking the new page very large. Even during off peak times now (in 2019), the threshold seems to be much higher, reducing the amount of randomness. Additionally, the rise in the popularity of HN increased the sheer volume of low quality content that languishes on the new page, which would reduce the exposure that any particular "good" submisison would get if it were among the 30 items on the new page that would randomly get boosted onto the front page. That doesn't mean there aren't still problems with the current system: most people seem to upvote and comment based on the title of the article and not the content (to check this, read the comments of articles that are mistitled before someone calls this out for a partiular post — it's generally quite clear that most commenters haven't even skimmed the article, let alone read it), but that's a topic for a different post.
