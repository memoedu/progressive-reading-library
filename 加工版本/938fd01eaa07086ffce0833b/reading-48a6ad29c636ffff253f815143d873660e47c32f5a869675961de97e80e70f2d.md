# Mind Maps 是在浪费时间吗？

## 正文

有时，能让 learning 更好的最 powerful 的 methods，也恰恰是最简单的。

在我最新的视频随笔中，我讨论了 retrieval practice 的 power，并解释了为什么 students 总是 underrate 它的 effectiveness：

## Mind Maps 是在浪费时间吗？

Mind maps 是一种很受欢迎的 studying 工具。但按照通常的 practiced 方式，它们的表现远远 underperform retrieval practice。

对此的一种 objection 是，我在视频中介绍的那个 [experiment](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1199327?referrer=https%253A%252F%252Fscholar.google.ca%252F) focused on memory，但 mind maps 应该是用于 understanding 的工具。

但如果 understanding 有什么用处，那就是能够针对 material 进行 reason flexibly，并用它来 answer 新类型的 questions。结果，那个 experiment showed，retrieval 在 inference questions 上也 beat mind maps，而不仅仅是在那些可以直接从 text 中 answered 的 questions 上。

Memory 对于 understanding 而言可能是 insufficient，但它 _始终_ 是 necessary。你无法 understand 自己无法 recall 的东西，因此，能够 boost memory 的 methods，往往也就是那些能够 boost understanding 的 methods。

另一种 objection 可能是，researchers 由于 focused on 在书本打开的情况下 made 的 concept maps，忽略了一个更 beneficial 的第三种 alternative：你可以把 concept maps 与 retrieval practice 进行 combine，从而比两者各自的效果都更好！

Researchers 也 tested 了这一点。在这篇 [paper](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2014/2014_Blunt_Karpicke_JEDP.pdf) 中，同一批 researchers 尝试了这种 combination。他们发现，向 mind mapping 中 adding retrieval practice，所 yields 的 memory boost 与单独进行 free recall 相同。

换句话说，retrieval practice 加上 mind mapping，会 beats 打开书本进行的 mind mapping，但 driving 这种 efficacy 的 active ingredient 是 retrieval，而不是 mind map。

即便如此，我也不会说 mind maps 完全 useless。我认为，它们可以帮助 organize 围绕一个大 topic 的 thinking。而且，如果你正在尝试 organize 自己的 thinking，你很可能就在 retrieving，因此它们可能是进行这类 practice 的一种简单方式。   我认为，所有这些 research 真正值得我们记住的，并不是 mind maps 毫无意义，而是 retrieval practice 是 robustly useful 且 powerful 的。

## 更 Harder 的 Learning 就更 Efficient 吗？

Retrieval practice 是 effortful 的，而且它带来的 learning 比那些较不 effortful 的 approaches 更好。人们很容易由此 conclude，更 more-effortful 的 studying 形式就更 effective。我在上述视频下收到的许多评论，都把这一点当作显而易见的道理。

但事实证明，这也不成立。

用于 retrieving knowledge 的那种 effort，比更 passive 的 review 更能 strengthen memory。但这并不 imply，所有书籍、lectures 或其他形式的“passive” instruction，都必然 inferior to “active” mental engagement。

事实上，如今已有 robust literature showing，在 learning 一项新 skill 的早期阶段，studying [worked examples](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2022/01/04/cognitive-load-theory/) 实际上 beats problem solving。同样，当 difficulty exceeds 某个 threshold 时，许多 study effects，例如 retrieval practice 的 efficacy，都会 disappear，甚至 reverse。这 suggests，successful learning 的关键在于 optimizing effort，而不是 maximizing 它。

更好的准则或许是：effective learning 是 effortful 的，但并非所有 effort 都是 effective 的。

## 要 Retrieve，而不要 Re-Read

尽管有这些需要注意的地方，我仍然认为，retrieval practice 是你可以掌握的最 powerful 的 learning strategies 之一。Memory 会通过 use 而 strengthens。如果你努力 remember，而不只是 review，你就会 recall 更多。

## 原文

Sometimes the most powerful methods for learning better are also the simplest.  

In my latest video essay, I discuss the power of retrieval practice, and explain why students consistently underrate its effectiveness:

## Are Mind Maps a Waste of Time?

Mind maps are a popular tool for studying. But, as they’re typically practiced, they underperform retrieval practice by a wide margin.

One objection to this was that [the experiment](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1199327?referrer=https%253A%252F%252Fscholar.google.ca%252F) I featured in the video focused on memory, but mind maps are supposed to be a tool for understanding.

But if understanding is good for anything it’s being able to reason flexibly about material and use it to answer novel types of questions. It turns out, the experiment showed that retrieval beat mind maps on inference questions, not just those which could be answered directly from the text.

Memory may be insufficient for understanding, but it is _always_ necessary. You can’t understand something you can’t recall, and so the methods that boost memory are often the same as those that boost understanding.

Another objection may be that, by focusing on concept maps made with the book open, the researchers ignored a more beneficial third alternative: that you could combine concept maps with retrieval practice and do better than both!

Researchers tested that, too. In this [paper](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2014/2014_Blunt_Karpicke_JEDP.pdf), the same researchers tried that combination. They found that adding retrieval practice to mind mapping yields the same boost to memory as free recall alone.

In other words, retrieval practice plus mind mapping beats mind mapping with an open book, but the active ingredient driving the efficacy is retrieval—not the mind map.

Even so, I wouldn’t say that mind maps are completely useless. I think they can help organize thinking about a large topic. And, if you’re trying to organize your thinking, you’re probably retrieving, so they may be an easy way to do that kind of practice.   I think the real takeaway from all this research isn’t that mind maps are pointless, but that retrieval practice is robustly useful and powerful.

## Is Harder Learning More Efficient?

Retrieval practice is effortful, and it leads to better learning than less effortful approaches. It’s tempting to conclude from this that more-effortful forms of studying are more effective. Many comments I got on the above video suggested this as an obvious truism.

But, it turns out, this isn’t true either.

The kind of effort used in retrieving knowledge can strengthen memory more than more passive review. But this doesn’t imply that all books, lectures or other forms of “passive” instruction are automatically inferior to “active” mental engagement.

Indeed, there’s a robust literature now showing that studying [worked examples](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2022/01/04/cognitive-load-theory/) actually beats problem solving in the early phase of learning a new skill. Similarly, when difficulty exceeds a certain threshold many study effects, like the efficacy of retrieval practice, disappear or even reverse. This suggests the key to successful learning is about optimizing effort, not maximizing it.

A better maxim might be that effective learning is effortful, but not all effort is effective.

## Retrieve, Don’t Re-Read

Despite these caveats, I think retrieval practice remains one of the most powerful learning strategies to have in your toolkit. Memory strengthens through use. You’ll recall more if you strive to remember, not just review.
