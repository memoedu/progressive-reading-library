# A breakthrough in moral psychology

---

## 正文

我说的是 Kahane, G., Everett, J. A. C., Earp, B. D., Caviola, L., Faber, N. S., Crockett, M. J., & Savulescu, J. (2017)。

也就是 \_[Beyond Sacrificial Harm: A Two-Dimensional Model of Utilitarian Psychology,](http://psycnet.apa.org/record/2017-57422-001?utm_content=buffere5ed2&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter.com&utm_campaign=buffer) \_——今年最好的 moral psychology paper！

在 psychology 领域，有一个特定领域，因其对 moral dilemmas，尤其是 trolley problem 的生动运用，而在圈外人中颇为显眼。这个领域就是 moral psychology，它 studies 人们如何对 moral issues 进行 reason，有时会用这些 dilemmas 来 probe 人们的 intuitions。

moral psychology 近来的发展历程（至少在 post-Kohlberg era）很大程度上源于 [Jonathan Haidt](https://www.amazon.com/Righteous-Mind-Divided-Politics-Religion/dp/0307455777) 的工作，他 postulated 了一系列 moral foundations（Care、Purity、Fairness、Authority、Loyalty 和 Liberty），用以 explain moral beliefs。当时有一个如今已成经典的 though experiment，询问 participants 如何看待吃掉自己刚刚死去的狗，或一对兄妹进行 safe, consensual sex。

对 Haidt 而言，morality 主要是 emotions 的问题。在他早期的工作中，他似乎还给 reason 留了一些余地，但在这个 theory 的最新版本中（见 The Righteous Mind 一书），reason 退居次要位置，仅仅充当 moral sentiments 的代言人。

Haidt 还因率先 studying [libertarians 的 moral judgments](http://righteousmind.com/largest-study-of-libertarian-psych/) 而颇为出名：这个 group 在每个国家的 politics 中都相对较小，但其成员在 philosophers 中却是 overrepresented。[1](https://nintil.com/a-breakthrough-in-moral-psychology/#1)

但 reason 似乎也发挥着作用。这时 Joshua Greene 登场了，他开始发表一系列 papers（其中的要旨收录在他的 [Moral Tribes](https://www.amazon.com/Moral-Tribes-Emotion-Reason-Between/dp/1594202605) 一书中），arguing morality 与 general reasoning 一样，follows 一个 two system model：其中有一种 automatic、基于 "gut-feeling" 的 mode，也有一种 rational、cold、evidence-based 的 model。他认为后者与 utilitarianism 是 linked 的。

另一个问题是，这本书在 philosophy 内部开辟了一条支线（例如可参见 [Erik Wielenberg 对这本书的 review](http://www.jstor.org.sci-hub.tw/stable/10.1086/676069?ai=v9&mi=w0q6y1&af=R&seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents)），探讨这样的 scientific studies 能否 support 或 debunk moral theories。如果确实 proved，一个真正 rational、没有任何 passions 和 biases 的 individual 会是 utilitarian（或 deontological，或 libertarian），这本身不就为那些 moral systems 提供了一个 argument 吗？（此外，这是否也 prove，我们可以拥有一种 purely rational morality，与 David Hume 的看法相反？）Research 目前还没有为这些问题给出 conclusive answer。

不过，还是回到正题。又过了一段时间，dual-system morality 开始在 theory 和 methodology 两方面受到一些 critiques，其中最引人注意的来自本文作者之一 Guy Kahane（Kahane et al., [2015a](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4642180/) 和 [2015b](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4259516/)）。这些 papers 展示的结果之一是，psychopaths 似乎是 highly utilitarian 的，这与 utilitarianism 所 defends 的对所有 sentient beings 的 impartial concern 并不 match。

除此之外，按我个人的理解，那些被用来 tease out utilitarianism 的 moral dilemmas 并不适合这一用途。不妨看看 [Greene et al. (2004).](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627304006348) 的 supplemental material 中提供的那些 dilemmas。我——一个并非 utilitarian 的人——在那些 utilitarianism tests 上 rank 很高。我的 answers 并不是以 utilitarianism 为 basis，因此它并没有 picking up 本应捕捉的东西。

这终于把我们带到了眼前这篇 paper。

作者们试图得到一种 utilitarianism 的 measure，能够真正 picks up general population 中的 utilitarian thinking。他们在很大程度上做到了，而且其 rigor 超过了一般 paper：

他们首先从 psychology、philosophy 以及自己的构思中 collected 了数百个可能的 questions，然后交给一个由 philosophers 组成的 expert panel 反复拷问，以确保这些 questions 确实触及所谓 utilitarianism 的核心，接着对 960 名 participants 的 responses 进行了 factor-analysed。对所得 factors loadings 进行了 analysed，以查看是否有任何 items 没有 load 到其中任何一个 factors，dropping 那些 "weak" items，并将这个过程 repeating 了十次，最后考察的不是一种，而是六种不同的 model fit metrics，从而得到一个 final model。

最终得到的是一个包含两个 subcomponent 的 scale（Oxford Utilitarianism Scale），随后又在另一个包含 282 名 participants 的 sample 中对其 coherence 进行了 tested。经受住这一连串 statistical analysis 后，他们得到了如下结果：

**Oxford Utilitarianism Scale (OUS)（牛津功利主义量表）**

1.  Impartial Beneficence subcomponent（公正无偏的行善子成分）

    1.  如果在 emergency 中 save 另一个人的 life 的唯一 way 是 sacrifice 自己的一条 leg，那么一个人就 morally required 作出这种 sacrifice。
    2.  从 moral point of view 来看，我们应该觉得自己有义务把自己的一个 kidneys give 给一个有 kidney failure 的人，因为我们并不需要两个 kidneys 才能 survive，实际上只需一个就能保持 healthy。
    3.  从 moral perspective 来看，人们应该 equally 地 care about 这个星球上所有 human beings 的 well-being；他们不应该 favor 那些在 physically 或 emotionally 方面与自己特别 close 的人的 well-being。
    4.  fail to help 某个人，与自己 actively harm 他们，是同样 wrong 的。
    5.  如果一个人能够把 money donate 给那些为将会 benefit a great deal 的人 provide effective help 的 causes，那么 keep 自己并不真正需要的 money 就是 morally wrong 的
2.  Instrumental Harm subcomponent（工具性伤害子成分）

    1.  如果 harming 一个 innocent person 是 helping 另外几个 innocent people 的 necessary means，那么 harm 这个人就是 morally right 的。

    2.  如果 ensure 人们的 overall well-being 和 happiness 的唯一 way，是在一段短暂、limited 的时期内 use political oppression，那么就应该 used political oppression。

    3.  如果为了 provide information，以 prevent 一枚会 kill 数百人的 bomb going off，torture 一个 innocent person 是 necessary 的，那么这样做就是 permissible 的。

    4.  有时，让 innocent people 作为 collateral damage 而 die 是 morally necessary 的——如果 overall 有更多人被 saved。

这个 scale picks up 了从 Kantianism 到 Act Utilitarianism 之间的 position gradient，较弱形式的 utilitarianism 和 virtue ethics 则位于其间。

![2017-12-28 15-32-31.png 的屏幕截图](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2017/12/28-1.png)

他们发现的两个 factors 在 validation sample 中并不是很 correlated（r=.14）。请注意，这些 factors 是在 assuming latent variables（factors）的前提下 extracted 的，而不是 principal components（后者会使它们在设计上 fully orthogonal），因此它们确实表现出一定 correlation，并不是坏事。但在一个 philosophers 的 sample 中，它们确实是 correlated 的。[2](https://nintil.com/a-breakthrough-in-moral-psychology/#2)

下方展示的这个 scale 的其他 correlations 表明，psychopathy 与 instrumental harm scale 之间存在 moderate correlation，但与 impartial beneficence scale 之间并没有。这可能就是上面引用的 experiments 中起作用的因素：psychopaths 对 harm 的 care 更少，却并不 endorse impartial beneficence。

Empathy 与 impartial beneficence scale 存在 low-medium correlation，与 instrumental harm scale 则有一定 negative correlation。作者们指出，这种与 empathy 的 association 可能使 utilitarianism 在 psychologically 方面变得 unstable：如果你对他人有 deep feelings，这会 leads to impartiality，但也会使你 reluctance to harm 人们以 pursue the greater good，反之亦然。

我还想补充，这听起来与 morality 的 dual-system model 不一致。empathic concern scale（[标为 EC 的 items](http://fetzer.org/sites/default/files/images/stories/pdf/selfmeasures/EMPATHY-InterpersonalReactivityIndex.pdf)）会问这样的问题：“我常常对那些比我不幸的人怀有 tender, concerned feelings”，“有时，当别人遇到问题时，我并不 feel sorry”，“当我看到有人被占便宜时，我会有些想保护他们”。

因此，在这里，empathy 的 feelings 可能在 driving utilitarianism（的 impartial beneficence component），正如 disgust 甚至 anger 的 feelings 可能是更传统的 moralities 背后的动力。不过，也许这只是诸如“我 believe 我 ought to help 那些比我不幸的人”之类表述的 proxy，而这些 questions 诉诸的是 beliefs，而非 feelings。这一点应该进一步 explored [3](https://nintil.com/a-breakthrough-in-moral-psychology/#more)。

![2017-12-28 15-36-44.png 的屏幕截图](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2017/12/28-2.png)

有意思的是，Religiosity 也与 impartiality scale 存在 associated（“爱你的邻人……”），而 political ideology 的两个 measures（social 和 economic）也表现出与 instrumental harm scale 的一定 association。

最后，作者们认为 scale 发布之后，下一步应该做什么？首先，少做一些 trolley problems，直接用 OUS 来 measure utilitarianism。此外，出于更 pragmatic 的目的，他们建议 utilitarians 把重点放在 moral impartiality 这一面，而不是 instrumental harm 这一面——这很有道理。

这篇 paper 在未来几年肯定会积累大量 citations，我敢说，它很可能会在这个领域占据 central position。我们大概仍会看到 trolley dilemmas，但它们如今会与一种真正 robust、theoretically sound 的 measure 搭配使用，而且这种 measure 确实 match "utilitarianism" 的 philosophical meaning。

在本文结束之前，还有一个这篇 paper 引出的问题，它与 paper 本身无关，但大概值得 exploring。paper 中说：

> 当今的 secular morality 可以被看作我们 circle of moral concern 的 gradual expansion：从那些与我们 emotionally close、physically near 或 similar 的人，扩展到涵盖整个 humanity，甚至所有 sentient life（Singer, 1981；另见 Pinker, 2011）。Bentham、John Stuart Mill，以及我们这个时代的 Peter Singer 等 Utilitarians，在这一过程中，以及更广泛的 progressive causes 中，都发挥了关键作用。他们一直是反对 sexism、racism 和 ‘speciesism;’ 的斗争中的领军人物；是 political 和 sexual liberty 的有影响力的支持者；也是在 developing countries 中尝试 eradicate poverty，以及在我们自身的 societies 内鼓励对 prenatal screening、abortion 和 euthanasia 持更 permissive attitudes 的关键行动者（Bentham, 1789/1983; Mill, 1863; Singer, 2011）

问题是：与同时代的其他 intellectuals 相比，历史上的 utilitarians 是否拥有一种更接近我们的 morality？（无论那些 intellectuals 是谁。）与当时 population 的平均水平相比，他们的 morality 当然确实更接近我们，但若要评判 intellectual merit，更好的衡量标尺应该是其他 intellectuals。

而我忍不住要说，expanding circle 的说法虽然乍看可信，却很可能并不正确，恕我不同意作者、Pinker 和 Singer。正如 [Huemer (2015)](https://link.springer.com.sci-hub.tw/article/10.1007/s11098-015-0588-9) 所说，在例如 premarital sex 或 capital punishment（或者 abortion，或者更广泛的 womenś rights）这些方面，从 traditional morality 转向更 modern morality，并不涉及 circle of concern 的 expansion。

同样，对 animals 的 welfare 的 concern 已存在数千年（参见 Hinduism 或 Jainism，甚至 [Judeo-Christianity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4093044/)；反对 animal mistreatment 的 Laws [直到晚得多才出现](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_animal_welfare_and_rights#cite_note-szucs-2)r，即 1600-1800 年）。此后，随着 animal biology 的 knowledge 扩展，laws 也发生了变化，但在我看来，这方面的 moral values 似乎并没有太大变化。到了某个时候，animal products 的 consumption 将会 drastically reduced，但这不会是因为 utilitarianism 或 moral values 的任何变化。它会发生，是因为[将会有 cheap、tasty 的 substitutes 可用](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultured_meat)，而且 ceteris paribus，人们 prefer 造成 less suffering 而非 more，这一 principle 可以追溯到有文字记录之时。

## 原文

I'm talking about Kahane, G., Everett, J. A. C., Earp, B. D., Caviola, L., Faber, N. S., Crockett, M. J., & Savulescu, J. (2017).

Aka \_[Beyond Sacrificial Harm: A Two-Dimensional Model of Utilitarian Psychology,](http://psycnet.apa.org/record/2017-57422-001?utm_content=buffere5ed2&utm_medium=social&utm_source=twitter.com&utm_campaign=buffer) \_the best moral psychology paper of the year!

In the field of psychology, there is a particular area that has become quite visible to outsiders for its colourful use of moral dilemmas, in particular the trolley problem. This area, moral psychology, studies the how people reason about moral issues, using sometimes those dilemmas to probe people's intuitions.

The recent story of moral psychology (At least, in the post-Kohlberg era) has its roots ,to a large extent, in the work of [Jonathan Haidt](https://www.amazon.com/Righteous-Mind-Divided-Politics-Religion/dp/0307455777), who postulated a series of moral foundations (Care, Purity, Fairness, Authority, Loyalty, and Liberty) to explain moral beliefs. Back from then there is a now classic though experiment, asking participants what they think of eating their recently deceased dog, or a brother and sister having safe, consensual sex.

For Haidt, morality is mostly a matter of emotions. In his early work, he seems to have left some room for reason, but in the latest editions of the theory (see the book The Righteous Mind), reason takes a back seat, and acts merely as a spokesperson of the moral sentiments.

Haidt was also notorious for being a pioneer of studying the [moral judgments of libertarians](http://righteousmind.com/largest-study-of-libertarian-psych/): a group that is relatively small in politics in every country, but that finds its members overrepresented among philosophers.[1](https://nintil.com/a-breakthrough-in-moral-psychology/#1)

But reason seemed to also play a role. Enter Joshua Greene, who began publishing a series of papers (the gist of them is compiled in his book [Moral Tribes](https://www.amazon.com/Moral-Tribes-Emotion-Reason-Between/dp/1594202605)), arguing that morality, like general reasoning, follows a two system model: there is an automatic, "gut-feeling" based mode, and a rational, cold, evidence-based, model. This second one he considers to be linked to utilitarianism.

As a separate issue, this book opened a sub-stream within philosophy itself (see, for example [Erik Wielenberg's review of the book](http://www.jstor.org.sci-hub.tw/stable/10.1086/676069?ai=v9&mi=w0q6y1&af=R&seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents)), on whether scientific studies like this can support or debunk moral theories. If it is indeed proved that a truly rational individual, devoid of all passions and biases would be utilitarian (or deontological, or libertarian) , doesn't that provide in itself an argument for those moral systems? (And, in addition, prove that we can have a purely rational morality, contra David Hume?) Research doesn't have a conclusive answer for these questions yet.

But anyway. Some time later, dual-system morality began to get some critiques, both in theory and methodology, most notoriously by Guy Kahane, one of the authors of the present paper (Kahane et al., [2015a](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4642180/) and [2015b](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4259516/)). Among other things, these papers show that psychopaths seemed to be highly utilitarian, which doesn't match with the impartial concern for all sentient beings that utilitarianism defends.

In addition to that, as a matter of personal interpretation, the moral dilemmas being used to tease out utilitarianism are not fit for purpose. Consider those available in the supplemental material of [Greene et al. (2004).](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0896627304006348) I -not an utilitarian- rank very high on those utilitarianism tests. The basis for my answers was not utilitarianism, so it wasn't picking up what is should.

This finally leads us to the paper at hand.

The authors try to get to a measure of utilitarianism that really picks up utilitarian thinking in the general population. And to a large extent they succeed, doing so with more rigor than the one deployed by your average paper:

They first collected hundreds of possible questions  from psychology, philosophy, and themselves, and then tortured it with an expert panel of philosophers to make sure the questions do get to the core of what is known as utilitarianism, and then factor-analysed the responses from 960 participants. The resulting factors loadings were analysed to see if any items did not load into any of them, dropping "weak" items, and repeating the process ten times, arriving at a final model by looking at not one but six different metrics of model fit.

The end result was a two subcomponent scale (Oxford Utilitarianism Scale), that itself was tested for coherence in a different sample of 282 participants. Having survived this barrage of statistical analysis, the arrived at this:

**Oxford Utilitarianism Scale (OUS)**

1.  Impartial Beneficence subcomponent

    1.  The only way to save another person’s life during an emergency is to sacrifice one’s own leg, then one is morally required to make this sacrifice.
    2.  From a moral point of view, we should feel obliged to give one of our kidneys to a person with kidney failure since we do not need two kidneys to survive, but really only one to be healthy.
    3.  From a moral perspective, people should care about the well-being of all human beings on the planet equally; they should not favor the well-being of people who are especially close to them either physically or emotionally.
    4.  It is just as wrong to fail to help someone as it is to actively harm them yourself.
    5.  It is morally wrong to keep money that one doesn’t really need if one can donate it to causes that provide effective help to those who will benefit a great deal
2.  Instrumental Harm subcomponent

    1.  It is morally right to harm an innocent person if harming them is a necessary means to helping several other innocent people.

    2.  If the only way to ensure the overall well-being and happiness of the people is through the use of political oppression for a short, limited period, then political oppression should be used.

    3.  It is permissible to torture an innocent person if this would be necessary to provide information to prevent a bomb going off that would kill hundreds of people.

    4.  Sometimes it is morally necessary for innocent people to die as collateral damage—if more people are saved overall.

This scale picks up the gradient of position that goes between Kantianism and Act Utilitarianism, with weaker forms of utilitarianism and virtue ethics in between.

The two factors they found were not very correlated in their validation sample (r=.14). Note that the factors were extracted assuming latent variables (factors), not principal components (that would have made them fully orthogonal by design), so the fact that they do show some correlation is not a bad thing. But in a sample of philosophers, they were indeed correlated. [2](https://nintil.com/a-breakthrough-in-moral-psychology/#2)

Other correlations of the scale, shown below, indicate that there is a moderate correlation between psychopathy and the instrumental harm scale, but not the impartial beneficence scale. This is what may be at play in the experiments cited above: psychopaths care less about harm, but do not endorse impartial beneficence.

Empathy had a low-medium correlation with the impartial beneficence scale, with some negative correlation with the instrumental harm scale. The authors remark that this association with empathy might be making utilitarianism psychologically unstable: If you have deep feelings for others that leads to impartiality, but also to reluctance to harm people to pursue the greater good, and viceversa.

I might add that this sounds at odds with the dual-system model of morality. The empathic concern scale ([items marked as EC](http://fetzer.org/sites/default/files/images/stories/pdf/selfmeasures/EMPATHY-InterpersonalReactivityIndex.pdf)) asks questions like "I often have tender, concerned feelings for people less fortunate than me", "Sometimes I don feel sorry for other people when they are having problems", "When I see someone being taken advantage of, I feel kind of protective towards them".

So here, feelings of empathy might be driving (the impartial beneficence component of) utilitarianism, in the same way that feelings of disgust or even anger may be behind more traditional moralities. But perhaps this is a proxy for things like "I believe I ought to help those less fortunate than me" or things like that, which are questions that appeal to beliefs and not feelings. This ought to be explored more [3](https://nintil.com/a-breakthrough-in-moral-psychology/#more).

To close this post, there is one question that the paper raises, which is unrelated to the paper itself, but that is probably worth exploring. The paper says that

> Modern-day secular morality can be seen as the gradual expansion of our circle of moral concern from those who are emotionally close, physically near, or similar to us, to cover the whole of humanity, and even all sentient life (Singer, 1981; see also Pinker, 2011). Utilitarians like Bentham, John Stuart Mill, and, in our time, Peter Singer, have played a pivotal role in this process, and in progressive causes more generally. They have been leading figures in the fights against sexism, racism, and ‘speciesism;’ influential supporters of political and sexual liberty; and key actors in attempts to eradicate poverty in developing countries as well as to encourage more permissive attitudes to prenatal screening, abortion, and euthanasia within our own societies (Bentham, 1789/1983; Mill, 1863; Singer, 2011)

The question is: Did historical utilitarians have a morality closer to ours, compared to other intellectuals of the time? (Whoever they may be) Surely they do have a morality closer to ours, compared to the average of the population back then, but the better yardstick for intellectual merit purposes should be other intellectuals.

And I can't help to make the comment that the expanding circle story, while initially plausible, is not probably correct, pace the authors, Pinker, and Singer. As [Huemer (2015)](https://link.springer.com.sci-hub.tw/article/10.1007/s11098-015-0588-9) says, switching from a traditional morality to a more modern morality in terms of, eg. premarital sex,  or capital punishment (Or abortion, or womenś rights more broadly) does not involve an expansion of the circle of concern.

Similarly, concern with the welfare of animals has existed for millenia (cf. Hinduism or Jainism, and even [Judeo-Christianity](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4093044/) Laws against animal mistreatment [appear much late](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_animal_welfare_and_rights#cite_note-szucs-2)r, in 1600-1800). Since then, the laws have changed as knowledge of animal biology has expanded, but it doesn't seem to me that in terms of moral values much has changed in that regard. At some point, consumption of animal products will be drastically reduced, but this won't happen because of utilitarianism or any changes in moral values. It will happen because [cheap, tasty, substitutes will be available](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultured_meat), and ceteris paribus people prefer to cause less suffering than more, a principle that goes as back as there is written records.
