### 引言

你能学到的最重要的 life skills 之一，就是如何做好 prioritise。我们所有人的 time、energy 和 resources 都是 limited。而人生就是不断 spending resources 来 achieve 我的 goals 的 continuous process。弄清楚_如何_ spend resources，才能 best achieve 你的 goals，对 outcome 至关重要。

然而，显而易见，99.9% 的人都不擅长这个（包括我！）。这也不难理解——prioritisation 是_困难的_，而你永远不可能把它做得完美。而且，人很容易陷入这样的陷阱：总是在 prioritising，不断因为 indecision 而 paralysed，却从未真正_做_任何事。但我认为，适量 prioritising 的 value _极其_高，尤其是与完全不 prioritising 相比，因此值得努力争取。

这篇文章很难写，因为我远算不上 prioritisation 方面的专家。而人们在 prioritise 上遇到的困难，分布在一个很广的 spectrum 上——许多人根本没有意识到自己_需要_ prioritise，另一些人却为 prioritisation 苦恼过度，由此产生的 paralysis 使他们什么也 achieving 不了。在这篇文章中，我将尝试概述：你为什么_应该_关心 prioritisation；关于 finding 需要 prioritise 的 goals 的一些想法；一股脑地给出一些关于如何实际 implement 它的建议；以及我认为人们在这个 spectrum 的两端常犯的错误。

### 为什么你应该 prioritise prioritisation

Prioritisation 是你能做的最重要的事情之一。世界是 _complicated_ 的。它充满了 opportunities 和 traps。有美好的事物，能为你的人生增添大量 value；也有不重要的 rabbit holes，你可能陷入其中，因而无法 achieving 本可以做到的事。正因为世界如此 complicated，我们能采取的 actions，其 pay-offs 有着_很大的_ differences。不同 actions 的 outputs 很容易就有_巨大的_ differences。我知道，[best charities](https://givewell.org/) 能做出的善事，是 average charities 的数十到数百倍。我知道，有些 projects 会让我满怀兴奋，另一些却让我觉得拖累。我知道，有些人会为我的生活带来快乐，另一些则不会。而我们的 intuitions 并不是这方面的可靠 guide，因为我们 evolved 时所处的世界要简单得多。但是，[我们的 intuitions 存在于我们的 minds 中，而我们的 goals 存在于世界中](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-12-the-map-and-the-territory)，你不应该让这一点阻碍自己。

而且，要认真对待 prioritisation 是_困难的_。它庞大、fuzzy、abstract，还会 triggers 许多众所周知的 biases。人很容易没能做到 [be deliberate](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/be-deliberate)，没有 track 自己的 actions 究竟_如何_帮助自己，也很容易跟着周围所有人做同样的事。要持续关注 alternatives，以及它们可能好多少，并不容易；它们 vague 而 abstract，而你眼下的 actions 却是 _concrete_ 的。人们对 [default action](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-15-the-illusion-of-doing-nothing) 有着强烈的 attachment。如果周围没有人认真对待 prioritisation，它就不会让人觉得_紧迫_。Humans 会 focus on short-term，而 neglect long-term，因此很容易陷入不断 busy-work 的 spirals——机械地做 problem sheets、处理无聊的邮件、维持 toxic friendships、困在不情愿承担的 obligations 之网中——然后才意识到，在过去一个月里，自己几乎_没有_花时间 working towards 自己的 high level goals。人很难对 _information_ 的 value 有 intuition，很难体会到花些时间 thinking 和 exploring 确实可能帮助自己，反而会觉得自己已经知道什么最好。

而这些还只是这里起作用的许多 biases 中的_一部分_。列出这一长串内容，是想说明：未能 prioritise 不应该令人意外，也不应该让人觉得是个人的失败。**未能 prioritise，是 human experience 的 default state**。你需要不断对抗那种 entropy，它会把你推向无法 act towards 自己的 goals 的状态。我们的 default settings 不会思考 prioritisation，而这一点需要你努力改变。

此外，人很容易觉得，prioritisation 应该只 apply to 某些 kinds 的 goals，比如工作。也就是那些直接 tangible、quantifiable，明确 focused on real-world results 的事情。但是，正如你可以 [optimise for any goal](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise)，所有 goals 都需要 prioritisation！如果我的 goal 是 feel happy and fulfilled，那么我就应该 prioritise 寻找良好的 friendships 和 relationships，消除生活中的 stress，并确保自己有良好的 sleep 和 rest。而我很容易没能 take actions，使自己朝这个方向前进。

### 理解 goals

prioritisation 中一个真正关键的 component，是知道自己的 _goals_ 是什么。这应该是最能 determine 你 take 哪些 actions 的 factor。例如，在攻读 degree 时，对我而言，optimise 自己多么努力地学习——有多 focus、花多少 time——感觉_远比_ optimise 自己_如何_学习更加 salient：选择哪些 courses、究竟要不要做 problem sheet，以及以怎样的 mindset 对待 course。自己有多努力，感觉非常 _tangible_，我能 control 它，也更容易 quantify，更符合好学生的角色。但它其实并不会造成那么大的 difference。归根结底，我还是做完了 sheets，也还是从中学到了一些东西。我可能会把它们做得稍好一些，或者多剩下一点时间，但靠“[just trying harder](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)”能挤出来的 efficiency 实在没那么多。然而，我的 goals 并不是“尽可能 efficiently 地做完我的 sheets”。如果我的 goal 是尽可能好地 learn content，那么有些 questions 完全是在浪费我的时间！如果我的 goal 是给 supervisor 留下好印象，让对方写出一封好的 reference，那么我应该 focus on conceptual clarity 和好的 write-ups！如果我想玩得开心，就应该选择我觉得自己最会喜欢的 courses！如果我想 gain 像 mathematician 那样 thinking 的 meta-skills，就应该深入最难的 courses 中的 rabbit holes，努力 understand _究竟_发生了什么！如果我想 learn _直接_ _useful_ 的 skills，就应该选择 stats courses！而 efficiently 地做 sheets _也许_能帮助我做到这些，但它很少是最 effective 的 path。理解我真正的 goals，会_极大地_ informs 我应该如何 acting。

而 finding 你的 goals 是困难的！尤其在你比较年轻时，你可能很难知道自己的 goals 应该是什么，于是放弃 prioritising。或者，你可能犯下这样的错误：_以为_自己知道 goals 是什么，拼命为 proxy goal 进行 optimising，却没有意识到自己错过了什么。我认为，有些人接受 prestigious 却空洞、令人不快的 corporate job，比如 investment banking，最终过得并不快乐，背后的关键错误就在这里。不过，尽管困难，我认为这是一个_真正_重要的 problem，而且是你能够 make progress 的。

Terminal Goals（终极目标）

想一想你有哪些_种类_的 goals，是有帮助的。有一种是 **terminal, or intrinsic, goals**——那些我因其本身而 value 的 high-level goals。常见的 examples：

-   Making the world a better place（让世界变得更美好）

-   Adding to the body of human knowledge（增进人类知识）

-   Feeling happy and fulfilled（感到快乐而充实）

-   Starting a family（组建家庭）

-   所爱之人的 happiness

-   Safety 和 stability

-   Having a legacy（留下传承）

对我而言，我最强烈的 terminal goals 是 feel happy and fulfilled，以及 make the world a better place（注意——[为了 _in order to feel happy_ 而 making the world a better place，与将其视为 intrinsic goal，两者之间存在重要的 distinction](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-8-what-altruism-means-to-me)）。但这_极其_ personal 而 subjective。我发现，这类事情确实需要你自己 introspect，审视自己真正 value 的是什么。不过，我发现，大多数人通常都多少知道自己的这些 goals 是什么。[一个有助于 exploring 这一点的 tool](https://programs.clearerthinking.org/intrinsic_values_test.html)。

一个好用的窍门：humans 非常 short-term focused，很容易过于关注当下。但 terminal goals 面向的是 long-term。我发现，想象 5 年或 20 年后的自己，再问问那时自己希望从人生中得到什么，会很有帮助。这能 abstracts out short-term concerns，也是一种很好的方式，帮助你 filter 出自己_真正_在意的东西。

提醒一下：一个_极其_常见的错误，是从 social cues 和自己的 culture 中获取 terminal goals。这很少行得通。你的 terminal goals _可能_与你的 culture 相符，但也可能不相符。一个 terminal goal 应该让你有 _visceral_ 的感受。它应该是你真正在意、愿意为之奋斗的东西。如果没有它，你会觉得人生_缺少_了什么。我认为，人们经常会觉得，比如，自己应该想要一个家庭，因为父母或 culture 对自己有这样的期待；或者觉得自己需要关注 prestige 或 making money，因为这是“大家都这样做”的事。不，不，千万不要！如果你是_因为它们本身_而想要这些，那太好了！但**不要让别人替你决定你的 terminal goals**。这些是你要用一生去 working towards 的东西。如果你仅仅因为 culture 告诉你应该这样做，就接受了错误的 goal，那么**你已经输了**。

Instrumental goals（工具性目标）

如果你能 take actions，明显而直接地 work towards 自己的 terminal goals，那太好了！但这很难，因为世界是 complex 的。这就引出了我要说的第二个 category：**instrumental goals**。这些是我在意的东西，_因为它们 bring me closer to 我的 terminal goals_，而不是因为它们本身具有 value。这是一个重要的 concept，因为世界是 complicated 的。要知道如何 directly achieve 你的 terminal goals 并不容易，而我们的 intuitions 很不擅长这个。Instrumental goals 是一种关键的 mental shortcut，能让我们在这件事上真正 make progress。

知道其中的 difference 至关重要，因为我_只有_在一个 instrumental goal 能 achieves 我的 terminal goal 时，才想去做它。所以我需要_知道_它是否 achieves 我的 terminal goals。人们没能做到 [be deliberate](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/be-deliberate) 的许多情况，最终都源于此——人很容易从 social norms 和 culture 中吸收一个 instrumental goal，_以为_它能 brings you closer to 你的 terminal goals，实际却没有。

常见的 examples：

-   Earning money（挣钱）

-   拥有 prestigious degree

-   为 job interviews 做 Prepping

-   为 exams 做 Cramming

-   结识新朋友

-   加入社团

在实践中，你要 understand 自己的 terminal goals，用它们来 find 自己的 instrumental goals，并使 instrumental goals 保持 grounded。然后，在日常生活中，你主要只需思考自己的 instrumental goals。它们可以变得非常 specific，而且 nested！例如，也许我的 terminal goal 是拥有一个家庭。我可以通过在 Google 获得 software engineer 这份 high-paying career 来 achieve 这一点。我可以通过 developing 一份优秀的 coding projects 的 portfolio 来 achieve 这一点。我可以通过变得有 _motivated_ 去 work on 一个 coding project 来 achieve 这一点。我可以通过结交对 coding 东西非常兴奋的朋友来 achieve 这一点。我可以通过参加当地的 hackers society，并尝试交朋友来 achieve 这一点。现在我们已经深入了 6 layers，但这确实可能是 achieve 你的 goals 的一种好办法！而且，你_不可能_对每一个 action 都走一遍这个 process；要取得任何 progress，你都_需要_把自己的 goals cache 为 specific 的 instrumental goals。

但这种 grounding 很容易丢失！也许我根本无法胜任 software engineer。也许 Google 不会对我的 portfolio 留下深刻印象，反而我应该为 coding interviews 做 prep。也许我首先应该 Work on _我自己的_ coding skills。定期抽出时间 reflect 自己是否_确实_在朝 terminal goals（甚至是更高层的 instrumental goals！）making progress，是很重要的，这样才能检查这种微妙的 balance 是否被打破。

### 如何 _Find_ Goals

谈论 goals 有多重要，以及如何 categorise 它们，当然很好。我也认为 conceptual clarity 是开始行动的重要一步。但人很容易感到 paralysed，觉得这些并不 actionable。那么，你怎样才能 make progress 呢？

Back-Chaining（反向推导）

将 terminal goals 转为 instrumental goals 的一种非常有用的 technique 是 **back-chaining**。你从 terminal goal 开始，设想比如 5 年后自己想处于什么状态。接着，你试着思考 achieving 这个 goal 的 _necessary_ 条件是什么。哪些 high level steps 最能让你接近它？也就是最高层的 instrumental goals。哪些事情又能让你接近那些 goals？以此类推。

如果对你而言，finding terminal goals 比 finding 好的 instrumental goals 更容易，那么 Back-chaining 可以是很好的起点（我就是这样！）。它也是非常有价值的 tool，能确保你在 achieving 自己的 goals 时是 deliberate 且 purposeful 的。有了清晰的 chain of cause and effect，就更容易保持 grounded，也更容易察觉自己何时不再 grounded。

这很难，需要时间和精力，但可能极有价值。将它组织成由 bullet points 构成的 nested tree 会有所帮助——从 terminal goal 开始，sub-bullets 是 high-level instrumental goals，sub-sub-bullets 则是_那些 goals_ 的 instrumental goals，以此类推。

例如：

-   Terminal goal：组建家庭

    -   找到稳定、充满爱意的伴侣关系

        -   Understand 自己对 partner _想要_什么

            -   Gain experience（积累经验）

            -   对此进行 Introspect

            -   问问朋友的想法

        -   处于一段伴侣关系中

            -   让自己置身于能认识许多单身人士的场合

            -   多出门！

            -   Develop 主动迈出第一步的 confidence

            -   让自己也有吸引力

                -   对我的 [communication skills](https://www.amazon.com/Nonviolent-Communication-Language-Marshall-Rosenberg/dp/1892005034) 进行 Work on

                -   Develop interests（培养兴趣）

                -   Develop 我的 social skills

                -   Develop empathy（培养共情能力）

        -   有时间投入一段伴侣关系

            -   找一份有良好 work/life balance 的工作

    -   能够养家

        -   有钱

            -   找一份收入高的工作

        -   保持 good health——如果我有 health problem

            -   Exercise regularly（规律锻炼）

            -   Sleep well（睡好觉）

            -   Eat healthily（健康饮食）

        -   另一种办法：找一位收入高的伴侣

    -   养育出色的孩子

        -   找一位擅长这件事的伴侣

            -   弄清楚该怎么做 ???

        -   有一份能让我为此腾出时间的工作

        -   让他们接受良好的 education

            -   住在有好 schools 的地区

                -   现在考虑这个毫无意义

这不是我的 terminal goal，因此我没有花太多时间 fleshing out _这个_ example。但希望它能让你更好地理解这样做的目的！其中有许多 uncertainty、不同的 options，以及关于_如何_ break things down 的 ambiguity。有一些 empirical uncertainty；有些 instrumental goals，我知道自己有，却不确定该如何 act upon。但这是真正做到 deliberate、拥有 direction 的关键第一步。它还可以将 terminal goal 从令人恐惧、complex 且 aversive 的东西，变得更 manageable！如果你知道自己对哪些 steps 感到困惑，就可以通过 doing research、running experiments、寻求建议等方式，开始 _solve_ 这些问题。

这里的 meta point 是，uncertainty 和庞大、open-ended 的 tasks 很容易令人 paralysed。找到 make progress、让事情 concrete 的方法，是实际迈出第一步的关键。我把这称为在 problem 上获得 **surface area**——learning、exploring，并最终更好地 understanding 它。这不会是 perfect 的，而且需要进一步的努力和 iteration。但它比什么都不做要好_得多_。

Iterative Brainstorming（迭代式头脑风暴）

另一个 problem 是，试图写出自己全部的 life priorities，可能会让人觉得 _overwhelming_。这是个大问题！它很 complex，也很困难，而我对此的具体看法每天都可能变化。这些担忧都合理，也构成了 paralysing uncertainty 的另一个来源。

应对 paralysing uncertainty 的一种 solution，是 _reframe the question_。你不是在试图 _perfectly understand_ 自己的 goals，只是在试图 make progress。

Iterative brainstorming 是应对它的一种 approach，其 goal 是_定期_花时间 thinking through high-level goals。每一次，你都重读之前写下的内容，然后 iterate。Edit 几处看起来不对的地方，add 上次遗漏的东西，等等。实际做时，你可以每周花一个周日早晨尝试 back-chaining，并检查自己是否正按照自己的 goals 生活。在这段时间里，你并不是要 perfectly understand 自己的 goals。你会一遍遍回到这里，阅读上次写下的内容，修改它，等等。因此，它是否 perfect 并不_重要_。你只是在养成 regular reflection 和缓慢、incremental progress 的习惯。归根结底，人生是马拉松，不是短跑。你现在_不_需要 perfectly understand 自己的 goals，而 finding goals 是一个 iterative、incremental 的 process。这只是让这一点更 explicit 了一些。

Value of Information（信息价值）

这些 approaches 最难的部分之一，是有时你就是觉得困惑。你知道得不够，无法在 finding goals 上有意义地 make progress。比如在 careers 上，这极其常见——如果你还处于人生的早期阶段，你不可能只是坐下来想一会儿，最后就完全确信自己_确切_知道哪种 career 适合自己。如果你觉得自己可以，那你几乎肯定是极度 overconfident！“哪种 career 最适合我”是关于世界的 question，而“我_认为_哪种 career 最适合我”是关于你头脑的 fact——[默认情况下，这两者不是同一回事](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-12-the-map-and-the-territory)。

对此的 solution，是对自己的 goals 保持 **uncertainty**。对自己的 terminal goals 有_一些_ confidence 是合理的，但 instrumental goals 相当 specific，而通向同一个 end goal 的 paths 有很多。也许你对自己的 terminal goals 也弄错了！

但这并不意味着拥有 goals 这个 concept 就行不通。如果你对自己的 long-term goals 感到 uncertain，_这_就是你应该 prioritising 的事情！你的首要 priority 应该是 explore、gain information，并变得_不那么_ uncertain。

实际做时，有几种方法：

-   真正去尝试！Work on projects、做 internships、获得新的 experiences，等等。你的 goals 是 grounded 于真实世界的，因此你需要 data 和 experience 来更好地 calibrate 自己

    -   很多时候，你需要对某一条 instrumental goals 的 chain 作出“commit”，并 pursue 它们，但也要留意：这可能不是最佳 path。

    -   世界是 uncertain 的，你永远不可能对通向自己 goals 的最佳 path 有_真正的_ certainty，因此你需要在 keeping your options open 与实际做事之间取得 balance——这是个很难的 balance，也没有什么神奇公式

-   向别人寻求建议！[把这件事做好是有 skill 的](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations)，人与人不同，仅仅 understand 他们的 preferences 并不足够。我会试着 understand 他们的 underlying models，以及他们_为什么_这么说

-   做好功课——research 你的 options！

你也_一定_应该 prioritise 自己 gather information 的方式！你永远不可能 perfectly certain，因此需要集中精力。Back-chaining 对此可能有用——它能帮助 identify，在_它如何 achieves 你的 terminal goals_ 这一点上，哪些事情最令你困惑。

对于 careers 之类的事情，想象一个 concrete hypothetical world 往往会有帮助。比如，如果我正在做 consulting internship，就想象自己已经当了 5 年 consultant，却对生活感到不满。然后 work backwards，试着 _explain_ 为什么事情出了问题。由此产生的 reasons 会是关键的 uncertainties，也应该是你最需要 explore 的事情！

让自己的 options 保持 open

应对 uncertainty 的另一种 solution，是寻找 _robust_ 的 goals——你的 terminal goals 存在，但你对它们有 uncertainty，而你希望朝自己大多数 plausible goals 前进。一种 robust approach 是寻找 **convergent instrumental goals** \- 对几乎所有 plausible terminal goals 都 robustly useful 的 goals。也就是能让你获得 information、skills、resources 的事情。能为你提供 options 的事情，等你以后更 understand 自己想要什么时，就可以 leverage 这些 options。它们可能并不 _perfect_ \- 可能存在一些 worlds，在其中这样做会浪费一些时间，但它们在_大多数_ worlds 中应该是 useful 的。

常见的 examples：

-   Gaining employable skills（学习如何 program、data science/ML、学习 stats、良好的 communication skills、良好的 writing skills）

-   Learning 新事物

    -   注意——对一些人来说，这是 terminal goal；对另一些人来说，它则纯粹是 instrumental 的——值得留意其中的 difference！

-   找到稳定、充满爱意的 romantic relationship

-   Exercising regularly（规律锻炼）

-   Eating healthily（健康饮食）

-   对自己的 mental health 进行 Working on

-   拥有良好的 social network

-   Building 良好的 meta-skills——保持 reflective、善于 introspecting、善于 [fixing 自己生活中的 problems](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)

    -   擅长 prioritisation！

### Conclusion（结语）

Goals _真的_很重要。它们令人困惑，也很难弄清，而且不可能完全弄对。但我认为，这是你所能思考的最重要的事情之一。不过，我觉得，对我们许多人来说，它从来都不像是一项 _priority_。或者，你感到无助，觉得自己并不真的 _能_通过思考自己的 goals 来 make progress。我最近在思考自己对 goals 有多困惑、这种困惑又多么令人 paralysing 时，意识到自己竟然从来没有真正 _做那件显而易见的事_，专门留出时间来思考它们。尽管这大概是我所能弄清的最重要的事情，而且会极大地 shape 我的人生 direction。我也意识到，这种 inconsistent 实在令人难堪。

所以，既然我目前有不少空闲时间，我现在每周都会 allocating 一个下午，专门 zooming out，思考这些事情！对我的 terminal goals 进行 Introspecting，再通过 back-chaining 来寻找我的 instrumental goals。这周日，我还会参加 Complice 的 [Goal-Crafting Intensive](https://complice.co/event/gci2020midyear?r=bl0mokil67)，这看起来也有助于真正 exploring 这些事情！（利益披露：如果你通过那个链接报名，我会获得一小笔推荐奖金。截至撰写本文时，European time zones 的场次还剩 1 个名额）

如果你也同样感到困惑，我强烈建议你**真正花时间思考这些事情**。这个世界的 default state 就是你不会 prioritise，因为这很难，而且我们都不擅长。我们很容易接触到这些想法，觉得它们很重要，然后为此感到内疚。也许还会突然涌起一阵 motivation，觉得自己将来某天一定会去做！这就是在 [pressing the Try Harder button](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)。如果这篇文章中的想法引起了你的共鸣，而且你想进一步思考自己的 high-level goals，那就想想你**现在立刻**能采取什么 action，确保未来的自己真的会思考这些事情。选定一个你会有空的具体时间，在日历上专门留出这一时段。

## 原文

_(This was a slightly too ambitious post to write in one day, so I’ve split it into two halves. This half motivates prioritisation + discusses finding goals,_ [_tomorrow’s_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/2glo4ww7hd035m56cc5l7zq9r7bxhc) _will discuss how to act towards your goals)_

### Introduction

One of the most important life skills you can ever learn is how to prioritise things well. We all have limited time, and energy, and resources. And life is a continuous process of spending resources to achieve my goals. And figuring out _how_ to spend resources to best achieve your goals is incredibly key to the outcome.

Yet this is very obviously something that 99.9% of people suck at (including me!). And this makes sense - prioritisation is _hard_ and you will never be perfect at it. And it’s easy to fall into traps like always prioritising, being constantly paralysed with indecision, and never really _doing_ anything. But I think that the value of prioritising the right amount is _incredibly_ high, especially compared to not prioritising at all, and thus worth striving for.

This is a difficult post to write, because I am far from an expert at prioritisation. And there’s a wide spectrum of how people struggle to prioritise - a lot of people have blind spots at the _need_ to prioritise at all, while some people agonise far too much about prioritisation, and the resulting paralysis holds them back from achieving anything at all. In this post, I am going to attempt to outline why you _should_ care about prioritisation, some thoughts on finding the goals _to_ prioritise, a brain dump of advice on how to actually implement it, and common mistakes I think people make at either end of this spectrum.

### Why you should prioritise prioritisation

Prioritisation is one of the most important things you can ever do. The world is _complicated_. It’s follow of opportunities, and traps. Beautiful things that can add a ton of value to your life, and unimportant rabbit holes you can fall down, and which hold you back from achieving what you could. And because the world is so complicated, the actions we can take have _big_ differences in their pay-offs. Different actions can easily have _massive_ differences in their outputs. I know that the [best charities](https://givewell.org/) can do tens to hundreds of time more good than average charities. I know that some projects will fill me with excitement, while others feel like a drag. I know that some people spark joy in my life, while others do not. And our intuitions are not a good guide to this, because we evolved in a far simpler world. But [our intuitions live in our minds, while our goals live in the world](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-12-the-map-and-the-territory), and you should not let this hold you back.

And prioritisation is _hard_ to take seriously. It’s big, and fuzzy, and abstract, and triggers so many well-known biases. It’s really easy to fail to [be deliberate](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/be-deliberate) and track _how_ your actions help you, and easy to fall into doing what everybody around you does. It’s difficult to keep track of the alternatives and how much better they could be, they are vague and abstract while your current actions are _concrete_. There’s a strong attachment to the [default action](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-15-the-illusion-of-doing-nothing). If nobody around you takes prioritisation seriously, it doesn’t feel _urgent_. Humans focus on the short-term, and neglect the long-term, and it’s easy to fall into spirals of constant busy-work - mindless problem sheets, boring emails, toxic friendships, a web of unwanted obligations - and to realise that in the last month you spent practically _no_ time working towards your high level goals. It’s hard to have an intuition for the value of _information_, that spending a while thinking and exploring could genuinely help you, and instead to think you already know best.

And these are just _some_ of the many biases at play here. The point of that long list is that failure to prioritise shouldn’t be surprising, and shouldn’t feel like a personal failure. **Failure to prioritise is the default state of the human experience**. You need to constantly fight against the entropy pushing you towards failing to act towards your goals. Our default settings do not think about prioritisation, and this is something you will need to try hard to change.

Further, it’s easy to think that prioritisation should only apply to certain kinds of goals, like your work. Things that are directly tangible and quantifiable, something explicitly focused on real-world results. But just as you can [optimise for any goal](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise), all goals need prioritisation! If my goal is to feel happy and fulfilled, then I should prioritise finding good friendships and relationships, removing stress from my life, and ensuring I have good sleep and rest. And it’s easy to fail to take actions that move me towards this.

### Understanding goals

A really key component of prioritisation is knowing what your _goals_ are. This is the factor that should most determine which actions you take. For example, in my degree it feels _much_ more salient to me to optimise how hard I’m working - how much I focus, how much time I spend - than it does to optimise _how_ I’m working - which courses I do, whether I bother doing the problem sheet at all, the mindset with which I approach the course. How hard I’m working feels very _tangible_, I have control over it, it’s much easier to quantify and to fit into the role of being a good student. But it just doesn’t make that big a difference. At the end of the day, I’ve still done the sheets, I’ve still learned something from them. I might do them to a slightly higher standard, or have a bit more time left over, but the amount of efficiency I can squeeze out by “[just trying harder](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)” just isn’t that high. But my goals are not “do my sheets as efficiently as possible”. If my goal is to learn the content as well as possible, some questions are a complete waste of my time! If my goal is to impress my supervisor so they write a good reference, I should focus on conceptual clarity and good write-ups! If I want to have fun, I should choose the courses I think I’ll most enjoy! If I want to gain the meta-skills of thinking like a mathematician, I should dive down rabbit holes of the hardest courses and seek to understand what’s _actually_ going on! If I want to learn _directly_ _useful_ skills, I should choose stats courses! And _maybe_ doing sheets efficiently helps me with these, but it’s rarely the most effective path. And understanding my true goals _massively_ informs how I should be acting.

And finding your goals is hard! Especially if you’re younger, it can be difficult to know what your goals should be, and so give up on prioritising. Or, you could commit the mistake of _thinking_ you know what your goals are, and optimising really hard for the proxy goal, and not realising what you’re missing. I think this is the key mistake behind somebody who takes a prestigious but soulless and unpleasant corporate job, like investment banking, and ultimately ends up not enjoying life. But, though hard, I think this is a _really_ important problem, and one you can make progress on.

Terminal Goals

It’s useful to think about which _kinds_ of goals you have. There are **terminal, or intrinsic, goals** - high-level goals I value for their own sake. Common examples:

-   Making the world a better place

-   Adding to the body of human knowledge

-   Feeling happy and fulfilled

-   Starting a family

-   The happiness of loved ones

-   Safety and stability

-   Having a legacy

For me, my strongest terminal goals are to feel happy and fulfilled, and to make the world a better place (note - [there’s an important distinction between making the world a better place _in order to feel happy_, and as an intrinsic goal](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-8-what-altruism-means-to-me)). But this is _extremely_ personal and subjective. I find this is the kind of thing you really just need to introspect on, and examine what you truly value. But I find that most people normally have some idea of what these are. [A useful tool for exploring this](https://programs.clearerthinking.org/intrinsic_values_test.html).

A good hack: humans are very short-term focused, and it’s easy to focus too much on the present. But terminal goals are for the long-term. I find it helpful to picture myself in 5 years, or 20 years, and ask what I’d want from my life then. This abstracts out from the short-term concerns, and is a good way to filter for what I _really_ care about.

A warning: A _super_ common mistake is to take your terminal goals from social cues, and your culture. This rarely works. Your terminal goals _may_ align with your culture, but they may not. A terminal goal should feel _visceral_. It should be something you genuinely care about, and something you would fight for. Something where your life would feel like it is _missing_ something if you don’t have it. I think people often, say, feel like they should want a family because their parents or culture expect it of them, or that they need to focus on prestige or making money because it’s the “done thing”. No, no, a thousand times no! If you want these _for their own sake_, then that’s awesome! But **do not let your terminal goals be dictated to you**. These are the things you will spend your life working towards. And if you take on the wrong goal, because that’s what your culture tells you to do, **you’ve already lost**.

Instrumental goals

If you can take actions that obviously and directly work towards your terminal goals, that’s amazing! But this is hard, because the world is complex. Which brings me to the second category: **instrumental goals**. These are things I care about, _because they bring me closer to my terminal goals_, rather than as a value in and of themselves. This is an important concept, because the world is complicated. It’s difficult to know how to directly achieve your terminal goals, and our intuitions suck at this. Instrumental goals are a key mental shortcut to actually make progress on this.

It’s key to know the difference, because I _only_ want to do an instrumental goal if it achieves my terminal goal. So I need to _know_ whether it achieves my terminal goals. Much of the failures of people to [be deliberate](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/be-deliberate) ultimately stem from this - it’s easy to absorb an instrumental goal from social norms and culture, and to _think_ it brings you closer to your terminal goals, but fail.

Common examples:

-   Earning money

-   Having a prestigious degree

-   Prepping for job interviews

-   Cramming for exams

-   Meeting new people

-   Joining a society

In practice, you want to understand your terminal goals and use them to find your instrumental goals, and keep your instrumental goals grounded. And then in day-to-day life, you want to mostly just think about your instrumental goals. These can get very specific and nested! For example, maybe my terminal goal is to have a family. I can achieve this by getting a high-paying career as a software engineer at Google. I can achieve this by developing a good portfolio of coding projects. I can achieve this by becoming _motivated_ to work on a coding project. I can achieve this by having friends who are really pumped about coding things. I can achieve this by going to a local hackers society, and trying to make friends. We’re now 6 layers deep, but this could genuinely be a good way to achieve your goals! And you _can’t_ go through this process for every action, you _need_ to cache your goals as specific, instrumental goals to make any progress.

But it’s easy for this grounding to get lost! Maybe I just wouldn’t cut it as a software engineer. Maybe Google wouldn’t be impressed with my portfolio, and instead I should prep for coding interviews. Maybe I should first be working on _my_ coding skills. It’s important to take regular time to reflect on whether you’re _actually_ making progress towards your terminal goals (or even your higher instrumental goals!), to check that this delicate balance isn’t broken.

### How to _Find_ Goals

It’s all well and good to talk about how important goals are, and how to categorise them. And I think conceptual clarity is an important step to getting started. But it’s easy to feel paralysed, and for this to not feel actionable. So how can you make progress?

Back-Chaining

A super useful technique for converting terminal goals to instrumental goals is **back-chaining**. You start with the terminal goal, an idea of where you want to be in, say, 5 years. And then you try to think about what would be _necessary_ for achieving this. What would be the best high level steps that get you closer to this? The highest level of instrumental goals. What would be the things that get you closer to that? Etc.

Back-chaining can be a good starting point if finding terminal goals is easier for you than finding good instrumental goals (it is for me!). And it’s a very valuable tool for ensuring that you’re deliberate and purposeful about achieving your goals. By having a clear chain of cause and effect, it’s much easier to remain grounded, and to notice when you stop being grounded.

This is hard, and takes time and effort, but can be extremely valuable. It helps to structure it as a nested tree of bullet points - begin with the terminal goal, the sub-bullets are high-level instrumental goals, sub-sub-bullets are instrumental goals for _those_, etc.

For example:

-   Terminal goal: Have a family

    -   Find a stable, loving relationship

        -   Understand what I _want_ in a partner

            -   Gain experience

            -   Introspect on this

            -   Ask friends for their thoughts

        -   Be in a relationship

            -   Put myself in situations where I meet a lot of single people

            -   Go outside more!

            -   Develop the confidence to initiate things

            -   Be desirable myself

                -   Work on my [communication skills](https://www.amazon.com/Nonviolent-Communication-Language-Marshall-Rosenberg/dp/1892005034)

                -   Develop interests

                -   Develop my social skills

                -   Develop empathy

        -   Have the time to devote to a relationship

            -   Find a job with a good work/life balance

    -   Be able to support a family

        -   Have money

            -   Find a well-paying job

        -   Be in good health - if I have a health problem

            -   Exercise regularly

            -   Sleep well

            -   Eat healthily

        -   Alt: Find a high-earning partner

    -   Raise awesome kids

        -   Find a partner who’d be good at this

            -   Figure out how ???

        -   Have a job which lets me take time for this

        -   Get them a good education

            -   Live in an area with good schools

                -   Pointless to think about right now

This is not a terminal goal of mine, and so I haven’t spent too much time fleshing out _this_ example. But hopefully that gave a better idea of the purpose of this! There’s a lot of uncertainty, different options, ambiguity about _how_ to break things down. There’s some empirical uncertainty, there are instrumental goals I know I have, but where I’m not sure how to act upon. But this is a key first step to truly being deliberate, and having direction. And it can turn a terminal goal from something terrifying, complex and aversive into something more manageable! If you know the steps you’re confused about, you can start to _solve_ this, by doing research, running experiments, asking for advice, etc.

The meta point is that it’s easy to be paralysed by uncertainty, and by big, open-ended tasks. Finding ways to make progress, make things concrete, is key to actually taking the first step. I call this getting **surface area** on the problem - learning, exploring, and ultimately understanding it better. This won’t be perfect, and will require further effort and iteration. But it’s a _lot_ better than doing nothing.

Iterative Brainstorming

Another problem is that it can feel _overwhelming_ to try to write out your entire life priorities. This is a big question! This is complex, and difficult, and my exact view on it will vary day to day. These are all valid concerns, and another source of paralysing uncertainty.

One solution to paralysing uncertainty, is to _reframe the question_. You aren’t trying to _perfectly understand_ your goals, you’re just trying to make progress.

Iterative brainstorming is an approach to this, where the goal is to _regularly_ spend time thinking through high-level goals. And each time, you read over what you had before, and iterate. Edit a few bits that seem off, add something you missed last time, etc. In practice, you might spend Sunday morning once a week trying to do back-chaining, and checking whether you’re living your life in accordance with your goals. And in this time, you aren’t trying to perfectly understand your goals. You’re going to be coming back to this again and again, reading through what you put last time, changing it, etc. So it doesn’t _matter_ if it’s perfect. You’re just making a habit of regular reflection and slow, incremental progress. And ultimately, life is a marathon, not a sprint. You _don’t_ need to perfectly understand your goals now, and finding goals is an iterative, incremental process. This just makes that a bit more explicit.

Value of Information

One of the hardest parts of these approaches is sometimes you just feel confused. You don’t know enough to be able to meaningfully make progress on finding goals. This is super common with, say, careers - if you’re early in your life and you can’t just sit down and think for a while, and end up fully convinced you know _exactly_ what career is right for you. If you think you can, you’re almost certainly being wildly overconfident! “Which career is best for me” is a question about the world, and “which career do I _think_ is best for me” is a fact about your mind - [by default these are not the same thing](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-12-the-map-and-the-territory).

The solution to this is to have **uncertainty** about your goals. It’s reasonable to have _some_ confidence in your terminal goals, but instrumental goals are pretty specific, and there are many paths to the same end goal. And maybe you’re mistaken about your terminal goals!

But this isn’t a deal-breaker for the concept of having goals. If you’re uncertain about your long-term goals, _this_ is the thing you should be prioritising! Your number one priority should be to explore, to gain information, and to become _less_ uncertain.

In practice, there are a few ways to do this:

-   Actually try things! Work on projects, do internships, have new experiences, etc. Your goals are grounded in the real world, so you need data and experience to better calibrate yourself

    -   Often, you want to “commit” to a particular chain of instrumental goals, and pursue those, but keep an eye out for the idea that this is not the best path.

    -   The world is uncertain, and you can never have _true_ certainty about the best path to your goals, so you need to balance between keeping your options open and actually doing things - this is a difficult balance and there’s no magic formula to it

-   Ask people for advice! There is [skill to doing this well](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations), people are different, and it’s not enough to just understand their preferences. I try to understand their underlying models, and _why_ they say what they do

-   Do your homework - research your options!

And you should _definitely_ prioritise how you gather information! You can never be perfectly certain, and need to focus your efforts. Back-chaining can be useful for this - it can help identify what things you’re most confused about, with regards to _how it achieves your terminal goals_.

For something like careers, it can often help to imagine a concrete hypothetical world. If, say, I’m doing a consulting internship, imagine that I’ve worked as a consultant for 5 years, and I feel dissatisfied with life. Then, work backwards and try to _explain_ why this went wrong. The reasons this generates will be key uncertainties, and should be things you should explore most!

Keeping your options open

An alternate solution to uncertainty is to seek _robust_ goals - your terminal goals exist, but you have uncertainty about them, and you want to be working towards most of your plausible goals. A robust approach is to look for **convergent instrumental goals** \- goals that are robustly useful, for almost all plausible terminal goals. Things that get you information, skills, resources. Things that give you options, which you could later leverage when you better understand what you want. These might not be _perfect_ \- there could be worlds where this wastes some time, but they should be useful in _most_ worlds.

Common examples:

-   Gaining employable skills (learning how to program, data science/ML, learning stats, good communication skills, good writing skills)

-   Learning new things

    -   Note - for some people this is a terminal goal, for others it’s purely instrumental - it’s worth looking for the difference!

-   Finding a stable, loving romantic relationship

-   Exercising regularly

-   Eating healthily

-   Working on your mental health

-   Having a good social network

-   Building good meta-skills - being reflective, good at introspecting, good at [fixing problems in your life](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)

    -   Being good at prioritisation!

### Conclusion

Goals are _really_ important. They’re confusing, and difficult, and impossible to get perfectly right. But I think this is one of the most important things you can ever think about. But I think for many of us, it never feels like a _priority_. Or, you feel helpless, and don’t feel like you really _can_ make progress by thinking about your goals. I recently realised, when thinking about how confused I was about my goals, and how paralysing this was, that I’d never actually _done the obvious thing_ and blocked out time to think about them. Even though this is approximately the most important thing I could possibly be figuring out, and will significantly shape my life’s direction. And that this was embarrassingly inconsistent.

So, since I currently have a surplus of free time, I’m currently allocating an afternoon a week to just zooming out, and thinking about this stuff! Introspecting on my terminal goals, and back-chaining to find my instrumental goals. And I’m doing the Complice [Goal-Crafting Intensive](https://complice.co/event/gci2020midyear?r=bl0mokil67) this Sunday, which also seems helpful towards actually exploring this! (Disclosure: I get a small referral bonus if you sign up via that link. And as of the time of writing, there’s 1 slot remaining in European time zones)

If you feel similarly confused, I strongly urge you to **actually spend time thinking about this stuff**. The default state of the world is that you will not prioritise, because this is hard, and we all suck at it. And it’s easy to encounter these ideas, think that it’s important, and feel guilty about it. Maybe feel a burst of motivation, and like you’ll definitely get round to it some day! This is [pressing the Try Harder button](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button). If the ideas in this post resonated with you, and you want to think more about your high-level goals, think about what action you can take **right now** to ensure that your future self actually thinks about this stuff. Pick a specific time when you’ll be free, and put a block on your calendar.
