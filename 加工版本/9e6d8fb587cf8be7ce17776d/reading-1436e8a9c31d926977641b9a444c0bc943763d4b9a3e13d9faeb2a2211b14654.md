# 229 Good Writing

## 阅读前置卡

- 价值判断：高。文章提出一个反直觉但可检验的写作主张：声音好的写作更可能把想法写对，并用排版重写、摇箱子、飞机形状等类比和作者自身写作经验加以论证，对写作者有直接可操作的启发，但论证主要依赖个人观察与类比，并非严格实证。
- 信息密度判断：高。全文围绕一个核心命题层层推进，几乎每段都引入新的区分或限定：两种 good、right 不等于 true、摇箱类比、作者是第一个读者、节奏与思想形状、说谎者的反例、内部一致与真理的收敛，信息密度高且少有冗余。
- 是否继续阅读：4 精细阅读。论证从反直觉命题出发，先消解荒谬感，再追加更强主张，随后用反例自我修正，节奏紧凑、悬念递进，读者会想继续看作者如何收束两种 good 的关系。

## 核心洞察

一、两种 good 并非无关：写作可以在两个意义上好：句子听起来好，以及想法是对的。通常人们把二者比作汽车的速度和颜色，认为互不相关；但本文主张声音好的写作更可能把想法写对，因为把其中一个推到极致时，另一个并不会被牺牲，反而常常同步改善。
[[#^pr-ef85581b0105|正文跳转]]

二、right 比 true 要求更多：把想法写对不只是说真的事情，而是把想法充分展开：得出最重要的结论，并把每个结论探索到恰当的细节程度。因此 right 的写作是在说正确的真事情，而不仅是罗列真命题。
[[#^pr-32c109d37b99|正文跳转]]

三、任意约束反而改善写作：排版时遇到某节多出一行，作者会重写该节使其短一行。这种任意约束本应让写作变糟，但作者发现结果总是自己更喜欢的版本。类似地，让任何人把随机段落改短或改长，他们大概都能想出更好的表达。
[[#^pr-88cf7f797d72|正文跳转]]

四、摇箱子类比：摇晃装满不同物体的箱子，摇晃本身不是为让任意两个物体更紧密配合而计算的，但反复摇晃会让物体找到极聪明的堆叠方式，因为重力不会让它们变得更松散，所以任何变化都只能是向好的变化。写作中重写别扭段落时，作者无法忍受让它变得更不真实，因此想法中的任何变化也必须是向好的变化。
[[#^pr-35056fd85888|正文跳转]]

五、作者是第一个读者：声音好让文章更容易读，而写作者在写作时花在阅读上的时间远多于写作，会把某些部分重读五十到一百遍，在脑中回放想法，像打磨木头的人一样检查是否有东西卡住。文章越容易读，就越容易注意到哪里不对劲。
[[#^pr-758dbb1ebc7e|正文跳转]]

六、节奏匹配思想形状：好写作的节奏不是音乐节奏或诗句格律，它不规则，因为必须与其中的想法匹配。简单想法只需陈述，微妙想法则需要更长更复杂的句子来引出全部含义。文章是被清理过的思路，有自然节奏，因此把节奏弄对可以作为把想法弄对的启发式。
[[#^pr-c7abddb4b45f|正文跳转]]

七、飞机形状而非汽车颜色：写作的声音更像飞机的形状，而不是汽车的颜色。如果它看起来好，正如 Kelly Johnson 常说的，它就会飞得好。这一类比把两种 good 从装饰性关联提升为功能性关联。
[[#^pr-846a03b9febe|正文跳转]]

八、仅适用于发展想法的写作：上述紧密联系只适用于用来发展想法的写作。如果想法先以其他方式存在，比如先造出东西或做完实验再写论文，想法更多存在于工作中而非写作中，写作即使想法好也可能差。教科书和通俗综述可能因此写得差，因为作者不是在发展想法，只是在描述别人的想法。
[[#^pr-850d5a5e860d|正文跳转]]

九、说谎者与内部一致：写出美丽而虚假的东西是可能的，但需要方法派表演：先让自己几乎相信它，呈现一条完美成形的思路，区别只在于它连接到世界的那个点。因此更准确的说法是：声音更好的写作更可能是内部一致的；如果作者诚实，内部一致与真理就会收敛。
[[#^pr-bccbb42382b1|正文跳转]]

十、反向推断更可靠：虽然不能安全地断定美丽的写作就是真的，但通常可以安全地断定其反面：看起来写得笨拙的东西通常也把想法搞错了。这为读者提供了一条更稳健的启发式。
[[#^pr-6b62f4587000|正文跳转]]

十一、绳子而非杆子：好写作的两种意义更像是同一个东西的两端，它们之间的联系不是刚性的，不是一根杆，而是一根绳子，由多重重叠的连接贯穿。但很难移动一端而不移动另一端，很难做到对而不听起来对。
[[#^pr-6e0b71413e90|正文跳转]]

十二、插入新点会破坏流动：最接近例外的情况是不得不回头在已写好的内容中间插入新点，这常会打乱流动，有时无法完全修复。其根源在于想法是树形的，而文章是线性的，把前者塞进后者必然遇到困难，甚至不得不求助于尾注。
[[#^pr-2beaee667916|正文跳转]]

# 229 Good Writing

May 2025

writing 可以在两种 senses 上是 good：它可以 sound good，而 ideas 可以是 right。它可以有漂亮、流畅的 sentences，也可以对重要的事情得出正确的 conclusions。这两种 good 似乎可能是不相关的，就像一辆车的 speed 和它被涂上的 color。然而我不认为它们是不相关的。我认为 sound good 的 writing 更可能是 right 的。

^pr-ef85581b0105

所以这里我们有了最令人兴奋的那种 idea：一个既显得 preposterous 又 true 的 idea。让我们来考察它。这怎么可能 true 呢？

我从 writing 中知道这是 true 的。你无法同时 optimize 两个不相关的东西；当你把一个推得足够远时，你最终总是会 sacrifice 另一个。然而无论我推得多用力，我从未发现自己不得不在 sound 最好的 sentence 和表达一个 idea 最好的 sentence 之间做出选择。如果我必须这样，那么在意 sentences 如何 sound 就会是 frivolous 的。但在实践中，它感觉与 frivolous 恰恰相反。修复 sound 不好的 sentences 似乎有助于 get the ideas right。[1](#fn1)

我说的 right 不只是 true 的意思。Getting the ideas right 意味着把它们 develop 好——得出最重要的 conclusions，并把每一个都 explore 到恰当的 detail 程度。所以 getting the ideas right 不只是说 true 的事情，而是说 right 的 true 事情。

^pr-32c109d37b99

试图让 sentences sound good 怎么会帮助你做到这一点呢？答案的线索是我 30 年前为我的第一本书做 layout 时注意到的一件事。有时当你 laying out text 时，你会运气不好。例如，你得到一个比页面多出一行的 section。我不知道普通的 typesetters 在这种情况下会怎么做，但我做的是 rewrite 这个 section，让它短一行。你会预期这样一个任意的 constraint 会让 writing 变得更糟。但我发现，令我惊讶的是，它从未如此。我最终总是得到我更喜欢的东西。

^pr-88cf7f797d72

我不认为这是因为我的 writing 特别粗心。我认为如果你指向任何人写的任何东西中的一个随机 paragraph，并告诉他们把它变得稍微短一点（或长一点），他们大概都能想出更好的东西。

对这个现象最好的 analogy 是当你 shake 一个装满不同 objects 的 bin。这些 shakes 是任意的 motions。或者更准确地说，它们并不是被计算来让任何两个特定的 objects 更紧密地 fit 在一起。然而反复 shaking 不可避免地让这些 objects 发现极其聪明的 packing 自己的方式。Gravity 不会让它们变得 less tightly packed，所以任何 change 都必须是向好的 change。[2](#fn2)

^pr-35056fd85888

writing 也是如此。如果你必须 rewrite 一段 awkward 的 passage，你绝不会以一种让它 _less_ true 的方式来 rewrite。你无法忍受那样，就像 gravity 无法忍受东西向上漂浮一样。所以 ideas 中的任何 change 都必须是向好的 change。

一旦你想到这一点，它就显而易见了。Sound good 的 writing 更可能是 right 的，原因与一个被充分 shaken 的 bin 更可能是 tightly packed 的相同。但还有别的东西也在起作用。Sounding good 不仅仅是一种随机的 external force，让一篇文章中的 ideas 变得更好。它实际上帮助你 get them right。

原因是它让 essay 更容易 read。Read 流畅的 writing 是更少的工作。这如何帮助 writer 呢？_因为 writer 是第一个 reader。_ 当我在写一篇 essay 时，我花在 reading 上的时间远远多于 writing。我会把某些部分 reread 50 或 100 次，在脑中 replay 其中的 thoughts，并像打磨一块木头的人那样问自己，有什么东西 catch 吗？有什么东西感觉不对吗？而 essay 越容易 read，就越容易注意到是否有东西 catch。

^pr-758dbb1ebc7e

所以是的，good writing 的两种 senses 至少以两种方式相连。试图让 writing sound good 会让你无意识地 fix mistakes，也帮助你自觉地 fix 它们；它 shakes the bin of ideas，也让 mistakes 更容易被看到。但既然我们已经消解了一层 preposterousness，我忍不住再加上一层。Sounding good 所做的仅仅是帮助你 get the ideas right 吗？Sound good 的 writing 是否 _inherently_ 更可能是 right 的？尽管这看起来可能很疯狂，我认为这也是 true 的。

显然在单个 words 的层面存在一种 connection。英语中有很多 words sound 起来像它们的意思，往往以极其微妙的方式。Glitter。Round。Scrape。Prim。Cavalcade。但 good writing 的 sound 更取决于你把 words 放在一起的方式，而在那个层面也存在一种 connection。

当 writing sound good 时，主要是因为它有好的 rhythm。但 good writing 的 rhythm 不是音乐的 rhythm，也不是 verse 的 meter。它没有那么规则。如果它那么规则，它就不会是 good 的，因为 good writing 的 rhythm 必须与其中的 ideas 相匹配，而 ideas 有各种不同的 shapes。有时它们很简单，你只需陈述它们。但其他时候它们更微妙，你需要更长、更复杂的 sentences 来 tease out 所有的 implications。

一篇 essay 是一条被清理过的 train of thought，就像 dialogue 是被清理过的 conversation，而一条 train of thought 有一种自然的 rhythm。所以当一篇 essay sound good 时，不仅仅是因为它有一种令人愉悦的 rhythm，而是因为它有它自然的 rhythm。这意味着你可以把 getting the rhythm right 用作 getting the ideas right 的 heuristic。而且不仅仅是在原则上：好的 writers 理所当然地同时做这两件事。我常常甚至不区分这两个问题。我只是想，呃，这 sound 不对；我在这里到底想说什么？[3](#fn3)

^pr-c7abddb4b45f

writing 的 sound 结果更像一架 plane 的 shape，而不是一辆车的 color。如果它看起来好，正如 Kelly Johnson 过去常说的，它就会 fly 得好。

^pr-846a03b9febe

不过，这仅适用于用来 develop ideas 的 writing。当你以某种其他方式拥有 ideas 然后再写它们时，它并不适用——例如，如果你 build 了某个东西，或者 conduct 了一个 experiment，然后写一篇关于它的 paper。在这种情况下，ideas 往往更多地存在于 work 中而不是 writing 中，所以即使 ideas 是好的，writing 也可能是坏的。教科书和通俗综述中的 writing 可能出于同样的原因是坏的：作者不是在 develop ideas，只是在描述别人的 ideas。只有当你为了 develop ideas 而 writing 时，做好这件事的两种 senses 之间才有如此紧密的 connection。

^pr-850d5a5e860d

好吧，很多人会想，到目前为止这似乎 plausible，但 liars 呢？一个 smooth-tongued liar 写出一篇完全 false 的美丽东西，难道不是众所周知地可能吗？

当然可能。但并非没有 method acting。写出美丽而 false 的东西的方法是，先让自己几乎相信它。所以就像写美丽而 true 的东西的人一样，你呈现的是一条完美成形的 train of thought。区别在于它 attach 到世界的那个点。你说的是某种如果某些 false premises 成立就会是 true 的东西。如果出于某种离奇的原因，一个国家的 jobs 数量是固定的，那么 immigrants 就真的会 taking our jobs。

所以说 sound 更好的 writing 更可能是 true 并不完全对。Sound 更好的 writing 更可能是 internally consistent 的。如果 writer 是诚实的，internal consistency 和 truth 就会 converge。

^pr-bccbb42382b1

但尽管我们不能安全地断定美丽的 writing 是 true 的，通常可以安全地断定其 converse：看起来写得笨拙的东西通常也把 ideas 搞错了。

^pr-6b62f4587000

确实，good writing 的两种 senses 更像是同一个东西的两端。它们之间的 connection 不是刚性的；good writing 的 goodness 不是一根 rod，而是一根 rope，其中贯穿着多重重叠的 connections。但很难移动一端而不移动另一端。很难做到 right 而不 sound right。

^pr-6e0b71413e90

# 229 Good Writing

May 2025

There are two senses in which writing can be good: it can sound good, and the ideas can be right. It can have nice, flowing sentences, and it can draw correct conclusions about important things. It might seem as if these two kinds of good would be unrelated, like the speed of a car and the color it’s painted. And yet I don’t think they are. I think writing that sounds good is more likely to be right.

So here we have the most exciting kind of idea: one that seems both preposterous and true. Let’s examine it. How can this possibly be true?

I know it’s true from writing. You can’t simultaneously optimize two unrelated things; when you push one far enough, you always end up sacrificing the other. And yet no matter how hard I push, I never find myself having to choose between the sentence that sounds best and the one that expresses an idea best. If I did, it would be frivolous to care how sentences sound. But in practice it feels the opposite of frivolous. Fixing sentences that sound bad seems to help get the ideas right. [1](#fn1)

By right I mean more than just true. Getting the ideas right means developing them well – drawing the conclusions that matter most, and exploring each one to the right level of detail. So getting the ideas right is not just a matter of saying true things, but saying the right true things.

How could trying to make sentences sound good help you do that? The clue to the answer is something I noticed 30 years ago when I was doing the layout for my first book. Sometimes when you’re laying out text you have bad luck. For example, you get a section that runs one line longer than the page. I don’t know what ordinary typesetters do in this situation, but what I did was rewrite the section to make it a line shorter. You’d expect such an arbitrary constraint to make the writing worse. But I found, to my surprise, that it never did. I always ended up with something I liked better.

I don’t think this was because my writing was especially careless. I think if you pointed to a random paragraph in anything written by anyone and told them to make it slightly shorter (or longer), they’d probably be able to come up with something better.

The best analogy for this phenomenon is when you shake a bin full of different objects. The shakes are arbitrary motions. Or more precisely, they’re not calculated to make any two specific objects fit more closely together. And yet repeated shaking inevitably makes the objects discover brilliantly clever ways of packing themselves. Gravity won’t let them become less tightly packed, so any change has to be a change for the better. [2](#fn2)

So it is with writing. If you have to rewrite an awkward passage, you’ll never do it in a way that makes it _less_ true. You couldn’t bear it, any more than gravity could bear things floating upward. So any change in the ideas has to be a change for the better.

It’s obvious once you think about it. Writing that sounds good is more likely to be right for the same reason that a well-shaken bin is more likely to be tightly packed. But there’s something else going on as well. Sounding good isn’t just a random external force that leaves the ideas in an essay better off. It actually helps you to get them right.

The reason is that it makes the essay easier to read. It’s less work to read writing that flows well. How does that help the writer? _Because the writer is the first reader._ When I’m working on an essay, I spend far more time reading than writing. I’ll reread some parts 50 or 100 times, replaying the thoughts in them and asking myself, like someone sanding a piece of wood, does anything catch? Does anything feel wrong? And the easier the essay is to read, the easier it is to notice if something catches.

So yes, the two senses of good writing are connected in at least two ways. Trying to make writing sound good makes you fix mistakes unconsciously, and also helps you fix them consciously; it shakes the bin of ideas, and also makes mistakes easier to see. But now that we’ve dissolved one layer of preposterousness, I can’t resist adding another. Does sounding good do more than just help you get the ideas right? Is writing that sounds good _inherently_ more likely to be right? Crazy as it may seem, I think that’s true too.

Obviously there’s a connection at the level of individual words. There are lots of words in English that sound like what they mean, often in wonderfully subtle ways. Glitter. Round. Scrape. Prim. Cavalcade. But the sound of good writing depends even more on the way you put words together, and there’s a connection at that level too.

When writing sounds good, it’s mostly because it has good rhythm. But the rhythm of good writing is not the rhythm of music, or the meter of verse. It’s not so regular. If it were, it wouldn’t be good, because the rhythm of good writing has to match the ideas in it, and ideas have all kinds of different shapes. Sometimes they’re simple and you just state them. But other times they’re more subtle, and you need longer, more complicated sentences to tease out all the implications.

An essay is a cleaned up train of thought, in the same way dialogue is cleaned up conversation, and a train of thought has a natural rhythm. So when an essay sounds good, it’s not merely because it has a pleasing rhythm, but because it has its natural one. Which means you can use getting the rhythm right as a heuristic for getting the ideas right. And not just in principle: good writers do both simultaneously as a matter of course. Often I don’t even distinguish between the two problems. I just think Ugh, this doesn’t sound right; what do I mean to say here? [3](#fn3)

The sound of writing turns out to be more like the shape of a plane than the color of a car. If it looks good, as Kelly Johnson used to say, it will fly well.

This is only true of writing that’s used to develop ideas, though. It doesn’t apply when you have ideas in some other way and then write about them afterward – for example, if you build something, or conduct an experiment, and then write a paper about it. In such cases the ideas often live more in the work than the writing, so the writing can be bad even though the ideas are good. The writing in textbooks and popular surveys can be bad for the same reason: the author isn’t developing the ideas, merely describing other people’s. It’s only when you’re writing to develop ideas that there’s such a close connection between the two senses of doing it well.

Ok, many people will be thinking, this seems plausible so far, but what about liars? Is it not notoriously possible for a smooth-tongued liar to write something beautiful that’s completely false?

It is, of course. But not without method acting. The way to write something beautiful and false is to begin by making yourself almost believe it. So just like someone writing something beautiful and true, you’re presenting a perfectly-formed train of thought. The difference is the point where it attaches to the world. You’re saying something that would be true if certain false premises were. If for some bizarre reason the number of jobs in a country were fixed, then immigrants really would be taking our jobs.

So it’s not quite right to say that better sounding writing is more likely to be true. Better sounding writing is more likely to be internally consistent. If the writer is honest, internal consistency and truth converge.

But while we can’t safely conclude that beautiful writing is true, it’s usually safe to conclude the converse: something that seems clumsily written will usually have gotten the ideas wrong too.

Indeed, the two senses of good writing are more like two ends of the same thing. The connection between them is not a rigid one; the goodness of good writing is not a rod but a rope, with multiple overlapping connections running through it. But it’s hard to move one end without moving the other. It’s hard to be right without sounding right.
