# Math Academy Success Stories

我不断看到 Math Academy 学生的 success stories。到目前为止，这样的故事已经不少了，所以我想，干脆翻翻我的 bookmarks，把它们整理成一篇 post。

这些故事 100% organic——我们没有付钱让任何人发布，也从未要求这些学生中的任何一位发布自己的进展。截至目前，Math Academy 在付费 posts 上的总支出为 $0。

这是一份持续更新的 document，离完整清单还差得很远——这里只收录了我亲自看到并 bookmarked 的故事。我会继续补充。

## 它的起步——在我们最初的 school program 中，8th graders 通过 AP Calc BC；疫情期间部署 fully automated learning system 后，我们的 learning outcomes 飙升；最初校内 program 之外的 students 使用该 system，也取得了同样惊人的 results

Math Academy 的 [original school program](https://www.mathacademy.us/) 是 Pasadena 的一个高度加速的 6-12th grade math program：6th graders 从 Prealgebra 开始，到 8th grade 时学完全部 high school math（Algebra 1、Geometry、Algebra 2、Precalculus）；然后在 8th grade 学习 AP Calculus BC 并参加 AP exam；到了 high school，则学习完整的 undergraduate math curriculum。

它是 [美国加速程度最高的 math program](https://www.washingtonpost.com/local/education/ap-calculus-eighth-graders/2021/07/30/2e2bfd5c-effb-11eb-bf80-e3877d9c5f06_story.html)，关于它还有许多其他 [news articles](https://mathacademy.us/press)，也有不少直接来自当事人的信息，包括一份 [2014-20 events summary](https://mathacademy.com/about-us)（从 Sandy 和 Jason 的视角出发）、一份 [2019-23 events summary](https://justinmath.com/bio/#2018-23)（从我的视角出发，重点讲述在 school program 中教学，以及落实 algorithms、将其转变为 fully automated system），以及我在 Anna Stokke 的 [Chalk and Talk Podcast #42](https://justinmath.com/chalk-and-talk-podcast-42) 中做的另一份 2014-20 events summary（我会在下面贴出相关片段）：

*   _“说回这些 8th graders 参加 AP Calc BC 的故事。我们最初是一个由 Jason 和 Sandy Roberts 创办的 nonprofit school program。他们的一个孩子 Colby 加入了四年级 math field day team，他的父母则在辅导这个 team。他们的孩子和朋友们都非常热衷于学习 math，所以他们参加了常规的四年级 field day 活动。但孩子们实在太兴奋了，不想只停留在四年级。他们经常问 Jason 和 Sandy 的一个问题是：‘math 的最高 level 是什么？’

    Jason 和 Sandy 不得不回答：‘嗯，它可以学得非常非常深入，不过就你们而言，我们姑且说是 calculus，因为 honors track 上的 high school seniors 学的就是这个。’接下来的问题当然是：‘我们什么时候能学？现在能学吗？明天能学 calculus 吗？’他们对此简直兴奋极了。

    Jason 和 Sandy 一直在教这些孩子 advanced math，甚至教到了五年级。他们学了不少 high school math，进展到可以开始学 calculus 的程度。后来事情一步步发展，最终变成了正式的 school program，不再只是抽离式课程，而成为每天都有的 Math Academy class。之后几年还有其他 cohorts 加入。

    后来，我们开始接收六年级学生，他们的 arithmetic 基础很扎实。他们可能知道 variable 是什么，但并不真正懂得如何 solve equations 或其他类似内容。他们大致处于初级 pre-algebra 水平。我们会逐步提升他们的水平，在接下来的两年——六年级和七年级——教完他们全部 high school math：Pre-algebra、algebra one、geometry、algebra two 和 pre-calculus。到了八年级，他们就能准备好学 calculus。

    接着，他们会参加 AP Calculus BC exam。后来，大多数在八年级参加 AP Calc BC exam 的学生都通过了，其中大多数通过者在 exam 上拿到了满分 5 分。

    有几件事我应该说明一下：这些学生不是 national talent search students。

    学生的选拔方式是：他们在 middle school math placement exam 中取得第 90 percentile 或更高的成绩。这项 exam 通常在二月或三月由学区所有五年级学生参加。随后，他们会获邀加入这个 program。这是一项七年级 math skills test，因此要求的 skill level 相当高，但它并不是为识别 math aptitude 而设计的。

    这个 program 也在 Pasadena Unified School District，其中约三分之二的学生符合联邦 free and reduced lunch program 的资格；相比之下，加州所有 K-12 学生中约有 44 percent 在 private schools 接受教育，而加州的平均比例是 11%。

    这并不是一群特别有天赋的学生，也不是从全国顶尖学生中偏选出来的一群。想象一所普通 school 和普通 honors class 里的孩子就好。他们的学习进度完全可以比目前快得多、快得多。

    Jason 和 Sandy 教学时，所有这些工作都是手动完成的，但效果已经很好。学生开始使用 Math Academy system 后，results 甚至更好了。Jason 厌倦了孩子们说‘我忘了做 homework’、‘哦，我忘带 pencil 了’之类的借口，也厌倦了他们拿各种理由不做作业。于是他干脆搭建了一个 system，由他挑选要做的 problems，孩子们只需在家 log in，然后在线完成这些 problems。

    这个 system 会自动为 problems 打分，跟踪所有孩子的 stats、全班的 accuracy 以及不同 topics。随着时间推移，它逐渐演变成一个承担越来越多教学工作的 system。

    2019 年夏天，Jason 把我拉进来，让我把这个 system 做成 fully automated platform，能够真正为 students 选择 learning tasks。于是我们构建了这个 automated task selection algorithm，并持续改进。到 2020 年疫情来袭时，摆在我们面前的大问题是：如何维持手动 instruction 所达到的效率水平？

    答案是：‘嗯，我们有一个还没完全成熟的 task selection algorithm。干脆趁着夏天把它全部部署到位，让整个 school program 都用上它。’我们确实这么做了。把他们放到这个 system 上后，我们的 AP Calc BC scores 就这样飙升了。”_

我们确实全力投入 automated system——它让我们能够最大限度地利用这些能够提升 learning 的 practice techniques，为每位学生选择符合其自身 knowledge profile 的 individualized learning tasks，让每位学生始终都在做最能推动其 learning 进展的确切 tasks。以下摘自我的 [2019-23 events summary](https://justinmath.com/bio/#2018-23)：

*   _2019 年夏天，我开始参与 Math Academy software 的核心开发。当时，这个 software 已经作为一种 tool 存在了好几年，Math Academy instructors 用它来创建和评分 assignments——他们会从 database 中手动挑选 problems（其中包含大量由 Math Academy 的 PhD mathematicians 团队撰写的内容），students 会在线完成 homework problems，而 software 会自动为 assignments 打分并跟踪每位学生的 grades。但要手动挑选一组 problems，既覆盖每天课堂所教的多个 topics，又能对先前学过的 topics 提供 spaced review，非常耗时。

    显然，尽管 students 学到了大量 math，但给班上每位学生布置相同的 assignment，仍然让大量 learning efficiency 无法实现：即使在同一个 class 中，不同 students 的 strengths 和 weaknesses 各不相同，已经准备好学习的 topics 不同，要在不同 topics 上达到足够的 mastery level 所需的 practice 量也不同。给每位学生布置相同的 assignment，几乎必然导致每个学生浪费大量时间，感到无聊或迷茫——无论哪种情况，他们都没有真正 learning。要解决这个问题，不同 students 就需要在不同时间学习不同 topics，在这些 topics 上进行不同量的 practice（包括 review），而且每位学生的 learning plan 都需要根据其个人表现持续调整。

    对 fully individualized learning 的需求，以及其他需求（例如 financial sustainability，以及持续维护多个 classes / teachers / schools 之间 accountability 和 standards 的努力），使 Jason 和 Sandy 意识到，唯一可行的前进方向是让这个 system 成为一个 fully-automated standalone online learning platform，面向公众商业化提供。Jason 了解我的背景后，请我开发一个 algorithm，自动分配 personalized learning tasks（根据每位学生各自的 knowledge profile 进行个性化），同时运用 mastery learning、spaced repetition 和 interleaving 等有效 learning techniques。

    到夏末时，我们有了一个 implementation——尽管它还很粗糙、脆弱，而且在许多方面并不完整，但已经足以找一名真实学生进行测试。在 2019-20 school year，我们从一位 independent learner 开始，她之前就读于 Math Academy，后来搬到了另一个州。她只使用这个 system 学习 AP Calculus BC（也就是说，没有外部帮助，也没有人为干预），并在 AP exam 中取得了 5 分。这证明了一个 concept：我们可以将 software 从手动创建 assignments 的 tool 升级为 fully-automated adaptive learning system，让 independent learners 无需任何人为干预也能使用。

    一个重要的转折点出现在 2020 年春季，COVID-19 pandemic 开始之际。我搬去和 Jason 及其家人一起隔离，由此在他家的客厅里临时搭建出一个 startup incubator。我们每天醒着的时间都在工作，常常工作到深夜，周末也不例外；到夏末时，我们已经准备好让整所 school 的 classes 都使用 automated system。在 2020-21 school year 和 COVID-19 pandemic 期间，automated system 证明比传统 remote instruction 有效得多；到 2021 年春季，Math Academy 几乎所有 school classes 都已在使用它（其中有几门课我亲自管理，作为临时的 usability lab，一直持续到 2023 年）。

    在2021-22学年，即使学校已经恢复线下教学，我们也达到了这样的水平：system 的效率是覆盖相同 material 的传统线下 classes 的4倍。一些看似不可能的事情开始发生，例如一些积极性很高的6th graders（他们从 Prealgebra 的中途开始）在一个学年内完成了通常属于高中阶段的全部 math（Algebra I、Geometry、Algebra II、Precalculus），并开始学习 AP Calculus BC。Math Academy 的 AP Calculus BC exam scores 上升了，大多数学生通过了 exam，而通过的学生中大多数取得了可能的最高分（5 out of 5）。另有四名学生通过我们的 system 学习 AP Calculus BC，他们与我们的 Pasadena school program 无关，完全独立于 classroom，其中除一人外，所有人都在 AP exam 中取得了满分5分（另一人得了4分）。

    最后，在2022-23学年，我们面向全世界开放了 [mathacademy.com](https://mathacademy.com/) 商业服务，获得了 accreditation，商业用户增长到数百名，并实现了 operating break-even。更多商业用户中的学生也在 AP Calculus BC exam 中取得了满分，包括一名 [elementary schooler](https://easyreadernews.com/redondo-11-year-old-tops-college-board-ap-calculus/)。_

是的，你没看错。我们有一名 elementary schooler 在 AP Calculus BC exam 中得了5分。

## 更多孩子在 standardized tests 中表现出色——并建立自信，不只是那些起步时就领先的孩子

AP Calc BC 的 stories 会成为头条，因为它们最引人注目，但实际上只是一个范围广得多的 pattern 的末尾。许多孩子正在使用 Math Academy 大幅超越年级水平，并在 standardized tests 中表现出色——SAT、MAP，或学校给他们安排的任何考试。

话虽如此，Math Academy 并不只是面向那些已经领先的孩子。许多家庭用它来追赶进度、建立自信，并达到（或完成）年级水平——而其中许多孩子站稳脚跟后，最终会远远超越年级水平。你可以用它从低于年级水平提升到年级水平，然后继续大幅超越。

## 加速的意义何在？——往届学生正在拓展自己感兴趣领域的边界

每当这类 success stories 传开时，总有人会以某种方式提出同一个问题：

*   _“太棒了。不过，你担心他们会感到无聊吗？既然他们现在已经学完了所有高中 math，在学校还能做什么？”_

但问题与其说是“他们能做什么？”，不如说是“他们有什么不能做？！”

在我们的 original program 中，孩子们学完 calc 后，马上就继续学 undergrad math，然后做一些很棒的 projects，比如复现 artificial intelligence 领域的 academic research papers：

*   **[Math Academy 的 Eurisko Sequence：五年之后——美国最先进高中 math/CS track 中涌现的 student outcomes](https://justinmath.com/math-academys-eurisko-sequence-5-years-later/)**
    2025 年 3 月，约 2700 字 • Eurisko 在 2020 至 2023 年运行期间，是美国最先进的高中 math/CS track。其最终让高中生完成 masters/PhD-level coursework（复现 artificial intelligence 领域的 academic research papers，并用 Python 从头构建所有内容）。现在下结论还为时尚早，第一届学生甚至还没有从大学毕业，但在 college admissions、accelerated graduate degrees、research publications 和 science fairs 等方面，已经出现了一些令人惊叹的 student outcomes。
*   **[我在教授美国最先进的高中 math/CS Sequence 时写的（免费）教科书](https://justinmath.com/textbook-for-the-most-advanced-high-school-math-cs-sequence-in-the-usa/)**
    2025 年 12 月，约 300 字 • 这本书逐步引导高中生完成 masters/PhD-level coursework：复现 artificial intelligence 领域的 academic research papers，并用 Python 从头构建所有内容。一位往届学生在进行研究前刚学完这本书；该研究后来赢得 Regeneron Science Talent Search 的第 1 名（250,000 美元），他还获 NASA 负责人亲自招募（签约奖励是乘坐战斗机），并以唯一作者身份在 The Astronomical Journal 发表了研究成果。

当然，顺理成章的后续问题是：“好吧，但完成 masters/PhD level coursework、从头复现 ML/AI research papers 之后，还能做什么？”答案是：拓展自己感兴趣领域的边界。

例如，往届学生 Matteo Paz 凭借出众的 math/coding chops，在高中时开展研究，“揭示了太空中 150 万个此前未知的 object，拓展了 NASA mission 的潜力”（这不是夸张说法，而是直接引用自 [Caltech 网站](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/exploring-space-with-ai)）。他以唯一作者身份在 The Astronomical Journal 发表了研究成果，并赢得 Regeneron Science Talent Search 的第 1 名（250,000 美元）。他还获 NASA 负责人亲自招募，签约奖励是乘坐战斗机。

## 这一切怎么可能？——Nerd Levels 1-3

与几乎所有其他 math learning program 不同，我们以 learning efficiency 为优化目标，也就是说，学生在 system 上进行的每一项操作，都旨在最大限度地提高他们在使用 system 的单位时间内学会并保留的 knowledge。我们通过向学生提供符合其自身 knowledge profile 和 pace of learning 的 individualized learning tasks 来实现这一点。

我们充分利用了大量关于 learning 的 cognitive science 研究，而这些研究直到现在才得以公之于众。[The Math Academy Way](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf) 以详尽的细节解释了这一切如何实现。以下是一些入门资料，大致按深入程度排列：

_Nerd Level 1:_ [为什么不直接从 textbook、MIT OpenCourseWare、Khan Academy 等资源中学习？](https://justinmath.com/why-not-just-learn-from-a-textbook)——我自己也从这类 resources 中学习过，虽然进步很大，但以我投入的 learning effort 而言，如果能更高效地利用时间，本可以走得更远。这正是 Math Academy 解决的问题。

_Nerd Level 2:_ 我们的 [pedagogy](https://mathacademy.com/pedagogy) 概览、我们的 [AI system](https://mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works) 如何运行（它是一个“expert system”，是对老派 AI 的致敬），以及对我们的 [spaced repetition system](https://justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures) 的深入介绍。

_Nerd Level 3:_ [The Math Academy Way](https://justinmath.com/books/#the-math-academy-way)，这是我们正在编写的 400 多页草稿书，汇编并综合了数百篇 scientific papers 中的 evidence，以回答以下问题：

1.  有哪些 techniques 可以最大限度地促进 student learning 和 talent development，尤其是在 math 领域？
2.  这些 techniques 为什么如此有效？如果它们确实如此有效，为什么又经常缺席于 traditional classrooms？
3.  Math Academy 如何运用这些 techniques？

> ## How It Started -- 8th graders passing AP Calc BC in our original school program; our learning outcomes skyrocketed during the pandemic when we deployed our fully automated learning system; students outside our original in-school program used the system and achieved similarly incredible results
>
> Math Academy’s [original school program](https://www.mathacademy.us/) is a highly accelerated 6-12th grade math program in Pasadena where 6th graders start in Prealgebra, and then learn the entirety of high school math (Algebra 1, Geometry, Algebra 2, Precalculus) by 8th grade, and then in 8th grade they learn AP Calculus BC and take the AP exam, and then in high school they study a full undergraduate math curriculum.
>
> It’s the [most accelerated math program in the USA](https://www.washingtonpost.com/local/education/ap-calculus-eighth-graders/2021/07/30/2e2bfd5c-effb-11eb-bf80-e3877d9c5f06_story.html), there have been plenty of other [news articles](https://mathacademy.us/press) written about it, and there’s plenty of other information straight from the horse’s mouth including a [summary of events 2014-20](https://mathacademy.com/about-us) (from Sandy and Jason’s perspective), a [summary of events 2019-23](https://justinmath.com/bio/#2018-23) (from my perspective, with a focus on teaching in the school program and getting the algorithms in place to turn it into a fully automated system), and another summary of events 2014-20 that I gave on Anna Stokke’s [Chalk and Talk Podcast #42](https://justinmath.com/chalk-and-talk-podcast-42) (I’ll paste the relevant snippet below):
>
> *   _"So back to this eighth graders taking AP Calc BC story. We originally started as a nonprofit school program founded by Jason and Sandy Roberts. One of their kids, Colby, was on the fourth-grade math field day team, and his parents were coaching that team. Their kid and his friends were all really excited about learning math, so they did the standard fourth-grade field day stuff. But the kids were so excited that they didn’t want to just stop at fourth grade. Something they would often ask Jason and Sandy was, “What’s the highest level of math?”
>
>     Jason and Sandy would have to say, “Well, it goes really, really high, but for your purposes, let’s just say it’s calculus, because that’s what seniors in high school take if they are on the honors track.” And the next question was, of course, “When do we get to learn it? Can we learn it now? Can we learn calculus tomorrow?” They were just so excited about it.
>
>     Jason and Sandy were teaching a bunch of these kids advanced math, even through fifth grade. They got up through a bunch of high school math and to the point where they could start learning calculus. One thing led to another, and this turned into an official school program that was not just a pullout class but became a daily Math Academy class. There were other cohorts that came in following years.
>
>     What this turned into was that we would get students in sixth grade who were solid on their arithmetic. They might know what a variable is, but they didn’t really know how to solve equations or anything. They were kind of at an early pre-algebra level. We would scaffold them up, teach them all of high school math within the next two years -- sixth and seventh grade. Pre-algebra, algebra one, geometry, algebra two, and pre-calculus. In eighth grade, they’d be ready to take calculus.
>
>     Then, they would take the AP Calculus BC exam. We got to the point where most of the students who took the AP Calc BC exam in eighth grade passed, and most who passed got a perfect five out of five on the exam.
>
>     A couple of things I should say, these are not national talent search students.
>
>     How the kids were selected was that they scored at or above the 90th percentile on a middle school math placement exam, which is typically taken by all fifth graders in the district around February or March. They were then invited to join the program. It's a seventh-grade math skills test, so it provides a somewhat high skill level, but it's not designed to identify math aptitude.
>
>     This is also in the Pasadena Unified School District, where about two-thirds of the student population qualifies for the federal free and reduced lunch program, and about 44 percent of all K-12 students are educated in private schools, compared to the California average of 11%.
>
>     This is not a particularly talented group of students. It's not a biased group of the top students in the nation. Just think of a standard school and kids in the standard honors class. They can be accelerated way, way, way higher than they currently are.
>
>     When Jason and Sandy were teaching, they were doing this all manually and achieving very good results. But these results got even better once students started working on the Math Academy system. Jason got tired of the kids saying, “I forgot to do my homework,” or “Oh, I forgot a pencil,” or all these excuses for not doing work. So, he just built a system where he could pick problems for them to do, and then all they had to do was log in at home and do the problems online.
>
>     It would automatically grade the problems and keep track of all the kids’ stats, keep track of the class accuracy, and various topics. Over time, this evolved into a system that did more and more of the teaching work.
>
>     In the summer of 2019, that’s when Jason pulled me in to make this system a fully automated platform that would actually select learning tasks for students. So, we built this automated task selection algorithm and continued refining it. By the time the pandemic hit in 2020, the big question was how to maintain this level of efficiency from manual instruction.
>
>     The answer was, “Well, we have this halfway baked task selection algorithm. Let’s just get it all in place over the summer and put the whole school program on it.” And that’s what we did. That’s how our AP Calc BC scores skyrocketed, from putting them on the system."_
>
> We really leaned into the automated system – it allowed us to leverage these learning-enhancing practice techniques to the fullest extent, selecting individualized learning tasks tailored to each student’s own knowledge profile, so that every student would always be working on the exact tasks that would move the needle most on their learning. From my [summary of events 2019-23](https://justinmath.com/bio/#2018-23):
>
> *   _I got involved at the core of Math Academy's software during the summer of 2019. At that time, the software had existed for several years as a tool that Math Academy instructors used to create and grade assignments -- they would manually select problems from the database (which contained a mountain of content written by Math Academy's team of PhD mathematicians), students would complete the problems online for homework, and the software would automatically grade the assignments and keep track of each student's grades. But it was very time-consuming to manually choose a mix of problems that covered the multiple topics taught during class each day and also provided spaced review on previously-learned topics.
>
>     It was clear that while students were learning an incredible amount of math, giving the same assignment to each student in a class still left a lot of learning efficiency unrealized: even within a single class, different students had different strengths and weaknesses, had different sets of topics that they were ready to learn, and needed different amounts of practice on different topics to reach a sufficient level of mastery. Giving each student the same assignment virtually guaranteed that every student wasted lots of time being bored or lost -- either way, not actually learning. To solve this problem, different students would need to learn different topics at different times, and get different amounts of practice (including review) on those topics, and each student's learning plan would need to continually adapt to their individual performance.
>
>     The need for fully individualized learning, as well as other needs (e.g., financial sustainability and the constant effort to maintain accountability & standards across multiple classes / teachers / schools) led Jason and Sandy to the realization that the only way forward was for the system to become a fully-automated standalone online learning platform, commercially available to the general public. Aware of my background, Jason asked me to develop an algorithm that would automatically assign personalized learning tasks (personalized to each student's individual knowledge profile) while leveraging effective learning techniques like mastery learning, spaced repetition, and interleaving.
>
>     By the end of summer the we had an implementation that -- despite being very rough, brittle, and in many ways incomplete -- was good enough to test out with a real student. During the 2019-20 school year, we started out with a single independent learner, a student who was previously in Math Academy and had moved to another state. She learned AP Calculus BC using only the system (i.e. no external help, no human intervention) and got a 5 on the AP exam. This proved the concept that we could upgrade the software from a manual assignment creation tool to a fully-automated adaptive learning system supporting independent learners without any human intervention.
>
>     A major transition point happened in Spring 2020, with the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. I moved in with Jason and his family to quarantine, which led to a makeshift startup incubator experience run out of his living room. We worked every waking hour, well into the night, every day including weekends, so that by the end of summer we were ready to run entire school classes on the automated system. During the 2020-21 school year and COVID-19 pandemic, the automated system proved to be significantly more effective than traditional remote instruction, and by spring 2021 nearly all of Math Academy's school classes were running on it (a couple of which I personally managed as a makeshift usability lab until 2023).
>
>     During the 2021-22 school year, even after school was back in person, we reached the point that the system was 4x as efficient as traditional in-person classes covering the same material. Seemingly impossible things started happening like some highly motivated 6th graders (who started midway through Prealgebra) completing all of what is typically high school math (Algebra I, Geometry, Algebra II, Precalculus) and starting AP Calculus BC within a single school year. Math Academy's AP Calculus BC exam scores rose, with most students passing the exam and most students who passed receiving the maximum score possible (5 out of 5). Four other students took AP Calculus BC on our system, unaffiliated with our Pasadena school program, completely independent of a classroom, and all but one of them scored a perfect 5 on the AP exam (the other one received a 4).
>
>     Finally, during the 2022-23 school year, we opened up [mathacademy.com](https://mathacademy.com/) commercially to the world at large, became accredited, grew to hundreds of commercial users, and hit operating break-even. Even more commercial students aced the AP Calculus BC exam, including an [elementary schooler](https://easyreadernews.com/redondo-11-year-old-tops-college-board-ap-calculus/)._
>
> Yes, you read that right. We had an elementary schooler get a 5 on the AP Calculus BC exam.

> ## What's the Point of Acceleration? -- Former students are pushing the boundaries of their fields of interest
>
> Every time one of these success stories goes around, someone asks a version of the same question:
>
> *   _"That's awesome. However, do you fear that they will get bored? What else will they do in school now that they finished all of high school math?"_
>
> But it’s less “what can they do?” and more “what CAN’T they do?!”
>
> In our original program, after kids learned calc, they moved right on to undergrad math, and then awesome projects like reproducing academic research papers in artificial intelligence:
>
> *   **[Math Academy’s Eurisko Sequence, 5 Years Later: Student Outcomes Emerging From the Most Advanced High School Math/CS Track in the USA](https://justinmath.com/math-academys-eurisko-sequence-5-years-later/)**
>     2025 Mar, ~2700 words • During its operation from 2020-23, Eurisko was the most advanced high school math/CS track in the USA. It culminated in high school students doing masters/PhD-level coursework (reproducing academic research papers in artificial intelligence, building everything from scratch in Python). It's still early and the first cohort hasn't even graduated from college yet, but there have already been some amazing student outcomes in terms of college admissions, accelerated graduate degrees, research publications, and science fairs.
> *   **[The (Free) Textbook I Wrote While Teaching The Most Advanced High School Math/CS Sequence In The USA](https://justinmath.com/textbook-for-the-most-advanced-high-school-math-cs-sequence-in-the-usa/)**
>     2025 Dec, ~300 words • It scaffolded high school students up to doing masters/PhD-level coursework: reproducing academic research papers in artificial intelligence, building everything from scratch in Python. A former student worked through it right before conducting research that won 1st place (\$250,000) in the Regeneron Science Talent Search, getting personally recruited by the head of NASA (with a fighter jet ride as a signing bonus), and publishing his results, solo-author, in The Astronomical Journal.
>
> Of course, the natural follow-up question is “okay, but what comes after masters/PhD level coursework, reproducing ML/AI research papers from scratch” – and the answer is: pushing the boundaries of their fields of interest.
>
> For instance, a former student, Matteo Paz, leveraged his outsized math/coding chops to – as a high schooler – conduct research that “revealed 1.5 million previously unknown objects in space, broadened the potential of a NASA mission” (not hyperbole, a direct quote from [Caltech’s website](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/exploring-space-with-ai)). He published his results solo-author in The Astronomical Journal and won 1st place (\$250,000) in the Regeneron Science Talent Search. He also got personally recruited by the head of NASA, with a fighter jet ride as a signing bonus.

> ## How is All This Even Possible? -- Nerd Levels 1-3
>
> Unlike virtually every other math learning program out there, we optimize for learning efficiency, meaning that every action a student takes on the system is intended to maximize the amount of knowledge they learn and retain per unit time they spend working on the system. We accomplish this by providing students with individualized learning tasks matched to their own knowledge profile and pace of learning.
>
> We leverage tons of research into the cognitive science of learning that hasn’t seen the light of day until now. [The Math Academy Way](https://www.justinmath.com/files/the-math-academy-way.pdf) explains in forensic detail how all this is possible. Here are some entrypoints, roughly in order of depth:
>
> _Nerd Level 1:_ [Why Not Just Learn From a Textbook, MIT OpenCourseWare, Khan Academy, etc.?](https://justinmath.com/why-not-just-learn-from-a-textbook) – I learned from those kinds of resources myself, and while I came a long way, for the amount of effort I put into learning, I could have gone a lot further if my time were used more efficiently. That’s the problem that Math Academy solves.
>
> _Nerd Level 2:_ Overview of our [pedagogy](https://mathacademy.com/pedagogy), how our [AI system](https://mathacademy.com/how-our-ai-works) works (it’s an “expert system,” throwback to old-school AI) and a deep dive into our [spaced repetition system](https://justinmath.com/individualized-spaced-repetition-in-hierarchical-knowledge-structures).
>
> _Nerd Level 3:_ [The Math Academy Way](https://justinmath.com/books/#the-math-academy-way), our 400+ page working draft book that compiles and synthesizes evidence from hundreds of scientific papers to answer the following questions:
>
> 1.  What techniques exist to maximize student learning and talent development, particularly in the context of math?
> 2.  Why are these techniques so impactful, and if they are indeed so impactful, then why are they so often absent from traditional classrooms?
> 3.  How does Math Academy leverage these techniques?
>
> The PDF is freely available, so you could probably get some good mileage out of dumping it into an LLM and asking questions.
>
