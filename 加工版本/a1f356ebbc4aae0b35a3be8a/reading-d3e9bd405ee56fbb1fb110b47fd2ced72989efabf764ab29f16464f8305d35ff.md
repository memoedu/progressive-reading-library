# Homeopathy（顺势疗法）

---

## 正文

我最近想知道 Soviet 的 [healthcare system worked](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/03/29/the-soviet-union-healthcare/) 得如何。起初，根据对 USSR 的大致了解，我认为它不会 work 得很好。在做过 literature review 后，我 confirmed 了确实如此。

但如果没有做过那次 literature review，就宣称它糟糕透顶，这样做算是 epistemically responsible 吗？大概算，但不能有太高的 confidence。在那次 review 之前，我只有自己的 opinion，其依据是关于 central planning 如何 works 的 background knowledge。也许我在这个具体 issue 上是 wrong。这不会是我第一次根据 background knowledge 说某件事确实如此，最后却发现自己是 wrong。因此，人们不应该过于坚定地 hold 那些在 literature review 之前形成的 opinions。

那么，我们是否必须亲自做 literature reviews，才能 believe 任何东西？那就太过头了。想象一下，你不得不把一生都花在阅读关于 astrology、homeopathy、conspiracy theories、alchemy、shamanism 或现存数千种 religions 的 primary、secondary 和 journal sources 上。做这一切，只是为了不 believe 或者 criticise 那些东西。你就没有时间做其他事情了。

另一个办法是看看那个 issue 上是否存在 expert consensus：如果有一大群人 studied 过这个 issue，并且得出了 conclusion，那么这就算是支持事情确实如此的 evidence。这种 evidence 甚至可能比你自己的 peer review 更 strong：如果你 review 某个 field 的 literature，却得出了与那个 field 的 experts 相反的 conclusions，你就必须找出这种 discrepancy 的原因。如果找不到，你大概就得坚持 consensus opinion。

## Homeopathy（顺势疗法）

关于 homeopathy，有一些 papers 在 debunking 它（[NHMRC, 2015](http://www.nhmrc.gov.au/_files_nhmrc/publications/attachments/cam02a_information_paper.pdf)、[Hahn 2013](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/24200828)、[Shang et al. 2005](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0140673605671772?np=y) 或 [Ernst 2002](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1365-2125.2002.01699.x/full)），但也有一些 published 在 journals 上的 papers 说它 works（[Mathie, 2014](http://systematicreviewsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/2046-4053-3-142)）。在这种情况下，合理的做法是 a) 采纳最新的 findings b) 遵循 consensus，以及 c) 看看 pro-homeopathy findings 是否受到过 criticised，或者 anti-homeopathy papers 是否得到了 homeopaths 的 credible answer。

因此，我们可以去看 cite 那篇文章的 papers，读一读 published 在 journal Homeopathy 上的 [Mathie, 2015](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1475491615000302)，而这本 journal 恰好被 indexed [在 JCR 中](http://about.jcr.incites.thomsonreuters.com/full-titles-2015.pdf)。他说，在 reviewing literature 之后，发现支持 homeopathy 的 positive effects 的 evidence 是 weak 的。NHMRC study 也曾得到 homeopaths 的 [replied to](http://www.britishhomeopathic.org/bha-blog/australian-report-misrepresents-clinical-research-evidence-in-homeopathy/)，他们指出了 literature 本身的内容。

至于 mechanism（对某种 substance 进行 diluting repeatedly），homeopaths 也有自己的 [papers](http://www.homeopathy-soh.org/research/branches-homeopathy-research/dilute-medicines)。所以，当你说“但是，对某种东西进行 diluting 会使它 vanish，到最后就只是 water”时，你得到的回答会是：“当然，这听起来 plausible，但事实证明并非如此。这里有它确实 work 的 evidence。”没想到吧？你以为 homeopaths 没有 cutting edge research 为自己撑腰？而且，其中许多还没有得到其他 scientists 的 replied to。不过，可以看[这里](http://scienceblogs.com/insolence/2012/12/17/just-how-stupid-do-homeopaths-think-we-are/)的一次此类 reply。关于它为何 physically implausible，请看 [Grimes (2012)](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2042-7166.2012.01162.x/abstract)

**我认为 homeopathy 并不 work，而 [medical scientists 的 consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeopathy#Evidence_and_efficacy) 也是如此。他们为使它 work 而 propose 的 mechanism 并不符合 physical sense。**要让我 believe 它 works，他们就需要给我非常 strong 的 evidence，证明它 works。既然 critics 说它不 work，而 homeopaths 自己也说他们的 evidence 充其量是 weak 的，那么可以得出的 plausible conclusion 就是：它确实不 work。也许将来会 shown 它 works，但这种可能性很小。

那么，我们应该认为 homeopathy 是 pseudoscientific 吗？这是个有趣的问题。看起来，homeopaths 正在做 science，试图 prove homeopathy works。他们在做 RCTs、publishing papers、answering criticisms，并尝试 study water 的 properties，看看他们 proposed 的 mechanism 可能如何 work。他们的 methodology 很可能是 flawed，但其他 fields 的许多 papers 的 methodology 也是如此。那篇 wiki article 对他们为什么有时可能 finding positive results 提供了一些 explanations。[这里](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience)有一些 pseudoscience 的 definitions。首先，Homeopathy 是 falsifiable，所以在 Karl Popper 看来，homeopathy 会是一门 science。Homeopathy 也 made predictions，而据其 followers 所说，这些 predictions 后来成为 true（根据他们的 research）：他们据称找到了支持 water 中存在 mechanisms 的 evidence。

我不想深入他们的 literature，去 assess 它并 check 是否存在某些 pseudoscience 的 symptoms（如那篇 wiki article 中所解释的），因为我觉得这毫无价值。

总之，我们有：

-   支持 homeopathy：一些支持其 principles 和 individual illnesses 的 weak meta-analytical evidence，以及一个小群体（homeopaths）的 endorsement
-   反对 homeopathy：对 laws of physics 的 Violation、medical scientists 的 consensus，以及若干 finding 没有 effects 的 meta-analysis。支持 homeopathy 的 evidence 大多来自 homeopaths。

通过这个 quick assessment，即使在庞大的 literature 中只读过不超过一两篇 papers，我也可以 safely dismiss homeopathy，而且我认为，这种方式连 homeopaths 自己也能理解，而不至于因为我的 rejection 对我生气。任何处在我的位置、拥有我所掌握的 information 的人，都不得不得出这个 conclusion。Homeopaths 也许认为，将来他们会找到支持它的更 strong 的 evidence，因此他们会继续尝试。但在那之前，他们必须理解：对于普通人，或者像我这样多做了一点功课的人来说，把 homeopathy 描述为 wrong 是 accurate 的。视你愿意给予多大程度的 charitable 理解而定，你也可以称它为 pseudoscience。我没有对 pseudoscience 这个 concept 做过 study。如果某个东西不是 science，那又如何？我们真的能为哪些是 science、哪些不是 science 找到严格的 criteria 吗？我更关心某个东西究竟是 right 还是 wrong。

## 在 information 很少时安全地 dismiss 和 accept 事物的一些 heuristics

从 simple procedures 开始；如果你认为自己的 belief 需要更多 confidence，再采用更耗时的 procedures。

1.  看看它是否 makes sense。这个办法通常在大多数时候都管用。这包括用你的 background beliefs 来 checking 一个 issue；希望这些 background beliefs 中包含对 laws of physics 的理解。
2.  看看关于那个 issue 的 consensus 是什么
    1.  这可以通过查找重要 institutions 的 statements 来做到
    2.  或者通过阅读 meta-analysis
3.  阅读针对 consensus positions 的 critiques。阅读针对那些 critiques 的 critiques。再阅读针对那些 critiques 的 critiques 的 critiques，如此继续，直到当年。Google Scholar 可以帮助你做到这一点。
4.  进行 full-scale literature review，阅读针对这个 issue 撰写的大多数 papers
5.  最后，自己 assess 这些 literature，寻找 methodological flaws。

## 理解人们为什么 disagree with 你

在寻找有关 homeopathy 的资料后，我觉得很容易理解为什么有人 believe in 它。有些人面对这个事实，会在心里扶额，或对这些人生气，或侮辱他们（“你怎么会这么蠢！它显然不 work！”）。我的反应则是理解 homeopathy 的 believer 的 beliefs 来自哪里。我 infer，believe in 它的人有两类：购买 homeopathy 的普通人，以及 homeopaths 自己。

对于第一类人，他们之所以开始 believe in 它，可能是因为周围有人试过，并说它 work；也可能他们在网上寻找 information，找到了 homeopathic websites，而这些网站告诉他们它 worked。或者，他们只是试了试，随后 got better，却不知道 [post hoc fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_hoc_ergo_propter_hoc)。

对于第二类人，他们读过 literature，也知道情况如何。他们已经走到了我的 heuristics 的 scale 中的 levels 4 或 5，却仍然 believe in homeopathy。这可能意味着，他们一开始就抱有 homeopathy worked 的 strong 或 moderately strong belief，而此后并没有找到 sufficient proof 来 abandon 那个 belief。他们不应该继续仅仅根据新的 evidence 来 adjusting 自己的 beliefs，而应该 review 自己最初的 opinion 究竟是否 correct。Studies 会不断出现，最终，如果他们诚实，而且 science 指向那个方向，他们就会不再 believing in homeopathy。

如果你想与 homeopaths 交流——我个人觉得这是浪费时间，但你也许不这么认为——务必理解他们，看看他们可能有哪些 evidence 来源，并解释这些 evidence 为什么不 work，而不是责怪他们愚蠢。人们不喜欢被说成愚蠢，而且这样做可能适得其反：这可能让他们更加 believe in homeopathy。

## 结论（Conclusion）

天下没有免费的午餐。Acquiring information 存在 opportunity costs。certainty 与工作量之间存在 tradeoff：为 acquire information 做的工作越多，你就能越 certain。因此，拥有一些 heuristics，让自己能凭少量 information，迅速对某个 belief 的 truth 达到足够高的 confidence levels，是有用的，甚至是必要的。它们是 heuristics，可能会 fail，因此，当新的 evidence 出现，或当你 reassess 自己已经知道的 evidence 时，要做好改变想法的准备。在这篇文章中，我提供了一些用来做到这一点的 heuristics。

一旦某个 belief 已被 dismissed 或 accepted，你可以维持那个 dismissal 或 acceptance；如果愿意，也可以偶尔 check 新的 evidence，看看它是否 support 你最初的 assessment。如果你碰巧持有一个在大多数 reasonable 的人看来是 false 的 belief，那么 burden of proof 就在你身上：你需要给出一个 low-acquisition-cost response，convince 他们，全世界都 wrong，而你才是 right。这基本上就是对 evidence 做一次 level-4/5 review，并且足够 charitable，让你的 critics 感觉自己得到了理解，他们的 criticisms 也得到了回应。

## 原文

I recently wondered how the Soviet [healthcare system worked](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/03/29/the-soviet-union-healthcare/). Initially, I thought, based on broad ideas about the USSR, that it wouldn't work very well. After a literature review, I confirmed that it was the case.

But would it have been epistemically responsible to proclaim that it was awful without having done that literature review? Probably yes, but without much confidence. Before the review, I just had my opinion, based on background knowledge about how central planning works. Perhaps I was wrong on this specific issue. It wouldn't have been the first time when I say that something is the case based on background knowledge, and end up being wrong. That's why one shouldn't hold those pre-literature review opinions too strongly.

So do we need to do literature reviews ourselves in order to believe anything? That would be too much. Imagine having to spend your life reading the primary, secondary, and journal sources for astrology, homeopathy, conspiracy theories, alchemy, shamanism, or the thousands of religions that are around. All that in order to not believe, or criticise those things. You wouldn't have time for doing other things.

Another alternative is to see if there is expert consensus on that issue: if there is a large group of people who have studied the issue, and they have come to a conclusion, that counts as evidence that such a thing is indeed the case. This evidence can be even stronger than your peer review: if you review the literature of a field, and come to conclusions opposite to that of the experts in that field, you have to find the reason for the discrepancy. If you can't, you probably have to stick with the consensus opinion.

## Homeopathy

For homeopathy there are some papers debunking it ([NHMRC, 2015](http://www.nhmrc.gov.au/_files_nhmrc/publications/attachments/cam02a_information_paper.pdf), or [Hahn 2013](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/24200828), or [Shang et al. 2005](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0140673605671772?np=y), or [Ernst 2002](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1046/j.1365-2125.2002.01699.x/full)) but then, there are also papers saying that it works published in  journals ([Mathie, 2014](http://systematicreviewsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/2046-4053-3-142)). In this cases, the reasonable thing to do is a) Go with the latest findings b) Go with the consensus and c) See if the pro-homeopathy findings have been criticised, or if the anti-homeopathy papers have gotten a credible answer from homeopaths.

So we can go to the papers that cite that one and read [Mathie, 2015](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1475491615000302) published in the journal Homeopathy, which happens to be indexed [in the JCR](http://about.jcr.incites.thomsonreuters.com/full-titles-2015.pdf). He says that there is weak evidence for positive effects of homeopathy after reviewing the literature. The NHMRC study was also [replied to](http://www.britishhomeopathic.org/bha-blog/australian-report-misrepresents-clinical-research-evidence-in-homeopathy/) by homeopaths pointing out to the literature itself.

As for the mechanism (diluting repeatedly some substance), homeopaths also have their [papers](http://www.homeopathy-soh.org/research/branches-homeopathy-research/dilute-medicines). So when you say "But diluting something will make it vanish, in the end it's just water" will be answered with "Sure that sounds plausible, but turns out it isn't. Here's  evidence that it does work". Didn't expect that? Thought homeopaths didn't have cutting edge research on their side? And many of them have not been replied to by other scientists. But see [here](http://scienceblogs.com/insolence/2012/12/17/just-how-stupid-do-homeopaths-think-we-are/) for one such reply. See [Grimes (2012)](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.2042-7166.2012.01162.x/abstract) for how it is physically implausible

**I think homeopathy does not work, and the [consensus of medical scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeopathy#Evidence_and_efficacy) thinks the same. The mechanism they propose for it to work does not make physical sense.** For me to believe that it works, they would need to give me very strong evidence that it works. Since the critics say it doesn't work, and the homeopaths themselves say their evidence is, at best, weak, the plausible conclusion to draw is that, indeed, it doesn't work. Perhaps in the future it will be shown that it works, but it is unlikely.

Should we consider homeopathy to be pseudoscientific then? This is an amusing question. Seemingly, homeopaths are doing science to try to prove homeopathy works. They are doing RCTs, publishing papers, answering criticisms, and trying to study the properties of water to see how their proposed mechanism might work. Their methodology is most probably flawed, but so is the methodology of many papers in other fields. In the wiki article there are some explanations for why they may be finding positive results sometimes. [Here](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudoscience)there are some definitions of pseudoscience. Homeopathy is falsifiable, for one, so for Karl Popper, homeopathy would be a science. Homeopathy also made predictions that, according to their followers, came to be true (according to their research): they supposedly found evidence for mechanisms in water.

I don't want to go deep into their literature to assess it and check if some symptoms of pseudoscience (as explained in the wiki article) are present, because I think it would be worthless.

To sum up, we have:

-   For homeopathy: Some weak meta-analytical evidence for the principles and individual illnesses, endorsement from a small group (homeopaths)
-   Against homeopathy: Violation of the laws of physics, the consensus of medical scientists, several meta-analysis finding no effects.Most of the evidence for homeopathy comes from homeopaths.

With this quick assessment, without having read more than one or two papers of a vast literature, I can safely dismiss homeopathy, in a way that I think, homeopaths themselves would understand, instead of getting mad at me for my rejection. Anyone in my position and with the information I have would have to conclude this. Homeopaths may think that in the future they will find stronger evidence for it, and so they will keep trying. But until then, they will have to understand that it is accurate for an average person, or someone who has done a bit more of work, like me, to describe homeopathy as wrong. Depending on how charitable you feel, you may call it pseudoscience too. I haven't done a study of the concept of pseudoscience. What if something is not a science? Can we really find tight criteria for what it is and isn't a science? I care more about whether something is right or wrong.

## Some heuristics to safely dismiss and accept things with little information

Start with simple procedures, move on to more time-consuming ones if you think you need more confidence in your belief.

1.  See if it makes sense. This usually works most of the time. This consists of checking an issue against your background beliefs, among which, hopefully, is an understanding the laws of physics.
2.  See what the consensus on that issue is
    1.  This can be done by finding statements from important institutions
    2.  Or by reading meta-analysis
3.  Read critiques of the consensus positions. Read critiques of those critiques. Read critiques of the critiques of the critiques, and so on until you reach the current year. Google Scholar can help you to do that.
4.  Do a full-scale literature review, read most of the papers that have been written on the issue
5.  And finally, assess the literature yourself, looking for methodological flaws.

## Understand why people disagree with you

After looking for resources on homeopathy, I think it is easy to understand why there are people who believe in it.Some people react to this fact by mentally facepalming, or getting angry with them, or insulting them ("How can you be so stupid! It is obvious that it doesn't work!"). I react by understanding the sources of the beliefs of the believer in homeopathy. I infer there are two types of people who believe in it: regular people who buy homeopathy, and homeopaths themselves.

For the first group, they probably came to believe in it because some people in their surroundings tried it, and said it work, and perhaps they looked for information on the net and found homeopathic websites and they told them it worked. Or maybe, they just gave it a try, and they got better, and they didn't know about the [post hoc fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post_hoc_ergo_propter_hoc).

For the second group, they have read the literature, and know what is going on. They have gone to levels 4 or 5 in my scale of heuristics, and still believe in homeopathy. This probably means that they began with a strong or moderately strong belief that homeopathy worked, and then they haven't found sufficient proof to abandon that belief. They should stop just adjusting their beliefs on the basis on new evidence and review whether their initial opinion was correct at all. Studies will keep coming, and in the end, if they are honest, and science points that way, they will stop believing in homeopathy.

If you want to engage with homeopaths -I personally find it a waste of time, but maybe you don't-make sure to understand them, and see what sources of evidence they may have, and explain why they don't work, rather than blaming people for their stupidity. People don't like being called stupid, and it may backfire: it may make believe even more in homeopathy.

## Conclusion

There ain't such thing as a free lunch. Acquiring information has opportunity costs. There is a tradeoff between certainty and amount of work: the more work you do to acquire information, the more certain you can be. So it is useful, or even, necessary, to have heuristics to quickly reach high enough confidence levels on the truth of a given belief with small amounts of information. They are heuristics, and may fail, so be prepared to change your mind when new evidence comes in, or when you reassess evidence you already knew. In this post, I offer some heuristics to do this.

Once a belief has been dismissed or accepted, you can stay with that dismissal or acceptance, and if you want, occasionally check new evidence to see if it support your initial assessment. If you happen to hold a belief that, for most reasonable people, is false, the burden of proof is on you to produce a low-acquisition-cost response to convince them that the world is wrong and you are right. This is basically doing a level-4/5 review of the evidence, and being so charitable that your your critics get the impression that they are being understood, and their criticisms are being addressed.
