## 正文

_此前：[2016](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2016)、[2017](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2017)_

今年，我达成了将自己 income 的 50% 用于 donating 的目标。这笔 donation 按照我去年决定的方式 split：

-   80% 给我个人的 [donor-advised fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donor-advised_fund)，以后再 granted 给某个具体的 charity。

-   18% 给 [GiveWell’s](http://www.givewell.org/) top charities（由 GiveWell 自行决定如何 allocated）。

-   2% 用于 support GiveWell 的 operations。

一些想法：

-   这样做感觉有些无聊，也有些怯懦，但因为这一整年我都住在塞内加尔，目前与 effective altruism community 相当脱节，所以我肯定不了解最新、最出色的 potential recipients。

-   去年，我考虑过 lowering 今年的 donation goal。最终我决定不这样做，因为我预计明年能够多 save 相当一笔钱。

-   去年，我对 Effective Altruism Funds 并不怎么感兴趣。最近的 [management changes](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/yYHKRgLk9ufjJZn23/announcing-new-ea-funds-management-teams)，以及它们重新开始更 active 的 grantmaking（[1](https://app.effectivealtruism.org/funds/far-future/payouts/3JnNTzhJQsu4yQAYcKceSi)、[2](https://app.effectivealtruism.org/funds/ea-community/payouts/2dyBJqJBSIq6sAGU6gMYQW)），让我更感兴趣了。如果到明年，我还没想到如何更好地使用我 donor-advised funds 中的钱，我会非常认真地考虑让 EA funds 来决定。

-   年底，我的 cash 又有些不足，这次是因为我拖着没有为许多非常昂贵的工作相关航班办理 reimbursed。由于今年 charitable tax deductibility cap 被 raised 到了 60%，我把一部分 donation 推迟到了 1 月（但仍将其 counted 在我 2018 年而非 2019 年的目标中）。

-   donor-advised fund 有一个被低估的 advantage：我的 bank（Ally）对 outgoing ACH transfers 设有每天 \$5,000 的 limit，但我的 DAF 允许我从它们那边 initiate 一笔 unlimited size 的 ACH。所以，实际上我先将全部 donation 都 donated 给了 DAF，再把其中的 20% forwarded 给 GiveWell。

## 原文

_Previously: [2016](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2016), [2017](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2017)_

This year, I met my goal of donating 50% of my income. The donation was split the same as I decided last year:

-   80% to my personal [donor-advised fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donor-advised_fund), to be granted to a specific charity later.

-   18% to [GiveWell’s](http://www.givewell.org/) top charities (to be allocated at GiveWell’s discretion).

-   2% to support GiveWell’s operations.

Thoughts:

-   It feels boring/wimpy to do this, but I’m pretty disconnected from the effective altruism community right now since I’ve been living in Senegal for the whole year, so I’m definitely not up to speed on the latest and greatest potential recipients.

-   Last year, I considered lowering this year’s donation goal. I eventually decided against because I expect to be able to save a fair amount more next year.

-   Last year I was unexcited about the Effective Altruism Funds. After the recent [management changes](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/yYHKRgLk9ufjJZn23/announcing-new-ea-funds-management-teams) and their return to more active grantmaking ([1](https://app.effectivealtruism.org/funds/far-future/payouts/3JnNTzhJQsu4yQAYcKceSi), [2](https://app.effectivealtruism.org/funds/ea-community/payouts/2dyBJqJBSIq6sAGU6gMYQW)), I’m more excited. If I haven’t thought of something better to do with the money in my donor-advised funds by next year, I’d strongly consider letting the EA funds decide.

-   I was slightly low on cash at the end of the year again, this time because I procrastinated on getting reimbursed for a lot of very expensive work-related plane flights. Since the charitable tax deductibility cap was raised to 60% this year, I postponed part of my donation until January (but still counted it towards my 2018, not 2019, goal).

-   An underrated advantage of a donor-advised fund is that my bank (Ally) places a limit of $5,000 per day on outgoing ACH transfers, but my DAF allows me to initiate an ACH of unlimited size from their side. So I actually donated all of my donation to the DAF, then forwarded 20% of it to GiveWell.
