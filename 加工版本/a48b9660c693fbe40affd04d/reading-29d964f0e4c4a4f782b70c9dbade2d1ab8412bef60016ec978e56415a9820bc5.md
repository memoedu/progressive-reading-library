# US Federal R&D spending decline: a breakdown

---

## 正文

在 US，federal R&D expenditures 自 1964 年达到 GDP 的 1.86% 这一 peak 以来已有下降。如今，它们为 GDP 的 0.61%。以下来自 [ITIF](https://itif.org/publications/2019/08/12/federal-support-rd-continues-its-ignominious-slide) 的内容代表了这一叙事：

![图片](https://itif.org/sites/default/files/chart%201.JPG)

或者，看看 [Gruber and Johnson](https://www.amazon.com/Jump-Starting-America-Breakthrough-Economic-American/dp/1541762487) 的说法，

> “从 1940 年到 1964 年，用于 research and development 的 federal funding increased twentyfold。在 mid-1960s 达到 peak 时，这笔 spending amount 约为 annual gross domestic product 的 2 percent——United States 每 fifty dollars 中大约有 one 用于 research and development 的 government funding（relative to GDP，相当于今天近 \$400 billion）。这对我们的 economy、Americans 以及世界的 impact 简直是 transformational。” $$...$$
> 
> “用于 research and development 的 Federal spending 在 1964 年 peaked，接近 economic output 的 2 percent，而在随后的 fifty years 里 fell 至仅约为 economy 的 0.7 percent。16 按今天 GDP 的同一 fraction 进行 Converted，这一 decline represents 每年约 \$240 billion，我们不再将其用于 creating 下一代 good jobs。我们应该在意吗？如果有 socially beneficial research 和 product development 可以去做，今天的 innovative companies 肯定会承担起来，不是吗？”

有两个原因，说明这张 chart 和整体叙事并不像看上去那么糟糕。

R&D spending 是 technological progress 的 drivers 之一，而 technological progress 通常（但并不 [necessarily](https://nintil.com/still-no-great-stagnation)！）又会 leads to economic growth。因此，无论来自哪里，较少的 R&D spending 都是坏消息。第二个原因可能在于，governments 所 fund 的 R&D 的 _kind_ 与 private sector 的不同。支持 R&D 的 government funding 的最有力 argument 在于 basic science；对于它，可以提出 public goods argument。R&D 越 closer to application，其 performer 就越能 captured 更多 benefits，而为其 funding 的理由也就越不充分。

这张 chart 可能具有 misleading 性，是因为这样的 decline 并未主要发生在 public funding body 更擅长的那类 research——basic research——上。这一 decline 是由 _development_ component 所 driven 的，尤其是由 1964 年前后 spending 一个格外大的 peak 所 driven 的。

![Federal R&D 的 Historical Trends | American Association for the Advancement  of Science（美国科学促进会）](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/2020-10/RDGDP.png)

以下是 R&D 中 D component 的完整情况（这些及其他 figures 取自[这里](https://www.aaas.org/programs/r-d-budget-and-policy/federal-rd-budget-dashboard)）：

![image-20210222134531158](https://nintil.com/images/2021-02-22-us-science-without-space/image-20210222134531158.png)

最后，federal level 的 basic research 起初确实 decline，但随后逐渐 recovered，直至 2004 年，此后又已 declined。

![image-20210222134348098](https://nintil.com/images/2021-02-22-us-science-without-space/image-20210222134348098.png)

那么，为什么会这样？看看 federal R&D expenditure 的 composition 随时间的变化，就不难明白原因：

![image-20210222135017385](https://nintil.com/images/2021-02-22-us-science-without-space/image-20210222135017385.png)

如果还不完全清楚，那么去掉 Space 和 Defense 后，情况是这样的：

![image-20210222135052119](https://nintil.com/images/2021-02-22-us-science-without-space/image-20210222135052119.png)

这里没有 dramatic decline。_确实存在_ decline，但不像 headline figure 中近乎 fourfold 的 drop，这里从 peak 到 bottom 的 drop 会小于 2x。如果考虑到那些 energy R&D 中有很多可能用于 nuclear weapons development，情况或许就更没有那么惊人了。

我 excluding Space（即 NASA）和 DoD 的原因在于，前者（chart 中的那个 peak 就是 Space Race）是一项独一无二的事业，heavily skewed towards highly specific systems（rockets 和 spacecraft）的 development。US DoD 所做的那类 expenditure 总体上也是如此，它 heavily skewed towards highly applied use cases；相较于更 basic 或 non-defense areas 中的其他种类 R&D，这些 use cases 不太可能 generate 人们希望从 R&D 中获得的那类 spillovers（我的[总体](https://nintil.com/was-world-war-ii-good-for-growth/)[看法](https://nintil.com/is-war-necessary-for-economic-growth-a-review-and-some-aviation-history-love/)是，在 non-military technology 的 progress 和总体 productivity 方面，defense R&D 很可能不如其 civilian counterpart）。在这里可以看到，DoD 所 funds 的内容中，只有极少部分是 Research，尤其是 [DARPA](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/DODDepts.png)，在整体 DoD budget 中只是一个很小的 entity。

![图片](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/DefRD.png)

AAAS dataset 并未追溯到 peak 之前，因此我们无法断言 DoD 当时是否在开展更多 basic science，但在这方面，它们在广泛的 funding ecosystem 中的 contribution 似乎很小，

![图片](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/2020-07/BasicRes_0.png)

我认为，这里更好的叙事是：federal R&D funding 的 composition 已经改变，health 以外的 areas，作为 GDP 的 fraction，已经 stagnated 或 shrunk。而如果你相信，相较于其他 areas，biology 最称得上是 [Endless Frontier](https://nintil.com/science-ending-frontier/)，那么这也未必是坏事。

那么，应该如何运用这一认识？首先，这一认识否定了一种按 GDP 的 percentage 来 allocating R&D 的 heuristic：“如果它 lower than historical trends，就 raise 它，直到 reaches historical average（或 maximum）。”如果将 Cold War 和 Space Race 视为具有各自 non-economic drivers 的 abnormal events，那么应该上升到哪个合适的 historical figure，就不清楚了。

但人们仍然可以说：“当时 economy 有能力 sustaining 超过 2% 的 federal R&D，因此如今应该仍然做得到；事实上，这笔钱可以 deployed 到一场**不过是针对 [Aging](https://nintil.com/longevity) 的 Space Race**，或者用于 Material Sciences。”这是一个更好、也更 valid 的 argument。

当然，这一 decline 令人担忧还有_其他_原因：可以提出这样的 argument，即撇开 GDP growth 不谈，US 需要 strong military，而 DoD R&D budget shrinking 并不是好消息。这是我在这里没有考虑的问题。

还可以进一步问，为什么是 2%，而不是更多？或者，更一般地说，一个非常有意思的问题是：应该用于 R&D 的 optimal share of GDP 是多少？在 politically feasible 的范围内尽可能多吗？在大多数关于 R&D funding 的 models 中，无论是否 formal，如果希望 increase GDP growth，这似乎确实是正确的 conclusion，因此问题就变成：从哪些地方争取更多 R&D funding 最好（reallocating budget、taxes？）。与此同时，还有一个如何花这笔钱的问题。我的 _intuition_ 是，在 on the margin 的意义上，这比仅仅 increasing budget 更重要。无论是 field 还是 mode of spending，并非所有 R&D 都是 created equal。

## 原文

In the US, federal R&D expenditures are down since their peak in 1964 at 1.86% of GDP. Now they are 0.61% of GDP. This from [ITIF](https://itif.org/publications/2019/08/12/federal-support-rd-continues-its-ignominious-slide) is representative of this narrative:

![img](https://itif.org/sites/default/files/chart%201.JPG)

Or here's [Gruber and Johnson](https://www.amazon.com/Jump-Starting-America-Breakthrough-Economic-American/dp/1541762487),

> “From 1940 to 1964, federal funding for research and development increased twentyfold. At its peak in the mid-1960s, this spending amount was around 2 percent of annual gross domestic product—roughly one in every fifty dollars in the United States was devoted to government funding of research and development (equivalent, relative to GDP, to almost $400 billion today). The impact on our economy, on Americans, and on the world was simply transformational.” \[...\]
> 
> “Federal spending on research and development peaked at nearly 2 percent of economic output in 1964 and over the next fifty years fell to only around 0.7 percent of the economy.16 Converted to the same fraction of GDP today, that decline represents roughly $240 billion per year that we no longer spend on creating the next generation of good jobs. Should we care? If there is socially beneficial research and product development to be done, surely the innovative companies of today will take this on?”

There are two reasons why this chart and overall story is not as bad as it seems.

R&D spending is one of the drivers of technological progress which in turn generally (But not [necessarily](https://nintil.com/still-no-great-stagnation)!) leads to economic growth. So less R&D spending, regardless of where it is coming from is bad news. The second reason would be that perhaps the _kind_ of R&D that governments fund is different from that of the private sector. The strongest argument for government funding of R&D is in basic science; the one for which one can make the public goods argument. The closer to application R&D is, the more benefits can be captured by their performer and less of a strong case for funding it.

The reason for why the chart may be misleading is that such a decline has not been mostly in the kind of research a public funding body would be better at -basic research-. The decline has been driven by the _development_ component and particularly it has been driven by a conspicuously large peak in spending around in 1964.

![Historical Trends in Federal R&D | American Association for the Advancement  of Science](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/2020-10/RDGDP.png)

Here's the full view for the D component of R&D (These and other figures are taken from [here](https://www.aaas.org/programs/r-d-budget-and-policy/federal-rd-budget-dashboard)):

![image-20210222134531158](https://nintil.com/images/2021-02-22-us-science-without-space/image-20210222134531158.png)

And lastly, basic research at a federal level did initially decline, but then gradually recovered up until 2004, having declined since.

![image-20210222134348098](https://nintil.com/images/2021-02-22-us-science-without-space/image-20210222134348098.png)

So why was that? Well, looking at the composition of federal R&D expenditure over time it's not hard to see why:

![image-20210222135017385](https://nintil.com/images/2021-02-22-us-science-without-space/image-20210222135017385.png)

If it's not completely clear, this is what this looks like without Space and Defense:

![image-20210222135052119](https://nintil.com/images/2021-02-22-us-science-without-space/image-20210222135052119.png)

Here there is no dramatic decline. There _is_ a decline, but rather than the almost fourfold drop in the headline figure, the drop here would be, peak to bottom, less than 2x. This may be even less dramatic if we consider that a lot of that energy R&D may have been for nuclear weapons development.

My reason for excluding Space (That is, NASA) and the DoD is that the former (that peak in the chart is the Space Race) was a one of a kind endeavor that was heavily skewed towards the development of highly specific systems (rockets and spacecraft). The same is true in general of the kind of expenditure done by the US DoD, it is heavily skewed towards highly applied use cases, and these are less likely to generate the kind of spillovers one wants R&D for, relative to other kinds of R&D in more basic or non-defense areas (My [general](https://nintil.com/was-world-war-ii-good-for-growth/) [view](https://nintil.com/is-war-necessary-for-economic-growth-a-review-and-some-aviation-history-love/) is that defense R&D is very likely not as good as its civilian counterpart for progress in non-military technology and productivity in general). You can see here that very little of what the DoD funds is Research, and in particular [DARPA](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/DODDepts.png) is a tiny entity within the overall DoD budget.

![img](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/2020-11/DefRD.png)

The AAAS dataset doesn't go before the peak so we can't say if the DoD was doing more basic science back then, but their contribution within the broad funding ecosystem seems small in that regards,

![img](https://www.aaas.org/sites/default/files/2020-07/BasicRes_0.png)

I think a better story here is that the composition of federal R&D funding has changed, areas other than health have stagnated or shrunk as a fraction of GDP. This is in turn not necessarily a bad thing if you believe that biology is the most [Endless Frontier](https://nintil.com/science-ending-frontier/) there is, relative to other areas.

Now, what should one do with this knowledge? The first thing is that this knowledge defeats one heuristic for allocating R&D as a percentage of GDP: "If it is lower than historical trends, raise it until it reaches the historical average (Or maximum)". If one takes the Cold War and the Space Race as abnormal events that had their own non-economic drivers then it's unclear what a good historical figure to climb up to would be.

But one could still say "The economy was capable of sustaining over 2% of federal R&D back then, so that should still be doable; in fact that money could be deployed towards a **Space Race but for [Aging](https://nintil.com/longevity)** or for Material Sciences". That is a better and more valid argument.

To be sure, there are _other_ reasons why the decline is troubling: One could make the argument that GDP growth aside, the US needs a strong military and the DoD R&D budget shrinking is not good news. This is something I have not considered here.

One could further say, why 2% and not more? Or in general, the very interesting question of what's the optimal share of GDP that should go to R&D? As much as politically feasible? On most models, formal or not, of R&D funding it seems that that is indeed the right conclusion if one wants to increase GDP growth, so the question then becomes what are the best places to scrape more R&D funding from (reallocating budget, taxes?). And there is a parallel question on how to spend that money. My _intuition_ is that this matters on the margin more than just increasing the budget. Not all R&D is created equal, both in field and mode of spending.
