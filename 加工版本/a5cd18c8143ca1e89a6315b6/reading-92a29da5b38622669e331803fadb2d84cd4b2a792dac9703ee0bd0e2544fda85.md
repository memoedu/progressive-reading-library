# 你是在过人生，还是只在规划自己的传记？｜Are You Living Life or Just Planning Your Biography?

---

## 正文

modern psychology 最令人惊讶的 findings 之一，是我们竟然如此不善于 guessing 什么会让我们 happy。我们不仅不善于 predicting 什么会让自己 satisfy，还经常 misremember 自己过去的 happiness。

在我看过的或许最精彩的线上演讲之一中，Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman 谈到了 experiencing and remembering selves 之间的 difference（如果你没时间观看，可以[点击这里下载](http://video.ted.com/talks/podcast/DanielKahneman_2010.mp4)，以后再看，它绝对值得你花上 20 分钟）。

Kahneman 的观点是，我们关于 ideal life 的讨论往往走错了方向。这是因为我们 confuse 了两个看似 similar、实际上却 distinct 的 concepts：

1.  我们 moment-to-moment 地 **experience** 到的 quality of life。
2.  我们 **remember** 的、并被 woven in 到我们人生 narrative 中的 quality of our life。

## Vacation Dilemma——Ideal Life 是被 Lived，还是仅仅被 Remembered？

收听本文

问题在于，我们的 experiential happiness 与我们从整体上思考自己的人生时所感到的 happy 程度，只是 loosely correlated。

Kahneman 举了一个为期两周的假期的例子。假如假期的每一刻都 equally enjoyable，那么两周的假期应该是一个星期的假期的 twice as good。毕竟，experiencing happiness 的 moments 有 twice as many。

![Experiencing 两周的假期](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/TwoWeeksExperienced.jpg)

然而，从 memory 的角度来看，两周的假期几乎不比一周的假期更好。这是因为这段时间并没有 added 新的 memories，因此所有 similar 的 happiness moments 就这样被 forgotten 了。

![Memory 并不会 count 分钟数](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/TwoWeeksRemembered.jpg)

conflict 就在这里。假设你正要决定下一次假期的安排，而且你相当有把握，这个假期会令你 satisfying。**你应该去一周，还是两周？**

Kahneman 又提出了一个让问题更加有趣的设问：如果你知道假期结束后，所有照片都会被销毁，而你还会服用一种 amnesiac drug，迫使你 forget 自己曾经度过这个假期，你还会选择同样的假期吗？

当我在这个博客上谈论 pursuit of the ideal life 时，这一发现提出了一个问题：究竟是什么 constitutes ideal life？是我们的 moment-to-moment experiences，还是仅仅是我们事后 weave 的 narrative？

## Biographer 的 Tyranny

designing a life 的难处在于，你的 experiencing self 没有投票权。只有那个 biographer，也就是你自身 remembers 过去、predicts 未来的那一部分，才能决定你选择什么职业、怎样的假期，以及与什么人共度时光。

这似乎实在不公平。人生过完之后，你真正能写下来的人生，无非是纸张和一些墨水。真正让人觉得重要的，是流经我们 consciousness 的那些零碎时光。

这个问题不只是我们常见的那种经历：做某件事，只是为了日后能谈论它。比如，有人跑马拉松，只为了“能说自己跑过”。我们的 experiences 之所以没有投票权，是因为它们早已被内心的 biographer 接管了。

我们并不以 experiential happiness 为依据来作出未来的 decisions，因为除了眼前这一小片当下，我们无法接触其他任何东西。其余一切都已被 converted 为 memories，并受制于 storyteller 的所有 biases。

## 去 Experiencing Ideal Life，而不只是 Narrating 它

我不知道你怎么想，但我觉得这种 biographical tyranny 不可接受。我不愿投入大量时间去 designing a life，只是为了能告诉自己它很棒，而真正 experience 它时却糟糕透顶。

![](http://www.assoc-amazon.com/e/ir?t=scottcom-20&l=ur2&o=1)

与所有 cognitive biases 一样，我不认为这里有什么简单的 solution。人难免犯错，不幸的是，人也难免 **[err systematically](http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html?ie=UTF8&location=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FPredictably-Irrational-Hidden-Forces-Decisions%2Fdp%2F006135323X%3Fie%3DUTF8%26s%3Dbooks%26qid%3D1274125531%26sr%3D8-1&tag=scottcom-20&linkCode=ur2&camp=1789&creative=9325)**。

不过，我尝试为自己思考人生的方式增添几个大方向，以避开那些最明显的陷阱。下面是我正努力培养的几个 mental habits，希望借此摆脱 biographical tyranny：

### #1 – 停下来，Observe 当下

Kahneman 解释说，middle moments 往往会在 memory 中被 washed out。我们会 accentuate 事情开始的时候、结束的时候，或发生 dramatic changes 的时候。

一种 counteracting force，就是问问自己此刻 feel 如何。不是进行 whole-life assessment，而是像按下秒表一样，checkup 自己的 instantaneous mental state。我相信，这样做帮助我更好地 recall 自己在一段时间内的感受，而不只是最后的感受。

![](http://www.assoc-amazon.com/e/ir?t=scottcom-20&l=ur2&o=1)

Eckhart Tolle 已经卖出了**[数千本书](http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html?ie=UTF8&location=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FPower-Now-Guide-Spiritual-Enlightenment%2Fdp%2F1577314808%3Fie%3DUTF8%26s%3Dbooks%26qid%3D1274125727%26sr%3D1-1&tag=scottcom-20&linkCode=ur2&camp=1789&creative=9325)**，宣扬停留在 present moment 的 pseudo-spiritual wisdom。姑且不论这大多只是把几千年前的忠告重新包装成 new-agey 的样子，我觉得，这类说法之所以受欢迎，部分原因还是与 biographical tyranny 这个问题有关。我们太常沉浸于对整个人生的思考、对未来的忧虑和对过去的遗憾，以至于没能留意那些真正 constitute 我们人生的零碎当下。

### #2 – 比起 Brief Events，更要 Emphasize Rewarding Routines

既然 middle-moments 和 sameness 会在我们人生的 biography 中被 washed out，那么在为未来作 decisions 时，有意识地给它们 weight higher 就是合理的。

换句话说，比起 one-time events，更值得关注的是你的 lifestyle 如何 affects 你的 routine。例如，回想我在 France 的这段生活时，我很可能会 remember 在 Barcelona 度过的那个美妙周末，或是我经历过的短暂 relationships。

然而，那些 experiences 无论多么 positive，所占的时间都比上课或步行去买食品杂货之类的事情少得多。我们每天做的事情，如果能 contribute positively to 我们的 well-being，或是 detract from 它，其重要程度就可能是那些较短的 events 的数千倍。

在为未来作 planning 时，这意味着我应该花多得多的时间作 decisions，让自己能够避开数百小时无聊的上课时间，而不是去避开诸如短暂却令人沮丧的停电 experience。其中一种或许更 memorable，但另一种却占据了我多得多的 experiential life。

### #3 – Create 一种 Way of Living，而不是一个 Goal

“人生是一段旅程，而不是一个目的地”虽然是句陈词滥调，但它仍然是对的。这也是我赞同 lifestyle design 这个 concept 的原因之一。因为它将许多人通常抱有的 accomplishment-oriented ambitions，转向了关注你实际上如何度过其间每一刻的追求。

现在，如果我要选择新的 challenges，我会确保自己选择的 challenges 能让我在通往目的地的路上感到 enjoyable。Ideally，我也会尽量选择那些一旦 reached，就能 improve 我的 Way of Living 的 goals。

对我来说，建立一家 online business 来支付我的全部开销，曾是其中一个主要的 goals，因为它让我能够自由地只做自己感兴趣的事情。达到不错的 physical shape 并做到 eating healthy，则是另一个，因为这让我有了更多 energy。

## 原文

One of the most startling findings of modern psychology is how bad we are at guessing what will make us happy. Not only are we lousy at predicting what will satisfy us, we often misremember our happiness in the past.

In perhaps one of the best online talks I’ve ever watched, Nobel laureate Daniel Kahneman speaks about the difference between the experiencing and remembering selves (if you don’t have time to watch it, [click here to download it](http://video.ted.com/talks/podcast/DanielKahneman_2010.mp4) and watch it later, it’s definitely worth the 20 minutes of your time).

Kahneman’s point is that our discussions about the ideal life are often misguided. This is because we confuse two seemingly similar but actually distinct concepts:

1.  The quality of life, as we **experience** it, moment-to-moment.
2.  The quality of our life, as we **remember** it and as it is woven in to the narrative of our lives.

## The Vacation Dilemma – Is the Ideal Life Lived or Just Remembered?

Listen to this article  

The problem is that our experiential happiness is only loosely correlated with how happy we feel when thinking broadly about our lives.

Kahneman gives the example of a two week vacation. Assuming the vacation was equally enjoyable in every moment, then a two week vacation should be twice as good as a one week vacation. After all, there are twice as many moments of experiencing happiness.

![Experiencing two weeks on vacation](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/TwoWeeksExperienced.jpg)

However, from the standpoint of memory, a two-week vacation is barely better than a one-week vacation. This is because there are no new memories added in this time, so all the similar moments of happiness are simply forgotten.

![Memory doesn't count the minutes](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2010/05/TwoWeeksRemembered.jpg)

Here we have the conflict. Say you’re about to decide your next vacation plans, which you’re reasonably confident will be satisfying. **Should you go for one week or two?**

Making the question more interesting, Kahneman asks, would you pick the same vacations if you knew that after, all the pictures would be destroyed and you’d take an amnesiac drug forcing you to forget ever having taken it?

When I talk about the pursuit of the ideal life here on this blog, this revelation asks the question, what constitutes the ideal life? Is it our moment-to-moment experiences or simply the narrative we weave afterward?

## The Tyranny of the Biographer

The difficulty with designing a life, is that your experiencing self doesn’t get a vote. It’s only the biographer, the part of yourself that remembers the past and predicts the future that gets a say in what careers you pick, vacations you choose and people you spend time with.

This doesn’t really seem fair. What you actually would write about your life after it has been lived is merely paper and some ink. It’s the slivers of time that pass through our consciousness that feel important.

This problem goes beyond the common experience of doing something for the purpose of talking about it later. Such as people who run marathons to “say they did it.” The reason our experiences don’t get a vote, is because they’ve already been taken over by the inner biographer.

We don’t base future decisions on experiential happiness because we don’t have access to anything but this sliver of now. Everything else has been converted to memories, and subject to all of the biases of the storyteller.

## Experiencing the Ideal Life, Instead of Simply Narrating It

I don’t know about you, but I find this biographical tyranny unacceptable. I wouldn’t want to invest a lot of time designing a life that I can tell myself is great, but is lousy when I actually experience it.

![](http://www.assoc-amazon.com/e/ir?t=scottcom-20&l=ur2&o=1)

As with all cognitive biases, I don’t think there is an easy solution. To err is human, and unfortunately, so is to **[err systematically](http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html?ie=UTF8&location=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FPredictably-Irrational-Hidden-Forces-Decisions%2Fdp%2F006135323X%3Fie%3DUTF8%26s%3Dbooks%26qid%3D1274125531%26sr%3D8-1&tag=scottcom-20&linkCode=ur2&camp=1789&creative=9325)**.

However, I have tried to add a few broad ways of thinking about my life to avoid the most obvious traps. Here are a few of the mental habits I’m trying to foster to escape biographical tyranny:

### #1 – Stop and Observe the Now

Kahneman explains that the middle moments are often washed out in memory. We accentuate when things start, when they end, or when something dramatic changes.

One counteracting force is simply to ask yourself how you feel at the current moment. Not a whole-life assessment, but a stopwatch checkup on your instantaneous mental state. Doing this, I believe, has helped me better recall how I’ve felt during a period, instead of just the end.

![](http://www.assoc-amazon.com/e/ir?t=scottcom-20&l=ur2&o=1)

Eckhart Tolle has sold **[thousands of books](http://www.amazon.com/gp/redirect.html?ie=UTF8&location=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.amazon.com%2FPower-Now-Guide-Spiritual-Enlightenment%2Fdp%2F1577314808%3Fie%3DUTF8%26s%3Dbooks%26qid%3D1274125727%26sr%3D1-1&tag=scottcom-20&linkCode=ur2&camp=1789&creative=9325)** preaching the pseudo-spiritual wisdom to stay in the present moment. Ignoring the fact it is mostly a rehash of millennia-old advice in a new-agey package, I feel some popularity of this comes back to the issue of biographical tyranny. We are so frequently absorbed in the thoughts of our life in totality, future worries, past regrets, that we fail to pay attention to the slivers of now that actually constitute our lives.

### #2 – Emphasize Rewarding Routines, over Brief Events

Since middle-moments and sameness are washed out in the biography of our lives, it makes sense to deliberately weight these higher in our decisions for the future.

To put it another way, it makes more sense to focus on how your lifestyle affects your routine, than one-time events. For example, in thinking of my stay here in France, I’m likely to remember my amazing weekend in Barcelona, or the brief relationships I’ve had.

Those experiences, however positive, composed a lot less time than, say, being in class or walking to get groceries. The things we do every day, if they contribute positively to our well-being or detract from it, may be thousands of times more important than shorter events.

When planning for the future, this means I should spend a lot more time on decisions that enable me to avoid the hundreds of hours of boring class time, instead of my brief, but frustrating experience without electricity, for example. One may be more memorable, but the other occupies far more of my experiential life.

### #3 – Create a Way of Living, Instead of a Goal

“Life is a journey, not a destination” is a clichÃ©, but it’s still true. That’s one of the reasons I’m a fan of the lifestyle design concept. Because it turns around the typical accomplishment-oriented ambitions many people have towards one that focuses on how you actually live all the moments in-between.

Now, if I pick new challenges, I make sure to pick ones that will be enjoyable on the way to my destination. Ideally, I also try to pick goals that, if reached, will improve the way I’m able to live.

Setting up an online business to pay for all my expenses had been a major one for me, since it allows me the freedom to only work on things I’m interested in. Getting in decent physical shape and eating healthy was another one, as it has given me more energy.
