_“怎样吃掉一头大象？一次吃一口。”_

如果你曾跑过几英里以上的路程，大概就能理解为什么需要控制自己的节奏。那些在比赛开始时就冲刺的跑者，远在冲过终点线之前就会筋疲力尽。同样的 principle 也适用于完成工作。我发现，maintaining weekly/daily to-do lists 是一种极其有效的工作 pacing 方法。

## Weekly/Daily To-Do Lists（每周／每日待办清单）

收听本文

WD To-Do List method 背后的 principle 很简单：

-   在每周结束时，写一份 list，列出你想 accomplished 的所有事情。
-   在每天结束时，写一份 list，列出那份 weekly list 中你想在明天 finished 的部分。

finish 你的 daily list 后，就停下来。不要再处理更多 projects 或 tasks。当天剩下的时间都可以用来放松。而在 finish weekly list 后，这一周的工作就结束了。这意味着，如果你在星期五下午之前 finish，就直到星期一早上才重新开始工作。

虽然这种 technique 听起来可能显而易见（事实也的确如此），但与通常采用的、保留 Next Action 或 project lists 的 _Getting Things Done_ approach 相比，使用 WD system 有一些关键 advantages。

## 为什么 Weekly/Daily To-Do List System 如此出色：

使用这种 method 几个月后，我发现它在几个关键方面胜过其他 systems：

### 1) WD system 能 manages 你的 energy**。**

问题不在于 time 耗尽，而在于 energy 耗尽。你每天可能有 24 小时，但其中许多时间都花在吃饭、睡觉，以及几小时 exhausting work 之后的放松上。任何不考虑这一点的 productivity system 都是 broken 的。

相比之下，Weekly/Daily system 会把你的 work 进行 blocks out，分成 manageable chunks。我不再试图每天 complete 所有事情，而只是 complete 自己的 daily list。整周也是如此。借助 WD system，你可以完成 maximum amount 的 work，同时给自己留出时间放松，享受 [selective unproductivity](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2008/04/03/how-to-keep-productivity-from-making-you-a-robot/)。

### 2) WD system 能 stops procrastination

当你看到面前堆积如山的 work，却无法 visualize 一个轻松的 finish 时，就可能发生 Procrastination。通过把 to-do list 进行 splitting up，分成 daily lists，你那些大象般庞大的 projects 就能变成一口大小的 tasks。

### 3) WD system 能让你变得 proactive

在 implementing 这种 method 之前的几年里，我的 system 是使用一份 daily to-do list。不幸的是，我发现这种 method 让我忽略了那些并不 urgent 的 bigger tasks。当你的 to-do list 上已经有十个 items 时，再给当天添加第十一个，似乎并不吸引人。

但在写 weekly list 时，你的心态就不一样了。有六天时间来 finish 所有事情（假设你休息一天），就更容易把那些 important 但 non-urgent tasks 加进去。

### 4) WD system 能防止你 burning out

之前我写过，上周我是如何无意中 [overloaded my schedule](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2008/04/02/20-tips-to-survive-when-you%e2%80%99ve-overloaded-your-schedule/) 的。虽然我当时在应付 2-3x 的 workload，但使用 Weekly/Daily system 让我没有 burning out，也没有 feeling stressed。通过把我的 work 进行 automatically dividing up，分成 weekly total 和 daily increments，我就能 focus on 下一口，而不是整头大象。

## 如何使用 Weekly/Daily To-Do List

这一节的标题可能看起来已经说得很明白了。写出你的 weekly list 和 daily lists，finish 它们，然后重复。但在使用这种 approach 几个月后，我发现有几个细微之处，你可能需要考虑。

## 对 Daily List 进行 Focus

weekly list 的作用，是作为写 daily lists 的 starting point。在你分出明天想处理的 chunk 后，这一周的其他 tasks 就不该再占据你的心思。你可以假装它们不存在，就好像世界上仅有的 tasks 就是明天的那些。

这种 approach 是一种极其有效的 stress-reliever。人很容易担心自己要怎样 finish 所有事情。但当“所有事情”变成明天的七八个 tasks 时，就更容易 manage 了。

## 不要对 Lists 进行 Expand

如果你比预期更早 finish daily 或 weekly list，你可能会忍不住想 expand。为什么不再加几项 activities 呢，反正你有时间，_对吧_？

这不是个好主意，因为它会妨碍你 focusing on daily list。一旦你给 expansion 留出可能，你的“所有事情”就不再是明天要 finish 的 tasks，而又变回了你那份 infinite to-do list。Stress 和 procrastination 很快就会随之而来。

显然，有时你不得不作出 adjustments。有些临时出现的 tasks 需要 appended 到你的 lists 中。但尽量不要仅仅因为有空闲时间，就 expanding 你的 lists。

## 开展 Monthly Review

WD system 忽略的一个方面是 monthly list。有些 projects 和 activities 可能太 large/non-urgent，以至于在 weekly list 中被 skipped。不幸的是，maintaining monthly list 所需的 effort 超过了它的价值。你很难提前一个月 predict 所有需要 accomplish 的 small tasks，因此它就不再与你的 weekly lists 相关。

相较之下，我喜欢定期做 monthly review。在那次 review 中，我会挑出几个自己想在当月 finish 的 larger projects。在写 weekly lists 时，我可以把这些记在心里。

Weekly/Daily To-Do List 并不 complicated。生活不必 complicated，也能运转良好。试着使用 WD system 一个月。你可以用铅笔和纸 setup 你的 lists，也可以选择我最喜欢的 tool，[Ta-Da List](http://www.tadalist.com/)。你又有什么损失呢？

_“How do you eat an elephant? One bite at a time.”_

If you’ve ever run more than a few miles, you probably understand why you need to pace yourself. Runners that sprint at the start of a race will be exhausted far before they cross the finish line. The same principle applies when trying to get work done. One solution for pacing my work that I’ve found incredibly effective is maintaining weekly/daily to-do lists.

## Weekly/Daily To-Do Lists

Listen to this article  

The principle behind the WD To-Do List method is simple:

-   At the end of the week, write a list containing everything you want to get accomplished.
-   At the end of the day, write a list containing what parts of that weekly list you want to be finished tomorrow.

After you finish your daily list, you stop. Don’t work on more projects or tasks. You have the rest of the day to relax. And after you finish the weekly list, you’re done for the week. This means if you finish by Friday afternoon, you don’t start work again until Monday morning.

Although this technique might sound obvious (and it is), there are some key advantages using a WD system has over the typical, _Getting Things Done_ approach of keeping Next Action or project lists.

## Why the Weekly/Daily To-Do List System Rocks:

After using this method for several months, I’ve found it beats the other systems in a few key places:

### 1) A WD system manages your energy**.**

The problem isn’t running out of time, it’s running out of energy. You may have 24 hours in the day, but many of those are taken away eating, sleeping and relaxing after a few hours of exhausting work. Any productivity system that doesn’t take this into account is broken.

A Weekly/Daily system, instead, blocks out your work into manageable chunks. Instead of trying to complete everything each day, I just complete my daily list. The same is true for the entire week. With a WD system you get a maximum amount of work done, while leaving yourself time to relax and enjoy [selective unproductivity](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2008/04/03/how-to-keep-productivity-from-making-you-a-robot/).

### 2) A WD system stops procrastination

Procrastination can happen when you see the mountain of work in front of you, and can’t visualize an easy finish. By splitting up your to-do list into daily lists, your elephant-sized projects can become bite-sized tasks.

### 3) A WD system makes you proactive

My system for a few years prior to implementing this method was to use a daily to-do list. Unfortunately, I found that this method made me lose sight of bigger tasks that weren’t urgent. When you already have ten items on your to-do list, adding an eleventh for the day doesn’t seem appealing.

But when you’re writing the weekly list, you’re in a different frame of mind. With six days to finish everything (assuming you take a day off), it is easier to put in those important, but non-urgent tasks.

### 4) A WD system keeps you from burning out

Earlier I wrote about how I accidentally [overloaded my schedule](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2008/04/02/20-tips-to-survive-when-you%e2%80%99ve-overloaded-your-schedule/) last week. Using the Weekly/Daily system kept me from burning out or feeling stressed, even though I was dealing with 2-3x the workload. By automatically dividing up my work into a weekly total and daily increments, I could focus on the next bite, instead of the entire elephant.

## How to Use a Weekly/Daily To-Do List

The heading for this section might seem pretty self-explanatory. Write out your weekly list and your daily lists, finish them, repeat. But after using this approach for a few months, there are a few nuances you might want to consider.

## Focus on the Daily List

The point of the weekly list is to serve as the starting point for writing daily lists. After you’ve broken off the chunk you want to handle tomorrow, the other tasks in the week shouldn’t be on your mind. You can pretend they don’t exist, as if the only tasks in the world were the ones tomorrow.

This approach is an incredible stress-reliever. It’s easy to worry about how you’re going to finish everything. But when “everything” becomes seven or eight tasks tomorrow, it becomes easier to manage.

## Don’t Expand the Lists

If you finish your daily or weekly list earlier than you expected, you might be tempted to expand. Why not add a few extra activities, you have the time, _right_?

This is a bad idea because it stops you from focusing on the daily list. As soon as you create the possibility for expansion, your “everything” goes from being the tasks to finish tomorrow, back to your infinite to-do list. Stress and procrastination soon follow.

Obviously, there will be times when you have to make adjustments. Last-minute tasks that need to be appended to your lists. But try to avoid expanding your lists just because you have free time.

## Take on a Monthly Review

One area the WD system ignores is a monthly list. There are some projects and activities that may be too large/non-urgent that they might be skipped under the weekly list. Unfortunately, maintaining a monthly list is more effort than it is worth. It’s hard to predict all the small tasks you’ll need to accomplish a month ahead of time, so it stops becoming relevant to your weekly lists.

Instead I like to do a regular monthly review. In that review, I’ll pick out a few larger projects I want to finish that month. I can keep these in mind when I write my weekly lists.
