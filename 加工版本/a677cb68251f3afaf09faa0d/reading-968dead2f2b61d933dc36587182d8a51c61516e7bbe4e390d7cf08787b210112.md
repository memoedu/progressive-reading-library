# What should we do about network-effect monopolies?

---

## 正文

如今，许多 large companies 都是 software monopolies，它们先将自己的 product 免费 give away，以获得 monopoly status，然后[就](https://github.com/uBlockOrigin/uBlock-issues/issues/338#issuecomment-496009417)[做出最](http://www.fbpurity.com/news/important-news-facebooks-legal-team-have-told-me-i-am-banned-from-facebook-because-of-f-b-purity/)[可怕的](https://www.theverge.com/2019/6/28/19154220/grubhub-seamless-fake-restaurant-domain-names-commission-fees)[事情](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/blog/amazon-counterfeit-fake-products/)，[一旦](https://www.theverge.com/2020/6/16/21293419/hey-apple-rejection-ios-app-store-dhh-gangsters-antitrust)[它们已经](https://www.polemicdigital.com/google-amp-go-to-hell/)[获胜](https://thetechnoskeptic.com/yelp-extortion-starring-role/)。（[此前谈过](https://www.benkuhn.net/skinner/)，[此前也谈过](https://www.benkuhn.net/product/)。）对此，我们能做些什么吗？

遗憾的是，“你就是 product”之所以是一种 popular business model，是有原因的：没有这类 business model，像 Facebook 这样的 businesses 确实会很难维持下去。

Facebook 如果向自己的 users charge money，无异于自寻死路，因为它全部的 selling point 就在于所有人都 uses 它，而“所有人”都_讨厌_付钱。在 US，Facebook 每年 makes 超过 \$25 per person on ads ([source](https://www.statista.com/statistics/251328/facebooks-average-revenue-per-user-by-region/)). Can you imagine if instead of ads they tried to charge people $25？

即使从 on the margin 的角度看，任何让 Facebook 流失 users 的事情，也会使它对其余 users 而言 less valuable——这就是一个 negative feedback loop。任何由 users 为其他 users create value 的 site 也是如此，比如 Twitter、Craigslist、Yelp 或 Wikipedia。（这些恰好是最 stagnant 的 popular websites 中的一些，并非偶然！）

事实上，这是 [network effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_effect) 和 low marginal costs 所带来的一个 fundamental problem。如果一家 company 想要 maintain 一个 network effect，就需要尽可能多的 users。如果它的 marginal cost 是 low，那么获得 users 最容易的办法就是将 product 免费 give away。要做到这一点，它们就必须由其他人付钱。而一旦开始由其他人付钱，它们就不可避免地会开始 prioritizing 那个人的 interests。

过去，对于其他 network-effect businesses，我们曾用几种不同的方式 addressed 这个问题：

-   regulation，即监管（例如 local utilities）

-   breakups，即拆分（例如 Bell）

-   standardization 和 interoperability，即标准化与互操作性（例如 email、Web、cryptocurrency）

到目前为止，对于 tech monopolies，人们似乎主要关注 breakups——例如将 Facebook 与 Instagram/Whatsapp 分开——但 standardization 似乎在过去带来了好得多的 outcomes。（比起 National Grid，我更喜欢 email 和 Web……）我很想看到对这个 option 的更多探索！

## 原文

Many large companies today are software monopolies that give their product away for free to get monopoly status, then do [the](https://github.com/uBlockOrigin/uBlock-issues/issues/338#issuecomment-496009417) [most](http://www.fbpurity.com/news/important-news-facebooks-legal-team-have-told-me-i-am-banned-from-facebook-because-of-f-b-purity/) [horrible](https://www.theverge.com/2019/6/28/19154220/grubhub-seamless-fake-restaurant-domain-names-commission-fees) [things](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/blog/amazon-counterfeit-fake-products/) [once](https://www.theverge.com/2020/6/16/21293419/hey-apple-rejection-ios-app-store-dhh-gangsters-antitrust) [they’ve](https://www.polemicdigital.com/google-amp-go-to-hell/) [won](https://thetechnoskeptic.com/yelp-extortion-starring-role/). ([Previously](https://www.benkuhn.net/skinner/), [previously](https://www.benkuhn.net/product/).) Can we do anything about this?

Unfortunately, “you’re the product” is a popular business model for a reason: businesses like Facebook would be really hard to support without them.

Facebook would be suicidal to charge its users money, because its entire selling point is that everyone uses it, and “everyone” _hates_ paying money. In the US, Facebook makes over $25 per person on ads ([source](https://www.statista.com/statistics/251328/facebooks-average-revenue-per-user-by-region/)). Can you imagine if instead of ads they tried to charge people $25 a year?

Even on the margin, anything that costs Facebook users also makes it less valuable for its remaining users—it’s a negative feedback loop. The same goes for any other site where users create value for other users, like Twitter or Craigslist or Yelp or Wikipedia. (It’s not an accident that these are some of the most stagnant popular websites!)

In fact, this is a fundamental problem with [network effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_effect) and low marginal costs. If a company wants to maintain a network effect, they need as many users as possible. If their marginal cost is low, then the easiest way to get users is to give the product away. To do that, they have to get paid by someone else. And when they start getting paid by someone else, they’ll inevitably start prioritizing that person’s interests.

Historically with other network-effect businesses, we’ve addressed this in a few different ways:

-   regulation (e.g. local utilities)

-   breakups (e.g. Bell)

-   standardization and interoperability (e.g. email, the Web, cryptocurrency)

So far for tech monopolies, people seem to be focused mostly on breakups—e.g. Facebook from Instagram/Whatsapp—but standardization seems to have produced much better outcomes in the past. (I like email and the Web a lot more than National Grid…) I’d be interested to see more exploration of that option!
