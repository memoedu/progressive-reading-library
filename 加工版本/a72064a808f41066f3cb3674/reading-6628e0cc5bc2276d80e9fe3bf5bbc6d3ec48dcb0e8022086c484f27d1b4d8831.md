# 在中途设定目标｜Set Goals in the Middle

---

## 正文

经典的 goal-setting theory 认为，你先 set a goal，然后 make a plan 来 achieve 它，最后开始 taking action。最近，我开始怀疑这种 approach 是否明智。现在，我倾向于认为，对于某些类型的 efforts，在中途 setting goals 更好。

起初，你可能会疑惑，怎么可能在中途 set goals。毕竟，难道不是 goal 本身 define 了 project 吗？不过，一般而言，making a plan 有许多不同的 aspects，其中一些未必是开始时就严格必需的。

任何 goal 或 project 通常都具有以下 qualities：

1.  一个 general ambition 或 motivation。（例如，get in shape、learn French）
2.  一个 specific target。（例如，lose 15 pounds、speak fluently）
3.  一个 time-frame 或 deadline。（例如，在 6 months 内）
4.  Constraints 或 methods。（例如，通过 eating fewer calories、每天 practicing）
5.  对所需 effort/time 的 Overall impression。（例如，每周几小时的 moderate effort）

这些只是 basics。一个 plan 还可以有更多 details：milestones、schedules、metrics、accountability systems、coaches，等等。

从这个想法可以清楚地看出，goal 实际上不是单一的东西，而是一组往往被 bundled together 的不同 features。其中一些可能是朝某个特定 direction 进行任何 voluntary action 都必需的，另一些则是 optional。事实上，除非你是一个非常 systematic 的 goal-setter，否则你很可能一直都在 undertake projects，而上面的一些 elements 并不齐全。

在妥当地 setting goals 时，我们需要回答的问题是：所有这些 elements 应该在什么时候到位？有些人主张，所有这些 elements 都应该在 action 开始之前到位。我想主张，对于 goals 的某个 subset，推迟 finalizing 它们或许更好。

## 哪些 Goals 应该在中途才开始？

我第一次无意间采用这种在中途 setting goals 的 process，是在与 Vat 一起 learn languages 的[我的 project](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/the-year-without-english-2/) 期间。我们的 plan 中有许多 aspects 已经 fixed：time-frame（在每个国家待三个月）、method（不 speaking English，进行一些 study），以及所需的 effort（接近 full-time，在可行范围内尽可能多地投入）。

我们没有 fix 的是 specific target。说实话，我们并不确定自己会 reach 什么 level，所以也就没有特意据此 make a specific goal（比如 aiming for conversational fluency，或者 pass 某个 language exam）。

最初，我觉得，与更典型的先 setting the goal 的 method 相比，这可能是一种 disadvantage。我当时就是这样处理 [MIT Challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/mit-challenge-2/) 的：在开始之前 setting 一个 ambitious target。

不过，经历这个 process 之后，我发现它比典型的 approach 好得多。在中途 Setting a goal，让人可以在掌握 information 时 fine-tune challenge level 和 expectations。我在 China 时也采用了同样的 approach，在停留结束时参加 [HSK 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanyu_Shuiping_Kaoshi) exam。重要的是，我是在来到 China 一个半月之后才作出这个决定的，那时我已经能够 anticipate 自己的 progress，并 decide 什么 level 是 achievable 的。

这提示了一条可能的 rule of thumb：当一个 goal 对于在某个特定 time-frame 内能够 reach 什么 achievable 的 level 存在 high uncertainty 时，最好在 process 的中途 set the specific target，而不是在一开始。

这并不意味着不能进行任何 planning，而只是说，你应当用自己最能 control 的 variables 来 plan：overall direction、time-frame、level of effort、strategies 和 constraints。

## 推迟 Goal-Setting 的更多理由

到目前为止，我一直在主张，highly-uncertain goals 应当在 efforts 的中途才 set，以便更 productively 地 set 正确的 challenge level，从而 maximize effort。不过，最近我看到一些 research，提示可能还有另一个理由。

goal-setting literature 相当一致地认为，goal-setting improves performance，difficult goals 通常比 easy 的 goals 效果更好，而且 feedback 是有益的，这些都在人们的意料之中。我觉得有趣的是，这种 trend 也有 counter-examples，尤其是在 individual tasks 的 level 上。（在[这里](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0749597887900458)阅读完整 paper。）

一些 research 表明，对于特别 complex 的 tasks，goal-setting 可能 reduce effectiveness。其 reasoning 是，complex tasks 需要你全部的 cognitive resources。当你 monitor 自己的 performance 时，这同样需要 cognitive resources。额外的 load 可能 impair 你的 performance。（完整 study 请点击[这里](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232524393_Ackerman_PL_Motivation_and_Cognitive_Abilities_An_IntegrativeAptitude-Treatment_Interaction_Approach_to_Skill_Acquisition_Journal_of_Applied_Psychology_74_657-690)。）

这意味着，当 subject 或 skill 相对 unknown 时，learning projects 会遭遇双重打击。这些 projects 存在 high-uncertainty，因此很容易 set 过大或过小的 goals。其次，这些 projects 在一开始是极其 cognitively demanding 的，而且常常令人沮丧，因为你无法 perform adequately。在这种情况下 Setting goals，如果你在 learning 时还惦记着那些 goals，就可能进一步 impede progress。

## 这 Apply 于所有 Goals 吗？

我认为，setting specific targets 这种 standard approach 在相对 known 的 domains 中 works well，因为在这些 domains 中，过去的 accomplishments 可以用作未来 success 的 benchmarks。这些 goals 更 straightforward，而且很可能从第一天起，就能受益于 hard target 所带来的更高 precision 和 motivation。

在 task-level 上，一旦你的 skill 变得更 mature，setting specific goals 很可能就会更有用。Goal-setting 是 deliberate practice 的一个重要 component：此时，一个人已经 achieved adequate performance，而现在的 goal 是 continued improvement。不过，在完全陌生、可能让你不堪重负的 areas 中，它们可能是 counterproductive 的。

如果你发现自己在问，自己究竟怎么可能知道什么是 achievable 的，这就是一个很好的信号，说明你应该等一等，再 set a specific target。相反，先让 effort 和 timeline 保持 constrained。对大多数人来说，这些通常有少得多的 uncertainty，因此更容易 keep up a goal，既不会 coasting（因为它太 easy），也不会 giving up（因为它太 hard）。

## 原文

Classic goal-setting theory says that you first set a goal, then you make a plan to achieve it and finally you start taking action. Recently, I’ve begun to question the wisdom of this approach. Now, I’m inclined to believe that, for certain types of efforts, you’re better of setting goals in the middle.

At first you might be wondering how it’s even possible to set goals in the middle. After all, doesn’t the goal define the project itself? But in general, there’s a lot of different aspects to making a plan, and some of them may not be strictly necessary to begin.

Any goal or project will usually have the following qualities:

1.  A general ambition or motivation. (e.g. get in shape, learn French)
2.  A specific target. (e.g. lose 15 pounds, speak fluently)
3.  A time-frame or deadline. (e.g. in 6 months)
4.  Constraints or methods. (e.g. by eating fewer calories, practicing every day)
5.  Overall impression of effort/time required. (e.g. a few hours per week of moderate effort)

That’s just the basics. A plan can have a lot more details: milestones, schedules, metrics, accountability systems, coaches, etc..

It’s clear from this idea that a goal really isn’t one thing, but a bunch of different features that tend to get bundled together. Some of these are likely necessary for any kind of voluntary action in a particular direction, but some are optional. In fact, unless you’re a very systematic goal-setter, you likely undertake projects all the time with some of the above elements missing.

The question we need to answer in setting goals properly is when should all of these elements be in place. Some argue that all of these elements should be in place before action begins. I’d like to argue that, for a subset of goals, it might be better to postpone finalizing them.

## Which Goals Should You Start in the Middle?

Listen to this article  

The first time I stumbled into this process of setting goals in the middle was during [my project](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/the-year-without-english-2/) with Vat to learn languages. Many aspects of our plan were fixed: the time-frame (three months in each country), method (not speaking English, some study) and effort required (nearly full-time, as much as feasible).

What we didn’t fix was a specific target. We honestly weren’t sure of what level we would reach, so we didn’t bother to make a specific goal out of it (say aiming for conversational fluency or to pass a particular language exam).

Initially I thought this might be a disadvantage over the more typical method of setting the goal first. That was how I handled the [MIT Challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/mit-challenge-2/), setting an ambitious target before starting.

After going through this process though, I found it much better than the typical approach. Setting a goal in the middle allows one to fine-tune the challenge level and expectations when you have information. I applied this same approach when in China to undertake the [HSK 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanyu_Shuiping_Kaoshi) exam at the end of my stay. Importantly, I only made this decision after a month and a half in China, where I could anticipate my progress and decide what level was achievable.

This suggests a possible rule of thumb: when a goal has high uncertainty as to what level is achievable to reach within a particular time-frame, it’s better to set the specific target in the middle of the process, not at the beginning.

This doesn’t mean no planning can occur, simply that you plan with the variables you have the most control over: overall direction, time-frame, level of effort, strategies and constraints.

## More Reasons to Postpone Goal-Setting

So far I’ve been arguing that highly-uncertain goals should be set in the middle of efforts so as to more productively set the correct challenge level to maximize effort. However, recently I’ve come across research that suggests there might be another reason too.

The goal-setting literature is fairly unanimous that goal-setting improves performance, that difficult goals typically do better than easy ones and that feedback is good, all things one would expect. What I found interesting was that there are counter-examples to this trend, particularly at the level of individual tasks. (Read the full paper [here](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0749597887900458).)

Some research shows that for particularly complex tasks, goal-setting can reduce effectiveness. The reasoning is that complex tasks require your full cognitive resources. When you monitor your performance, this too requires cognitive resources. The added load can impair your performance. (For the full study, click [here](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/232524393_Ackerman_PL_Motivation_and_Cognitive_Abilities_An_IntegrativeAptitude-Treatment_Interaction_Approach_to_Skill_Acquisition_Journal_of_Applied_Psychology_74_657-690).)

This suggests a double-whammy for learning projects when the subject or skill is relatively unknown. These projects suffer from high-uncertainty, therefore it’s very easy to set goals way too large or too little. Second, these projects are incredibly cognitively demanding in the beginning, and are frequently frustrating because you can’t perform adequately. Setting goals in this setting may further impede progress if you’re mindful of those goals while learning.

## Does This Apply to All Goals?

I think the standard approach of setting specific targets works well in relatively known domains, where past accomplishments can be used as benchmarks for future success. These goals are more straightforward and likely benefit from the increased precision and motivation that a hard target can create from the first day.

On a task-level, setting specific goals are likely to be more useful once your skill has become more mature. Goal-setting is an important component of deliberate practice, when one has already achieved adequate performance and now the goal is continued improvement. However, they may be counterproductive in completely new areas where you may get overwhelmed.

If you find yourself asking how you can possibly know what’s achievable for you, that’s a good sign you should wait to set a specific target. Keep the effort and timeline constrained instead. For most people, these usually have a lot less uncertainty, and so it’s easier to keep up a goal without coasting (because it was too easy) or giving up (because it was too hard).
