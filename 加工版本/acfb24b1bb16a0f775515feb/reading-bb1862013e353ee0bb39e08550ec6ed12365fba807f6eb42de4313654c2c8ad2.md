# 《The One-Human Business: How To Earn In The Age of AI》

这段话过去一年一直在我脑海里挥之不去：

> 我和几位科技公司 CEO 朋友的小群聊里，有个赌池，赌第一家单人十亿美元公司会在哪一年出现。没有 AI 的话，这原本是难以想象的事，而现在它将会发生。
>
> – Sam Altman

几年前，我开始注意到有些人已经建立起 \$1 to \$1000 万规模的单人 business。这些人主要是以教育为基础的 creators，通过 social media 吸引 audience，销售 coaching 和 courses 的组合（因为 tech 让一个人能够做到这些——建立 audience 不花钱，创造信息也不花钱；与 e-commerce 或 freelance/agency work 等其他 business models 相比，一个人可以用很少的时间投入完成这两件事）。

但现在情况不同了。大不一样。

如今，一个人能做的事比去年多得多，而普通人完全没有意识到这个机会，或者他们只是因为自己喜欢的名人这么说，就觉得“AI 不好”。

这件事的美妙之处在于，_你不需要赚到接近 \$1 billion, or even \$100 万，就能过上很棒的生活。_

大多数人只想用收入替代工资，能够负担一些小小的奢侈享受，而不必担心银行账户余额有多低。

我自己开始做整个 online business 这件事时，只是想每天中午都能吃得起 Chipotle。很感激地说，我们早已远远超过了这个目标。

从小到大，我就不想走默认的那条路。

我很留意身边的人，感觉大家的常态就是：为了十年后会毫无用处的教育去上学，找一份会让你产生依赖的工作，和一个你厌恶的人结婚，被承诺退休，却始终无法真正停止工作。这让我感到恶心。

所以我意识到，某种形式的 entrepreneurship 是唯一的出路。也是唯一能让我掌控的不只是自己的财务状况的道路。

于是我就这么做了。

我一次又一次地失败。做 Fitness YouTube channel、dropshipping minimalist wallets、卖 blue light glasses、做 freelance SEO、Facebook ads 和 web design，直到最后因为学生债务和信用卡债务越积越多，不得不找份工作。

我把那份工作看作一个 deadline。如果我不尽快做成，就会像其他人一样变得安于现状。

大约 7 年前，我终于辞掉那份工作，开始做 freelance web design。

大约 6 年前，我开始做 social media，用 digital products 赚了大约 $10,000。

第二年，\$100,000. Then $800,000。之后大约达到 \$4 million (my tax bill had officially quadrupled to what my old salary was). Then as I dove into software, my personal income stabilized around \$100 万到 200 万（税前），同时我开始建立更长期的事业。

关于这件单人 business 的事，我有很多想分享，尤其是现在 AI 给了会使用它的人巨大优势，让我能够做到比以前更多的事。

那么，我们开始吧。

### 你的 interests（unique value）+ Social media（free distribution）+ AI（cheap force multiplication）= 一门 profitable one-human business

作为一门 one-person business，你会在 social media 上发帖。

我知道，真扫兴。早上花 30 分钟写几句话，再发到全世界任何人都有可能看到的地方，实在是太难了。但眼下 attention 就在 social media 上，而你大概没有 capital 投入 paid ads，也负担不起付给 creators \$2500 让他们为你的 product 发一条 reel（没错，现在的价格就是这么离谱）。

如果你知道怎么把 content 做好，一条 content 就能免费传播给数百万人。对大多数人来说，你大概每个月能获得 100-500,000 次 impressions。你只需要让其中极小一部分人购买你的 product 或 service。

我也假设你不想开一家 service business，然后淹没在 client work 里。你想要某种形式的自由和 creative lifestyle。我个人更喜欢写 content、打造能在我睡觉时照常运行的 products。

接下来我会给你几个可以马上开始使用的“AI employees”，不过，了解这种 one-person business 由哪些部分组成会有所帮助。

**1) Brand - 你如何向世界展现自己或自己的 business。**

在你的个人生活中，人们想到你的名字时，会把什么和你联系起来？他们觉得你很酷吗？觉得和你相处很愉快吗？觉得你乐于助人、值得信赖吗？

Brand 就是这些，只不过对象是你的 business。就像一个 digital storefront。

它能让人们选择购买你的 app、course、product 或 service，而不是别人的，即使别人的 product 更差。它会影响你如何 marketing 你的 product，以及你面向谁做 marketing。过去 marketers 的收入是 indie programmers 的 10 倍，就是因为他们给了人们想要的东西。

在当今世界，作为一门 one-person business，你的 brand 很可能会是一个 _personal brand_。即便你不是 one-person business，也可以看看 Elon Musk、Mr. Beast 等人。只要你能做好，personal brand 就是最有效的 growth strategies 之一。

它是你的 values、worldview 和 story 的外化呈现。

我很快会帮你打造一个独特、无可替代的 brand，为你提供所有 content pillars。这会很值得。

**2) Offer - 人们付钱让你帮他们取得某种结果。**

你需要一个 product 或 service。

这很明显……没有它，你赚不到钱。

但 _offer_ 是人们认为那个 product 或 service 具有的价值。它是你对它的包装方式，也就是这笔 _deal_。这是你能学到的最赚钱的技能之一，因为一旦你把 offer 做好，landing page、copywriting、marketing 等等都会容易得多。

这才是你的竞争所在。不是你卖的 product，而是你提供的 deal，也就是价值相对于价格的多少。

你可以看看 1of10 这样的 tool，它很适合 YouTube research；但使用 Eden，你能获得面向 _所有_ platforms 的 research、AI usage、social scheduling、一整套 second brain 和 workspace，等等。如果你允许我稍微自夸一下，选择显而易见。

Offer creation 就是调动 4 个 levers 的艺术。

1.  **The dream outcome** - 客户真正想要什么

2.  **The delivery mechanism** - 你如何带他们实现目标（course、coaching、supplement 等）

3.  **The speed of results** - 你能多快带他们实现目标

4.  **The guarantee** - 如果失败，会发生什么

我会给你一个可以聊天的 AI，帮你了解 offer creation，并打造一个让人无法拒绝的 offer。

**3) Distribution - 你如何传播自己的 brand 和 offer。**

> 先建立 distribution，然后想做什么就做什么。
>
> – Jack Butcher

我们生活在 internet age，在这里 attention = leverage。

你可以拥有最好的 brand，也可以拥有最好的 product，但如果没人知道你的存在，这些都毫无意义。

就我们这里讨论的内容而言，Distribution 涵盖了从 marketing、sales、content、ads 到 landing page 和 website 的一切。

它就是人们如何找到你、了解你并付钱给你（这就是“funnel”）。

举例来说，你是通过 social media content 找到我的。你正在 Substack 或 X 上通过 email 阅读这篇内容。如果我能充分展现我的知识的价值，你也许会逐渐信任我，最终使用 [Eden](https://eden.so)（最棒的 content research/creation tool），或者报名参加[￼Creator Bootcamp](https://eden.so/bootcamp)。不是因为我把它推销给你，而是因为你认为它对实现自己的目标有用。

眼下，小型 digital business 获取 attention 的方式就是 social media。你不需要数百万次 views，也能接近替代你目前的收入。

Social media 可以免费开始，而 content 是无需许可的 leverage。

我们把事情简化一下。

要开启这个 one-person business，你每天的 routine 是这样的：

**每天在选定的 short-form platform 上 Post 1-3 times。** Substack、X、LinkedIn、Instagram 等都可以。选一个你最有感觉的平台。它们都行。先从一个 short-form platform 开始。把它做好。等你积累了一批表现不错的 ideas 后，把这些 ideas 用到其他 platforms 上，更快地扩大影响力。

**每周 Post 1 long-form piece。Substack 或 X。** 大概选 Substack，因为你的 followers 是 email subscribers。如果你的 main platform 是 X，那也把 article cross post 到那里。

花 30 minutes 写 content，再花 30 minutes 写 long-form piece 的一个 section。说真的，你每天只需要 1 hour。

**每天 Promote 你的 offer 一次。** 把它放进 long-form piece 里。在当天表现最好的 post 下方的 reply 中链接到它。如果你不让人们购买你的 product，显然就赚不到钱。

就我个人而言，我喜欢在 newsletter/article 中 Promote，然后在一条 short-form post 的 reply 里链接到那篇 article——这样人们就会 subscribe 我的 newsletter；如果他们读了，就更有可能购买我的 offer。

当然，这件事还有不少细节，但这就是基础。

你得经历相当多的 trial and error，才能让自己的 posts 开始获得 traction；根据 platform 去 research 和尝试各种 growth strategies 也许是明智的（别只试一次，失败了就哭诉它不管用。）

现在，有几件事可以让 AI 来做。

*   **Brand Strategist** - 制定 personal brand strategy，让你不会和其他人混在一起；并研究你的 content 表现如何，以提出 growth tactics。

*   **Content Strategist** - Research social media 上哪些内容表现出色，了解你之前的所有 content，将它们与你的 brand strategy 配合起来，然后给你提供一周的 ideas 供你发布（或者按你的喜好先 draft content，再由你编辑）。

*   **Long-Form Specialist** - 了解 long-form frameworks 和 retention strategies，帮助你用 _你的 ideas_ 以结构良好的方式 outline newsletter、article 或 video script。

*   **Short-Form Specialist** - 将你的粗略 ideas 转化为 post drafts，供你 review、edit 并安排在所有 platforms 上发布。

*   **Offer Consultant** - 帮助你把自己的经验、兴趣和专业知识转化为 compelling offer，并帮你决定从哪种 product 或 service 开始。

*   **Marketing Strategist** - 获取你的 offer、brand strategy 和 customer avatar，然后帮你生成 social promotions、email promotions 和 landing page copy。

### 如何创建自己的 AI team（一般而言，使用 AI 的最佳方式）

这一节会让不少人失去兴趣。

因为尽管我们在谈 AI，但在一个追逐廉价 dopamine 的世界里，这个 process 并不容易。要理解并落实它，需要一定程度的 systems thinking。

AI 的问题在于，人们把它想成某种全能的存在。

而既然它并非全能，当它没有完全照着他们的想法去做时（因为他们自己也不知道想要什么），他们就会认定它只会生成 slop。

昨天，我看到有个人恳求企业不要用 ChatGPT 或 AI 创建网站，因为它“看起来很糟糕”，而且你一眼就能看出那是 slop。

如果你真的用过 AI，就会觉得这很可笑。

说得没错……如果你只是让它一次性生成整个网站，此后再也不花功夫。但如果你给它 examples 和清晰的 instructions，至少就有了一个更好的起点。之后，你得开始深入细节。你得让它调整 border radius、drop shadows、padding、column layout，以及你原本就会在 Figma、Photoshop、Webflow 或 Framer 中处理的其他一切。process 并没有改变，只是那个装满旋钮和数字的 toolbar 变成了 natural language。

如今，告诉 AI“创建一个网站”“写一篇 newsletter”或“写一条 viral tweet”，就像过去套用 Wordpress template。人们看得出来。过去的 slop 一点也不比现在少，但 AI 却成了一个人们 _真的_ 讨厌的特殊案例。

你可以就此打住，做出一个相当糟糕、丝毫无法让你的 brand 脱颖而出的网站；也可以把它当作一个 _starting point_，继续 iterate，就像你可以 build 一个 app，然后花上好几周不断解决问题，直到它真正成为一个好 app。

人们眼中现代 Wordpress template 的 baseline 会不断提高，迫使人们要么培养 taste 和 experience，要么只能默认被视为平庸。

这也是我对用 AI 写作的看法正在改变的部分原因，因为如果你像真正写作时那样对待它，就会发现自己能拥有更多 control、更多 creativity，以及更多机会去创作真正独特、此前无人想到过的东西。但人们会说“像我一样写这篇 newsletter”，却指望它考虑到自己真正写作时做出的几百甚至几千个决定……显然，AI 不会凭空把那些决定从你脑子里提取出来，再完全照你的想法去做，因为你根本没告诉它要这么做。

它听起来像 AI slop，只是因为那个人 _选择_ 以那种状态发布，而且没有足够的 taste 察觉问题，也没有足够的知识继续 iterate，直到它变成只有他们自己才能创作的东西。

说到这里，你究竟该怎么做？

我们怎么才能创建一个真正优秀的 weekly content strategist？

**1) 你需要知道好内容是什么样的，或者有一个好内容的例子**

说到“好”的 content，有很多因素在起作用。

有 post、article 或 newsletter 的 structure；有 creator 的 vibe 或 energy signature；有贯穿其 brand 的主线，以及他们持续融入其中、用来传达 worldview 的 big ideas；还有他们偶然发现的、能帮助自己脱颖而出的 unique ideas。这些都需要纳入考量。

AI 不会告诉你这些。你只能通过经验，或向拥有这类具体知识的人请教来发现（这就是为什么读这篇文章会让你略占优势）。

但现在你既然知道自己需要这些，就得把 _reference、example 或 instructions_ 提供给 AI，因为它不知道如何独自完成。

这意味着，你要提供能代表自己想创作的 content 类型的 YouTube titles 和 video transcripts。这也意味着 reels transcripts、Substack notes 或 articles、X posts 或 articles，或大量你喜欢的写作风格 examples。

对于 web design，这通常就意味着在你逐步制作页面的各个 section 时，给 AI 大量 screenshots，或者详细写出你想要什么。

我几乎每次开始 AI 或 agent chat 时都是这么做的，除非只是问个简单问题。

如果我知道自己想要什么，就会给出详细的 instructions。我写过 2000 word prompts，教它像我一样写 tweets，结果非常接近。光让它看看我最好的 tweets，你是做不到这一点的，因为它会试图把那些 tweets 套成 template，也不会考虑写下它们时经历的 _thinking_ process。

**如果我不知道自己想要什么**，就会开始 research 和提问，直到我弄清楚，并用正确的信息填充 context。

也就是说，我不会只说“给我写一篇 newsletter”。

我会提出问题，帮助自己判断一篇好 newsletter 应该是什么样。

就你而言，你可以这样问：“我想写出世界上最出色、最吸引人的 newsletter 之一。要与众不同，听起来也不机械。我们把这当作一次 brainstorming session，直到找到我喜欢的风格。先问我一些 clarifying questions，了解你需要我提供什么，才能做到这一点。我们再接着往下聊。”

谈话结束时，让它把所有内容浓缩成一份 document。

这样，你就有了一份随时能在其他 chats 中参考的 document。

**2) 你需要理解能带来结果的 process，或者先采用别人的 process，直到你形成自己的**

对于 content strategist 来说，这并不像“research 表现最好的 posts，再给我一些 ideas”那么简单。稍微高于平均水平的人会这么做。普通人则只会让 AI 写 viral posts。

我想自己的 process，大概是这样的：

*   读书、保存 ideas、和朋友交谈、听长篇讲座，从中寻找网上没人谈论的 ideas

*   选择一个 idea，它处于以下三者的交集：（1）我很想写的内容；（2）人们能从中获得巨大益处的内容；（3）已经得到验证且表现良好的内容

*   围绕 article/script 进行 title ideas 的 Brain dump，并找出最适合放进这篇作品的 ideas

*   考虑哪种 long-form structure 最适合承载这些 ideas，然后开始 outline

*   根据这个 outline，brainstorm 10-20 个 short-form content ideas，供我拿来发挥

这也考虑到我已经处理好了 branding 这一部分——那是完全不同的一件事，它能让 content strategist 理解我的 worldview、influences、mission、brand enemy 等，进一步打磨 ideas，让它们更有我的特色。

难点在于，如何把这些变成 AI 能帮上忙的 process。

显然，AI 不能替我读书，也不能替我 _discover_ ideas。这是无法外包的优势。

但我可以把所有这些内容收集到我的 [Eden library](https://eden.so) 中，它会自动为 ideas 添加 tags 并将它们连接起来；这样 AI 每周就能从这批 ideas 中提取内容，给我几个可能表现不错的选项。也就是说，我能把更多精力放在阅读和学习上，而不是把它们当作可以外包的事，因为我享受这个 process。

然后，我主要要做的是指导 AI 如何进行有效的 research，哪些 ideas 值得关注（名人 posts 的 viral 并不能说明什么在 social media 上表现良好。小到中型账号的 outlier posts 才是），如何将合适的 topics 与我自己的 novel ideas 匹配起来，以及提供我过去奏效的 content structures 示例。

然后，让它把这些内容输出成一张我可以使用的表格，你就有了类似 [Weekly Strategist AI](https://app.eden.so/ai/cb565512-8701-4273-838f-6cfc47ad65c9) 这样的东西，它每周都会 autopilot 运行。

**3) 你需要持续改进并迭代自己构建的 system，就像你一直以来都需要做的那样。**

我们在这里构建的是一个 system。

如果你曾用过 Notion template，或有过任何形式的 morning routine……你就会明白，有时 system 并不总是可靠的，或者你会超越它，或者某件事会打乱它，导致它不适用于特定情形。

但这并不意味着 system 完全不起作用，也不意味着你要彻底放弃它。这意味着你要迭代它，让它变得 adaptable。

对于 AI 来说，这意味着每当你注意到哪里出了问题，就告诉它问题所在。改进 system。提出解决方案，或通过交流，直到你们决定出更好的做法。

我觉得大多数人都太害怕 uncertainty 了。

他们无法理解这一点。

这也没关系；这些人需要走进 arena，在他们还不知道要外包什么之前，别再试图把一切都外包给 AI。

但对于理解这一点的人来说，你在当今世界能做到的事情确实是 revolutionary。

希望这对你有帮助，也希望这些 custom AIs 能为你所用。如果你运用了这封信中学到的东西，它们确实相当强大。

回头聊。

> ### Your interests (unique value) + Social media (free distribution) + AI (cheap force multiplication) = A profitable one-human business
>
> As a one-person business, you’re going to post on social media.
>
> Boooo, I know. It’s so hard to write a few words for 30 minutes in the morning and post it where everyone else in the world has the potential to see it. But social media is where the attention is right now, and you probably don’t have the capital to invest in paid ads or paying creators \$2500 to post one reel about your product (yes, the prices are that absurd nowadays).
>
> One piece of content can be spread to millions of people for free, if you understand how to make it good. For most people, you can probably get 100-500,000 impressions a month. All you need to do is get a tiny fraction of those people to buy your product or service.
>
> I’m also assuming that you don’t care to start a service business and drown in client work. You want some form of freedom and a creative lifestyle. I personally prefer writing content and building products that can run while I sleep.
>
> I’m going to give you a few “AI employees” you can just start using immediately below, but it helps to know what comprises this type of one-person business.
>
> **1) Brand - How you present yourself or your business to the world.**
>
> In your personal life, when people think of your name, what do they associate with you? Do they think you’re cool? Fun to be around? Helpful and trustworthy?
>
> Brand is that, but for your business. Like a digital storefront.
>
> It’s what makes people buy your app, course, product, or service over someone else’s, even if it’s a worse product. It’s what shapes how you market your product and who you market it to. This is how marketers made 10x more than indie programmers in the past, because they gave people what they wanted.
>
> As a one-person business, in today’s world, your brand is probably going to be a _personal brand._ And even if you aren’t a one-person business, look at Elon Musk, Mr. Beast, and the rest. A personal brand is one of the most effective growth strategies, if you can pull it off.
>
> It’s your values, worldview, and story externalized.
>

> **2) Offer - What people pay you for that helps them get a result.**
>
> You need a product or service.
>
> That’s obvious... you can’t make money without one.
>
> But an _offer_ is the perceived value of that product or service. It’s how you package it. It’s the _deal._ This is one of the most profitable skills you can learn, because once you nail the offer, the landing page, copywriting, marketing, and the rest become that much easier.
>
> That’s what you compete on. Not the product you’re selling, the deal you’re offering. The amount of value compared to price.

>
> 2.  **The delivery mechanism** - how you get them there (course, coaching, supplement, etc)

> 3.  **The speed of results** - how fast you get them there

> ### The AI team that used to cost $500k per year
>
> Okay.
>
> Let’s simplify this.
>
> To start this one-person business, this is your daily routine:
>
> **Post 1-3 times a day on a short-form platform of choice.** Substack, X, LinkedIn, Instagram, etc. Pick the one you vibe with the most. They all work. Start with one short-form platform. Get good at it. And once you have a backlog of ideas that have done well, take those and grow faster on other platforms.
>
> **Post 1 long-form piece a week. Substack or X.** Probably Substack because your followers are email subscribers. If your main platform is X, then cross post the article there too.
>
> Write content for 30 minutes and a section of your long-form piece for 30 minutes. 1 hour a day is really all you need.
>
> **Promote your offer once a day.** Plug it inside your long-form piece. Link to it in a reply under your best post for that day. If you don’t ask people to buy your product, you obviously aren’t going to make any money.
>
> Personally, I like to promote in my newsletter/article, then link to that article as a reply to one of my short-form posts - that way people subscribe to my newsletter, and if they read it, they’re more likely to buy my offer.
>
> There’s a bit more to this of course, but this is the base.
>
> You will have to go through quite a bit of trial and error until you start gaining traction on your posts, and it may be wise to research and experiment with various growth strategies depending on the platform (don’t just try one, fail, and cry because it didn’t work.)
>
> Now, there are a few things we can have AI do here.

> *   **Brand Strategist** - Create a personal brand strategy so you don’t blend in with everyone else, and study how your content is doing to propose growth tactics.
>

>
> *   **Offer Consultant** - Helps take your experience, interests, and expertise and turn it into a compelling offer, and helps you decide between what product or service to start with.

> ### How to create your own AI team (the best way to use AI in general)
>
> This section is going to lose quite a few people.
>
> Because even though we’re talking about AI, it’s a difficult process in a world of cheap dopamine seekers. It takes a certain level of systems thinking to understand and implement.
>
> The problem with AI is that people think of it as some kind of all powerful being.
>
> And since it’s not an all powerful being, when it doesn’t do exactly what they want (because they don’t know what they want), they just assume it’s only capable of generating slop.
>
> Yesterday, I saw some guy was begging businesses to not create their website with ChatGPT or AI because it “looks bad” and you can tell it’s slop.
>
> If you actually use AI, you find this laughable.
>
> It’s true... if you just have it one-shot the entire website and put no effort in beyond that. But if you give it examples and clear instructions, you’re off to at least a better start. After that, you have to start digging into the details. You have to tell it to tweak the border radius, the drop shadows, the padding, the column layout, and everything else you would already do in Figma, Photoshop, Webflow, or Framer. The process hasn’t changed, but the toolbar filled with knobs and numbers has turned into natural language.
>
> Telling AI to “create a website” or “write a newsletter” or “write a viral tweet” today is like using a Wordpress template in the past. People can tell. There was just as much slop in the past as there was today, but AI is some special case that people _really_ dislike.
>
> You can stop there and have a pretty terrible website that does not make your brand stand out in the slightest, or you can use that as a _starting point_ and iterate further, just like you can build an app and resolve the issues for weeks on end until it’s actually a good app.
>
> The baseline for what is considered a modern Wordpress template will continue to rise, forcing people to either develop taste and experience or default to being perceived as average.
>
> This is part of why my opinion around writing with AI is changing, because if you treated it like you did when you actually write, you’d realize you’d have more control, more creativity, and more opportunity to create something truly unique that nobody has even thought to do before. But people say “write this newsletter like me” and expect it to account for the hundreds, if not thousands of decisions they make when actually writing... obviously the AI isn’t just going to pull those out of your head and do exactly what you want, because you didn’t tell it to do that.
>
> It only sounds like AI slop because the person _chose_ to post it in that state, and doesn’t have the taste to notice it or the knowledge to iterate until it becomes something only they could create.
>
> With that out of the way, how do you actually do this?
>
> How can we create something like a weekly content strategist that’s actually good?
>
> **1) You need to know what good looks like, or have an example of what good looks like**
>
> When it comes to “good” content, there’s a lot at play.
>
> There’s the structure of the post, article, or newsletter. There’s the creator’s vibe or energy signature. There’s the throughline of their brand and big ideas they incorporate consistently to get their worldview across. And there’s the unique ideas they stumble across that help them stand out from everyone else. All of this needs to be accounted for.
>
> No AI is going to tell you that. You can only find it through experience or mentorship from someone who has that specific knowledge (that’s why reading this gives you a slight advantage).
>
> But now that you know you need those things, you need the _reference, example, or instructions_ to give to the AI because it doesn’t know how to do it on it’s own.
>
> That means YouTube titles and transcripts of videos that represent the type of content you want to create. It means reels transcripts, Substack notes or articles, X posts or articles, or ample examples of the style of writing you like.
>
> For web design, this usually just means giving the AI a ton of screenshots as you work through each section of the page you’re creating, or writing out in detail what you want.
>
> That’s how I start almost every AI or agent chat that isn’t just a simple question.
>
> If I know what I want, then I give detailed instructions. I’ve written 2000 word prompts teaching it to write tweets like I do, and it gets extremely close. You wouldn’t be able to do that just by asking it to look at my best tweets, because it will try to templatize those and doesn’t account for the _thinking_ process that went into writing them.
>
> **If I don’t know what I want**, then I start researching and asking questions until I have clarity and fill the context with the right information.
>
> Meaning, I don’t just say “write me a newsletter.”
>
> I ask questions that help me decide what a good newsletter looks like.
>
> In your case, you could ask something like, “I want to write one of the greatest, most engaging newsletters in the world. Something that stands out and doesn’t sound robotic. Let’s treat this as a brainstorming session until we land on a style that I like. Start by asking clarifying questions on what you need from me to make this happen. We’ll go from there.”
>
> At the end of that conversation, ask it to condense everything into one document.
>
> Now you have a document you can reference in other chats at any time.
>
> **2) You need to understand the process that gets results, or start with someone else’s process until you develop your own**
>
> For a content strategist, this isn’t as simple as “research top performing posts and give me ideas.” That’s what the slightly above-average person would do. The average person would just ask the AI to write viral posts.
>
> When I think of my own process, it’s something like this:
>
> *   Read books, save ideas, have conversations with friends, listen to long lectures for ideas that nobody is talking about online
>
> *   Pick an idea at the intersection of (1) what I am excited to write about (2) what people will massively benefit from and (3) what is already validated and performing well
>
> *   Brain dump title ideas for the article/script, and find the best ideas that would fit into that piece
>
> *   Consider which long-form structure would be the best to house those ideas and start outlining it
>
> *   From that outline, brainstorm 10-20 short-form content ideas that I can take and run with
>
> This is also considering I have the branding side of things down, which is an entirely different story - that’s what would allow the content strategist to understand my worldview, influences, mission, brand enemy, and more that would further refine the ideas and make them unique to me.
>
> The difficult part is turning that into a process that AI can help with.
>
> It obviously can’t read books for me or _discover_ ideas for me. That’s the edge that can’t be outsourced.
>
> But I can capture all of this into my [Eden library](https://eden.so), which auto-tags and connects ideas, so the AI can pull from that bank of ideas each week to give me a few options that are likely to do well. Meaning, I can focus _more_ on reading and learning rather than treating it as something to outsource, because I enjoy that process.
>
> Then, the main thing I need to do is instruct the AI on how to perform good research, what ideas are worthwhile (celebrity posts that went viral aren’t a signal as to what’s performing on social media. Small to mid-size account’s outlier posts are), how to match the right topics to my own novel ideas, and examples of my own content structures that have worked in the past.
>
> Then you have it spit that out into a table I can work from and you have something like the [Weekly Strategist AI](https://app.eden.so/ai/cb565512-8701-4273-838f-6cfc47ad65c9) that runs on autopilot every week.
>
> **3) You need to continuously improve and iterate the system you build, as you’ve always needed to** do.
>
> We are building a system here.
>
> And if you’ve ever used a Notion template or had any form of morning routine... you understand that sometimes a system isn’t always reliable, or you outgrow it, or something throws a wrench in it and it doesn’t work for that specific situation.
>
> But that isn’t a reason to think that the system doesn’t work altogether. And it doesn’t mean that you abandon it entirely. It means you iterate and make it adaptable.
>
> With AI, this means that whenever you notice something wrong, you tell it what’s wrong. You improve the system. You propose a solution or converse until you decide on a better way of doing it.
