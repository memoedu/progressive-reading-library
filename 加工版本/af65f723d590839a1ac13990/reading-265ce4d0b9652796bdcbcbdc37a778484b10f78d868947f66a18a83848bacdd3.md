# Against civilizational inadequacy

---

## 正文

Louie Helm 最近因为[“Asparagus Metabolizes Into 的 Neurotoxin 比 PCP 的 Powerful 程度高出 Over 5x”](http://rockstarresearch.com/asparagus-metabolizes-into-neurotoxin-over-5x-more-powerful-than-pcp/)而担心起来。Eliezer 读了他的帖子后，[也相当担心](https://www.facebook.com/yudkowsky/posts/10153417840014228?pnref=story)：

> 天哪。除非有人 debunks 这件事，否则 asparagus 就永远不会出现在我的菜单上了。说真的，太吓人了。

（值得肯定的是，Eliezer 在最初发帖约 45 分钟后，就在评论中认可了 James Wu 的一次 successful debunk。）

Eliezer 最喜欢的话题之一是“civilizational inadequacy”——大致是这样一种 idea：到处都有大量 _huge gaping problems_，“任何 sane civilization”都会立即 fix 它们，但由于 civilizational inadequacy，我们仍然存在这些问题。（某种 gestalt overview 见[这里](http://yudkowsky.tumblr.com/post/81447230971/my-april-fools-day-confession)，其他提及见[这里](https://intelligence.org/2014/10/16/agi-outcomes-civilizational-competence/)或[这里](http://yudkowsky.tumblr.com/writing/inexploitability)。）

civilization 是 inadequate 这一 observation 显然在一定程度上是 correct 的：有时人们会做蠢事，有时 large groups of people 比其 parts 的 maximum 还要愚蠢。这并没有什么新鲜的（或 controversial 的）。我不赞同的是 _acting on 那些 heavily informed by civilizational inadequacy 的 priors_。Civilization 或许是 inadequate，但很多时候，它仍比任何抱怨它的 individual person 更 adequate。

这场 asparagus 恐慌就是一个明显的 example。只有对 civilizational inadequacy 抱有相当 extreme 的 belief，才会在听到这样的 allegation——每次你吃一份 asparagus，你的 body 都一直在 metabolizing 相当于 four typical doses of PCP 的 neurotoxin——时，完全不产生以下疑问：

-   为什么相当于 two strong doses of PCP 的东西没有任何你实际能注意到的 side effects

-   为什么那么多写过 asparagine 被 metabolized into DMSO 的人，没有一个发出警报

-   为什么你 cited 的那些关于 DMSO neurotoxicity 的 papers，没有一篇探讨它们对于那些并未 experiencing medical-level dosages of highly-concentrated DMSO 的人所受 effects 有何 implications

这些顾虑中的任何一个，或者对 civilization 实际上可能是 adequate 的任何希望，都应当促使人在 publishing 前带着一点 skepticism 去 check sources 和 math；到那时，你就会发现 James Wu 在评论中提到的所有出色论点。

### 附录：James Wu 对该文章的 debunking

> 我对这篇文章的质量非常失望。它读起来像是 clickbait fearmongering，虽然至少 cites sources，但对 sources 的理解实在欠缺。作者似乎把 describing local concentrated doses 的 effects 的 studies 与 overall dose 混为一谈，又把 in vitro studies 与一般意义上的 food toxicology 混为一谈。
> 
> Article #1，“universal solvent DMSO 的 Unexpected low-dose toxicity”，describes 向 eye 中 inject 5 μl 的 1% DMSO 时会发生什么，并观察由此对 retina 造成的 damage。把_这件事_ Describing 为“然而，近年来，researchers 已经 found，低至 0.000015mg 的 DMSO 就足以 permanently destroy rats 中的 neurons”，是……真的、真的、真的很糟糕的 science journalism。
> 
> 首先，DMSO 在 eye 中的 toxicity 总体上是 well-known 的，并不是什么突然的 discovery（[http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/1124535](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/1124535)）。刻意不如实称之为 retinal damage，却说成“destroy neurons”，连带引入这个说法所具有的所有 generalized connotations，往好了说也是 disingenuous。
> 
> 而且，请注意关键所在。1% DMSO！这项 study 并没有像文章的叙述似乎暗示的那样，find 如果 rat ingested 那 5 μl 会发生什么；他们是把那种 1% solution 直接 injected 到 eye 中！然后 surveyed retinal damage！拿 local cells 在被 injected 以 1% concentration 的 undiluted \[chemical of choice\] 时会 die 这一 fact，来 argue 绝不应该吃 \[food containing chemical\]，这种 fearmongering 足以让 Food Babe 感到骄傲。
> 
> 例如，aspartame 的 safety profile 是 food additive 历史上研究最充分的话题之一。到目前为止，支持其 safety 的 evidence 比大多数 over-the-counter drugs 的还要好。Aspartame 会 metabolizes into methanol。难道我们应该根据把 methanol inject 到 eye 中时会发生什么，来 judge aspartame 的 safety 吗？
> 
> article #1 的 conclusion 并没有说“researchers 建议对 DMSO-containing substances 在 humans 中的 toxicity 开展更多 research”；如果他们真的认为它有 human toxicity implications，就肯定会这样说。它说的反而是“compute absolute DMSO final concentrations，并在 DMSO vehicle control 之外 include 一个 untreated control group，以 check for solvent toxicity。”因为它的 relevant 之处在这里：researchers 在 lab settings 中 using DMSO 来 carry 其他 compounds。而不是像文章暗示的那样，在一般意义上的 food toxicology 中。
> 
> 最后，0.000015mg 是从哪里来的？我得不出那个 number。完全得不出（[https://www.wolframalpha.com/input/…](https://www.wolframalpha.com/input/?i=5%20%CE%BCl%20*%201.1004%20g%2Fcm)）。
> 
> Article #2，“DMSO 在 Developing Central Nervous System 中 Produces Widespread Apoptosis”，同样 tests 浸在 0.5% 和 1.0% concentration 中的 in vitro cells。同样是高得离谱的 concentrations。（还记得要喝多少 alcohol，才能让你的 BAC 达到 0.08% 吗？你得直接喝下那个量的 6 倍的 DMSO solution，才能达到 0.5%。这跟“吃 asparagus”有点不一样吧？）。
> 
> 当然，那些都是非常 toxic 的 levels，在这些 levels 下很容易 assess cellular damage。但 low-dose toxicity 呢？嗯，这项 study 中 tested 的 lowest dose 是 0.3 ml/kg（99.9% pure DMSO）。注意，这里是每 /kg bodyweight。这意味着，即使 human toxicity profiles 与 rat toxicity profiles 是 identical 的（实际上，即使 accounted for body mass，humans 对 chemical exposure 的 tolerate 能力也远好于 rats），对于一个 8 lb 的婴儿，那仍然 equivalent to 超过 1mg 的 pure DMSO exposure，对于一个 grown adult，则是 24 mg 的 exposure。这可远不是被拿来 fearmongered 的 4mg DMSO dose；就连文章也承认，后者只是天真地 counting DMSO 的 total metabolite production，没有 taking into account 进一步的 metabolism 或 excretion，而非 toxicity 中真正重要的东西：某一时刻 body 内的 concentration。
> 
> 所以，基本上，吃下 500g 的 asparagus（那可是一 lb 的 asparagus！我一次可吃不了那么多，你呢？）可能 produce 4mg 的 _total sulfur-containing volatiles_，其中一小部分可能是 DMSO，而其中又只有一小部分可能在某一时刻 stay in the body，这却被拿来 fearmonger……什么呢，一项 evaluates rats 而非 humans 的 toxicity study，在 0.3ml/kg、也就是 equivalent to 24 mg 的 exposure 下，可能 cause 小到需要在 rat study 中才能 detected 的 _cellular apoptosis above background_？注意，这项 study 的作者完全没有提及 food，而是说需要关注的 population 是 exposed to DMSO、“接受 bone marrow transplantation”的儿童。
> 
> 最后，声称或暗示 asparagus 近年来变得更 toxic，因为如今的 asparagus 可能由于 sulfur-containing fertilizer 而变得更 neurotoxic，这有点荒唐。如果所暗示的是 sulfur fertilizer -> sulfur-containing compounds -> DMSO，那么请想想，早在 1700s，就已经有人描述 asparagus 会 leads to foul-smelling sulfur-containing urine 这件事（“asparagus……使 urine 带有 foetid smell”，“把我的夜壶变成一瓶香水”，“吃下几根 Asparagus 的 Stems，就会让我们的 Urine 带上 disagreable Odour” [http://www.smithsonianmag.com/…/why-asparagus-makes…/](http://www.smithsonianmag.com/ist/?next=/science-nature/why-asparagus-makes-your-urine-smell-49961252/)）。
> 
> 如果 sulfur metabolites 的 concentration 真有 increased，urine 难道不该更 foul-smelling 吗？
> 
> 而且，那也说明对为什么会使用 sulfur-based fertilizers 缺乏理解。它们之所以才被 INVENTED，就是因为一个世纪的 agriculture 导致 sulfur depletion，让东西都不肯 grow 了……

## 原文

Louie Helm recently got worried that [“Asparagus Metabolizes Into Neurotoxin Over 5x More Powerful Than PCP”](http://rockstarresearch.com/asparagus-metabolizes-into-neurotoxin-over-5x-more-powerful-than-pcp/). Eliezer read his post and [got pretty worried too](https://www.facebook.com/yudkowsky/posts/10153417840014228?pnref=story):

> Yikes. Until and unless someone debunks this, asparagus is permanently off my menu. Seriously, yikes.

(To Eliezer’s credit, he admitted a successful debunk from James Wu in the comments about 45 minutes after he initially posted.)

One of Eliezer’s favorite topics is “civilizational inadequacy”—roughly, the idea that there are tons of _huge gaping problems_ everywhere, that “any sane civilization” would fix immediately, but we still have them because civilizational inadequacy. (See [here](http://yudkowsky.tumblr.com/post/81447230971/my-april-fools-day-confession) for a sort of gestalt overview, or [here](https://intelligence.org/2014/10/16/agi-outcomes-civilizational-competence/) or [here](http://yudkowsky.tumblr.com/writing/inexploitability) for other mentions.)

The observation that civilization is inadequate is obviously correct to some degree: sometimes people do dumb things and sometimes large groups of people are dumber than the maximum of their parts. There’s nothing new (or controversial) there. My problem is with _acting on priors heavily informed by civilizational inadequacy_. Civilization may be inadequate, but quite a lot of the time it’s still more adequate than any individual person complaining about it.

The asparagus scare is an obvious example. It takes pretty extreme belief in civilizational inadequacy to hear the allegation that your body has been metabolizing the neurotoxin equivalent of four typical doses of PCP every time you eat a serving of asparagus, and not wonder anything like:

-   why the equivalent of two strong doses of PCP don’t have any side effects that you actually notice

-   why any of the numerous people who wrote about asparagine being metabolized into DMSO didn’t raise any alarms

-   why any of the papers you cited on DMSO neurotoxicity didn’t wonder about the implications for the effects of people not experiencing medical-level dosages of highly-concentrated DMSO

Any of these concerns, or any hope that civilization might actually be adequate, should compel one to check the sources and math with a bit of skepticism before publishing, at which point you’d find all the excellent points that James Wu mentioned in the comments.

### Appendix: James Wu’s debunking of the article

> I’m very disappointed in the quality of this article. It reads like clickbait fearmongering, and while it at least cites sources, its understanding of the sources is sorely lacking. The author seems to confuse studies describing effects of local concentrated doses with overall dose, and in vitro studies with food toxicology in general.
> 
> Article #1, “Unexpected low-dose toxicity of the universal solvent DMSO”, describes what happens when you inject 5 μl of 1% DMSO into the eye, and seeing resulting damage to the retina. Describing _that_ as “However, in recent years, researchers have found that as little as 0.000015mg of DMSO is enough to permanently destroy neurons in rats” is… really, really, really bad science journalism.
> 
> For one, the toxicity of DMSO in the eye in general is well-known, and is not some sudden discovery ([http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/1124535](http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/1124535)). To specifically not call retinal damage as-is, and instead say “destroy neurons” with all the generalized connotations that it carries, is disingenuous at best.
> 
> And notice the key point. 1% DMSO! The study did not find what would happen if those 5 μl were ingested by the rat, as the article’s writeup seems to imply; they injected that 1% solution directly to the eye! Then surveyed for retinal damage! Using the fact that local cells die when being injected with 1% concentration of undiluted \[chemical of choice\] to argue that \[food containing chemical\] should never be eaten is such fearmongering as to make the Food Babe proud.
> 
> The safety profile of aspartame, for example, is one of the most well-studied topics in food additive history. At this point the evidence for its safety is better than most over-the-counter drugs. Aspartame metabolizes into methanol. Should we judge the safety of aspartame based on what happens when we inject methanol into the eye?
> 
> The conclusion of article #1 doesn’t say “researchers suggest more research on the toxicity of DMSO-containing substances in humans”, as it surely would if they at all thing it had human toxicity implications. Instead it says “compute absolute DMSO final concentrations and include an untreated control group in addition to DMSO vehicle control to check for solvent toxicity.” Because that’s where it’s relevant, for researchers using DMSO to carry other compounds in lab settings. And not, as implied in general food toxicology.
> 
> And finally, where did 0.000015mg come from? I can’t get that number. At all ([https://www.wolframalpha.com/input/…](https://www.wolframalpha.com/input/?i=5%20%CE%BCl%20*%201.1004%20g%2Fcm))
> 
> Article #2, “DMSO Produces Widespread Apoptosis in the Developing Central Nervous System”, again tests in vitro cells immersed in 0.5% and 1.0% concentration. Again, stupidly high concentrations. (Remember how much alcohol you have to drink to get your BAC to 0.08%? 6 times that would be how much DMSO solution you’d have to directly drink to get to 0.5%. A bit different from “eating asparagus”, eh?).
> 
> Of course, those are very toxic levels where it’s easy to assess cellular damage. But what about low-dose toxicity? Well, 0.3 ml/kg (of 99.9% pure DMSO) was the lowest dose tested in the study. Note that this is /kg of bodyweight. That means, EVEN IF human toxicity profiles were identical to rat toxicity profiles (in actuality, humans tolerate chemical exposure way better than rats even when accounted for body mass), that would still be equivalent to over 1mg of pure DMSO exposure for an 8 lb baby and 24 mg of exposure for a grown adult. Hardly the fearmongered 4mg DMSO dose that even the article admits, is naively counting total metabolite production of DMSO without taking into account more metabolism or excretion, instead of what really matters in toxicity, the concentration in the body at one time.
> 
> So basically, eating 500g of asparagus (that’s a lb of asparagus! I don’t eat that much at a time, do you?) may produce 4mg of _total sulfur-containing volatiles_ of which a small portion may be DMSO, of which a small portion may stay in the body at a time, is being to fearmonger… what, a toxicity study that evaluates rats, not humans, at 0.3ml/kg, or equivalent to 24 mg of exposure, may cause _cellular apoptosis above background_ small enough that it needs to be detected in a rat study? Note the study authors take no mention of food, and instead that the population of concern are children exposed to DMSO “who undergo bone marrow transplantation”.
> 
> And finally, the point or implication that asparagus has turned more toxic in recent years because today’s asparagus has potentially turned more neurotoxic from sulfur-containing fertilizer is kind of ridiculous. If the implication is being made that sulfur fertilizer -> sulfur-containing compounds -> DMSO, consider that the knowledge that asparagus leads to foul-smelling sulfur-containing urine was described in as early as 1700s (“asparagus…affects the urine with a foetid smell”, “transforms my chamber-pot into a flask of perfume”, “A few Stems of Asparagus eaten, shall give our Urine a disagreable Odour” [http://www.smithsonianmag.com/…/why-asparagus-makes…/](http://www.smithsonianmag.com/ist/?next=/science-nature/why-asparagus-makes-your-urine-smell-49961252/)).
> 
> Shouldn’t urine be more foul-smelling if there were at all a increased concentration of sulfur metabolites?
> 
> And that would be a poor understanding of why sulfur-based fertilizers are used. They were only INVENTED because things have refused to grow due to sulfur depletion for a century of agriculture…
