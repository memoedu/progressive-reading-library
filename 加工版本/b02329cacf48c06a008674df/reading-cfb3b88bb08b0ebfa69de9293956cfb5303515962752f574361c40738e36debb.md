## 正文

我写这篇文章，是为了回应 Quillette 杂志的 Claire Lehmann 所写的[这篇文章](http://quillette.com/2016/11/30/stop-calling-people-low-information-voters/)。

这篇文章本身主要针对 Jason Brennan 最近的著作 Against Democracy (PUP, 2016)，但在一定程度上，也可以将其视为对类似著作的 critique，例如 Bryan Caplan 的经典之作 The Myth of the Rational Voter (PUP, 2008) ，或 Ilya Somin 的 Democracy and Political Ignorance (SUP, 2013)。

Lehmann 首先复述了 'low information voters' 最近受到的诸多 criticisms；在这极富娱乐性的一年里，我们已经见识了 Brexit 或即将到来的 Trumpenreich 等令人兴奋的剧情转折。

但她说，事情并不止于此：

> 然而，取决于你如何解读，low information people 也可能较不容易产生 [rationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalization_\(psychology\))，而 high information people 可能更容易陷入 [ad hoc hypothesizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_hoc_hypothesis)。intelligence 或 need for cognition 的水平高，并不自动 indicate 一个人是 [high in rationality](http://www.nytimes.com/2016/09/18/opinion/sunday/the-difference-between-rationality-and-intelligence.html?_r=0)。这也不能让我们对一个人的 practical wisdom 了解多少。

虽然她没有为前两个 claims 提供 evidence，但它们是 plausible。第三个 claim 有一个通往 NYT 的链接作为支持，也同样是 plausible。最近的 research（[Teovanović, Kneẑevic & Stankov, 2015](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160289615000380)）确实 support，rationality 与 intelligence 之间只是 weakly correlated (rs<0.25)。关于 personal characteristic 对 decisionmaking 的 effect 的 review，请参见 [Toet et al. (2015)](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Alexander_Toet/publication/309589969_Effects_of_personal_characteristics_on_susceptibility_to_decision_bias_a_literature_study/links/5818b4fe08ae1f34d24aa29c.pdf)。

high information voters 也更容易产生 rationalisation 吗？Dan Kahan 及其同事的研究（例如[这里](http://scholarship.law.gwu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1298&context=faculty_publications)）经常被 cited 来支持这一点。在我认为是他最近的一篇 paper（[Kahan, 2016](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2794799)）中，他用下面这张图简明地 summarises 了这个问题：on average，intelligence 并不 affect 人们对 climate change 的 opinion。更糟糕的是，如果我们把 US citizens decompose 为 liberals 和 conservatives，intelligence 并不会 lead to convergence，而是 lead to divergence！

[![屏幕截图-2016-12-06-22-31-19](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-1.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-22-31-19/)

这引出了对 epistocrats 的一项指责：他们真正想做的是 impose 自己的 prejudices 和 values；他们是 misanthropes，把大多数人视为\_inferior voters\_。

具体而言，她以一名年轻的 PhD graduate 会比一名退役归来的 Army veteran 拥有更多发言权为由，嘲讽 Brennan 的 epistocratic proposal。如果这听起来还不算糟（对我而言确实不算），她接着便告诫我们，想想一位 social sciences PhD，而他的产出恰好会成为 [New Real Peer Review](https://twitter.com/RealPeerReview) 的绝佳素材。

文章最后一部分为 'low information voters' 辩护，称诸如 'immigration boosts economic growth' 这样的 research findings 在 'smart voters' 中广为传播，却忽视了那些 indicate immigration 也会 reduces social trust 的 research。High information voters 通常住在更优越的社区，只不过是生活在泡泡里，不了解 'low information voters' 的现实处境。那么，如果存在 valid、research backed 的 reason 来 oppose immigration，那些 political scientists 又有什么资格告诉他们，可以或不可以 vote for 什么？prefer social trust 和 homogeneity 而非 economic growth，难道就是 irrational 吗？

最近被用于反对 epistocratic arguments 的更多 evidence，可以在 Jason Weeden 和 Rob Kurzban 的博客[这里](http://www.pleeps.org/2016/11/14/would-clinton-have-defeated-trump-in-an-epistocracy/)找到：即使最近的 US election 中只有 educated voters 参与 voted，Trump 也会获胜。

对于这些 arguments，我有一些评论要说。

首先，low information voters _就是_ low information voters；虽然他们被这样称呼时可能会生气，但这并不能改变他们确实如此。也就是说，按照 political 或 economic knowledge 的 objective measures，他们就是如此，而且他们正是这样被 defined 的。甚至 low information voter 这个 term 或许都太客气了。如果一个人并不 informed，那么他很可能只是在 vote at random。但除此之外，大多数 voters 还是 biased。Brennan 在书中把前者称为 hobbits，把后者称为 hooligans。

_恕我不同意_ Lehmann，epistocracy 并不是要 imposing 一套自称 epistocrats 的人所喜欢的 _values_，而是要 reshaping democracy，使 knowledge 在最终的 decision 中拥有更大的发言权。如今的 Democracies 已经在一定程度上 implement 了这一点：通常，一个人只有超过规定年龄才能 vote；而且，一个人的 vote 通常并不直接用于 decide 某个 issue，而是被用来 select 某个人（通常比 voter 更 informed），由他来 decide。此外，还有大量 non-democratic institutions（Judicial branches of government、independent regulatory agencies 和 Central Banks）。由此便有了那句俏皮话：现实中存在的 democracy 之所以能运转得这么好（或者，考虑到 electorate 的 ignorance，它并没有像人们可能预想的那样糟糕），正是因为它的 nondemocratic elements。

其次，epistocracy 不一定就是 the rule of the smart。如果让这个 system 良好运转所需的是一项 rationality test，再加上一项 political、economic 和 natural scientific knowledge test，那就这样做。如果这比 democracy 运转得更好，那么 epistocrats 就会 advocate 这种做法。这就绕过了 Lehmann 提出的 criticisms。例如，我们有 preliminary evidence 表明，scientific curiosity 并不会在 Republicans 和 Democrats 的 responses 中 induce divergence（[Kahan et al., 2016](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2816803)）。因此，在 elaboration 这个 epistocratic procedure 时，就可以使用这一点。如果什么都不起作用，那么就不应该 implemented epistocracy。

这样会产生什么作用吗？原则上，rationality、intelligence，或更广义的 knowledge，与一个人会 vote 什么之间，并不存在深层的 metaphysical relation。也就是说，一个人完全可以是 highly informed racist。不过在实践中，那些 correlations 确实存在，Jason Brennan 已经在书中 documented 了这一点：

[![屏幕截图-2016-12-06-23-37-20](https://nintil.com/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/07-2.png?w=640)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-23-37-20/)

[![屏幕截图-2016-12-06-23-37-41](https://nintil.com/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/07-3.png?w=640)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-23-37-41/)

因此，informed 的人不会 voting for Trump。首先，如果 electorate 通过 epistocratic means 被 restricted，却仍然出现相同的 candidates，这并不 plausible。其次，voters 不会 vote for Trump。但 Weeden 不是刚刚 proved 了 informed 的人就是 Trumpists 吗？

Brennan 的回应有（或会有）两个方面。首先，对 politics 拥有大量 knowledge 的人（ANES surveys 的正是这一点），并不一定特别 rational 或 unbiased。他们完全可能只是 fanatics。

[![屏幕截图-2016-12-07-17-48-25](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-4.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-48-25/)

[![屏幕截图-2016-12-07-17-49-07](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-5.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-49-07/)

其次：我们必须 correct for demographic factors。除了 information，身为 white、black、Jewish、male、female、old 或 young，都可能对你的 preferences 产生 effect。Althaus 和 Caplan 在各自的 studies 中 control for demographic factors，而 Kurzban 和 Weeden 并没有这样做。（因此，如果我们希望有一项用于 vote 的 test，就需要比 ANES test 做得更好。请注意，Jason Brennan 并不建议使用 ANES test 作为 decide 谁应该 vote 的 test。有关其中的问题类型，请参见 Appendix。

[![屏幕截图-2016-12-07-17-51-18](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-6.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-51-18/)

Brennan 真正提出的建议比这更 sophisticated：所 proposed 的 epistocratic methods 之一，是 enlightened preferences。其核心是，如果你神奇地变成 maximally informed，你会 vote for 什么？做法是询问人们的 demographics（或者从 Census data 中 collecting 这些信息），然后让他们填写 questionnaire。接着，他们——每一个人！——都会 vote。之后，通过 running a linear regression 来 corrected Votes，并 extrapolating 出假如每个人都是 maximally informed 时会出现的 outcome，然后 implementing 它。

请注意，Brennan 同样并不建议我们彻底放飞自我，明天就 implement epistocracy。他 suggest 我们应该认真对待它，并认为它很可能会比 democracy 更好，

[![屏幕截图-2016-12-07-18-01-12](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-7.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-18-01-12/)

[![屏幕截图-2016-12-07-18-01-48](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-8.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-18-01-48/)

## Conclusion（结论）

如果我们接受 factual questions 存在 right 和 wrong，也接受通常使用 true facts 会使任何 objective 得到 better attainment，那么认为 epistocracy 在 theory 上会比 democracy 有所 improve，似乎是合理的。因此，以 small scale 来 run pilot tests，看看会发生什么，是值得的。也许人们对这个 concept 本身的 reject 如此强烈，以至于它是 unworkable 的，并且会发生 revolts。在这种情况下，academics 应当转而说服人们：voting 确实如同 plumbing license，而不是 moral merit 的徽章。做到这一点后，就可以尝试 epistocracy。

## 原文

I write this article in response to [this piece](http://quillette.com/2016/11/30/stop-calling-people-low-information-voters/) written by Claire Lehmann at Quillette magazine.

The article itself is aimed mostly at Jason Brennan's recent work Against Democracy (PUP, 2016), but to some extent it could be read as a critique of similar works like Bryan Caplan's classic The Myth of the Rational Voter (PUP, 2008) , or Ilya Somin's Democracy and Political Ignorance (SUP, 2013).

Lehmann first retells the many criticisms that 'low information voters' have been getting lately, during this highly entertaining year that has given us exciting plot twists such as Brexit or the upcoming Trumpenreich.

But that's not the whole story, she says:

> Depending on how you spin it, however, low information people might also be less prone to [rationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationalization_\(psychology\))and high information people might be more vulnerable to [ad hoc hypothesizing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_hoc_hypothesis). Being high in intelligence or a need for cognition does not automatically indicate that one is [high in rationality](http://www.nytimes.com/2016/09/18/opinion/sunday/the-difference-between-rationality-and-intelligence.html?_r=0). Nor does it tell us much about a person’s practical wisdom.

While she doesn't provide evidence for her first two claims, they are plausible. The third claim, backed by a link to the NYT is also plausible. Recent research ([Teovanović, Kneẑevic & Stankov, 2015](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160289615000380)) does support that rationality is only weakly correlated with intelligence (rs<0.25). For a review of the effect of personal characteristic on decisionmaking, see [Toet et al. (2015)](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Alexander_Toet/publication/309589969_Effects_of_personal_characteristics_on_susceptibility_to_decision_bias_a_literature_study/links/5818b4fe08ae1f34d24aa29c.pdf).

Are high information voters also more prone to rationalisation? The work of Dan Kahan and colleagues (for example [here](http://scholarship.law.gwu.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1298&context=faculty_publications)) is often cited in support of this. In what I believe is his most recent paper ([Kahan, 2016](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2794799)), he has this picture that handily summarises the problem: intelligence does not affect people's opinion of climate change, on average. And what is worse, if we decompose US citizens into liberals and conservatives, intelligence does not lead to convergence, but to divergence!

[![captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-22-31-19](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-1.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-22-31-19/)

This leads into an accusation of epistocrats: what they are really trying to do is impose their own prejudices and values, they are misanthropes who consider most people\_inferior voters\_.

In particular, she mocks Brennan's epistocratic proposal by suggesting that a young PhD graduate would have more of a say than an Army veteran returning from service. If this doesn't sound bad (To me it doesn't), she then admonishes us to think of a social sciences PhD whose output would be perfect [New Real Peer Review](https://twitter.com/RealPeerReview) material.

The final part of the essay defends 'low information voters' by saying that research findings such as 'immigration boosts economic growth' are publicised among the 'smart voters', while ignoring the body of research that indicates that immigration also reduces social trust. High information voters, generally in more privileged neighbourhoods, would be simply living in a bubble, unaware of the reality of the 'low information voters'. And then, if there is a valid, research backed reason to oppose immigration, who are those political scientists to tell them what can or can't vote for? Is it irrational to prefer social trust and homogeneity over economic growth?

Further evidence recently used against epistocratic arguments can be found in Jason Weeden and Rob Kurzban's blog [here](http://www.pleeps.org/2016/11/14/would-clinton-have-defeated-trump-in-an-epistocracy/): even if only educated voters had voted in the recent US election, Trump would have won.

To these arguments, I have to make some comments.

First, low information voters _are_ low information voters, and while they may get angry when called so, that doesn't stop them from being so. That is, by objective measures of political or economic knowledge, that's what they are, and that's how they are defined. And even the term low information voter is perhaps too kind. If one is not informed, one may well just vote at random. But the majority of voters are, in addition, biased. Brennan calls the former hobbits, and the latter, hooligans, in his book.

_Pace_ Lehmann, epistocracy is not about imposing a set of _values_ that self-declared epistocrats like, but about reshaping democracy so that knowledge has a greater say in the final decision .  Democracies today already implement some measure of this: typically one cannot vote unless one is over a given age, and typically one's vote does not directly count to decide an issue, but instead it is used to select someone (typically better informed than the voter) who will decide. In addition there are plenty of non-democratic institutions (Judicial branches of government, independent regulatory agencies, and Central Banks). Hence the quip: actually existing democracy work as well as it does (or it doesn't work as badly as one might expect given the electorate's ignorance) in virtue of its nondemocratic elements.

Next, epistocracy is not necessarily the rule of the smart. If what is required for the system to work well is a rationality test, plus a political, economic, and natural scientific knowledge test, so be it. If that works better than democracy, then epistocrats will advocate that. This bypasses the criticisms Lehmann makes. For example, we have preliminary evidence that scientific curiosity does not induce divergence in the responses of Republicans and Democrats ([Kahan et al., 2016](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2816803)). Thus that could be used during the elaboration of the epistocratic procedure. If nothing works, then epistocracy shouldn't be implemented.

Would this do anything? In principle, there is no deep metaphysical relation between rationality, intelligence, or knowledge more broadly, and what one would vote. That is, one can be a highly informed racist. In practise, though, those correlations do exist, as documented by Jason Brennan in his book:

[![captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-23-37-20](https://nintil.com/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/07-2.png?w=640)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-23-37-20/)

[![captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-23-37-41](https://nintil.com/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/07-3.png?w=640)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-06-23-37-41/)

Hence, the informed wouldn't be voting for Trump. In the first place, it is implausible that if the electorate were restricted by epistocratic means, one would have the same candidates. And then, voters wouldn't vote for Trump. But didn't Weeden just proved that the informed are Trumpists?

Brennan's response is (or would be) twofold. First, people with lots of knowledge about politics (what the ANES surveys) need not be particularly rational or unbiased. They can just be fanatics.

[![captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-48-25](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-4.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-48-25/)

[![captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-49-07](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-5.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-49-07/)

Second: we have to correct for demographic factors. In addition to information, being white, or black, or Jewish, or male, or female, or old, or young, might have an effect on your preferences. Althaus and Caplan control for demographic factors in their studies, which is what Kurzban and Weeden didn't do. (Hence, if we would wish to have a test to vote, we would need to do better than the ANES test. Note that Jason Brennan DOES NOT suggest using the ANES test as a test to decide who should vote. See the Appendix for what sort of questions are in there.

[![captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-51-18](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-6.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-17-51-18/)

What Brennan does suggest is more sophisticated than that: One of the epistocratic methods that is proposed is that of enlightened preferences. In essence, what would you vote for if you, magically became maximally informed? This is done by asking people about their demographics (or collecting that from Census data), and then having them fill a questionnaire. Then they - everyone! - would vote. Votes would then be corrected by running a linear regression, and extrapolating the outcome that would be the case if everyone were maximally informed, and implementing that.

Note that Brennan also DOES NOT suggest we should go full yolo and implement epistocracy tomorrow. He suggest we should take it seriously, and that it is likely that it will be better than democracy,

[![captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-18-01-12](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-7.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-18-01-12/)

[![captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-18-01-48](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/12/07-8.png)](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/12/07/in-defense-of-brennanian-epistocracy/captura-de-pantalla-de-2016-12-07-18-01-48/)

## Conclusion

If we accept that there is such a thing as right and wrong regarding factual questions, and that generally using true facts will lead to better attainment of whatever objective, then it seems reasonable that epistocracy will improve, in theory, upon democracy. So it would be worthwhile to run pilot tests at small scale to see what happens. Perhaps people reject the concept itself so strongly that it is unworkable, and revolts happen. In that case academics should turn to convince people that voting is indeed like a plumbing license, not a badge of moral merit. Once that is done, epistocracy could be attempted.
