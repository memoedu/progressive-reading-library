## 正文

_这些是粗略而不完整的 working notes，用来帮助我想清楚如何使用这个 site。主要是写给我自己看的。不过，我也会与朋友分享，作为讨论如何最好地 design 一个 website 来支持 creative work 的谈资。我才刚开始思考这个问题，因此这些 notes 很天真，也非常粗糙。不过，也许有几位读者会有兴趣，在我思考这个问题时从旁看看。这里的许多内容并不适用于其他人：它们很大程度上取决于我的 values、creative goals 和个人 working style 中那些独有的特点。_

_首次 release：10-11-2021_

_最近 updated：10-13-2021_

> “如果着眼长远，你会在 personal site 上写些什么，又会怎样写？……如果你在接下来的 60 年里持续写作（哪怕只是极其偶尔地写），你能创作出怎样的作品？——[Gwern Branwen](https://www.gwern.net/About)

大多数 personal websites 的目的，是与他人建立 personal connection，或确立自己的专业地位。它们通常不是为了帮助 author 思考和创作，除非顺带起到了这种作用。

这些事情——personal connection、professional marketing——很重要。但如果把它们作为 website 的 purpose，我实在兴奋不起来。不过，用这个 website 来增强我思考和创作的能力，这个想法确实让我兴奋。

以下是几个给过我启发的例子：[Gwern Branwen](https://gwern.net/)、[Cosma Shalizi](http://bactra.org/)、[Bret Victor](https://worrydream.com/)、[SlateStarCodex](https://slatestarcodex.com/)、[Terry Tao](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/)、[Devon Zuegel](https://devonzuegel.com/)、[Piero Scaruffi](https://www.scaruffi.com/)。我想，这些 sites 在许多情况下，都极大地帮助了 authors 思考和创作。

下面是一些想法。先从几条比较重要的 principles 说起，再加上一些不太适合放在文章后面的杂想。其中一些内容会在后文重复出现。

-   这个 site 的 purpose，是帮助我 think well[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn1)。这个 purpose 与 online writing 中许多广泛采用的 norms 是 in opposition to 的。我想，它可能带来的益处，比我过去（天真地）以为的要大得多。
-   Committed writing——那种仿佛用血写成的 writing——是一种极其出色的 self-transformation 方法。
-   这里的每一篇作品，都应该 durably level up 我的 understanding。这并不意味着，它不会同时也是某个更长远过程中的一个短暂部分。
-   当 stakes 更高时，我会想得更好。raise the stakes 的一种方式，是与那些我希望得到其好评的人分享 work。
-   让 Depth 优先于 engagement 和 audience-building。让一位才华出众、知识渊博的人对一篇作品作出强烈而深入的回应，远胜于让十万人作出浅薄的回应。
-   用 File systems 来 organize projects 糟糕透了。通过这个 site，我可以 design 一个更能 supports 我的 work 的 interface。举例来说：site navigation 和 organization 应该帮助我 generate 新的 ideas，并帮助我看清我的 work 中的 structure。
-   这个 site 是通向我过去的 thinking 和未来可能开展的 creative work 的 interface。这意味着，site 的 organization 和 design 是 first-class objects。随着我的 understanding 变化，organization 也会随之变化，因为 organization expresses 那份 understanding。因此，我应该预期自己会经常 reorganize 和 consolidate notes。这两者都是一种 meaning-making。
-   学会 think fluently 很重要。这意味着，要学会 identify discomfort，也要学会如何 respond to 它。凡是能让 good thoughts 更容易产生的事，都要加倍投入[2](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn2)。
-   与此同时：有时 being stuck 也有价值。我还不明白该如何 resolve 这种 tension。
-   让 site 采用 append-mainly 的方式。如果我每周 add 一个新的 document，持续 10 年，也只有 500 个 documents，几分钟就能读完完整的 titles 列表。即便持续 40 年，也只有 2,000 个 documents。任何合理的 subset——比如关于 tools for thought 或 matter 的未来的 materials——读完其全部 titles，也只需要一分钟左右。这就让我有可能看到并 navigate 自己几乎全部的 thinking。事实上，即便是尚未完成的 projects，我也可以 maintain complete、ordered lists。
-   让 include images、video 和 sound 快得惊人，而且轻而易举。总的来说，要把 good habits 和 thinking 通过 automatic procedure 来 reify。尤其是：要让 publish 正在进行中的东西变得毫不费力。理想情况下，deploy 应该只花费 order 1 second 的时间。
-   优秀、深思熟虑、支持你的 critics 弥足珍贵。糟糕的 critics 会 damage 你 explore、speculate 和做一个 beginner 的 freedom。采取必要措施，让糟糕的 critics 远离你的生活。
-   做 gross retrofitting，而不是 detailed 的改造。也就是说，像把过去的 work 纳入 site organization 这样的事情值得做。但像 updating design、meta-data 等事情就不值得做：保留那些 legacy stuff。我预计，这会（大幅）减少最终花在 maintenance 上的时间：你希望过去 work 的 maintenance cost 能 approach zero，即使代价是某种 inconsistency。
-   Meta-thinking——关于 writing 的 writing、关于 thinking 的 writing——名声不佳。有那么多 productivity bloggers 或 self-help writers，其 productivity 的主要形式就是写 productivity！我知道，自己这样做时也会觉得不好意思——写这份 document 就让我不好意思！但 anchoring 在“做 meta-thought 是不好的”这一想法上，是个错误。进行_一些_这样的 thinking，会让你大有收获；你只是不希望它占据你的全部生活！但如果它确实大幅改变了你在生活其他方面的行动方式，那么这很可能就是值得花的时间。

## 通过投入 design，加深你对 work 的 emotional engagement

[Gwern Branwen 的 site](https://gwern.net/) 中，design 是让我非常欣赏的一点。我不是说想模仿他的[具体 design choices](https://www.gwern.net/Design)，尽管它们确实能激发灵感。但从 Branwen 的 site 上，可以清楚看到他在 design 上投入的时间和精力。他很用心。

人们重视这类 design 的一个常见理由，是让 reader 的 experience 更好。

但一个更好的理由是，它能让 writer 的 experience 更好。尤其是，_improving design 可以成为增强你思考和创作能力的一种方式_。当你在 design 上投入时间，当某样东西看起来、感觉起来都很美，你自己也会更认真地对待它。

（顺便说一句：mathematics 的学生开始使用像 LaTeX 这样严肃的 typesetting system 时，也会发生这种事。不知为何，当 work 在 page 上被 beautifully typeset 出来时，它就显得更真实了。当他们第一次在 professionally typeset 的 journal 或 book 中看到自己的 work 时，这种 effect 又会被 compounded。对许多书籍作者而言，第一次把一本 bound paper copy 拿在手里，是一种深刻的体验。这让 work 获得了一种此前没有的真实感。）

design 在情感上的重要性，对我来说是个挑战。我并不是一位称职的 website designer。而不幸的是，上面几段话还有另一面：做得糟糕，会主动促成你与 work 之间错误的 emotional relationship。你会得到与 LaTeX effect 相反的结果——如果你让自己的 work _看起来_ amateurish，那么 content 在你自己眼里也会显得更 amateurish。这实在很糟糕。

最显而易见的 solution 是“请一位专业人士来做 design”。但在这里，考虑到 design goal，这行不通：我要 create 一个 supports 我的 thinking 和 creative work 的 website。从某种意义上说，我_必须_是 principal designer。当然，我可以、也应该向比我更懂的人寻求帮助。这个 solution——我目前还处于 implementing 的早期——很直接：

-   反复、反复、再反复地寻求专业帮助。请一位 designer 看看这个 site，对它进行 critique，identify 哪些地方 working well，哪些地方 working poorly。
-   与此同时，认真研究和学习其他人在做的事情。
-   并且不断回到那个 fundamental question：什么最能增强我思考和创作的能力？

只管围绕这几点持续 iterating。也要记住，追求的是_这看起来、感觉起来都极其出色_。但同时也要明白，这是一个持续的过程。如果某样东西眼下感觉 amateurish，那就应该_留意_它——并制定修正的计划！

如果我把学习 design 的 elements、improving site 的 design，当作一项认真而长期的 project，它就会逐渐变好很多。尤其是，如果我把它当作一项终身 project。它会逐渐从“看起来很糟糕”，变成“看起来很平庸”，再到“看起来还不错”，如此继续，直到“看起来、感觉起来都极其出色”。当然，这需要在几十年里投入数百小时（很可能是数千小时）的 work。但回报是，我会对自己的 work 感到极大的愉悦和享受。这值得投入时间！

## 那些 design 表面上很糟糕的优秀 sites，又该怎么看？

如上所述，我非常欣赏 [Cosma Shalizi](http://bactra.org/) 和 [Piero Scaruffi's](https://www.scaruffi.com/) sites。但从 design 来看，两者似乎都停留在 1995 年。

该如何让这一点与上面的观点相协调？

我确信，如果我让自己的 site 保持他们那样的 design aesthetic，就会对我看待自己作品的感受产生强烈的负面影响。

话虽如此，这两位作者都在 design 的非传统方面投入了大量心血。尤其是，他们都对 information 的 organization 做过大量思考：包括 categories、hierarchies、navigation、information density，以及许多其他 elements。

它或许并不漂亮，当然，人们也可以对他们的选择有所挑剔。但其中仍然有很多值得学习的东西。虽然这不是人们通常所理解的 design，但它确实是 information design，而且在许多方面都极其出色。因此，这些 sites 既有很多值得学习之处，也有不少尚未充分发挥的潜力。

## 将 Design 视为寻找新的 fundamental objects 和 actions，以支持 thought

我在上文使用“design”时，取的是人们谈论 web design 时常用的那种（非常粗略的）含义——选择好的 typefaces 和 layouts，让各项事物能够为读者有效地运作。但还有一种截然不同的含义，那就是富有想象力的 interface design。在那里，问题是寻找新的 fundamental objects 和 actions；在我们的情形中，目的是支持 thinking 和 creating。

这方面，gwern.net 做得格外好。只举一个例子：每个 webpage 顶部都有对这个 webpage 的 importance 的 rating；他对 contents 有多 certain；这个 page 有多 finished；哪些内容 links 到这个 page；以及所有 links 的 bibliography。

这些都是在 webpage 上并不常见的、可以执行的 actions。它们改变了读者以及作者（对后者的影响或许更大）的体验。它们是 interface design 中相当深刻的 ideas[3](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn3)。

更深一层说，Gwern Branwen 的 webpages 是真正 interlinked 的。每个 page 都是通向 site 其余很大一部分内容的 narrative interface（而且往往非常 sophisticated）。这正是许多人早期以为 web 会成为的样子。Branwen 做到了；Shalizi 和 Scaruffi 也做到了。但放眼整个 web，这种情况却少得惊人。

## 妨碍 fluent writing 的因素

-   “我不能这样说！”的任何变体。
-   “如果别人觉得我很蠢怎么办？”
-   “如果别人觉得这很无聊怎么办？”
-   “如果别人觉得我很无知怎么办？”
-   “如果别人觉得我在试图显得高人一等怎么办？”
-   “如果这还不够好怎么办？”
-   “如果 sharing 我的 ideas 导致我被 scooped 了怎么办？”
-   “这是我工作的一部分吗？”
-   刚刚进入某个领域时伴随而来的模糊担忧。你应该为自己是个 beginner 而_感到自豪_。
-   预想到 low quality criticism 时随之变化的种种担忧。这很奇怪：我收到过很多我明知是 very low quality 的 critique[4](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn4)。然而，就像 Pavlov's dog 一样，它还是会对我产生 conditions 的作用，让我担心未来的 low quality critiques。

一般来说，任何让人感到不安或难堪的东西，都可能 disrupt fluency。而为了获得 fluency，下很大的功夫也是值得的。你要避免 disrupting ideas 的 flow。上述任何一项只要减少 10%，都非常值得。我预计，许多问题都可以通过在文章顶部使用 scoping statements 来 set expectations，从而得到部分解决。当然，目标是 set expectations，以帮助你 think fluently and well。理想情况下，你会怀着一种永不满足的积极心态来工作。

## 主要的 page types

-   **Essays:** 它们面向（相对）广泛的 audience，并希望获得广泛的 dissemination。它们仍可能像 working notes 一样包含 scoping remarks，但这些 remarks 更可能是_顺带提及_，而不是放在顶部。
-   **Working notes:** 它们面向相对狭窄的 audience，有时 audience 只有一个人（我），或两个人（我 + 另一个人）。我发现，它们是一个很有力量的思考 context。
-   **Ongoing:** 用于我打算偶尔重新查看并修改的东西——例如 planning documents。其中一种 subtype 是 **accretions**，用于 quotes files 之类会随时间 accrete 的东西。

**Page types 比我以为的更重要：**从前面提到的那些给我启发的 sites 中，就能看出这一点。许多作者都认真思考过自己 site 上不同的 page types。尤其是 gwern.net 的 metadata，对一个 page 的 purpose 做出了非常 fine-grained 的说明。其中很大一部分，是为了让你自己与这个 page 的关系恰到好处：当这种关系合适时，write 应该会变得相对容易。而如果你感到吃力，那可能意味着你正在试图 write 错误类型的 page。

## 关于 blogging（以及一般的 online writing）周围的 assumptions

对于 blogging，以及一般的 online writing，你需要_摒弃_许多学来的东西。

确实有许多很棒的 blogs，能够支持作者的 thinking——Terry Tao、Tim Gowers、Scott Alexander，以及许多其他人的 blogs。但我常常想，他们是不是在与这种 form 对抗。

（这不是说 blogs 对许多其他 purposes 而言并不特别出色。而且，正如这些例子所表明的，它们也可以被用来支持 thinking。但这看起来确实有点像一场斗争。）

blogging 的一个问题是，人们 subscribe 的是 _blog_，而不是让每篇 post 找到自己的 audience。这会鼓励 lowest common denominator writing。尤其是，它创造了一个 depth 会受到 penalized 的 context[5](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn5),[6](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn6)。如果把 Gwern Branwen 的 articles 当作 blog 的素材，人们会觉得很奇怪：关于 Dark Net、spaced repetition memory systems、anime、machine learning 等主题的极其 in-depth 的 pieces。让 site 成为 not-a-blog，会 disaggregates audience，并且（我推测）更容易为每一篇作品召集_合适的_（或者至少更适合的）community。

在这一点上，比 blogs 做得更好的 media form 是 academic journals。虽然可以 subscribe 一份 journal，但人们很少会期待把整份 journal 从头读到尾。原因在于，这种 form 的设计是为了让每篇 article _找到自己的 audience_。例如：title 和 abstract 的设计，是为了让人极其容易决定_不读_一篇 paper。它们在说：“哦，除非你有这些 interests 和这样的 background，否则你应该忽略这篇。”它们有时被描述为 papers 的“marketing”，但实际上，把它们理解为 anti-marketing 更恰当。尤其是：它们 set 合适的 expectations，并帮助_赶走_不合适的读者。这不仅对读者极其宝贵，对作者而言或许更是如此。

另一个问题是，blogging 是 by-default ephemeral 的。它不是通向你过去的 thoughts 或未来的 plans 的 interface。它让这样的 organization 成为事后才考虑的事情，而不是 first-class object。

我一直在大谈 blogging 的负面之处！当然，blogs 和 blogging 也有许多让我欣赏的地方。不过，我希望上述思考已经表明，反思 blogging 中哪些地方行不通，是有用的；反思它作为 thinking 和 creating 的 medium 时，是什么让人感到不自在，也是有用的。下一节将讨论其中一种尤其有害的形式。

## 不要以 build an audience 或 engagement 为目标。而要 Anti-market

online writing 中最糟糕的 ideas 之一，就是毫无限定地认为你应该努力 build an audience 或 build engagement。如果你是想卖东西，这或许不错。但如果要 support good thinking[7](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn7)，就需要加上许多 qualifiers。事实上，engagement 可能是一种诅咒，会主动 damaging good thinking[8](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn8)。我在上文已经提到过其中一种形式：它可能 inhibit depth。但还有其他问题。

假设你正在对某件事 thinking deeply。你会经常停下来。陷入困境。Backtrack，一遍又一遍——再一遍又一遍。你会犯错，有时还会犯严重的错误。有些错误会让你感到难堪。你会有 half-baked、incomplete 和 not-quite-working 的 ideas。偶尔，你的 ideas 会糟糕透顶。你会从各种地方汲取 ideas，其中往往包括只有极少数人才可能知道的 technical ideas。而你会希望尽可能迅速地 use 那些 ideas，进行 freely associating。

然而，这些 actions 几乎都违背了关于如何 build an audience 和 engagement 的 conventional wisdom。

面对上述说法，一个自然的回应是：那么，为什么不干脆私下 thinking 呢？为什么要把它强加给广大公众？

这是一个 false dichotomy。“只为自己 think 和 write”与“努力为尽可能庞大的 audience write”之间，有着极大的空间。而不同 types 的 creative work，最适合在这个空间中的不同位置进行。真正的问题在于：你究竟想处在这个空间的什么位置？或者换个说法：怎样 define audience，才能最好地 support 你的 creative work？

我确实在 site 的 local version 中保留了一些 private notes。这让我能够 reuse site 的许多习惯、infrastructure 和 workflow。但 private notes 的难处在于，我们对于 good work 的许多 standards 都是通过社会交往维系的。大多数人都有这样的发现：你以为自己 understand 某件事，随后试着向别人 explain，却发现自己的 understanding 有着重要的缺口。或者，你发现在与别人交谈时，自己会自然而然地 improve explanations。我经常发现，自己会突然用非常有帮助的方式把事情提炼清楚。我对此有点不好意思：这里有一种爱表现、想取悦别人的欲望。但我想，这大概极其普遍。人类似乎并不是为独自 think 而生的。

只为自己 writing，有一种奇怪的困难。据我所知，几乎没有人能 productively 地做到这一点[9](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn9):[10](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn10)。当 stakes 更高时，我们会 think 得更好，而 raising the stakes 的最佳方式之一，就是把 document 变成你要与那些你希望获得其认可的人分享的东西。哪怕只与这样一个人分享，也会给你的 thinking 的 quality 带来巨大的不同。

Terry Tao 的 blog 或许是这条 rule 最好的 exception。许多 pieces 都要求大量 background，而我推测大多数 readers 并不具备这些 background。从某种意义上说，这就是他为 blog 建立的 expectation：大多数 entries 对大多数 readers 来说，大部分内容都将是 inaccessible 的。但从这个意义上说，这是一个非常不寻常的 blog，subverting 了关于 blogging 的 conventional assumptions。[↩︎](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fnref5)

6.  从这个角度看 Scott Alexander 的 writing，也很有意思。与 Tao 不同，Alexander 的 entries 相对容易阅读。把它们 bundle 成一个 blog 是合理的：它们有 common themes，而且相对 accessible。另一个有利因素是，Alexander 是一位出色的 writer，能够吸引各种各样的人进入许多不同的 subjects；他非常善于使 common themes explicit。[↩︎](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fnref6)

7.  这让我想起一个危害极大的 idea：人生成功 is equivalent to 赚很多钱。它与那个 idea 的一个相似之处在于，当你身处一个许多人都相信它的环境（比如 Silicon Valley）时，它就会开始侵染你的 thinking，而这种方式极难察觉，也极难摆脱。[↩︎](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fnref7)

8.  这也是一个隐蔽的 idea。它影响着 tech 和 media 中各式各样的 cultural norms，而这两个领域都默认 engagement 是好的，是 value 的标志。如果你接触这些文化，就很容易吸收一些带有这种 presumption 的 norms。[↩︎](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fnref8)

9.  也有著名的 exceptions。我很喜欢 Simone Weil 的 writing，其中许多似乎主要出于个人原因而写，后来才 published。但这些 writing 虽然极富启发性，也有许多 shortcomings。我相信，如果 Weil 是为不止她自己一个人的 audience writing，其中一些 shortcomings 或许就能避免。举个例子：她的许多 arguments 都是 abstractly 提出的，没有 reference to specific examples。只为自己 writing 时，这种 abstract argumentation 很有诱惑力：你心里往往有 specific examples，并不需要使它们 explicit。如果她是为更广泛的 audience writing，我相信——这只是我的推测——Weil 会把 examples 写下来。而我自己的经验是，当你做这些 detailed work 时，往往会在自己的 abstract argumentation 中 uncover problems，进而带来 improvement。[↩︎](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fnref9)

10.  当然，许多人会保留 private working notes，作为最终产生 public output 的 projects 的一部分。我自己也做过不少这样的事，但几乎总觉得这些 notes 有种隐隐令人不满意的地方。更详细地想清楚其中的原因，或许会有帮助。[↩︎](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fnref10)

## 原文

[![](https://michaelnotebook.com/assets/home.png)](https://michaelnotebook.com/index.html)

_Rough and incomplete working notes, to help me think through how to use this site. Intended primarily for my own use. But I will also share this with friends, as grist for conversations about how to best design a website to support creative work. I'm a beginner at thinking about this question, so the notes are naive and very rough. Still, perhaps a few readers may be interested in peeking over my shoulder as I think through the question. Much of this won't apply to other people: it depends strongly on idiosyncracies of my values, creative goals, and personal working style._

_Initial release: 10-11-2021_

_Last updated: 10-13-2021_

> “What and how do you write a personal site with the long-term in mind?… What sort of writing could you create if you worked on it (be it ever so rarely) for the next 60 years? – [Gwern Branwen](https://www.gwern.net/About)

Most personal websites aim at personal connection with other people, or at establishing oneself professionally. They're not usually about helping the author think and create, except incidentally.

Those things – personal connection, professional marketing – are important. But as the purpose for a website I can't get excited about them. But I can get excited about the idea of using this website to enhance my ability to think and create.

Here's a few examples I've drawn inspiration from: [Gwern Branwen](https://gwern.net/), [Cosma Shalizi](http://bactra.org/), [Bret Victor](https://worrydream.com/), [SlateStarCodex](https://slatestarcodex.com/), [Terry Tao](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/), [Devon Zuegel](https://devonzuegel.com/), [Piero Scaruffi](https://www.scaruffi.com/). In many cases I suspect those sites have greatly helped the authors think and create.

A few thoughts follow below. Let's begin with a few of the more important principles, plus a few miscellanea that don't really belong later in the essay. Some of this is repeated later in the essay.

-   The purpose of the site is to help me think well[1](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn1). This purpose is in opposition to many widely-used norms in online writing. I suspect that the possible upside is a lot higher than I have (naively) historically thought.
-   Committed writing – the sort written in blood – is an exceptional method of self-transformation.
-   Each piece here should durably level up my understanding. This does not mean it won't also be a transitory part of some longer game.
-   I think better when the stakes are higher. One way to raise the stakes is to share work with people whose good opinion I desire.
-   Depth over engagement and audience-building. Far better to have one brilliant, knowledgeable person respond strongly and in depth to a piece than to have a hundred thousand respond shallowly.
-   File systems are terrible ways to organize projects. With this site I can design an interface that better supports my work. For one thing: the site navigation and organization should help me generate new ideas, and help me see the structure in my work.
-   This site is an interface to my past thinking and possible future creative work. This means the organization and design of the site are first-class objects. As my understanding changes, so too will the organization, since the organization expresses that understanding. Thus, I should expect to reorganize and consolidate notes often. Both are a type of meaning-making.
-   Learning to think fluently is important. This means learning to identify discomfort, and learning how to respond to it. Double down on whatever makes it easy to have good thoughts[2](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn2).
-   At the same time: there is sometimes value in being stuck. I don't yet understand how to resolve that tension.
-   Make the site append-mainly. If I add one new document a week for 10 years that's only 500 documents, and I can read a complete list of titles in a few minutes. Over 40 years it's only 2,000 documents. Any reasonable subset – say, the materials on tools for thought, or the future of matter – will take only a minute or so to read all the titles. That makes it possible to see and navigate my thinking more or less entire. Indeed, I can maintain complete, ordered lists even for incomplete projects.
-   Make it drop-dead fast and easy to include images, video, and sound. In general, reify good habits and thinking in automatic procedure. In particular: make it trivial to publish something that's in progress. Ideally, it should take order 1 second to deploy.
-   Good, thoughtful, supportive critics are worth their weight in gold. Bad critics damage your freedom to explore, to speculate, to be a beginner. Do what is necessary to keep bad critics out of your life.
-   Do gross retrofitting, not detailed. That is, it's worth doing things like bringing old work into the site organization. But it's not worth doing things like updating the design, meta-data, and so on: keep the legacy stuff. I expect that will (greatly) diminish the eventual time spent in maintenance: you want the maintenance cost of old work to approach zero, even at the expense of some inconsistency.
-   Meta-thinking – writing about writing, writing about thinking – has a bad reputation. So many productivity bloggers or self-help writers whose main form of productivity is writing about productivity! I know I feel sheepish when I do it – I feel sheepish about this document! But anchoring on “it's bad to do meta-thought” is a mistake. You benefit greatly from doing _some_ thinking like this; you just don't want it to take over your life! But if it's genuinely making big changes to the way you act in the rest of your life, then it's likely time well spent.

## Investing in design as a way of deepening your emotional engagement with the work

The design is something I greatly admire about [Gwern Branwen's site](https://gwern.net/). I don't mean I want to emulate his [specific design choices](https://www.gwern.net/Design), though they are a source of stimulation. But the time and effort put into the design are clear in Branwen's site. He cares.

One conventional reason people care about such design is to make the reader's experience better.

But a better reason is that it makes the writer's experience better. In particular, _improving the design can be a way of enhancing your ability to think and create_. When you invest time in the design, when something looks and feels beautiful, you take it more seriously yourself.

(Aside: this happens to students of mathematics when they begin using a serious typesetting system like LaTeX. Somehow the work seems more real when it is beautifully typeset on the page. This effect is compounded when they first see their work in a journal or book that is professionally typeset. For many authors of books, it's a profound experience to have a bound paper copy in their hand for the first time. It makes the work real in way it wasn't before.)

The emotional importance of design is a challenge for me. I am not a competent website designer. And unfortunately the paragraphs above have a complement: doing a poor job actively fosters the wrong emotional relationship to the work. You get the opposite of the LaTeX effect – if you make your work _look_ amateurish, the content will seem more amateurish to you. That's really bad.

The most obvious solution is “get a professional to do the design”. But that's not possible here, given the design goal: to create a website which supports my thinking and creative work. In some sense I _must_ be the principal designer. But, of course, I can and should get assistance from people who know more than me. The solution – which I'm still in the early days of implementing – is straightforward:

-   Get professional assistance, over and over and over. Ask a designer to look over the site, to critique it, to identify what is working well and what is working poorly.
-   At the same time, seriously study and learn from what other people are doing.
-   And keep coming back to the fundamental question: what would most enhance my ability to think and create?

Just keep iterating on these points. And keep in mind that the aspiration is _this looks and feels superb_. But also understanding that this is an ongoing process. If something feels amateurish right now, that's something to _notice_ – and to have a plan to fix!

If I make a serious long-term project of learning the elements of design, and improving the design of the site, then it will gradually get much better. Especially if I treat this as a lifetime project. It will gradually go from “looks terrible” to “looks mediocre”, to “looks okay”, and so on, up to: “looks and feels superb”. Of course, this will take hundreds (and probably thousands) of hours of work, over decades. But the reward is a feeling of great pleasure and enjoyment of my own work. That's worth the investment of time!

## What about great sites whose design is superficially terrible?

As mentioned above, I greatly admire [Cosma Shalizi](http://bactra.org/) and [Piero Scaruffi's](https://www.scaruffi.com/) sites. But design-wise both appear stuck in 1995.

How to reconcile this with the points made above?

I'm confident that if I left my site with their design aesthetic it would strongly negatively impact how I felt about my own work.

That said, both those authors have put a tremendous amount of work into unconventional aspects of the design. In particular, they've both thought a great deal about the organization of information: about categories, hierarchies, navigation, information density, and many other elements.

It may not be beautiful, and certainly one may quibble with their choices. But there is nonetheless a great deal to be learned. While it's not what people often think of as design, it is information design, and in many respects it's extraordinarily good. So there's both a lot to be learned from these sites; and also quite a bit they leave on the table.

## Design as finding new fundamental objects and actions to support thought

The sense in which I used “design” above was in the (very rough) sense people often use when they're talking about web design – choosing good typefaces and layouts, making things work effectively for the reader. But there's a very different meaning, which is of imaginative interface design. There the question is to find new fundamental objects and actions; in our case, with the object of supporting thinking and creating.

This is something gwern.net does unusually well. As just one example: at the top of each webpage is a rating of the webpage's importance; of how certain he is of the contents; of how finished the page is; what links to the page; and a bibliography of all links.

These are all unusual actions to be able to perform on a webpage. They change the experience of both the reader and (perhaps even more) of the author. They are quite deep ideas of interface design[3](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn3).

More profoundly, Gwern Branwen's webpages are truly interlinked. Each page is a (often very sophisticated) narrative interface to a large chunk of the rest of the site. This is what many people thought the web was going to be early on. It's true of Branwen; it's true of Shalizi and Scaruffi too. But it's shockingly rare on the web as a whole.

## Obstacles to fluent writing

-   Any variation on: “I can't say that!”
-   “What if people think I'm stupid?”
-   “What if people think this is boring?”
-   “What if people think I'm ignorant?”
-   “What if people think I'm trying to be superior?”
-   “What if this isn't good enough?”
-   “What if sharing my ideas results in my being scooped?”
-   “Is this part of my job?”
-   The vague worries associated to being a beginning in some area. You want to _feel pride_ in being a beginner.
-   The changing worries associated to anticipating low quality criticism. It's strange: I've received a lot of critique that I know was very low quality[4](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn4). And yet, like Pavlov's dog, it conditions me to worry about low quality future critiques anyway.

In general, anything which produces a feeling of unease or embarassment can disrupt fluency. And it's worth working very hard to gain fluency. You want to avoid disrupting the flow of ideas. A reduction of 10% in any one of the above is tremendously worthwhile. I expect that many can be addressed in part through use of scoping statements at the top of a piece, to set expectations. The goal, of course, is to set expectations to help you think fluently and well. Ideally, you'll work with a kind of insatiable positivity.

## Main page types

-   **Essays:** These are intended for a (relatively) broad audience, and to have wide dissemination. They may still include scoping remarks, like working notes, but those remarks are more likely to be done _en passant_, rather than at the top.
-   **Working notes:** These are intended for a relatively narrow audience, sometimes an audience of one (me), or two (me + one other person). I find them a powerful context in which to think.
-   **Ongoing:** For things – like planning documents – which I intend to occasionally revist and change. A subtype is **accretions**, for things like quotes files, which accrete over time.

**Page types matter more than I think:** You can see this in the inspiration sites I mentioned above. Many of the authors have thought hard about the different types of page on their site. gwern.net's metadata, in particular, gives a very fine-grained account of the purpose of a page. Much of this is about getting your own relationship to the page right: when that relationship is right, it should make it relatively easy to write. And if you're struggling, it may mean that you're trying to write the wrong kind of page.

## On the assumptions around blogging (and online writing in general)

You need to _unlearn_ a lot from blogging, and from online writing in general.

It's true there are many tremendous blogs, blogs which support the thinking of the authors – Terry Tao, Tim Gowers, Scott Alexander, and many others. But I often wonder if they're fighting against the form.

(This is not to say that blogs aren't exceptionally good for many other purposes. And, as those examples show, they can be made to work as supports for thinking. But it does seem to be a bit of a battle.)

One trouble with blogging is that people subscribe _to the blog_, rather than each post finding its own audience. This encourages lowest common denominator writing. In particular it creates a context in which depth is penalized[5](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn5),[6](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn6). Gwern Branwen's articles would be considered strange as fodder for a blog: extremely in-depth pieces about the Dark Net, about spaced repetition memory systems, about anime, about machine learning, and so on. Making the site not-a-blog disaggregates the audience, and (I presume) makes it easier to convene the _right_ (or at least a more apt) community for each individual piece.

A media form which does this better than blogs is academic journals. Although it is possible to subscribe to a journal, it's very uncommon to expect to read a journal entire. And the reason is that the form is designed so that each article _finds its own audience_. For instance: the title and abstract are designed to make it super-easy to _not read_ a paper. They say “Oh, unless you have these interests and this background, you should ignore this”. They're sometimes described as “marketing” for papers, but really they're better thought of as anti-marketing. In particular: they set the right expectations, and help _drive away_ the wrong kind of reader. That's invaluable not just for the reader, but perhaps even more for the author.

Another problem is that blogging is by-default ephemeral. It's not an interface to your past thoughts or your future plans. It makes such organization an afterthought, not a first-class object.

I've been dwelling hard on the negatives of blogging! Of course, there's much I admire about blogs and blogging. Still, I hope the above reflections show that it's useful to reflect on what doesn't work about blogging; what makes it uncomfortable as a medium for thinking and creating. The next section looks at a particularly pernicious form.

## Don't aim to build an audience or engagement. Anti-market instead

One of the worst ideas in online writing is the unqualified idea that you should be trying to build an audience or to build engagement. This may be good if you're trying to sell something. But it needs a lot of qualifiers if it's to support good thinking[7](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn7). In fact, engagement can be a curse, actively damaging to good thinking[8](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn8). I've already alluded to one form above: the way it may inhibit depth. But there are other problems too.

Suppose you're thinking deeply about something. You'll stop often. Get stuck. Backtrack, over and over – and over and over – again. You'll make mistakes, sometimes bad ones. Sometimes ones that you'll find embarrassing. You'll have half-baked and incomplete and not-quite-working ideas. Occasionally you'll have ideas that are truly dreadful. You'll pull ideas from all kinds of places, often including technical ideas only a tiny handful of people can be expected to know. And you'll want to use those ideas as rapidly as you are able, freely associating.

And yet almost all of these actions violate the conventional wisdom about how to build an audience and engagement.

A natural response to the above is: well, why not just do the thinking in private, then? Why impose it on the public at large?

This is a false dichotomy. There's a very large gap between “think and write for just oneself”, and “try to write for the largest possible audience”. And different types of creative work are best done at different places within that gap. That's really the question: where exactly in that gap do you want to be? Or, to put it another way: how best to define the audience to support your creative work?

I do keep some private notes in my local version of the site. It lets me reuse a lot of the habits and infrastructure and workflow of the site. But the challenge of private notes is that many of our standards for good work are held socially. Most people find this: you think you understand something, then try to explain it to others, and find your understanding has important holes. Or you find that when you speak to others you naturally improve your explanations. I often find that I will all of a sudden boil stuff down in really helpful ways. I'm a little sheepish about this: there's a kind of ham-ish desire to please others. But I think it's probably extremely common. Humans don't seem to be made to think alone.

There's something strangely difficult in writing just for oneself. As far as I can tell, almost no-one can do it productively[9](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn9):[10](https://michaelnotebook.com/wn/website_enhance.html#fn10). We think better when the stakes are higher, and one of the best ways of raising the stakes is to make a document into something you're sharing with people whose good opinion you desire. Even just sharing with one such person makes an enormous difference to the quality of your thinking.
