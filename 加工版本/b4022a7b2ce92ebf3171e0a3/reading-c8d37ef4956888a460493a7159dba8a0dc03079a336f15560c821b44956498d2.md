# 被低估的 science 的 examples

---

## 正文

Braben 在 [Scientific Freedom](https://nintil.com/review-scientific-freedom) 中提出的一个观点，也是其他人在别处提出过的观点，是 science 并不像看起来那样欢迎新的 ideas。peer review 的 process 阻碍了新的 breakthroughs，而一个人眼中的 crankery，在另一个人眼中却是 brilliant insight，至少通常的说法是这样。

你可能想到的这类 examples 都很久远：Galileo 与 heliocentrism，或者 Semmelweis 与 hand washing，以及更一般意义上的 germ theory of disease。因此，人们可能认为 science _过去_是那样，而如今它已经 self-correcting 到不再有这个问题的程度：Peers 如今是 fair judges；great ideas 会立即被 domain experts recognized。

但事实并非如此。与过去相比，情况是否有所改变，很难说，但我在这里想提出一个较小的观点：**有 non-trivial number 的 key discoveries 曾在某个时候被 leading scientists 和 expert commissions rejected、mocked 或 ignored**。当然，它们最终 succeeded 了，这也是我们知道这些 cases 的原因。但_它们本来可以更早、更快地 succeeded_。

我在这里想表达的重点不是这些具体 examples 本身，而是这些 examples 揭示了 science 的什么特征。在其中许多 cases 中，papers 在遭到一两家 journals rejected 后，最终还是 published 了。在另一些 cases 中，ideas 被 ignored 了几十年，或者受到 vigorously debated。

最令人担忧的不是 publications 中的 peer review，毕竟 journals 很多，虽然在 _Cell Structure and Function_ 上 publishing 看起来不如一篇 _Nature_ publication 那么好，但总还是有意义的，尤其当它确实重要时。问题出在 giving grants 的时候。这些 examples 表明，非常重要的 science 成就在 journal 的情境下并没有被 peers judged 为重要；因此，假设同样这些 peers 在 NIH 的 study section 中 handing out grants 时，可能决定丢弃一份开展他们认为无足轻重的 research 的 proposal，并非不合理。

当然，要求一个 resources 并非 infinite 的 system 能够对哪些工作获得 funded 或 published 做到 perfect filtering，是不合理的。一定数量的 false negatives 是必须接受的。但_如果_有人能比某个 discipline 的 peer reviewers 做得更好，或者至少以不同方式 rate proposals，那么就可能找到那些 false negatives，并使它们获得 funded。这也正是 Braben 通过 Venture Research 所做的事。

如果能收集到更多 examples，那就太好了；如果你知道一些，请发送到 [\[email protected\]](https://nintil.com/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection)。

## 被低估的 science 的 examples

## ornithine cycle（鸟氨酸循环）

来源：[Krebs (1973)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1016/0307-4412\(73\)90048-4), [Krebs (1970)](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/405199), [Kulkarni & Simon](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0364021388900201) (1998)

Hans Krebs 最著名的成就是他对 Krebs cycle 的 discovery，它也叫 tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA)，又叫 citric acid cycle (CAC)，处于 cellular energy production 的核心。在他 discovery TCA 之前，Krebs 正在研究 ornithine（或 urea cycle）。当时他三十出头，在 Otto Warburg 的 lab 工作，他的 research 涉及将 slices of liver 与各种 reagents 一起 suspending，以观察 liver 会如何处理它们（最终，他们 searching for 的是 uric acid）。结果，开创了 liver slice technique 的 Warburg 认为，这项 technique 只对 respiration 或 glycolysis 这样的 catabolic processes 有用，却不适用于新的 proteins 的 synthesis。

> **Warburg 并不 support Krebs’ idea**，或许是因为他认为 energy-absorbing reactions（与 oxidation reactions 相对）不会在 tissue slices 中 go forward。**1931 年，当 Krebs 获得自由，可以 initiate 自己的一项 major research enterprise 时，他决定开始进行此前构想的那类 experiments。**

> 绝大多数 scientists 接受了 1932 年 presented 的 evidence，认为它 convincing，并热情地发表了评论。Warburg 安排 Kaiser Wilhelm Gesellschaft 向我发出正式邀请，由其 President Max Planck 签署，邀请我到 Berlin 就这一 subject 发言。$$...$$ **但也有 adverse criticisms。** $$...$$ 直到 1956 年，我自己在 Oxford 的 Department 中的 Bronk 和 Fisher 仍 reported ~，在某些 conditions 下，citrulline 在 promoting urea formation from ornithine 方面不如 ornithine effective，因此他们 concluded，citrulline 不可能是 intermediate。London 和 Trowell 的 results 是由 inadequate perfusion technique 造成的。Bach 和 Williamson 的 conclusions 则基于一个 wrong assumption，即 arginine 和 ornithine 会 readily penetrate liver slices。事实上，rates of penetration 是 relatively slow 的。虽然 Bronk 和 Fisher 的 observations 是 correct 的，但他们的 interpretation 是 mistaken 的。

> 我的 idea 是，在 saline medium 中 incubated 的 liver slices 的 in vitro system，提供了一种 novel 且 easy 的 method，能够在各种 conditions 下 measuring accurately urea synthesis 的 rate。由于一只 small rat 的 liver 能够 supplies 众多 samples，因此可以 carry out 许多 parallel tests。此前从未有过 comparable method。**许多 biochemists 和 physiologists——包括 Otto Warburg——得知这一点时都相当惊讶**：slices 能够 performing synthetic energy- requiring processes

> 另一个推动力，尤其是在我早年的时候，是我渴望向**那些怀疑我能否在这个 field 取得成功的人**证明，我选择 scientist 这一职业是对的。这些人包括我的父亲，他是一位 surgeon，也是他启发我从事 medicine；**我的老师 Otto Warburg（我想是这样）**——以及我自己。

## Jet engines（喷气发动机）

来源：[Ricon (2015)](https://nintil.com/is-war-necessary-for-economic-growth-a-review-and-some-aviation-history-love/), [Ricon(2016)](https://nintil.com/aircraft-progress-before-and-after-world-war-ii#:%7E:text=In%20its%20present%20state%2C%20and,stringent%20weight%20requirements%20imposed%20by), [Ruttan (2006)](https://www.amazon.com/Vernon-Ruttan-Procurement-Technology-Development/dp/B01FMVMQ1M)

Jet engines 由 von Ohain 和 Whittle 同时 invented（在 1937 年）。von Ohain 立即获得了 Ersnt Heinkel（那家[同名](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinkel) aircraft manufacturer 的所有者）的支持，Whittle 却没有。他最初遭到 UK 的 Air Ministry 的抵制，而 US 的 research 则因 NACA（NASA 的前身）以及 National Academy of Sciences 对这项 technology 极为悲观的 assessments 而陷入停滞：

> 1920s 后期，在 Royal Air Force (RAF) Staff College 任职期间，Whittle 制订了一份 jet aircraft development 的 proposal。**Air Ministry 和 aircraft industry 都认为 Whittle 的 proposal 太过 radical**。1935 年，在两位前 RAF officers 的 financial assistance 下，他成立了一家 private firm，Power Jets，以开发他的 ideas。在 Thompson Huston Company 的协助下，Whittle 于 February 1937 准备好了一台可供 testing 的 engine。虽然这次 test 仅 partly successful，Air Ministry 还是给了 Power Jets 一笔 small grant，以继续 tests。在 October 1938 的 successful tests 后，Whittle 获得了 Air Ministry 的一份 contract，用于 build 一台 flight engine。$$...$$ Air Ministry 最初缺乏热情，是因为不愿将 funds 从 improve piston-propeller engine 的 performance 的工作中 divert 出来 (Cook 1991, pp. 97–114; Bobo 2001)。

> 即使在 1930s 后期 Germany 和 Britain 正在开展 advanced jet aircraft research and development 时，**NACA 的 staff 和 board 仍然对 gas turbines 用于 aircraft propulsion 的 technical and economic viability 持 skeptical 态度** (Hunsaker 1952)。

> _就其目前的状态而言，即使考虑到在采用近期提出的 higher temperatures 后可能实现的 improvements，gas turbine engine 也很难被认为在 airplanes 上有 feasible application，主要是因为难以 complying with aeronautics 所施加的 stringent weight requirements。_目前 airplanes 使用的 internal-combustion engine equipment 的 weights 约为 1.1 pounds per hp，而以现有 materials，让 gas turbine 接近这样的数值似乎是不可能的。
> 
> National Academy of Sciences，Committee on Gas Turbines（1940 年 6 月）

## mRNA vaccines（mRNA 疫苗）

来源：[STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2020/11/10/the-story-of-mrna-how-a-once-dismissed-idea-became-a-leading-technology-in-the-covid-vaccine-race/) (2020)

这些 vaccines 最近因其在 COVID 中的 application 而备受关注，但在 research 刚开始时，参与其中的关键 scientist Katalin Kariko 难以获得 funding，几乎放弃：

> Katalin Karikó 在整个 1990s 不断遭到拒绝。**她的 work 试图 harness the power of mRNA 来 fight disease，但对 government grants、corporate funding，甚至她自己 colleagues 的 support 而言，都显得太过异想天开**。$$...$$
> 
> 这确实是一个障碍，现在可能仍然如此，但 Karikó 坚信自己能绕过它。很少有人像她一样有信心。
> 
> “每天晚上我都在忙：grant、grant、grant，”Karikó 回忆道，说的是她为获得 funding 所做的努力。“可得到的答复总是不、不、不。”
> 
> 到 1995 年，在 University of Pennsylvania 任教六年后，Karikó 被降职了。她原本正走在晋升 full professorship 的道路上，但**由于没有资金来 support 她在 mRNA 方面的 work，她的上司觉得再坚持下去毫无意义**。
> 
> 她又回到了 scientific academy 的较低层级。
> 
> “通常，到了这一步，人们就会告别离开，因为实在太难受了，”Karikó 说。$$...$$
> 
> Weissman 说，**那项 discovery 从 2005 年开始在一系列 scientific papers 中得到 described，但起初基本无人注意**，不过，对那些在这项 technology 的艰难岁月中始终坚守信念的 mRNA researchers 来说，它证明了他们的坚持并没有错。它也为随后即将到来的 vaccine 冲刺鸣响了发令枪。

与此相关，那篇 [introducing](https://twitter.com/goodwish916/status/1329234124394041345) stabilized coronavirus spikes 的 paper——这些 spikes 是 COVID vaccines 的组成部分——曾五次遭拒，其中包括 Cell、Nature 或 Science 的拒绝。

## Airplanes（飞机）

来源：[New York Times](http://www.bretswanson.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Flying-Machines-Which-Do-Not-Fly-graphic-NYT-1903-headline-2.0.pdf) (1903)、[The Engineering Magazine](https://paleofuture.gizmodo.com/doubts-about-the-airplane-in-1909-emotion-has-run-away-1782882911) (1909)、[Ricon (2015)](https://nintil.com/linear-models-comments-on-ridley/)

如今，Airplanes 的 safe 和 widespread 已经令人习以为常。但就在不那么久以前，还有人这样写道：

> 或许可以设想，一种真正能够 fly 的 flying machine，可能要靠 mathematicians 和 mechanicians 共同且持续的努力，经过一百万到一千万年才能 evolved 出来；当然，前提是我们在此期间能够 eliminate 一些小小的缺陷与窘境，例如 inorganic materials 中 weight 与 strength 之间现有的 relation。毫无疑问，这些 problems 对感兴趣的人颇有吸引力，但在普通人看来，这些努力似乎可以用在更有成效的地方。\[注：The Wright brothers 在两个月后成功 flown 了他们的第一架 airplane\]

> 我们并不质疑 Wrights 的 mechanical achievement 是否有趣或卓越。显然，没有理由认为他们不能大幅 better 任何 recorded performance——fly 到数千 feet 的 high，或达到数百 miles 的 distance。我们的 skepticism 只针对 aeroplane 目前或未来可能取得的任何 achievement 的 utilitarian value。**我们不相信它将来会成为任何意义上的 commercial vehicle。我们不相信它会在 sport 领域占据多么重要的地位。我们不相信它的 military importance 像通常所认为的那样大，也不相信它会 extend 到 scouting 和 courier service 的 range 之外（偶然情况除外）。**

此外，就 aerodynamic theory 整体而言，下面两段引文相隔 75 年，恰好说明：即使某件事看起来没有前景或用处，它最终也可能成为一门 discipline 的核心：

> 截至目前，Mathematics 用于 flying 几乎毫无用处（14th Annual Report of the Aeronautical Society of Great Britain, 1879）
> 
> 人们发现，来自 pure mathematicians 乐土的 Mathematical theories 适合 describe aircraft 所 produced 的 airflow，其 accuracy 如此出色，以至于可以 applied directly 于 airplane design。（Theodore von Karman, 1954）

## ATP 的 synthesis 的 mechanism

来源：Cross ([2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-018-05880-w))

ATP 是 cell 的 energy currency，主要由 ATP synthase 来 manufactured，而 ATP synthase 又是最美丽的 proteins 之一。它是一个真正的 motor，由 protons 来 driven，能够 rotates。Paul Boyer 因此在 1997 获得了 Nobel。然而，这个 field 的 leading journal，The Journal of Biological Chemistry，拒绝 publish 他的 work。

> 正如 transformational ideas 经常遇到的情况，**早期的 reactions 是 negative。当 _Journal of Biological Chemistry_ rejected 我们的 manuscript 时**，其中包含 supporting 这个 concept 的 data，Boyer 毫无怨意地告诉我，他能理解他们为什么这样做——“这是一个非常惊人的 claim。”

## Oxidative phosphorylation（氧化磷酸化）

来源：Wikipedia ([2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative_phosphorylation)), Prebble ([2002](https://www.cell.com/trends/biochemical-sciences/pdf/S0968-0004\(02\)02059-5.pdf))

这也简称 Oxphos，是 ATP synthase 所 embedded 的[整个 process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative_phosphorylation)。Peter Mitchell 不得不艰难抗争，甚至在他因自己的 contribution 于 1978 获得 Nobel Prize 后，这场抗争仍在继续！他在 Cornwall 建立了自己的 private research institute，仅仅依靠 private funding 才得以坚持下去。

> ‘1950s 后期之后的那些年对我而言格外艰难，当时大多数 biochemists（但不包括 David Keilin）都在 rejecting 有关 chemiosmotic coupling 的 suggestions。我不得不付出极大的努力，才能让同事们认真对待这些 ideas。如今，这项 work 已经变得如此理所当然，仿佛 chemiosmotic theory 从一开始就是 self-evident，这让我有些难过 $$...$$
> 
> 或许 Efraim Racker 的话概括了这些困难：他认为 Mitchell 的 ideas，即他的‘hypothetical proton gradient 和 imaginary membrane potential’，就像‘宫廷小丑或末日先知的宣言’

## Semiconductor heterostructure（半导体异质结构）

来源：[Kroemer](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2000/kroemer/biographical/) (2000)

> 最后一步发生在 1963，当时我在 CA 的 Palo Alto 的 Varian Associates 工作。一位同事——Dr. Sol Miller——做了一场关于新的 semiconductor diode laser 的 research colloquium。他 reported 说，experts 已经 concluded，在 room temperature 下 achieve steady-state population inversion 是 fundamentally impossible 的，因为 injected carriers 会过快地从 junction 的 opposite side diffuse out。我立即反驳：“但那是一堆……；你只需要给 outer regions 一个 wider energy gap。”我把这个 idea 写下来，并**将 paper submitted 给 _Applied Physics Letters_，却被 rejected。别人劝我不要抗争这次 rejection，而是将它 submit 给 _Proceedings of the IEEE_，它在那里被 published，却遭到 ignored**。我还写了一份 patent，那可能比 _Proc. IEEE_ 上的那篇更好的 paper。
> 
> 接着，最后的讽刺来了：我**被 refused 用于 work on 这种新的 laser 的 resources**，理由是它不可能有任何 applications。碰巧，Gunn effect 刚刚被 discovered，而我一直对 hot-electron negative-resistance effects 感兴趣，因此我在接下来的 ten years 里 worked on Gunn effect，没有参与 laser 最后的 technological realization。

## Lasers（激光）

Polanyi ([2020](http://sites.utoronto.ca/jpolanyi/profile/profile2.html#:%7E:text=In%201960%20Polanyi%20submitted%20a,Maser%20Dependent%20on%20Vibrational%20Excitation%22.&text=Additionally%2C%20Polanyi%20noted%20that%20such,paper%20as%20lacking%20scientific%20interest.)?), Nature ([2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys1675))

John Polanyi 帮助 pioneer 了 chemical lasers 这一 field，而 Theodore Maiman 被公认为 laser 的 inventor。前者获得了 Nobel prize。这里无需解释 lasers 有什么用途。

> _Physical Review Letters_ 以缺乏 scientific interest 为由 rejected 了这篇 paper。不久之后，他们又以同样的理由 rejected 了 T. Maiman 关于第一个 operating laser 的 report。Polanyi 在 Georgian Bay 的一个岛上度假时，十分偶然地读到了这第二次 rejection 的消息。1960 的 September 返回 Toronto 后，他将完全相同的 manuscript submitted 给 _[Journal of Chemical Physics](http://eir.library.utoronto.ca/eir/detail.cfm?Resources__ID=1338&T=J)_，它很快便在那里 published。
> 
> 此后，vibrational lasers，尤其是 chemical lasers（分别由 C.K.N. Patel 和 G.C. Pimentel 开创），已经 developed 成最 powerful 的 infrared radiation 的 sources。对于那些坚持要求 applications 有明显前景的 basic research 的 sponsors，Polanyi 喜欢问他们：他们是否会有足够的远见，去 support 对 barely detectable luminescence 的 studies，以此作为 development 现存最 powerful 的 lasers 的手段。

> 众所周知，Maiman 的 write-up 被 _Physical Review Letters_ rejected，该刊 editors judged 它不过是 Charles Townes 等人领导的、与 masers 相关的一连串 research 中的又一小段。同样广为人知的是，laser 被称为“一个正在寻找问题的 solution”——Townes 曾说，他并未将此视为笑话，而是将其视为挑战

## radio-immunoassay（放射免疫测定）

来源：Watts ([2011](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736\(11\)61078-7/fulltext))

这种 technique 涉及 using 一个附着着 radioactive isotope 的 anti-body，这个 antibody 会 bind 到所关注的 target，而这就能通过 radiation 显现出来。它是由 Rosalyn S. Yalow developed 的，并使她在 1977 年获得了 Nobel。但走到这一步并不容易：

> 通过 Using immunoassay，她和 Berson 已经 shown，在 insulin-treated research participants 的 circulation 中存在 insulin binding antibody。这一 finding 与 1950s 的 immunology 正统观念相悖，后者认为 immune system 无法 recognise 像 protein molecules 这么小的 entities。**_Science_ rejected 了这篇 paper；_Journal of Clinical Investigation_ 也如此**。但在 paper 的标题经过一处不动声色的改动后，后者让步了。这份 report 于 1956 年发表。后来，Yalow 很喜欢在 lectures 中展示 rejection letter 的幻灯片。
> 
> **尽管起初人们不愿 accept 他们的 findings，他们却没有因此退缩**，Yalow 和 Berson 坚持了下来。随后一篇于 1960 年 published、讨论 use RIA 来 measure 人体中的 endogenous insulin 的 paper，正如 Professor Jesse Roth 所形容的那样，“席卷了整个 field”。Roth 是一位如今在纽约 North Shore Long Island Jewish Health System 工作的 endocrinologist；他在 early 1960s 初次遇见 Yalow 和 Berson 时，还是一位一心想学习如何 do research 的年轻 physician。“1960 年的 paper 很长，也很详细，”他说，“因为他们试图应对**所有针对 RIA 的 opposition**。”做到这一点后，clinicians 开始看到了 RIA 的吸引力。

## Endosymbiotic theory（内共生理论）

来源：Yandell ([2015](https://www.the-scientist.com/careers/riding-out-rejection-35867)), Slavov ([2014](https://majesticforest.wordpress.com/2014/08/15/papers-that-triumphed-over-their-rejections/))

高中时第一次学到 mitochondria，我心想，嗯，这些看起来像小小的 bacteria，会不会是 cells 不知怎么把它们吃了下去，然后成了它们的一部分？结果还真是，而且 Lynn Margulis 早在 60s 就已经 shown 了这一点。但要 convincing scientific community 她是对的，可不是件容易的事！

> Lynn Margulis：1966 年，我写了一篇关于 symbiogenesis 的 paper，题为“The Origin of Mitosing \[Eukaryotic\] Cells”，讨论除 bacteria 以外所有 cells 的 origin。（bacterial cells 的 origin 就是 life 本身的 origin。）这篇 paper 被大约十五家 scientific journals rejected，因为它存在缺陷；另外，它太新了，没有人能够 evaluate 它。最后，The Journal of Theoretical Biology 的 editor James F. ## Quasicrystals（准晶体）

来源：Efron ([2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/cxo.12972)), Wikipedia ([2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman))

Crystals 是 periodic 的 ordered structures，由一个不断 repeats itself 的 basic unit formed。Quasicrystals 是 ordered 的，但不是 periodic 的。听起来似乎没什么，却显然颇有争议！

> Dan Shechtman 将一篇关于 quasicrystals 的 paper submitted 给 Physical Review Letters，但**它被 rejected，理由是它不会引起 physicists 的任何兴趣**。随后，他将自己的 work submitted 给 journal Metallurgic Transactions，后者 published 了这篇 paper；这项 work 为 Shechtman 在 2011 年赢得 Nobel Prize 奠定了基础。

> 从 Shechtman 在 1984 年 published 关于 quasicrystals 的 findings 那天起，直到 [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling) 去世（1994），**Shechtman 一直遭受他针对 non-periodic interpretation 的敌意**。“很长一段时间，我都是在与全世界对抗，”他说。“我受到嘲笑，还被人说教，讲解 crystallography 的 basics。**反对我的 findings 的领头人是两度获得 Nobel Laureate 的 Linus Pauling**，他是 American Chemical Society 的偶像，也是全世界最著名的 scientists 之一。多年来，直到生命的最后一天，他都在反对 crystals 中的 quasi-periodicity。他错了，而过了一段时间，知道他是错的，我反而开始享受这场 scientific battle 的每一刻。”

> [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling) 有句著名的话：“**根本不存在 quasicrystals，只有 quasi-scientists**。”$$[17$$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman#cite_note-reuters20111005-17) Pauling 显然不知道 [H. Kleinert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagen_Kleinert) 和 K. Maki 于 1981 年写的一篇 paper，其中已经指出，non-periodic 的 [Icosahedral Phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_Phase) 有可能存在于 [quasicrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal) 中$$[18$$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman#cite_note-18)（参见[历史说明](http://users.physik.fu-berlin.de/%7Ekleinert/icosahedral/index.html)）。**Shechtman 的 research group 负责人叫他“回去读 textbook”，几天后又“以给团队‘丢脸’为由要求他离开。**”$$[19$$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman#cite_note-19) Shechtman 感到沮丧。$$[17$$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman#cite_note-reuters20111005-17) 他的 paper 一经 publication，其他 scientists 就开始 confirm 并 accept 关于 quasicrystals 的 existence 的 empirical findings。

## 关于 kinetic theory of gases 的早期 work

> 1820 年 5 月 24 日，一篇题为《对 Heat、Gases、Gravitation 等的 Causes、Laws 和 Principal Phenomena 的 Mathematical Inquiry》的 manuscript 被 submitted 给 Davies Gilbert，以便在 Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society 上 publication。作者是 John Herapath (I790-I868)，他的 article 包含了对 kinetic theory of gases 的 comprehensive（尽管有些 faulty）exposition。Sir Humphry Davy 于 1820 年 11 月 30 日出任 Royal Society 的 Presidency，此后主要由他决定这篇 article 的命运，他也为此给 Herapath 写过几封信。**在 Royal Society 对其 publication 存在相当大的 opposition 这一点变得明确之后，Herapath withdrew 了这篇 article**，转而将它寄给 Annals of Philosophy，最终于 I82I (i) 在那里刊出。**Herapath 的 theory 几乎没有受到 scientists 的注意，直到三十五年后**，kinetic theory 才由 Joule、Kronig、Clausius 和 Maxwell revived。$$...$$
> 
> 虽然我们认为 Davy 应对 Herapath 的 theory 遭到 rejection 负主要责任，但应该清楚，Davy 的过错主要在于，他与同时代的大多数人一样，对 theoretical physics 抱有偏见。\* **Waterston 关于 kinetic theory 的 work 在二十五年后被 Royal Society completely suppressed** ( 5)，与他不同，**Herapath 至少获得了公开阐述自己 views 的机会，却仍未能使任何 scientists 接受这些 views。**值得注意的是，即使在十九世纪初的英国，像 Davy 这样有影响力的 scientists 支持 heat 是一种 mode of motion 的 idea，与这一 idea consistent 的某个特定 theory 的 mathematical formulation 也无法赢得任何支持。

## planarians 的 brain 之外的 memories 的 Persistance

来源：Duhaime-Ross ([2015](https://www.theverge.com/2015/3/18/8225321/memory-research-flatworm-cannibalism-james-mcconnell-michael-levin))

Planarians 是 flatworms，具有一种特殊能力：即使被切成很小的碎块，也能 growing back。每个碎块都能 regrow 出一条完整的 worm。Planarians 的 head 中有一个 main ganglia，算是一种 primitive 的“brain”。关于 memories 如何 stored，标准答案是某种围绕 brain 中的 synapses 的说法。所以，当你切掉一只 planarian 的 head，而它又 grows 出一个新的时，你可能教过它的任何东西（比如 To avoid light）都应该已经被 forgotten。但事实并非如此。不知怎的，memories 被 stored 在 body 的其他地方。如果你以前没接触过这件事，可能会觉得这完全不可信，但这正是 [James McConnell](https://www.theverge.com/2015/3/18/8225321/memory-research-flatworm-cannibalism-james-mcconnell-michael-levin) 早在 70 年代就发现的现象；这一 finding 一直 controversial，最终被 rejected 和 ignored，直到 Michael Levin 把它从湮没中重新发掘出来，用更现代的 techniques 表明：没错，它确实会[发生](https://jeb.biologists.org/content/216/20/3799)。

这是否 generalizes 到其他 species，以及背后的 mechanism 是什么，仍然 unknown。与这份列表中的其他 findings 不同，这一项尤其仍有争议，不过争议程度或许比以前有所减轻。我选择它，是想说明，当我们面对一个仍处于我们通常的 model of the world 之外的 finding 时，那是什么感觉；因为在那些看起来足够 plausible、却仍处于 science 外缘灰色地带的事例中，它最接近这样的情形。据我所能查到的资料，还没有对 Levin 的 experiment 的 formal critique，也没有 failure to replicate。

> 然而，McConnell 的 study 得出的惊人 takeaway——neurons 之外的 cells 也能 store information——使许多人开始质疑这项 study 的 methodology 和 conclusions。1960 年，他 published 了第二个 experiment，把他的 theories 推到了所有人想象之外。这次，scientific community 完全困惑了。
> 
> 这项 study 被广泛称为“the cannibalism experiment”，它 tested 了 McConnell 的另一个 theory：memory 可以通过一种叫作“memory-RNA”的东西，从一条 flatworm transferred chemically 到另一条。McConnell 提出，Memory-RNA 是 RNA 的一种特殊 form——RNA 是填补 DNA 与 proteins 之间空隙的 genetic information 的 intermediary form——能够在 brain 之外 store long-term memories。至少可以说，他的 method 是 unorthodox：McConnell 把 trained flatworms 的碎块喂给它们那些 untrained 的同类。McConnell 声称，结果是，这些 untrained flatworms performed 了 trained flatworms 先前 learned 的 behaviors。简而言之，死去的 flatworms 的 memories 找到了一个新家。
> 
> **“Biologists 和 chemists 都说‘不可能’，”**Reeva Kimble 回忆道，她在 1959 年和 1960 年期间为 McConnell 做 undergraduate research。Reeva 后来嫁给了 Daniel Kimble；这名学生曾为 McConnell 的第一个 regeneration experiment gathered data。McConnell 的反对者无法理解他的 findings。Reeva 说，对他们而言，**“没有 mechanism 可以用来理解他的 result，所以它必定是一派胡言，**”$$...$$
> 
> L’etoile 坦言，McConnell 的失败仍然给 Levin 的 study 笼罩着巨大的阴影。她说，这是“experimental biology 领域的一个警示故事”，带着“许多历史包袱”。她说，**如此多的 research groups 在 ‘60s 和 ‘70s 都难以 replicating flatworm training protocols，这意味着，要说服她，门槛已经非常高了**。“我想，我对 \[flatworm\] training 抱有很大的 skepticism。”

## 为什么会发生这种事？

前些时候我写过两篇文章，[论 beliefs 的明确 rejection 和 acceptance](https://nintil.com/on-the-express-acceptance-and-rejection-of-beliefs/)，讨论为什么 discard 你一开始就认为很不可能的 evidence 和 theories 是 rational 的。在理想世界中，information acquisition 和 verification 是 costless 的，但一旦引入 costs，你就回到了现实世界，需要决定优先关注什么。因此，某个 field 的 experts 可能根据自己先前的 experience 和 knowledge，rationally rejected 新的、令人惊讶的、出乎意料的 ideas。有些人将这种倾向称为 _[Semmelweis reflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semmelweis_reflex#:%7E:text=The%20Semmelweis%20reflex%20or%20%22Semmelweis,norms%2C%20beliefs%2C%20or%20paradigms.)_。

在这些 examples 中，许多都具有以下共同线索，或是其中几项的组合：

-   某人在一个 field 内被视为 outsider
-   supports 或 undermines claims 的 data 是 noisy 的。我认为这才是背后的核心原因，是大多数问题的根源。而这又是因为缺乏能够 produce precise observations 的 tooling，或者缺乏用来 increase sample sizes 的 funding。
-   结论是 right，但 reasons 是 wrong 的。这些 reasons 可能被驳倒。Rejecting "A->B" 并不意味着 "B" 是 false，而只是意味着 "A" 不是 B 的好 reason。

这些 rationales 可能相互影响，形成恶性的 feedback loops，形成我所说的 _scientific flip-flopping_。如果某个 idea 因为 right 或 wrong reasons 被过早 rejected，而这个 field 已经转向别处，那么这个 idea 可能很难再次得到 reconsidered。oncoviruses 或 cancer immunotherapy 就是如此：

## 原文

One of the points that Braben makes in [Scientific Freedom](https://nintil.com/review-scientific-freedom), and one that others have made elsewhere, is that science is not as welcoming of new ideas as it may seem. The process of peer review stands in the way of new breakthroughs, and one man's crankery is another's brilliant insight, or so goes the story.

The examples of this that probably come to mind are old: Galileo and heliocentrism or perhaps Semmelweis and hand washing and in general the germ theory of disease. So one may think that science _used to_ be like that and now it is self-correcting to the point where it's not an issue: Peers are now fair judges; great ideas are immediately recognized by domain experts.

But this is not true. It's hard to say if the situation has changed relative to how it used to be, but here I want to make the smaller point that **a non-trivial number of key discoveries have been at some point rejected, mocked, or ignored by leading scientists and expert commissions**. Sure, eventually they succeeded, this is why we know about these cases. But _they could have succeeded earlier, faster_.

The point I want to make here is not the precise examples themselves; rather what the examples reveal about science. In many of these cases the papers were eventually published when one or two journals rejected them. In others, the ideas were ignored for decades, or were vigorously debated.

The most worrying thing is not peer review in the context of publications, after all there are a lot of journals and while publishing in _Cell Structure and Function_ looks not as nice as a _Nature_ publication, it's still something, especially if it's actually important. The issue is when giving grants. The examples illustrate that very important feats of science weren't judged as such by peers in a journal context; so it is not unreasonable to assume that the same peers when handing out grants in a study section at NIH may decide to bin a proposal to do research they consider of little consequence.

Of course, it would be unreasonable to ask of a system with non-infinite resources to be able to do perfect filtering of what gets funded or published. A number of false negatives is to be accepted. But _If_ one could do better than the peer reviewers in a given discipline, or at least rate proposals differently, then one could find those false negatives and get them funded. This is again what Braben did with Venture Research.

I'd be great to collect even more examples, if you have some send them to [\[email protected\]](https://nintil.com/cdn-cgi/l/email-protection).

## The ornithine cycle

Source: [Krebs (1973)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1016/0307-4412\(73\)90048-4), [Krebs (1970)](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/405199), [Kulkarni & Simon](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0364021388900201) (1998)

Hans Krebs is most famous for his discovery of the Krebs cycle aka the tricarboxylic acid cycle (TCA) aka the citric acid cycle (CAC) which lies at the heart of cellular energy production. Prior to his discovery of the TCA, Krebs was working on the ornithine (or urea cycle). Back then in his early thirties, Krebs was working in the lab of Otto Warburg and his research involved suspending slices of liver with various reagents to see what the liver would do with them (Ultimately, uric acid is what they were searching for). Well turns out that Warburg - who had pioneered the liver slice technique - thought that it was only useful for catabolic processes like respiration or glycolysis. But not the synthesis of new proteins.

> **Warburg did not support Krebs’ idea**, perhaps because he thought that energy-absorbing reactions (as contrasted with oxidation reactions) would not go forward in tissue slices. **When Krebs got freedom to initiate a major research enterprise of his own, in 1931, he decided to begin experiments of the sort he had conceived.**

> The great majority of scientists accepted the evidence presented in 1932 as convincing and commented enthusiastically. Warburg arranged a formal invitation for me by the Kaiser Wilhelm Gesellschaft, signed by Max Planck, the President, to speak on the subject in Berlin. \[...\] **But there were also adverse criticisms.** \[...\] As late as 1956 Bronk and Fisher reported ~ from my own Department at Oxford that under certain conditions citrulline is less effective than ornithine in promoting urea formation from ornithine and they concluded that citrulline cannot be an intermediate. The results of London and of Trowell were due to inadequate perfusion technique. The conclusions of Bach and Williamson were based on the wrong assumption that arginine and ornithine readily penetrate liver slices. In fact the rates of penetration are relatively slow. While the observations of Bronk and Fisher were correct their interpretation was mistaken.

> My idea was that the in vitro system of liver slices, incubated in a saline medium, provided a novel and easy method for measuring accurately the rate of urea synthesis under a variety of conditions. As one small rat liver supplies numerous samples, it is possible to carry out many parallel tests. No comparable method had been available before. **Many biochemists and physiologists—including Otto Warburg—were quite surprised to learn** that slices are capable of performing synthetic energy- requiring processes

> Another driving force, especially in my earlier days, was my ambition to justify my choice of career as a scientist vis-à-vis **those who were doubtful about my ability to make a success in this field**. These included my father, a surgeon who had inspired me to take up medicine, **my teacher Otto Warburg (I believe)**—and myself.

## Quasicrystals

Source: Efron ([2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/cxo.12972)), Wikipedia ([2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman))

Crystals are ordered structures that are periodic, formed of a basic unit that repeats itself. Quasicrystals are ordered but not periodic. Sounds innocent enough, but apparently quite controversial!

> Dan Shechtman submitted a paper on quasicrystals to Physical Review Letters, but **it was rejected on the grounds that it would not be of any interest to physicists**. He then submitted his work to the journal Metallurgic Transactions, which published the paper; this work formed the basis of Shechtman winning the Nobel Prize in 2011.

> From the day Shechtman published his findings on quasicrystals in 1984 to the day [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling) died (1994), **Shechtman experienced hostility from him toward the non-periodic interpretation**. "For a long time it was me against the world," he said. "I was a subject of ridicule and lectures about the basics of crystallography. **The leader of the opposition to my findings was the two-time Nobel Laureate Linus Pauling**, the idol of the American Chemical Society and one of the most famous scientists in the world. For years, 'til his last day, he fought against quasi-periodicity in crystals. He was wrong, and after a while, I enjoyed every moment of this scientific battle, knowing that he was wrong."

> [Linus Pauling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linus_Pauling) is noted saying "**There is no such thing as quasicrystals, only quasi-scientists**."\[[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman#cite_note-reuters20111005-17) Pauling was apparently unaware of a paper in 1981 by [H. Kleinert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagen_Kleinert) and K. Maki which had pointed out the possibility of a non-periodic [Icosahedral Phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icosahedral_Phase) in [quasicrystals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasicrystal)\[[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman#cite_note-18) (see the [historical notes](http://users.physik.fu-berlin.de/%7Ekleinert/icosahedral/index.html)). **The head of Shechtman's research group told him to "go back and read the textbook" and a couple of days later "asked him to leave for 'bringing disgrace' on the team.**"\[[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman#cite_note-19) Shechtman felt dejected.\[[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shechtman#cite_note-reuters20111005-17) On publication of his paper, other scientists began to confirm and accept empirical findings of the existence of quasicrystals.

>

## Early work on the kinetic theory of gases

Source: Brush ([1963](https://www.jstor.org/stable/531271?seq=1))

## Persistance of memories outside of the brain in planarians

Source: Duhaime-Ross ([2015](https://www.theverge.com/2015/3/18/8225321/memory-research-flatworm-cannibalism-james-mcconnell-michael-levin))

Planarians are flatworms that have the peculiar ability of growing back even if they are chopped into tiny bits. Each bit can regrow a full worm. Planarians have a main ganglia in their head, a sort of primitive "brain". The standard answer for how memories are stored in some story about the synapses in the brain. So when you chop off the head of a planarian and grows a new one, anything you may have taught it (To avoid light say) should have been forgotten. But no. Somehow, the memories are stored somewhere else in the body. This probably sounds utterly implausible to you if you haven't come across this before, but this is what [James McConnell](https://www.theverge.com/2015/3/18/8225321/memory-research-flatworm-cannibalism-james-mcconnell-michael-levin) found back in the 70s and the finding remained controversial, ultimately rejected and ignored until it was fished back from obscurity by Michael Levin, who used more modern techniques to show that yes, it [happens](https://jeb.biologists.org/content/216/20/3799).

Whether this generalizes to other species or what's the mechanism behind it remains unknown. Unlike the other findings in this list, this one in particular remains disputed, but perhaps to a lesser extent than it used to. I've chosen it to illustrate what it feels like to be looking at a finding that still stands outside of our usual model of the world, as is the closest thing that seems plausible enough that is yet in that gray area that is the outer rim of science. As far as I was able to find, there has been no formal critique or failure to replicate Levin's experiment.

> But the fantastic takeaway of McConnell’s study — that cells other than neurons could store information — caused many to question the study’s methodology and conclusions. In 1960, he published a second experiment, one that pushed his theories far beyond what anyone would have imagined. This one left the scientific community completely stumped.
> 
> Widely known as "the cannibalism experiment," the study tested another McConnell theory: that memory could be transferred chemically from one flatworm to another through something called "memory-RNA." Memory-RNA, McConnell suggested, was a special form of RNA — the intermediary form of genetic information that fills the gap between DNA and proteins — that could store long-term memories outside the brain. His method was unorthodox, to say the least: McConnell fed bits of trained flatworms to their untrained brethren. As a result, McConnell claimed, the untrained flatworms performed behaviors that the trained flatworms had previously learned. In short, the dead flatworms’ memories had found a new home.
> 
> **"Biologists and chemists said ‘no way,’"** recalls Reeva Kimble, who did undergraduate research for McConnell in 1959 and 1960. Reeva later married Daniel Kimble, the student who gathered data for McConnell’s first regeneration experiment. McConnell’s opponents couldn’t make sense of his findings. For them, **"there was no mechanism to understand his result, so it had to be hogwash,**" Reeva says. \[...\]
> 
> L’etoile readily admits that McConnell’s failures continue to loom large over Levin’s study. It’s a "cautionary tale in the field of experimental biology" — one that comes with "a lot of baggage," she says. **The fact that so many research groups had trouble replicating flatworm training protocols in the ‘60s and ‘70s means that the bar to convince her has been set very high**, she says. "I think I have a great deal of skepticism about \[flatworm\] training."

## Why does this happen?

I wrote two articles a while back, [On the express rejection and acceptance of beliefs](https://nintil.com/on-the-express-acceptance-and-rejection-of-beliefs/) on how it is rational to discard evidence and theories that you consider very unlikely to begin with. In an ideal world, information acquisition and verification is costless, but when you introduce costs you are back to the real world where you need to prioritize what to pay attention to. So new, surprising, unexpected ideas may rationally be rejected by people that are experts in a given field, on the grounds of their prior experience and knowledge. Some have given this tendency the name of the _[Semmelweis reflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semmelweis_reflex#:%7E:text=The%20Semmelweis%20reflex%20or%20%22Semmelweis,norms%2C%20beliefs%2C%20or%20paradigms.)_.

From many of these examples, a common thread is the following or a combination thereof:

-   Someone is seen as an outsider within a field
-   The data that supports or undermines claims is noisy. This I think is the core underlying reason, the root of most evil. In turn, the reason for this is lack of tooling to produce precise observations, or funding to increase sample sizes.
-   Being right, for the wrong reasons. The reasons may be shot down. Rejecting "A->B" does not mean "B" is false, it just means "A"was not a good reason for B.

These rationales can play into each other into nasty feedback loops, into what I call _scientific flip-flopping_. If an idea has been prematurely rejected for the right or wrong reasons and the field has moved on, it may be difficult for the idea to be reconsidered again. This was the case for oncoviruses or cancer immunotherapy:

![flip-flopping in cancer immunotherapy](https://nintil.com/images/2020-11-30-discoveries-ignored/flipflop.png)

The development of cancer immunotherapy, from Parish (2003)

Plausibly then, as a heuristic for detecting huge if true, yet ignored findings:

1.  Get a team of outsiders (With the right scientific mindset, they need to understand what they read!)
2.  Find areas where flip-flopping has occurred, or where there are anomalies, or intense debate about whether they are true or not. Find "huge if true" results.
3.  Fund them
4.  ???
5.  Profit

The problem is that in the process of doing 2. one may find also things that were rightly confined to the dustbin of history like [N rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N_ray) or [cold fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_fusion).
