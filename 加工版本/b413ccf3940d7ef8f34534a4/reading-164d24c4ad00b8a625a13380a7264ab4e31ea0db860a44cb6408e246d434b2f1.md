## 正文

最近一篇 Vox 文章汇集了一些[对 Gates foundation 的 criticism](http://www.vox.com/2015/6/10/8760199/gates-foundation-criticism?utm_content=buffer69218&utm_medium=social&utm_source=facebook.com&utm_campaign=buffer)。他们的主要论点是：

-   accountability 很差。我不知道该如何 evaluate 这一点，也不知道与其他 aid organizations 相比，Gates 的 accountable 程度如何。说到让 individual charity 更 accountable 或 effective，我不禁觉得，_一定_有更好的切入点——比如最近众多备受瞩目的 billionaire 向 Ivy League schools 的 donations。如果 academics 抱怨 Gates 的 accountability，却不抱怨 [Paulson](http://www.nytimes.com/2015/06/04/education/john-paulson-gives-400-million-to-harvard-for-engineering-school.html) 的，就很难不把这看作仅仅是他们增加自身对 global health spending 的 control 的手段。

-   可能存在 misprioritization（例如，比起 obesity/cancer/diabetes，更 focusing 于 richer countries 或 infectious diseases）。他们为此引用了[这](http://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2808%2961485-3/fulltext?version=printerFriendly)[两篇](http://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2809%2960571-7/abstract) Lancet 文章。

    关于 BMGF supports rich countries 的 accusation，似乎是基于他们提供 research grants 这一事实。拜托，这还用说。大部分 research 都发生在 US，因为好的 universities 和 doctors 都在那里。这并不意味着它对 poorer countries 没有帮助。

    关于他们 disproportionately 地 support infectious diseases 的 accusation，似乎是基于一张展示各种 categories 的 DALY burden 与 spending 的 plot，并指出其中的各种 outliers（polio 的 spending per DALY burden 很高，而“non-communicable disease”的 spending per DALY burden 很低，等等）。这同样显得相当不靠谱，因为并非每个 DALY 的 avert 成本都同样高；那些 lower-DALY causes 仍有可能更加 tractable。对于 non-communicable diseases，情况尤其如此：它们从 Gates 以外的地方（例如 NIH）获得了大量 funding，因为它们也是 rich people 的问题。

-   对 technology 的 emphasis 过多。

    > academic literature 中出现的另一个担忧是，Gates Foundation 过于 focused 于 drugs、vaccines，以及其他针对 global health problems 的 technological solutions。相比之下，许多 researchers 更希望 focus 于那些不那么令人兴奋却至关重要的工作，即 strengthening poorer countries 的 health systems。

    对此我无法多作评论，只能指出，到目前为止，我所知道的最 cost-effective 的 global health intervention 是 smallpox 的 eradication。如果 Gates 正争取再实现一个这样的案例（例如 malaria eradication），未来可能会极其 effective，但他们在这方面的大量 research funding 尚未结出成果。另一方面，strengthening local health systems 似乎也非常有用，但我很不了解这会包含哪些类型的 interventions，或者会有什么样的 results。

-   对 strict IP rules 的 Support，而这些 rules 会阻止 poor countries making cheap generic drugs。以我有限的了解来看，这似乎就是 Gates 在犯傻。可能与 [Microsoft 的前 CTO 在 running a patent troll](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_Ventures) 有关（Myhrvold 和 Gates 是好友）。

总的来说，我觉得这些意见还算有说服力。Gates 大概应该在 IP law 方面闭嘴，也许还应该更多地投入 infrastructure，而不只是 tech。但仅凭那些 Lancet 文章中那种 superficial analysis，就声称他们在“misfinancing global health”，实在相当扯淡。

## 原文

A recent Vox article puts together some [criticism of the Gates foundation](http://www.vox.com/2015/6/10/8760199/gates-foundation-criticism?utm_content=buffer69218&utm_medium=social&utm_source=facebook.com&utm_campaign=buffer). Their main points:

-   Poor accountability. I have no idea how to evaluate this one or how accountable Gates is compared to other aid organizations. In terms of making individual charity more accountable or effective, I can’t help but think that there _have_ to be better places to start—like the numerous recent high-profile billionaire donations to Ivy League schools. If academics complain about Gates’ accountability and not about [Paulson](http://www.nytimes.com/2015/06/04/education/john-paulson-gives-400-million-to-harvard-for-engineering-school.html)’s, it’s hard not to see it as simply a ploy to increase their own control over global health spending.

-   Potential misprioritization (eg focusing richer countries or infectious diseases over obesity/cancer/diabetes). They cite [these](http://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2808%2961485-3/fulltext?version=printerFriendly) [two](http://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736%2809%2960571-7/abstract) Lancet articles for this.

    The accusation that BMGF supports rich countries seems to be based on the fact that they make research grants. Well, duh. Most of the research happens in the US because that’s where the good universities and doctors are. That doesn’t mean it’s not helpful to poorer countries.

    The accusation that they support infectious diseases disproportionately seems to be based on a plot of DALY burden versus spending for various categories, and pointing to various outliers (polio has high spending per DALY burden, while “non-communicable disease” has very low spending per DALY burden, etc.) Again this seems pretty bogus, as not every DALY is equally expensive to avert; it may be that the lower-DALY causes are nevertheless more tractable. This is especially true for non-communicable diseases, which have a bunch of funding from places other than Gates (e.g. NIH) because they’re also rich people problems.

-   Too much emphasis on technology.

    > Another concern that comes up in the academic literature is that the Gates Foundation is too focused on drugs, vaccines, and other technological solutions for global health problems. But many researchers, by contrast, would prefer a focus on the less exciting but crucial work of strengthening the health systems of poorer countries.

    I can’t comment much on this, except to note that so far the most cost-effective global health intervention I’m aware of is the eradication of smallpox. If Gates is shooting for another case of that (e.g. malaria eradication), it could be tremendously effective in the future, but much of their research funding here has yet to bear fruit. On the other hand, strengthening local health systems also seems super useful, but I’m very unfamiliar with what types of interventions this would comprise or what kind of results.

-   Support for strict IP rules, which prevent poor countries from making cheap generic drugs. From my limited knowledge, this just seems to be Gates being dumb. Possibly related to [Microsoft’s former CTO running a patent troll](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_Ventures) (Myhrvold and Gates are buddies).

All in all, I find this moderately convincing. Gates should probably just shut up about IP law, and maybe work more in infrastructure, not just tech. But claiming they’re “misfinancing global health” based on the kind of superficial analysis in those Lancet articles is pretty BS.
