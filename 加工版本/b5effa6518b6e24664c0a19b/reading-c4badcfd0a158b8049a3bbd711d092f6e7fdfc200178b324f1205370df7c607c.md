## 正文

Kenneth Arrow 在 1963 年写了一篇 paper，题为 Uncertainty and the Welfare Economics of Medical Care。这篇 paper 经常出现在关于 healthcare 是否可以交给 market（[就像 bread 一样](https://krugman.blogs.nytimes.com/2009/07/25/why-markets-cant-cure-healthcare/)），还是应当有 [heavy state involvement](https://www.bloomberg.com/view/articles/2017-06-01/americans-sure-seem-to-like-universal-health-care) 的 debates 中。在这里，我会解释这篇 paper 说了什么，以及它在多大程度上是正确的。

那么，让我们看看[这篇 paper](https://web.stanford.edu/%7Ejay/health_class/Readings/Lecture01/arrow.pdf) 说了什么。

首先，Arrow 并不打算让这篇 paper 超出“针对 medical care 的 specific differentia 所作的一项 exploratory 且 tentative 的 study”这一范围。既然他这样说，那么在 paper 中发现错误也就不足为奇，何况它已经是几十年前的文章了。不过，另一方面，Arrow 获得过 Nobel Price，或许他确实很有先见之明，整篇文章都经受住了时间的考验。

随后，他 claims，这个 industry 的“special features”本质上源于这样一个事实：从 individual perspective 来看，healthare demand 是 strongly uncertain 的；而当 markets fail 时，societies 会在某种程度上建立 non-market social institutions，来 fix 那些 market failures。但他说，这些 institutions 会在 healthcare sector 中 induce non-competitive practices，从而阻碍它进一步接近 optimality。

### Medical-care market 的 Special characteristics

#### Demand（需求）

(Unpredictability) 首先，与 food 或 clothing 不同，对 healthcare 的 demand 并没有那么 predictable。人们只有在 ill 时才会 purchased 它，而 illness 很难 predict。也就是说，demand 是由 exogenous shocks 而非个人自己的 planning 所 driven 的。Arrow 觉得很难想到还有哪些 goods and services 也具有这种情况。他能想到的是 legal services（他还指出，它与 healthcare sector 存在一些 similarities）。

(Criticality) 其次，illness 可以 cause death、permanent injuries，以及 ability to earn 的 loss。鉴于我们的生活目标需要我们活着，并且保持 relatively healthy，因此保持 healthy 对我们至关重要。food 也同样如此，但只要有 sufficient income，就可以 avoided food deprivation。

在这一点上，Arrow 基本是正确的。第一种 special characteristic 的 examples 可以列为：healthcare、legal services、gadget repairing、home maintenance 或 car maintenance。对于第二种 characteristic，我们可以举出 healthcare、food、water 和 housing。因此，只有 healthcare 同时 meets 这两个 condition：它是唯一可能具有 unpredictable demand 的 critical good。

或者，准确地说，healthcare 的一部分是如此。就像某些通常被归入同一 category 的 goods 一样，healthcare 可以被 unbundled：cancer 和 heart diseases 可能相当 unpredictable 且 fatal，flu、headaches 和 cold 则可能是 mild 且 fairly predictable 的（通常在冬季前后）。既 predictable 又 critical 的 malaises 很少，其中 aging 大概是最主要的一种。对于 highly unpredictable 且 low criticality 的情况，mosquito bites 就是一个 example。

#### physician 的 Expected behaviour

(Moral provision) Healthcare 是一种 service，而不是 physical good。Arrow 说，Producing healthcare 与 providing 它是 synonymous 的。customer 无法在 consuming product 之前 test 它，而这种 relationship 中存在 trust 的成分。barber 也是如此。但对 physician 所要求的 ethical standards 更高。physician 必须 honestly 将 patient's interests 纳入考虑，并采取行动 further 这些 interests，而不是采取行动 pursue 自己的 private gain。作为 physicians 具有 special moral status 的 example，他提到了以下 special characteristics：

-   physicians 之间缺乏 advertising 和 overt price competition
-   physicians 为 further treatment 提供的 Advice 应当与 self-interest 相分离
-   有人主张，treatment 必须由“objective needs”所 dictated，而不能受 financial considerations 所 limited
-   人们依靠 physician 作为 expert 来 truthfully certifying illnesses 的 existence，这应当压过他取悦 customers 的愿望。
-   Nonprofit hospitals 相较于 for-profit hospitals 占据主导地位（3 per cent 在 for profit，30 per cent 在 voluntary non-profits。其余在 state owned hospitals）。Arrow posits 两种可能的 explanations

这些条目中，至少有两项相当不正确，其余几项也有争议。从最后一项说起，他对于 1963 年 US 的说法或许是正确的。但他没有进一步考察其他地方（或许当时无法获得这些 information）。如果他这样做了，就会发现以下情况（来自 [OECD data, 2015)](http://stats.oecd.org/Index.aspx?DataSetCode=HEALTH_REAC#)

![hospown.jpg](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2017/07/12-1.jpg)

在我们拥有 data 的 countries 中，除 Korea、Luxemburg 和 US 外，大多数 private hospitals 都是 for-profit。即使[在 US](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/hus/2015/089.pdf)，尽管如 Arrow 所说，早在 1963 年 non-profit hospitals 的数量是另一类的 10x，但到 1975 年就只有 4.3x 了。到 2013 年，更是只有 2.7x。

接下来是 physicians 之间缺乏 advertisement 和 overt price competition。这一点也需要拆开来看。我们同样看不到特定个人出于 commercial purposes 而发布的 advertisements。较小的 entities 通常依靠 word of mouth 和 small scale networks 来获得 reputation 和 customers。bakeries 和 small medical practices 都是如此。对于较大的 entities，advertising 确实更有意义。我们能找到 health insurance 的 advertising（[这里](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9YiTIYO-2A) 或[这里）。](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KcG0tyRetcc)我们甚至能找到 hospitals 的 advertising（[这里](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giQ_UDakSBA)、[这里](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wQ2TN_gI3sE)、[这里，](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fC6Dj-2KecY)[这里](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_hvWt1NMEZM)）我承认，hospitals 的 advertisement 确实不多。这可能是因为，人们并不太在意某个特定的 brand（很多情况下，对 supermarkets 也是如此）。你在一家能获得的 services，在其他任何一家也都能获得，而且很多人会去最近的一家。少数例外是自己所在 city 或 country 的 elite hospitals，它们可能拥有 state of the art facilities 和 highly skilled specialists。这些 hospitals 也会受到 local network advertisement effects 的影响。在某个 city，可能有两三家这样的 hospitals；考虑到 hospitals 往往长期留在同一个地方（几十年），它们会逐渐变得广为人知。这在某种程度上与 hotels 类似。出于同样的原因，Hotels 也不怎么 advertise。

接下来，price competition 在 US 确实是一个问题。在 US，很难在网上找到 treatments 的 prices。在 [UK](https://www.bmihealthcare.co.uk/hospitals/bmi-the-blackheath-hospital/treatments-prices)，[似乎](http://www.privatehealth.co.uk/conditions-and-treatments/hip-replacement-total/costs/)并没有那么难。在 Spain 则更难。我的总体 assessment 是，确实没有多少 price competition——正如 Arrow 所说。为什么 UK 的情况如此，而 US 却不是这样？这值得进一步 investigation，但也再次说明，缺乏 price competition 并不是这个 market 的 intrinsic characteristic。

接下来是据说 physicians 具有的那一组额外 ethical obligations。但在这一点上，乍看之下，他们与以下这些人并没有什么不同：为某个用途向你推荐最佳 cuts of meats 的 butcher，你雇来帮助 improve business 的 data science consultant（比如我！），car mechanic，甚至 CEO。在这些情况下，对于自己真正需要什么，你知道的比 professional 少，因此，要实现充分 fruitful 且 honest 的 transaction，就需要某种 intrinsic moral motivation——至少在 short term 是如此。在 long term，reputational effects 可能会发挥作用——。因此，这并不是 healthcare 的 moralisation 所独有的 special feature，而是因为 doctors 作为 professionals，知道的比你多。幸运的是，不仅在这些情况下，而且在大多数情况下，人们都希望把自己的工作做好。拥有“a good doctor”的 self-image，对一个人来说可能非常重要。（例如，参见[这里](https://www.mercatus.org/system/files/Manish-Success-Seeking--Entrepreneurship.pdf)的 section 2）。

#### Product uncertainty（产品不确定性）

（Asymmetric information）对于许多 commodities，你可以从自己或他人的经验中了解所购买的 good 的 nature。对于 illness，这就更难了。你大致知道什么样的芒果是好芒果，以及它应该卖多贵。否则，你也很容易找到一个吃芒果的朋友。但对于 cancer 或 hip surgery，要做到这一点就难得多。你事先并不知道自己将得到什么。physician 清楚这一点，而且比 patient 知道得更多。Arrow 说，这并不常见。

虽然总体而言确实如此，但上一节提到的 services 并非如此。比如，除了大致情况之外，你并不知道 repair a car 需要些什么……除非你想知道。hip replacement surgery 涉及什么？我不知道，但我可以在 google 上搜索“[hip surgery](http://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Hip-replacement/Pages/Introduction.aspx)”，了解它需要些什么。你可以看到，它涉及带有 anesthesia 的 surgery，cost 将在 8 到 16 k£ 之间，还涉及 removing 那个 damaged hip joint，并 replaced 为 metallic 或 ceramic artificial joint。你还可以看到 side effects 和 expected recovery time。这大大 closes 了 knowledge 方面的 gap。也许这些 information 在 1963 年还不容易获得，但现在已经很容易了。

#### Supply conditions（供给条件）

（Licensing）一种 commodity 的 supply 与其 production 所带来的 expected return 是 tied 的。对于 healthcare，某些因素在 returns 与 supply 之间 insert a wedge。其中之一是 licensing，它会 restricts supply。这项 policy 通常以希望 provide 一个 minimal level 的 quality 为理由。我个人怀疑，要得到 decent healthcare（或许多其他 regulated professions 中的 services），是否需要目前的 standards，但那是另一个问题。在美国，physicians 的 education 也受到 tightly regulated，导致 expensive education，并进一步增加了 physicians 的 supply 所受的 limitations。

#### Pricing practices（定价惯例）

（Price discrimination）这个 sector 会 practices 它。Hospitals 对 rich 的 charge 更多，对 poor 的更少（对真正的 poor 甚至不收费）。hospitals 一方也反对 pre-payment，以及 closed-panel practice（把 patient binding 到某个特定的 physicians group）。Arrow 并未为此提供 evidence，不过第一部分似乎足够 plausible，可以不再进一步调查就接受。第二部分很难理解，因为一种常见的 insurance offering 恰恰就是把 user tie 到与 insurance groups tied 的 hospitals 或 medical experts。

### 在 certainty 下与 competitive model 的 Comparison

#### Nonmarketable commodities（不可市场交易的商品）

（Negative externalities）以 vaccination 来 fixing 一种 negative externality 的例子。

（Other-regarding preferences）人们关心他人的 health。人们通常并不关心其他人在做什么，所以这是另一个区别。

嗯，是的。

（Increasing returns）Hospitals 表现出这种特征。Equipment 和 specialists 是 indivisible，所以把它们 aggregate 到一些 units（hospitals）中是合理的，这些 units 比 components 处于 separate 状态时 work better。这可能 decrease competition。

不过，正如他所承认的，transportation 和在 cities 中的 concentration 会 ameliorates 这个问题。此外，世界许多地方普遍存在 small clinical practices，这表明 returns 总体上并没有那么 increasing。不过，对于某些 surgeries 或某些 high-end equipment，可能确实如此。

（Licensing 和 educational costs）上文已讨论

Arrow 说，如果 requirements 被 relaxed，output 的 quality 就会更低。如果 education 在一定程度上起的是 signaling 作用，而不是 human capital buildup 的作用，或者存在比当前做法更快的 training doctors 的方式，又或者某些 procedures 可以交给 less trained specialists（他们会 perform 得 equally well），那就未必如此。

（Price discrimination）Arrow 说，Price discrimination 与 competitive model 是 inconsistent with 的。这里，我想知道现实中 price discrimination 究竟存在到什么程度。我猜，在 hospitals 不 publish 其 prices 的地方，它会更 prevalent，因为如果 publish 了，之后就很难对某些人 charge more。如果 hospitals 试图以 rich 为代价来 subsidise poor，这种 practice 也有一定 rationale。因此，就美国而言，我愿意承认 Arrow 是对的。

（Moral hazard）处于 insured 状态会 reduces 一个人 avoid illness 的 incentives；另一方面，insurance companies 会试图尽可能少地 pay claims。

（Predictability 和 insurance）一种 illness 越是 unpredictable，risk averse individuals 就越愿意付钱来 insure against 它。

（Pooling of unequal risks）Arrow 说，理论上，insurance companies 会向 less healthy 的人 charge 更多，但似乎存在 equalise costs 的倾向，而如果 markets 是 competitive 的，这种倾向就不会存在。

（Gaps in coverage）并非每个人都处于 insured 状态。

#### 结论

那么，是什么使 healthcare 具有 special 之处？总结上文，把 healthcare dividing 为其 components，再加上一些具有类似“奇怪”features 的例子：

| Externality | Uncertainty | Life Critical | Price | Information assymmetry | Moralisation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Communicable diseases (vaccines) | Yes | High | Some | Low | Medium |
| Major medical treatments (cancer, heart surgery) | No | Very high | Yes | Very high | High |
| Minor medical expenses (painkillers, cold medicine, medical checkups) | No | High | Not usually | Low | Low |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Car breakdown / Legal services | No | Very high | No | Medium | High |
| Food | No | None | Yes | Low | Low |
| Consulting services | No | Medium | No | High | High |

围绕 healthcare 的大部分 debate 往往集中在其中的一部分：major medical treatments（其次是 drug prices）。人们很少知道自己何时会需要它们，它们非常 expensive，而且你并不确切知道 treatment 会涉及什么（最初是如此）。由于大多数人 cannot afford 它们，通常就需要 insurance。Insurance 未必 expensive（但也可能如此）。在美国，[individual insurance](http://www.kff.org/other/state-indicator/individual-premiums/?currentTimeframe=0&sortModel=%7B%22colId%22:%22Average%20Monthly%20Premiums%22,%22sort%22:%22desc%22%7D) market 中的 monthly premia 可以在 150 到 470$ 之间，而在西班牙，你花大约 115€ 就能得到 [market](https://www.sanitas.es/) 上最好的 insurance，花 50€ 就能得到一份 cheap 的。

在许多人心中，这些 treatments 也是 moralised 的：它们\_必须 \_对所有人 available，而不考虑 cost（！）。有些人认为，这个 market 的某些 features 证明 healthcare 的 market 不起作用。一个例子是 pre-existing condition coverage。一个患有 cancer 的人 purchases 一份新的 policy（即使他能买到！），其因 cancer 产生的 expenses 也不会被 covered。但这是一个 feature，而不是 bug：premium 实际上就是你的 expected cost 加上一项 markup。如果 cost 是 known with certainty 的，那么 premium 就 equals 那个 total amount。试图通过 mandating 每个人 pays the same，或要求 insurer cover everyone 等方式来“修复”market 的这一 feature，会 undermines market 的 workings。如果真的希望 cover 那些人，向他们 redistribute 会好得多。

这篇 paper 的第二个方面，是解释这些 peculiarities 如何 incentivise institutions 的 creation，以克服它们。当存在 budget contrained、risk averse 的人，以及 highly uncertain 的 pricey treatments 时，就会出现 Insurance。

这个 market 的其他 features 并不像 Arrow 在 paper 中解释的那样 clear cut（medical ethics 的 uniqueness、hospitals 作为 non-profits 的 status，或 medical licensing）。

我还想补充，为了克服这些 peculiarities，我们真正会期待看到哪些 special features？hospitals 的 Transparent pricing、让 healthcare-shopping 更容易的网站，以及 lifelong all-risk health insurance。正如我用几个例子所展示的，这些 features 并非到处都一样。美国在 transparency 方面 ranks very low，而英国 ranks high。在西班牙，很容易获得 affordable cover for life（甚至 covering diabetes 等 non-preexisting chronic conditions），但在英国，除了 cancer 之外，并非如此。为什么这些 markets 会不同？我不知道，但这是一个值得研究的问题。

这些 points 和许多其他 points 已经在[这篇文章](https://www.theatlantic.com/business/archive/2012/03/liberals-are-wrong-free-market-health-care-is-possible/254648/)中得到讨论，只是我最初没有注意到。[这](https://www.cato-unbound.org/issues/april-2015/end-asymmetric-information)另一篇文章讨论了现代 asymmetric information 的 relevance。[这里是](http://johnhcochrane.blogspot.co.uk/2017/06/noahlogic.html) John Cochrane 对 Noah Smith 的回应。

我目前正在读 Jonathan Gruber 的 Public Finance，其中以现代方式论述了这个主题。我还没有读到 health insurance 那一节，所以目前我能说的是，读完 Arrow 的 paper 并没有让我相信 healthcare 不能主要交给 market。我不指望这篇文章会让你在这个问题上转而支持我的立场。要做到这一点，我需要一篇专门针对这个问题的文章，而不只是对一篇年代久远但有趣的 paper 做 critical assessment。我大概还应该看看 Handbook of Health Economics 的第 20-25 章。

要对这个问题做更完整的 assessment，就需要介绍关于这个问题最新的 theoretical treatment、世界各地现代 healthcare markets 如何运作的 empirical evidence（甚至超出美国或欧洲的范围），以及 healthcare markets 如何变成如今模样的 historical evidence。

最后，我想提到，有些人严重误解了 Arrow 在 paper 中所说的话。他在其中并没有主张或为 single payer system 辩护。他甚至没有像某些人所说的那样，声称“[medicine 的 laissez-faire solution 是 intolerable 的](https://www.google.co.uk/search?q=arrow+healthcare+intolerable+laissez-faire&oq=arrow+healthcare+intolerable+laissez-faire&aqs=chrome..69i57.5991j0j9&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8#q=arrow+healthcare+intolerable+laissez-faire&start=10)”。最后这句话他确实说过，但指的是 medical licensing，而且那不是他本人的看法，而是他所说的一般 social consensus。

如果你有关于这个 market、认为我应该阅读的资料，请务必在评论中告诉我。这个 debate 的局面似乎是，economists 之间存在一种 consensus，认为 healthcare 中的 free market 太过麻烦；但又有人能轻易指出那些 arguments 中的漏洞，随后却无人回应。我坚持自己的原则：在存在 scientific consensus 的情况下，除非有理由怀疑它，否则就应当尊重它，而这个案例似乎正是那些例外之一。

#### 对我这篇文章的 Critique

最近有人把你那些关于 healthcare 的博客文章链接发给了我，我发现 criticize 它还能获得报酬，所以我就来试一试：

首先是你那篇[关于 Ken Arrow 的 paper 的文章](https://nintil.com/kenneth-arrow-on-the-welfare-economics-of-medical-care-a-critical-assessment)。我认为，那个 section 中几乎所有与 Arrow 的 disagreement 都是 incorrect 的。

在那篇文章的 **Section 1.2** 中，你提出了这样的 claim：因为 hospital advertisements 存在，Arrow 就一定是 incorrect 的。我不赞同这一点，因为我不认为 healthcare services 的 advertisements 有你所假定的那么大的 effect。我在考察 customers 如何 choose 他们前往的 hospitals 时，发现 distance 和 physician referrals 是两个最大的 factors。一项[考察 hospital selection 的 German study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1300/J375v17n02_03) 发现，对 patient 的 choice 具有最大 influence 的是 physician referrals，而 pricing/advertisement 甚至不在 factors 的列表中。这与[来自 US 的 evidence 是 consistent 的](https://www.jstor.org/stable/29771616?seq=1)。

接着，你提出了这样的 claim：在 US 很难 shop around，是因为 prices 很难查到，因此，这不是 market 的 intrinsic characteristic。我认为这同样是 incorrect 的，因为我找到了大量 evidence，表明 price transparency 并不起作用，而且在 determining patient 去哪里这件事上，physician referrals 起着最大的作用（见上面的 hospital studies）。直觉上，确保所有 prices 都能查到，似乎就会让 shop 更容易，因此也会有更多人这样做。毕竟，如果人们连 prices 都看不到，又怎么能 shop around 呢！遗憾的是，[transparent pricing 似乎也没有帮助](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/10.1377/hlthaff.2016.1636)，主要是因为大多数人根本不使用它们。这也不是因为缺少鼓励或热情。这项 [study](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/full/10.1377/hlthaff.2016.1471?journalCode=hlthaff) surveyed 了 2,996 名 non-elderly Americans，发现尽管绝大多数人 strongly agreeing，认为 shopping around 是个好主意，但其中只有 13% 真正 sought out price info，而只有 3% 在 receiving care 之前真正 compared prices。若想进一步阅读，我建议你看看这些：$$[1$$](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/pdf/10.1377/hlthaff.2015.0746) 和 $$[2$$](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/article-abstract/2518264)。

这些 results 也并非 US 所独有。一项[关于 Singapore 的 transparent pricing 的 effects 的 study](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Rasyad_Parinduri/publication/236985073_The_Effect_of_Publishing_Hospital_Charges_on_Healthcare_Costs_Evidence_from_Singapore/links/0a85e539666901a3d1000000.pdf) 发现，在 price transparency legislation 的 implementation 之后的那些年里，没有 evidence 表明 prices 有任何 marked decrease。更有意思的是，这篇 [research paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0275074007301041) 发现，当 government 放松 regulations 时，Singapore 的 healthcare costs 实际上反而 _increased_ 了，因为 hospitals 购买了 expensive new technology，并 focused on premium care，却忽视了面向 poorer citizens 的 lower levels。这导致 government 再次收紧管控。

最后，一项考察人们为 MRIs 而 shop around 的 rates 的 [study](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24869) 发现，人们通常会在他们的 doctors 建议的地方做 M.R.I.s。事实上，patients 开车前往做 M.R.I. 的地方时，平均会路过另外六个能够更 cheaply 完成该 procedure 的地方。请阅读[这篇文章](https://www.nytimes.com/2018/07/30/upshot/shopping-for-health-care-simply-doesnt-work-so-what-might.html)，了解对这项 study 更深入的 explanation。这意味着，缺少 price shopping 并非 major services 所独有。

我认为，人们不 shop around 这一事实是一种 intrinsic market failure，这与你在文章中的 claim 不同。

**Section 1.3** 的问题源于同一个先入为主的 notion：人们不 shop，是因为 prices 和 info 很难找到。实际情况并非如此。正如我之前说过的，人们很少 seek out price info，information 也是如此。虽然 information 可能很容易找到，但人们很少使用它。

在 **Section 8.2** 中，你 refute 了 Scott 引自 BCBS 的 statistics。我想，这一点我没法责怪你，但我不赞同 government healthcare 并非更 efficient 这一 notion。我认为 Scott 用了一个不好的 example。我想提供一个来自 CMS 的 counterexample。看看[这里](http://content.healthaffairs.org/content/early/2016/11/22/hlthaff.2016.1330.full.pdf+html) CMS 关于 National Health Expenditures 的 data（不知道为什么，这个链接会给你一个 security warning，但它是安全的，因为这是一个关于 government data 的 pdf download）。federal government 在 health care 上的 spending 总额 greater than private insurance spending（1.2 Trillion vs. 1.1 Trillion），然而 government 的 administrative costs 是 \$42 billion, vs private net cost of insurance of \$210 billion。这是一个非常有力的 argument，表明 government 可以更 efficient。这意味着直接 saving \$160 billion，而且许多人认为 government 仍然是 inefficient 的，costs 还可以更低。

此外，一项 [mercatus center study](https://www.mercatus.org/system/files/blahous-costs-medicare-mercatus-working-paper-v1_1.pdf)（顺便说一句，这是一个带有 Libertarian 倾向的 think tank）发现，改为 single payer 会让我们节省 admin costs。data 在第 7 页。其中显示，在 single payer 下，Overall NHE 会 decrease。

关于 **Section 8.5**，我认为有关 for-profits 和 nonprofits 的 evidence 比你所呈现的要 mixed 得多。因为 research 表明，[public hospitals 至少与 private 一样 efficient，甚至可能更 efficient](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6033142/)，或者得出的是[略微倾向于 public 更好的 inconclusive results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21796588/)。我们也知道，Private hospitals 对 financial incentives 的 respond 更强，而这可能会变成一件坏事。

然后，你以 Singapore 为 example，说明 market 如何能够 work，以及它的 system 能够通过 market mechanism 来 replicated。我不明白，在 replicating 像 Singapore 那样的 heavy price controls 等 policy 的同时，你如何还能维持 free market。关于 Brian Caplan 的 points，他认为 Singapore 的 system 之所以 works，是因为 extensive cost sharing。虽然 cost sharing 确实 reduces total healthcare expenditure，但一项[考察 HDHPs 的 evidence 的 study](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/132/3/1261/3769421?redirectedFrom=fulltext) 表明，cost-sharing 似乎并不会 decrease prices。这个 conclusion 得到了[进一步的 evidence](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4204109/) 的支持。Caplan 随后认为，Singapore 的 healthcare 中存在大量 comparison shopping，但这似乎也没有多少 evidence。正如我上面所说的，一项[关于 Singapore 的 transparent pricing 的 effects 的 study](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Rasyad_Parinduri/publication/236985073_The_Effect_of_Publishing_Hospital_Charges_on_Healthcare_Costs_Evidence_from_Singapore/links/0a85e539666901a3d1000000.pdf) 发现，在 price transparency legislation 的 implementation 之后的那些年里，没有 evidence 表明 prices 有任何 marked decrease。customers choose 前往哪些 hospitals，很可能取决于 distance 和 physician referrals，与大多数其他 developing nations 一样。我认为你应该看看[这篇文章](https://www.nytimes.com/2017/10/02/upshot/what-makes-singapores-health-care-so-cheap.html?module=inline)和[这一篇](https://theincidentaleconomist.com/wordpress/what-can-the-u-s-health-system-learn-from-singapore/)。singaporean system 很可能不是 reproducible 的。

关于 India，你提出，private hospitals 正在以十分之一的 cost delivering world-class care，但我对这在 developed nations 中的 reproducible 程度存有疑问。India 的 Wages、costs of living、drugs 等都要低得多，所以我不明白，这怎么能成为与 US 进行的一个好的 comparison。

我会回应所有评论，不过请注意，Non-Non Libertarian FAQ 写于 2017 年 1 月 1 日之前，我的 [blog](https://nintil.com/prove-wrong-get-money) 中的政策将这一天设为付款的截止日期（此后我一直没有改过）。

**Price competition，等等**。看来，我确实犯了一个错误，infer 出了“prices available imply price competition”。无论 empirical evidence 如何，我都觉得自己的这一 claim 不够扎实，所以这是一个错误。不过，关于 price availability，我不认为你的 evidence 有那么好。这里的问题是，choice 取决于作出该 choice 的 costs 和 benefits。例如，如果 price information 没有用，或者很难找到，那么这种“price transparency”就起不了多大作用。同样，如果 patients 认为大多数 hospitals 都一样，他们就不会在它们之间 discriminate。

具体的 evidence

\* Desai et al. 关于“cost reduction tool”的 paper，其本身就在 intro 中指出，其他 work 之所以得出 conflicting findings，是因为所用的 tools 和所研究的 patient populations 不同。这项 study 中的许多 providers 都缺少 quality info（假设你附近有一家地方，评价很好，但贵了 10%，还有另一家更便宜，但你对它一无所知。单凭这一点就可能解释某个 decision）。最后，人们确实会 search，而且 tool 可用的时间越长，他们 search 得就越多。也许，人们需要一些时间，才会在下次考虑这些 shoppable procedures 时想到这个 tool。authors 在 policy implications 中提到了其中一些情况，并指出，它确实 decrease 了 imaging 的 average price paid。

\* Mehrotra et al. 如果说说明了什么，那似乎反而是在 corroborate 我的观点。在我看来，如果让人们更容易 price shop，他们就会 price shop，而这应该会对 prices 施加一些 pressure。单凭这一点，并不能解释 healthcare 为什么 expensive 的所有原因。不过，在这一点上，如果人们得到 sufficient information，能够据此行动（并从中 benefit substantially），却没有这样做，那我就是错的。paper 指出了一个看起来很重要的 caveat：如果 patients 无论去哪里都付同样的钱（他们有一个足够好的地方，而且它是 in network 的），他们可能就不会 price shop。我还要补充，如果某人加入了 HMO（碰巧我就是，加入的是 Kaiser），也不会 priceshop。他在先前 picked 一个 healthcare plan 时，已经这样做过了。paper 指出：“75 percent 的 respondents 表示，他们不知道有任何 resource 能让他们 compare costs among providers（data not shown）”，以及“不出所料，respondents 最有可能为 outpatient procedures 这类 higher-cost services 寻找 cost information。”如果你知道 pricing info 很少，那么不花多少时间寻找它也是合理的。而且，即使找到了，也可能非常 obscure，难以 interpret（你看过这个吗？有人能根据自己的情况看懂[那个](https://www.ucsfhealth.org/about/pricing-transparency)吗？）最后，paper 指出了一点，我认为这是最重要的：“Price information 必须对 patients 更 accessible，也更 comprehensible。”

\* Sinaika et al. 与 Castlight study 类似，但其范围似乎比人们在 Castlight 中 searching 的内容更 comprehensive（不只是 labwork 和 imaging，还有 colonoscopies、surgery 等）。它也显示了同样的 pattern：随着时间推移，tools 的 usage 大幅 increases。另外：“我们的 finding 表明，out-of-pocket expenses 更高的人更有可能 search 这个 Member Payment Estimator，这很可能 reflects 了这样一个事实：拥有 generous insurance 的 consumers 是 insulated from price variation 的，而且对 price 的 sensitive 程度较低”，以及“Whaley 和 coauthors 发现，与 nonsearchers 为这些 services 支付的 payments 相比，searching for price information 与 advanced imaging 和 laboratory tests 的 lower payments 存在 associated 关系。”（所以，有时确实能看到 cost 方面的 effects。）

关于 doctors 以及人们照他们说的做这一点，是的，确实如此，而且这也合理。但为什么 _doctors_ 会让 patients 去更 expensive 的地方？究竟有哪些 incentives 让 costs 保持在高位？这些 doctors 知道确实有更便宜的地方吗？其中一项 studies 写道：“今后，我们的 work suggests，health care funders 可以为 physicians 提供 potential locations for care 的 prices 信息，并 incentivizing 他们作出更 cost-efficient referrals，从而更 effectively steer patients towards high productivity providers。这些 results 与 Ho and Pakes (2014) 是 consistent 的；该研究发现，对 physician referrals 拥有更 direct control 的 insurers 能够获得 lower priced secondary care；这些 results 也与 payment reform 的 research 是 consistent 的，后者 suggests，当 physicians 得到 incented 去 make referrals to lower priced locations 时，他们确实会这样做（Song et al. 2014; Carroll et al. 2018）。”

此外，今年 US 正在实行“price transparency”。这会起很大作用吗？我预计不会，原因见[这里](https://khn.org/news/as-hospitals-post-sticker-prices-online-most-patients-will-remain-befuddled/)。总之，从总体上说，“真正的 price transparency 从未被尝试过”，因此，这里的 evidence 不足以让我认为，它的缺失是这个 market 的 intrinsic part，或认为它的存在不会 induce price competition。什么能 falsify 这一点？比如，Amazon 开设了一家新的 hospital chain，并在其网站上列出你为任何项目实际要付的 exact prices，让你能确切看到自己的 bank account 会少多少钱。假设这些 prices 比周围其他 hospitals 更便宜。如果人们没有成群涌向那家 hospital，那我就是错的。总之，我觉得 Frakt 和 Mehrotra 的[这篇文章](https://www.mfprac.com/web2020/07literature/literature/Misc/HealthTransparency_Frankt.pdf)与我的想法很接近：是的，price transparency 迄今令人失望，但这不意味着它不应成为 broader strategy 的一部分。拿 healthcare 作一个类比，curing cancer 并不会让 life expectancy improve 多少（因为你仍会得 CVD）。CVD 也是如此（你仍会得 cancer）。把两者都 Cure，就会得到 substantial benefits。对于 healthcare system，piecemeal changes 在单独尝试时可能显得 ineffective。

因此，我仍然坚持，从 theory 上说，缺少 price competition 并不是 healthcare market 的 intrinsic characteristic，即使我们眼下确实看不到多少 competition。

## 原文

Kenneth Arrow wrote a paper in 1963, Uncertainty and the Welfare Economics of Medical Care. This paper tends to appear in debates regarding whether healthcare can be left to the market ([like bread](https://krugman.blogs.nytimes.com/2009/07/25/why-markets-cant-cure-healthcare/)), or if it should feature [heavy state involvement](https://www.bloomberg.com/view/articles/2017-06-01/americans-sure-seem-to-like-universal-health-care). Here I explain what the paper says, and to what extent it is true.

So lets see what [the paper](https://web.stanford.edu/%7Ejay/health_class/Readings/Lecture01/arrow.pdf) says.

Well, first of all, Arrow doesn't intent for the paper to be anything more than "an exploratory and tentative study of the specific differentia of medical care". If he says that, one wouldn't be surprised to find to find mistakes in the paper, as it is also quite a decades old. But on the other hand, Arrow won a Nobel Price, perhaps we was truly prescient and the whole thing has stood the rest of time.

He than claims that the "special features" of the industry essentially stem from the fact that healthare demand is strongly uncertain from an individual perspective, and that when markets fail, societies will, to some extent, come up with non-market social institutions to fix those market failures. But, he says, these institutions induce non-competitive practices in the healthcare sector, which prevents it from moving closer to optimality.

### Special characteristics of the Medical-care market

#### Demand

(Unpredictability) First, unlike food or clothing, demand for healthcare is not as predictable. They are only purchased when one is ill, and illness is be hard to predict. That is, demand is driven by exogenous shocks, and not by one's own planning. Arrow finds it hard to think of goods and services for which the same thing holds. He can think of legal services (and he points out that there are some similarities with the healthcare sector).

(Criticality) Second, illness can cause death, permanent injuries, and loss of the ability to earn. Given that our life goals need us to be alive, and relatively healthy, it is of supreme importance to us to be healthy. The same holds for food, but food deprivation can be avoided if one has sufficient income.

Here Arrow is largely correct. The list of examples for the first special characteristic would be: healthcare, legal services, gadget repairing, home maintenance, or car maintenance. For the second characteristic, we can cite healthcare, food, water, and housing. Thus only healthcare meets both condition: It is the only possible critical good that has an unpredictable demand.

Or, to be precise, part of healthcare is. Like with some goods usually bunched in a single category, healthcare can be unbundled: cancer and heart diseases can be quite unpredictable and fatal, flu, headaches and cold  can be mild and fairly predictable (usually around winter). There are few predictable and critical malaises, among them aging is probably the chief one. For highly unpredictable and low criticality, mosquito bites is one example.

#### Expected behaviour of the physician

(Moral provision) Healthcare is a service, not a physical good. Producing healthcare is synonymous with providing it, says Arrow. The customer cannot test the product before consuming it, and there is an element of trust in the relationship. This also holds for a barber. But the ethical standards required of the physician are higher. The physician must honestly take the patient's interests into consideration and act to further them instead of acting to pursue his own private gain. As an example of the special moral status of physicians, he mentions these special characteristics:

-   Lack of advertising and overt price competition between physicians
-   Advice given by physicians for further treatment is supposed to be divorced from self-interest
-   It is claimed that treatment must be dictated by "objective needs" and not limited by financial considerations
-   The physician is relied on as an expert in certifying the existence of illnesses truthfully, outweighing his desire to please his customers.
-   Nonprofit hospitals predominate over for-profit hospitals (3 per cent in for profit, 30 per cent in voluntary non-profits. The remainder is in state owned hospitals). Arrow posits two possible explanations

Two of this items are at least quite false, the others are argueable. Starting from the bottom, he might have been true about the US in 1963. But he didn't look further beyond (Perhaps the information wasn't available then). Had he done so, he would have found this (from [OECD data, 2015)](http://stats.oecd.org/Index.aspx?DataSetCode=HEALTH_REAC#)

![hospown.jpg](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2017/07/12-1.jpg)

For the countries that we have data for, most private hospitals are for-profit, except Korea, Luxemburg, and the US. And even [in the US](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/hus/2015/089.pdf), while there were 10x more non-profit hospitals back in 1963 as Arrow says, but 1975 there were only 4.3x more. By 2013, only 2.7x more.

Next, lack of advertisement and overt price competition between physicians. This also has to be unpacked. We don't see either advertisements from particular individuals for commercial purposes. Smaller entities usually rely on word of mouth and small scale networks to gain reputation and customers. This holds for bakeries and small medical practices. For larger entities, advertising does make more sense. We can find advertising for health insurance ([here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v9YiTIYO-2A)  or [here).](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KcG0tyRetcc) We can even find advertising for hospitals ([here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giQ_UDakSBA), [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wQ2TN_gI3sE), [here,](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fC6Dj-2KecY) [here](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_hvWt1NMEZM)) I will grant that there is't much advertisement for hospitals, it is true.  This may be because people don't care much for a particular brand (like, in many cases, with supermarkets). The services you can get at one, you can get at all of them, and many people will go to the nearest one. The rare exceptions would be elite hospitals in one's own city or country where they may have state of the art facilities and highly skilled specialists. These hospitals would also fall under the local network advertisement effects. In a given city, there may be two or three of these, and given that hospitals tend to stay in the same place for a long time (decades), they will get to become well known. This is to some extent like hotels. Hotels don't advertise that much for the same reasons.

Next, price competition is indeed an issue in the US. It is difficult to find prices for treatments online in the US. In the [UK](https://www.bmihealthcare.co.uk/hospitals/bmi-the-blackheath-hospital/treatments-prices) it doesn't[seem](http://www.privatehealth.co.uk/conditions-and-treatments/hip-replacement-total/costs/) that difficult. It is more difficult for Spain. My overall assessment is that indeed there is little price competition -as Arrow says. Why the situation in the UK is like that and not in the US? This deserves further investigation, but again shows that lack of price competition is not an intrinsic characteristic of this market.

Next we have the extra bundle of ethical obligations that physicians are said to have. But in this regard, they are not prima facie dissimilar from a butcher counseling you on what the best cuts of meats are for a given purpose, or a data science consultant (like me!) you hire to help you improve your business, or a car mechanic, or even a CEO. In those cases, you know less than the professional about what you really need, so some intrinsic moral motivation is needed for a fully fruitful and honest transaction -at least in the short term. In the long term, reputational effects may kick in-. So this is not a special feature of the moralisation of healthcare, but of the fact that doctors as professionals know more than you do. Fortunately, not only in these cases but in most, people want to be good at doing their jobs. Having a self-image of "a good doctor" can be very important for one. (See section 2 [here](https://www.mercatus.org/system/files/Manish-Success-Seeking--Entrepreneurship.pdf) for example).

#### Product uncertainty

(Asymmetric information) With many commodities, you can learn from your or other's experience about the nature of the good purchased. With illness, it is more difficult. You sort of know what a good mango is, and how pricey it should be. If not, you can easily find a mango-eating friend. But it is far harder to do for cancer, or hip surgery.  You don't know beforehand what you will be getting. The physician is aware of this, and knows more than the patient. Arrow says this is uncommon.

While in general it is true, it is not true for the services mentioned in the previous section. You don't know what is needed to repair a car beyond broad details, for example.... unless you want to. What does a hip replacement surgery involve? No idea, but then I can google "[hip surgery](http://www.nhs.uk/conditions/Hip-replacement/Pages/Introduction.aspx)" on google and get an idea of what it takes. You can see that it will involve surgery with anesthesia, that it will cost between 8 and 16 k£, that it involves removing the damaged hip joint and replaced with a metallic or ceramic artificial joint. You can also see the side effects, and expected recovery time. This greatly closes the gap in knowledge. Perhaps this information wasn't easily available in  1963, but now it is.

#### Supply conditions

(Licensing) The supply of is commodity is tied to the expected return from its production. For healthcare some elements insert a wedge between returns and supply. One is licensing, which restricts supply. This policy tends to be justified by the desire to provide a minimal level of quality. I personally doubt that to get decent healthcare (or services in many other regulated professions) the current standards are needed, but that's another matter. In the US, education of physicians is also tightly regulated, resulting in expensive education, and adding to the limitations on the supply of physicians.

#### Pricing practices

(Price discrimination) The sector practices it. Hospitals charge the rich more and the poor less (even nothing for the truly poor). There is also opposition to pre-payment from the side of hospitals, and to closed-panel practice (binding the patient to a particular group of physicians). Arrow provides no evidence for this, altough the first part seems plausible enough to accept it without further investigation. The second part is hard to understand, as one common insurance offering is precisely to tie the user to hospitals or medical experts tied to the insurance groups.

### Comparison with the competitive model under certainty

#### Nonmarketable commodities

(Negative externalities) The case of vaccination as fixing a negative externality.

(Other-regarding preferences) People care for other people's health. People usually don't care about what other people do, so this is another difference.

Well, yes.

(Increasing returns) Hospitals show them. Equipment and specialists are indivisible, so it makes sense to aggregate them in units (hospitals) that work better than if the components were separate. This can decrease competition.

But, as he admits, transportation and concentration in cities ameliorates this issue. In addition, the prevalence of small clinical practices in many parts of the world suggest that returns are not that increasing in general. They may be for certain surgeries, or certain high-end equipment.

(Licensing and educational costs) Discussed above

Arrow says that if the requirements were relaxed, the output would be of lower quality. This need not be so if education acts to some extent a signaling rather than as human capital buildup, or if there are faster ways of training doctors than currently practiced, or if some procedures can be left to less trained specialists (Who will perform them equally well).

(Price discrimination) Price discrimination, says Arrow, is inconsistent with the competitive model.  Here I wonder to what extent price discrimination exists in reality. I imagine that it will be more prevalent there were hospitals do not publish their prices, as if they did it would be difficult afterwards to charge more to some. There is also some rationale for that practice if hospitals seek to subsidise the poor at the expense of the rich. I am thus willing to concede that Arrow is right for the US.

(Moral hazard) Being insured reduces one's incentives to avoid illness, and on the other hand insurance companies will try to pay as little claims as possible.

(Predictability and insurance) The more unpredictable an illness is, the more risk averse individuals will be willing to pay to insure against it.

(Pooling of unequal risks) In theory, insurance companies would charge more people who are less healthy, but there appears to be a tendency to equalise costs, which would not exist if markets were competitive, says Arrow.

(Gaps in coverage) Not everyone is insured.

#### Conclusion

What then, makes healthcare special? Summarising the above, and dividing healthcare into its components, plus adding some of examples that share similar "weird" features:

| Externality | Uncertainty | Life Critical | Price | Information assymmetry | Moralisation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Communicable diseases (vaccines) | Yes | High | Some | Low | Medium |
| Major medical treatments (cancer, heart surgery) | No | Very high | Yes | Very high | High |
| Minor medical expenses (painkillers, cold medicine, medical checkups) | No | High | Not usually | Low | Low |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |
|  |  |  |  |  |  |
| Car breakdown / Legal services | No | Very high | No | Medium | High |
| Food | No | None | Yes | Low | Low |
| Consulting services | No | Medium | No | High | High |

Most of the debate around healthcare tends to focus on a piece of it: major medical treatments (and secondly on drug prices). One rarely knows when one will need them, they are very expensive, and you don't know exactly what the treatment will involve (initially). Because most people cannot afford them, insurance is typically needed. Insurance need not (but can) be expensive.  In the US, monthly premia can range from 150 to 470$ [in the individual insurance](http://www.kff.org/other/state-indicator/individual-premiums/?currentTimeframe=0&sortModel=%7B%22colId%22:%22Average%20Monthly%20Premiums%22,%22sort%22:%22desc%22%7D) market, while in Spain, you can get the best insurance available in the [market](https://www.sanitas.es/)for around 115€, and a cheap one for 50€.

Those treatments are also moralised in the minds of many people: they \_have to \_be available to everyone, without regards for the cost (!). Some people think some features of this market are proof that the market for healthcare doesn't work. One example is pre-existing condition coverage. Someone with cancer who purchases a new policy (even if he manages to!), won't be covered for expenses due to cancer. But this is a feature, not a bug: The premium is really the expected cost to you plus a markup. If the cost is known with certainty, then the premium equals the total amount. Trying to "fix" this feature of the market by doing things like mandating that everyone pays the same, or that insurer cover everyone undermines the workings of the market. If one really wishes to cover those people, it would be far better to redistribute towards them.

The second aspect of the paper is to explain how these peculiarities incentivise the creation of institutions to overcome them. Insurance is what happens when you have budget contrained, risk averse people, and highly uncertain pricey treatments.

Other features of this market are not as clear cut as Arrow explains in his paper (the uniqueness of medical ethics, or the status of hospitals as non-profits, or medical licensing).

What special features, I add, would we really expect to see to overcome the peculiarities? Transparent pricing at hospitals, websites to do healthcare-shopping easier, and lifelong all-risk health insurance. As I've shown in with a few examples, these features are not similar everywhere. The US ranks very low on transparency, while the UK ranks high. It is easy to get affordable cover for life in Spain (covering even non-preexisting chronic conditions like diabetes), but not so in the UK, except for cancer. Why are these markets different? I don't know, but it is a topic worth looking into.

These points and many others have been already addressed in [this article](https://www.theatlantic.com/business/archive/2012/03/liberals-are-wrong-free-market-health-care-is-possible/254648/), which I didn't initially notice. [This](https://www.cato-unbound.org/issues/april-2015/end-asymmetric-information) other article deals with the relevance of asymmetric information in modern times. [Here's](http://johnhcochrane.blogspot.co.uk/2017/06/noahlogic.html) John Cochrane reply to Noah Smith.

I am currently reading through Jonathan Gruber's Public Finance, where a modern treatment of this topic is presented. I haven't gotten to the health insurance section, so for now I can say that reading Arrow's paper fails to convince me that healthcare cannot be mostly left to the market. I don't expect that this post will win you over to my side of the question. For that I would need something focused specifically on that, not just a critical assessment of an old interesting paper. I should probably also have a look at chapters 20-25 of the Handbook of Health Economics.

A fuller assessment of the question would involve a presentation of the most up to date theoretical treatment of the issue, empirical evidence on how modern day healthcare markets work around the world (beyond the US or Europe even), and historical evidence about how healthcare markets became what they are now.

Finally, I want to mention that some people grossly misunderstand what Arrow says in his paper. There he is not arguing or defending a single payer system. He is not even saying that "[the laissez-faire solution for medicine is intolerable](https://www.google.co.uk/search?q=arrow+healthcare+intolerable+laissez-faire&oq=arrow+healthcare+intolerable+laissez-faire&aqs=chrome..69i57.5991j0j9&sourceid=chrome&ie=UTF-8#q=arrow+healthcare+intolerable+laissez-faire&start=10)" as some people have said. That last line he does say, but referring to medical licensing, and that is not something he believes, but something that he says is the general social consensus.

If you have sources that deal with this market that I should read, please do tell me in the comments. The landscape of the debate seems one where there is consensus among economists that a free market in healthcare is too troublesome, yet then there are people who easily poke holes in those arguments and then they go unanswered. I stick to my principle that one should defer to a scientific consensus where it exists unless there are reasons to doubt it, and this case seems like one of those exceptions.
