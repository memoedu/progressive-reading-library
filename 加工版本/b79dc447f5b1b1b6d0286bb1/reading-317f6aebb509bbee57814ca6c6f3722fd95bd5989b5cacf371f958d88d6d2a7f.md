# A short rant about contra dance etiquette

---

## 正文

偶尔，在谈论 contra dances 中邀请别人 dance 的 etiquette 时，会有人提出这样的看法：如果有人邀请你 dance，而你 turn them down，你就有义务 sit out 那场 dance（例如 [1](http://www.cincinnaticontradance.org/etiquette.htm)、[2](http://bookloversunite.wordpress.com/tag/contra-dancing/)）。

等等，不好意思，_什么_？怎么到现在还有人觉得这是个好主意？

先说说 contra 中的 partner-finding model 已经有多 broken。在许多 dances 中，women 邀请 men 来 dance 就是 Just Not Done。而即使在可以这么 Done 的地方，这也只是 rule 的例外，因此依然存在着几乎同样强烈的 hunter/hunted dynamic。我的许多女性朋友担心，如果自己邀请一位男性 dance，别人会“想得太多”。这对所有人都很糟糕，原因显而易见：women 没法 choose 自己和谁 dance，而 men 则一直在 competing。

现在来看看“如果你 refuse 一场 dance，就必须 sit out”这条 rule 的 effect。被邀请的一方几乎总是 women，因此这条“rule”的 burden 就落在她们身上。这相当糟糕，原因有两个：

1.  Women 对自己和谁 dance 本来就远没有那么多 say，而这基本上 obliterates 了任何剩余的 choice。你要么和第一个邀请你的人 dance，要么完全不 dance。

2.  缺乏 partner choice 对 women 的 hurts 比对 men 更大。Contra 是一个非常 welcoming 的 community，这本来很好，但也意味着大多数 dances 都至少会有几个“让人不舒服的老男人”（或者没那么老的男人）。即使没有这条 rule，我也知道有些女性因为 refusing 和他们 dance 而感到不自在，最终因此彻底失去了对 contra 的兴趣。这种 regressive 的“rule”让情况变得更糟。

你也许会辩称，这条 rule 的存在是为了鼓励人们做到 inclusive。某些 refusing dances 的方式可能会对 community 造成 damaging 的影响；例如，只和彼此 dance 的 experienced dancers 所组成的 cliques，可能会让一场 dance 对 newbies 显得 unwelcoming。

然而，即使没有人在 newbies 邀请时 explicitly refusing 和他们 dance，这种情况似乎照样会发生（你好啊，Concord！）。所以，“dance 或 sit out”这条 norm 确实给 contra community 造成了 unintentional damage（它 forcing women 和让自己不舒服的人 dance，或者在想 dance 时只能 sit out），_而且_它也没有 prevent 它“本想”阻止的 damage（exclusive cliques 仍然可以 form）：这显然是个 failure。要 discourage 诸如 refusing newbies 这样的 anti-social behavior，正确的做法是建立 reward 与 newbies 一起 dancing 的 social norms，而不是制定完全 miss their target 的 roundabout rules。

那么，这个理由也就站不住脚了。我猜，dance-or-sit-out norm 至今仍然存在的真正原因，是为了保护人们的 egos。但在实践中，作为一个几乎总会处在被 refused 那一方的人，我觉得这是个 fruitless goal。如果有人其实不愿意和我 dancing，我是能看出来的。如果他们只是因为愚蠢的 etiquette 而被迫这么做，他们不会开心，我也就不会开心！事实上，这比直接被 rejected 要无趣得多，而无论如何，每个人都应该学会如何面对 rejected！

所以，dancers，拜托了：如果你不想和某个人 dance，你可以 refuse！把我们的 social mores 从十九世纪的深处拖出来，带到 2013 年的阳光下吧！

## 原文

Occasionally, while talking about the etiquette of asking people to dance at contra dances, someone voices the opinion that if someone asks you for a dance and you turn them down, you’re obliged to sit out that dance (e.g. [1](http://www.cincinnaticontradance.org/etiquette.htm), [2](http://bookloversunite.wordpress.com/tag/contra-dancing/)).

And, I’m sorry, _what_? How do people still think this is a good idea?

Let’s start with how broken the partner-finding model already is in contra. At many dances it’s Just Not Done for women to ask men to dance. And even where it is Done, it’s an exception to the rule, so there’s almost as much of a hunter/hunted dynamic going on. Many of my women friends worry that people will “read too much into it” if they ask a man to dance. This sucks for everyone for obvious reasons: women don’t get to choose who they dance with and men are constantly competing.

Now consider the effect of the “if you refuse a dance, you must sit out” rule. The parties being asked are nearly always women, so the burden of the “rule” falls on them. This is pretty terrible for a couple reasons:

1.  Women already have much less say in who they dance with, and this essentially obliterates any remaining choice. You dance with the first person who asks you or not at all.

2.  Lack of partner choice hurts women more than men. Contra is a very welcoming community, which is great except that it means that most dances have at least a couple “creepy old guys” (or not-so-old guys). Even without this rule I’ve known women feel uncomfortable refusing to dance with them and this turning them off contra entirely. This kind of regressive “rule” makes the situation even worse.

You might argue that this rule exists to encourage people to be inclusive. Some forms of refusing dances can be damaging to the community; for instance, cliques of experienced dancers who only dance with each other can make a dance unwelcoming to newbies.

However, this seems to happen even without people explicitly refusing to dance with newbies when asked (hi Concord!). So the norm of “dance or sit out” does unintentional damage to the contra community (by forcing women to dance with people who make them uncomfortable, or sit out when they want to dance), _and_ doesn’t prevent the damage it’s “intended” to (exclusive cliques can still form): a clear failure. The right way to discourage anti-social behavior like refusing newbies, is to have social norms that reward dancing with newbies, not roundabout rules that miss their target entirely.

So much for that reason, then. I’m guessing the real reason the dance-or-sit-out norm still exists is to protect people’s egos. But in practice, as someone who would almost always be on the wrong side of being refused, that’s a fruitless goal. I can tell if someone would rather not be dancing with me. If they’re forced to because of silly etiquette, they won’t have fun, and so I won’t have fun either! In fact, it’ll be much less fun than just getting rejected, which is something everyone should learn how to deal with anyway!

So, dancers, please: if you don’t want to dance with someone, you can refuse! Drag our social mores out of the depths of the nineteenth century and into the daylight of 2013!
