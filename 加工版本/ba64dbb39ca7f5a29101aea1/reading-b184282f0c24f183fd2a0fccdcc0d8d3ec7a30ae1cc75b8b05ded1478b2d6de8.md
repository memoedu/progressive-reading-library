Scientists 正在 getting older。有些人对此表达了担忧。虽然这种担忧的动机并不总是明确说出，但通常归结为两点：第一，是 fairness 的问题。Science 的 getting older 可能意味着它没有给 younger scientists 腾出空间；打个比方，older scientists 会终身占据数量有限的椅子。第二，是 efficiency 的问题，这或许才是最主要的担忧。这种观点认为，science 尤其迫切需要 young scientists 加入，让他们不受 prior knowledge 和 dogmas 的影响，以高于其 older peers 所能企及的 rate，持续带来 breakthroughs。

历史上，许多 scientists 都赞同后一种观点，从 physicists 到 molecular biologists，其中有些人的确在很年轻时就进行了自己的 breakthrough research。Einstein 在他的 annus mirabilis 时是 26 岁，Newton 在自己的 annus mirabilis 时是 23 岁，而 Darwin 在 conceived 那个 theory of evolution by natural selection 时是 29 岁（当时他刚读过 Malthus）。

> 一个新的 scientific truth 并不是通过 convincing 它的 opponents、让他们恍然大悟而 triumph，而是因为它的 opponents 最终死去，而熟悉它的新一代逐渐成长起来（Planck）

> 我坚信，encourage innovation 的唯一办法，就是把它交给年轻人。年轻人的一大 advantage 在于他们是 ignorant。因为我认为，science 中的 ignorance 非常重要。如果你像我一样知道得太多，就无法尝试新事物。我总是在自己完全 ignorant 的 fields 中工作……（[Brenner](https://www.openingscience.org/tag/sydney-brenner/)）

> 一个人如果在 30 岁之前没有对 science 作出自己的 great contribution，此后就永远不会作出了。（[Einstein](https://www.nature.com/articles/150698a0)）

> Age 当然是一阵让每个 physicist 都必定害怕的寒热。一旦过了三十岁，他便生不如死（[Dirac](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Scientific_Elite/HAHCzJfmD5IC?hl=en&gbpv=1&bsq=Jungk)）

> 少数 naturalists 具有很强的 flexibility of mind，而且已经开始 doubt 那个 immutability of species，他们或许会受到本书的影响；但我满怀信心地望向未来，望向年轻而崭露头角的 naturalists，他们将能够以 impartiality 看待这个问题的双方。（[Darwin](http://darwin-online.org.uk/Variorum/1860/1860-482-c-1861.html)）

> Men of science 应当被勒死\[在他们六十岁生日时\]，免得 age 使他们变得顽固，不愿 reception 新的 truths，成为 progress 的绊脚石；他们曾经理所应当地赢得的 influence 越大，这种危害就越严重（[Huxley](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Life_and_Letters_of_Thomas_Henry_Huxley/LO4EAAAAYAAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=life+letters+huxley+%E2%80%9Clest+age+should+harden+them+against+the+reception+of+new+truths,+and+make+them+into+clogs+upon+progress&pg=PA117&printsec=frontcover)）

> 我并不指望自己的 ideas 一下子就被 adopted。人的 mind 会形成固定的看待事物的方式。那些在职业生涯的大部分时间里，始终按照某种 point of view 来 envisaged nature 的人，只有经过艰难的努力才能接受新的 ideas。因此，必须由时间的流逝来 confirm 或 destroy 我所提出的 opinions。与此同时，我十分欣慰地看到，年轻人正开始不带 prejudice 地 study 这门 science，而 mathematicians 和 physicists 也带着 fresh mind 接触 chemical truths——所有这些人都已不再 believe in phlogiston。（[Lavoisier](https://www.google.com/books/edition/The_Edge_of_Objectivity/ZeI9DwAAQBAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&bsq=I%20do%20not%20expect%20my%20ideas%20to%20be%20adopted%20all%20at%20once)）

> 任何 mathematician 都绝不应让自己忘记，相比其他任何 art 或 science，mathematics 更是一场年轻人的游戏（[Hardy](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/180301-i-had-better-say-something-here-about-this-question-of)）

但这是真的吗？让我们一探究竟！

## Scientists 正在 getting older

无论考察 US 的 [NIH](https://science.sciencemag.org/content/322/5903/848)，还是整体的 scientific workforce，result 都基本相同：scientific workforce 正在 getting older。在 NIH，2016 年首次 [obtain](https://www.aamc.org/news-insights/nih-research-community-target-next-generation-scientists#:%7E:text=According%20to%20NIH%20data%2C%20the,the%20average%20age%20is%2045.) R01 grant 的 average age 是 43 岁，高于 1980 年的 35.7 岁。**请注意，这里指的是 Pricipal Investigators，而不是所有受到 NIH grants 资助的人**（后者包括 PhD students 和 postdocs，他们的人数可能是 PIs 的 5-6 倍）

![image-20201210115846378](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210115846378.png)

无论你[如何 slice 这些 data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5488913/)，如今获得 grants 的 oldest scientists 年龄更大了，而 youngest 的那些也不如过去那么年轻：

![一个存放图片、插图等内容的外部文件。Object name 是 pnas.1609996114sfig01.jpg](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/pnas.1609996114sfig01.jpg)

而且，这不仅仅发生在 NIH。[scientific workforce](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/114/15/3879.full.pdf) 历来就比 US 的 average worker 年龄更大，如今还在继续 getting older：

![image-20201210120637390](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210120637390.png)

对于 scientific workforce 的 getting older，有[四个核心 explanations](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/114/15/3879.full.pdf)。

其一很简单：由于 reduced fertility rates 与 increased life expectancy 的共同作用，整体 workforce 正在 getting older。foreign scientists 向 US 的 immigration 稍微改善了这一 trend，因为他们往往更年轻（[Figure SI-20](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/suppl/2017/03/21/1611748114.DCSupplemental/pnas.1611748114.sapp.pdf)），但这种 effect 不足以 offset 更广泛的 trend。

第二个 [explanation](https://www.the-scientist.com/news/with-end-of-mandatory-retirement-us-schools-face-new-challenges-59299) 是，直到 1994 年以前，US 的 professors 都必须在 70 岁时 forcibly retire（Europe 仍在实行这项 policy），这给 scientific careers 的 length、进而也给 average age 设置了一个 artificial cap（[这里是](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED347868.pdf)促成这次 policy change 的一份报告；他们发现，对 older scientists 的 productivity 的担忧是 unfounded）。不过，这两个 factors 实际上并不能 explain 多少 increase；近年来，占这一 effect 最大份额的，反而是 boomers 的 retirement（第三个 explanation）。

> 首先，我们 show，庞大的 baby boom cohort 的 aging 所体现的“demographic momentum”，已经 driven 了近期 scientific workforce 的 rapid aging 中的很大一部分；随着 baby boom 中较晚的 cohorts 走过六十多岁和七十岁出头的阶段，这种作用还将持续二十年。不过，自 1993 年以来，scientists 从 employment 中 retire 的 rate 出现 sharp declines，这可以 account for scientists 的 mean age 的 increase 中的 8%。retirement 的 decline 最有可能是由 universities 在 1994 年 elimination mandatory retirement 所 triggered。我们还 find，整体 workforce 的 aging（由 lower fertility 导致）account for scientific workforce 的 mean age 的 increase 中的 13%。其次，我们 show，当我们的 analysis 于 1993 年开始时，scientific workforce 距离其 implied steady-state age distribution 还非常远（on average 年轻 4.9 y）。值得注意的是，即使到了 2008 年，scientific workforce 仍然远未达到 steady state——当前的 entry、exit 和 transition rates imply，scientific workforce 的 mean age 还将在这一水平上再 increase 2.3 y。

一旦达到 steady state，driving factor 就会重新回到单纯的 population aging，这就引出了稍后将讨论的第四个 explanation（burden of knowledge）。

## establishment 是否 discriminate against 年轻的 scientists？

Scientists 获得首次 grants 的时间越来越晚。一种可能的原因是，存在某种针对较年轻 scientists 的 bias。这可能来自他们的 PI，或整个 academic community，也可能来自 assign grants 的特定 review committees（在 NIH，这些委员会称为 study sections）。这种 discrimination 可能以多种形式出现。年长的 academics 可能 discriminate against 较年轻的同行，因为后者想要 explore 他们不认可的新 ideas（他们认为这些不是好的 science），或者因为后者尚未掌握按照他们喜欢的方式 writing grants 的技巧。

NIH 确实有一些专门面向 New Investigators（寻求首次 grant 的人）和 Early Stage Investigators（ESI，获得 PhD 尚未超过 10 年的人）的 programs，而且多年来一直如此，不断对面向这些 groups 的特定 awards 进行 [reviewing](https://grants.nih.gov/policy/early-investigators/history.htm) 和调整。特别值得注意的是 DP5（[Early independence Award](https://commonfund.nih.gov/earlyindependence)）和 DP2（[New Innovator Award](https://commonfund.nih.gov/newinnovator)）grants，以及在 study sections 对 ESIs 进行 assessment 时给予特殊待遇的明确 policy（[这些](https://youtu.be/lzBhKeR6VIE?t=301)就是具体的做法）。

在更 granular 的层面，也可以看到，一个 age group 中有多少 scientists 会 apply for a grant，与有多少人获得 grants 之间存在 [rough correlation](https://faseb.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1096/fj.201800639)（这里 RPG 是 Research Project Grants，是包括 R01 grants 在内的一个 category）。情况并非一直如此，年长 PIs 的 success ratio 过去似乎更高。如果这里存在 bias，那也一定很小。

![image-20201210124856037](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210124856037.png)

当把 data 分为 clinical science 和 basic science，并将 analysis 限制在 2010 年之前的 time frame 时，这些年轻 basis science investigators 的 success rate 更低（[Figure 5](https://www.pnas.org/content/114/25/6498)）。所以，或许过去存在相当大的 bias，但由于 NIH policies，如今已不再如此（在 review 期间给予 ESIs 特殊考虑，是从 2008 年开始的做法）？这似乎取决于如何对 data 进行划分。NIH 有更新的 data，而这些 data 表明，尽管从 2004-2006 年开始出现了挤压，使首次申请的 PIs 与 established PIs 的 success 趋于接近，但这些 rates 已经开始[再次 diverge](https://report.nih.gov/nihdatabook/category/15)：

![image-20201210150810154](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210150810154.png)

但请注意，按 age 进行 broken down 时，并没有看到这一现象。这意味着，bias 并非针对年轻的 investigators，而是针对 _new_ investigators（他们通常年轻，但并非总是如此）；毕竟，New Investigator category 中有 15.7% 的 R01 applicants 超过 55 岁。对这一 pattern，一种 plausible explanation 是：[grantsmanship 是一门必须学习的技巧](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/668237)。了解你的 study section 喜欢什么需要时间，而 newcomer 可能难以 acquiring 这样的 knowledge。此外，如果刚进入某个 field 的人（因为他们年轻，或来自另一个 field）带来了新的 ideas，而这些 ideas 可能对该 [field](https://peerj.com/preprints/26465.pdf) 产生 disproportionate effect，那么这种 bias 可能尤其成问题。

> 如果一所 university 可以 hire 一位多接受了几年 training、有更多 publications，并且计划继续开展一个已经 productive 的 research program 的人，那么它为什么要 hire 一位仅接受了几年 postdoc training、就提出要 undertake 一个 novel research program 的 scientist 呢？

所以：如果存在 bias，它针对的是那些必须学习如何获得经费的 newcomers，而不一定是年轻人。因此，workforce 越来越年长，是由于“自然”的力量，而不是某种将年轻 researchers 拒之门外的 bias。这让我们转向 scientific productivity 及其与 age 的 relation，但在此之前，请注意，这些 results 会因 institute 而异。在 [NIDCR](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/grants-funding/funded-research/funding-outcomes/r01-success-early-investigators) 和 NCI（[Table 1](https://gsspubssl.nci.nih.gov/blog/articles?funding_patterns/2019)），情况恰好相反：在前者，new investigators 获得 funding 的可能性是其他人的两倍；在 NCI，ESIs 获得 funding 的可能性则高出 50%。

另一个问题是，reviewers 可能会（有意识或无意识地）将 reputation、prestige 或 eminence 作为 approve 一份 grant application 的 criteria。这种做法有一定的 rationality，但也意味着 newcomers 面临一个 Catch-22：他们可能因为在 field 中不为人知而无法获得 grants，又因为无法获得 grants 来在该 field 中开展 reasearch，而在这个 fiel 中不为人知。

## 日益增加的 burden of knowledge

从 graduate education 到成为 academic 所需时间更长，一个原因是 learn 需要时间。我们如今知道的比过去更多！早在 2009 年，Benjamin Jones 就把这个问题称为“burden of knowledge”。Matt Clancy 在[这里](https://mattsclancy.substack.com/p/are-ideas-getting-harder-to-find)对此有一篇很好的讲解，也讨论了将这些 knowledge 分散到多个人头脑中的 effect，

> 另一方面，burden of knowledge 本身就可能让 breakthroughs 更加困难！正如一篇[之前的 newsletter](https://mattsclancy.substack.com/p/innovation-in-teams) 中更详细讨论的那样，有一些 evidence 表明，teams 较不可能 produce breakthrough innovations。这可能是因为，当 ideas 分散在多个人的头脑中时，就更难发现它们之间的 unexpected connections。在这种情况下，burden of knowledge 可能变得 self-perpetuating。

[Azoulay et al. (2013)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/668237) 说，我们对此几乎无能为力！

> 如果 increased knowledge burden 是我们 interpret 在 figure 2 中 documented 的 pattern 时最 relevant 的视角，那么，一般的 scientific agencies，尤其是 NIH，似乎几乎没有什么能够或应该做的事来 counteract 这一 trend。

但这就像对 Tesla 说：“如果缺少 charging stations 是人们不买 electric cars 的一个 relevant reason，那你也没什么办法。”不！正确的做法是思考，是否有办法 reduce 为 acquire 这些 knowledge 所需的时间。Academic learning 不应该花那么长时间。它_不能_花那么长时间。academic education 究竟能被 compressed 到什么程度，我不知道，但根据我自己学习各个 areas 的经验，在寻找该学什么的过程中存在大量 inefficiencies。或许，这个问题的 solution 是一个 science 的 Wikipedia，以及维持它运转所需的 social norms，有点像 [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/)，也就是一套不断更新的 systematic reviews。然后再加入一些 SRS。[或许](https://numinous.productions/ttft/)可以在 papers 中 embed prompts，让它们更容易被记住；而 memory 中有了更多 facts，就有更大的可能发现新的 connections。尽管这些想法很有意思，但这里不是深入讨论它们的地方，所以我将继续下一节。

## cognitive skills 会随着 age 而 decline 吗？

## Intelligence（智力）

简而言之，是的，会。它们 decline 的 rates 不同，而这正是理解不同 fields 中 success 随 age 呈现的某些 patterns 的关键。

首先，就 general intelligence 而言，被接受的 finding 是，fluid intelligence 在[我们 30 多岁时](https://versatilebeing.com/2018/11/18/book-notes-intelligence-all-that-matters/)开始 declining。

![图片](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/img_4742.jpg)

这个 finding 一直备受争论，一些 researchers 认为，这种 decline 要到人生晚得多的时候才会发生。最终，在这个问题上我信任的 Stuart Ritchie 更倾向于 early-decline model，我也是如此。这场 controversy 归根结底源于这样一个事实：随着时间推移，cognitive skills 一直在 improving：如今一个 70 岁的人的 functioning 比一个出生于 50 年前的人更高。如果把出生于不同时期的人的 data 进行 pools（即一项 cross-sectional study），这个 background trend 就会 distort 这些 findings，让 decline 看起来很早就发生了。因此，我们应当 track 同一批人，开展 cohort 或 longitudinal study，看看他们一生中会发生什么。这也有自身的问题：如果你更频繁地 testing 人们，他们可能会学会在 tests 中表现得更好！最终，必须对这两种 effects 都进行 correct。这些 baseline cross-sectional 与 longitudinal effects [看起来是这样的](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2683339/)：

![image-20201210153251037](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210153251037.png)

这是 aggregate level 上的情况；在 individually 的层面，毫不意外，存在[大量 variation](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-devpsych-121318-085204)。一个 75 岁或以上的人仍可能比一个 50 岁的人更加 mentally agile。回到 science 的例子，lithium-ion battery 的 pioneer [John Goodenough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Goodenough) 已经 98 岁，仍在 leading 他的 lab；尽管他不像从前那样 lucid（他的一位 PhD students 告诉我的），他仍成功地使 lab 保持在这个 field 的 forefront。此外，他在[超过 50 岁时](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2019/10/advanced-chemistryprize2019.pdf)才完成了他的 Nobel Prize-winning work。最近，他还尝试追求 [high risk science](https://spectrum.ieee.org/energywise/energy/environment/a-glass-battery-that-keeps-getting-better)，其 risky 程度或许高到可能 violate [thermodynamics](http://lacey.se/2020/03/13/braga-goodenough-glass-battery-part-iii/) 的 laws，实际上无法 work（这个 [battery](https://spectrum.ieee.org/energywise/energy/batteries-storage/john-goodenough-glass-battery-news-hydroquebec) 将被 commercially developed，到时候我们就会知道它是否 succeeded）。无论如何，他正冒着 reputation 受损的风险尝试新事物，这与年老 scientist 变得 conservative 和 risk averse 的 stereotype 相反。作为一个 thought experiment，如果你可以在 status quo 与让 John Goodenough 提前 retiring + 给这个 field 增添一位年轻的 PhD student 之间选择，似乎应当选择前者。George Church（他 66 岁）headed 的 lab 是另一个在人生较晚阶段仍做出出色 work 的例子。

请注意，[无论你如何使用大脑](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/116/6/2021.full.pdf)，这种 decline 都是真实的：年轻时的 IQ predicts 年长时的 IQ，无论 profession 是什么，也无论是否参与涉及 "cognitive-intellectual" abilities 的活动。因此，我们可以有把握地 infer，这些 curves 对 scientists 尤其适用，正如它们对 general population 适用一样；不过要注意，他们的 starting points 通常会更高，因此一个 70 岁的普通 scientist 的 functioning 会优于同龄的普通人。

## Creativity（创造力）

Intelligence 并不是唯一重要的因素；要考察 scientific skill，或许还应看看 creativity 如何 changes。与 intelligence 相比，Creativity 是一个更不牢靠、constructed 得不那么 rigorously 的 construct，因此这些 results 不如 intelligence 方面的 results 那样 robust。如何 test creativity 呢？例如，可以让人们为一个给定的 object 想出 alternative uses。在一个涵盖 15-64 岁、共 293 人的 sample 中，未发现 [age 的 effect](https://product.design.umn.edu/courses/pdes2701/documents/5701papers/01creativity/kudrowitz13.pdf)；而[一项考察](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276529317_The_effects_of_age_on_divergent_thinking_and_creative_objects_production_a_cross-sectional_study/link/560fb10908aec422d11357e1/download)包括更年长者在内、共 150 人的 sample 的 study，则在另一种 test 中发现了强烈的 age-dependent effects，其中较年长的 subjects 的 scoring 更低（Figure 3）。

creativity 这个 field 的知名 researcher [Simonton](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781118392966.ch24) 发现了一种与 IQ 相似的 decline 的 pattern；他声称，这种 pattern [适用于整个范围](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254734326_Creative_Productivity_A_Predictive_and_Explanatory_Model_of_Career_Trajectories_and_Landmarks)的 creative endeavors，从 artists 到 scientists 都是如此。与 crystallized 和 fluid intelligence 的 patterns 一致，对于 scholars（想想 historians），这种 decline 不那么明显，或许是因为，尤其对于 History 而言，对广泛 facts 的 raw knowledge 比能够 rapidly generate creative 的新 hypothesis 和 inventions 更重要，因此年长的 historians 仍然能够 contribute。Seligman、Forgeard 和 [Kaufman](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Seligman-Forgeard-Kaufman-2016.pdf)（2016）指出，人们可能与 creativity 联系起来的大多数 abilities 都存在 general decline，从 intelligence（前面已经讨论过）到 originality，或 mind-wandering / having task-unrelated thoughts 都是如此。与 Simonton 一样，他们认为，年长的 subjects 仍然可以通过利用以 heuristics 形式存在的 wisdom 来保持 creative。在 science 中，较年轻的 researcher 或许能够以年长者做不到的方式，rediscover from first principles 如何做某件事；但凭借数十年从事这项 work 的经历，一位资深 scientist 可以直接 _see it_，对先前的 examples 进行 pattern matching。但这不能替代 sparks of insight。毕竟，这些 heuristics 是 based on _先前的_ examples 的。如果有某个新事物不 fit 过去的 experience，heuristic 可能会将其视为 implausible，而一位 reasoning from first principles 的年轻 scientist 则可能认为它是 doable。这或许就是 [Clarke's first law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarke%27s_three_laws) 的来源：

> 当一位 distinguished 但年迈的 scientist states 某件事是 possible 时，他几乎肯定是 right 的。当他 states 某件事是 impossible 时，他很可能是 wrong 的。

## Scientific productivity 与 age（科学生产力与年龄）

好，刚才讨论的是一般的 cognitive skills（因为我没能找到专门考察 scientists 的 study）。据此，从 theoretically 来说，年长的 scientists 可能可以用他们一生中积累的 knowledge 和 heuristics 来弥补 fluid cognitive skills 的 loss。因此，为了更好地理解 age 与 contributions to science 之间的 relation，我们必须把范围缩小到专门考察 scientists 的 studies。这些 studies 有多种类型，最简单的只是 measure 每年 written 的 papers 的 number。按照这个 metric，[一些 work](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/7/2/e013572.abstract) 发现，[scientists](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0004048) 会越来越多地 publish，直到他们满 50 岁，此后开始缓慢 decline。此外，最后提到的这篇 paper 还发现，大约在 40 岁时，这些 scientists 所 cite 的 literature 的 age 开始 increase，这反映出他们可能没有跟上较近的 trends，而是在一个 established paradigm 内 working。这篇 paper 还说，即使到了晚年，scientists 仍会继续 publishing high impact work（以 citations 来 measured）。

在较早的 work 中，[Cole (1979)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/226868) 发现，从 mathematics 到 sociology 的一系列 disciplines 中，都存在类似的 pattern。

![image-20201211140341164](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211140341164.png)

对于 citations，pattern 就没有那么 clear；在我看来，这与一生中任何时候 producing highly cited work 的 likelihood 都差不多这一情况是 compatible 的。Citations 并不是很好的 proxy，因为它也把 writing reviews 考虑在内（reviews 并不是 cutting edge science，但它们也可能是 highly cited）。

![image-20201211141626780](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211141626780.png)

即使考察 mathematics——一个我们可能预期较年轻的 scientists 会具有 advantage 的 field（因为相较于比如 biology，mathematics 更 relies on fluid intelligence）——Cole 在 examines 一个 cohort 的 mathematicians 多年来的 publish 情况时，也没有发现各个 age 与 citations 或 papers published 之间存在 correlation。他还发现，某个时间点的 publications 可以 predict 后来的 publications，因此，可以预期那些刚取得 PhD 后几年里的“strong publishers”，几十年后仍会如此。

时至今日，这仍然是对于广义的 scientific productivity 的 accepted view，被称为 [random impact rule](https://www.scholars.northwestern.edu/en/publications/quantifying-the-evolution-of-individual-scientific-impact)：highly impactful work 可能在任何时候出现，而一旦出现，接下来 ~4 年内往往还会有更多这样的 work 随之而来，这就是“[hot streak](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0315-8)” effect。对于大多数 scientists（68%），这种情况只发生一次，因此，如果一位 scientist 已经 produced 过一阵 highly cited work，那么这种情况很可能不会再在这位 scientist 身上发生。[Nobel Prize winners](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rsif.2020.0135) 的特殊之处在于，他们更常出现两次这样的 hot streakas，而且每次 lasts 的时间（5.2 年）都比一般的 scientist 更长。

## Highly impactful contributions（具有高度影响力的贡献）

对于那些被直接 judged 为 impactful 的 contributions（例如 Nobel Prize），情况可能并非如此；它们处于所有 scientific work 的 distribution 的 far right tail。[Li et al. (2019)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10113092) 和 Li et al.（[2020](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rsif.2020.0135)）发现，random impact rule 总体上也 applies to Nobel Laureates 所做的 work，但有一个关键的 exception：通向 Nobel Prize 的 work 往往出现在人生较早的阶段，这与 random impact rule 所 predict 的情况相反。如果从他们的 careers 中 removed 那项 work，Nobel Laureates 的 work 也会 follows random impact rule；authors 将此 interpret 为 data 中的 bias：因为 Prize 不能在死后 awarded，所以其中 represented 的年长 researchers 会更少。

1995 年之后的 Nobel-winning research 完成时的 average age 是 44 岁（[Bjørk, 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11192-019-03065-4)），这个 age 在过去[曾经更低](https://physicstoday.scitation.org/do/10.1063/PT.5.2012/full/)（另见 Handbook of Genius 的 [chapter 20](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781118367377.ch20)），而对于 physics，这个 age 还要略低一些（42）。Nobel Prize 的 [winners 和 great inventors](https://www.mitpressjournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1162/rest.2009.11724#:%7E:text=The%20mean%20age%20at%20great,in%20the%20early%20life%20cycle) 都呈现类似的 pattern。

![image-20201211151303997](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211151303997.png)

但 Einstein 呢？modern physics 的诞生是那个印证 rule 的 exception，而这一点可以从 data 中直观看出，这些 data 同样来自 chapter 20。

![image-20201211152119178](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211152119178.png)

根据同一个 source，这背后的 rationale 是，theoretical breakthroughs 通常由较年轻的 scientists 完成，或许是因为 experiments 需要对 field 有更深的 knowledge，而 theory 坐在扶手椅上就能做（想想 Galileo 和 Einstein 的 _Gedankenexperiments_）。对于 modern physics，在那个变化时期，可能是旧的 theories 和 heuristics 突然变得 invalid，给那些较年轻的 scientists 留出了空间，并使年长的 scientists 无法 using 他们的 comparative advantage。这自然引出了下一个问题：这仅仅是因为年长的 scientists 相对不太能够做 novel work 吗？还是因为他们不愿意做；也就是说，因为他们 reject 新的 paradigm？

![image-20201211152513043](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211152513043.png)

Dietrich 和 Srinivasan（[2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/j.2162-6057.2007.tb01281.x)）考察的不是 Nobel Laureates 在何时 published 他们的关键 work，而是通向那项 work 的 insights 在何时出现；按照 definition，这个时间更早，而且在他们看来早得足以把 curves 进一步 shift 到左侧，以至于他们 conclude：_paradigm-busting ideas 极大多数 occur 于 20’s 和 early 30’s 的人，这 indication 了 nimble prefrontal cortex，因而也就是 chronological age，是一个 critical factor。_他们还 examine，是否是 career age（而非 chronological age）determines 一位 scientist 何时会有 Nobel-worthy ideas，并发现，在一个 field 中 active 满第二个十年后，这类 ideas 会 sharp drop off，而大多数 ideas 都 occurring 在第一年。不过，他们的 paper 没有按时间 break down patterns，因此，较近的 Laureates 仍有可能比他们所说的更年长；它也没有 account for 因不存在 posthumus prices 而 introduced 的 bias。

这里的 findings 是 aggregated 的，我们可以聚焦于 history of science 中的特定时期：在那些时期，做出关键 advances 的不是年轻的 scientists，而是中年的 scientists。[Wray (2004)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:SCIE.0000037367.99073.bb) 描述的一个这样的 case，就是 1877 至 1898 年间 bacteriology 的 development。

![image-20201211154848530](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211154848530.png)

但即便如此！请注意，虽然这使 discoveries 更偏向中年的 scientists，但它也确实表明，在这个特定时期，_年长的 scientistists 甚至更不 likely 有 contributed_：

![image-20201211190306849](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211190306849.png)

\[2\]. 这些 ideas 是从 papers 的 titles 和 abstracts 中 extracted 的 keywords 及其 combinations。他们还 checked 了一种可能性，即较年轻的 researchers 只是在 using 一些 buzzwords，而这些 buzzwords 只是较早用语的 synonyms；他们没有发现这种情况。

对这个问题较近的一次尝试是 [Packalen & Bhattacharya (2015)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/703160)。他们的做法是，取 1946 年起的所有 biomedical publications，并 compare 年轻与年长的 scientists 在 cite 新 ideas[2](https://nintil.com/age-and-science/#sidenote-2) 方面的 tendency。他们发现，较年轻的 scientists 确实更常 cite novel ideas，但在 team setting 中，一位年轻的 author 与更有经验的 authors 的 combination 会带来最大的 effect，超过一个由年轻 scientists 组成的 team 所会 produce 的 effect；这大概也是 science 中常见的 standard combination，即一位年长的 PI 加上较年轻的 postdocs。这个 effect 的 magnitude 是，进入 careers 已有 40 年的 researchers cite 新 ideas 的比例略低（15%）于刚起步的 researchers（22%）。

因此，in conclusion，看起来 very impactful work 可以在人生中的任何时候出现。

## 年长的 scientists 是否较不 receptive 于新的 ideas？

\[3\]. 我认为这通常并不成立；是的，tenured professor 不必那么担心自己的 career，但他们如今多了些“要养活的人”，因此，training 新的 scientists 并确保 \*他们\* successful 的 burden，抵消了 tenure 可能带来的 advantage。他们仍然必须 pursue 能让 \*其他人\* 获得 tenure 的东西。

本文开头提到的 Planck's principle 指出，年长的 scientists 与其说是被 convinced 自己错了，不如说是在去世时把自己的错误一起带走。根据上述考虑，有一些 a priori reason 让人认为这是真的，但也可以提出：他们已经 accrued 更多 knowledge，而且如果存在 tenure system，让年长的 scientists 能够自由地 pursue 新的 ideas，而不必害怕被 fired[3](https://nintil.com/age-and-science/#sidenote-3) ，那么获得 tenure 也可能有利于 finding 和 pursuing 新的 ideas。所以，这最终是一个 empirical question！

一种办法是考察 history of science 中的特定事件，看看不同 ages 的 scientists 如何反应。以 Darwinism 为例。[Hull et al. (1978)](https://science.sciencemag.org/content/202/4369/717.long) studied 年轻的 scientists 相较年长者是否 acceptance 更快。请记得，Darwin 本人认为年轻人对 natural selection 更 open。在 Darwin 的书 publication 十年后，他们的 sample 中有 67 位 scientists，其中 50 位已经 converted；当时，accepters 的 average age 是 39.6，而仍未被 convinced 的人则是 48.1，但这些 averages 掩盖了大量 variability，最终 age 所 explains 的 variation in acceptance rates 不到 10%。此外，在那些 accepted 该 theory 的 scientists 中，age 似乎并不影响他们多早开始 accept 它。其他考察 cliometrics 或 plate tectonics 的 adoption 的 studies 也 find 类似的 results：要么 effect 很小，要么完全没有 effect（[Levin 1995](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030631295025002003)）。即使考察一个最终并不 correct 的新 development（polywater），也没有 effect：年轻的 scientists 并不更 likely 相信它（[Diamond 1988](http://www.artdiamond.com/DiamondPDFs/PolywaterEpisode.pdf)）。

Handbook of Genius 的 Chapter 26，_对 scientific innovation 的 Openness_，有更大的 sample size，涵盖从 1543 到 1960s 的 28 场 scientific controversies，涉及将近四千位 scientists。在这里，age 似乎确实有 effect，social attitudes 的一项 metric（即 liberal）也有 effect。除了某些情况，eminence 与成为 early adopter 并不 correlate；但在 controlling for age 时，eminence（根据获得的 awards 和 prizes rated）与较不 likely adopt Darwinism 呈 negatively associated。

> 一位比 sample 的 mean age 更年轻的 scientist，support 一项新的 scientific innovation 的可能性，是 age 高于 mean 的 scientists 的 1.6 倍；$$..$$ Comparing supporters 与 opponents，两者的 mean age difference 是 14.6 年。然而，在最坚定的 supporters 和 opponents 之间，age disparity 几乎有两倍那么大。

然而，这是一个非常 heterogeneous 的 sample 的 average：

> 当 age 这样的 predictor 的 effect sizes 在不同 historical event 之间存在很大差异时，就会发生 Statistical heterogeneity。heterogeneity 的 Evidence 通常指向重要的 moderator effects。例如，age 与 acceptance 新的 science theories 之间的 correlation，从 Freud’s psychoanalytic ideas 被 reception 期间的 −.50（n = 218, p < .001），变化到 19 世纪以 vitalistic 方式 refute doctrine of spontaneous generation 期间的 +.09（n = 43, p = .55）；在前一种情况下，年轻人对这些 theories 表现出更大的 support，而后一种 conservative stance 通常更能吸引年长的 scientists。

这为 Planck's principle 提供了一些 support；但它仍留下了一些没有回答的问题。也许 age _过去_确实与对新 ideas 较少的 openness correlated，但如今已不再如此；而且无论如何，这并非每一场 controversy 都具有的 universal feature。这篇 paper 并未考察 effect 是否随时间发生了变化。

最近一次尝试 assess 这个问题的是 Azoulay et al.（[2019](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/shared/ods/documents/?PublicationDocumentID=5663)），他们提出了一个相关但不同的问题：他们问的不是年长是否让 scientists 较不 likely convert 到新的 theories，而是当一位“star scientist”去世时，那个 field 是否会出现 changes。他们 find 确实如此，并将这 interpret 为一种 evidence：这些 scientists 的 eminence 阻碍了可能持有不同 perspectives 的 outsiders（来自其他 fields）向那个 field 作出 contributing。他们具体 test 的是（针对 life sciences，以及出生于 1899 及之后的 scientists）：

> 452 位 eminent scientists 的 premature death，如何 alter 他们在去世前几年 actively published 的 subfields 的 vitality（以 publication rates 和 funding flows measured），并与 matched control subfields 进行 compared。$$...$$
> 
> 出乎我们意料的是，接过 leadership 衣钵的并不是 subfield 内部的 competitors，而是来自其他 fields 的 entrants，他们进入其中，填补 star 缺席留下的空缺。重要的是，**来自 outsiders 的这波 contributions surge 所 draws upon 的是不同的 scientific corpus，而且 disproportionately likely 被 highly cited**。因此，与 Planck 的 contention consistent，**一位 luminary 的离世，为 fields 朝能够 advance scientific frontier 的 novel directions 进行 evolve 提供了机会**。

人们_可以_将此视为 Planck's principle 的一个很好的 exemplar。毕竟，Planck 并未谈论 youth，只是说存在一些不肯被 convinced 的顽固者，他们会被 newcomers replaced。那些 newcomers 不一定年轻，incumbents 也不一定年长。paper 本身也尝试考察 effeect 是否由 age driven（p. 25），但情况似乎并非如此。

paper 本身承认，这些 contributions 与其说是 overthrow 既有的 paradigms，不如说更像是在 extend 或 complement 它们，

> 然而，重要的是要注意，上述 findings 并不 imply entrants 的 published results 必然 contradict 或 overturn 某个 subfield 内 prevailing scientific understanding 和 assumptions。我们利用 Funk and Owen-Smith (2017) 最近提出的一项 measure，为这些 contributions 的 disruptive impact 提供 indirect evidence。他们的 index 通过 measuring 未来基于 focal idea 的 ideas 是否也依赖其 acknowledged predecessors，来 captures 一个 idea 在多大程度上 consolidates 或 destabilizes status quo。Appendix E 的 Table E4 中的 results suggest，这些 contributions 并没有 radically disrupt 该 subfield。相反，它们似乎 reflect 了无数“small r,” permanent revolutions 的 impact，在这些 revolutions 中，新的 ideas 脱颖而出，却不一定 eclipsing 先前的 approaches。$$...$$
> 
> 综合来看，这些 results suggest，当 star 还活着时，outsiders 不愿 challenge 一个 field 内的 hegemonic leadership。它们也 highlight 了若干即使在她离世后仍可能 constrain entry 的 factors。Intellectual、social 和 resource barriers 似乎都在 impeding entry 中发挥作用；outsiders 只会 entering 那些 topology 为“foreign” ideas 的 support 和 acceptance 提供较不 hostile landscape 的 subfields。

Azoulay 所 estimated 的 effect magnitude 是 nontrivial；在“super star”去世的 fields 中，来自并非这位 super star 的 collaborators 的 scientists 的 publications，比预期多出 8.55%（Table 3）；来自 collaborators 的 contributions 则下降 20%。Funding 分别以类似的 magnitudes 上升和下降（这些都是_每年_的变化）。non-collaborators 的 high impact（>99th percentile）publications 的 number 上升了 37%（Table 4），这更加令人惊讶，甚至触发了我的 [effect is too large heuristic](https://jasoncollins.blog/2017/07/06/the-effect-is-too-large-heuristic/)。以 absolute terms 并从更长期的视角看这个 field，difference 是[惊人的](https://www.nber.org/digest/mar16/does-science-advance-one-funeral-time)

![summary 的图片](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/scientists.jpg)

另一个有意思的 finding 出现在 Figure E1（p. 81），它显示，fields 平均而言以 normal-curve 的方式诞生和 decay，而这些 trends 的 effect 完全盖过了 paper 中 studies 的任何东西。Figure E4 显示，对于这些 superstar scientists，productivity 会一直 increases 到 ~45 岁，随后缓慢 declines。对于 top 1% 的 publications，这种 decline 更加明显；也就是说，平均而言，scientists 在 ~45 岁之前 publishing 一篇 highly cited paper 的 odds 会有所 increase，随后这种产出 good work 的 rate 开始 steady decline。这符合以下看法：一般而言，age 对 producing highly cited papers 并不重要，但对于比这更 demanding 的 standards，它确实重要。

## 结论（Conclusion）

是的，scientists 日益年长是个坏消息。在 micro level，cognitive decline 确实存在，而在 macro level，Nobel Prize-worthy achievements 看来确实 disproportionately 由较年轻的 scientists 完成。不过，这并没有乍看之下那么糟糕。并不是说 _只有_ young scientists 才能做到，也不是说那些 creative scientists 几乎全都年轻。仅凭历史形成的天真看法是 biased：是的，历史上确实有许多非常年轻（不到 30 岁）的 scientists 从事那些 key research，但时代已经变了。Young researchers 的 comparative advantage 是他们较高的 fluid intelligence，而当需要学习的东西没有那么多时（全新的 fields 出现，或者像两个世纪前那样，我们对世界还知之甚少），older scientists 的 comparative advantage 就被 negates 了：他们确实知道得更多，而且能够用 experience-based heuristics 来 guide 他们的 research（也就是说，当时关于 Newtonian paradigm 中什么方向 promising 的 heuristics，在 applied 到新生的 modern physics paradigm 时，可能并不 work）。

随着我们 accumulated 更多 knowledge，为了 make a contribution 所需的 background knowledge 已经 increased，与此同时，new scientists 开始 practicing 的年龄，以及他们开展 high impact research 时的年龄，也都提高了。ideal researcher 应当年轻，而且对自己的 field 拥有 deep knowledge，但 gaining 这些 knowledge 需要时间。这样的 profile 非常罕见；或许这就是为什么 random impact hypothesis 成立，而与此同时 Nobel Prizes 的获得者又略微年轻：在 individuals 内部，knowledge 与 intelligence 是 inversely correlated，而摆脱这一关系的 outliers，正是那些获得 Nobel prizes 的人。

关于 Planck's principle 的 Research 显示，它有一定道理，尤其是考虑到 Handbook of Genius 的 Chapter 26 中所包含的 research（但需要注意，他们并没有考察 historical trends）。older 或 eminent scientists 眼下的情况（即 Azoulay paper 所 capturing 的 dynamics），与其说是 resistance 去 adopt 一个新的 "paradigm"，不如说是没有把新的 ideas 纳入考虑，或者没有使用新的 methods。因此，假设某个 field 的一个 key tenet 被 upended；我不会预期 older scientists 会在 accepting 新的 view 时特别迟缓。他们可能迟缓的地方，反而在于没有意识到：只要尝试这种他们以前从未用过的新 method，或者从别处 imported 一些 knowledge，他们就能 achieve 更多。

总而言之，

1.  science 的 aging 是多种 factors 共同 driven 的，包括整个 society 的 aging，尤其是 baby boom cohort 的 aging、mandatory retirement 的终结，以及为了 make a contribution 于 science 而 required knowledge 的 increase。从 problem-solving 的角度看，最后这一项是可以更直接 addressed 的。
2.  Older scientists 的精力可能不如年轻时，但实际上，具体的 experiments 会由其他 lab members 完成，因此这种 decline 在实践中的影响可能没有看起来那么大。
3.  Younger scientists 更有可能 explore 新的 ideas。这并不意味着这样的 research 更 promising，或更有可能是更 impactful 的。花 20 年把一个 idea 推进到其 ultimate consequences，可能比尝试刚出现的新事物更有价值。
4.  现实中，science 是在 social setting 中进行的，而不是独自进行的。由 older 和 younger scientists 组成的 Teams 是 science 中的常态。或许，如果 _所有人_ 都明显年长（或年轻），我们会看到 lab 的 productivity 出现问题（就平均而言），但我们距离这种情形还很远。
5.  Productivity（每年 published 的 papers 的 count）会 increases，直到约 ~40-50 岁，随后随年龄 declines
6.  一般来说，producing 一篇 highly cited paper 的 likelihood 在一生中 stays constant（即 random impact rule）。在某些 historical periods（向 modern physics 的 transition），有小概率并非如此，反而是 younger researchers 更有可能开展这种 highly valuable research。
7.  向 modern physics 的 transition 是一次 unique event，为那个时代的 younger scientists 开辟了机会。但我们不应从他们确实做出了许多 key discoveries 这一事实 infer，只有他们才能做到，或一般而言只有 young scientists 才能做出这些 discoveries。
8.  scientist 最有机会 achieve Nobel-worthiness 的 "ideal age" 是 field dependent；杰出的 mathematicians 和 physicists 比杰出的 biologists 和 chemists 更年轻。
9.  从历史上看，older scientists 一般而言可能较不愿意接受一个新的 paradigm，但这因 field 而有很大差异，而且尚不清楚如今是否仍然成立。在所有 eminent scientists 都较年长的情况下，eminence 会使 scientists 更不愿意 adopt 来自其他 fields 的新 methods 或 ideas。需要对近期的 "paradigm shifts" 开展一项 study，才能看出这是否仍然成立。

我们可以如何 leverage 这些 knowledge，让 science 再次伟大？尽管第 1-9 点涉及种种 heterogeneity 和 individual differences，但似乎很清楚：如果有人给我两位 researchers 的一叠 papers，并告诉我他们的年龄，那么，比起年龄，research 本身更能告诉我谁在做有意思的事情。要指出那些正在做出各自 field 中最佳工作的 older scientists，是非常容易的。

Scientists are getting older. Some have expressed concern at that fact. While the motivations for that concern are not always explicit, it usually boils down to two: One, a matter of fairness. Science getting older may mean that it's not making room for younger scientists; older scientists would be sitting in a limited number of chairs for life, so to speak. And two, a matter of efficiency, perhaps the dominant concern. This is the idea that science has a crucial need for young scientists in particular to come in untainted by prior knowledge and dogmas and provide a steady stream of breakthroughs at a rate higher than what their older peers can hope to accomplish.

Historically many scientists have subscribed to this latter view, from physicists to molecular biologists, some of whom indeed did carry out their breakthrough research when they were quite young. Einstein was 26 during his annus mirabilis, Newton was 23 during his and Darwin was 29 when he (after reading Malthus) conceived the theory of evolution by natural selection.

> A new scientific truth does not triumph by convincing its opponents and making them see the light, but rather because its opponents eventually die, and a new generation grows up that is familiar with it (Planck)

> I strongly believe that the only way to encourage innovation is to give it to the young. The young have a great advantage in that they are ignorant. Because I think ignorance in science is very important. If you’re like me and you know too much you can’t try new things. I always work in fields of which I’m totally ignorant… ([Brenner](https://www.openingscience.org/tag/sydney-brenner/))

> A person who has not made his great contribution to science before the age of 30 will never do so. ([Einstein](https://www.nature.com/articles/150698a0))

> Age is, of course, a fever chill that every physicist must fear. He’s better dead than living still when once he’s past his thirtieth year ([Dirac](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Scientific_Elite/HAHCzJfmD5IC?hl=en&gbpv=1&bsq=Jungk))

> A few naturalists, endowed with much flexibility of mind, and who have already begun to doubt on the immutability of species, may be influenced by this volume; but I look with confidence to the future, to young and rising naturalists, who will be able to view both sides of the question with impartiality. ([Darwin](http://darwin-online.org.uk/Variorum/1860/1860-482-c-1861.html))

> Men of science ought to be strangled \[at their sixtieth birthday\] lest age should harden them against the reception of new truths, and make them into clogs upon progress, the worse, in proportion to the influence they had deservedly won ([Huxley](https://www.google.com/books/edition/Life_and_Letters_of_Thomas_Henry_Huxley/LO4EAAAAYAAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&dq=life+letters+huxley+%E2%80%9Clest+age+should+harden+them+against+the+reception+of+new+truths,+and+make+them+into+clogs+upon+progress&pg=PA117&printsec=frontcover))

> I do not expect my ideas to be adopted all at once. The human mind gets creased into a way of seeing things. Those who have envisaged nature according to a certain point of view during much of their career, rise only with difficulty to new ideas. It is the passage of time, therefore, which must confirm or destroy the opinions I have presented. Meanwhile, I observe with great satisfaction that the young people are beginning to study the science without prejudice, and also the mathematicians and physicists, who come to chemical truths with a fresh mind - all these no longer believe in phlogiston. ([Lavoisier](https://www.google.com/books/edition/The_Edge_of_Objectivity/ZeI9DwAAQBAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&bsq=I%20do%20not%20expect%20my%20ideas%20to%20be%20adopted%20all%20at%20once))

> No mathematician should ever allow himself to forget that mathematics, more than any other art or science, is a young man's game ([Hardy](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/180301-i-had-better-say-something-here-about-this-question-of))

But is it true? Let's find out!

## Scientists are getting older

One can look at the [NIH](https://science.sciencemag.org/content/322/5903/848) in the US or at the scientific workforce in general, but the result is essentially the same, the scientific workforce is getting older. At NIH, the average age to [obtain](https://www.aamc.org/news-insights/nih-research-community-target-next-generation-scientists#:%7E:text=According%20to%20NIH%20data%2C%20the,the%20average%20age%20is%2045.) the first R01 grant in 2016 was 43 years, up from 35.7 in 1980. **Note that this is for Pricipal Investigators and not everyone that is being funded by NIH grants** (Including PhD students and postdocs that probably outnumber PIs by a factor of perhaps 5-6)

![image-20201210115846378](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210115846378.png)

It doesn't matter [how you slice the data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5488913/), the oldest scientists that are getting grants are older now, and the youngest ones are not as young as they used to:

![An external file that holds a picture, illustration, etc. Object name is pnas.1609996114sfig01.jpg](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/pnas.1609996114sfig01.jpg)

And furthermore it's not just NIH. [The scientific workforce](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/114/15/3879.full.pdf) who has historically been older than the average worker in the US keeps getting older:

![image-20201210120637390](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210120637390.png)

The reason for the scientific workforce getting older has [four core explanations](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/114/15/3879.full.pdf).

One is simply that the workforce at large is getting older due to a combination of reduced fertility rates and increased life expectancy. The immigration of foreign scientists to the US slightly improves the trend as they tend to be younger ([Figure SI-20](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/suppl/2017/03/21/1611748114.DCSupplemental/pnas.1611748114.sapp.pdf)) but the effect is not sufficient to offset the broader trend.

The second [explanation](https://www.the-scientist.com/news/with-end-of-mandatory-retirement-us-schools-face-new-challenges-59299) is that it used to be the case up until 1994 that professors in the US had to forcibly retire at age 70 (Europe continues this policy), instituting an artificial cap to the length of scientific careers and thus the average age ([Here's](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED347868.pdf) one of the reports that led to the policy change; they found concerns about the productivity of older scientists unfounded). These two factors however don't really explain much of the increase, rather it's the retirement of boomers that accounts for the largest share of the effect in recent years (the third explanation).

> First, we show that “demographic momentum” in the form of the aging of the large baby boom cohort has driven much of the recent rapid aging of the scientific workforce, and will continue to do so for the next two decades as the later cohorts of the baby boom pass through their 60s and early 70s. However, sharp declines since 1993 in the rate at which scientists retire from employment can account for 8% of the increase in the mean age of scientists. The decline in retirement was most likely triggered by the elimination of mandatory retirement at universities in 1994. We also find that the aging of the workforce as a whole (due to lower fertility) accounts for 13% of the increase in the mean age of the scientific workforce. Second, we show that the scientific workforce was very far from its implied steady-state age distribution when our analysis begins in 1993 (4.9 y younger on average). Strikingly, the scientific workforce remains far from steady state even as of 2008—current entry, exit, and transition rates imply that the mean age of the scientific workforce will increase by another 2.3 y from that level.

Once steady state is reached then we'll be back to plain population aging as the driving factor, leading to the fourth explanation (the burden of knowledge) that will be discussed later.

## Does the establishment discriminate against young scientists?

Scientists are getting their first grants later. One possibility for why this may be is some kind of bias against younger scientists. This could come from their PI or from the academic community in general, or from the specific review committees that assign grants (study sections in the case of NIH). This discrimination may come in various forms. Older academics may discriminate against younger ones because they want to explore new ideas they don't approve of (They think are not good science), or because they haven't yet mastered the art of writing grants in the way they like it.

NIH does have some programs specifically targeted both at New Investigators (Those that seek their first grant) and Early Stage Investigators (ESI, Those that are within 10 years of getting their PhD) and has done so for years, constantly [reviewing](https://grants.nih.gov/policy/early-investigators/history.htm) and tweaking specific awards targeted at these groups. Of particular note are the DP5 ([Early independence Award](https://commonfund.nih.gov/earlyindependence)), and the DP2 ([New Innovator Award](https://commonfund.nih.gov/newinnovator)) grants, as well as the explicit policy to give special treatment to ESIs during their assessment by study sections (Here's what [these](https://youtu.be/lzBhKeR6VIE?t=301) look like).

At a more granular level, one can also see that there is a [rough correlation](https://faseb.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1096/fj.201800639) between how many scientists of an age group apply for a grant and how many get them (Here RPG is Research Project Grants, a category which includes R01 grants). This was not always the case, the success ratio of older PIs seemed to be higher in the past. If there is a bias here, it must be small.

![image-20201210124856037](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210124856037.png)

When one splits the data into clinical science and basic science and restricts the analysis to the pre-2010 time frame, the success rate for these young basis science investigators is lower ([Figure 5](https://www.pnas.org/content/114/25/6498)). So maybe there used to be substantial bias, but not any longer thanks to the NIH policies (Giving special consideration to ESIs during review is a practice that started in 2008)? That seems to depend on how you cut your data. The NIH has more recent data, and this shows that while there was a squeeze starting in 2004-2006, bringing together success for first time and established PIs, these rates have started to [diverge again](https://report.nih.gov/nihdatabook/category/15):

![image-20201210150810154](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210150810154.png)

But note, this is NOT seen when broken down by age. What this means is that the bias is not against young investigators, but against _new_ investigators (Who will often but not always be young), after all 15.7% of R01 applicants that were under the New Investigator category were over 55. A plausible explanation for this pattern is that [grantsmanship is an art that has to be learned](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/668237). Knowing what your study section likes takes time, and a newcomer may have trouble acquiring such knowledge. Moreover this bias may be specially problematic if those people that are new to a field (because they are young or they come from another field) are bringing in new ideas that can have a disproportionate effect on the [field](https://peerj.com/preprints/26465.pdf).

> Why would a university hire a scientist proposing to undertake a novel research program after only a few years of postdoc training, when the institution could hire someone with several more years of training, many more publications, and a plan to continue an already productive research program?

So: If there's a bias, it's against newcomers that have to learn how to get money, not necessarily young people. So the workforce is getting older due to "natural" forces, not a bias that keeps young researchers out. That leads us to look at scientific productivity and its relation to age, but before do note that these results vary by institute. At [NIDCR](https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/grants-funding/funded-research/funding-outcomes/r01-success-early-investigators) as well as the NCI ([Table 1](https://gsspubssl.nci.nih.gov/blog/articles?funding_patterns/2019))the opposite holds: new investigators are twice as likely to get funding in the former case and 50% more likely in the NCI's case for ESIs.

An additional problem is that reviewers may use (consciously or unconsciously) reputation, prestige, or eminence as a criteria to approve a grant application. There is some rationality to this practice but it also means there's a Catch-22 for newcomers, they may not get grants because they are not known in the field, and they are not known in the fiel because they don't get grants to do reasearch in that field.

## The increasing burden of knowledge

A reason why it takes longer to go from graduate education to being an academic is that it takes time to learn. We now know more than we used to! Benjamin Jones dubbed this issue the "burden of knowledge" back in 2009. Matt Clancy has a good explainer of this [here](https://mattsclancy.substack.com/p/are-ideas-getting-harder-to-find), with some discussion on the effect of splitting this knowledge across multiple heads,

> On the other hand, the burden of knowledge may, itself, make breakthroughs more difficult! As discussed in more detail in a [previous newsletter](https://mattsclancy.substack.com/p/innovation-in-teams), there is some evidence that teams are less likely to produce breakthrough innovations. This might be because it’s harder to spot unexpected connections between ideas when they are split across multiple people’s heads. In that case, the burden of knowledge can become self-perpetuating.

[Azoulay et al. (2013)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/668237) say that there is little we can do about it!

> If increased knowledge burden is the most relevant prism through which we should interpret the pattern documented in figure 2, there would seem to be very little that scientific agencies in general, and the NIH in particular, could or should do to counteract this trend.

But this would be like saying Tesla "If lack of charging stations is a relevant reason why people don't buy electric cars there's little you can do" No! The right thing is to think if there are ways to reduce the time that is required to acquire such knowledge. Academic learning should not take that long. It _can't_ take that long. To what extent can academic education be compressed I don't know, but from my own experience learning about various areas there are lots of inefficiencies in finding the right things to learn. Maybe the solution to the problem is a Wikipedia of science and the social norms required to keep it working, sort of like the [SEP](https://plato.stanford.edu/), a collection of rolling systematic reviews. Then add some SRS. [Maybe](https://numinous.productions/ttft/) one could embed prompts on papers so that are more easily remembered, and with more facts in memory, more potential for finding new connections. While these are interesting thoughts, this is not the place to pursue them, so I'll move ahead to the next section.

## Do cognitive skills decline with age?

## Intelligence

Shortly put, yes they do. They do so at different rates and this is key to understand some patterns of success by age in different fields.

First, looking at general intelligence, the accepted finding is that fluid intelligence starts declining in [our 30s](https://versatilebeing.com/2018/11/18/book-notes-intelligence-all-that-matters/).

![img](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/img_4742.jpg)

This finding has been hotly debated, with some researchers arguing that the decline doesn't happen until much later in life. Ultimately Stuart Ritchie, who I trust for this, prefers the early-decline model and so do I. The controversy boils down to the fact that over time cognitive skills have been improving: A 70 year old today has higher functioning than one born 50 years ago. If one pools data for people born at different times (a cross-sectional study), that background trend will distort the findings, making it seem like decline happens early. So we should track the same people, doing a cohort or longitudinal study, and see what happens over their lifetime. This also has its own problem: if you are testing people more often they may learn to do better on the tests! Ultimately one has to correct for both effects. This is what the baseline cross-sectional vs longitudinal effects [look like](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2683339/):

![image-20201210153251037](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201210153251037.png)

This is at an aggregate level, individually unsurprisingly there is [a lot of variation](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-devpsych-121318-085204). One person aged 75 or older can still be more mentally agile that someone who is 50. To turn it back to the case of science, lithium-ion battery pioneer [John Goodenough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_B._Goodenough) is 98 years old and still leading his lab and while he's not as lucid as he used to (One of his PhD students tells me), he has managed to maintain the lab at the forefront of the field. Plus he did his Nobel Prize-winning work when he [was over 50](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2019/10/advanced-chemistryprize2019.pdf). He also recently has tried to chase [high risk science](https://spectrum.ieee.org/energywise/energy/environment/a-glass-battery-that-keeps-getting-better), perhaps so risky that it may violate the laws of [thermodynamics](http://lacey.se/2020/03/13/braga-goodenough-glass-battery-part-iii/) and actually not work (The [battery](https://spectrum.ieee.org/energywise/energy/batteries-storage/john-goodenough-glass-battery-news-hydroquebec) will be commercially developed so that's when we will know if it succeeded). In any case, he's risking his reputation to try something new, contrary to the stereotype of the old scientist that turns conservative and risk averse. As a thought experiment, if you could choose between the status quo or prematurely retiring John Goodenough + adding a new young PhD student to the field, it seems the former is to be preferred. The lab headed by George Church (He's 66) is another example of great work being done later in life.

Note that this decline is real [no matter how you use your brain](https://www.pnas.org/content/pnas/116/6/2021.full.pdf): IQ at young age predicts IQ at older age regardless of profession or engagement in "cognitive-intellectual" abilities, so we may safely infer that these curves apply to scientists in particular as they do for the general population, with the caveat that their starting points will be typically higher, so the average scientist at 70 years old will be better functioning that the average person of the same age.

## Creativity

Intelligence is not all that matters, plausibly for scientific skill one should look at how creativity changes. Creativity is a flimsier and less rigorously constructed construct compared to intelligence so these results are not as robust as those for intelligence. How do you test creativity? You can ask people to come up with alternative uses for a given object for example. In a sample of 293 people covering ages 15-64 there was [no effect of age](https://product.design.umn.edu/courses/pdes2701/documents/5701papers/01creativity/kudrowitz13.pdf) to be found while [a study looking](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276529317_The_effects_of_age_on_divergent_thinking_and_creative_objects_production_a_cross-sectional_study/link/560fb10908aec422d11357e1/download) at a sample of 150 people, including even older people found strong age-dependent effects, with older subjects scoring lower (Figure 3) in a different test.

[Simonton](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781118392966.ch24), a known researcher in the field of creativity finds a pattern of decline similar to that of IQ; a pattern he claims [fits the whole range](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254734326_Creative_Productivity_A_Predictive_and_Explanatory_Model_of_Career_Trajectories_and_Landmarks) of creative endeavors from artists to scientists. Fitting with the patterns for crystallized and fluid intelligence, for scholars (think historians) the decline is less noted, perhaps because for History in particular raw knowledge of a broad range of facts is more important than being able to rapidly generate creative new hypothesis and inventions, so older historians can still contribute. Seligman, Forgeard and [Kaufman](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Seligman-Forgeard-Kaufman-2016.pdf) (2016) point to a general decline of most abilities that one may associate with creativity, from intelligence (already discused) to originality, or mind-wandering / having task-unrelated thoughts. Like Simonton, they say older subjects can still be creative by leveraging their wisdom in the forms of heuristics. In the case of science, a younger researcher may be able to rediscover from first principles how to do something in a way an older one cannot, but by force of decades of doing the work, a veteran scientist can just _see it_, pattern matching to previous examples. But this is no substitute for sparks of insight. After all these heuristics are based on _previous_ examples. If there's something new that doesn't fit past experience, the heuristic may regard that as implausible, whereas a younger scientist reasoning from first principles may think it's doable. This may be the source of [Clarke's first law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarke%27s_three_laws),

> When a distinguished but elderly scientist states that something is possible, he is almost certainly right. When he states that something is impossible, he is very probably wrong.

## Scientific productivity and age

Ok that was for cognitive skills in general (As I couldn't find a study looking specifically at scientists). Based on that it would be theoretically possible that older scientists can make up for their loss of fluid cognitive skills with knowledge and heuristics that they accrue over their lifetime. So to understand better the relation between age and contributions to science one we have to narrow down to studies that look specifically at scientists. Out of these there are various types, the most simple ones just measure number of papers written per year. By that metric, [some work](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/7/2/e013572.abstract) find [that scientists](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0004048) increasingly publish until they turn 50, initiating then a slow decline. Moreover, this last paper also finds that around age 40 the age of the literature these scientists cite starts to increase, reflecting that they may not be keeping up with more recent trends, working inside an established paradigm instead. The paper also says that even until later in life scientists keep publishing high impact work (As measured by citations).

In older work, [Cole (1979)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/226868) found that there is a similar pattern at work across a range of disciplines spanning mathematics to sociology.

![image-20201211140341164](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211140341164.png)

With citations a pattern is less clear, and to my view compatible with the likelihood of producing highly cited work at any point in life being similar. Citations is not that great of a proxy, because it's also considering writing reviews (Which are not cutting edge science, yet they can be highly cited).

![image-20201211141626780](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211141626780.png)

Even when looking at mathematics, a field where we may expect younger scientists to have an advantage (In as much as mathematics relies more on fluid intelligence relative to say, biology), Cole examines a cohort of mathematicians as they publish over the years and finds no correlation with either citations or papers published at each age. He also finds that publications at one point in time predict publications later, so "strong publishers" in their years immediately after their PhD can be expected to continue to be so decades after.

This remains to this day the accepted view for scientific productivity broadly speaking, known as the [random impact rule](https://www.scholars.northwestern.edu/en/publications/quantifying-the-evolution-of-individual-scientific-impact), that highly impactful work may come at any time, and when it does it tends to be followed by more within the next ~4 years, a "[hot streak](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0315-8)" effect. For most scientists (68%) this happens just once, so if a scientist has already produced a burst of highly cited work, that probably won't happen again for that individual scientist. [Nobel Prize winners](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rsif.2020.0135) are peculiar in that for them having two of these hot streakas is more common, each of which lasts for longer (5.2 years) than for the average scientist.

## Highly impactful contributions

This may not be true for contributions directly judged to be impactful (like the Nobel Prize), the far right tail of the distribution of all scientific work. [Li et al. (2019)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10113092) and Li et al. ([2020](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/abs/10.1098/rsif.2020.0135)) find that the random impact rule also applies to the work that Nobel Laureates do in general with one crucial exception: The work that leads to the Nobel Prize tends to happen earlier in life, against what the random impact rule would predict. If that work is removed from their careers, Nobel Laureates' work also follows the random impact rule, which the authors interpret as a bias in the data: because the Prize cannot be awarded posthumously, there will be fewer older researchers represented in it.

The average age when Nobel-winning research post 1995 is done is 44 ([Bjørk, 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11192-019-03065-4)), an age that [used to be lower](https://physicstoday.scitation.org/do/10.1063/PT.5.2012/full/) in the past (see also [chapter 20](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/9781118367377.ch20) from the Handbook of Genius) and that is a bit lower (42) for physics. The pattern is similar for both Nobel Prize [winners and great inventors](https://www.mitpressjournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1162/rest.2009.11724#:%7E:text=The%20mean%20age%20at%20great,in%20the%20early%20life%20cycle).

![image-20201211151303997](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211151303997.png)

What about Einstein though? The birth of modern physics is the exception that confirms the rule and that can be eyeballed in the data, again from chapter 20.

![image-20201211152119178](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211152119178.png)

The rationale for this is, following the same source, that theoretical breakthroughs are usually done by younger scientists, perhaps because experiments require deeper knowledge of the field and theory can be done from an armchair (Think of Galileo and Einstein's _Gedankenexperiments_). In the case of modern physics, during that period of change it may have been that old theories and heuristics are suddenly invalid, leaving room open for those younger scientists, and preventing older scientists from using their comparative advantage. That naturally leads to the next question: Is this just because the older scientists are relatively less able to do novel work? Or is it because they don't want to; namely because they reject the new paradigm?

![image-20201211152513043](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211152513043.png)

Dietrich and Srinivasan ([2011](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/j.2162-6057.2007.tb01281.x)) look not at when Nobel Laureates published their key work, but at when the insights leading to that work happened, by definition earlier, and in their view sufficiently early to shift the curves further to the left so much that they conclude that _paradigm-busting ideas occur overwhelmingly to people in their 20’s and early 30’s, as indication that a nimble prefrontal cortex, and thus chronological age, is a critical factor._ They also examine whether career age (As opposed to chronological age) determines when a scientist will have Nobel-worthy ideas and find a sharp drop off after the second decade of being active in a field, with most of the ideas occurring during the first year. However, their paper does not break down patterns by time, so it's still possible that more recent Laureates are older than what they suggest; nor does it account for the bias introduced by the non-existence of posthumus prices.

The findings here are aggregated and one can zoom in to particular episodes in the history of science where it wasn't young scientists doing the key advances but middle-aged ones. One such case described in [Wray (2004)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1023/B:SCIE.0000037367.99073.bb) is the development of bacteriology in the period 1877 to 1898.

![image-20201211154848530](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211154848530.png)

But still! Do note that while this skews the discoveries towards middle-aged scientists, it does also show that _older scientistists are even less likely to have contributed_ in this particular episode:

![image-20201211190306849](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/image-20201211190306849.png)

\[2\]. These ideas are keywords and combinations thereof extracted from the papers titles and abstracts. They also checked for the possibility of younger researchers just using buzzwords that are just synonyms of older ones, and they don't find that they do.

A more recent stab at this problem is [Packalen & Bhattacharya (2015)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/703160). What they do is take all biomedical publications from 1946 and compare the tendency of young and old scientists to cite new ideas[2](https://nintil.com/age-and-science/#sidenote-2) . They find that indeed younger scientists do cite novel ideas more often but in a team setting, a combination of a young and more experienced authors lead to the largest effect, more than what a team of young scientists would produce, and probably the standard combination one finds in science, with an older PI and younger postdocs. The magnitude of the effect is that researchers 40 years into their careers cite new ideas somewhat less (15%) than the researchers just starting out would (22%).

So in conclusion, it looks like very impactful work can happen at any point in life.

## Are older scientists less receptive to new ideas?

\[3\]. I think this is not true in general; yes a tenured professor doesn't have to worry as much about their career but they now have extra 'mouths to feed' and so the burden of training new scientists and making sure that \*they\* are successful defeats that potential advantage from tenure. They still have to pursue whatever gets \*the rest\* tenure.

Stated at the beginning of this post, Planck's principle states that older scientists are not so much convinced that they are wrong, but that they rather take down their wrongness with them when they die. There is some a priori reason to think that this is true from the considerations above but then one could argue that having accrued more knowledge, and if there's a tenure system in place allowing older scientists to freely pursue new ideas without fear of being fired[3](https://nintil.com/age-and-science/#sidenote-3) , having obtained it could also be beneficial to finding and pursuing new ideas. So it's ultimately an empirical question!

One route is to look at particular episodes in the history of science and see how scientists of different ages reacted. Take Darwinism. [Hull et al. (1978)](https://science.sciencemag.org/content/202/4369/717.long) studied whether acceptance was faster among younger relative to older scientists. Recall that Darwin himself thought that it was the younger ones that were more open to natural selection. Ten years after the publication of Darwin's book, 50 of the 67 scientists in their sample had been converted; and by that time the average age of the accepters was 39.6 vs 48.1 for those who remained unconvinced, but these averages hide lots of variability and ultimately age explains less than 10% of the variation in acceptance rates. Moreover, considering those scientists that accepted the theory, age did not seem to matter for how early they came to accept it. Other studies looking at the adoption of cliometrics, or plate tectonics find similar results, either the effect is small or there is no effect at all ([Levin 1995](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/030631295025002003)). There is no effect either if one considers a new development that ultimately was not correct (polywater), young scientists were no more likely to buy into it ([Diamond 1988](http://www.artdiamond.com/DiamondPDFs/PolywaterEpisode.pdf)).

Chapter 26 of the Handbook of Genius, _Openness to scientific innovation_ has a larger sample size, spanning 28 scientific controversies from 1543 to the 1960s, covering almost four thousand scientists. And here there does seem to be an effect from age, as well as a metric of social attitudes (being liberal). What did not correlate with being an early adopter was eminence except in some cases; but when controlling for age, (rated based on awards and prizes won) eminence was negatively associated with being less likely to adopt Darwinism.

> A scientist who was younger than the mean age of the sample was 1.6 times more likely to support a new scientific innovation than were scientists who were above the mean;\[..\] Comparing supporters with opponents, the mean age difference was 14.6 years. Among the most committed supporters and opponents, however, the age disparity was almost twice as large.

This however is the average of a very heterogeneous sample:

> Statistical heterogeneity occurs when the effect sizes for a predictor such as age differ substantially from one historical event to another. Evidence for heterogeneity generally points the way to important moderator effects. As an example, the correlation between age and acceptance of new theories in science varies from −.50 during the reception of Freud’s psychoanalytic ideas (n = 218, p < .001), with younger people showing greater support for these theories, to +.09 during 19th-century vitalistic efforts to refute the doctrine of spontaneous generation – a conservative stance that generally appealed to older scientists (n = 43, p = .55).

This lends some support to Planck's principle; but it leaves questions unanswered. Perhaps it _used to_ be the case that age correlated with less openness to new ideas but it doesn't anymore; and in any case it's not a universal feature of every controversy. The paper doesn't look at whether the effect has changed over time.

The latest attempt at trying to assess this question, Azoulay et al. ([2019](https://mitsloan.mit.edu/shared/ods/documents/?PublicationDocumentID=5663)) who ask a related, but different question, they ask not if being old makes scientists less likely to convert to new theories, but whether when a "star scientist" dies you see changes in that field. What they find is that it's the case, and they interpret this as evidence that the eminence of such scientists prevents outsiders (from other fields) that may have different perspectives from contributing to that one field. What they exactly test is (in the life sciences and scientists born 1899 and later):

> how the premature death of 452 eminent scientists alter the vitality (measured by publication rates and funding flows) of subfields in which they actively published in the years immediately preceding their passing, compared to matched control subfields. \[...\]
> 
> To our surprise, it is not competitors from within a subfield that assume the mantle of leadership, but rather entrants from other fields that step in to fill the void created by a star’s absence. Importantly, **this surge in contributions from outsiders draws upon a different scientific corpus and is disproportionately likely to be highly cited**. Thus, consistent with the contention by Planck, **the loss of a luminary provides an opportunity for fields to evolve in novel directions that advance the scientific frontier**.

One _could_ take this to be a good exemplar of Planck's principle. After all Planck didn't say anything about youth, just that some recalcitrant individuals exist that are not convinced, they get replaced by newcomers. Those newcomers need not be young and the incumbents need not be old. In the paper itself they also try to see if the effeect is driven by age (p. 25) but this does not seem to be the case.

The paper itself acknowledges that these contributions do not so much overthrow existing paradigms but more like extend or complement them,

> It is important to note, however, that the findings above do not imply that the published results of entrants necessarily contradict or overturn the prevailing scientific understanding and assumptions within a subfield. We provide indirect evidence regarding these contributions’ disruptive impact by leveraging a measure recently proposed by Funk and Owen-Smith (2017). Their index captures the degree to which an idea consolidates or destabilizes the status quo, by measuring whether the future ideas that build on the focal idea also rely on its acknowledged predecessors. The results in Table E4 of Appendix E suggest that these contributions do not radically disrupt the subfield. Rather, they appear to reflect the impact of a myriad “small r,” permanent revolutions whereby new ideas come to the fore without necessarily eclipsing prior approaches. \[...\]
> 
> Taken together, these results suggest that outsiders are reluctant to challenge hegemonic leadership within a field when the star is alive. They also highlight a number of factors that may constrain entry even after she is gone. Intellectual, social, and resource barriers all seem to play a role in impeding entry, with outsiders only entering subfields whose topology offers a less hostile landscape for the support and acceptance of “foreign” ideas.

The magnitude of the effect estimated by Azoulay is nontrivial; fields where a "super star" dies see 8.55% more publications from scientists that are not collaborators of the super star (Table 3) than would be expected; contributions from collaborators go down 20%. Funding goes up and down by similar magnitudes respectively (All of this is _per year_). The number of high impact (>99th percentile) publications by non-collaborators goes up by 37% (Table 4) which is even more astonishing, even triggering my [effect is too large heuristic](https://jasoncollins.blog/2017/07/06/the-effect-is-too-large-heuristic/). Looking at the field in absolute terms and on a longer term view the difference is [staggering](https://www.nber.org/digest/mar16/does-science-advance-one-funeral-time)

![Image of the summary](https://nintil.com/images/2020-12-07-age-and-science/scientists.jpg)

Another interesting finding is in Figure E1 (p. 81) that shows that fields on average in a normal-curve way are born and decay, and the effect of this trends completely overshadows anything studies in the paper. Figure E4 shows that for these superstar scientists, that productivity increases up until the age of ~45 and then it slowly declines. This decline is more marked for the top 1% of publications; that is, on average scientists will increase their odds of publishing a highly cited paper somewhat until ~45 and then this rate of production of good work initiates a steady decline. This fits with the idea that in general age doesn't matter for producing highly cited papers, but it does for more demanding standards than that.

## Conclusion

Yes, scientists getting older is bad news. At the micro level, cognitive decline is real and at the macro level it does seem that Nobel Prize-worthy achievements are disproportionately done by younger scientists. However this is not as bad news as it may initially seem. It's not that _only_ young scientists can do it; or that it's almost all of those creative scientists are young. A naïve historically informed view of the subject is biased: Yes historically it has been a lot of very young (under 30) scientists doing that key research but times have changed. Young researchers have as a comparative advantage their higher fluid intelligence, and when there isn't that much to learn (Completely new fields open up, or we didn't know that much about the world as it was the case two centuries ago) then that negates the comparative advantage of older scientists, that they do know more and can use experience-based heuristics to guide their research (i.e. the heuristics about what's promising in a Newtonian paradigm may not work when applied, back then, to the nascent modern physics paradigm).

As we have accumulated more knowledge, the background knowledge required to make a contribution has increased and concurrently has the age new scientists start practicing at, as well as the age when they do their high impact research. The ideal researcher would be young and have a deep knowledge of their field but gaining that knowledge takes time. This profile is very rare; perhaps this is why the random impact hypothesis is true while at the same time Nobel Prizes are somewhat younger, knowledge and intelligence are inversely correlated within individuals and the outliers that escape from this are the ones who get Nobel prizes.

Research on Planck's principle shows that there is some truth to it, especially on the light of the research contained in Chapter 26 of the Handbook of Genius (with the caveat that they didn't look into historical trends). What is going on right now (The dynamics that the Azoulay paper is capturing) with older or eminent scientists is not so much resistance to adopt a new "paradigm" but failure to take into consideration new ideas or perhaps using new methods. So suppose that a key tenet of a field gets upended; I wouldn't expect older scientists to be particularly late in accepting the new view. Rather, where they may be slow is in noticing that they could achieve more just if they tried this new method they have never used before, or if they imported some knowledge from elsewhere.

To sum up,

1.  The aging of science is driven by a combination of factors including the aging of society in general, the aging of the baby boom cohort in particular, the end of mandatory retirement, and the increase in required knowledge to make a contribution to science. From a problem-solving point of view, this latter is the one that can be more directly addressed.
2.  Older scientists may not have as much energy as they did when they were younger, but in practice the actual experiments will be done by other lab members, so this one form of decline may not practice as much as it seems.
3.  Younger scientists are more likely to explore new ideas. This does not mean that such research is more promising or likely to be more impactful. Taking an idea to its ultimate consequences for 20 years may be more valuable than trying the new thing coming up.
4.  Realistically science happens in a social setting, not alone. Teams of older and younger scientists are the norm in science. It may be the case that if _everyone_ is markedly old (or young) we'd see issues with the productivity of a lab (on average), but we are far from this scenario.
5.  Productivity (count of papers published per year) increases up until ~40-50 and then declines with age
6.  In general, the likelihood of producing a highly cited paper stays constant through life (The random impact rule). There is a small chance that in some historical periods (The transition to modern physics) this is not true, and younger researchers are more likely to do this kind of highly valuable research instead.
7.  The transition into modern physics represents a unique event that opened up opportunities for younger scientists of that age. But from the fact that they did make a lot of key discoveries we should not infer that it is only them that could have done so, or that it is only young scientists that can make those discoveries in general.
8.  The "ideal age" where a scientist has the best change to achieve Nobel-worthiness is field dependent; great mathematicians and physicists are younger than great biologists and chemists.
9.  Historically older scientists were probably less likely to buy into a new paradigm in general, but this varies a lot by field, and it's unclear if it continues to hold. Inasmuch as all eminent scientists are older, eminence makes scientists less likely to adopt new methods or ideas from other fields. A study of recent "paradigm shifts" would be needed to see if this continues being true.

How may one leverage this knowledge to make science great again? Well, despite all the heterogeneity and individual differences involved in points 1-9, it seems clear that if I am handed a pile of papers from two researchers and I'm told their ages, the research is going to be more informative about who is doing interesting stuff relative to their age. It's very easy to point to older scientists that are doing the best work of their field.
