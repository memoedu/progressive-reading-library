# Nintil - Tools for knowledge

---

## 正文

如今，Nintil 的文章以相当传统的方式诞生。我把 Papers 存起来阅读，沿着 citations 找到更多材料，加入收藏。然后逐一阅读这些材料，把 sections 和 figures 进行 copy pasted，供日后 search 和使用。我不做 note-taking；进入新的 domains 时，我不知道什么是 relevant，因此可能会试图记下太多东西。相反，我依靠阅读量来弄清什么是 relevant，而同样的阅读量也带来了一个 built-in SRS，帮助我记住自己正在写的内容：如果我读了 10 篇关于同一事物的 reviews，最后又把它们读了两遍（最初一遍，写文章时再一遍），那些 concepts 就会被反复 revised。这并不是按照 optimal schedule 进行的，但它仍然是这个过程的 built-in 部分。

Nintil 的文章同样以传统方式被阅读，唯一的新东西或许是偶尔出现的 sidenote。这并不理想，我可以在一篇文章上投入一个月的工作（其中的大部分内容我或许都能记住），但如果你只读一遍，又不是实实在在地每天都在阅读这个 topic 的内容，你就记不住。

我们知道，有些 learning 方式比单纯阅读更好，而且不需要大量 human intervention（tutoring）：利用 testing effect（仅仅是被 tested，以及努力尝试 answer a question，就能帮助你记住）、spaced repetition（随着时间推移，以 set intervals 反复看到同一个 fact）。我在那篇关于 Bloom 的 [two sigma](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/) 的文章中谈过这一点。

所以你可能会问：“嘿，José，你为什么不在 Nintil 里放些 SRS 之类的东西？”原因是我没有多少 incentive 这么做，正如有些人指出的，我在一定程度上是为自己写作，而花时间思考该把什么放进 SRS system，就意味着没有把这些时间投入阅读和写作。当然，除非有简单易用的现成 tools，让我不必考虑这些，或许能让我 highlight sentences，将它们放进 system，并以相同的 style 把 prompts 穿插在 main text 中。不管怎样，既然 content 是我自己写的，而且我的 memory 足够好，那么如果我需要了解 X，我几乎总是知道到哪里去找 X；在一定程度上，knowledge 可以被 offloaded 到你写下的东西和 Google 上。

从作者的角度来看，还可以有更多 tooling。如果我想了解某件事，为什么一开始就得写长达 >14k words 的博客文章？为什么我非得读上数百篇 papers，再把拼图拼起来？Systematic reviews 还不够好，有些会漏掉另一些大篇幅讨论的内容，更糟的是，它们还会说出 contradictory 的东西。因此，我最终会看大量材料，试图写出这样一篇东西：如果我做得对，你应该不需要再读其他任何东西（就我想达到的 depth 而言），所有古怪的 nuances 和 quirks 都应该包含在内，每一处 conflicting evidence 都应该得到 explained，并被 made coherent。由于时间限制，我并不总能做到这一点（而且我还想写其他东西），但目标大致如此。不过，想象一下这样的东西已经存在：你输入“[Immunosenescence](https://nintil.com/immunosenescence)”，就会被带到一个讨论该 topic 的地方，那里先展示一个更简单的 version，而 user 可以 select 一个更 expanded 的 version。这篇文章不会充斥着“也许”和“或许”，而是采用更 solid 的 assertions，并为其精心斟酌措辞；比如，你不说“Protein A 可能 involved in process B”，而说“Protein A 是 most likely involved in process B”，并且“most likely”被 tabulated 为表示 70% confident。任何人都可以对其中的任何内容提出 dispute，并将其加入其中。这听起来像 wikipedia，但我不知道，如果有人试着这样做（让某些 pages 遵循这些特殊 rules），是否会被允许。

如果 scientific publications 附带 metadata，说明它们的 paper 对某些 claims 提供了多少 support，以及每项 assays 如何为 conclusion 增加 weight，那么这样的东西或许就能运作起来。接下来，人们会 dispute 某篇 paper 是否真的像看起来那样属于 strong evidence，但至少你可以让任何人都看明白这些 disputes。

这样的 system 还会有另一个 feature，就是按 concepts 进行 browsing。和前面一样，你可能遇到了 Protein A。但 Protein A 是从哪里来的？是否已经发现它 implicated 于这个 unrelated process？或许已经有 evidence 表明 protein A causes B，而且 B causes C，但实际上还没有人 checked A 是否会 lead to C，这就指向了一些可能有意思的 connections。

这会解决我目前的一个问题：我想让某些文章保持最新（比如 Longevity FAQ），但与此同时，我还有一些更 specialised 的文章（比如上面那篇 immunosenescence 文章），所以如果出现了新东西，我可能需要查清自己有没有提到过它；如果提到过，就检查是否需要 updating。然后对其他所有文章重复这个过程。

这种设想的理想 version 会是一个 science 的 centralized repository，但也可以做一个 personal version，这样我就能让 Nintil 的文章成为 underlying graph 的 linearized views。

## 原文

Nintil posts these days are born in a fairly traditional way. Papers are stashed and read, following citations to find more things to add to the stash. The stash is worked through, meaning being read and having sections and figures copy pasted for later search and use. No note-taking takes place; when getting into new domains I don't know what's relevant so I may try to keep track of too many things. Instead, I rely on volume to figure out what's relevant, and that same volume of reading leads to a built-in SRS that helps me remember what I'm writing about: if I'm reading 10 reviews about the same thing, and I end up reading them twice (once at first and a second time when I write the post) the concepts are going to be revised over and over. Not in an optimal schedule, but it's still built-in into the process.

Nintil posts are likewise consumed in a traditional way, the only novel addition may be the occasional sidenote. This is not ideal, I can put a month of work into the post (which I may mostly remember) but you will not if you just read it once and you are not reading about the topic literally every single day.

We know there are better ways of learning than just reading that don't require extensive human intervention (tutoring): leveraging the testing effect (merely being tested and going through the effort of trying to answer a question helps you remember), spaced repetition (seeing the same fact repeatedly over time at set intervals). I talked about this in my Bloom's [two sigma](https://nintil.com/bloom-sigma/) post.

So you may ask "Hey José why don´t you put some SRS in Nintil or something", the reason is that I have little incentive to do that, as some have noted I write to some extent for myself and time thinking about what to put in the SRS system is time not invested in doing the reading and writing. Unless of course there are easy readymade tools where I don't have to think about that, perhaps where I can highlight sentences and have those put in the system and have prompts interspersed with the main text, in the same style. In any case, given that I'm writing the content and that my memory is sufficiently good, if I need to know about X I know where to find X almost always; to some extent knowledge can be offloaded to your writings and Google.

From the writer's point of view there is more tooling that could exist. Why do I need to write >14k words long blogposts in the first place if I want to know about something? Why do I have to read hundreds of papers and piece together a puzzle?. Systematic reviews are not good enough, some miss what others talk about extensively, or worse they will say contradictory things. So I end up looking at a lot of things to try to produce something that if did it right, you shouldn't need to read anything else (for the level of depth I'm targeting), all the weird nuances and quirks should be there and every conflicting evidence being explained and made coherent. I don't always do that because of time constraints (And I want to write about other things) but that's more or less the goal. But imagine this existed already, that you type "[Immunosenescence](https://nintil.com/immunosenescence)" and you are led to a single place that talks about that topic, showing first a simpler version and the user could select a more expanded one. This article wouldn't deal in maybes and perhaps, but in more solid assertions with curated wording around them; so if instead of saying "Protein A might be involved in process B" you say "Protein A is most likely involved in process B" and "most likely" is tabulated to mean 70% confident. And anyone could dispute anything there and have it added. This sounds like wikipedia, but I ignore if someone tried to do this (Setting some pages to be under these special rules) it would be allowed.

Perhaps something like this could be made to work if scientific publications were accompanied with metadata that states how much support their paper lends to certain claims, how each of the assays lends weight to the conclusion. You would then have disputes about whether a given paper actually is as strong evidence as it seems, but at at least you could make those disputes legible to anyone.

Another feature of a system like this would be browsing by concepts. As before, you may have come across Protein A. But where does Protein A come from? Has it been found to be implicated in this unrelated process? Perhaps there is evidence that protein A causes B and that B causes C, but no one has actually checked if A would lead to C, pointing to potentially interesting connections.

This would solve a problem I currently have, which is that I want to keep certain posts up to date (Like the Longevity FAQ) but at the same time I have more specialised posts (Like the immunosenescence one above), so if something new comes I may need to find out if I have mentioned that, and if so, check if it needs updating. Then repeat for all the other posts.

The nice version of this would be a centralized repository of science, but there could be a personal version of this one could make, so I could make Nintil posts would be linearized views of the underlying graph.
