# Surviving an open-plan office

---

## 正文

看来和 San Francisco 的其他所有 startup 一样，Theorem 也在 open plan office 里工作。

这并非出于自愿。很久以前，[野蛮人大军](http://www.forbes.com/sites/kevinkruse/2012/08/25/facebook-unveils-new-campus-will-workers-be-sick-stressed-and-dissatisfied/)席卷了 Silicon Valley，高喊着“每人的 square footage 要更少！”和“要 increased collaboration！”，把沿途的每一堵 interior wall 都拆了。面对如此凶猛的攻势，Theorem 除了畏缩、屈服，再尽量不发出太大的声音之外，什么也做不了。[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/oo/#fn:1)

不幸的是，Theorem 和_其他 companies_ 共用一间 open plan office。这些 companies 的人不讲礼貌，大声互相讲笑话，还在自己的 desks 旁开 all-hands standup meetings[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/oo/#fn:2)；而如果我每次想让他们安静时都真说出口，他们大概就不会怎么喜欢我了。

身处 open plan office 的腹地，你会学到很多东西。你会知道，在有谈话声的 background 下 write computer programs 是多么不可能。你会知道，哪些同事关心 Warren Buffett 有多少钱。但最重要的是，你会学会生存之道。

以下就是我的心得。

### 音乐（Music）

我在这里提起这个，只是因为总有人向我推荐它，好像这样就能让我_更少_ distracted。我是个 musician。这招没用。我已经试过大概无数种不同的 genres 了。毫无成效。

通过 headphones 来 piping [brown noise](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_noise)，效果稍微好了一点，但 set up 起来实在很烦人。（结果发现，在 2015 年，要从 computer 上 play brown noise，最方便的办法居然是 run 一个 command-line program。）

另一方面，我的室友坚信，同时听两首不同的 songs 非常管用。我想，这就证明了，无论一个 noise-blocking 的主意听起来有多疯狂，总会有人用着有效。和这份清单里的其他办法一样，你的实际效果可能不同。

### 耳塞（Earplugs）

Earplugs 是我最先尝试的东西。不幸的是，它们只起了一部分作用；它们让我稍微不那么容易注意到周围有人开始谈话，但一旦我注意到了，它们就无法帮我忽略谈话声。由于往耳朵里塞东西有点不舒服，我决定还是不值得。

另外，人们会没注意到 earplugs，就试着跟我说话，然后因为我没有回应而不高兴。我考虑过在额头上贴一张巨大的“请勿打扰”告示，但又觉得那会比 earplugs 更不舒服。

### 折叠隔断（Folding room dividers）

有一件买来的东西出乎意料地管用，那就是[这个 Japanese-style folding screen](http://smile.amazon.com/Legacy-Decor-4-panel-Screen-Divider/dp/B004YZT70G/) 的 eight-panel version。（我是在它降价到大约 \$80 时买的。）我把它围在自己的 desk 周围，以 block out 坐在我对面的人发出的 visual conversational cues。

结果发现，对我来说，谈话的 visual cues 似乎几乎和 conversational noise 本身一样 distracting，所以这确实 resolved 了我许多 distraction 问题。但光靠 screen，我还是能听到人们的谈话，并因此 get distracted。我用来 solve 这个问题的是……

### 霰弹枪射击用耳罩（Shotgun earmuffs）

我最后真正用上的东西里，重要性排在其次的是[这副 earmuffs](http://smile.amazon.com/Protection-Professional-Folding-Padded-Comfortable-Satisfaction/dp/B00NKSMPZW/)，它是为 shotgun range designed 的，比 earplugs（它最接近的竞争对手）更 comfortable，也更 insulating。额外的好处是，它们既能达到相同效果，又比在额头上贴一张巨大的“请勿打扰”告示（稍微）没那么傻气。

### 居家办公（Work from home）

只要能这么做而不惹麻烦，这显然就是最好的选择：没有什么能像远离 10 miles 那样 attenuates noise。我平均大概每周 work from home 一次，而且通常会 productive 得多（另外还能多出 90 minutes 不用 commute 的时间，也是个不错的额外好处）。不幸的是，working remotely 会让“与 teammates 进行 collaborate”“participate in discussions”或“替别人 solve problems”之类的事情变得更难，这意味着，如果你这么做太频繁，有时会有人不开心。

### 错时作息（Shifted schedule）

我考虑过试着 shift 我的 schedule，好在 office 没那么热闹的时候多待一会儿——比如，把在那里的时间从 10-6 改成 7:30-3:30。坏处是，工作日夜晚的社交活动对早起的人并不友好，尤其是那些需要睡 10 hours 的人。好处则是，周围没其他人的时候，在 office 的 Wii U 上玩 Super Smash Brothers 要容易得多。

总之，我对 Smash 的在意程度（目前）还比不上我的社交生活，所以一直没抽出空来试这个办法。不过，如果那些不那么极端的措施都失败了，这似乎会是个不错的选择。

### 降噪耳机（Noise-canceling headphones）

我在一家 Bose 店里试过几副，但它们的效果和我想要的恰好相反——它们 attenuated 了一般的 background noise（那些不会让人 distracted 的 buzzes 或 roars），却把附近的谈话声原封不动地留下来了！等 Bose 改出“signal-canceling”的 headphones 时，请告诉我。

## 原文

Like apparently every other startup in San Francisco, Theorem works in an open plan office.

Not by choice. It was long ago that the [barbarian hordes](http://www.forbes.com/sites/kevinkruse/2012/08/25/facebook-unveils-new-campus-will-workers-be-sick-stressed-and-dissatisfied/) swept across Silicon Valley with battle cries of “less square footage per head!” and “increased collaboration!”, demolishing every interior wall in their path. Against such ferocity, Theorem could do nothing but cower and submit and try not to make too many loud noises.[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/oo/#fn:1)

Unfortunately, Theorem works in an open plan office with _other companies_. Companies who scorn politeness, who tell loud jokes to each other and hold all-hands standup meetings at their desks[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/oo/#fn:2) and who would probably not like me very much if I told them to be quiet as often as I want to.

In the depths of an open plan office, you learn many things. You learn how impossible it is to write computer programs with conversation in the background. You learn which of your coworkers care about how much money Warren Buffett has. But most importantly, you learn the tools of survival.

Here’s what I know.

### Music

I’m only mentioning this here because people keep suggesting it to me as though it would make me _less_ distracted. I’m a musician. It doesn’t. I’ve tried about a zillion different genres. No luck.

I had a little bit more success with piping [brown noise](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_noise) through my headphones, but it was actually really annoying to set up. (It turns out the most convenient way to play brown noise from a computer, in the year 2015, is to run a command-line program.)

On the other hand, my roommate swears by listening to two different songs simultaneously, which I guess is proof that no matter how crazy a noise-blocking idea might sound, there’s someone for whom it’ll work. As with the rest of this list, your mileage may vary.

### Earplugs

Earplugs were the first thing I tried. Unfortunately, they only partially worked; they made it a bit less likely that I would notice conversations starting up around me, but once I did notice, they didn’t help me tune it out. Since sticking things in my ear is kind of uncomfortable, I decided it wasn’t worth it.

Also, people would not notice the earplugs and try to talk to me and then get annoyed when I didn’t respond. I considered taping a giant “DO NOT DISTURB” sign to my forehead, but I decided that would be even less comfortable than the earplugs.

### Folding room dividers

A surprisingly helpful purchase was the eight-panel version of [this Japanese-style folding screen](http://smile.amazon.com/Legacy-Decor-4-panel-Screen-Divider/dp/B004YZT70G/). (I got one when it was marked down to about $80.) I wrapped it around my desk to block out the visual conversational cues from the people sitting across from me.

It turns out that visual cues of conversation seem to be nearly as distracting to me as the conversational noise itself, so this actually resolved a lot of my distraction problems. But with the screen alone, I could still hear people’s conversations and get distracted by them. I solve that problem with…

### Shotgun earmuffs

The next-most-important thing I actually ended up using is [this set of earmuffs](http://smile.amazon.com/Protection-Professional-Folding-Padded-Comfortable-Satisfaction/dp/B00NKSMPZW/) designed for a shotgun range, which are both more comfortable and more insulating than earplugs (their closest competitor). As an added bonus, they’re (slightly) less dorky than taping a giant “DO NOT DISTURB” sign to my forehead while achieving the same effect.

### Work from home

To the extent that I can get away with it, this is clearly the best option: nothing attenuates noise like being 10 miles away. I work from home maybe once a week on average and tend to be way more productive (and the extra 90 minutes of non-commute time is a nice bonus too). Unfortunately, working remotely makes it harder to do things like “collaborate with teammates” or “participate in discussions” or “solve other people’s problems for them,” which means that people sometimes get sad when you do it too often.

### Shifted schedule

I’ve considered trying to shift my schedule to spend more time in the office when it’s less active—being there, say, 7:30-3:30 instead of 10-6. The downside is that weeknight social events are not kind to early risers, especially those who need 10 hours of sleep. The upside is that it’s a lot easier to play Super Smash Brothers on the office Wii U when there’s no one else around.

Anyway, I don’t care about Smash as much as my social life (yet), so I haven’t gotten around to trying this. But it seems like a good option if my less extreme measures fail.

### Noise-canceling headphones

I tried some of these in a Bose store, but they had the opposite effect of what I wanted—they attenuated general background noise (non-distracting buzzes or roars) while leaving nearby conversations intact! Let me know when Bose comes out with “signal-canceling” headphones instead.
