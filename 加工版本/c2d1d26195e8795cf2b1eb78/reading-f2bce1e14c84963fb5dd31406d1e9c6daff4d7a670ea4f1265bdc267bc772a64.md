# Nintil - P-zombies are still undead

---

## 正文

有些人似乎难以 _conceiving_ philosophical zombies。在这篇文章中，我试图表明它们是 conceivable。（而我只为这一 claim 作 defend）我在这里并不 endorsing 任何特定的 philosophy of mind。

请注意，根据 [Philpapers survey](http://philpapers.org/surveys/results.pl?affil=Target+faculty&areas0=13&areas_max=1&grain=coarse)，P-zombie inconceivability 是 minority position。

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jyS4VFh3xOU

Robin Hanson 在[这里](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2016/06/once-more-with-feeling.html)说

> Carroll 启发我试着提出一个我认为值得提出的 point，即使它也会被忽视。我的目标是那些认为 philosophical zombies make sense 的人。据说，Zombies 和真实的人一样，有着相同的 physical brains，而这些 brains 经由相同的 causal history 形成。唯一的 difference 是，真实的人确实会“feel”，而 zombies 不会。但由于这种“feeling”state 被 presumed 为对 behavior 有 zero causal influence，zombies 的 act 与真实的人完全一样，包括满怀热情、条理清晰地 claiming 自己不是 zombies。那些认为自己能够 conceive 这样的 zombies 的人，看到了一个“hard question”：那些 claim 自己会 feel、而且在其他方面也 act 得仿佛会 feel 的 physical systems 中，哪些实际上确实会 feel。（以及还有哪些其他 systems 也会 feel。）

Eliezer Yudkowsky[也](http://lesswrong.com/r/discussion/lw/nqv/zombies_redacted/)认为 zombies 是 problematic 的

> 当你望着美丽的落日，想到你内在那份安静地观看落日的 inner awareness 时，Zombies 看起来是 conceivable 的；那份 awareness 似乎可以 vanish，而不改变你走路或微笑的方式；这顺从了那个听起来颇为 plausible 的 generalization：“inner listener 没有 outer effects”。当你大声说出下面这段话时，那个 generalization 就应该\_不再\_显得 possible：“等等，我此刻正在自己的 auditory cortex 中 thinking 这个 thought，而那个 thought 能让我的嘴唇动起来，把我对自己安静的 inner listener 的 awareness translating 成嘴唇的 motion，这意味着 consciousness 是这个 universe 中 minimal closure of causality 的一部分。”关于 conceivability argument，我想不出还有什么可说的。那些 zombies 已经死了。

我将在这里试着帮助任何人 conceive P-zombies。

**(I)**

我们先从 trivial zombie case 开始。在这个 hypothetical world 中，有一些 beings，它们像 humans，却不是 conscious 的。在这个 world 中，没有与 consciousness 有关的 vocabulary——没有关于 pain、unconsciousness、redness、qualia 等的谈论——。

conceiving 这个 world 有什么问题吗？

当你初到那里时，你会看到你预期会看到的东西：城市、道路、人们做着人们会做的事，等等。起初你会认为他们是 conscious 的，但仔细观察后，你就会意识到，他们从不谈论任何 mental 的东西！

我想你能够 conceive 这一点。但仅凭这一点，你还没有加入 Zombie-ism 的行列：

> 如果 zombies 要成为 physicalism 的 counterexamples，那么它们仅仅在 behaviorally 和 functionally 上像 normal human beings 是不够的：许多 physicalists accept，我们自己的仅仅 behavioral 或 functional duplicates 可能 lack qualia。\*\*Zombies 必须在 _all_ physical respects 上像 normal human beings。\*\*

这些 lite-zombies 不会 engage in 某些 regular humans 确实会 engage in 的 activies，例如 discussing consciousness。

**(II)**

现在，让我们转向 almost-full zombie world，在那里，zombies 确实会谈论我们所谈论的一切。

human 与 P-zombie 之间的一段 hypothetical dialogue 可以是这样的：

H: 那片 leaf 是什么 colour？

Z: Green，为什么问这个？

H: 但你这么说是什么意思？

Z: 我的意思是，那片 leaf 有一种 colour，而那种 colour 是 green。说得 Most precisely 一些，当我 see 那片 leaf 时，某些 wavelengths 通过我的 eyeballs 进入，并在我 brain 的某个 region 中 induce 某种 activity，causing 我 utter 刚才 uttered 的那些词。'Seeing green' 是那个 process 的 shorthand，_即[谈论某些 physical systems 的 behavior 的一种特定方式](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2016/06/once-more-with-feeling.html)，_例如 brain。

这个 almost-full zombie world 还不是那个 zombie world。Daniel Dennett 在[这里](https://ase.tufts.edu/cogstud/dennett/papers/unzombie.htm) argues，P-zombies 必须在每一种 possible circumstance 中都像 humans 一样 act。不过，也可以说，有些 humans 可能不相信 consciousness（proclaiming 它是一种 illusion，是 cognitive algorithm 的 product），因而最终会那样说话。那些 humans 及其 zombie versions 会 produce 相同的 behaviour，因此，至少对于一个 humans 的 set 而言，zombie-ism 是 true 的。也就是说，存在这个 world 的一个 subset，使得为这个 world 的那个 subset create 一个 Zombie World 是 possible 的。

那么，我们大多数并非 insane、而且 recognise consciousness 的 obviousness 的人又如何呢？

我的 zombie version 必须写出与我相同的文章，并且以与我相同的方式谈论 consciousness。在这样做的时候，这个 zombie 不会 confused/understand 任何东西，不会有 beliefs/thoughts/preferences，他所说的任何东西也都不会是 justified 或 unjustified 的，等等。这些全都是 mental categories。如果这个 zombie 说 '我正处于 pain 中'，那么那个 event 就与由 computer compiled 和 executed 的一个简单的 print("I am in pain") 属于相同的 category。也就是说，它在 trivial functionalist sense 上是 true 的（它所做的，是那些我们通常认为会 feel pain 的 systems 所做的），但在 strict sense 上是 false 的（没有 associated qualia of pain，也没有 preference against pain）。

**(III)**

让我们直面 anti-zombie arguments，并表明它们为何 fail。首先是 SEP 中的两个（我不讨论 verificationism 或 functionalism，因为它们基本上都在对正在讨论的 question 进行 beg the question）

> _Zombies’ utterances_。假设我 smell 到正在烘焙的咖啡豆，并说：‘嗯！我喜欢那个 smell！’每个人都会理所当然地 assume，我是在谈论自己的 experience。但现在，假设我的 zombie twin produces 相同的 utterance。他似乎也在谈论一种 experience，但实际上并非如此，因为他只是一个 zombie。他是 mistaken 的吗？他在 lying 吗？他的 utterance 能以某种方式被 interpreted 为 true，还是完全没有 truth value？Nigel Thomas (1996) argues，‘zombiphiles 对这些 questions 采取的任何立场，都会让他们陷入严重的麻烦’。

他是 mistaken 的吗？不，他不是。他只是在做他本来就该做的事。

他在 lying 吗？不。通常意义上的 Lying requires intention to deceive（或其他某种相关的 mental property）。

这个 utterance 是 true 的吗？正如我上面所讨论的，在 strict sense 上，不是。在 loose、metaphorical、poetic 的 sense 上，是。我们可以用自己的 mental categories 来谈论 zombies，因为这样做是有用的，就像我们用这些 categories 来 understand 其他人一样。

> 可以说，phenomenal consciousness 无论是 actual 还是 possible，都涉及能够 refer to 并 know about 自己的 qualia，这一点是 _a priori_ true 的。如果这点是对的，那么任何 zombie-friendly account 都面临一个问题。根据得到广泛 accepted 的 causal theory of reference，reference 和 knowledge require 我们受到所 known 或 referred to 的东西的 causally affected (Kripke 1972/80)；而且，假如这一点是 true 的，那么 suppose 它同样是 _a priori_ true 的似乎也很合理。在此基础上，在那些其 conceivability 似乎 follow from zombies 的 conceivability 的 epiphenomenalistic worlds 中——即 qualia 是 inert 的 worlds——**我们的 counterparts 无法 know about 或 refer to 他们的 qualia。这 contradicts 了 phenomenal consciousness requires reference to qualia 这一 assumption**，由此 follows，这样的 epiphenomenalistic worlds 终究并不是 possible 的。因此，zombies 在相关意义上也不是 conceivable 的，因为它们的 conceivability 会 _a priori_ leads 到一个 contradiction。总而言之：如果 zombies 是 conceivable 的，那么 epiphenomenalistic worlds 也是。但根据 causal theory of reference，epiphenomenalistic worlds 不是 conceivable 的；所以 zombies 也不是 conceivable 的。

确实如此！Zombies 并不像我们那样 know 任何东西。但这个 argument 是好的，因为它与 Yudkowsky 提出的那个有关，我们稍后会讨论它。

[Hanson 的 argument](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2016/06/once-more-with-feeling.html) relies on 对 'interaction' 的一种不太好的 understanding

> 这些 claims 全都 follow from 我们非常 standard 且 well-established 的 info theory。我们通过与事物 interacting 来 get 关于它们的 info，使我们的 states become correlated with 那些事物的 states。但按照 assumption，这个 hypothesized 的额外“feeling”state 从不与任何东西 interacts。你为何觉得非得非常自信地 assert 自己确实会 feel，其实际 reason 与你实际上是否确实会 feel 之间 _没有 causal connection_。即使它不是 true 的，你也同样有可能这么说。对于那些一个人永远无法 get 任何 info 的 states，hypothesizing 并 forming beliefs 究竟能有什么意义？

Epiphenomenalists 可以 hold，那个额外的 "feeling" 与 physical world 之间存在 interaction。Causality 会从 brain 通向 consciousness，而不会反过来。这样，关于 world 的 information 被 reflected 为 conscious experience，但 conscious experiences 本身不会 causing 任何东西。brain 会做它自己的事，generating consciousness，并使你 believe，你之所以做自己所做的事，是因为这个或那个 mental property。所以，是的，你本来可以（在存在一个 zombie 版的你的 universe 中）说出完全相同的话，而没有与之 associated 的 conscious experience。正如 Max Tegmark 所说，consciousness 可以是 'information 正在被 processed 时的 feels like'。([Tegmark, 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.1219))

其次，一个人可以 get 许多关于 conscious experience 的 info：某种东西 feels like 什么，是关于某种东西的 knowledge。Hanson [rejects 这一点](https://twitter.com/robinhanson/status/748699590187155456)，说他指的是 information 的 technical definition——在 information theory 中的定义——。但 information theory 无法（至少截至目前）capture mental events 这一事实，并不是说 knowing 某种东西 feels like 什么就是在通常使用的 information 意义上 having information 的理由。

Eliezer Yudkowsky 的 [argument](http://lesswrong.com/r/discussion/lw/nqv/zombies_redacted/) 更好，但它最终也 fails。

他说

> 如果某种东西没有 causal effect，你就无法 know about 它。territory 必须与 map causally entangled，map 才能与 territory correlate。'see' 某种东西，就是受到它的 affected。如果一个据称是 physical 的 thing 或 property 对我们 universe 的其余部分绝对没有 causal impact，那么[我们是否甚至能够谈论它，都是一个严肃的问题](http://lesswrong.com/lw/f1u/causal_reference/)，更不用说 justifiably knowing 它在那里了。

这就是我之前已经回答过的 point：在 epiphenomenalism 中，causality 沿一个 direction 运行。

> 一个 [standard point](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/) 是——zombie-ist philosophers 也 accept 这一点！——Zombie World 的 philosophers 与我们自己的 philosophers 是 atom-by-atom identical 的，因此会写出关于 philosophy of consciousness 的 identical papers。到了这里，Zombie World 就不再是 inner listener 这一 idea 的 intuitive consequence 了。Philosophers 撰写关于 consciousness 的 papers，至少 _似乎_ 是 consciousness 对 world 产生的一个 effect。你可以提出巧妙的 reasons 来 argue 为何并非如此，但你必须足够巧妙。你已经不再是直接诉诸 intuition 了。

看起来如此。这也是我在上面提出 limited Zombie World scenario 的部分原因：因为一般而言，试图 conceive P-zombies 的人所遇到的困难，似乎来自 P-zombies 做着那些看起来在没有 consciousness 时完全 impossible 做到的事。

> Zombie-ists 是 _property dualists_——他们不相信独立的 soul；他们相信，我们的 universe 中的 matter 具有超出 physical 的额外 properties。“超出 physical”？这是什么意思？意思是那些额外 properties 存在，但它们不像 electrical charge 或 mass 的 properties 那样 influence atoms 的 motion。这些额外 properties 无法被 third parties experimentally detectable；_你_从自身额外 properties 的 \_inside\_知道自己是 conscious，但任何 scientist 都不可能从外部直接 detect 这一点。

Yudkowsky asserts，Zombie-ists 是 property dualists，但按我的理解，能够 conceive Zombies 并不 imply 某种特定的 philosophy of mind。例如，我可以认为，我们的 universe 由 Alien Gods created 是 possible 的——但实际情况并非如此。这不会让我成为 Alien God 的信徒。Yudkowsky 说，epiphenomenalism（以及其他 Chalmersian views）是 implausible 的，而且存在更 plausible 的 views——这话没问题。但这对 criticise zombies 没什么帮助。

Yudkowsky 为 consciousness proposes 的一种 plausible theory 是

> [Not-quite-faith-based](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2008/01/gray-fallacy.html) 的 reductionism：

-   > [That-which-we-name](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2008/04/reductive-refer.html) 的“consciousness”发生在 physics _within_，其方式尚未被理解，正如人类过去三千次遇到神秘事物时发生的情况一样。

-   > 你的 intuition 认为，任何 material substance 都不可能 [add up](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2008/03/wrong-questions.html) 成 consciousness，这个 intuition 是 incorrect 的。如果你\_确实\_ _exactly_ 知道自己为什么谈论 consciousness，这会给你带来新的 insights，其形式是你现在无法预料的；之后你就会意识到，你关于 normal physics 没有容纳 consciousness 的余地的 arguments 是 [flawed](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2008/03/dissolving-the.html) 的。

将那个 theory Assuming 为 true，并不能消灭 Zombies。

他在[别处](http://lesswrong.com/lw/p9/the_generalized_antizombie_principle/)说

> Consciousness，无论它是什么——一种 substance、一个 process，还是某种 confusion 的名称——都不是 epiphenomenal；你的 mind 能够当场察觉 inner listener 正在 listening，并大声说出来。\_我已经打出这一段文字的事实 \_至少会 _seem_ 能够 refute 那种认为 consciousness 没有 experimentally detectable consequences 的 idea。

不尽如此。consciousness causes 某些东西，这是 plausible 的，我也赞同这一 view。但我们 act，而 consciousness 什么也不做，同样是 plausible 的——是 possible 的。Consciousness 只是对 system 刚刚所做之事的一种 ex-post feeling。

你，你的 brain 可能已经做了 X（可以由 physics perfectly explainable），然后 conscious I 才被告知这一 decision，甚至可能还附带一种已经 chosen 的 feeling。要我说，这种 view 确实得到了一些 neuroscientific research 的 support，因此我们不能贸然 reject 它。而如果我们不能这样做，Eliezer 兴致盎然地写出段落，也不是对 Zombie-ism 的 rebuttal。

\*\*(IV) \*\*

如果以上内容未能在你的 _mind_ 中呈现一幅清晰的 P-zombie 图景，那就最后再施展一次 nigromancy。

请 Assume 这个 disjunction 是 true 的：Either consciousness 是 universe 的某种 fundamental component（Panpsychism），or 这个 universe 中的 laws of physics entail，consciousness 是某些 systems 具有的一种 property。

这样，我们就把 epiphenomenalism 和令 Yudkowsky 困扰的怪异之处排除在讨论之外。

现在，想象一个普通人（不是 Zombie）被要求说出一个介于 1 和 10 之间的 number。那里发生的是，soundwaves 在 air 中 travel，在 ear 的 tympanic membrane 中 induce 某些 movement，继而如此。vibrations 最终到达 cochlea，后者将把它们作为 electrical impulses transmit 到 brain，它们在那里将与其他 impulses interact。过一会儿，一些 impulses 将向下传递，move vocal folds 和 diaphragm，从而 produce speech。

伴随这一过程的，或许是“我该 choose 哪个 number？”这样的 inner monologue，以及一种自己确实在 freely choosing 某个东西的 feeling。

在上述 process 中，想象自己能够进入 brain，在一切发生时 trace 它们。或许你甚至掌握了 Equations of Consciousness，它们 explain，这里和那里的一群 interconnected neurons 如何 gather 一些 [proto-conscious](http://consc.net/papers/panpsychism.pdf) events，并 produce consciousness 或其他什么。比如说，如果这些 proto-conscious events 被 integrated 到一个更大的 whole 中，它们就会 create 具有 varying complexity 的 subjective experience。

现在，既然你已经想象了 hard problem of consciousness 如何被 solved，就想象一个与我们的 universe 不同的 universe。这个 universe 只有 two dimensions，而且 universe 整体的 entropy _stays constant_。此外，这里没有 Equations of Consciousness，也没有 protoconscious events。这里有四处行走的 2D beings，而且它们显然不是 conscious。想象 insects 和 simple reptiles 之类的东西。这些在某种意义上是 zombies——但不是我们想要的 zombies。

你刚刚想象了一个由 zombies 构成的 universe。现在迅速从 2 改为 3 dimensions，并从 deltaS=0 改为 deltaS>0。

在你的 mind 中回想刚才看到的东西：所有在 brain 中四处传递、以 produce 一个 response 的 electrical impulses。其中没有 consciousness involved。

**(V)**

P-zombies 有什么意义？在我看来，至少可以 illustrate 我们目前对 consciousness 缺乏 understanding，并 substanti ate 那五个不能被同时 accepted、共同 compose [Mind-Body problem](http://www.owl232.net/mind.htm) 的 premises 之一

1.  一个 system 的 properties 是其 components 的 properties、activities 和 arrangements 的 necessary consequence
2.  People 由 atoms、quarks 等构成。
3.  Atoms 是 purely physical 的东西：它们的 properties、activities 和 relations 全都是 physical 的。
4.  People 具有 mental states
5.  任何 ascribing 一个 mental predicate 的 statement 都不能从任何一组 purely physical descriptions 中被 derived / 某个给定 mental state 的 existence 并非任何 purely physical state 的 existence 的（metaphysically）necessary consequence

Zombie argument（或 Mary's room，或 inverted qualias\* 的 case）给了我们认为 5 是 true 的理由。无论 consciousness 在我们的 universe 中如何 works 的真相是什么，它仍然可能以任何其他方式存在。或许一个具有 consciousness 的 universe 会不同于一个没有它的 universe。

但仅仅 accepting consciousness 不是一种 passive inert 的东西——从而与 Yudkowsky 达成一致——还不够。我原本仍然可能是一种 passive inert 的东西。

我不认为这 refutes physicalism。我们仍可能发现 consciousness particles 或 consciousness equations。但它确实 suggest，physicalism 可能是 wrong 的。

\*或许这个例子甚至比 zombies 更好：为了 motivate physical 与 mental 之间不存在 necessary connection，想想你看作 red 的东西，你原本可能看作 yellow，反过来也一样。我们可能生活在同一个 universe，以同样的方式交谈，却仍然具有不同的 qualias。一个仅 examining physical properties 的 external observer，将无法猜出这个 universe 中的 qualias 是怎样的。这难道不是在针对 physicalism beg the question 吗？归根结底，这是一种 philosophical argument，使用我们目前拥有的 knowledge 构建，用来看看最 plausible、最值得相信的是什么：我们究竟会不会找到关于 consciousness 的 physical laws 和 explanations？此前在这场游戏中的尝试（那个臭名昭著的 elan vital）都得到了成功的 explained。但这一次似乎不同。如今我们正在深入 probing brain，正在摸索着走向对 Physics 的 understanding；尽管 physics 中还有少数 unsolved problems，我们却连将 Physics 那座庞大体系与 conscious experience link 起来的方法都没有。[string theory 或 loop quantum gravity](https://www.quantamagazine.org/20150803-physics-theories-map/) 中没有任何东西能够 explain consciousness。这一事实，加上 imagining zombies 的轻而易举，再加上 physical 与 subjective 之间看似存在的 conceptual gap，构成了反对 physicalism 为 true 的有力 arguments。

有些人承认这一点，却[走了另一条路：deny consciousness](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/materialism-eliminative/)。说它是一种 illusion。因为它不符合、或预期不会符合 scientific understanding。我认为，这本质上是对 physicalism 的一种 reductio ad absurdum。如果有什么是 evident 的，那就是我们是 conscious。正如 Descartes 那句名言所说，不可能有任何事情比这更 certain。无论一个人得出什么 conclusion，如果它与这个事实 conflicts，就意味着这个人应当 reject argument 中的其他某个 premises。

我们仍然可能以某种方式同时拥有 physicalism 和 consciousness。这就是 David Pearce 的 N[on-materialist physicalism proposal](https://www.physicalism.com/)。但如果我们最终 prove，physicalism 不允许我们将 consciousness 作为 real phenomenon 来 explain——从而迫使我们成为 eliminativists，否则就会陷入 logical inconsistency——那将算作对 physicalism 的 refutation，而不是对 consciousness 的 refutation。

我不认为 Zombie argument entails 这一点，因此，如果一个人 accepts conceiving zombies 是 possible 的，也不必因此被迫相信多少其他东西。它确实 show，存在一个 possible universe，其中 physics 无法 explain consciousness。我们生活在这样的 universe 中吗？几十年后我们或许就会知道。我希望不是，否则我们就得永远与这个谜团相伴！

编辑补记 (31/07/16) 看来 Yudkowsky 对 zombies 的 concept 与我的不同。他的 concept 基于对 Chalmer 的 The Conscious Mind 的一种解读，而这种解读是 plausible 的。但最近 Chalmers 说过，zombies 不 imply epiphenomenalism，因此，尽管在他的 concept 下它们可能是 implausible 的，在我的 concept 下却并非如此。参见[这个视频](https://t.co/fDtivsmWka)的 17:03 至 20:25。

附加内容：你知道吗，或许任何特定 task 都不 required consciousness（就我们已经 tested 的范围而言），但它会让某些 activities 更容易或更 efficient？（S[hea and Frith 2016](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005)）

> 有大量 evidence 表明，consciousness，或与 consciousness 密切相关的一种 attentional phenomenon，对于许多形式的 learning、memory 和 voluntary control of behaviour 都很重要。然而，关于 consciousness 的 scientific research 中的一种主要 strategy，是寻找更强的东西：那些_只有_使用 conscious representations 才能 performed 的 tasks。例如，已有各种主张认为，下列活动需要 consciousness：integrate 或 bind perceptual features（[Dehaene and Naccache 2001](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-11)；[Baars 2002](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-2)；[Tononi 2004](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-93)）；在没有 stimulation 时使一个 representation 保持 online（[Greenwald et al. 1996](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-36)；[Dehaene and Naccache 2001](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-11)）；或者将 motivational states 与 causal learning 进行 integrate（[Dickinson and Balleine 2009](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-13)）。

## 原文

Some people seem to have problems _conceiving_ philosophical zombies. In this post, I try to show that they are conceivable. (And I only defend that claim) I am not endorsing any particular philosophy of mind here.

Note that according to the [Philpapers survey](http://philpapers.org/surveys/results.pl?affil=Target+faculty&areas0=13&areas_max=1&grain=coarse), P-zombie inconceivability is the minority position.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jyS4VFh3xOU

Robin Hanson, [here](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2016/06/once-more-with-feeling.html) says

> Carroll inspires me to try to make one point I think worth making, even if it is also ignored. My target is people who think philosophical zombies make sense. Zombies are supposedly just like real people in having the same physical brains, which arose the through the same causal history. The only difference is that while real people really “feel”, zombies do not. But since this state of “feeling” is presumed to have zero causal influence on behavior, zombies act exactly like real people, including being passionate and articulate about claiming they are not zombies. People who think they can conceive of such zombies see a “hard question” regarding which physical systems that claim to feel and otherwise act as if they feel actually do feel. (And which other systems feel as well.)

See also this tweet

https://twitter.com/robinhanson/status/749031454127099904

Eliezer Yudkowsky[also](http://lesswrong.com/r/discussion/lw/nqv/zombies_redacted/)sees zombies as problematic

> Zombies looked conceivable when you looked out at a beautiful sunset and thought about the quiet inner awareness inside you watching that sunset, which seemed like it could vanish without changing the way you walked or smiled; obedient to the plausible-sounding generalization, "the inner listener has no outer effects".  That generalization should \_stop \_seeming possible when you say out loud, "But wait, I am thinking this thought right now inside my auditory cortex, and that thought can make my lips move, translating my awareness of my quiet inner listener into a motion of my lips, meaning that consciousness is part of the minimal closure of causality in this universe."  I can't think of anything else to say about the conceivability argument.  The zombies are dead.

I'll try here to help anyone conceive P-zombies.

**(I)**

Let's start by the trivial zombie case. In this hypothetical world, you have beings that are like humans but are not conscious. In this world, there is no vocabulary relating to consciousness - no talk of pain, unconsciousness, redness, qualia, etc-.

Is there any problem conceiving this world?

When you first arrive there, you see what you would expect to see: cities, roads, people doing what people do, etc. You would initially think that they are conscious, but on a closer look, you would realise that they never talk about anything mental!

I guess that you can conceive this. But with this, you haven't yet joined the ranks of Zombie-ism:

> If zombies are to be counterexamples to physicalism, it is not enough for them to be behaviorally and functionally like normal human beings: plenty of physicalists accept that merely behavioral or functional duplicates of ourselves might lack qualia. \*\*Zombies must be like normal human beings in _all_ physical respects. \*\*

These lite-zombies do not engage in certain activies that regular humans do engage in, like discussing consciousness.

**(II)**

Now, let us move on to the almost-full zombie world, where zombies do talk about everything we talk about.

A hypothetical dialogue between a human and a P-zombie could go like this:

H: What colour is that leaf?

Z: Green, why?

H: But what do you mean by that?

Z: I mean that the leaf has a colour, and that colour is green. Most precisely, when I see the leaf, certain wavelengths enter through my eyeballs and induce certain activity in a certain region of my brain, causing me to utter the words just uttered. 'Seeing green' is shorthand for that process, _[a particular way of talking about the behavior of certain physical systems](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2016/06/once-more-with-feeling.html),_ like the brain.

This almost-full zombie world is not yet the zombie world. Daniel Dennett argues [here](https://ase.tufts.edu/cogstud/dennett/papers/unzombie.htm) that P-zombies have to act in every possible circumstance like humans. It could be said, however, that there are humans who could disbelieve in consciousness (proclaiming it to be an illusion, a product of a cognitive algorithm), and thus end up talking like that. Those humans and their zombie versions, would produce the same behaviour, hence at least for a set of humans, zombie-ism is true. That is, there exist a subset of the world such that it is possible to create a Zombie World for that subset of the world.

Now, what about most of us who are not insane and recognise the obviousness of consciousness?

My zombie version would have to write the same posts I do, and talk about consciousness in the same way as I do. In doing so, the zombie would not be confused/understand anything, have beliefs/thoughts/preferences, be justified or unjustified in anything he says, etc. Those are all mental categories. If the zombie says 'I am in pain' that event is in the same category as a simple print("I am in pain") compiled and executed by a computer. That is, is is true in a trivial functionalist sense (It does what systems that we usually think feel pain do), but false in a strict sense (There is no associated qualia of pain, or preference against pain).

**(III)**

Let us confront anti-zombie arguments and show why they fail. First, two  in the SEP (I do not address verificationism or functionalism because they basically beg the question being discussed)

> _Zombies’ utterances_. Suppose I smell roasting coffee beans and say, ‘Mm! I love that smell!’. Everyone would rightly assume I was talking about my experience. But now suppose my zombie twin produces the same utterance. He too seems to be talking about an experience, but in fact he isn't because he's just a zombie. Is he mistaken? Is he lying? Could his utterance somehow be interpreted as true, or is it totally without truth value? Nigel Thomas (1996) argues that ‘any line that zombiphiles take on these questions will get them into serious trouble’.

Is he mistaken? No, he is not. He just does what he is supposed to do.

Is he lying? No. Lying in the usual sense requires the intention to deceive (or some other related mental property).

Is the utterance true? As I discuss above, in a strict sense, no. In a loose, metaphorical, poetic, sense, yes. We could talk about zombies using our mental categories because it is useful to do so in the same way we use them to understand other people.

> Arguably it is _a priori_ true that phenomenal consciousness, whether actual or possible, involves being able to refer to and know about one's qualia. If that is right, any zombie-friendly account faces a problem. According to the causal theory of reference — which is widely accepted — reference and knowledge require us to be causally affected by what is known or referred to (Kripke 1972/80); and it seems reasonable to suppose that this too is true _a priori_ if at all. On that basis, in those epiphenomenalistic worlds whose conceivability seems to follow from the conceivability of zombies — worlds where qualia are inert — **our counterparts cannot know about or refer to their qualia. That contradicts the assumption that phenomenal consciousness requires reference to qualia**, from which it follows that such epiphenomenalistic worlds are not possible after all. Therefore zombies are not conceivable in the relevant sense either, since their conceivability leads _a priori_ to a contradiction. To summarize: if zombies are conceivable, so are epiphenomenalistic worlds. But by the causal theory of reference, epiphenomenalistic worlds are not conceivable; so zombies are not conceivable either.

Indeed! Zombies do not know anything in the sense we do. But this argument is good, as is related to the one Yudkowsky makes, which we will discuss later.

[Hanson's argument](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2016/06/once-more-with-feeling.html) relies on a not particularly good understanding of 'interaction'

> These claims all follow from our very standard and well-established info theory. We get info about things by interacting with them, so that our states become correlated with the states of those things. But by assumption this hypothesized extra “feeling” state never interacts with anything. The actual reason why you feel compelled to assert very confidently that you really do feel has _no causal connection_ with whether you actually do really feel. You would have been just as likely to say it if it were not true. What could possibly be the point of hypothesizing and forming beliefs about states about which one can never get any info?

Epiphenomenalists could hold that there is interaction between that extra "feeling" and the physical world. Causality would run from the brain to consciousness, and not the other way around. This way, the information about the world is reflected as a conscious experience, but conscious experiences themselves would not be causing anything. The brain would be doing its thing, generating consciousness and making you believe you are doing what you do because this or that mental property. So yes, you could have (in a universe where there is a zombie you) said the exact same thing without the conscious experience associated with it. As Max Tegmark puts it, consciousness could be 'what information being processed feels like'. ([Tegmark, 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.1219))

Secondly, one can get lots of info about conscious experience: what something feels like is knowledge about something. Hanson [rejects this](https://twitter.com/robinhanson/status/748699590187155456), saying that he meant the technical definition of information -within information theory-. But the fact that information theory cannot (as of today, at least) capture mental events is no reason to say that knowing what something feels like is having information in the commonly used sense of information.

Eliezer Yudkowsky's [argument](http://lesswrong.com/r/discussion/lw/nqv/zombies_redacted/) is better, but it also ultimately fails.

He says that

> If something has no causal effect, you can't know about it.  The territory must be causally entangled with the map for the map to correlate with the territory.  To 'see' something is to be affected by it.  If an allegedly physical thing or property has absolutely no causal impact on the rest of our universe, there's [a serious question about whether we can even talk about it](http://lesswrong.com/lw/f1u/causal_reference/), never mind justifiably knowing that it's there.

Which is the point that I have answered before: With epiphenomenalism, causality runs in one direction.

> It is a [standard point](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/)—which zombie-ist philosophers accept!—that the Zombie World's philosophers, being atom-by-atom identical to our own philosophers, write identical papers about the philosophy of consciousness. At this point, the Zombie World stops being an intuitive consequence of the idea of an inner listener. Philosophers writing papers about consciousness would _seem_ to be at least one effect of consciousness upon the world.  You can argue clever reasons why this is not so, but you have to be clever.  You are no longer playing straight to the intuition.

Seemingly. This is in part the reason why I presented the limited Zombie World scenario above: because in general what seems to cause trouble to people trying to conceive P-zombies are P-zombies doing things that seem totally impossible to do without consciousness.

> Zombie-ists are _property dualists_—they don't believe in a separate soul; they believe that matter in our universe has additional properties beyond the physical. "Beyond the physical"?  What does that mean?  It means the extra properties are there, but they don't influence the motion of the atoms, like the properties of electrical charge or mass.  The extra properties are not experimentally detectable by third parties; _you_ know you are conscious, from the \_inside\_of your extra properties, but no scientist can ever directly detect this from outside.

Yudkowsky asserts that Zombie-ists are property dualists, but as I read it, being able to conceive Zombies does not imply a particular philosophy of mind. I can, for example, hold that it is possible - but not the case - that our universe was created by Alien Gods. That doesn't make me an Alien God believer. Yudkowsky says that epiphenomenalism (and other Chalmersian views) is implausible and there are more plausible views -fair enough-. But that does little to criticise zombies.

One plausible theory that Yudkowsky proposes for consciousness is

> [Not-quite-faith-based](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2008/01/gray-fallacy.html) reductionism:

-   > [That-which-we-name](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2008/04/reductive-refer.html) "consciousness" happens _within_ physics, in a way not yet understood, just like what happened the last three thousand times humanity ran into something mysterious.

-   > Your intuition that no material substance can possibly [add up](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2008/03/wrong-questions.html) to consciousness is incorrect.  If you\_actually\_knew _exactly_ why you talk about consciousness, this would give you new insights, of a form you can't now anticipate; and afterward you would realize that your arguments about normal physics having no room for consciousness were [flawed](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2008/03/dissolving-the.html).

Assuming that theory as true doesn't kill Zombies.

[Elsewhere](http://lesswrong.com/lw/p9/the_generalized_antizombie_principle/) he says that

> Consciousness, whatever it may be—a substance, a process, a name for a confusion—is not epiphenomenal; your mind can catch the inner listener in the act of listening, and say so out loud.  \_The fact that I have typed this paragraph \_would at least _seem_ to refute the idea that consciousness has no experimentally detectable consequences.

Not quite. It is plausible, and I subscribe to the same view, that consciousness causes stuff. But it is also plausible - possible - that we act and consciousness doesn't do anything. Consciousness is just an ex-post feeling of what the system just did.

You your brain could have done X (perfectly explainable by physics) and then the conscious I is notified of the decision, perhaps even with an attached feeling of having chosen. This view, I would say, does get some support from neuroscientific research, so we cannot reject it out of hand. And if we can't, Eliezer's enjoyable paragraph writing is no rebuttal to Zombie-ism.

\*\*(IV) \*\*

One final act of nigromancy, if the above failed to put a nice picture of a P-zombie in your _mind_.

Assume this disjunction is true: Either consciousness is some fundamental component of the universe (Panpsychism), or that the laws of physics in this universe entail that consciousness is a property that certain systems have.

With this, we remove the epiphenomenalism and the weirdness that was bothering Yudkowsky out of the table.

Now, imagine that a regular person (not a Zombie) is asked to say a number between 1 and 10. What happens there is that soundwaves travel through the air, induce some movement in the tympanic membrane in the ear, and in turn. The vibrations end up in the cochlea, which will transmit them as electrical impulses to the brain, where they will interact with other impulses. After a while, some impulses will come down and move the vocal folds and the diaphragm so as to produce speech.

This is accompanied by, perhaps, an inner monologue of 'Which number should I choose?' and a feeling of actually freely choosing something.

In the above process, imagine being able to get into the brain, to trace everything as it happens. Perhaps you even have the Equations of Consciousness at your disposal, that explain that this bunch of interconnected neurons here and there gather some [proto-conscious](http://consc.net/papers/panpsychism.pdf) events and produce consciousness or whatever. The proto-conscious events, say, create a subjective experience of varying complexity if they are integrated into a greater whole.

Now, once that you have imagined how the hard problem of consciousness is solved, imagine a universe different from ours. This universe has only two dimensions, and the entropy of the universe as  whole _stays constant_. In addition, there are no Equations of Consciousness, and no protoconscious events. There are 2D beings that walk around, and are obviously not conscious. Imagine things like insects and simple reptiles. These are zombies -in a sense- but not the zombies we want.

You just imagined an universe of zombies. Now quickly change from 2 to 3 dimensions, and from deltaS=0 to deltaS>0.

Bring to your mind what you saw earlier: all the electrical impulses going around in the brain to produce a response. No consciousness involved.

**(V)**

What's the point of P-zombies? As I see it, at least to illustrate our present lack of understanding regarding consciousness, and to substantiate one of the five premises that cannot be accepted together, and that compose the [Mind-Body problem](http://www.owl232.net/mind.htm)

1.  The properties of a system are a necessary consequence of the properties, activities, and arrangements of the components
2.  People are made of atoms, quarks, etc.
3.  Atoms are purely physical things: their properties, activities and relations are all physical.
4.  People have mental states
5.  No statement ascribing a mental predicate can be derived from any set of purely physical descriptions / The existence of a given mental state is not a (metaphysically) necessary  consequence of the existence of any purely physical state

The Zombie argument (or Mary's room, or the case of inverted qualias\*) give us reason to think that 5 is true. Regardless of what's the truth regarding how consciousness works in our universe, it still could have been in any other way. Perhaps a universe with consciousness will be different as a universe without it.

But accepting that consciousness it not a passive inert thing -and thus agreeing with Yudkowsky- does not do. I could still have been a passive inert thing.

I don't think this refutes physicalism. We may still find consciousness particles, or consciousness equations. But it does suggest that it might be wrong.

\*Perhaps this is even better than zombies: To motivate that there is no necessary connection between the physical and the mental, think that what you see as red you might see as yellow, and viceversa. We could live in the same universe, talk in the same way, and still have different qualias. An external observer, examining only physical properties, would not be able to guess how the qualias are in the universe. Doesn't this beg the question against physicalism? In the end in boils down to a philosophical argument, build with the knowledge we now have, to see what is the most plausible thing to believe: will we ever find physical laws and explanations for consciousness, or not? Previous attempts at this game (the infamous elan vital) were successfully explained. But this time seems different. Now we are probing deep into the brain, we are groping our way towards understanding Physics, and while there are a few unsolved problems in physics, we don't even have a way to link that enormous edifice of Physics to conscious experience. There is nothing within [string theory or loop quantum gravity](https://www.quantamagazine.org/20150803-physics-theories-map/) to explain consciousness. This fact, plus the easiness of imagining zombies, plus the seeming conceptual gap between the physical and the subjective are strong arguments against physicalism being true.

Some people, admitting this,[take a different route: deny consciousness](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/materialism-eliminative/). To say that it is an illusion. That because it doesn't fit or it is not expected to fit within a scientific understanding That, I think, is essentially a reductio ad absurdum of physicalism. If something is evident is that we are conscious. Like Descartes famously said, there cannot be anything more certain than that. Whatever conclusion one arrives at , if it conflicts with that fact, it means that one ought to reject one of the other premises in the argument.

We still could have, somehow, physicalism and consciousness. This is David Pearce's N[on-materialist physicalism proposal](https://www.physicalism.com/). But if we ever prove that physicalism does not allow us to explain consciousness as a real phenomenon -thus forcing us to be eliminativists on pain of logical inconsistency- that would count as a refutation of physicalism, not of consciousness.

I don't think the Zombie argument entails that, so one is not forced to believe much else if one accepts that conceiving zombies is possible. It does show that there exists a possible universe in which physics does not explain consciousness. Do we live in such a universe? We might know in some decades. I hope we don't, we'd have to live with the mystery forever!

> There is plenty of evidence suggesting that consciousness, or an attentional phenomenon closely related to consciousness, is important for many forms of learning, memory and voluntary control of behaviour. However, a leading strategy in scientific research on consciousness is in search of something stronger: tasks that can _only_ be performed using conscious representations. For example, it has been variously claimed that consciousness is required in order: to integrate or bind perceptual features ([Dehaene and Naccache 2001](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-11); [Baars 2002](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-2); [Tononi 2004](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-93)); to keep a representation online in the absence of stimulation ([Greenwald et al. 1996](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-36); [Dehaene and Naccache 2001](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-11)) or to integrate motivational states with causal learning ([Dickinson and Balleine 2009](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-13)).
> 
> The history of this enterprise is not encouraging. Almost all proposed functions have been matched by plausible findings where the effect is shown to be produced in the absence of consciousness ([Faivre et al. 2014](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-19); [Soto et al. 2011](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-88) and [Winkielman et al. 2005](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-101), respectively). Certainly, there is no clear case of a task for which consciousness is required. Disputes arise because of the intricacies of measuring consciousness and its absence – such that some researchers doubt that there is any non-chance performance without consciousness ([Newell and Shanks 2014](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-66)). So the case that there is actually any cognitive processing of non-conscious representations is far from conclusive ([Phillips 2015](https://nc.oxfordjournals.org/content/2016/1/niw005#ref-79)).
