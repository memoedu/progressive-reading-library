# Post 29: Meaningful Rest — Neel Nanda

---

## 正文

一个练习：设一个 [5 分钟计时器](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/post-28-on-creativity-the-joys-of-5-minute-timers)，列出你感到 tired 和 low-energy 时 _想_ 做的事。然后，再设一个 5 分钟计时器，列出那些你 **做完之后感到 rejuvenated** 的事——也就是你在 low-energy 时 _喜欢_ 做的事。

如果你和我有些相似，这两份 lists 基本上就是 disjoint 的！当我 tired 时，我 _想_ compulsively check 各种东西——Facebook、电子邮件、新闻。我想 procrastinate：compulsively scroll through Reddit、低俗的网络小说、最新的网络漫画。但 empirically，做完这些之后，我并没有感到更快乐，也没有更 energised。我常常反而觉得更 tired！而那些让我为自己 _做过_ 而感到高兴的事，往往完全不同：散步、meditating、读书。通常都是要出门、远离屏幕的事。然而，当我 tired 时，我却不会 reflexively 选择这些事。这既是个谜（似乎有着 [solid neuroscientific basis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/87mdaCvCyo5bkk8hE/not-for-the-sake-of-pleasure-alone)），又 _显然糟糕透顶_。

这很糟糕，因为我想做的那些事就是 _default actions_——那些开始时 [no activation energy](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower) 的事，那些我会 reflexively 选择的事。而当我 tired 时，我缺乏 willpower 去做任何更有追求的事，只会选择最容易做的事。这就 creates 了一个 **feedback loop**——我 tired，于是做自己想做的事，却没有 rejuvenated，反而变得更 tired，如此等等。这既消耗了大量时间，甚至还不能让我在这个过程中感到快乐！

所以，这是个问题。而解决它是件大事，因为 well-rested 和 high-energy _极其_ 重要。这对我的 productivity 至关重要——当我 tired 时，我发现自己很容易陷入 procrastination，无法取得进展，只是在徒劳地兜圈子。_而且_，这也损害了我的 happiness——tired 会让我 irritated、fatigued、insecure，等等。我注意到，在这个问题以及 sleep 等相关问题上取得进展后，我的 overall happiness 有了 significant increases。

更糟的是，这和我的 default working style 结合起来，后果非常不好。我有一种强烈的 neurotic desire，想要 _完成_ 事情，并且 fixate 于自己的 total output，而不是工作的时间。我经常逼自己完成手头的 task，远远超出分配好的工作时间，不做完就不肯休息。在这个过程中，我会逼自己越过 strain 的临界点，直到感到 drained 才休息。而一旦 drained，我就只会做那些自己 _想_ 做的 default 的事，在那个 loop 里困上一阵子，这一天基本上也就到此为止了。

这是帮助我厘清自己的 [inner and outer optimiser](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise) 之间区别的例子之一——要 maximise global productivity，并不总是应该听从脑海里那个 neurotic 的声音，照它说的去 maximise 当下的 productivity。而且，我需要学会即使在并不 _觉得_ 必要时也休息，因为 empirically，我对于什么时候需要 rest、什么时候不需要的 intuitions 烂透了。

以上说了这么多这个问题 _是什么_，但该怎么办呢？我的 underlying model 是，这个问题关乎 **defaults**。生活中的大多数情境都有一种 default response，照着做不费力气。要 _deviate_ 于 default response，我就需要消耗一种 scarce resource——**willpower**。tired 本质上就是 willpower 不足，而问题在于，我在 low willpower 时的 default actions 并不会 regenerate willpower。（我在[这篇文章](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)中更详细地阐述了这个 model。）

对此的 solution 分为两部分——学会在完全 drained 之前休息，这样我就能 resist 那个 default，并采取 rejuvenating actions。同时，lower rejuvenating actions 的 **activation energy**，以 **shape the default** \- 让它们尽可能接近 default。

shaping the default 这种 mindset 很有价值，因为它表明，我不能 _只_ 在自己真的 tired 时才做 rejuvenating 的事，不能等到觉得确实有必要时才去做。default 是由我的 habits 和实际采取的 actions 形成的。当我感到 compulsion，想要瘫着刷消息，又没有 _强烈_ 觉得自己需要 rest 时，这依然不是个好主意。因为我不只是在选择瘫着消磨一次时间——我还让未来的自己每次 tired 时陷入这个 loop 的可能性略微提高了。我 _需要_ 在自己 _确实_ 有 energy 做出选择时去做 rejuvenating 的事，让它成为一种 habit，这样在缺乏 energy 时，做起来就更容易。休息，尤其是 rejuvenating 的休息，必须成为一个 **point of principle**——我为这件事本身而做，而不是因为我想做，并且 _觉得_ 自己需要。因为后者并不是一种能 generalise 到我 _确实_ 需要 rejuvenating break 时的 policy，而到了那些时候，除了自己的 default policy，我什么都做不了。

对于究竟该做什么，我一直有意说得比较模糊，因为我预计这会因人而异，差别很大——我建议对那些 _你_ 做完后感到 rejuvenated 的事进行 [brainstorming](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/post-28-on-creativity-the-joys-of-5-minute-timers)，并建立一个 system，让做这些事成为 default。不过，如果能有所帮助，下面是我的过程：

在 brainstorming 什么能让我 rejuvenated 时，我发现，待在户外、远离屏幕，做些感觉 pleasant 又 virtuous 的事，是 per unit time 内最好的 rejuvenation 来源。一个简单的做法就是去花园里躺上 10 分钟，读一本纸质书（这也意味着我终于开始真正读完待读清单里的书了！）。而真正做这件事，一开始有着 high activation energy——我需要起身走到花园里，它带来的 dopamine rush 也不如查看消息，等等。所以，有一周，我制定了一项 policy：休息时 _总是_ 这样做，并确保自己坚持——休息时锁住手机、屏蔽 distracting websites，在开始和结束时设置简短的提示音。我也 shaped 了自己的 environment，让这件事更容易做到，确保手边始终放着正在读的书。我并没有完全坚持下来，做这件事也确实需要一些 energy。但把它变成 habit，significantly lowered 了 activation energy，也让我对它 built positive associations，以至于现在做起来感觉容易 _太多_ 了。我并不严格恪守它，但现在，当我 tired 时，做这件事已经容易 _得多_ 了。

这只是对我有效的做法，我并不指望它能 generalise！但我希望它背后的 underlying philosophy 可以。即使你想不出一个完美的 system，不太确定什么能让自己 rejuvenating，或者一想到 tired 时还要坚持一个 system 就觉得 aversive，我仍然建议你 [running the experiment](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/the-value-of-experiments)！我发现，自己对于什么会有效、什么不会有效的 intuitions 并不十分 reliable，没有什么能替代直接 gathering data。而且，这是个 cheap test——浪费时间的 downside 很低，而变得更善于 rest 的 upside 却 _极其_ 高！我当时完全不确信自己的 experiment 会有效，但事后看来，它非常有价值。

所以，对于自己是否 intrinsically want 去做那些能让自己 rejuvenate 的事，你满意吗？你对自己的 energy levels 满意吗？如果不满意，你能为此做些什么？

_（在这篇文章里，我主要关注的是在一个工作日中穿插小规模的 meaningful breaks。我认为，休息更长时间也极其有价值（而且这方面我做得差得多——欢迎建议！）。关于这个话题的思考，我强烈推荐_ [_这篇文章_](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/ximou2kyQorm6MPjX/rest-days-vs-recovery-days) _。）_

## 原文

An exercise: Set a [5 minute timer](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/post-28-on-creativity-the-joys-of-5-minute-timers), and list the things you _want_ to do when you feel tired and low-energy. Then, set another 5 minute timer, and list the things you **feel rejuvenated after having done** - the things you _like_ doing when low-energy.

If you’re anything like me, these lists are basically disjoint! When I’m tired, I _want_ to compulsively check things - Facebook, email, the news. I want to procrastinate: to compulsively scroll through Reddit, trashy web-fiction, the latest webcomics. But, empirically, after doing this I don’t feel any happier or more energised. Often, I feel even more tired! While the things I feel happy _to have done_ tend to be completely different: going for a walk, meditating, reading a book. Generally, things that involve going outside and getting away from screens. Yet, these are not the things I reflexively reach for when tired. This is both a puzzle (which seems to have a [solid neuroscientific basis](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/87mdaCvCyo5bkk8hE/not-for-the-sake-of-pleasure-alone)) and _obviously terrible_.

This is terrible, because the things I want to do are the _default actions_ - the things that take [no activation energy](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower) to start, the things I reflexively reach for. And when I’m tired, I lack the willpower to do anything more ambitious, and will just reach for whatever is most available. And this creates a **feedback loop** - I am tired, so I do the things I want to do, I am not rejuvenated and made more tired, etc. This both consumes a lot of time, and doesn’t even make me happy in the process!

So, this is a problem. And fixing this is a big deal, because being well-rested and high-energy is _super_ important. It’s key to my productivity - when I’m tired, I find it easy to procrastination, fail to make progress, and go in circles of wasted motion. _And_ it’s bad for my happiness - being tired makes me irritated, fatigued, insecure, etc. I’ve noticed significant increases in my overall happiness after making progress on this problem, and related problems like my sleep.

Worse, this combines really badly with my default working style. I have a strong neurotic desire to _finish_ things, and to fixate on my total output rather than time spent working. I’ll often push myself to complete my current task, going well beyond my allocated working time, and not being willing to take a break until I’m done. And in the process, I’ll push myself past the point of strain, and only take a break when I’m feeling drained. And once drained I’ll only do the default things I _want_ to do, be stuck in that loop for a while, and basically be done for the day.

This was one of the examples that helped me clarify the difference between my [inner and outer optimiser](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise) - the way to maximise global productivity isn’t always to follow the neurotic voice in my head telling me how to maximise productivity in the moment. And I need to learn how to take breaks even when it doesn’t _feel_ necessary, because my intuitions for when rest is and is not necessary are empirically shite.

So, that’s a lot on what the problem _is_, but what to do about it? My underlying model is that the problem is one of **defaults**. Most situations in my life have a default response, and that takes no effort to follow. To _deviate_ from the default response, I need to spend a scarce resource - **willpower**. Being tired is essentially being low on willpower, and the problem is that my default actions when low willpower do not regenerate willpower. (I elaborate far more on this model in [this post](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower))

The solution to this is two-fold - learn to take breaks before being completely drained, so I can resist the default and take rejuvenating actions. And lower the **activation energy** of rejuvenating actions, to **shape the default** \- making them as close to the default as possible.

The mindset of shaping the default is valuable, because it shows that I can’t _only_ do rejuvenating things when I’m really tired, and wait until it feels really necessary. The default is formed by my habits, and the actions that I actually take. When I feel the compulsion to veg out and scroll through my messages, and don’t feel _strongly_ in need of rest, this is still a bad idea. Because I’m not just choosing to veg out once - I’m making it slightly more likely that I’ll fall into this loop every time my future self is tired. I _need_ to do the rejuvenating thing at the times when I _do_ have the energy to choose it, so that it becomes a habit, and it’s easier to do at the times when I lack the energy. Taking breaks, and especially rejuvenating ones, must be a **point of principle** - something I do for its own sake, rather than because I want to and _feel_ like I need it. Because the latter is not a policy that will generalise to the times when I _do_ need to take a rejuvenating break, and at those times I can’t do anything _but_ my default policy.

I’ve been deliberately vague about exactly what to do, I expect this to vary a lot between people - I recommend [brainstorming](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/post-28-on-creativity-the-joys-of-5-minute-timers) the things that _you_ feel rejuvenated after doing and building a system such that doing those is the default. But, if it helps, this was my process:

When brainstorming what rejuvenated me, I found that being outside, away from screens, and doing something that felt pleasant and virtuous was the best source of rejuvenation per unit time. And an easy way to do this was to go lie down in my garden for 10 minutes and read a physical book (which also meant I began to actually work my way through my to-read list!). And actually doing this initially had high activation energy - I needed to get up to walk to the garden, there was less of a dopamine rush than checking messages, etc. So, for a week, I made a policy of _always_ doing this during breaks and ensured I stuck to it - locking my phone and blocking distracting websites during breaks, having brief pings at the start and end. And I shaped my environment to make it easier, making sure to always have my current book close to hand. And I didn’t perfectly stick to this, and it took some energy to do. But making it a habit significantly lowered the activation energy, and built positive associations with it, to the point that it now feels _way_ easier. And I don’t stick to it religiously, but it’s now _much_ easier to do when I feel tired.

This is just what worked for me, and I don’t expect this to generalise! But I hope the underlying philosophy behind it does. And even if you can’t come up with a perfect system, aren’t super sure what you find rejuvenating, or find it aversive to imagine sticking to a system while tired, I recommend [running the experiment](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/the-value-of-experiments) anyway! I find my intuitions for what will and will not work aren’t strongly reliable, and there’s no substitute for just gathering data. And it’s a cheap test - the downside of wasted time is low, while the upside of becoming better at rest is _super_ high! I wasn’t at all convinced that my experiment would work, but in hindsight it was super valuable.

So, are you satisfied that you intrinsically want to do the things that rejuvenate you? Are you satisfied with your energy levels? And if not, what can you do about that?

_(In this post, I’ve mostly focused on taking meaningful breaks on a small scale, throughout a work day. I think taking longer breaks is also extremely valuable (and something I suck way more at - advice appreciated!)
