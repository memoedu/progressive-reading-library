# Channel factors

---

## 正文

_或者，“如何不对自己犯 fundamental attribution error”；或者，“如何做到那件你一直因自己没做而懊恼的事”；或者，“finding 和 solving 那些 trivial 却 leveraged 的 inconveniences”。_

social psychology 的一个关键 insights 是，我们对 events 的 reactions 在很大程度上 dependent on 所讨论的 situation 的细微 details，而且往往几乎 independent of personality。例如，假设有一群人彼此 playing Prisoner’s Dilemma，而你想弄清谁会 defect。大多数人在这里的 theory of mind 大致是：“人们 defect，是因为他们 self-interested、脾气不好，或者 vindictive。”所以，你可能会问某个 player 的朋友，这个人有多 cooperative，并据此猜测谁会 cooperate。

不幸的是，这个 approach 完全 useless。无论朋友是否认为他们 cooperative，人们 cooperate 的可能性都是 equally likely。在 Prisoners’ Dilemma 中，你的朋友对你的 personality 的 assessment _与你的 behavior 完全不 correlate。_

幸运的是，还有别的东西是相当不错的 predictor！具体来说，game 的 instructions 把它称为“Community Game”还是“Wall Street Game”，这件事_非常_重要。[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/channel-factors/#fn:1)

![展示 Liberman 的 results 的 graph。](https://s3.amazonaws.com/bknet/liberman_2004.png)

没错，instructions 中的_短短一个短语_，重复两次，就 causes cooperation rates 达到 double。如果你总喜欢把自己想成某种 agent，认为自己的 decisions 是由 rational ego controlled，而不是由很久以前偶然听到的几个词 controlled，你可能会觉得这有点令人担忧。

另一方面，如果你喜欢把自己想成一个更愿意拥有 true beliefs 的人，你可能会兴奋，因为你的 beliefs 刚刚变得更 truer 了！你大概有时无法对自己的 decisions 施加 egoistic control，而如果你把自己的 brain 完全理解成一个 consciously-controlled ego，那么当这种事发生时，你就会非常困惑，随口抛出“下次更努力一点”“多些 willpower”“别犯傻”“别 get distracted”之类的 solutions。

这些都是 fake solutions。它们本来完全可以 solve 你的 problem，只不过它们其实不是你能够采取的 actions。

所以，你应该 change 自己的 model。看似 trivial 的 circumstances changes，却会 disproportionately change 你的 behavior，这样的 examples 随处可见。literature 中的另一个 example 是：对于那些被建议接受 tetanus inoculations 的学生，如果给他们一张前往大学 health center 的地图以及开放时间，他们这样做的可能性就会高得多，而这些信息大多数学生本来就知道。[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/channel-factors/#fn:2)（当然，除非给学生地图，否则接受 inoculation 的 intent 几乎没有 predictive power。）

这类事物的 psychology term 是“channel factor”，它大概是我这学期学到的最有用的 psychology concept。自从掌握它以后，我就发现它经常出现。例如：

-   对于用牙线清洁牙齿，我的一个 channel factor 是那些小塑料牙线棒的 availability。显然，用牙线清洁牙齿有足够的好处，即使没有方便的新式工具帮忙，我也应该做。不过，虽然我在某种程度上明白这一点，但没有这些塑料辅助工具时，用牙线清洁牙齿实在太费时间、太不舒服，我始终懒得去做。

-   对于听音乐，以及给朋友、家人或那些让我在电话里等上一阵子的人打电话：wireless headphones。以前我没有意识到，把手机紧贴耳朵走来走去有多不舒服，直到我买了一副[这样的耳机](http://www.amazon.com/LG-Electronics-HBS-730-Bluetooth-Headset/dp/B009A5204K/ref=sr_1_2?ie=UTF8&qid=1394508524&sr=8-2&keywords=lg+tone)，突然就对打电话热衷多了。

-   对于定期洗衣服：洗衣凝珠。我以前总有个问题，就是把洗衣液落在宿舍的公共洗衣房里，结果好心人会把它收走，我还没意识到它不见了，它就已经消失了。这样一来，我就没了洗衣液，想到洗衣服就觉得烦，又懒得去买新的洗衣液。

共同之处在于，这些 inconvenience 是如此 trivial，以至于在我专门开始留意 channel factors 之前，甚至没有_注意到_它们。在我平时对自己的 model 中，我太 sane 了，不会仅仅因为没有某样能让手指在清洁牙齿时稍微舒服一点的东西，就不用牙线。哎呀。

总之，这形成了一个 heuristic：当我因自己‘到头来’没有去做某件自己‘想’做的事而懊恼时，就寻找 channel factors。到目前为止，它在揭示具有 large effects 的 small modifications 方面相当成功：

-   一用完就 closing browser tabs

-   像躲瘟疫一样 avoiding checking email

-   通过 [mightytext](http://mightytext.net/)，从我的 computer 上 send texts[3](https://www.benkuhn.net/channel-factors/#fn:3)

你发现过自己的 channel factors 吗？我还遗漏了什么？

## 原文

_Or, “how not to make a fundamental attribution error on yourself;” or, “how to do that thing that you keep being frustrated at yourself for not doing;” or, “finding and solving trivial but leveraged inconveniences.”_

One of the key insights of social psychology is that our reactions to events are hugely dependent on the fine details of the situation in question, and often pretty much independent of personality. For instance, suppose you have a bunch of people to playing the Prisoner’s Dilemma with each other, and you want to figure out who will defect. Most people’s theory of mind here says something like, “people defect because they’re self-interested or grumpy or vindictive.” So you might ask a player’s friends how cooperative they were, and use that to guess who will cooperate.

Unfortunately, this approach is totally useless. People are equally likely to cooperate whether or not their friends think they’re cooperative. In the Prisoners’ Dilemma, your friends’ assessment of your personality _does not correlate at all with your behavior._

Fortunately, there’s something else which is a pretty good predictor! In particular, it matters a _lot_ whether the instructions of the game called it the “Community Game” or the “Wall Street Game.”[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/channel-factors/#fn:1)

![A graph of Liberman&rsquo;s results.](https://s3.amazonaws.com/bknet/liberman_2004.png)

Yep, a _single phrase_ of the instructions, repeated twice, causes cooperation rates to double. If you ever like to think of yourself as some kind of agent whose decisions are controlled by a rational ego (instead of some random words you heard once upon a time), you might find that a bit worrying.

On the other hand, if you like to think of yourself as the kind of person who prefers to have true beliefs, you might be excited because your beliefs just got truer! You probably sometimes fail to impose egoistic control on your own decisions, and if you understand your brain purely as a consciously-controlled ego then you will be really confused when this happens, and spit out solutions like “try harder next time” or “have more willpower” or “don’t be dumb” or “don’t get distracted” or something.

These are fake solutions. They would totally solve your problem, except that they’re not really actions you can take.

So you should change your model. There are examples everywhere of seemingly trivial changes to circumstances that disproportionately change your behavior. Another example in the literature is that students advised to get tetanus inoculations were far more likely to do so if they were given a map to the university health center and times of operation, which the majority of the students already knew.[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/channel-factors/#fn:2) (Of course, intent to get an inoculation had almost no predictive power unless students were given a map.)

The psychology term for such things is a “channel factor,” and it’s probably the most useful psychology concept I’ve learned this semester. Since acquiring it, I’ve noticed it cropping up a lot. For instance:

-   For flossing, a channel factor for me is availability of those little plastic flossy things. Obviously flossing is good enough that I should do it even without handy newfangled devices to help. But while I know this on some level, flossing is time-consuming and unpleasant enough without the plastic aids that I can never really be bothered.

-   For listening to music and calling friends/family/people who put me on hold for a while: wireless headphones. I didn’t realize how uncomfortable it was to walk around with a phone jammed up to my ear until I bought a pair of [these](http://www.amazon.com/LG-Electronics-HBS-730-Bluetooth-Headset/dp/B009A5204K/ref=sr_1_2?ie=UTF8&qid=1394508524&sr=8-2&keywords=lg+tone) and suddenly became much more excited about phone calls.

-   For doing laundry routinely, detergent packets. I used to have the problem of leaving my detergent in my dorm’s public laundry room, where it would get removed by well-meaning people and disappear before I realized that it was gone. Then I wouldn’t have any detergent, and I would feel bad about doing laundry and be too lazy to buy new detergent.

The common thread is that the inconvenience is so trivial that I didn’t even _notice_ it until I was specifically on the lookout for channel factors. My usual model of myself is too sane to do things like not floss because I didn’t have the thing that made my fingers slightly more comfortable while I do it. Whoops.

Anyway, this results in a heuristic of, when I am frustrated by the fact that I ’end up’ not doing something I ‘want’ to do, looking for channel factors. So far, it’s been pretty successful at uncovering small modifications with large effects:

-   closing browser tabs as soon as I’m done with them

-   avoiding checking email like the plague

-   using [mightytext](http://mightytext.net/) to send texts from my computer[3](https://www.benkuhn.net/channel-factors/#fn:3)

Have you discovered any channel factors of your own? What am I missing?
