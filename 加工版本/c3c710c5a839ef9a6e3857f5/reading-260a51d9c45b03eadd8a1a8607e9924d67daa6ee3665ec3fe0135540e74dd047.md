# Nintil - Notes on 2020

---

## 正文

年初我在伦敦，后来在 3 月初去杰克逊霍尔参加了一场私人会议。会议快结束时，UK->USA 的旅行禁令公布了，但我没怎么在意，继续着原来的安排。除了 science 和 technology 的 advances，我通常不太关注时事（反复出现的噪声），而 [Covid](https://www.theverge.com/2016/5/5/11592622/this-is-fine-meme-comic) 不过是其中最新的一件。几年后我会回头看看，在恰当的背景下弄清 Covid 究竟带来了什么。因此，我没有去阅读和思考围绕最新的 OWS、dexamethasone、T cell 和 microcovid 的种种风波，而是照常写博客。今年写的 38 篇文章中，只有 5 篇提到了 Covid，其中两次提及还只是为了说 Nintil 没有 Covid 相关内容。其中 12 篇是每月的 Links 文章，其余 27 篇中，7 篇讨论 aging，另有几篇讨论 meta-science。与 2019 年相比，我写的字数翻了一番（今年超过 100k，最长的是我的 Longevity FAQ 和关于 immunosenescence 的 review）。总的来说，对 Nintil 而言，这是很棒的一年。

2019 年初，我[开始](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1097267295292932096)学习 biology，同年晚些时候，大约在 11 月，我开始阅读关于 longevity 的 underpinnings 的资料，并在两三个月后发表了 [Longevity FAQ（常见问题解答）](https://nintil.com/longevity)。在那之前，我对 biology 的了解是 0。我学过 aerospace dynamics 和 industrial engineering，因此对 electronics、finance、fluid mechanics、thermodynamics 以及其他各种学科颇有了解；但考虑到我也写过一两篇标题颇具挑衅意味的文章，叫作“（fundamental）physics 已死（而 biology 才是未来）”，学习 biology 似乎是理解未来几十年 science 中那些关键 revolutions 的关键，可我竟然对此一无所知！在此之前，我曾不同程度地涉足 economic history、monetary theory 或 psychology 等差别很大的领域，因此这是我第一次自行深入探索“hard science”。为了 synthesize 和 navigate 其他 literatures 而 developed 的 techniques 和 heuristics，能在 biology 中 work 吗？回头来看，答案是肯定的，不过除了我自己的感受，很难为此提供什么 proof。在这个过程中，我还意识到，只要我们有更好的 summarization tools，就能大幅缩短 navigate 新 literature 所花的大量时间。我写的所有东西从第二天起就开始过时，因为 biology 的进展实在太快，而要让所有内容始终保持最新，就会耗费大量时间。我曾指出，burden of knowledge 是 science 日益老龄化的一个原因，但除此之外，它也可能是 science 越来越慢的一个原因。怎样才能最好地减少了解所有事物的一切所需的时间？

今年晚些时候，我也搬到了旧金山，到目前为止，我很喜欢这座城市。关于湾区衰落的文章已经很多了，但我认为，那些宣告它死亡的报道实在言过其实。只要离开市中心和米慎区，环境就会好得多。距离我住处几分钟的地方，就有一片真正的森林和一道峡谷，而且我可以在那里晒着太阳散步，_即使在冬天！_ 在我以前住的伦敦市中心，要做到这一点就没那么容易。明年，等出行方便些，我打算去奥斯汀、波士顿或迈阿密看看那些地方是什么样的，因为我确实去过那里。

2021 年有什么计划？新年伊始，我会在刚开始的 _资助 People，而非 Projects_ 系列中写几篇文章。要是能回答“structure science 的最佳方式是什么”，那就太好了。也许到年底，我们就会有答案。我也在做一些与 longevity 相关的事情，但还需要一段时间才能公开。Longevity 与 meta-science 很像，因为这两个 disciplines 都能为更多事情创造条件。更好的 science 如同上涨的潮水，托起所有 research 的船只，而 longevity research 尤其能托起所有人生计划。从某种意义上说，更好的 science 是在 improving 每 unit of time 内可以做到的事情，而 longevity 则是在 increasing 可拥有的（quality-adjusted）time 的 units 数量。

## 原文

I started the year in London, attending later in in early March a private conference in Jackson Hole. Near the end of the conference the travel ban UK->USA was announced but I continued without paying much attention to it. Other than advances in science and technology I tend to pay little attention to current events (noise that repeats itself) and [Covid](https://www.theverge.com/2016/5/5/11592622/this-is-fine-meme-comic) was just the last of them. In a few years I'll look back and see exactly what Covid did in its proper context. So instead of reading and thinking about the latest OWS, dexamethasone, T cell, and microcovid drama, I just kept blogging as usual. I've written just 5 posts that mention Covid this year out of 38 I've written, and two of those mentions were just to say that Nintil was Covid free. Twelve of those posts were the monthly Links posts and out of the other 27, seven were about aging, with a few others about meta-science. Relative to 2019 I've written twice as many words (Over 100k this year, with the longest being my Longevity FAQ and my review on immunosenescence). It has, overall, been a great year for Nintil.

Early in 2019 I [started](https://twitter.com/ArtirKel/status/1097267295292932096) learning about biology and later that same year, around November I started reading about the underpinnings of longevity, publishing two or three months later [The Longevity FAQ](https://nintil.com/longevity). Prior to that I knew 0 about biology. Having studied aerospace dynamics and industrial engineering I knew a bunch about electronics, finance, fluid mechanics, thermodynamics and assorted subjects but given that I had also written one or two of these provocatively titled "(fundamental) physics is dead (and biology if the future)", learning biology seemed key to understand the key revolutions in science over the coming decades, yet I knew nothing about it! Prior to this I had ventured into areas as disparate as economic history, monetary theory or psychology in various depths, so this was my first self-guided dive into "hard science". Could the techniques and heuristics developed to synthesize and navigate other literatures work in biology? The answer in retrospective was yes though it is hard to offer any proof of that other than what it seems like to me. In the process I also realized that a lot of the time spent in navigating a new literature could be dramatically shortened if only we had better tools for summarization. Everything I've written starts to be out of date the day after because biology moves so fast, and trying to keep everything up to date would consume a lot of time. I pointed to the burden of knowledge as a reason why science is getting older, but beyond that it may be a reason why science is getting slower. What's the best way to reduce the time to know everything about everything?

I moved to San Francisco as well later this year, and so far I'm enjoying the city. Lots has been written about the decline of the Bay Area but I think reports of its death are greatly exaggerated. Once one gets away from Downtown and the Mission, things are nicer. I have a literal forest and canyon a few minutes from where I live and can go on walks there under the sun _in winter!_ Not as easy to do that in central London where I used to live. Next year once traveling is easier I intend to visit Austin, or Boston, or Miami to see what those places are like as I've really been there.

What is the plan for 2021? The year will start with some posts in the _Fund People, not Projects_ series that I just started. Having an answer to "What's the best way to structure science" would be nice. Maybe by the end of year we'll have that answer. I am also working on some longevity-related stuff but that will take a while to be made public. Longevity is like meta-science in that both disciplines act as enablers of so much more. Better science is a rising tide that lifts all the research boats and longevity research in particular lifts all life projects. In some sense better science is improving what can be done per unit of time, and longevity is increasing how many units of (quality-adjusted) time there are.
