## 正文

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/taking-action-is-hard.png)

你本该写一篇文章，却在 procrastinate。地下室里的跑步机正在积灰。你想学一门语言、创业或换个职业，但这些想法都没有下文。Inaction 是我们都经历过的事情。

Inaction 比其他任何事情都更是我们 failures 和痛苦的 cause。如果我们能 consistently 做那些明知自己应该做的事，生活就会轻松得多。你的 projects 会更 successful。你的 goals 会成为现实。你的生活可以更好。

我们都知道 action 很难。但为什么呢？为什么我们在 take action 时会如此挣扎？

我们很容易把这视为理所当然，认为 laziness 只是 human psyche 的一种 intrinsic feature。但我们也可以设想另一种情形：action 的发生并不伴随这么多 inner struggle。

事实上，要考虑这种可能性，你甚至不必想象一个 hypothetical universe，因为在我们的世界里，有些人似乎格外擅长 taking action。Arnold Schwarzenegger 搬到 America，成为著名的 bodybuilder、actor、entrepreneur，随后又成为 politician。Elon Musk 创办 PayPal、Tesla、SpaceX 等公司。Marie Curie 作为一位丧偶的母亲，一边养育家人，一边赢得两项 Nobel prizes。

take action 的 ability 与最终的 success 之间，似乎存在惊人地高的 correlation。Action 与 success 如此 closely matched，使得我们在 inaction 中的挣扎更加令人费解。如果生活中的 success 往往就像“做事、从中学习、重复”这么简单，为什么我们许多人还会陷入 laziness、self-sabotage 和 procrastination 的 loops？

## 关于行动困难的一些可能的 Explanations

收听这篇文章

action 之所以困难，可能有许多原因。不过，在尝试提出一些 explanations 之前，我想先看看一些解释得不太好的 theories：

1.  **Talent**。Talent、intelligence 和 serendipity 都是你 execute 的 ability 的 multipliers。显然，Marie Curie 是 brilliant。那份 brilliance，加上选中了恰到好处的 research project，促成了她 discovering radium，并创造历史。但尽管 talent 显然是 success 的一个要素，它似乎与 taking action 并没有那么 correlated。这个世界既有光芒燃尽的 brilliant 明星，也有建立帝国的 mediocre 头脑。
2.  **Preferences**。我并不想过 Elon Musk 那样的工作生活。我可以接受这意味着自己大概不会对世界产生相似程度的影响。这是 preferences 上的 difference，它或许可以解释我们所看到的人们愿意付出多少努力的部分差异。但 preferences 无法解释，为什么我们会如此频繁地与自己挣扎。既然知道自己最终会写完那篇文章，为什么还要一直 procrastinate？既然知道自己永远达不到能够使用那门语言的程度，为什么还要花上几周去_开始_学习？Preferences 可以解释为什么不去尝试，却无法很好地解释我们在 inaction 中的 inner struggles。
3.  **Capacity for effort**。也许 effort 只是一种不同的 talent _类型_，与 intelligence 不同。拥有较大的 capacity，你就可以轻松做许多事。拥有较低的 capacity，做什么都会是一番挣扎。尽管这个 explanation 似乎确实有部分道理，却不符合我们所经历的 struggle 的 pattern。如果你的 capacity for effort 较低，为什么它不只是让你慢下来，反而让你长期经历一阵阵 activity 的爆发，随后 goals 和 projects 又不可避免地崩溃？
4.  **Motivation**。许多最难 taking action 的人，恰恰最有理由改变。与那些 hyper-fit、试图再把 bodyfat percentage 降低 1% 的人相比，过着极其 unhealthy lifestyle 的人会从一次小小的 intervention 中 benefit 最多。如果你把 motivation define 为 objectively 有充分理由去 act，那么 action 中仍有很大的 gap 需要解释。如果你把 motivation define 为一种 subjective feeling，那么我们就只是回到了最初的疑问：为什么有些人对自己应该做的事情 feel motivated，另一些人却不会？

我对于 inaction 的确切 causes 的思考还没有完全成形，但我想在这里尝试充实几种 possibilities，也给自己一些进一步探究的方向。

## 可能性 #1：Confidence

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/motivation-from-expectation.png)

与 goal-setting 相关的一类流行 theories，被称为 [expectancy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expectancy_theory)。这些 theories 本质上认为，你 complete 一项 task 的 motivation，既取决于你 anticipate 会获得的 reward 的 value，也取决于你对自己实际上会得到那份 reward 的 expectation。

然而，你对 success 的 expectations 也取决于你的 motivation。这就 creates 了一个 feedback loop：你对自己能够 sustain motivation long-term 的 ability 的 expectation，会 influences 你对 success 的 expectation，从而 influencing 你长期的 motivation。

一个 positive feedback loop 可以 create 一个具有两种可能 dynamics 的 system：一方面，当你认为 success 越来越有可能时，你就会越来越 motivated，形成 accelerating commitment；另一方面，当你越来越 discouraged 时，也会形成 lowered expectations 的 spiral。

由于你对一项 pursuit 的 expectations 往往会 influence 未来的 pursuits，这也可能 stabilize 为你生活中一种普遍的行动或 inaction 的 response。如果你的 projects 往往 fail，你的 expectations 就会低落，motivation 也会 withers。如果你的 projects 往往 succeed，你的 expectations 就会提高，motivation 也会保持 strong。

## 可能性 #2：Social-Desirability Bias

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/consciousness-as-pr.png)

关于 conscious mind 的一种看法是：它发挥的作用更像 brain 的公关经理，而不是 CEO；它会为自身 behavior confabulating 听起来合理的 explanations，而不是真正发号施令。

如果是这样，这就 suggests，我们许多 action 上的 failures 都是 intentional。我们未能 take action，是因为 mind 中 drive 我们 behavior 的 unconscious 部分已经决定不去 take action。

不过，这作为一种可能的 explanation，有趣之处在于，inaction 有时[并不是 socially acceptable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_desirability_bias)。因此，即使我们并不在乎，也需要假装 taking action，好让别人觉得我们确实在乎。这种假装甚至可能是[完全 unconscious](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2018/03/06/book-club-the-elephant-in-the-brain-february-2018/) 的。说谎最容易的方式，就是相信自己说的是真话，因此你甚至可能让自己相信，你想 pursue 一个 goal，而实际上你的 unconscious mind 并没有 committed 于它。

这可能 manifest 在一个对作业 procrastinates（却不知道为什么）的学生身上：他并不真的想学习，却又必须获得父母的认可和经济支持。Inner friction 和 anguish，可能就是他为了显得自己正在努力 take action 而付出的代价。

## Possibility #3：Daydreams 与 Reality 的感受不同

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/theory-vs-reality.png)

[Construal level theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construal_level_theory) 这一观点认为，我们有两种 characteristic modes 来看待事物——一种是 abstract（或 far-mode）的 view，另一种是 concrete（或 near-mode）的 view。

许多宏大的 goals 存在 far-near incompatibility，这可能使它们难以付诸 take action。你在思考自己的 fitness goal 时，可能想到的是 losing weight、being healthy 和 looking great（这些都是 abstract、idealized goals）。然而，当你去 gym 时，你大多想的是自己呼吸多么急促、汗水如何沿着脸颊滴落，以及这让你多么不舒服。

在艰难的 projects 上反复 starting 和 stopping 的 Chronic issues，很可能取决于这种 mental states 的 incompatibility。那个 dreams up 这个 goal 的人，与那个对它 executes 的人并不相同，而 coordinating 这两个版本的自己可能很难。

## Possibility #4：别当出头鸟

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/culture-props-action.png)

我们对于 ambition 本身的 hardwiring，可能是基于我们的 environment 而形成的。我们的许多 ancestors 生活在这样的时代：出人头地、taking actions 超出 cultural expectations，都可能让你被杀害或流放。因此，我们的 natures 可能在试图探查 taking actions 的 cost-benefits，并愿意在早期的 ventures 遭到 punished 时，退回平静的 conformity。

这或许会形成一种 theory of motivation，认为“你的 culture 要求你做什么，你就做什么”；而对于不同种类 actions 的 ventures，如果它们不能 yield 丰厚的 rewards，就会受到强烈 discouragement。

这也可能 explain，为什么 inaction 似乎会发生在生活的某些方面（starting your own business），却不会发生在另一些方面（按时上班）。在后一种情况下，存在一种强烈的 cultural expectation，即每个人都需要按时上班；但没有人会因为你没有 starting a company 而责怪你。

我们的 inner struggles，可能是我们的 unconscious minds 在试图找到最适合我们的 action-taking 的 frontier，根据 environment 的 feedback，adjusting 我们的 ambitions 和整体的 tendency to act。

## Possibility #5：我们太过 Short-Sighted

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/now-vs-future.png)

也可能仅仅是因为，我们的现代 environment 是和平、稳定的，并且允许 skills 和 resources 的 long-term accumulation，与我们 evolved 以求 survive 的那个 environment 大不相同。

具体而言，在当下的 satisfaction 与未来的 long-term rewards 之间，人类会 [exhibit inconsistent time preferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_discounting)。Procrastination 可能是一种 delaying tactic，用来避免此时此刻 wasting energy，即便你认为自己可能会在不远的将来更加努力地工作。

accumulate resources 的 ability 很可能直到 Neolithic revolution 之后才出现；那时，grain agriculture 使一些人能够 save（以及 steal），也使 long-term planning 确实能够带来 evolutionary success。

这个 time-scale 可能太短，不足以 effectively modify 我们的 innate psychology，但它可能已经使我们能够 create 一些 cultural institutions，来 counteract 我们惯常的 short-sightedness。由于 inaction 最 prevalent 的情形，仍然是我们面对没有强烈 cultural pressures 的 activities 时，这或许说明，我们在 inaction 上的 gap，源于默认采用了 ancestors 那种 myopic 的生活方式。

## 我们怎样才能更善于 Taking Action？

为什么我们难以 take action，以及我们怎样才能 improve 这一点，这个话题令我着迷。一方面是因为它表面上似乎如此简单（“去做就是了。”），另一方面是因为它背后如此复杂（当“去做就是了”不管用时，你该怎么办？）。

对于这个问题，Traditional approaches 常常把 human will 当作单一的事物来关注。但实际情况是，我们的 minds 极其复杂，具有许多不同的 integrating control mechanisms，既有 conscious 的，也有 unconscious 的。因此，要 take action，你不仅需要向自己的 conscious mind 提供新的 inputs，把你推向正确的方向，而且这些 nudges 还需要传递到你所拥有的所有其他 control systems，才能让你继续朝那个方向前进。

我还有许多 questions：

-   是否存在不同 types 的 inaction，还是它们都有相似的 core explanation？
-   日常的 inaction 与遭受 depression 或 anxiety 的人所面临的 clinical difficulties，区别在哪里？
    -   这些仅仅是我们都会面对的 slumps 和 avoidance 的 extreme versions，还是它们有 separate mechanisms？
-   这个 system 中的哪些 points 最为 flexible，能够以最少的 effort 来 adjusting long-term action？

随着了解的深入，我会尽力与你分享。

## 原文

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/taking-action-is-hard.png)

You’re supposed to write an essay, but you procrastinate. The treadmill is collecting dust in your basement. You want to learn a language, start a business or change careers, but those ideas go nowhere. Inaction is something we’ve all experienced.

Inaction, more than anything else, is the cause of our failures and our miseries. If we could consistently do the things we know we ought to, life would be much easier. Your projects would be more successful. Your goals would become a reality. Your life could be better.

We all know action is hard. But why? Why do we struggle so much to take action?

It’s easy to simply take this for granted, to assume laziness is just an intrinsic feature of the human psyche. But it’s also possible to imagine an alternative where action happened without so much inner struggle.

Indeed, you don’t even have to imagine a hypothetical universe to consider this possibility, because there are people who exist in our world who seem to be extraordinarily good at taking action. Arnold Schwarzenegger moving to America, becoming a famous bodybuilder, actor, entrepreneur, then politician. Elon Musk starting PayPal, Tesla, SpaceX and more. Marie Curie winning two Nobel prizes while raising a family as a widowed mother.

There seems to be an amazingly high correlation between the ability to take action and eventual success. Action and success are so closely matched, that it makes the struggles we have with inaction all the more perplexing. If success in life is often as simple as “do things, learn from them, repeat” why do many of us get caught in loops of laziness, self-sabotage and procrastination?

## Some Possible Explanations for the Difficulty of Doing

Listen to this article  

There’s a lot of possible reasons why action is hard. Before I try to offer some explanations, however, I want to look at some theories that don’t do the job very well:

1.  **Talent**. Talent, intelligence and serendipity are multipliers of your ability to execute. Obviously, Marie Curie was brilliant. That brilliance, combined with picking just-the-right research project, contributed to her discovering radium and making history. But while talent is obviously an ingredient in success, it doesn’t seem to be all that correlated with taking action. The world is populated by brilliant stars that flame out and mediocre minds that build empires.
2.  **Preferences**. I have no desire for Elon Musk’s working life. I’m okay with the fact that this means I probably won’t have a similar level of impact on the world. That’s a difference in preferences, which likely explains part of the range we see in how much people are willing to work hard. But preferences can’t explain why we struggle with ourselves so much. Why procrastinate forever on an essay you know you’ll do eventually? Why _start_ learning the language for weeks if you know you’ll never get to a level where you can use it? Preferences can explain failing to try, but they don’t work well to explain our inner struggles with inaction.
3.  **Capacity for effort**. Perhaps effort is simply a different _kind_ of talent, different from intelligence. Have a large capacity and you can easily do a lot of things. Have a low capacity and everything is a struggle. While this explanation does seem partly true, it doesn’t match the pattern of struggle we experience. If your capacity for effort is lower, why wouldn’t this just slow you down, rather than have you experience chronic bursts of activity with inevitable crashes in your goals and projects?
4.  **Motivation**. Many of the people who have the hardest time taking action have the most reason to change. Someone leading a grossly unhealthy lifestyle will benefit the most from a small intervention, compared to the hyper-fit trying to lower bodyfat percentage by another 1%. If you define motivation as objectively having a good reason to act, then there’s still a big gap in action that needs explaining. If, instead, you define motivation as a subjective feeling, then we’re simply back to our original puzzle: why do some people feel motivated to do the things they should while others don’t?

My own thinking about the exact causes of inaction aren’t fully developed yet, but I want to try to flesh out some possibilities here, as well as give myself some directions for digging in further.

## Possibility #1: Confidence

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/motivation-from-expectation.png)

A popular class of theories related to goal-setting are those known as [expectancy theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expectancy_theory). These are essentially theories that say your motivation to complete a task depends both on the value of the reward you anticipate receiving, as well as your expectation that you’ll actually get that reward.

Your expectations of success, however, also depends on your motivation. This creates a feedback loop where your own expectation of your ability to sustain motivation long-term influences your expectation of success, thus influencing your motivation long-term.

A positive feedback loop can create a system where there are two possible dynamics: an accelerating commitment as you think success is more and more likely, and thus get more and more motivated, as well as a spiral of lowered expectations as you get increasingly discouraged.

Since your expectations of one pursuit often influence future pursuits, this is also something that might stabilize into a general response of doing or inaction in your life. If your projects tend to fail, your expectations are low and motivation withers. If your projects tend to succeed, your expectations go up and motivation stays strong.

## Possibility #2: Social-Desirability Bias

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/consciousness-as-pr.png)

One view of the conscious mind is that it functions more as the brain’s public relations manager than as its CEO, confabulating reasonable-sounding explanations for its behavior rather than actually calling the shots.

If so, this suggests that many of our failures of action are intentional. We fail to take action because the unconscious parts of our mind that drive our behavior have decided not to take action.

Where this gets interesting as a possible explanation, however, is that sometimes inaction [isn’t socially acceptable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_desirability_bias). Thus, we need to feign taking action in order to suggest to others that we do care, even when we don’t. This pretending may even be [completely unconscious](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2018/03/06/book-club-the-elephant-in-the-brain-february-2018/). The easiest way to lie is to believe you’re telling the truth, so you may even convince yourself you want to pursue a goal when really your unconscious mind is not committed to it.

This might manifest itself in the student who procrastinates on his homework (but doesn’t know why) because he doesn’t really want to study, but has to get the approval and financial support of his parents. Inner friction and anguish may be the price he pays for needing to appear like he’s trying to take action.

## Possibility #3: Daydreams Feel Different From Reality

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/theory-vs-reality.png)

[Construal level theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construal_level_theory) is the idea that we have two characteristic modes of viewing things—an abstract (or far-mode) and a concrete (or near-mode) view.

Many big goals have a far-near incompatibility which can make them hard to take action on. You might think about your fitness goal in terms of losing weight, being healthy and looking great (all abstract, idealized goals). Yet, when you go to the gym you mostly think about how hard you’re breathing, the sweat dripping down your face and how uncomfortable it makes you.

Chronic issues of starting and stopping difficult projects likely depend on this incompatibility of mental states. The person who dreams up the goal is different from the person who executes on it, and coordinating those two versions of yourself may be hard.

## Possibility #4: Don’t Stick Your Neck Out

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/culture-props-action.png)

It may be that our hardwiring for ambition itself is based on our environment. Many of our ancestors lived in times when standing out, taking actions that go beyond cultural expectations could get you killed or exiled. Our natures, then, may be trying to sniff out the cost-benefits of taking actions, willing to retreat to placid conformity in case those early ventures get punished.

This might make for a theory of motivation that says, “do whatever your culture requires you to do,” with ventures into different kinds of actions being strongly discouraged if they don’t yield big rewards.

This may also explain why inaction seems to happen with some areas of life (starting your own business), but not others (showing up to work on time). In the second case, there’s a strong cultural expectation that everybody needs to show up on time to work, whereas nobody will blame you for not starting a company.

Our inner struggles may be our unconscious minds trying to find the frontier of action-taking that we’re best suited for, adjusting our ambitions and overall tendency to act based on feedback from the environment.

## Possibility #5: We’re Too Short-Sighted

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/now-vs-future.png)

It may also be simply that our modern environment, which is peaceful, stable and allows for long-term accumulation of skills and resources, is quite different from the one we’ve evolved to survive in.

In particular, human beings [exhibit inconsistent time preferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbolic_discounting) for satisfaction here and now, versus long-term rewards in the future. Procrastination may be a delaying tactic to avoid wasting energy here and now, even if you think you might work harder in the not-so-far future.

The ability to accumulate resources likely only started after the Neolithic revolution, where grain agriculture allowed some to save (and steal) and where long-term planning could actually result in evolutionary success.

This may be too short a time-scale to effectively modify our innate psychology, but it may have allowed us to create cultural institutions that counteract our typical short-sightedness. Since, once again, inaction is most prevalent when we’re facing activities without strong cultural pressures, this may suggest that our gap of inaction is due to defaulting to our ancestral, myopic approach to life.

## How Can We Get Better at Taking Action?

This subject, why we struggle to take action and how we can improve it, is one that fascinates me. Both because it seems so simple on the surface (“Just do it.”) but so complicated underneath (what do you do when “Just do it” doesn’t work?).

Traditional approaches to this problem have often focused on human will as single thing. But the reality is that our minds are fabulously complicated things, with many different integrating control mechanisms, both conscious and unconscious. To take action, then, you need to not only have new inputs to your conscious mind to nudge you in the right direction, but those nudges need to translate into all the other control systems you possess to keep you headed in that direction.

I have a lot of remaining questions:

-   Are there different types of inaction, or do they all have a similar core explanation?
-   What separates everyday inaction from the clinical difficulties people who suffer depression or anxiety face?
    -   Are these simply extreme versions of the slumps and avoidance we all face, or are they separate mechanisms?
-   What points in the system are most flexible for adjusting long-term action for the least amount of effort?

As I learn more, I’ll do my best to share it with you.
