# 227 The Origins of Wokeness

## 阅读前置卡

- 价值判断：高。文章对 wokeness 的起源、机制与应对提出了系统性的因果解释和可操作建议（如以宗教习俗为类比、对异端定义的抗体），并给出可检验的预测（如取消文化随社交媒体兴起、2020 年前后达到峰值），对理解当代文化与制度变迁有较高参考价值。
- 信息密度判断：高。全文以论点—机制—例证—脚注的方式密集推进，几乎每段都引入新的因果环节或反例（如 1960 年代学生运动为何未产生 PC、纸媒地理市场与中立性、DEI 官僚的激励），信息点密集且相互关联。
- 是否继续阅读：4 精细阅读。论证线索清晰、层层递进，从定义到起源再到机制与对策，且包含大量可继续追踪的脚注与概念（如 prig、orthodoxy 替代美德、取消 mob），阅读动力强。

## 核心洞察

一、prig 的古今同构：文章用“prig”这一 18 世纪词汇把 wokeness 放进更长的历史序列：每个社会都存在一类被浅薄而严苛的道德纯洁吸引的人，他们通过攻击违规者来展示纯洁。变化的不是这类人的存在，而是他们执行的规则内容——维多利亚英格兰是基督教美德，斯大林俄国是正统马列主义，而当代 woke 则是社会正义。
[[#^pr-6b9ece318d2c|正文跳转]]

二、定义：攻击性表演式社会正义：文章把 political correctness 与 wokeness 定义为同一种东西：对社会正义的攻击性表演式关注。真正的问题被定位在“表演性”而非社会正义本身；种族主义等被承认为真实问题，但批评指向的是以浅薄而攻击性的方式处理边缘群体议题，例如专注于让人因用词不当而陷入麻烦。
[[#^pr-f744201b0cff|正文跳转]]

三、起源地：人文与社会科学：文章推断 political correctness 并非从大学外部传入，而是在大学内部最极端；也并非从数学、硬科学或工程开始，而是从人文与社会科学开始。原因是这些领域为注入政治提供了更大空间：物理教授仍可抗议但政治信念不影响工作，而社会学与现代文学研究可以随研究者意愿政治化。
[[#^pr-1126352ea5ec|正文跳转]]

四、1960 年代为何没有直接产生 PC：文章提出一个成功理论必须解释为何 PC 没有更早发生。1960 年代学生抗议没有导致 PC，恰恰因为它们是学生运动，没有真正权力；学生可以谈论妇女解放和黑人权力，但那不是课堂上被教授的内容。直到 1970 年代初，当年的抗议者完成博士论文并被聘为教授，才逐渐同时获得人数与权力。
[[#^pr-4da00ebd193a|正文跳转]]

五、从抗议到惩罚的转折：文章以亲历者视角标记 1988 年前后为转折点：抗议变成惩罚，反对男性沙文主义的抗议转变为向大学当局正式投诉性别歧视。关键机制是 1960 年代激进者获得终身教职，变成二十年前他们抗议的建制，从而不仅能公开谈论理念，还能执行理念；新道德规则允许学生攻击教授，类似文化大革命中动员年轻一代对付政治对手。
[[#^pr-94e04386be0d|正文跳转]]

六、正统作为美德的替代品：文章指出，当社会存在异端与正统概念时，正统就成为美德的替代品：一个人可以是世界上最坏的人，但只要正统就比所有不正统者更好，这使正统对坏人极具吸引力。要让这种替代品有效，正统必须困难；PC 的规则浅薄、复杂且频繁变化，正好成为真正美德的完美替代品，导致跟不上道德时尚的好人被品格堪忧者打倒。
[[#^pr-8cd008acb3d1|正文跳转]]

七、道德执行者的供给缺口：文章提出一个促成因素：到 1980 年代，在文化精英中宗教与性方面的道德纯洁已近乎死信，做宗教徒或处女往往需要隐瞒而非宣扬。因此天生喜欢充当道德执行者的人极度缺乏可执行之物，一套新的规则正是他们等待的东西。1960 年代左派宽容的一面反而帮助创造了不宽容一面盛行的条件。
[[#^pr-5d91368241ec|正文跳转]]

八、苏联解体与马克思主义失宠：文章把苏联帝国垮台列为另一个可能促成因素：在 PC 作为竞争者出现之前，马克思主义一直是左派道德纯洁的热门焦点，但东欧亲民主运动使其失去大部分光彩，尤其是 1989 年柏林墙倒塌。作者回忆在剑桥二手书店看着死气沉沉的苏联研究区，心想那些人接下来会唠叨什么，答案就在眼皮底下。
[[#^pr-a2ffadfa857a|正文跳转]]

九、敌意环境标准扩大禁忌半径：文章指出 PC 第一阶段在女性中更受欢迎，部分因为 1980 年代中期性骚扰定义从明确性挑逗扩展到制造敌意环境。大学内典型指控是女学生说某教授让她感到不舒服，这种模糊性把禁止行为半径扩大到谈论异端观点，因为那些也会让人不舒服。达尔文男性变异更大假说导致 Larry Summers 被赶下哈佛校长职位即为例证。
[[#^pr-6e871c59bb66|正文跳转]]

十、幽默的消毒作用与幻觉：文章认为 PC 在 1990 年代后半期燃尽，一个主要原因是它字面上变成笑话，为喜剧演员提供素材，而幽默是对付装腔作势最强大的武器之一，因为 prigs 没有幽默感、无法以同样方式回应。但作者随即指出这是一种幻觉：大学内部余烬仍在燃烧，创造它的力量仍在，教授成为院长和系主任，还出现专注社会正义的新系所与行政人员爆炸。
[[#^pr-4518bd4558ad|正文跳转]]

十一、社交媒体与愤怒的流行：文章把 PC 第二波归因于社交媒体兴起，尤其是 Tumblr 和 Twitter，其显著特征是取消 mob。作者以自己 2007 至 2014 年运营论坛的经验量化：如果某件事让用户感到愤怒，它被点赞的可能性大约高出三倍。愤怒并非愉快感觉，人们却寻求并分享它，这种倾向不是 wokeness 造成，而是这一代社交媒体的固有特征，却使其成为煽动 wokeness 的完美机制。
[[#^pr-f05f69c7a143|正文跳转]]

十二、群聊使 mob 自然形成：文章强调推动 wokeness 的不只是公共社交网络，群聊应用也至关重要，尤其在取消的最后一步。如果一群试图让某人被解雇的员工只能用电子邮件，组织 mob 会很困难；但一旦有了群聊，mobs 就会自然形成。
[[#^pr-04b15a7ea908|正文跳转]]

十三、纸媒中立性的地理约束：文章提出报纸的中立性主要是印刷时代地理市场约束的产物：在纽约时报投放广告的百货公司想触达自由派与保守派所有人，所以时报必须同时服务两者，并把中立视为纸媒记录者的职责。在线出版使报纸能够、甚至被迫转向服务由意识形态而非地理定义的市場，多数报纸倒向左派，同时右派也出现自己的新闻业，新闻业从伟大的集中力量变成伟大的极化力量之一。
[[#^pr-5d3a40ec0566|正文跳转]]

十四、社交媒体与新闻的廉价点击循环：文章描述一种新新闻变体：某人在社交媒体上说争议言论，几小时内变成新闻故事，愤怒读者再在社交媒体发布链接推动进一步争论。这是最廉价的点击来源，无需维持海外分社或支付数月调查，只需盯着 Twitter 找争议言论并转载加评论激怒读者。社交媒体兴起与新闻极化相互强化。
[[#^pr-07a1efb33a00|正文跳转]]

十五、职业化官僚与 DEI 激励：文章指出 PC 两波最大差异之一是第二波常由专业人士驱动，出现新的行政阶层，其工作基本是执行 wokeness，类似苏联政治委员，常可通过头衔中的 inclusion 辨认。这些官僚追求 woke 议程如同工作取决于此，因为确实如此：雇人监视某类问题，他们就会找到它，否则存在没有正当理由；他们还参与招聘，试图确保雇主只雇政治信念相同者，最恶劣案例是大学要求教职候选人提交 DEI 声明作为初筛。
[[#^pr-6642cf20cfc1|正文跳转]]

十六、病毒式传播的循环机制：文章把 wokeness 描述为心智病毒，其病毒性来自定义新类型的不当行为。多数人害怕不当，从不完全确定社会规则，尤其规则变化快时；若被告知违反某条未知规则，默认反应是相信，尤其多人这样说。这形成指数增长配方：狂热者发明新不当，同类采纳以展示美德，足够多后更大群体因恐惧跟随，新不当牢固确立，并提高规则变化速度，加速循环。
[[#^pr-568dcbfb5497|正文跳转]]

十七、组织比个人更易被俘获：文章指出对个人成立的机制对组织更甚，尤其没有强大领导者的组织。这类组织一切基于最佳实践，没有更高权威；若新最佳实践达到临界规模，就必须采纳，且不能像不确定时那样拖延，因为它可能此刻就在犯不当。因此一小群狂热者通过描述组织可能犯的新不当来俘获它出奇容易。
[[#^pr-2576bde1b0d0|正文跳转]]

十八、以宗教习俗为处理模板：文章提出应对 wokeness 的简单有原则方式：沿用处理宗教的既有习俗。Wokeness 实际上是一种宗教，只是上帝被受保护群体取代，马克思主义也有类似形式。你可以表达宗教身份并解释信仰，但若同事不同意，不能称他们为异教徒，不能禁止他们说出与教义矛盾的话，也不能坚持组织采纳你的宗教为官方宗教。
[[#^pr-1792ac8104bb|正文跳转]]

十九、抗体：对抗异端定义：文章认为无法摆脱攻击性循规蹈矩者，也无法阻止新意识形态吸引他们，因此必须在下游一步把他们封住。他们横冲直撞时总会做一件事暴露自己：定义新的异端来惩罚人。因此保护自己免受未来类似爆发的最佳方式，是拥有对抗异端这一概念的强大抗体，对定义新形式异端保持有意识的偏见，每当有人试图禁止我们以前能说的话，初始假设应是他们错了，举证责任在他们。
[[#^pr-183d0f3f61fb|正文跳转]]

二十、衡量标准：可说真话不减少：文章给出一个简洁的衡量标准：我们不能说的真话的数量不应该增加；如果它增加了，那就是出了问题。只要攻击性循规蹈矩者继续通过禁止异端暴露自己，我们就能注意到他们何时在某种新意识形态背后对齐，若总在此时反击，运气好的话就能当场阻止他们。
[[#^pr-d6e2ba5f2727|正文跳转]]

# 227 The Origins of Wokeness

January 2025

“prig”这个词现在不太常见，但如果你查一下它的定义，听起来会很熟悉。Google 的定义就不错：

> 一个 self-righteously moralistic person，其 behaves as if superior to others。

这个词的这个含义起源于18世纪，而它的年代是一条重要线索：它表明，尽管 wokeness 是一个相对晚近的现象，它却是一个古老得多的现象的实例。

有某一类人，会被一种浅薄而严苛的 moral purity 所吸引，并通过攻击任何违反规则的人来展示自己的 purity。每个社会都有这样的人。唯一变化的是他们所执行的规则。在 Victorian England，那是 Christian virtue。在 Stalin 的 Russia，那是 orthodox Marxism-Leninism。对于 the woke 来说，那是 social justice。

^pr-6b9ece318d2c

所以，如果你想理解 wokeness，要问的问题不是人们为什么这样行事。每个社会都有 prigs。要问的问题是，为什么我们的 prigs 在这些 ideas 上、在这个时刻如此 priggish。而要回答这个问题，我们就必须问 wokeness 是何时、何地开始的。

第一个问题的答案是1980年代。Wokeness 是 political correctness 的第二波、更具攻击性的浪潮，它始于1980年代末，在1990年代末平息，然后在2010年代初卷土重来，最终在2020年的 riots 之后达到顶峰。

political correctness 到底是什么？经常有人要求我定义这个 term 和 wokeness，这些人认为它们是无意义的 labels，所以我就来定义。它们有相同的定义：

> 一种对 social justice 的 aggressively performative focus。

^pr-f744201b0cff

换句话说，就是人们在 social justice 上做 prigs。而这才是真正的问题——the performativeness，而不是 social justice。[1](#fn1)

例如，Racism 是一个真实的问题。不是 the woke 所相信的那种规模的问题，但是一个真实的问题。我不认为任何通情达理的人会否认这一点。political correctness 的问题不在于它关注 marginalized groups，而在于它这样做的方式浅薄而具有攻击性。politically correct 的人不是走出去、安静地帮助 marginalized groups 的成员，而是专注于让人们因为用错词谈论他们而陷入麻烦。

至于 political correctness 是从哪里开始的，如果你想一想，你大概已经知道答案了。它是从 universities 之外开始、然后从这个外部来源传播到 universities 的吗？显然不是；它在 universities 里一直是最极端的。那么它在 universities 里的哪里开始？它是从 math、或 the hard sciences、或 engineering 开始，然后从那里传播到 the humanities and social sciences 的吗？这些是些有趣的画面，但不，显然它是从 the humanities and social sciences 开始的。

为什么是那里？又为什么是那时？1980年代在 the humanities and social sciences 里发生了什么？

一个成功的关于 political correctness 起源的理论，必须能够解释为什么它没有更早发生。例如，为什么它没有在1960年代的 protest movements 期间发生？它们所关心的是大致相同的问题。[2](#fn2)

1960年代的 student protests 没有导致 political correctness，原因恰恰就在于此——它们是 student movements。它们没有任何真正的权力。学生们也许大谈 women’s liberation 和 black power，但那不是他们在课堂上被教授的内容。还不是。

但在1970年代初，1960年代的 student protestors 开始完成他们的 dissertations 并被聘为 professors。起初他们既没有权力，人数也不多。但随着更多同龄人加入他们、上一代 professors 开始退休，他们逐渐两者兼具。

^pr-4da00ebd193a

political correctness 之所以从 the humanities and social sciences 开始，是因为这些 fields 为注入 politics 提供了更大的空间。一个1960年代的 radical 如果得到一份 physics professor 的工作，仍然可以参加 protests，但他的 political beliefs 不会影响他的工作。而 sociology 和 modern literature 中的 research 则可以随你所愿地变得政治化。[3](#fn3)

^pr-1126352ea5ec

我目睹了 political correctness 的兴起。1982年我开始上大学时，它还不是个东西。如果有人说了一些女学生认为 sexist 的话，她们可能会反对，但没有人会因此被 _reported_。1986年我开始读 grad school 时，它仍然不是个东西。但到1988年它肯定是个东西了，而到1990年代初，它似乎弥漫于 campus life。

发生了什么？protest 是如何变成 punishment 的？为什么1980年代末是反对 male chauvinism（过去是这么叫的）的 protests 转变为向 university authorities 正式投诉 sexism 的那个节点？基本上，1960年代的 radicals 拿到了 tenure。他们变成了二十年前他们所抗议的 Establishment。现在他们所处的地位，不仅能公开谈论他们的 ideas，还能执行它们。

^pr-94e04386be0d

一套可供执行的新 moral rules，对某一类学生来说是激动人心的消息。让它尤其激动人心的是，他们被允许攻击 professors。我记得当时注意到了 political correctness 的这一方面。它不单纯是一场 grass-roots student movement。它是 faculty members 鼓励学生攻击其他 faculty members。在这一点上，它就像 the Cultural Revolution。那也不是一场 grass-roots movement；那是 Mao 把年轻一代放出来对付他的 political opponents。而事实上，当 Roderick MacFarquhar 于1980年代末在 Harvard 开始教授一门关于 the Cultural Revolution 的课时，许多人把它看作对时事的评论。我不知道它实际上是不是，但人们认为它是，这意味着相似之处是显而易见的。[4](#fn4)

College students larp。这是他们的天性。通常无害。但 larping morality 结果成了一种有毒的组合。其结果是一种 moral etiquette，肤浅但非常复杂。想象一下，你必须向一位来自另一个星球的善意访客解释，为什么使用“people of color”这个短语被认为特别开明，但说“colored people”会让你被解雇。以及为什么现在恰恰不该使用“negro”这个词，尽管 Martin Luther King 在他的 speeches 中不断使用它。没有任何 underlying principles。你只能给他一长串要记住的 rules。[5](#fn5)

这些 rules 的危险不仅在于它们为不警觉的人制造了 land mines，还在于它们的繁复使它们成为 virtue 的有效替代品。每当一个社会有 heresy 和 orthodoxy 的概念时，orthodoxy 就成为 virtue 的替代品。你可以是世界上最坏的人，但只要你是 orthodox，你就比所有不是的人都好。这使 orthodoxy 对坏人非常有吸引力。

但要让它作为 virtue 的替代品起作用，orthodoxy 必须是困难的。如果成为 orthodox 所要做的只是穿某件衣服或避免说某个词，人人都知道去做，那么显得比别人更有 virtue 的唯一方式就是真正有 virtue。political correctness 那些浅薄、复杂且频繁变化的 rules 使它成为 actual virtue 的完美替代品。其结果是一个这样的世界：跟不上当前 moral fashions 的好人被那些如果你能看到其品格就会让你惊恐退缩的人打倒。

^pr-8cd008acb3d1

political correctness 兴起的一个重大促成因素是缺乏其他可供 moral purity 的东西。前几代 prigs 主要是对 religion 和 sex 做 prigs。但到1980年代，在 cultural elite 中，这些是最死寂的 dead letters；如果你是 religious，或是 virgin，这往往是你倾向于隐瞒而不是宣扬的事。所以那种喜欢当 moral enforcers 的人，已经变得极度缺乏可供执行的东西。一套新的 rules 正是他们一直在等待的。

^pr-5d91368241ec

说来奇怪，1960年代 left 中宽容的那一面，帮助创造了不宽容的那一面得以盛行的条件。老派、随和的 hippy left 所倡导的宽松 social rules 成了主导的 rules，至少在 elite 中是如此，而这让天生不宽容的人没有什么可供不宽容的了。

另一个可能的促成因素是 Soviet empire 的垮台。在 political correctness 作为竞争者出现之前，Marxism 一直是 left 上 moral purity 的一个热门焦点，但 Eastern Bloc 国家的 pro-democracy movements 使它失去了大部分光彩。尤其是1989年 Berlin Wall 的倒塌。你不能站在 Stasi 一边。我记得1980年代末在 Cambridge 一家二手书店里看着死气沉沉的 Soviet Studies 区，心想“那些人接下来会唠叨些什么呢？”结果答案是就在我眼皮底下。

^pr-a2ffadfa857a

我当时注意到的关于 political correctness 第一阶段的一件事是，它在 women 中比在 men 中更受欢迎。正如许多 writers（也许最雄辩的是 George Orwell）所观察到的，women 似乎比 men 更被成为 moral enforcers 的想法所吸引。但 women 倾向于成为 political correctness 的 enforcers 还有另一个更具体的原因。当时有一股对 sexual harassment 的巨大反弹；1980年代中期正是 sexual harassment 的定义从明确的 sexual advances 扩展到制造“hostile environment”的那个节点。在 universities 内，典型的指控形式是（female）student 说某位 professor 让她“feel uncomfortable”。但这种指控的模糊性使 forbidden behavior 的半径扩大到包括谈论 heterodox ideas。那些也会让人不舒服。[6](#fn6)

^pr-6e871c59bb66

提出 Darwin 的 greater male variability hypothesis 也许能解释 human performance 中的一些 variation，这算 sexist 吗？显然，sexist 到足以让 Larry Summers 被赶下 Harvard 校长的位置。一位听了他在其中提到这个 idea 的 talk 的 woman 说，这让她感到“physically ill”，她不得不中途离场。如果 hostile environment 的检验标准是它让人感觉如何，这听起来确实像是一个。然而，greater male variability 解释 human performance 中一些 variation 似乎确实合理。那么哪一个应该占上风，comfort 还是 truth？如果 truth 应该在任何地方占上风，那当然应该是在 universities；那本该是它们的专长；但从1980年代末开始的几十年里，politically correct 的人试图假装这种冲突不存在。[7](#fn7)

political correctness 似乎在1990年代后半期燃尽了。一个原因，也许是主要原因，是它字面上变成了一个笑话。它为 comedians 提供了丰富的素材，而 comedians 对它施展了他们惯常的消毒作用。Humor 是对付任何形式的 priggishness 的最强大武器之一，因为 prigs 没有幽默感，无法以同样的方式回应。Humor 是击败 Victorian prudishness 的东西，而到2000年，它似乎对 political correctness 也做了同样的事。

^pr-4518bd4558ad

不幸的是，这是一种幻觉。在 universities 内，political correctness 的余烬仍在明亮地燃烧。毕竟，创造它的那些力量仍然在那里。发起它的那些 professors 现在正成为 deans 和 department heads。而且除了他们的 departments 之外，现在还出现了一堆明确专注于 social justice 的新 departments。Students 仍然渴望有可供 moral purity 的东西。而且 university administrators 的数量出现了爆炸式增长，其中许多人的工作涉及执行各种形式的 political correctness。

在 2010 年代早期，political correctness 的余烬重新燃起。这个新阶段与最初的那个阶段之间有几处不同。它更加 virulent。它进一步蔓延到现实世界中，尽管它在 universities 内部仍然燃烧得最旺。而且它牵涉到更多种类的 sins。在 political correctness 的第一个阶段，人们实际上只会被指控三件事：sexism、racism 和 homophobia（当时这是为此目的而发明的一个 neologism）。但从那时到 2010 年之间，很多人花了很多时间试图发明新种类的 -isms 和 -phobias，并看看哪些能够 stick。

第二个阶段在多重意义上是 political correctness metastasized。它为什么在那个时候发生？我的猜测是，这是由于 social media 的兴起，尤其是 Tumblr 和 Twitter，因为 political correctness 第二波最显著的特征之一就是 _cancel mob_：一群愤怒的人在 social media 上联合起来，让某人被 ostracized 或被 fired。事实上，political correctness 的第二波最初被称为 “cancel culture”；直到 2020 年代它才开始被称为 “wokeness”。

social media 有一个方面起初几乎让所有人都感到惊讶，那就是 outrage 的流行。用户似乎 _like_ being outraged。我们现在对这个想法如此习惯，以至于把它视为理所当然，但它其实相当奇怪。Being outraged 并不是一种愉快的感觉。你不会指望人们去寻求它。但他们确实会。而且最重要的是，他们想要分享它。我碰巧在 2007 年到 2014 年运营一个 forum，所以我实际上可以量化他们有多想分享它：如果某件事让他们感到 outraged，我们的用户给它 upvote 的可能性大约高出三倍。

^pr-f05f69c7a143

这种向 outrage 的倾斜并不是由于 wokeness。它是 social media 的一个固有特征，或者至少是这一代 social media 的特征。但它确实使 social media 成为煽动 wokeness 火焰的完美机制。[8](#fn8)

不过，推动 wokeness 兴起的并不只是公共 social networks。Group chat apps 也至关重要，尤其是在最后一步，cancellation。想象一下，如果一群试图让某人被 fired 的 employees 只能使用 email 来做这件事。组织一群 mob 会很困难。但一旦你有了 group chat，mobs 就会自然形成。

^pr-04b15a7ea908

political correctness 第二波中的另一个促成因素是 the press 的 polarization 急剧增加。在 print era，newspapers 被迫保持，或至少看起来保持，politically neutral。在 New York Times 上投放广告的 department stores 想要触达该地区的所有人，无论是 liberal 还是 conservative，所以 the Times 必须同时服务两者。但 the Times 并不把这种 neutrality 视为强加给他们的东西。他们把它作为自己作为 _paper of record_ 的职责来拥抱——作为旨在成为其时代编年史的大 newspapers 之一，从一个 neutral point of view 报道每一个足够重要的 story。

在我成长的过程中，papers of record 似乎是永恒的、几乎神圣的制度。像 New York Times 和 Washington Post 这样的 papers 拥有巨大的 prestige，部分是因为其他 news 来源有限，但也因为它们确实做出了一些努力来保持 neutral。

不幸的是，事实证明 paper of record 主要是 print 所施加的约束的产物。[9](#fn9) 当你的 market 由 geography 决定时，你必须保持 neutral。但在线出版使 newspapers 能够——事实上可能迫使它们——转向服务于由 ideology 而非 geography 定义的 markets。大多数仍在营业的 newspapers 都倒向了它们本就倾向的方向：left。2020 年 10 月 11 日，New York Times 宣布：“The paper is in the midst of an evolution from the stodgy paper of record into a juicy collection of great narratives。”[10](#fn10) 与此同时，某种意义上的 journalists 也兴起以服务 the right。于是 journalism，在上一个时代曾是伟大的 centralizing forces 之一，现在变成了伟大的 polarizing ones 之一。

^pr-5d3a40ec0566

social media 的兴起与 journalism 日益加剧的 polarization 相互强化。事实上，出现了一种新的 journalism 变体，其中包含一个通过 social media 的 loop。某人会在 social media 上说一些 controversial 的话。几小时内它就会变成一个 news story。愤怒的 readers 随后会在 social media 上发布指向该 story 的链接，在网上推动进一步的争论。这是可以想象到的最廉价的 clicks 来源。你不必维持 overseas news bureaus，也不必为长达数月的 investigations 付费。你所要做的只是盯着 Twitter 寻找 controversial remarks，然后把它们 repost 到你的网站上，再加上一些 additional comments 来进一步激怒 readers。

^pr-07a1efb33a00

对 the press 来说，wokeness 里有钱可赚。但他们并不是唯一从中获利的人。这是 political correctness 两波之间最大的差异之一：第一波几乎完全由 amateurs 驱动，但第二波往往由 professionals 驱动。对一些人来说，这就是他们的全部工作。到 2010 年，已经兴起了一个新的 administrators 阶层，他们的工作基本上就是 enforce wokeness。他们扮演的角色类似于苏联时期被派到军事和工业组织中的 political commissars：他们并不直接处于组织工作的流程之中，而是从旁观察，以确保在做这件事的过程中不会发生任何 improper 的事。这些新的 administrators 往往可以通过其头衔中的 “inclusion” 一词来辨认。在 institutions 内部，这是 wokeness 的首选 euphemism；例如，一份新的 banned words 清单通常会被称作 “inclusive language guide”。[11](#fn11)

这个新的 bureaucrats 阶层追求一个 woke agenda，就好像他们的工作取决于此，因为确实如此。如果你雇人来监视某一类问题，他们就会找到它，因为否则他们的存在就没有正当理由。[12](#fn12) 但这些 bureaucrats 还代表着第二种、甚至可能更大的危险。许多人参与了 hiring，并且在可能的时候，他们试图确保其雇主只雇用那些与他们持有相同 political beliefs 的人。最恶劣的案例是一些 universities 开始要求 faculty candidates 提交的新 “DEI statements”，以证明他们对 wokeness 的 commitment。一些 universities 把这些 statements 用作 initial filter，只考虑在这些 statements 上得分足够高的 candidates。你那样是雇不到 Einstein 的；想象一下你反而会得到什么。

^pr-6642cf20cfc1

wokeness 兴起中的另一个因素是 Black Lives Matter movement，它始于 2013 年，当时一名白人男子在 Florida 杀死一名黑人青少年后被 acquitted。但这并没有 launch wokeness；到 2013 年时它已经进行得很顺利了。

Me Too Movement 也是如此，它在 2017 年关于 Harvey Weinstein 强奸女性历史的首批 news stories 之后迅速兴起。它加速了 wokeness，但在 launch 它方面并没有扮演 80 年代那一版在 launch political correctness 时所扮演的同样角色。

Donald Trump 在 2016 年的 election 也加速了 wokeness，尤其是在 the press 中，在那里 outrage 现在意味着 traffic。Trump 让 New York Times 赚了很多钱：在他第一个 administration 期间，headlines 提到他名字的频率大约是前几任 presidents 的四倍。

在 2020 年，我们看到了最大的 accelerant，当时一名白人 police officer 在视频中使一名黑人 suspect 窒息而死。此时，隐喻性的火变成了字面意义上的火，因为暴力 protests 在全美爆发。但事后看来，这被证明是 peak woke，或者接近 peak。就我所见过的每一项衡量标准而言，wokeness 在 2020 年或 2021 年达到顶峰。

wokeness 有时被描述为一种 mind-virus。使它具有 viral 性的是，它定义了新类型的 impropriety。大多数人都害怕 impropriety；他们从来都不完全确定 social rules 是什么，或者自己可能正在违反哪些 rules。尤其是当 rules 变化很快时。而且由于大多数人本来就担心自己可能正在违反他们不知道的 rules，如果你告诉他们正在违反某条 rule，他们的默认反应就是相信你。尤其是如果有多个人告诉他们。这反过来又是 exponential growth 的配方。Zealots 发明一些新的 impropriety 来避免。最先采纳它的是 fellow zealots，他们渴望有新的方式来 signal their virtue。如果有足够多这样的人，最初的 zealots 群体之后就会跟着一个更大的群体，其动机是 fear。他们并不是试图 signal virtue；他们只是试图避免惹上麻烦。此时，新的 impropriety 已经牢固确立。此外，它的成功提高了 social rules 的变化速度，记住，这正是人们对自己可能违反哪些 rules 感到紧张的原因之一。于是这个 cycle 加速了。[13](#fn13)

^pr-568dcbfb5497

对 individuals 来说成立的事，对 organizations 来说更是如此。尤其是没有强大 leader 的 organizations。这类 organizations 一切都基于 “best practices” 来做。没有更高的权威；如果某种新的 “best practice” 达到 critical mass，他们 _must_ adopt it。而在这种情况下，organization 不能做它在不确定时通常做的事：delay。它可能此刻就在犯 improprieties！所以，一小群 zealots 通过描述这类 organization 可能犯下的新 improprieties 来 capture 它，是出奇地容易。[14](#fn14)

^pr-2576bde1b0d0

这种 cycle 究竟如何结束？它最终会导致 disaster，人们开始说够了。2020 年的 excesses 让很多人这么说。

自那以后，wokeness 一直在逐渐但持续地 retreat。Corporate CEOs，从 Brian Armstrong 开始，已经公开 reject 它。Universities，以 University of Chicago 和 MIT 为首，已经明确确认了它们对 free speech 的 commitment。Twitter，可以说是 wokeness 的 hub，被 Elon Musk 收购以 neutralize 它，而他似乎成功了——顺便说一句，并不是通过像 Twitter 过去 censor right-wing users 那样 censor left-wing users，而是两者都不 censor。[15](#fn15) Consumers 已经断然 reject 了那些在 wokeness 中走得太远的 brands。Bud Light brand 可能因此受到了永久性损害。我不会声称 Trump 在 2024 年的第二次胜利是对 wokeness 的 referendum；我认为他赢了，就像 presidential candidates 总是会赢那样，因为他更 [_charismatic_](https://paulgraham.com/charisma.html)；但 voters 对 wokeness 的 disgust 一定起了作用。

那么我们现在该怎么办？wokeness 已经在 retreat。显然我们应该帮助它继续下去。最好的办法是什么？更重要的是，我们如何避免第三次 outbreak？毕竟，它似乎曾经死过一次，但回来时比以往更糟。

事实上，还有一个更雄心勃勃的目标：有没有办法防止未来任何类似的 aggressively performative moralism 的 outbreak——不只是 political correctness 的第三次 outbreak，而是下一个类似的东西？因为一定会有下一个东西。Prigs 天生就是 prigs。他们需要 rules 来 obey 和 enforce，而既然 Darwin 已经切断了他们传统的 rules 供应，他们就不断渴望新的 rules。他们所需要的只是有人通过定义一种新的方式来做 morally pure 来与他们相向而行，然后我们就会再次看到同样的 phenomenon。

让我们从较容易的问题开始。是否存在一种简单、有原则的方式来应对 wokeness？我认为有：沿用我们已有的处理 religion 的 customs。Wokeness 实际上就是一种 religion，只不过 God 被 protected classes 取代了。它甚至不是第一个这类 religion；Marxism 也有类似的形式，God 被 the masses 取代了。[16](#fn16) 而且我们已经有 well-established customs 来处理组织内部的 religion。你可以表达自己的 religious identity 并解释你的 beliefs，但如果你的 coworkers 不同意，你不能称他们为 infidels，也不能试图禁止他们说出与其 doctrines 相矛盾的话，或者坚持要求组织将你的 religion 采纳为 official religion。

^pr-1792ac8104bb

如果我们不确定该如何应对 wokeness 的任何特定 manifestation，那就想象我们面对的是某种其他 religion，比如 Christianity。我们是否应该在组织内部设置一些人，其工作就是 enforce woke orthodoxy？不，因为我们不会设置一些人，其工作是 enforce Christian orthodoxy。我们是否应该 censor 那些其工作与 woke doctrines 相矛盾的 [_writers_](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/2023/02/17/roald-dahl-books-%20rewritten-offensive-matilda-witches-twits/) 或 [_scientists_](https://x.com/KoustaStavroula/status/1562034502897106946)？不，因为我们不会这样对待那些其工作与 Christian teachings 相矛盾的人。是否应该要求 job candidates 写 DEI statements？当然不；想象一下雇主要求提供一个人的 religious beliefs 的证明。是否应该要求学生和员工参加 woke indoctrination sessions，在其中他们被要求回答关于自己 beliefs 的问题以确保 compliance？不，因为我们绝不会梦想以这种方式对人们的 religion 进行 catechizing。[17](#fn17)

一个人不应该因为不想看 woke movies 而感到难过，就像一个人不会因为不想听 Christian rock 而感到难过一样。在我二十多岁时，我曾多次开车穿越 America，听着当地的 radio stations。偶尔我会转动旋钮，听到一些新歌。但一旦有人提到 Jesus，我就会再次转动旋钮。哪怕是最微小的一点被 preached to，也足以让我失去兴趣。

但同样地，我们不应该自动拒绝 the woke 所相信的一切。我不是一个 Christian，但我能看出许多 Christian principles 是好的。仅仅因为一个人不认同宣扬这些 principles 的 religion 就将其全部抛弃，那会是一个错误。那会是 religious zealot 才会做的事。

如果我们有真正的 pluralism，我想我们就能免受未来爆发的 woke intolerance 的影响。Wokeness 本身不会消失。在可预见的未来，仍会继续存在一撮撮的 woke zealots，发明新的 moral fashions。关键在于不要让他们把自己的 fashions 当作 normative。如果他们愿意，他们可以每隔几个月改变其 coreligionists 被允许说的话，但绝不能允许他们改变我们被允许说的话。[18](#fn18)

更普遍的问题——如何防止类似的 aggressively performative moralism 的爆发——当然更难。在这里，我们要对抗的是 human nature。总会有 prigs。尤其是，他们当中总会有 enforcers，即那些 [_aggressively conventional- minded_](https://paulgraham.com/conformism.html)。这些人天生如此。每个社会都有他们。所以我们能做的最好的事，就是把他们 bottled up。

The aggressively conventional-minded 并不总是 on the rampage。通常他们只是 enforce 手边最近的任何 random rules。只有当某种新的 ideology 让其中许多人同时指向同一个方向时，他们才会变得危险。这就是在 Cultural Revolution 期间发生的事，以及在我们经历过的两波 political correctness 中程度较轻地（感谢上帝）发生的事。

我们无法摆脱 the aggressively conventional-minded。[19](#fn19) 而且即使我们想，也无法阻止人们创造吸引他们的新 ideologies。所以如果我们想把他们 bottled up，就必须在 downstream 一步来做。幸运的是，当 the aggressively conventional-minded go on the rampage 时，他们总会做一件事暴露自己：他们定义新的 [_heresies_](https://paulgraham.com/heresy.html) 来惩罚人们。因此，保护自己免受 wokeness 之类事物未来爆发的最佳方式，就是拥有对抗 heresy 这一概念的强大 antibodies。

^pr-183d0f3f61fb

我们应该对定义新形式的 heresy 有一种有意识的 bias。每当有人试图禁止说我们以前能够说的话时，我们的 initial assumption 应该是他们错了。当然只是我们的 initial assumption。如果他们能证明我们不应该再说它，那我们就应该停止。但 burden of proof 在他们身上。在 liberal democracies 中，试图阻止某件事被说出来的人通常会声称他们不仅仅是在进行 censorship，而是在试图防止某种形式的 “harm”。也许他们是对的。但再一次，burden of proof 在他们身上。仅仅声称 harm 是不够的；他们必须证明它。

只要 the aggressively conventional-minded 继续通过禁止 heresies 来暴露自己，我们就总能注意到他们何时在某种新 ideology 背后 aligned。如果我们总是在那时进行反击，运气好的话，我们就能 stop them in their tracks。

我们 [_can’t say_](https://paulgraham.com/say.html) 的 true things 的数量不应该增加。如果它增加了，那就是出了问题。

## 原文

# 227 The Origins of Wokeness

January 2025

The word “prig” isn’t very common now, but if you look up the definition, it will sound familiar. Google’s isn’t bad:

> A self-righteously moralistic person who behaves as if superior to others.

This sense of the word originated in the 18th century, and its age is an important clue: it shows that although wokeness is a comparatively recent phenomenon, it’s an instance of a much older one.

There’s a certain kind of person who’s attracted to a shallow, exacting kind of moral purity, and who demonstrates his purity by attacking anyone who breaks the rules. Every society has these people. All that changes is the rules they enforce. In Victorian England it was Christian virtue. In Stalin’s Russia it was orthodox Marxism-Leninism. For the woke, it’s social justice.

So if you want to understand wokeness, the question to ask is not why people behave this way. Every society has prigs. The question to ask is why our prigs are priggish about these ideas, at this moment. And to answer that we have to ask when and where wokeness began.

The answer to the first question is the 1980s. Wokeness is a second, more aggressive wave of political correctness, which started in the late 1980s, died down in the late 1990s, and then returned with a vengeance in the early 2010s, finally peaking after the riots of 2020.

What was political correctness, exactly? I’m often asked to define both this term and wokeness by people who think they’re meaningless labels, so I will. They both have the same definition:

> An aggressively performative focus on social justice.

In other words, it’s people being prigs about social justice. And that’s the real problem – the performativeness, not the social justice. [1](#fn1)

Racism, for example, is a genuine problem. Not a problem on the scale that the woke believe it to be, but a genuine one. I don’t think any reasonable person would deny that. The problem with political correctness was not that it focused on marginalized groups, but the shallow, aggressive way in which it did so. Instead of going out into the world and quietly helping members of marginalized groups, the politically correct focused on getting people in trouble for using the wrong words to talk about them.

As for where political correctness began, if you think about it, you probably already know the answer. Did it begin outside universities and spread to them from this external source? Obviously not; it has always been most extreme in universities. So where in universities did it begin? Did it begin in math, or the hard sciences, or engineering, and spread from there to the humanities and social sciences? Those are amusing images, but no, obviously it began in the humanities and social sciences.

Why there? And why then? What happened in the humanities and social sciences in the 1980s?

A successful theory of the origin of political correctness has to be able to explain why it didn’t happen earlier. Why didn’t it happen during the protest movements of the 1960s, for example? They were concerned with much the same issues. [2](#fn2)

The reason the student protests of the 1960s didn’t lead to political correctness was precisely that – they were student movements. They didn’t have any real power. The students may have been talking a lot about women’s liberation and black power, but it was not what they were being taught in their classes. Not yet.

But in the early 1970s the student protestors of the 1960s began to finish their dissertations and get hired as professors. At first they were neither powerful nor numerous. But as more of their peers joined them and the previous generation of professors started to retire, they gradually became both.

The reason political correctness began in the humanities and social sciences was that these fields offered more scope for the injection of politics. A 1960s radical who got a job as a physics professor could still attend protests, but his political beliefs wouldn’t affect his work. Whereas research in sociology and modern literature can be made as political as you like. [3](#fn3)

I saw political correctness arise. When I started college in 1982 it was not yet a thing. Female students might object if someone said something they considered sexist, but no one was getting _reported_ for it. It was still not a thing when I started grad school in 1986. It was definitely a thing in 1988 though, and by the early 1990s it seemed to pervade campus life.

What happened? How did protest become punishment? Why were the late 1980s the point at which protests against male chauvinism (as it used to be called) morphed into formal complaints to university authorities about sexism? Basically, the 1960s radicals got tenure. They became the Establishment they’d protested against two decades before. Now they were in a position not just to speak out about their ideas, but to enforce them.

A new set of moral rules to enforce was exciting news to a certain kind of student. What made it particularly exciting was that they were allowed to attack professors. I remember noticing that aspect of political correctness at the time. It wasn’t simply a grass-roots student movement. It was faculty members encouraging students to attack other faculty members. In that respect it was like the Cultural Revolution. That wasn’t a grass-roots movement either; that was Mao unleashing the younger generation on his political opponents. And in fact when Roderick MacFarquhar started teaching a class on the Cultural Revolution at Harvard in the late 1980s, many saw it as a comment on current events. I don’t know if it actually was, but people thought it was, and that means the similarities were obvious. [4](#fn4)

College students larp. It’s their nature. It’s usually harmless. But larping morality turned out to be a poisonous combination. The result was a kind of moral etiquette, superficial but very complicated. Imagine having to explain to a well-meaning visitor from another planet why using the phrase “people of color” is considered particularly enlightened, but saying “colored people” gets you fired. And why exactly one isn’t supposed to use the word “negro” now, even though Martin Luther King used it constantly in his speeches. There are no underlying principles. You’d just have to give him a long list of rules to memorize. [5](#fn5)

The danger of these rules was not just that they created land mines for the unwary, but that their elaborateness made them an effective substitute for virtue. Whenever a society has a concept of heresy and orthodoxy, orthodoxy becomes a substitute for virtue. You can be the worst person in the world, but as long as you’re orthodox you’re better than everyone who isn’t. This makes orthodoxy very attractive to bad people.

But for it to work as a substitute for virtue, orthodoxy must be difficult. If all you have to do to be orthodox is wear some garment or avoid saying some word, everyone knows to do it, and the only way to seem more virtuous than other people is to actually be virtuous. The shallow, complicated, and frequently changing rules of political correctness made it the perfect substitute for actual virtue. And the result was a world in which good people who weren’t up to date on current moral fashions were brought down by people whose characters would make you recoil in horror if you could see them.

One big contributing factor in the rise of political correctness was the lack of other things to be morally pure about. Previous generations of prigs had been prigs mostly about religion and sex. But among the cultural elite these were the deadest of dead letters by the 1980s; if you were religious, or a virgin, this was something you tended to conceal rather than advertise. So the sort of people who enjoy being moral enforcers had become starved of things to enforce. A new set of rules was just what they’d been waiting for.

Curiously enough, the tolerant side of the 1960s left helped create the conditions in which the intolerant side prevailed. The relaxed social rules advocated by the old, easy-going hippy left became the dominant ones, at least among the elite, and this left nothing for the naturally intolerant to be intolerant about.

Another possibly contributing factor was the fall of the Soviet empire. Marxism had been a popular focus of moral purity on the left before political correctness emerged as a competitor, but the pro-democracy movements in Eastern Bloc countries took most of the shine off it. Especially the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989. You couldn’t be on the side of the Stasi. I remember looking at the moribund Soviet Studies section of a used bookshop in Cambridge in the late 1980s and thinking “what will those people go on about now?” As it turned out the answer was right under my nose.

One thing I noticed at the time about the first phase of political correctness was that it was more popular with women than men. As many writers (perhaps most eloquently George Orwell) have observed, women seem more attracted than men to the idea of being moral enforcers. But there was another more specific reason women tended to be the enforcers of political correctness. There was at this time a great backlash against sexual harassment; the mid 1980s were the point when the definition of sexual harassment was expanded from explicit sexual advances to creating a “hostile environment.” Within universities the classic form of accusation was for a (female) student to say that a professor made her “feel uncomfortable.” But the vagueness of this accusation allowed the radius of forbidden behavior to expand to include talking about heterodox ideas. Those make people uncomfortable too. [6](#fn6)

Was it sexist to propose that Darwin’s greater male variability hypothesis might explain some variation in human performance? Sexist enough to get Larry Summers pushed out as president of Harvard, apparently. One woman who heard the talk in which he mentioned this idea said it made her feel “physically ill” and that she had to leave halfway through. If the test of a hostile environment is how it makes people feel, this certainly sounds like one. And yet it does seem plausible that greater male variability explains some of the variation in human performance. So which should prevail, comfort or truth? Surely if truth should prevail anywhere, it should be in universities; that’s supposed to be their specialty; but for decades starting in the late 1980s the politically correct tried to pretend this conflict didn’t exist. [7](#fn7)

Political correctness seemed to burn out in the second half of the 1990s. One reason, perhaps the main reason, was that it literally became a joke. It offered rich material for comedians, who performed their usual disinfectant action upon it. Humor is one of the most powerful weapons against priggishness of any sort, because prigs, being humorless, can’t respond in kind. Humor was what defeated Victorian prudishness, and by 2000 it seemed to have done the same thing to political correctness.

Unfortunately this was an illusion. Within universities the embers of political correctness were still glowing brightly. After all, the forces that created it were still there. The professors who started it were now becoming deans and department heads. And in addition to their departments there were now a bunch of new ones explicitly focused on social justice. Students were still hungry for things to be morally pure about. And there had been an explosion in the number of university administrators, many of whose jobs involved enforcing various forms of political correctness.

In the early 2010s the embers of political correctness burst into flame anew. There were several differences between this new phase and the original one. It was more virulent. It spread further into the real world, although it still burned hottest within universities. And it was concerned with a wider variety of sins. In the first phase of political correctness there were really only three things people got accused of: sexism, racism, and homophobia (which at the time was a neologism invented for the purpose). But between then and 2010 a lot of people had spent a lot of time trying to invent new kinds of -isms and -phobias and seeing which could be made to stick.

The second phase was, in multiple senses, political correctness metastasized. Why did it happen when it did? My guess is that it was due to the rise of social media, particularly Tumblr and Twitter, because one of the most distinctive features of the second wave of political correctness was the _cancel mob_ : a mob of angry people uniting on social media to get someone ostracized or fired. Indeed this second wave of political correctness was originally called “cancel culture”; it didn’t start to be called “wokeness” till the 2020s.

One aspect of social media that surprised almost everyone at first was the popularity of outrage. Users seemed to _like_ being outraged. We’re so used to this idea now that we take it for granted, but really it’s pretty strange. Being outraged is not a pleasant feeling. You wouldn’t expect people to seek it out. But they do. And above all, they want to share it. I happened to be running a forum from 2007 to 2014, so I can actually quantify how much they want to share it: our users were about three times more likely to upvote something if it outraged them.

This tilt toward outrage wasn’t due to wokeness. It’s an inherent feature of social media, or at least this generation of it. But it did make social media the perfect mechanism for fanning the flames of wokeness. [8](#fn8)

It wasn’t just public social networks that drove the rise of wokeness though. Group chat apps were also critical, especially in the final step, cancellation. Imagine if a group of employees trying to get someone fired had to do it using only email. It would be hard to organize a mob. But once you have group chat, mobs form naturally.

Another contributing factor in this second wave of political correctness was the dramatic increase in the polarization of the press. In the print era, newspapers were constrained to be, or at least seem, politically neutral. The department stores that ran ads in the New York Times wanted to reach everyone in the region, both liberal and conservative, so the Times had to serve both. But the Times didn’t regard this neutrality as something forced upon them. They embraced it as their duty as a _paper of record_ – as one of the big newspapers that aimed to be chronicles of their times, reporting every sufficiently important story from a neutral point of view.

When I grew up the papers of record seemed timeless, almost sacred institutions. Papers like the New York Times and Washington Post had immense prestige, partly because other sources of news were limited, but also because they did make some effort to be neutral.

Unfortunately it turned out that the paper of record was mostly an artifact of the constraints imposed by print. [9](#fn9) When your market was determined by geography, you had to be neutral. But publishing online enabled – in fact probably forced – newspapers to switch to serving markets defined by ideology instead of geography. Most that remained in business fell in the direction they’d already been leaning: left. On October 11, 2020 the New York Times announced that “The paper is in the midst of an evolution from the stodgy paper of record into a juicy collection of great narratives.” [10](#fn10) Meanwhile journalists, of a sort, had arisen to serve the right as well. And so journalism, which in the previous era had been one of the great centralizing forces, now became one of the great polarizing ones.

The rise of social media and the increasing polarization of journalism reinforced one another. In fact there arose a new variety of journalism involving a loop through social media. Someone would say something controversial on social media. Within hours it would become a news story. Outraged readers would then post links to the story on social media, driving further arguments online. It was the cheapest source of clicks imaginable. You didn’t have to maintain overseas news bureaus or pay for month-long investigations. All you had to do was watch Twitter for controversial remarks and repost them on your site, with some additional comments to inflame readers further.

For the press there was money in wokeness. But they weren’t the only ones. That was one of the biggest differences between the two waves of political correctness: the first was driven almost entirely by amateurs, but the second was often driven by professionals. For some it was their whole job. By 2010 a new class of administrators had arisen whose job was basically to enforce wokeness. They played a role similar to that of the political commissars who got attached to military and industrial organizations in the USSR: they weren’t directly in the flow of the organization’s work, but watched from the side to ensure that nothing improper happened in the doing of it. These new administrators could often be recognized by the word “inclusion” in their titles. Within institutions this was the preferred euphemism for wokeness; a new list of banned words, for example, would usually be called an “inclusive language guide.” [11](#fn11)

This new class of bureaucrats pursued a woke agenda as if their jobs depended on it, because they did. If you hire people to keep watch for a particular type of problem, they’re going to find it, because otherwise there’s no justification for their existence. [12](#fn12) But these bureaucrats also represented a second and possibly even greater danger. Many were involved in hiring, and when possible they tried to ensure their employers hired only people who shared their political beliefs. The most egregious cases were the new “DEI statements” that some universities started to require from faculty candidates, proving their commitment to wokeness. Some universities used these statements as the initial filter and only even considered candidates who scored high enough on them. You’re not hiring Einstein that way; imagine what you get instead.

Another factor in the rise of wokeness was the Black Lives Matter movement, which started in 2013 when a white man was acquitted after killing a black teenager in Florida. But this didn’t launch wokeness; it was well underway by 2013.

Similarly for the Me Too Movement, which took off in 2017 after the first news stories about Harvey Weinstein’s history of raping women. It accelerated wokeness, but didn’t play the same role in launching it that the 80s version did in launching political correctness.

The election of Donald Trump in 2016 also accelerated wokeness, particularly in the press, where outrage now meant traffic. Trump made the New York Times a lot of money: headlines during his first administration mentioned his name at about four times the rate of previous presidents.

In 2020 we saw the biggest accelerant of all, after a white police officer asphyxiated a black suspect on video. At this point the metaphorical fire became a literal one, as violent protests broke out across America. But in retrospect this turned out to be peak woke, or close to it. By every measure I’ve seen, wokeness peaked in 2020 or 2021.

Wokeness is sometimes described as a mind-virus. What makes it viral is that it defines new types of impropriety. Most people are afraid of impropriety; they’re never exactly sure what the social rules are or which ones they might be breaking. Especially if the rules change rapidly. And since most people already worry that they might be breaking rules they don’t know about, if you tell them they’re breaking a rule, their default reaction is to believe you. Especially if multiple people tell them. Which in turn is a recipe for exponential growth. Zealots invent some new impropriety to avoid. The first people to adopt it are fellow zealots, eager for new ways to signal their virtue. If there are enough of these, the initial group of zealots is followed by a much larger group, motivated by fear. They’re not trying to signal virtue; they’re just trying to avoid getting in trouble. At this point the new impropriety is now firmly established. Plus its success has increased the rate of change in social rules, which, remember, is one of the reasons people are nervous about which rules they might be breaking. So the cycle accelerates. [13](#fn13)

What’s true of individuals is even more true of organizations. Especially organizations without a powerful leader. Such organizations do everything based on “best practices.” There’s no higher authority; if some new “best practice” achieves critical mass, they _must_ adopt it. And in this case the organization can’t do what it usually does when it’s uncertain: delay. It might be committing improprieties right now! So it’s surprisingly easy for a small group of zealots to capture this type of organization by describing new improprieties it might be guilty of. [14](#fn14)

How does this kind of cycle ever end? Eventually it leads to disaster, and people start to say enough is enough. The excesses of 2020 made a lot of people say that.

Since then wokeness has been in gradual but continual retreat. Corporate CEOs, starting with Brian Armstrong, have openly rejected it. Universities, led by the University of Chicago and MIT, have explicitly confirmed their commitment to free speech. Twitter, which was arguably the hub of wokeness, was bought by Elon Musk in order to neutralize it, and he seems to have succeeded – and not, incidentally, by censoring left-wing users the way Twitter used to censor right-wing ones, but without censoring either. [15](#fn15) Consumers have emphatically rejected brands that ventured too far into wokeness. The Bud Light brand may have been permanently damaged by it. I’m not going to claim Trump’s second victory in 2024 was a referendum on wokeness; I think he won, as presidential candidates always do, because he was more [_charismatic_](https://paulgraham.com/charisma.html); but voters’ disgust with wokeness must have helped.

So what do we do now? Wokeness is already in retreat. Obviously we should help it along. What’s the best way to do that? And more importantly, how do we avoid a third outbreak? After all, it seemed to be dead once, but came back worse than ever.

In fact there’s an even more ambitious goal: is there a way to prevent any similar outbreak of aggressively performative moralism in the future – not just a third outbreak of political correctness, but the next thing like it? Because there will be a next thing. Prigs are prigs by nature. They need rules to obey and enforce, and now that Darwin has cut off their traditional supply of rules, they’re constantly hungry for new ones. All they need is someone to meet them halfway by defining a new way to be morally pure, and we’ll see the same phenomenon again.

Let’s start with the easier problem. Is there a simple, principled way to deal with wokeness? I think there is: to use the customs we already have for dealing with religion. Wokeness is effectively a religion, just with God replaced by protected classes. It’s not even the first religion of this kind; Marxism had a similar form, with God replaced by the masses. [16](#fn16) And we already have well-established customs for dealing with religion within organizations. You can express your own religious identity and explain your beliefs, but you can’t call your coworkers infidels if they disagree, or try to ban them from saying things that contradict its doctrines, or insist that the organization adopt yours as its official religion.

If we’re not sure what to do about any particular manifestation of wokeness, imagine we were dealing with some other religion, like Christianity. Should we have people within organizations whose jobs are to enforce woke orthodoxy? No, because we wouldn’t have people whose jobs were to enforce Christian orthodoxy. Should we censor [_writers_](https://www.telegraph.co.uk/news/2023/02/17/roald-dahl-books-%20rewritten-offensive-matilda-witches-twits/) or [_scientists_](https://x.com/KoustaStavroula/status/1562034502897106946) whose work contradicts woke doctrines? No, because we wouldn’t do this to people whose work contradicted Christian teachings. Should job candidates be required to write DEI statements? Of course not; imagine an employer requiring proof of one’s religious beliefs. Should students and employees have to participate in woke indoctrination sessions in which they’re required to answer questions about their beliefs to ensure compliance? No, because we wouldn’t dream of catechizing people in this way about their religion. [17](#fn17)

One shouldn’t feel bad about not wanting to watch woke movies any more than one would feel bad about not wanting to listen to Christian rock. In my twenties I drove across America several times, listening to local radio stations. Occasionally I’d turn the dial and hear some new song. But the moment anyone mentioned Jesus I’d turn the dial again. Even the tiniest bit of being preached to was enough to make me lose interest.

But by the same token we should not automatically reject everything the woke believe. I’m not a Christian, but I can see that many Christian principles are good ones. It would be a mistake to discard them all just because one didn’t share the religion that espoused them. It would be the sort of thing a religious zealot would do.

If we have genuine pluralism, I think we’ll be safe from future outbreaks of woke intolerance. Wokeness itself won’t go away. There will for the foreseeable future continue to be pockets of woke zealots inventing new moral fashions. The key is not to let them treat their fashions as normative. They can change what their coreligionists are allowed to say every few months if they like, but they mustn’t be allowed to change what we’re allowed to say. [18](#fn18)

The more general problem – how to prevent similar outbreaks of aggressively performative moralism – is of course harder. Here we’re up against human nature. There will always be prigs. And in particular there will always be the enforcers among them, the [_aggressively conventional- minded_](https://paulgraham.com/conformism.html). These people are born that way. Every society has them. So the best we can do is to keep them bottled up.

The aggressively conventional-minded aren’t always on the rampage. Usually they just enforce whatever random rules are nearest to hand. They only become dangerous when some new ideology gets a lot of them pointed in the same direction at once. That’s what happened during the Cultural Revolution, and to a lesser extent (thank God) in the two waves of political correctness we’ve experienced.

We can’t get rid of the aggressively conventional-minded. [19](#fn19) And we couldn’t prevent people from creating new ideologies that appealed to them even if we wanted to. So if we want to keep them bottled up, we have to do it one step downstream. Fortunately when the aggressively conventional-minded go on the rampage they always do one thing that gives them away: they define new [_heresies_](https://paulgraham.com/heresy.html) to punish people for. So the best way to protect ourselves from future outbreaks of things like wokeness is to have powerful antibodies against the concept of heresy.

We should have a conscious bias against defining new forms of heresy. Whenever anyone tries to ban saying something that we’d previously been able to say, our initial assumption should be that they’re wrong. Only our initial assumption of course. If they can prove we should stop saying it, then we should. But the burden of proof is on them. In liberal democracies, people trying to prevent something from being said will usually claim they’re not merely engaging in censorship, but trying to prevent some form of “harm”. And maybe they’re right. But once again, the burden of proof is on them. It’s not enough to claim harm; they have to prove it.

As long as the aggressively conventional-minded continue to give themselves away by banning heresies, we’ll always be able to notice when they become aligned behind some new ideology. And if we always fight back at that point, with any luck we can stop them in their tracks.

The number of true things we [_can’t say_](https://paulgraham.com/say.html) should not increase. If it does, something is wrong.
