## 正文

**TL;DR（简而言之）** 如果你相信这些关键 claims：“在我有生之年，AI causing x-risk 的 chance 为 >=1%，而 bio causing x-risk 的 chance 为 >=0.1%”，这就足以 justify EA 中与核心 action relevant 的 points。在大多数 reasonable moral views 下，这显然都很重要，而 intro materials 中常见的对 longtermism、future generations 以及 moral philosophy 其他细节的 discussion，是不必要的干扰。

_感谢 Jemima Jones 的督促与鼓励。本文的部分灵感来自 Holden Karnofsky 出色的_ [_Most Important Century_](https://www.cold-takes.com/most-important-century/) _系列。_

**_Disclaimer（免责声明）_**_：我最近开始在 Anthropic 工作，但本文完全代表我个人的观点，而非我雇主的观点_

## Introduction（引言）

我全职从事 AI Safety 工作，主要 goal 是 reducing 来自 AI 的 x-risk。我认为自己的工作非常重要，并预计它将占据我一生 impact 的绝大部分。同时，我对 total utilitarianism 持高度 skeptical 态度，对 person-affecting views 隐约有些认同，对 currently alive people 的 prioritise 程度略高于 near future people，且显著高于 distant future people，也并不真正认同自己是 longtermist。尽管我与 EA 中一些常见 moral views 存在这些重大分歧，而这些 moral views 又经常被用来 justify 关键的 longtermist conclusions，我认为这对我的 actions 基本上没有重要 implications。

EA 中的许多人非常喜欢 philosophical discussions 和 debates。这很容易理解！对于一个由 moral philosophy academics 创立的 movement，你还能有什么别的期待呢？我自己也很享受其中一些 discussions。但我经常看到，moral philosophy 中重要且 controversial 的 beliefs 被随意放进 introductory EA material（尤其是 introductory pitches 和 intro fellowships），例如 strong longtermism、astronomical waste argument、对 future people 与 currently existing people 进行 equally 的 valuing，等等。我认为这没有必要，应该少这样做，而且这样会使这些 introductions 的 effective 程度显著降低。

我认为，对于大多数关键 EA conclusions，有两个 sufficient claims：“在我有生之年，AI causing human extinction 的 chance 为 >=1%”，以及“在我有生之年，biorisk causing human extinction 的 chance 为 >=0.1%”。这两个 claims 我都相信，而且我认为，要让大多数 EA pitches 成立，并尝试说服某人把职业生涯投入 AI 或 bio 工作，你至少需要 justify 其中一个。**这些 claims 真的很古怪**。显然，我们的世界并不是一个大多数聪明人都相信这些 claims 的地方！如果你刚接触 EA ideas，听到这样一个可能带来足以改变你人生道路的 implications 的 idea，**保持 skeptical 是正当且正确的**。而当你提出一个 complex 且古怪的 argument 时，将你的 case distill 为尽可能精简的一系列 claims，_真的_很重要——每增加一个 point，就多了一处 inferential distance，也多了一处可能让人不再跟得上你的地方。

我理想中的 EA intro fellowship 或 EA pitch（与一位感兴趣且投入的交谈对象进行 >=10 minute 的对话），会先介绍这些 claims 及其 minimum viable case，以及 EA 中与之相关的一些关键 insights 和 doing good 的 mindset，然后深入探讨它们，以及对方不认同或感到困惑／skeptical 的 points。如果有人开展这样的 fellowship，我会很兴奋！

## 我的 Minimum Viable Case 版本

_下面是一个 rough outline，说明我会如何向一个聪明、投入但刚接触 EA 的人提出 minimum viable case——它旨在提供启发与 intuitions，也是我会用来开启对话／Q&A 的内容，但并不打算单凭它就构成一个 airtight case！_

### Motivation（动机）

下面是我最喜欢的一些例子，展示了世界得到重大改善的方式：

-   Norman Borlaug 的 Green Revolution——一位 plant scientist 对 breeding high-yield dwarf wheat 的 study 改变了世界，将 India 和 Pakistan 从 grain importers 转变为 grain exporters，并很可能挽救了超过 250 million 人的生命

-   smallpox 的 eradication——这是 global coordination 与 competent public health efforts 共同成就的一项极其雄心勃勃、史无前例的壮举，eradicated 了一种在人类历史上已 killed 超过 500 million 人的 disease

-   Stanislav Petrov 在看到 Soviet early warning system（falsely）reporting 一次 US attack 时，选择_不_ start 一场 nuclear war

-   过去几百年的 industrial and scientific revolutions，正是它们造就了[这张令人惊叹的 graph](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/reconstruction-of-historical-global-extreme-poverty-rates-1820-2017-roser-and-hasell-2021-and-world-bank2020)。

![](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5ed9272a58c63e6402d3fefb/48d24154-5e30-4450-a940-b7751a1e2b17/reconstruction-of-historical-global-extreme-poverty-rates-1820-2017-roser-and-hasell-2021-and-world-bank2020.png)

看着这些以及其他例子，如果我想成为一个能够 achieve massive amounts of good 的人，有几点启示就变得很清楚：

-   要愿意**胸怀大志**

-   要愿意**相信**，也愿意**做古怪的事情**。如果我能找到一个大多数人不相信的重要 idea，并能投入其中、认真对待它，我就能大有作为。

    -   如果一件事显而易见、众所周知，那么很可能已经有别人做过了！

    -   不过，反过来说，_大多数_古怪的 ideas 都是错的——别把头脑敞开得太大，以至于脑子都掉了出来。

-   寻找 **high-leverage!**

    -   世界很大，而且 inter-connected。如果你想产生 massive impact，就需要借助某种强大的东西来使它 leveraged——一个 idea、一种新的 technology、exponential growth，等等。

当我从这个角度看今天的世界时，我本质上是在寻找那些可能变得举足轻重的事物。过去那些真正重大、改变世界的事物，大多是某种重大的 emerging technology，能够 unlocking 新的 capabilities 和新的 risks。Agriculture、computers、nuclear weapons、fossil fuels、electricity，等等。而当我寻找_现在_正在 emerging、仍处于起步阶段但极具 potential 的 technologies 时，AI 和 synthetic biology 远远高于其余事物。

**注意：** 无论是 focusing on highly leveraged positive outcomes，还是 negative outcomes，这些 arguments 都大致同样成立。实际上，根据我对 AI 和 bio 的了解，我认为存在 plausible negative outcomes，而且 reducing 这些 outcomes 的 likelihood 是 tractable 的，也比 ensuring positive outcomes 更重要。不过，我也愿意认真考虑相反的 arguments。

### AI - ‘在我的一生中，AI 有 >=1% 的 chance 导致 x-risk’

human brain 是 generally intelligent system 的一个 natural example。Evolution produced 了它，尽管存在许多重大的 constraints，例如 biological energy 极为昂贵、需要 fit through birth canals、使用极其 inefficient 的 optimisation algorithm，以及 intelligence 并不明显 increasing reproductive fitness。虽然 evolution 拥有 four billion years 可供利用这一重大优势，但在我看来，humanity 能做得更好是 highly plausible 的。而且，没有理由认为 human intelligence 应当成为 digital intelligence 的 capabilities 的 limit。

从 outside view 来看，这件事**极其重要**。我们正在考虑 creation 一个第二种 intelligence species！这似乎是 human civilisation 的 trajectory 中最重要的部分之一——与 humanity 的诞生、agriculture 的 invention 以及 Industrial Revolution 相提并论。而确保这件事顺利发展似乎至关重要，尤其是如果这些 systems 最终比我们 smarter 得多。一个 less intelligent species 的 default fate 似乎很可能与 gorillas 一样——humanity 并没有真正对 gorillas 怀有主动的恶意，但它们基本上只是因为我们希望它们 survive 才能 survive。

此外，我们还有具体理由认为，这件事可能非常可怕！AI systems 大多看起来像 optimisation processes，它们能找到 creative 且 unexpected 的方式来 achieve 这些 objectives。而且，[specifying 正确的 objective 是一个出了名的 hard problem](https://deepmind.com/blog/article/Specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-AI-ingenuity)。我们也有充分理由相信，这样的 system 可能有 instrumental incentive 去 seek power 并 compete with humanity，尤其是如果它具有以下三个 properties：

-   **Advanced capabilities** \- 它至少在某些种类的重要且困难的 tasks 上具有 superhuman capabilities

-   **Agentic planning** \- 它能够根据 world 的 models 来 making and executing plans，以 achieve objectives

-   **Strategic awareness** \- 它能 competently reason about gaining and maintaining power over humans and the real world 所产生的 effects

关于这个问题更为 rigorous 的 analysis，请参阅 [Joseph Carlsmith 的出色 report](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/PC6QavgNDQjHbutAq/an-170-analyzing-the-argument-for-risk-from-power-seeking-ai#HIGHLIGHTS_)。我认为，这个 argument holds 绝非_显而易见_，但在我看来，我们 create 一个受到 incentivised 去 seek power 的 superhuman intelligence，而它又以 causes human extinction 的方式 successfully executes 这一行动，这种情况具有足够的 plausibility，因此我愿意给 AI causing human extinction 至少 1% 的 chance（我的 fair value 大概是 10-20%，具有 high uncertainty）。

最后，还有 timelines 的问题。就我个人而言，我认为 deep learning language models 之类的东西有很大 chance 能 scale to human-level intelligence，甚至超越这一水平（这也是我目前 research 的一个关键 motivation）。我觉得，[关于 timelines 的 bio-anchors 和 scaling based methods](https://www.cold-takes.com/forecasting-transformative-ai-the-biological-anchors-method-in-a-nutshell/) 相当有说服力，可以作为 timelines 的 upper bound，而这个 upper bound 完全在我的一生之内。但即使 deep learning 只是一时风潮，AI 这个 field 存在至今也还不到 70 年！而经历一个 paradigm 需要 10-30 年。在我的一生中，我们通过其他某个 paradigm 来 produce human-level AI，似乎是 highly plausible 的（不过，reducing 来自某个未知的未来 AI paradigm 的 risk，确实似乎远不那么 tractable）

### Bio - ‘在我的一生中，Biorisk 有 >=0.1% 的 chance 导致 x-risk’

我希望，这个 claim 现在看起来比在 2019 年时 reasonable 得多！虽然 COVID 远远算不上 x-risk，但它显然是我一生中亲历的最严重的 global disasters 之一，而全世界都极其缺乏准备，在 response 的许多方面也处理得一团糟。15 million 人已经 died，更多人曾 hospitalised，数百万人患有 long-term debilitating conditions，几乎每个人的生活都受到了长达两年的严重干扰。

而情况原本可能糟糕得多得多！仅考虑 natural pandemics，想象一下具有 smallpox 的 lethality（30%）的 COVID。或者，具有 Spanish Flu 的 age profile 的 COVID（在 young, healthy adults 中 most lethal，因为它使身体的 immune system 转而攻击自身）。

而当我们考虑 synthetic biology 时，情况就变得可怕得多。我们生活在这样一个世界：有多家 labs 从事 gain of function research，做着一些疯狂的事情，例如试图 breed 具有 human-to-human transmissible 特性的 Avian Flu（30% mortality），而且并非所有 DNA synthesis companies 都会阻止你尝试 print smallpox viruses。无论 COVID 是否_确实_是一次 lab leak，它可能来自针对 coronaviruses 的 gain-of-function research，这至少似乎是 plausible 的。而这些还是相对 low-tech 的 methods。[synthetic biology 的 Progress 发生得很快](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/cost-of-sequencing-a-full-human-genome)！

![](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5ed9272a58c63e6402d3fefb/c88927d0-8e3a-41e7-b9ba-7bddc0ead881/cost-of-sequencing-a-full-human-genome.png)

在我看来，某人最终 produces 一种 engineered pathogen，能够 creating 一场远比任何 natural 的情况更严重的 pandemic，而它又因 accident、terrorism 或 act of war 而被 released 到世界上，这是 highly plausible 的。尚不清楚这是否真的能够 cause human extinction，但某种足够可怕、well-deployed 程度足够高且具有 long incubation period 的东西能够做到这一点，是 plausible 的。而在我看来，某种 kills 99% 的人的东西（这是一个低得多的门槛）可能 lead to human extinction，也是 plausible 的。Biorisk 并非我的 field，我对它的思考远少于对 AI 的思考，但考虑到这些 arguments，我一生之内的 0.1% 似乎是一个 reasonable lower bound。

## 补充说明（Caveats）

-   这些 beliefs 真的很奇怪！人们初次接触它们时持 skeptical 态度，是正确而健康的。

    -   不过，在我看来，这些 arguments 足够 strong，而 implications 也足够 important，因此，不花至少几个小时仔细读完 arguments，并试着弄清楚 _你_ believe 什么以及为什么，就 dismiss 它们，是 unreasonable 的。

    -   此外，如果你不同意它们，那么我所 dismissing 的、围绕 strong longtermism 等的 moral claims，可能就重要得多。但你也应该不同意 EA movement 的绝大多数 allocating resources 的方式！

-   对于某种会 kills _几乎_ 所有人的事物，或会 causes 不一定 permanent 的 collapse of civilisation 的事物，其 case 要比某种会 kills _确确实实_ 每一个人的事物的 case 强得多。这真是一个很高的门槛！Human extinction 意味着 killing 每一个人，包括 Australian farmers、nuclear submarines 和 bunkers 里的人，以及 space 中的人。

    -   如果你是 longtermist，那么这个 distinction 非常重要，但我个人没有那么在意。在我看来，collapse of human civilisation 极其糟糕！而 averting 这种情况，似乎是值得我为之度过一生的 goal。

    -   相比 bio，我更容易看出 AI 如何 causes extinction

-   这里有一个 implicit claim：将你的大量 resources 投入 averting 极其糟糕的 outcomes 的 risks，是 reasonable 的，即便最终可能发现，我们生活的世界里根本不需要这些努力。我认为在意这一点是正确的，但不同意这一点也很 reasonable！

    -   这与我们应该 maximise expected utility 的想法有关，但在我看来，它在重要意义上更 weaker。即便你不同意 maximising expected value 的 formalisation，你很可能仍然同意，确保 bridges 和 planes 的 safety records 远好于 0.1%，是极其重要的

        -   不过，我们面对的 probabilities 虽然 small，却不是 infinitesimal。这使我们免于 Pascal’s Mugging 之类的 objections——[AI x-risk 的 1% chance _并不是_ Pascal’s Mugging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JRuNA2eK7w0)。

    -   在 intellectually 层面接受这些 arguments，却在 emotionally 层面觉得无法 motivate 自己用一生去 reducing tail risks，也是 reasonable 的。这些事情很难，而且可能令人沮丧，带来沉重的情绪负担！

        -   就我个人而言，我发现从其他来源获得 motivation 更容易，比如 intellectual satisfaction 和 social proof。我喜欢与 EAs 相处的一个重要原因是，这让我更切身地感到 AI Safety work 很能激励我，而且是 high-status 的！

-   同意这些 arguments，却认为别的事情是 _更大_ 的 problem，也很 reasonable！虽然我个人会不同意，但以下任何一种似乎都是 justifiable positions：climate change、progress studies、global poverty、factory farming。

-   有不少人确实 identify 为 EAs，却会不同意这些 claims，也不同意 prioritising AI 和 bio x-risk。对这些人，我说声抱歉！这篇文章针对的是 EA movement 中相当大的一部分（许多 EA community builders、CEA、80K、OpenPhil 等），他们似乎在 AI 和 bio x-risk reduction 上投入了大量 resources

-   这个 argument 有一个 flaw：它可能传达了‘reduce AI 和 bio x-risk’这些 beliefs，却没有传达其背后的 generators，即 cause neutrality，以及仔细 searching 做好事的 best ways。很可能，在早期 EA 中也能提出类似 arguments，建立一个“让我们 fight global poverty”的 movement，却始终没有接受 longtermism。也许，一个以我所呈现的 narrative 为基础的 movement，会错过下一个 Cause X，未能在应该 pivot 时 pivot，或者以其他方式呈现出 poor epistemic health。

    -   我认为这是一个 valid concern！但我也认为，支持“天哪，AI 和 bio risk 似乎是极其重大的事情，而世界在这些事情上严重失职”的 arguments 相当 reasonable，我也愿意作出这个 trade-off。“去从事 reducing AI 和 bio x-risk 的工作”是我非常愿意 signal boost 的主张！

    -   但我一直有意强调，这里谈的是 _intro_ materials。我理想中的、进入 EA movement 的 pipeline，仍然会强调 good epistemics、cause prioritisation 和 cause neutrality、独立思考等。但我会将这样的 belief 放在最显著的位置：AI 和 bio x-risk 是 substantial 的，而 reducing 它们是最大的 priority；同时鼓励人们认真思考，形成自己的 beliefs

-   AI case 的另一种 framing 是：“天哪，AI 似乎真的很重要”，因此，altruists 的一项关键 priority，就是确保它发展顺利。

    -   这在我看来是 plausible 的——AI 出问题的 downside 似乎可能是 human extinction，但 AI 发展得非常顺利的 upside，则可能是 humanity 一个好得多、好得多的 future。

    -   除了标准的 alignment failure example 之外，这还可能以许多方式 lead to bad outcomes！[也许在节奏飞快的 AI 世界里，coordination 会变得困难得多](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/LpM3EAakwYdS6aRKf/what-multipolar-failure-looks-like-and-robust-agent-agnostic)，进而 leads to war，或者我们会 pollute 自己直至死亡。也许它会 massively accelerates technological progress，让我们 discover 一种比 nukes 更 dangerous、Nash equilibria 更糟糕的 technology，而我们没有及时 solve coordination problem。

        -   我觉得更难想象这些 alternate scenarios 会确确实实 leading to extinction，但它们可能更 plausible，而且仍然极其糟糕！

    -   对于这种 framing，还有一些相当 strong 的其他 arguments。我觉得其中一个很有说服力：在用于 train ML models 的 compute 的 exponential growth，与 Moore’s Law 中[每个 chip 的 transistors 数量的 exponential growth](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/transistors-per-microprocessor)之间 drawing an analogy。

        -   进一步说，[从历史上看，大多数 AI progress 都是由不断增加的 computing power，以及 leverage 它们的 simple algorithms 所 driven 的](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html)。而且，[AI systems 所使用的 compute 的数量正在 growing exponentially](https://openai.com/blog/ai-and-compute/)（每 3.4 months 就 doubling——相比之下，Moore’s Law 是 2 years！）。虽然 doubling 的 rate 很可能会 slow down——当你在 compute 上的支出还低于支付给顶尖 AI researchers 的数百万薪酬时，增加这项支出，比达到 Google 每年 R&D 的 $26bn 这类数字的 order of magnitude 后容易得多——但它似乎也极不可能完全停止。

        -   在这种 framing 下，现在从事 AI 工作，就 analogous to 在 90s 从事 computers 工作。虽然当时可能很难 _准确地_ predict computers 将如何改变世界，但毫无疑问，它们确实改变了世界；而一个有抱负的 altruist，很可能能够对这一过程获得显著的 influence，并推动它变得更好。

        -   我也觉得这种 framing 很能激励我——即便我所担心的、例如围绕 inner alignment 的 specific stories 是错的，我仍然可以相当确信，AI 中正在发生 _某些_ 重要的事情，而我的 research 很可能让我处于一个有利的位置，能够 influence 这一切，使其变得更好。

            -   我从事 [interpretability research](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)，而这类 robustness arguments，正是我觉得这项工作特别能激励我的原因之一！

## 原文

**TL;DR** If you believe the key claims of "there is a >=1% chance of AI causing x-risk and >=0.1% chance of bio causing x-risk in my lifetime" this is enough to justify the core action relevant points of EA. This clearly matters under most reasonable moral views and the common discussion of longtermism, future generations and other details of moral philosophy in intro materials is an unnecessary distraction.

## Introduction

I work full-time on AI Safety, with the main goal of reducing x-risk from AI. I think my work is really important, and expect this to represent the vast majority of my lifetime impact. I am also highly skeptical of total utilitarianism, vaguely sympathetic to person-affecting views, prioritise currently alive people somewhat above near future people and significantly above distant future people, and do not really identify as a longtermist. Despite these major disagreements with some common moral views in EA, which are often invoked to justify key longtermist conclusions, I think there are basically no important implications for my actions.

Many people in EA really enjoy philosophical discussions and debates. This makes a lot of sense! What else would you expect from a movement founded by moral philosophy academics? I’ve enjoyed some of these discussions myself. But I often see important and controversial beliefs in moral philosophy thrown around in introductory EA material (introductory pitches and intro fellowships especially), like strong longtermism, the astronomical waste argument, valuing future people equally to currently existing people, etc. And I think this is unnecessary and should be done less often, and makes these introductions significantly less effective.

I think two sufficient claims for most key EA conclusions are “AI has a >=1% chance of causing human extinction within my lifetime” and “biorisk has a >=0.1% chance of causing human extinction within my lifetime”. I believe both of these claims, and think that you need to justify at least one of them for most EA pitches to go through, and to try convincing someone to spend their career working on AI or bio. **These are really weird claims**. The world is clearly not a place where most smart people believe these! If you are new to EA ideas and hear an idea like this, with implications that could transform your life path, **it is right and correct to be skeptical**. And when you’re making a complex and weird argument, it is _really_ important to distill your case down to the minimum possible series of claims - each additional point is a new point of inferential distance, and a new point where you could lose people.

My ideal version of an EA intro fellowship, or an EA pitch (a >=10 minute conversation with an interested and engaged partner) is to introduce these claims and a minimum viable case for them, some surrounding key insights of EA and the mindset of doing good, and then digging into them and the points where the other person doesn’t agree or feels confused/skeptical. I’d be excited to see someone make a fellowship like this!

## My Version of the Minimum Viable Case

_The following is a rough outline of how I’d make the minimum viable case to someone smart and engaged but new to EA - this is intended to give inspiration and intuitions, and is something I’d give to open a conversation/Q&A, but is not intended to be an airtight case on its own!_ 

### Motivation

Here are some of my favourite examples of major ways the world was improved:

-   Norman Borlaug’s Green Revolution - One plant scientist’s study of breeding high-yield dwarf wheat, which changed the world, converted India and Pakistan from grain importers to grain exporters, and likely saved over 250 million lives

-   The eradication of smallpox - An incredibly ambitious and unprecedented feat of global coordination and competent public health efforts, which eradicated a disease that has killed over 500 million people in human history

-   Stanislav Petrov choosing _not_ to start a nuclear war when he saw the Soviet early warning system (falsely) reporting a US attack

-   The industrial and scientific revolutions of the last few hundred years, which are responsible for [this incredible graph](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/reconstruction-of-historical-global-extreme-poverty-rates-1820-2017-roser-and-hasell-2021-and-world-bank2020).

![](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5ed9272a58c63e6402d3fefb/48d24154-5e30-4450-a940-b7751a1e2b17/reconstruction-of-historical-global-extreme-poverty-rates-1820-2017-roser-and-hasell-2021-and-world-bank2020.png)

When I look at these and other examples, a few lessons become clear if I want to be someone who can achieve massive amounts of good:

-   Be willing to be **ambitious**

-   Be willing to **believe** and **do weird things**. If I can find an important idea that most people don’t believe, and can commit and take the idea seriously, I can achieve a lot.

    -   If it’s obvious, common knowledge, someone else has likely already done it!

    -   Though, on the flipside, _most_ weird ideas are wrong - don’t open your mind so much that your brains fall out.

-   Look for **high-leverage!** 

    -   The world is big and inter-connected. If you want to have a massive impact, it needs to be leveraged with something powerful - an idea, a new technology, exponential growth, etc.

When I look at today’s world through this lens, I’m essentially searching for things that could become a really big deal. Most things that have been really big, world-changing deals in the past have been some kind of major emerging technology, unlocking new capabilities and new risks. Agriculture, computers, nuclear weapons, fossil fuels, electricity, etc. And when I look for technologies emerging _now_, still in their infancy but with a lot of potential, AI and synthetic biology stand well above the rest. 

**Note:** These arguments work about as well for focusing on highly leveraged positive outcomes or negative outcomes. I think that, in fact, given my knowledge of AI and bio, that there are plausible negative outcomes, and that reducing the likelihood of these is tractable and more important than ensuring positive outcomes. But I’d be sympathetic to arguments to the contrary.

### AI - ‘AI has a >=1% chance of x-risk within my lifetime’

The human brain is a natural example of a generally intelligent system. Evolution produced this, despite a bunch of major constraints like biological energy being super expensive, needing to fit through birth canals, using an extremely inefficient optimisation algorithm, and intelligence not obviously increasing reproductive fitness. While evolution had the major advantage of four billion years to work with, it seems highly plausible to me that humanity can do better. And, further, there’s no reason that human intelligence should be a limit on the capabilities of a digital intelligence.

On the outside view, this is **incredibly important**. We’re contemplating the creation of a second intelligence species! That seems like one of the most important parts of the trajectory of human civilisation - on par with the dawn of humanity, the invention of agriculture and the Industrial Revolution. And it seems crucial to ensure this goes well, especially if these systems end up much smarter than us. It seems plausible that the default fate of a less intelligent species is that of gorillas - humanity doesn’t really bear gorillas active malice, but they essentially only survive because we want them to.

Further, there are specific reasons to think that this could be really scary! AI systems mostly look like optimisation processes, which can find creative and unexpected ways to achieve these objectives. And [specifying the right objective is a notoriously hard problem](https://deepmind.com/blog/article/Specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-AI-ingenuity). And there are good reasons to believe that such a system might have an instrumental incentive to seek power and compete with humanity, especially if it has the following three properties:

-   **Advanced capabilities** \- it has superhuman capabilities on at least some kinds of important and difficult tasks

-   **Agentic planning** \- it is capable of making and executing plans to achieve objectives, based on models of the world

-   **Strategic awareness** \- it can competently reason about the effects of gaining and maintaining power over humans and the real world

See [Joseph Carlsmith’s excellent report](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/PC6QavgNDQjHbutAq/an-170-analyzing-the-argument-for-risk-from-power-seeking-ai#HIGHLIGHTS_) for a much more rigorous analysis of this question. I think it is by no means _obvious_ that this argument holds, but I find it sufficiently plausible that we create a superhuman intelligence which is incentivised to seek power and successfully executes on this in a manner that causes human extinction that I’m happy to put at least a 1% chance of AI causing human extinction (my fair value is probably 10-20%, with high uncertainty).

Finally, there’s the question of timelines. Personally, I think there’s a good chance that something like deep learning language models scale to human-level intelligence and beyond (and this is a key motivation of my current research). I find the [bio-anchors and scaling based methods of timelines](https://www.cold-takes.com/forecasting-transformative-ai-the-biological-anchors-method-in-a-nutshell/) pretty convincing as an upper bound of timelines that’s well within my lifetime. But even if deep learning is a fad, the field of AI has existed for less than 70 years! And it takes 10-30 years to go through a paradigm. It seems highly plausible that we produce human-level AI with some other paradigm within my lifetime (though reducing risk from an unknown future paradigm of AI does seem much less tractable)

### Bio - ‘Biorisk has a >=0.1% chance of x-risk within my lifetime’

I hope this claim seems a lot more reasonable now than it did in 2019! While COVID was nowhere near an x-risk, it has clearly been one of the worst global disasters I’ve ever lived through, and the world was highly unprepared and bungled a lot of aspects of the response. 15 million people have died, many more were hospitalised, millions of people have long-term debilitating conditions, and almost everyone’s lives were highly disrupted for two years. 

And things could have been much, much worse! Just looking at natural pandemics, imagine COVID with the lethality of smallpox (30%). Or COVID with the age profile of the Spanish Flu (most lethal in young, healthy adults, because it turns the body’s immune system against itself).

And things get much scarier when we consider synthetic biology. We live in a world where multiple labs work on gain of function research, doing crazy things like trying to breed Avian Flu (30% mortality) that’s human-to-human transmissible, and not all DNA synthesis companies will stop you trying to print smallpox viruses. Regardless of whether COVID was _actually_ a lab leak, it seems at least plausible that it could have come from gain-of-function research on coronaviruses. And these are comparatively low-tech methods. [Progress in synthetic biology happens fast](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/cost-of-sequencing-a-full-human-genome)! 

![](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5ed9272a58c63e6402d3fefb/c88927d0-8e3a-41e7-b9ba-7bddc0ead881/cost-of-sequencing-a-full-human-genome.png)

It is highly plausible to me that someone eventually produces an engineered pathogen capable of creating a pandemic far worse than anything natural and that, whether by accident, terrorism, or an act of war, it is released into the world. It’s unclear that this could actually cause human extinction, but it’s plausible that something scary enough and well-deployed enough with a long incubation period could. And it’s plausible to me that something which kills 99% of people (a much lower bar) could lead to human extinction. Biorisk is not my field and I’ve thought about this much less than AI, but 0.1% within my lifetime seems like a reasonable lower bound given these arguments.

## Caveats

-   These are really weird beliefs! It is correct and healthy for people to be skeptical when they first encounter them.

    -   Though, in my opinion, the arguments are strong enough and implications important enough that it’s unreasonable to dismiss them without at least a few hours of carefully reading through arguments and trying to figure out what _you_ believe and why.

    -   Further, if you disagree with them, then the moral claims I’m dismissing around strong longtermism etc may be much more important. But you should disagree with the vast majority of how the EA movement is allocating resources!

-   There’s a much stronger case for something that kills _almost_ all people, or which causes the not-necessarily-permanent collapse of civilisation, than something which kills _literally_ everyone. This is a really high bar! Human extinction means killing everyone, including Australian farmers, people in nuclear submarines and bunkers, and people in space.

    -   If you’re a longtermist then this distinction matters a lot, but I personally don’t care as much. The collapse of human civilisation seems super bad to me! And averting this seems like a worthy goal for my life. 

    -   I have an easier time seeing how AI causes extinction than bio

-   There’s an implicit claim in here that it’s reasonable to invest a large amount of your resources into averting risks of extremely bad outcomes, even though we may turn out to live in a world where all that effort was unnecessary. I think this is correct to care about, but that this is a reasonable thing to disagree with!

    -   This is related to the idea that we should maximise expected utility, but IMO importantly weaker. Even if you disagree with the formalisation of maximising expected value, you likely still agree that it’s extremely important to ensure that bridges and planes have safety records far better than 0.1%

        -   But also, we’re dealing with probabilities that are small but not infinitesimal. This saves us from objections like Pascal’s Mugging - [a 1% chance of AI x-risk is _not_ a Pascal’s Mugging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JRuNA2eK7w0).

    -   It is also reasonable to buy these arguments intellectually, but not to feel emotionally able to motivate yourself to spend your life reducing tail risks. This stuff is hard, and can be depressing and emotionally heavy! 

        -   Personally, I find it easier to get my motivation from other sources, like intellectual satisfaction and social proof. A big reason I like spending time around EAs is that this makes AI Safety work feel much more viscerally motivating to me, and high-status!

-   It’s reasonable to agree with these arguments, but consider something else an even _bigger_ problem! While I’d personally disagree, any of the following seem like justifiable positions: climate change, progress studies, global poverty, factory farming.

-   A bunch of people do identify as EAs, but would disagree with these claims and with prioritising AI and bio x-risk. To those people, sorry! I’m aiming this post at the significant parts of the EA movement (many EA community builders, CEA, 80K, OpenPhil, etc) who seem to put major resources into AI and bio x-risk reduction

-   This argument has the flaw of potentially conveying the beliefs of ‘reduce AI and bio x-risk’ without conveying the underlying generators of cause neutrality and carefully searching for the best ways of doing good. Plausibly, similar arguments could have been made in early EA to make a “let’s fight global poverty” movement that never embraced to longtermism. Maybe a movement based around the narrative I present would miss the next Cause X and fail to pivot when it should, or otherwise have poor epistemic health.

    -   I think this is a valid concern! But I also think that the arguments for “holy shit, AI and bio risk seem like really big deals that the world is majorly missing the ball on” are pretty reasonable, and I’m happy to make this trade-off. “Go work on reducing AI and bio x-risk” are things I would love to signal boost!

    -   But I have been deliberate to emphasise that I am talking about _intro_ materials here. My ideal pipeline into the EA movement would still emphasise good epistemics, cause prioritisation and cause neutrality, thinking for yourself, etc. But I would put front and center the belief that AI and bio x-risk are substantial and that reducing them is the biggest priority, and encourage people to think hard and form their own beliefs

-   An alternate framing of the AI case is “Holy shit, AI seems really important” and thus a key priority for altruists is to ensure that it goes well. 

    -   This seems plausible to me - it seems like the downside of AI going wrong could be human extinction, but that the upside of AI going really well could be a vastly, vastly better future for humanity. 

    -   There are also a lot of ways this could lead to bad outcomes beyond the standard alignment failure example! [Maybe coordination just becomes much harder in a fast-paced world of AI](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/LpM3EAakwYdS6aRKf/what-multipolar-failure-looks-like-and-robust-agent-agnostic) and this leads to war, or we pollute ourselves to death. Maybe it massively accelerates technological progress and we discover a technology more dangerous than nukes and with a worse Nash equilibria and don’t solve the coordination problem in time.

        -   I find it harder to imagine these alternate scenarios literally leading to extinction, but they might be more plausible and still super bad!

    -   There are some alternate pretty strong arguments for this framing. One I find very compelling is drawing an analogy between exponential growth in the compute used to train ML models, and the [exponential growth in the number of transistors per chip](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/transistors-per-microprocessor) of Moore’s Law. 

        -   Expanding upon this, [historically most AI progress has been driven by increasing amounts of computing power and simple algorithms that leverage them](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html). And [the amount of compute used in AI systems is growing exponentially](https://openai.com/blog/ai-and-compute/) (doubling every 3.4 months - compared to Moore’s Law’s 2 years!). Though the rate of doubling is likely to slow down - it’s much easier to increase the amount of money spent on compute when you’re spending less than the millions spent on payroll for top AI researchers than when you reach the order of magnitude of figures like Google’s $26bn annual R&D - it also seems highly unlikely to stop completely. 

        -   Under this framing, working on AI now is analogous to working with computers in the 90s. Though it may have been hard to predict _exactly_ how computers would change the world, there is no question that they did, and it seems likely that an ambitious altruist could have gained significant influence over how this went and nudged it to be better.

        -   I also find this framing pretty motivating - even if specific stories I’m concerned by around eg inner alignment are wrong, I can still be pretty confident that _something_ important is happening in AI, and my research likely puts me in a good place to influence this for the better. 

            -   I work on [interpretability research](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html), and these kind of robustness arguments are one of the reasons I find this particularly motivating!
