# Loving boundaries: Conditional love applied to parenting and therapy

---

## 正文

我过去一直以为，爱一个人可以是 unconditionally **或者** conditionally，却从未真正想过，两者可以同时做到。但今天我突然意识到，这不仅确实可能，而且值得提倡！

Unconditional love 无须多作介绍：它意味着 fully、deeply 地 welcoming 一个人本来的样子。一种常见看法是，这是父母对婴儿的 love，而且我们能找到的也基本仅限于此，但对任何人都给予 unconditionally 的 love 完全是可能的。事实上，它正是最有力的几种 therapy 中的 active ingredient！（这也是 coach [Joe Hudson](https://www.artofaccomplishment.com/podcast/how-love-gets-confused) 能在几分钟内让人[哭出来](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a132K8oo88A)的方式）。

一个 [cynic](https://www.reddit.com/r/changemyview/comments/1fvkwlq/cmv_love_is_inherently_conditional/) 可能会说，adult love 不是、也_不可能_是这样的：你爱朋友和伴侣，是因为他们的 _specific traits_；如果他们不具备或失去了那些 traits，这段 relationship 就失去了其存在的一个 grounds。他们可能会说，随着时间推移，relationship 会通过 [crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallization_\(love\)) 而 stabilizes，但它永远不会变得 unconditional。

我第一次接触到将 conditional love 视为好事的 concept，是在 Erich Fromm 的一本旧书中：[_The Art of Loving_](https://lohiatoday.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/theartofloving.pdf) _（1956）：_

> Fatherly love 是 conditional love。它的 principle 是：“我爱你，因为你 fulfill 我的 expectations，因为你 do your duty，因为你像我。”$$...$$
> 
> infant 在 physiologically 和 psychically 两方面都需要母亲的 unconditional love 和 care。孩子在六岁之后开始需要父亲的 love、他的 authority 和 guidance。母亲的 function 是让他在生活中感到 secure，父亲的 function 是 teaching 他，guiding 他去 cope with 孩子出生其中的那个特定 society 向他提出的那些 problems。$$...$$ . Father's love 应当由 principles 和 expectations 来 guided；它应当是 patient 和 tolerant，而不是 threatening 和 authoritarian。它应当给予成长中的孩子越来越强的 sense of competence，并最终允许他成为自己的 authority，不再需要父亲的 authority $$...$$
> 
> 此外，mature person 在 loves 时，同时带着 motherly 和 fatherly conscience，尽管两者似乎相互 contradict。如果他只 retain 自己的 fatherly conscience，他就会变得 harsh 和 inhuman。如果他只 retain 自己的 motherly conscience，他就容易 lose judgment，并 hinder 自己和他人的 development。从 mother-centered 到 fathercentered attachment 的这一 development，以及它们最终的 synthesis，构成了 mental health 和 achievement of maturity 的 basis。这一 development 的 failure 中，存在着 neurosis 的 basic cause。

有些读者可能更愿意把“conditional love”称为别的什么，比如“对 behaviors 的 conditional approval”，但在我看来，那既削弱了原意，又冗长拗口。为了传达我用“conditional love”所指的东西，以及我为什么喜欢这个说法，我能做的最好尝试，就是下面这个简短片段：

> 父亲：儿子，我为你骄傲。你做得很好，也很努力。
> 
> 儿子：\[眼眶湿润\] 谢谢你，爸爸（拥抱）
> 
> \[儿子为什么哭了？他正在 receiving conditional love。爸爸原则上也可能并不感到骄傲，但他看到了儿子的努力，认为它有价值，并给予了肯定。\]

Conditional love 只不过是我们具有 preferences 这一事实的 reflection，这也包括父母对孩子的 preferences，即便他们希望自己没有这样的 preferences。父母可能希望孩子不要在沙发上跳，不要吃糖，或者希望他们学习某个特定职业所需的专业。如果父母认为自己应当“只以 unconditionally 的方式”爱孩子，那么他们就无法 authentically 做自己：他们会成为虚假的父母，假装“一切都没问题”，而内心深处却在伪装自己的 emotions 和 preferences。

孩子看到父母 authentically 对自己有所 wants，却又隐藏那些 wants，于是便学到：“wanting”（尤其是向别人有所求）是不可以的。如果他们得到了 unconditional love，他们或许会觉得，自己“just being”于这个世界，根本上就是没问题的；但向别人提出 demands、接受别人的 demands，甚至拥有自己 strong 的 preferences，都可能让他们觉得有些奇怪。extreme case 是，一个成年人如同一团 undifferentiated 的东西，aimless 地漂过一生，相对 content，却始终没有真正走向何处，直到晚年才开始问自己：“这有什么意义？”或者“我想要的究竟是什么？”

另一种 failure mode——缺少 unconditional love，却有大量 conditional love——或许更容易被看见：一个人工作努力、成就很多，但内心始终感到 empty，仿佛死了一般。他们同样会在晚年问自己：“这有什么意义？”或者“我_真正_想要的究竟是什么”，并发现，即便 satisfying 了自己 strong 而 clear 的 wants，也永远不够。

理解 parental praise 的一种方式，是把父母看作某种 ideal 的更有智慧的 embodiment；这个 ideal 是孩子渴望成为的样子，由父母与孩子共同 co-created。在这种情况下，“我为你骄傲”是来自孩子所 endorsed 的 ideal 的 validation；孩子认为，在评判自己的 ideals 时，这个 ideal 比自己是更好的 judge。最终，孩子可以 internalize 这一点，成长为 healthy adults，能够 self-author 自己的 values，并 set 自己的 goals，但他们总得从某个地方起步！而 copying 自己的父母就是一个不错的起点。这与 client 和 therapist、学生和老师之间的 relation 没有什么不同，只不过 parent-child relation 是 nonconsensual 的（我们并不选择自己的父母）。

最后，这个 concept 对 therapy 的一个 practical implication 是：针对 attachment disturbances 的最有力的 intervention——[Ideal Parent Figure](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0393711528?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback)（IPF）Protocol（guided [meditation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=12bnuBKqGRU) 见这里）——是 incomplete 的：它要求 patient 想象一位 infinitely loving、没有自己 agenda 的父母。这确实是一个非常好的 unconditional love generator，但其中没有任何东西能 build conditional love。

Ideal Parent 从不会与孩子处于 conflict，protocol 也从不让 client 接触父母给出的 healthy 的“不”。我做过 IPF protocol 超过 21 次，它的效果确实如宣传的那样 intensely，但并不适用于所有情况：有一次，某个人对我稍微有些生气，我就 froze 了。我想：“好吧，我们能用 IPF 来处理这个吗”……于是我想到，可以加入这样一位父母：他看见你在做孩子可能想做的各种事情（比如在沙发上跳），然后以 loving 但 firm 的 presence，阻止你做那些他希望你停止做的事。

这很有效，因为在 visualization 中，你可以选择自己想要被阻止做的任何事情：你是在 endorsing 自己的 limits。现实中的父母则必须承受孩子的 anger，因为他们会以孩子尚未能理解的方式 restricting 孩子。因此，相比于如神祇般的 IPF，一位 enlightened、但仍然是独立个体的父母，似乎是用于 interiorizing healthy parental relationships 的更 healthier 的 model。

Schema therapy 确实已将这个 concept 纳入其 domains 之一，即 [Impaired Limits domain](https://claude.ai/share/5eb06633-dc65-4e94-8737-58363950a1c9)，其中包括 entitlement/grandiosity 和 lack of self-control 这些 issues。这里，我借助 LLM-powered 的方式快速尝试了一下，创建一段 IPF-flavored 的 [meditation](https://claude.ai/share/5eb06633-dc65-4e94-8737-58363950a1c9)，以 address 这个 schema。

> 想象自己是一个想要某样东西的孩子——想继续玩、想拿某样东西、想逃掉一项家务、想赢——而 ideal parents 说不，或者说“还不行”，或者“先做这个，再做那个”。需要 evoke 的 crucial details 是：父母在 hold the line 时，脸上仍然保持 warm；他们并不 angry、anxious 或 punitive；他们不说教；他们 tolerate 孩子的 frustration，既不 withdrawing love，也不 giving in。然后——这就是 therapeutic payload——将 attention 引向_之后_发生的事情：frustration crests，随后 passes，孩子发现自己 survived 了这一切，父母依然在那里，依然 warm，而孩子内在的某个部分 settles 了下来。对于 Insufficient Self-Control，你可以把它 extend 成这样一个场景：父母坐在孩子身旁，陪伴孩子完成一项 boring 或 effortful 的 task，把自己的 calm 借给孩子，并庆祝 completion。对于 Entitlement，则可以想象这样一个场景：父母 lovingly insist on reciprocity——轮流时等待自己的轮次、considering 手足的 feelings——同时传达出，孩子受到 loved，是_作为家庭中普通的一员_，而不是因为 special。

你可以根据需要，将这些 insights apply 于其他 Schemas，或者只是采用这一总体想法（“[good enough parent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_enough_parent)”），再 apply 于你自己的具体情况。

## 原文

I used to think that you could love someone unconditionally **or** conditionally, and I never really thought that you could do both at the same time, but it just occurred to me today that this is indeed not only possible but advisable!

Unconditional love needs no introduction: it’s welcoming someone as they are, fully and deeply. A common attitude is that this is the love of parents for their babies and that’s very much as far as we can find it, but it is perfectly possible to love anyone unconditionally. It is in fact the active ingredient in the most potent forms of therapy! (it is also how coach [Joe Hudson](https://www.artofaccomplishment.com/podcast/how-love-gets-confused) makes people [cry](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=a132K8oo88A) in a few minutes).

A [cynic](https://www.reddit.com/r/changemyview/comments/1fvkwlq/cmv_love_is_inherently_conditional/) may say that adult love is not and _cannot_ be like this: you love your friends and spouse for _specific traits_ and if they don’t have or lose those traits, the relationship loses one of its grounds for being. Over time the relationship stabilizes via [crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallization_\(love\)) but it never becomes unconditional, they may say.

I was first introduced to the concept of conditional love as a good thing in an old book from Erich Fromm: [_The Art of Loving_](https://lohiatoday.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/theartofloving.pdf) _(1956):_

> Fatherly love is conditional love. Its principle is "I love you because you fulfill my expectations, because you do your duty, because you are like me." \[...\]
> 
> The infant needs mother's unconditional love and care physiologically as well as psychically. The child, after six, begins to need father's love, his authority and guidance. Mother has the function of making him secure in life, father has the function of teaching him, guiding him to cope with those problems with which the particular society the child has been born into confronts him.\[...\] . Father's love should be guided by principles and expectations; it should be patient and tolerant, rather than threatening and authoritarian. It should give the growing child an increasing sense of competence and eventually permit him to become his own authority and to dispense with that of father \[...\]
> 
> Furthermore, the mature person loves with both the motherly and the fatherly conscience, in spite of the fact that they seem to contradict each other. If he would only retain his fatherly conscience, he would become harsh and inhuman. If he would only retain his motherly conscience, he would be apt to lose judgment and to hinder himself and others in their development. In this development from mother-centered to fathercentered attachment, and their eventual synthesis, lies the basis for mental health and the achievement of maturity. In the failure of this development lies the basic cause for neurosis.

Some readers may prefer to call "conditional love" something else like "conditional approval of behaviors" but to me that's a watered-down mouthful. The best I can do to convey what I'm pointing at with "conditional love" and why I like those words is a brief vignette:

> Father: Son, I'm proud of you. You did well, you worked hard.
> 
> Son: \[tearing up\] thank you dad (hug)
> 
> \[why does the son cry? he's receiving conditional love. The dad in principle could not have been proud yet he saw the son's work, deemed it valued, and gave it a thumbs up.\]

Conditional love is just a reflection of the fact that we have preferences, and that includes parents towards their children, even if they wish they didn’t have such preferences. Parents may wish for their children to not jump on a sofa or to study a certain career or to not eat sweets. If a parent thinks that they are supposed to love their children “only unconditionally” then they won’t be able to authentically be themselves: they’ll be phony parents that pretend that “everything is okay” when deep down they are faking their emotions and preferences.

Seeing that their parents authentically want things from them but hide those wants, children thus learn that “wanting” (particularly from others) is not okay. They may feel fundamentally okay “just being” in the world if they got unconditional love but asking and receiving demands from others, or even having your own strong preferences may feel kind of weird. The extreme case is an adult that drifts through life aimless as an undifferentiated blob of sorts, relatively content, but never really getting anywhere, eventually wondering later in life “what’s the point” or “what is it that I want”.

The other failure mode (lacking unconditional love) but having plenty of conditional love is perhaps more visible: someone that works hard, achieves a lot but always feels empty and dead on the inside. They too will wonder later in life “what’s the point” or “what is it that I _really_ want", noticing that satisfying their strong and clear wants too is never enough.

A way to think of parental praise is thinking of the parent as the wiser embodiment of an ideal the child aspires to be, co-created between parent and child. In that case “I’m proud of you” is validation from an ideal endorsed by the child, deemed as a superior judge of the child’s own ideals than the child themselves. Eventually children can internalize this and grow up as healthy adults that can self-author their values and set their own goals, but they have to start somewhere! And copying their parents is a fine starting point. This is no different from the relation between client and therapist or student and teacher, only that the parent-child relation is nonconsensual (we don't choose our parents).

Lastly, one practical implication for therapy that this concept has is that the most powerful intervention for attachment disturbances, the [Ideal Parent Figure](https://www.amazon.com/dp/0393711528?lv=shuf&channelId=500&plpRedirect=mhFallback) (IPF) Protocol (see here for the guided [meditation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=12bnuBKqGRU)), is incomplete: it asks the patient to imagine an infinitely loving parent that has no agenda of their own. This is indeed a really good unconditional love generator, but there is nothing in it that will build conditional love.

The Ideal Parent is never in conflict with the child, the protocol never exposes the client to a healthy “No” from the parent. I’ve done the IPF protocol more than 21 times and it does work as intensely as advertised, but it does not work for everything: Someone was slightly angry at me and I froze and I thought: “okay could we use the IPF for this”... and I came up with the idea of adding a parent that sees you doing various things that a child might want to do (like jumping on the sofa) and from a place of loving but firm presence, stop you from doing the things the parent wants you to stop doing.

This works well because in the visualization you get to choose whatever is it that you want to be stopped from doing: you are endorsing your own limits. Real life parents will have to bear the anger of their children for restricting them in ways they may not yet understand. Thus, the enlightened but still individual parent (as opposed to the deity-like IPF) seems like a healthier model for interiorizing healthy parental relationships.

Schema therapy does have this concept built in as one of their domains, the [Impaired Limits domain](https://claude.ai/share/5eb06633-dc65-4e94-8737-58363950a1c9), which include the issues of entitlement/grandiosity and lack of self-control. Here I did a quick LLM-powered attempt to create an IPF-flavored [meditation](https://claude.ai/share/5eb06633-dc65-4e94-8737-58363950a1c9) to address this schema.

> Imagine themselves as a child wanting something — to keep playing, to grab something, to skip a chore, to win — and the ideal parents saying no, or "not yet," or "first this, then that." The crucial details to evoke: the parents' faces stay warm while they hold the line; they aren't angry, anxious, or punitive; they don't lecture; they tolerate the child's frustration without withdrawing love and without giving in. Then — and this is the therapeutic payload — guide attention to what happens _after_: the frustration crests, passes, and the child discovers they survived it, the parents are still there, still warm, and something in the child settles. For Insufficient Self-Control, you can extend this into a scene of the parents sitting alongside the child through a boring or effortful task, lending their calm, celebrating completion. For Entitlement, a scene where the parents lovingly insist on reciprocity — waiting a turn, considering a sibling's feelings — while communicating that the child is loved _as an ordinary member of the family_, not for being special.

One could apply these insights as needed for other Schemas, or simply take the general idea (the "[good enough parent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_enough_parent)") and apply it to your particular case.
