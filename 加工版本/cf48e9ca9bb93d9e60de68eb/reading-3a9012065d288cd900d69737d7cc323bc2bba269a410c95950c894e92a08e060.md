# Don't just debias

---

## 正文

人们喜欢谈论如何 avoiding bias。看看关于 [cognitive bias](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_bias) 的大量文献、[Overcoming Bias](http://www.overcomingbias.com/) 博客、Less Wrong 的[首页](http://lesswrong.com/)等等，就知道了。

Reducing bias 是有用的。但为什么人们没有同样在意 variance 呢？

Variance 是你可能出错的另一种方式。如果你正在 making predictions，bias 就是你出错的 _predictable_ 方式——也许你总以为事情所需的时间会比实际更少。另一方面，variance 是你出错方式中的 _unpredictable_ component：也许你的 time estimates 通常会 off by 50%，但 equally split 于 too high 和 too low。在 statistics 中，一个 estimator 的 _total error_（它与 true value 之间的 squared distance）is equal to [它的 variance plus 它的 bias 的 square](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimator#Relationships_among_the_quantities)。

在 statistics 中，我们有 _bias-variance tradeoff_ 这一概念：当你在 comparing 同一 quantity 的 estimators 时，往往可以在一个具有 lower bias 的和一个具有 lower variance 的之间选择。对此的一种 intuition 是，你可以取一个 biased estimator，通过 subtracting 一个关于 bias 的 estimate，使它变成 unbiased——但如果你对 bias 的 estimate 是 incorrect，那么这种 incorrectness 就会被 added 到原来 estimator 的 variance 中，因此你最终会得到一个 higher-variance estimator。事实上，high-variance estimator 往往会有 worse overall error。

如果你有办法得到大量 independent estimates，那么你就不必在意 high variance——你只需得到更多 estimates，并对它们全部 take the average，就能 reduce 这个 variance。但如果你只有一个 single estimate，那么 variance 带来的问题即使不比 bias 更大，也可能同样大。

同样，在现实生活中，仅仅注意到自己有某种 cognitive bias 并不足够。首先，哪怕只是考虑 compensating for 你的 bias，你也不仅需要知道自己_确实_是 biased，还需要知道_程度有多大_。然后，你还需要知道自己并非只是在 trading bias for variance。这意味着，用于“overcoming cognitive bias”的 naive strategies 可能是 ineffective，甚至是 harmful。

例如，为了在 guessing 一个 number 时 avoid anchoring bias，当你认为自己已经被 anchored downwards 时，你可能会尝试将自己的 estimate“shade up”。但你对 anchoring 的 susceptibility 很可能会随时间以及你正在 estimating 的对象而 varies，而且 anchoring bias 的 magnitude 并非 well-understood（据我所知），所以这很可能会向你的 guess 中 introduce 大量额外的 variance。如果不 doing the experiments，我并不清楚进行这种 compensatory de-biasing 是否值得。当然，对于像 confirmation bias 或 self-serving bias 这样 less-easily-measured 的 biases，情况还会更糟。

## 原文

People like to talk about avoiding bias. Witness the large literature on [cognitive bias](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_bias), the blog [Overcoming Bias](http://www.overcomingbias.com/), Less Wrong’s [front page](http://lesswrong.com/), etc.

Reducing bias is useful. But why don’t people care as much about variance?

Variance is another way you can be wrong. If you’re making predictions, bias is the _predictable_ way in which you’re wrong—perhaps you always think things will take less time than they actually do. On the other hand, variance is the _unpredictable_ component of how you’re wrong: perhaps your time estimates are typically off by 50%, but equally split between too high and too low. In statistics, the _total error_ of an estimator (its squared distance from the true value) is equal to [its variance plus the square of its bias](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimator#Relationships_among_the_quantities).

In statistics we have the idea of a _bias-variance tradeoff_: when you’re comparing estimators of the same quantity, you often can choose between one with lower bias, and one with lower variance. One intuition for this is that you can take a biased estimator and make it unbiased by subtracting an estimate of the bias—but if your estimate of the bias is incorrect, then that incorrectness gets added to the original estimator’s variance, so you end up with a higher-variance estimator. Often, in fact, the high-variance estimator will have worse overall error.

If you have a way of getting a lot of independent estimates, then you don’t care about high variance—you can just get more estimates and take the average of all of them to reduce the variance. But if all you have is a single estimate, then variance can be just as much of a problem as bias, if not more.

Similarly, in real life, simply noting that you have some cognitive bias isn’t enough. First of all, to even think about compensating for your bias, you need to know not just _that_ you are biased, but _how much_ so. Then, you need to know that you’re not just trading bias for variance. That means that the naive strategies for “overcoming cognitive bias” can be ineffective or even harmful.

For instance, to avoid anchoring bias when guessing a number, you might try to “shade up” your estimate when you think you’ve been anchored downwards. But your susceptibility to anchoring probably varies by time and by what you’re estimating, and the magnitude of anchoring bias is not well-understood (as far as I know), so this will probably introduce a lot of extra variance into your guess. Without doing the experiments, it’s not clear to me whether it’s worth it to do this kind of compensatory de-biasing. Of course, the situation is even worse for less-easily-measured biases like confirmation bias or self-serving bias.
