哲学（Philo）

-   [《The Cambridge Handbook of Classical Liberal thought》（剑桥 Classical Liberal thought 手册）](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2018/08/cambridge-handbook-of-classical-liberal-thought/)

-   几个月前，我在一条 [footnote](https://nintil.com/2017/12/28/a-breakthrough-in-moral-psychology/) 中提到，探究一下[“Classical utilitarians 的 moralities 比其他 philosophers 的更 similar to 我们的 moralities。（也就是说，Kant 是个 racist）”](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/will-macaskill-moral-philosophy/)这一说法会很有趣。

    -   好吧，我至少找到了一位 philosopher，他和 utilitarians 一样，defended rights of women、opposed slavery、promoted free trade，等等：[Condorcet](http://twin.sci-hub.tw/5795/f8b354a84e9e1550db2828c401c8b13e/10.2307@2639965.pdf)。他也是 explicitly antiutilitarian 的，而且更进一步，他 believed in individual rights；与 utilitarian thinking 相比，这个 idea 与当今普遍的 moral views 更 closely aligned。当然，Condorcet 并没有对所有事情都发表 opinions。Bentham 很早就是 gay rights 的 defender，但他并没有公开大力宣扬这一点，他唯一涉及这个问题的作品是在他去世后才出版的。例如，JS Mill 就没有写过这样的 defence。Absence of evidence 并不是 evidence of absence，所以，当时许多 leading philosophers 可能有 similar opinions，只是不敢把它们写下来。

    -   另一方面，我们也能找到 conservative utilitarians 的例子，只需要[读一读 Mill](http://oll.libertyfund.org/titles/241#lf0223-10_footnote_nt_877_ref) 就行：

    -   > 在 Dr. Whewell 提到的那些站在 orthodoxy 一边、属于 utilitarian school 的众多 writers 之外，还可以加上几位被他遗漏的人，他们至少同样知名；例如 Essays on the Characteristics 的作者 John Brown；Soame Jenyns，以及比他更著名的 reviewer，Dr. Johnson；他们所有人都像 Bentham 一样明确地 laid down 了这一 doctrine：utility 是 morals 的 foundation。这一系列 writers 在 Paley 那里达到了顶峰；他的 treatise 毫不回避或绕弯子地 proclaiming，不仅 expediency 是 virtue 的 end，而且（这是一个很不一样的 doctrine）simple self-interest 是 virtue 的 motive，并从这些 premises 中 deducing 出所有 orthodox conclusions，因而成为 Church of England 的两所 Universities 之一的 moral philosophy text-book。但随后发生了变化，曾经是 orthodoxy 的 defenders 最喜爱的 theory 的 utilitarian doctrine，开始被其 assailants 使用。

    -   粗略考察之下，作者们归于 utilitarians、视为 progressive 的那些 views，似乎即便不是当时大多数 leading intellectuals 所持有的，也至少为其中相当多的人所持有。最初带头反对 slavery 的并不是 utilitarians，而是 Quakers；早期的 animal rights society RSPCA（位于 England）似乎也没有 utilitarian origin。women's rights 也是如此。不过，有人可能会 argue，只有 utilitarians 才把所有“right opinions bundled together”。这不是真的：我已经举过 Condorcet 的例子；但确实，对于那个时代的许多 intellectuals，我们会 agree with 他们的某些 views，却不会 agree with 另外一些。作为 abolition of slavery 的 campaigner 和 animal rights 的 promoter，Wilberforce 也 opposed pornography 和其他“vices”。

    -   我心目中用来与 utilitarians 进行 compare 的 peers，应该出生于 1750 年左右，而且应该是 atheist；但我能找到的大多数人（Godwin、Helvétius、d'Holbach）也都是 utilitarians 或 proto-utilitarians，例外还是 Condorcet。

Philo

-   [The Cambridge Handbook of Classical Liberal thought](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2018/08/cambridge-handbook-of-classical-liberal-thought/)

-   A few months ago, I mentiond in a [footnote](https://nintil.com/2017/12/28/a-breakthrough-in-moral-psychology/) that it would be interesting to look into the claim ["Classical utilitarians had moralities more similar to ours than other philosophers. (i.e. Kant was a racist)"](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/will-macaskill-moral-philosophy/).

    -   Well, I found at least one philosopher who, like the utilitarians, defended the rights of women, opposed slavery, promoted free trade, etc: [Condorcet](http://twin.sci-hub.tw/5795/f8b354a84e9e1550db2828c401c8b13e/10.2307@2639965.pdf).  He was explicitly antiutilitarian too, and to top that off, he believed in individual rights, an idea that is more closely aligned with today's common moral views than utilitarian thinking. Surely, Condorcet did not voice opinions on everything. Bentham was an early defender of gay rights, but he wasn't outspoken about it, his only work dealing with the issue was published after he died. JS Mill, for example, did not write such a defence. Absence of evidence is no evidence of absence, so it could have been the case that many leading philosophers of the time had similar opinions, but they did not dare write them down.

    -   On the other hand, we can also find examples of conservative utilitarians, and we just have to [read Mill](http://oll.libertyfund.org/titles/241#lf0223-10_footnote_nt_877_ref) for that:

    -   > To the many writers on the side of orthodoxy, of the utilitarian school, mentioned by Dr. Whewell, might be added several, of at least equal note, whom he has omitted; as John Brown, the author of Essays on the Characteristics; Soame Jenyns, and his more celebrated reviewer, Dr. Johnson; all of whom, as explicitly as Bentham, laid down the doctrine that utility is the foundation of morals. This series of writers attained its culmination in Paley, whose treatise, proclaiming without evasion or circumlocution, not only expediency as the end, but (a very different doctrine) simple self-interest as the motive, of virtue, and deducing from these premises all the orthodox conclusions, became the text-book of moral philosophy in one of the two Universities of the Church of England. But a change ensued, and the utilitarian doctrine, which had been the favourite theory of the defenders of orthodoxy, began to be used by its assailants.

    -   From casual examination, the views the authors attribute to the utilitarians as progressive seem to have been held by a substantial number, if not most, of the leading intellectuals of the day. The charge against slavery was initially led not by utilitarians but by Quakers, and the early animal rights society RSPCA (in England) doesn't seem to have had an utilitarian origin. Ditto for women's rights. It can be argued, however, that only utilitarians have all "the right opinions bundled together". This is not true: I have given the example of Condorcet, though it is true that many intellectuals of that time, we would agree with some of their views but not the others. Wilberforce, the campaigner for the abolition of slavery and promoter of animal rights, also opposed pornography and other "vices".

    -   The peers I have in mind to compare with the utilitarians should have been born around 1750, and they should be atheist, but most of those who I can find (Godwin, Helvétius, d'Holbach) were also utilitarians or proto-utilitarians, with the exception, again, of Condorcet.

    -   Perhaps it would be ore useful to look further into the past, at the Leveller movement
