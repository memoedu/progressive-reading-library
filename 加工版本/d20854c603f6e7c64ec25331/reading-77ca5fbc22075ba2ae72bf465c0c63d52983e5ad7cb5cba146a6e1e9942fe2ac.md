# P-zombies do not imply physicalism is false

---

## 正文

在[上一篇文章](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/07/10/p-zombies-are-still-undead/)中，我反对 Eliezer Yudkowsky、Robin Hanson 等人的看法，argued Philosophical zombies 是 conceivable。在这篇文章中，我将 challenge 针对 physicalism 的 P-zombie argument。

P-zombies 通常被 invoked 来 argue [physicalism](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/) 是 false。philosophers 似乎都同意情况如此：

> 看来，如果 zombies 确实是 possible，那么 physicalism 就是 false，而某种 dualism 就是 true。对于许多 philosophers 而言，这就是 zombie idea 最重要的意义。$$...$$
> 
> 然而，并非所有人都同意 physicalism entails zombies 的 impossibility。一种 suggestion 是，physicalists 可以承认存在 possible worlds，它们在所有 purely physical 方面都是我们这个 world 的 exact duplicates，但在那些 worlds 中，那些在我们的 world 中 give rise to consciousness 的 physical properties 却受到阻碍，无法这样做：那些 worlds 中有些东西会 block consciousness。在这种情况下，physicalists 可以 consistently allow zombie worlds 的 possibility（Leuenberger 2008。关于这样的 ‘blockers’，参见 Hawthorne 2002b；Chalmers 2010, 163-165）。然而，这种 approach 显然与坚持 conscious states 要么 identical with、要么 constituted by physical 或 functional states 是 inconsistent 的。因此，physicalists 能否 consistently allow consciousness-blockers 的 possibility，尚不清楚。([SEP](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/))

这里给出这个 [argument](https://www.reddit.com/r/askphilosophy/comments/356oux/is_the_philosophical_zombie_argument_correct_ie/)：

> 1.  Conceivably，存在你的一个 _physical duplicate_（即在每个方面都 physically identical 的东西），它 lacks qualia。
> 2.  Conceivability 会 implies possibility。
> 3.  Therefore，possibly，存在你的一个 physical duplicate，它 lacks qualia。
> 4.  Therefore，你的 qualia 不是 physical states。（因为 identity 是 necessary；如果你的 qualia 与你的 brain 的（proper subparts）是 identical to 关系，那么拥有这个 brain 却没有这些 qualia 就是 impossible 的。）
> 5.  Therefore，存在一个并非 completely physical 的 mind。
> 6.  Therefore，token-physicalism 是 false。

请注意，physicalists 通常认为 Physicalism 是 contingent truth，而不是 necessary truth。也就是说，按照 physicalists 的看法，可以有 non-physicalist worlds。它只是在我们的 universe 中碰巧为 true，就像 planets exist 那样，而不像 1+1=2 那样。

> 有些 philosophers（例如 Davidson 1970）认为，physicalism 如果确实为 true，那就是 conceptual 或 necessary truth。但大多数人认为它是 contingent，是一个关于我们这个 world 的 truth，而这个 world 原本可能并非如此。([SEP](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/))

如果 physicalism 是 necessary truth，那么 P-zombies 就会与它 incompatible，因而 physicalism 就会被这个 thought experiment 所 refuted。但它并不是 necessary truth，因此 P-zombies 对它并不构成问题。P-zombies argument 仅仅 shows，可以存在 physicalism 为 false 的 universes。

举个 example：可以同时把以下四个 propositions 都视为 true：

1.  在我们的 universe 中，physicalism 是 true。Consciousness 是一种 epiphenomenon。某些 physical systems 会 give rise to 它，但它本身不起任何作用。
2.  在另一个 universe 中，physicalism 是 true。Consciousness 是一种 real causal entity，它 affects environment，也受到 environment 的影响。（这似乎就是 Eliezer Yudkowsky 在[这里](http://lesswrong.com/r/discussion/lw/nqv/zombies_redacted/)的 position）
3.  在其他 universes 中，physicalism 是 false。Dualism 是 true。
4.  在其他 universes 中，physicalism 是 true。Consciousness 并不 exist，而那个 universe 中的 beings 是 P-zombies。

也就是说，consciousness 在不同的 universes 中可能以不同方式 work。当然，除非有人提出令人信服的 argument，说明某个 consciousness 的 model 是 necessary truth。

Universes 1 和 2 是 externally similar 的，只有知道它们各自相关的 physical laws，你才能看出哪一个有 P-zombies，哪一个有 regular humans。

不过，或许有一种办法可以把它们区分开：设想我们发现了 consciousness 在我们的 universe 中如何 works，并且能够依据 systems 的 external characteristics，确切地判断哪些是 conscious、哪些不是。那么，就会有一组 physical laws（也许还有 fundamental consciousness particles 或类似的东西），能够 explains consciousness 如何 works。如果我们接着前往 Universe 2，那些 laws 就不会在那里，因此你就会知道，那是一个 P-zombie universe。

一种 reply 可能是：Universe 2 与 Universe 1 有相同的 physical laws 和 fundamental entities，却仍然 lack consciousness。但这对 physicalism 而言是 begs the question。我主张，不可能 conceive 在一个像我们的 universe 这样的 universe 中，有像我们这样的 beings，具备所有 govern consciousness 的 laws，却是 unconscious。如果那些 laws 存在，_它们就会是_ conscious。

如果我在这里 outlined 的内容是对的，那么我就是在没有做过完整 literature review 的情况下，反对 philosophers 的 consensus，因此我的 conclusion 是 tentative。我再陈述一遍我的 argument：

1.  Physicalism 不是 metaphysically necessary（philosophers 的 Consensus）
2.  各种 consciousness 的 models 都是 metaphysically possible（philosophers 的 Consensus？）
3.  P-zombies 是 conceivable（来自上一篇文章）
4.  P-zombies 是 possible（由 3 得出）
5.  存在这样的 possible universes：其中 physicalism 是 true/false，而 P-zombies 会 exist/不会 exist（由 1,2,4 得出）
6.  Hence，physicalism 的 truth 与 P-zombie thesis 的 truth 是 independent 的（任何 combination 都是 possible）（由 5 和 independence 的 Definition 得出）
7.  Hence，accepting 关于其中一者的某个 conclusion，并不 entail 关于另一者的任何内容。
8.  Hence，accepting P-zombies 的 possibility 并不 refute physicalism。

## 原文

In a [previous post](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/07/10/p-zombies-are-still-undead/) I argued against Eliezer Yudkowsky, Robin Hanson, and others, that Philosophical zombies are conceivable. In this one I challenge the P-zombie argument against physicalism.

P-zombies are usually invoked to argue that [physicalism](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/) is false. Philosophers seem to agree that this is the case:

> It seems that if zombies really are possible, then physicalism is false and some kind of dualism is true. For many philosophers that is the chief importance of the zombie idea. \[...\]
> 
> However, not everyone agrees that physicalism entails the impossibility of zombies. One suggestion is that physicalists can concede there are possible worlds which are exact duplicates of our world in all purely physical respects, but where the physical properties which give rise to consciousness in our world are prevented from doing so: there are items in those worlds which block consciousness. In that case physicalists can consistently allow the possibility of zombie worlds (Leuenberger 2008. On such ‘blockers’ see Hawthorne 2002b; Chalmers 2010, 163-165). This approach, however, is obviously inconsistent with maintaining that conscious states are either identical with or constituted by physical or functional states. So it is not clear that physicalists can consistently allow the possibility of consciousness-blockers.([SEP](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/))

Here, [the argument](https://www.reddit.com/r/askphilosophy/comments/356oux/is_the_philosophical_zombie_argument_correct_ie/):

> 1.  Conceivably, there is a _physical duplicate_ (i.e. something physically identical in every way) of you that lacks qualia.
> 2.  Conceivability implies possibility.
> 3.  Therefore, possibly, there is a physical duplicate of you that lacks qualia.
> 4.  Therefore, your qualia are not physical states. (Because identity is necessary; if your qualia are identical to (proper subparts of) your brain, then it's impossible to have the brain without the qualia.)
> 5.  Therefore, there is a mind that's not completely physical.
> 6.  Therefore, token-physicalism is false.

Physicalism, note, is usually considered by physicalists to be a contingent truth, not a necessary one. That is, there can be non-physicalist worlds, according to physicalists. It just so happens that it is true in our universe, in the same way that planets exist, but not in the same way that 1+1=2.

> Some philosophers (e.g. Davidson 1970) have thought of physicalism as a conceptual or necessary truth, if it is true at all. But most have thought of it as contingent, a truth about our world which might have been otherwise. ([SEP](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/))

If physicalism were a necessary truth, then P-zombies would be incompatible with it, and so physicalism would be refuted by the thought experiment. But it is not a necessary truth, so P-zombies pose no problem to it. The P-zombies argument just shows that there can be universes where physicalism is false.

An example: It is possible to hold these four propositions as true at the same time:

1.  In our universe, physicalism is true. Consciousness is an epiphenomenon. Certain physical systems give rise to it, but it doesn't do anything.
2.  In another universe, physicalism is true. Consciousness is a real causal entity that affects, and is affected by, the environment. (This seems to be Eliezer Yudkowsky's position [here](http://lesswrong.com/r/discussion/lw/nqv/zombies_redacted/))
3.  In other universes, physicalism is false. Dualism is true.
4.  In other universes, physicalism is true. Consciousness does not exist, and beings are P-zombies in that universe.

That is, consciousness could work differently in different universes. That is, unless someone has a convincing argument for one model of consciousness as a necessary truth.

Universes 1 and 2 are externally similar, and only if you knew the relevant physical laws in each of the you could see which one has P-zombies and which one has regular humans.

There could be, though, one way to tell them apart: imagine that we discover how consciousness works in our universe, and which precise systems are conscious are which aren't based on external characteristics of them. Then, there would be a set of physical laws (And perhaps fundamental consciousness particles or something like that) that explains how consciousness works. If we then go to Universe 2, those laws wouldn't be there, and so you would know that that is a P-zombie universe.

A reply could be that Universe 2 has the same physical laws and fundamental entities than Universe 1, and still lack consciousness. But that begs the question against physicalism. I claim that it is not possible to conceive of beings like us in an universe like ours, with whatever laws govern consciousness, but that are unconscious. If those laws are there, _they will be_ conscious.

If I am right in what I outlined here, I find myself against the consensus of philosophers without having done a full literature review, so my conclusion is tentative. I state my argument once more:

1.  Physicalism is not metaphysically necessary (Consensus of philosophers)
2.  Various models of consciousness are metaphysically possible (Consensus of philosophers?)
3.  P-zombies are conceivable (From previous post)
4.  P-zombies are possible (From 3)
5.  There are possible universes where physicalism is true/false and P-zombies exist/do not exist (From 1,2,4)
6.  Hence, the truth of physicalism and of the P-zombie thesis are independent (Any combination is possible)  (From 5, Definition of independence)
7.  Hence, accepting one conclusion regarding one of them does not entail anything about the other.
8.  Hence, accepting the possibility of P-zombies does not refute physicalism.
