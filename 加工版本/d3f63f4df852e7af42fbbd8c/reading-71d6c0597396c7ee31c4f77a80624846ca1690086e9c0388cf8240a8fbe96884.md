# Small company or big company?

---

## 正文

一位最近正在 job search 的朋友问我：

> 我很可能会面临一个选择：去一家 larger company 工作……还是去一家 startup……我想知道，你对这个问题有没有什么特别的看法。
> 
> 长远来看，我希望去一家 non-startup 工作，这是出于对 hours/general quality of life，以及 salary 和 stability 的考虑，要兼顾家庭生活，而且我感兴趣的 projects 通常未必有良好的 short-term revenue 前景。……我担心，startups 往往在 crowded marketplace 中与其他 startups 进行 competing，\[而在\] startup 工作就意味着要 selecting 一家最终会“win”的公司，要么通过在 industry 中 getting a hold，要么通过 getting bought up。Choosing 一家“loses”的公司，是否意味着我的工作可能完全白费？……当然，Larger companies 在 significantly shaking things up 方面的过往表现也不怎么样。

我是这样回复的：

-   我不认为 startup vs big company 是 quality of life 的 high order bit。比如，Wave 的大多数人都保持合理的工作时长，也休很多假；而我认识的、在 Microsoft 工作过的人，真的是每一个，都说那里的 work environment 是 dystopian。[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/bigco/#fn:1)

-   一家 startup 的 success 的 determining factor 不是 competition，而是它们能否真正做出足够好的东西，让自己不至于 die。许多 spaces 都有不止一家 successful companies，或者有一些 successful products，它们过去分别属于不同的 companies（随便举些例子——ridesharing：Uber/Lyft/Grab/Didi/等等；messaging apps：Whatsapp/Messenger/Line/Viber/WeChat/Telegram/Signal；money transfer：WorldRemit/Azimo/Remitly）。确实，在这些 spaces 中，top company 的 valuable 程度往往是 second biggest 的 5x，但这并不意味着 second biggest company 就是 useless。首先，preventing 一个 monopoly 的 emerging 就能 produces 大量 social value :P 从这个意义上说，在 second most successful company 工作并帮助它们 compete，实际上可能让你拥有 higher leverage。

-   不过，确实，如果你在一家正在 making 一个 bad product 的 startup 工作，你的工作就会是 useless。不要这样做（只要你能 a priori 判断出来）。但你的 uncertainty 的焦点不应该是“这家 startup 会不会被 competitors killed”，而应该是“这家 startup 会不会 make 出比 status quo 好 10x 的东西”，后者更容易 evaluate。

-   你可以通过一家 startup 是否正在 growing really quickly（而且它的 retention 是否 good），来判断它是否\[已经\] made 出了人们想要的东西。在 early stages，[Paul Graham 说](http://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html)，5-7% \[每周\]被认为是 good，10%+ 则被认为是 exceptional；对于 retention 的 benchmarks，我了解得没那么多，因为这取决于 product。Fast growth 确实很好，不仅因为它意味着你在做 valuable 的事情，还因为你会被迫做许多新事情，并 solve 许多 growth-induced problems。Fast user growth 通常会 translates directly into fast personal growth（至少我在 Wave 的经历是这样）。

-   \[对于你感兴趣的领域而言\]，缺少 short term revenue opportunities 是一个更有说服力的、不去 startups 工作的理由，但也有一些 startups 计划先 raise 大量 money，再长期做 R&D（例如 Magic Leap）。

-   Salary：任何好的 startup 给你的 pay 都应该远远足够维持生活\[对于一个刚大学毕业、没有 dependents 的人而言\]，即使在 NYC 也是如此，前提是你不大幅提高自己的生活水平。不过，big company 很可能让你更快地 pay off loans 或买房。补充：我之前可能低估了这里的 delta；可以看看 [comp.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/comp.html)，那里有一些关于各家 big companies 可能给出多少 pay 的杂乱 data。

-   总的来说，我认为，如果你已经知道一家 company 的 product、plans 和 work environment 是什么样的，那么 startup vs. established company 这个 distinction 就不会 add 任何 information（补充：除非你是在 optimizing for money）。也就是说，它可能在你最初的 sourcing phase 有用（与其查看“augmented reality startups”的名单并逐一 evaluate 每个 opportunity，不如查看“big company augmented reality initiatives”的名单并做同样的事，这可能更值得花时间）——但一旦你已经到了有几个 accepted job offers 的阶段，就应该主要看这些 jobs 的实际细节。

-   再重申一个更具体但很重要的点：如果你在乎 quality of life，就绝对不应该去 Microsoft 工作。

## 原文

A friend doing a job search recently asked me:

> A choice I will likely have is whether to work at a larger company… or a startup… I was wondering if you had any particular feelings on this question.
> 
> In the long run I’m hoping to work at a non-startup due to hours/general quality of life as well as salary and stability for the considerations of family life and the fact that the projects I’m interested in don’t necessarily have good prospects as short-term revenue generally. … I worry that startups are often competing with other startups in a crowded marketplace \[and that working\] at a startup means selecting one that will eventually “win” either through getting a hold in the industry or getting bought up. Choosing one that “loses” means my work may go entirely to waste? … Larger companies of course don’t have a great track record at significantly shaking things up.

Here’s what I replied with:

-   I don’t think startup vs big company is the high order bit for quality of life. For instance, most people at Wave work reasonable hours and take lots of vacation, whereas literally everyone I know who’s worked at Microsoft says the work environment is dystopian.[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/bigco/#fn:1)

-   The determining factor in a startup’s success isn’t competition, it’s whether they can actually make something good enough that they don’t die. Many spaces have multiple successful companies, or successful products that used to be different companies (random examples—ridesharing: Uber/Lyft/Grab/Didi/etc.; messaging apps: Whatsapp/Messenger/Line/Viber/WeChat/Telegram/Signal; money transfer: WorldRemit/Azimo/Remitly). It’s true that the top company is often 5x more valuable than the second biggest in these spaces, but that doesn’t mean the second biggest company is useless. For one thing, preventing a monopoly from emerging produces a ton of social value :P In that sense, you might actually have higher leverage by working at the second most successful company and helping them compete.

-   That said, it’s true that if you work at a startup that is making a bad product your work will be useless. Don’t do that (to the extent that you can tell a priori). But your point of uncertainty shouldn’t be “will this startup be killed by competitors,” it should be “is this startup going to make something 10x better than the status quo,” which is easier to evaluate.

-   You can tell whether a startup has \[already\] made something people want by whether it’s growing really quickly (and its retention is good). At the early stages [Paul Graham says](http://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html) 5-7% \[a week\] is considered good and 10%+ is considered exceptional; I don’t know as much about benchmarks for retention as it depends on the product. Fast growth is really good not only because it means you’re doing something valuable, but also because you will be forced to do a lot of new stuff and solve lots of growth-induced problems. Fast user growth usually translates directly into fast personal growth (or that’s been my experience at Wave).

-   Lack of short term revenue opportunities \[for your areas of interest\] is a more compelling reason not to work at startups, but there are also startups that plan to raise a lot of money and then do R&D for a long time (e.g. Magic Leap).

-   Salary: any good startup should pay you more than enough to live on \[as a recent college grad with no dependents\], even in NYC, if you don’t ratchet up your standard of living a bunch. But a big company would probably let you pay off loans or buy a house a lot faster. EDIT: I probably understated the delta here; check out [comp.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/comp.html) for messy data on what various big companies might pay.

-   In general I think the startup vs. established company distinction doesn’t add any information if you already know what the company’s product, plans and work environment are like (EDIT: unless you’re optimizing for money). That is, it might be useful in your initial sourcing phase (it might be a better use of time to look at a list of “big company augmented reality initiatives” and evaluate each opportunity, than to do the same for “augmented reality startups”)—but once you’re at the level of having a few accepted job offers you should be looking mainly at the actual details of the jobs.

-   To reiterate a more specific but important point, if you care about quality of life you should DEFINITELY not work at Microsoft.
