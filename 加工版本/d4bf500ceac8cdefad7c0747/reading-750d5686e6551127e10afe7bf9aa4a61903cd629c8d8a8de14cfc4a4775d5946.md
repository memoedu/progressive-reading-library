# 起点

---

## 正文

我大约在 2 年前开始 meditate。从那以后，尤其是在过去这一年里，我的日常 experience 出现了一些变化，我觉得值得记录下来，供将来参考，也希望能激励其他可能正在考虑开始 meditation practice 的人。

需要提醒的是，meditation 并不是我在这段时间里唯一做过的事。我妈妈在今年早些时候[去世](https://nintil.com/mom/)了，而我此前还连续几个月，每周两次接受一种格外 intense 的 [somatic psychotherapy](https://joannlovascio.com/)。我不能声称，如果没有这些其他事情，我仍然会有如今这些 experience。我绝不是在说“meditate 两年，你就能到我现在的程度”。当然，有些人花十年才能到我达到的程度，另一些人的 progress 则比我快得多。但我达到的状态，似乎符合人们预期通过 meditates 会获得的那些变化，因此我仍然觉得这篇文章的标题是恰当的。

我所做的 meditation 类型随着时间发生了变化：最初，我大致按照 The Mind Illuminated 这本书的方法来做，后来转向 just sitting（或 shikantaza），之后又转向 anapanasati。在这个过程中，我也偶尔做 jhana 和 metta practice，但并不规律；而且，我越来越多地在做某种 nondual practice，有点像 just sitting，但又是 resting in awareness。

这段时间里，我参加了 4 次每次约一周的 retreats，以及 3 次较短的周末 retreats。其中两次严格来说不是 meditation retreats，而是更偏向 embodiment/feelings 的[某种活动](https://sleepawake.camp/)。

最后，这里我关注的是自己 state 的 permanent changes，而不是一次性的 attainments 或 experiences。我有过一些在这个过程中常会遇到的经典 insights：impermanence、emptiness、no-self，以及对 [nonduality](https://nintil.com/nonduality/) 的 glimpses，也 experienced 过具有特定风味的 altered states（[jhanas](https://nintil.com/jhanas/)）。但我的 default experience 并不是时时刻刻都充分具有这些 insights 的力量。

## 起点

我觉得，解释一下刚开始时我的 mind 处于怎样的状态很有用，因为不同人的 minds 差别很大，而很多 meditation 文章对此讨论不足。

-   我没有 inner monologue。在我的记忆中，我从来没有过，尽管最近我学会了随意“开启”它，并在其中发现了大量 self-hate，随后便迅速将其 uproot。我猜这就是它一开始被关闭的原因。
-   因此，如果有人问我平时在想些什么，我当时会回答：“大多数时候，我没有 thoughts。”这是因为我把 thoughts 等同于“verbal 或 image-based 的 internal simulations of reality”。我仍然在琢磨这个问题。thoughts 能不能是 body 中的 sensations，而不是在 mind's ear 中听到的 words，或在 mind's eye 中看到的 images？人能有 unconscious thoughts 吗？现在我倾向于回答能，但当然，由于它们自身的性质，刚开始时这一点对我并不明显。我确实能感到一种可高可低的“background pre-thought brain noise”。在旧金山四处散步时，它似乎相当低，只有走路、阳光、微风，以及我的 attention 在 awareness 中的各种 sensations 和 objects 之间游移。过去没有、现在也没有那么多 elaboration 在发生。
-   我一开始就有相当好的 attention control 和 meta-cognitive awareness。那时，我能够将它铺得很宽，只是与自己的全部 experience 同在，也能像激光一样将它 focus 得很窄，去看某样东西，同时不丢失对自己所处 context 的把握。也许这是我童年不停读书的结果。我怀疑，这也是我很少迟到的原因。
-   我过去很少 bored，现在仍然如此。我可以边散步边看周围的东西，而不觉得需要听音乐或有声书，也可以只是坐在那里看树，而不需要改变什么。这并不能用 seek novel experiences 的 motivation 较低来解释。在 personality 的 Openness to Experience dimension 上，我的排名往往很高；我喜欢学习新东西，也喜欢古怪的派对创意，而且在 [Burning Man](https://nintil.com/burning-man/) 玩得非常开心。
-   我当时相当 disembodied（coordination 较差，难以在自己的 body 中 feeling emotions），而且 dissociated。我很少哭。
-   我过去有时会感到大量 blindingly intense anger，让我几个小时、甚至一整天都 nonproductive（尤其是在工作场合，每当我认为某个人没有做、说或想“正确”的事情时）

-   我深深 trust，meditation 和 awakening 都非常值得追求！
-   我会把 meditation 的整体 effects 概括为“给 experience 的齿轮加上润滑油”，一切都感觉更顺畅。我的 experience 的“frames”flow 得更流畅：我正在做 X，接着就在做 Y，而不会回想 X，或 projecting 到下一件事 Z。
-   我现在远没有以前那么 anxious，尽管那时我并不会把自己描述为一个有 anxiety 的人，而是认为自己的 anxiety 处于正常人的水平；过去，我的 solar plexus 有一个 semi-permanent 的 anxiety 结团（与我大学时走进考场前会有的那种 sensation 相同，只是弱一些），如今它大多已经消失了。过去，我会认为 anxiety 是 human experience 中不可避免的一部分。现在我不再这样想。你确实可以 permanently get rid of 自己大部分、甚至全部的 anxiety。
-   我的 doubts 少了，对正在发生的事情有了更多 trust，也更深切地感到，我此刻正在做的就是我“应该”做的。事实上，我现在会把出现 doubt 的时刻（我应该做 X 还是 Y？）看作 stuck emotion 或类似东西的 evidence。这可能也是我目前没有全职工作的原因之一；辞职时我有些 fear，而我凭着对自己以后能找到出路的些许 faith，overcome 了它。
-   我不再那么容易被 counterfactuals（“如果你当时做了 X，而不是 Y，会怎样？”）攫住，因为我现在感到，只可能发生一件事，而那件事就是在当时情境下应该发生的正确的事。这既很酷，也很奇怪：几周前，我的车在洛杉矶抛锚，不得不叫拖车，之后我还得坐飞机过去，再把它往北开回来。即便是_那件事_，我也有同样的感觉，尽管我多少还是希望它没有发生。
-   很难描述这种变化：colors 更 brighter，flavors 更 intense，space 也更 spacious。我的 sense of smell 可能也更 sharper 了。换一种说法，我的 experience 更 denser 或更 richer 了。
-   我以前说话非常快，可能与 anxiety 有关，如今我的语速比以前慢了。
-   以前，待在满屋都是说话声的地方，我会 sensorially overwhelmed，似乎是我的 attention 在试图理解周围的一切。现在，我更能把它当作一种 social soundbath，并将自己的 attention focus 在我所处的 conversational space 的 narrow slice 上，因此 overwhelmed 的程度轻了很多。
-   在 social situations 中，我有时会在 micro（交谈期间）和 macro level experience 各种各样的 stuckness。这类情况的一些 examples：
    -   Macro：迟到进入派对时，我会 experience 一些 discomfort，觉得加入一个已经开始的活动很 awkward，不知道该说什么。后来，我逐渐注意到，迟到进入派对意味着先看到 human connection 正在发生，接着因为自己还没有参与其中而 sad，而那份 sadness getting stuck，让我更难加入。如今，我基本上就是直接走进去，trust 自己会和对的人交谈，说出对的话。
    -   Micro：交谈时，如果对方的视线越过我、四处张望，我就会因为自己不是个有趣的人而 feel bad，而那股 sad aura 会伴随我一会儿。现在，我只把它看作这段交谈或许该结束的信号，并欣然将其视为与新的人交谈、或做其他事情的机会。这种新的 action 似乎会自行发生，不需要我刻意尝试。同样，我也会注意到自己何时开始做同样的事，而不是强迫自己继续和面前的人交谈。
-   我隐约感到，experience 的每个 frame 都藏着一个 teaching、一个彩蛋，或某种类似的东西。每个 frame 似乎都更有趣了。
-   我 feel 到更多 emotions，而它们也更快地得到 processed；它们要么得到 accepted（就像好的 psychotherapy 会引导你去做的那样，针对那些重大的、stuck 的 trauma-type 东西），要么转变为 action。
-   我甚至不记得上一次自己 got triggered 是什么时候了，现在我远不那么 take things personally 了。
-   我的 conversational approach 已经转向更接近 Art of Accomplishment 所称的 [VIEW](https://www.artofaccomplishment.com/podcast/introduction-to-view)（尽管我并没有刻意尝试这样做，除了他们的 Connection Course，也没有做过太多 AoA work）。例如，如果有人告诉我他们去巴塞罗那旅行，以及他们在圣家堂的 experience，我不太会插话，喋喋不休地说起自己对那座建筑的 experience，而是会问他们的 experience。我猜，随着我注意到并着手处理自己的 insecurities（meditation 最终会把它们带出来），我对被看见的 craving 也减少了。
    -   交谈中分享的内容，常常带着一种“妈妈你看！快看！这是我做的！我做成了一件事！”的意味，让人感觉很急迫，而且无视了先前说过的话。最近，我开始在自己和别人身上注意到这类情况。
    -   这种 behavior 的极端 case：A：“我有过一段 sad experience…… :(”B：“哦，是啊，我也有过一段 sad experience！让我告诉你！10 年前……”。\[相比之下，Enlightened 的 B：“哦，再多说说？”\] B 显然有这个问题。我在这里要说的是，这个问题比我以前想的更深，也更微妙。

## 原文

I started to meditate around 2 years ago. Since then, and particularly within this past year, there are a number of changes in my day to day experience that seemed worth documenting for future reference and hopefully as motivation for others that may be considering starting a meditation practice.

A note of caution is that meditation is not the only thing that I've done during this time. My mom [died](https://nintil.com/mom/) earlier this year, and I had been doing a particularly intense form of [somatic psychotherapy](https://joannlovascio.com/) for a few months twice a week. I cannot claim that absent these other things I would have experienced what I have experienced. I am definitely not saying "meditate for two years and you'll get to where I am". Certainly some people take a decade to get where I got and others make much faster progress than I have. But the place where I got seems to line up with the sorts of changes one is expected to get if one meditates, so the title of this post still strikes me as fair.

The sort of meditation I've been doing has changed over time: initially I was roughly doing what the book The Mind Illuminated prescribed, then I switched to just sitting (or shikantaza), then anapanasati. Along the way I did jhana and metta practice here and there but not regularly, and increasingly I've been doing some form of nondual practice that's kind of like just sitting but resting in awareness.

During this period of time I did 4 ~week long retreats and 3 shorter weekend ones. Two of those retreats were not strictly meditation retreats but rather [something](https://sleepawake.camp/) more embodiment/feelings oriented.

Lastly, here I focus on permanent changes in my state, not one-off attainments or experiences. I've had some of the classic insights one encounters along the way: impermanence, emptiness, no-self, and glimpses of [nonduality](https://nintil.com/nonduality/) and experienced particular flavors of altered states ([jhanas](https://nintil.com/jhanas/)). But my default experience does not have the full force of these insights all the time.

## The starting point

I think it's useful to explain where my mind was at when I started, because minds are very different and this goes underdiscussed in lots of meditation writing.

-   I had no inner monologue. I have never had one that I can remember, though I recently learned to "turn it on" at will, finding a bunch of self-hate in it, which I then proceeded to swiftly uproot. I guess that's why it was off to begin with.
-   Accordingly, I would have replied that "Most of the time I have no thoughts" if asked what I usually think about. This is because I equated thoughts with "verbal or image-based internal simulations of reality". I am still grappling with this question. Can thoughts be sensations in the body instead of words one hears in the mind's ear or images one sees in the mind's eye? Can one have unconscious thoughts? Now I am inclined to say yes, but of course by their own nature this wasn't obvious to me when I started. I do have a sense of "background pre-thought brain noise" that can be higher or lower. Walking around San Francisco it seems fairly low and there's just the walking, the sun, the breeze, and my attention going around various sensations and objects in my awareness. There wasn't and isn't that much elaboration going on.
-   I started with fairly good attention control and meta-cognitive awareness. Back then I could spread it wide to just be with all of my experience or focus it narrowly like a laser to look at something, without losing track of the context I'm in. Perhaps I got this way spending my childhood reading books nonstop. I suspect this is behind why I am rarely late to things.
-   I was and continue to be rarely bored. I can be going on a walk looking at what's around me without feeling the need to listen to music or an audiobook, or just sit there looking at trees without needing to change anything. This is not explained by a low motivation to seek novel experiences. I tend to rank really high in the Openness to Experience dimension of personality, enjoy learning new things, and enjoy weird party concepts, and had a great time at [Burning Man](https://nintil.com/burning-man/).
-   I was quite disembodied (poor coordination, difficulty feeling emotions in my body) and dissociated. I rarely cried.
-   I used to feel a lot of blindingly intense anger sometimes that would leave me nonproductive for hours if not the whole day (especially in a work setting, whenever I thought someone wasn't doing, saying, or thinking the 'right' thing)

-   There is a deep trust that meditation and awakening are very worthwhile things to pursue!
-   I would summarize the effects of meditation overall as "oil for the gears of experience", everything feels more smooth. The "frames" of my experience flow more nicely: I am doing X then I'm doing Y without thinking back about X or projecting into the next thing Z.
-   I am way less anxious, though back then I wouldn't have self-described as someone with anxiety, I thought I had the normal human level of it; I used to have this semi-permanent knot of anxiety in my solar plexus (a weaker version of the same sensation I used to have before going into an exam in college), now that's mostly gone. In the past I would have seen anxiety as an unavoidable part of the human experience. Now I don't think so anymore. You can indeed permanently get rid of most if not all of your anxiety.
-   I have fewer doubts and more trust in what's happening, there's a deeper sense that what I'm doing right now is what I "should" be doing. In fact I now see moments of doubt (Should I do X or Y?) as evidence of a stuck emotion or something like that. This probably contributed to me not currently being employed full-time; there was some fear in quitting which was overcome with some faith that I'll figure things out down the road.
-   I am less grabbed by counterfactuals ("What if you had done X instead of Y?"), as there's now a sense that only one thing can happen and that one thing is the right thing that should happen given the situation. This is both cool and weird: a few weeks ago my car broke down in LA and had to get towed, and I then had to fly there and drive it back up. Even _that_ felt that way, even if I had some preference for it not to have happened.
-   In a way that's hard to describe, the colors are brighter, the flavors more intense, and the space is more spacious. I might have a sharper sense of smell as well. Another way of saying that is that my experience is denser or richer.
-   I used to speak very fast which was probably anxiety related, now I speak slower than I used to.
-   I used to be sensorially overwhelmed in rooms full of people talking, seemingly with my attention trying to understand everything around me. Now I am better able to take that as a sort of social soundbath and focus my attention on the narrow slice of the conversational space I'm in, so I am far less overwhelmed.
-   In social situations, sometimes I would experience a whole variety of stuckness at the micro (during a conversation) and macro level. Some examples of this sort of thing:
    -   Macro: I would experience some discomfort at arriving at a party late, feeling awkward at joining something that had already started, wondering what to say. I then progressively noticed that coming to a party late involved witnessing human connection happening, then feeling sad I'm not yet in it, and that sadness getting stuck making it more difficult for me to join. Now, I mostly just walk in, I trust that I'll talk to the right person and say the right thing.
    -   Micro: During a conversation if someone would be looking around past me I would feel bad at not being an interesting person and that sad aura would be with me for a bit. Now I just take it as a sign that the conversation is perhaps due for an end and welcome that as an opportunity to talk to a new person or do whatever else. This new action seems to just happen without me trying. Similarly I notice when I start doing that same thing instead of forcing myself to talk to the person in front of me.
-   There's this vague sense that every frame of experience has a hidden teaching, an Easter egg, or something of that sort. Every frame seems more interesting.
-   I feel more emotions and they get processed faster; they either get accepted (as in what good psychotherapy gets you to do, for the big stuck trauma-type stuff) or they turn into action.
-   I don't even remember the last time I got triggered, I take things way less personally now.
-   My conversational approach has shifted closer to what Art of Accomplishment calls [VIEW](https://www.artofaccomplishment.com/podcast/introduction-to-view) (though I haven't deliberately tried to do this nor done that much of AoA work besides their Connection Course). For example if someone tells me about their trip to Barcelona and their experience of the Sagrada Familia I am less likely to jump in to yap about my own experience of that building but rather I'll be asking them about their experience. I guess as I've noticed and worked on my own insecurities (which meditation will eventually bring up) I have less of a craving to be seen.
    -   Often what gets shared in a conversation has a "Look mom! Look! I made this! I did a thing!" quality to it that feels urgent and dismissive of what was said before. I have started noticing this sort of thing lately in myself and others.
    -   Extreme case of this behavior: Person A: "I had a sad experience... :(" Person B: "Oh yeah I had a sad experience too! Let me tell you! 10 years ago...". \[vs Enlightened person B: "Oh say more?"\] Person B clearly has that problem. I claim here that this runs deeper and subtler than I previously thought.
