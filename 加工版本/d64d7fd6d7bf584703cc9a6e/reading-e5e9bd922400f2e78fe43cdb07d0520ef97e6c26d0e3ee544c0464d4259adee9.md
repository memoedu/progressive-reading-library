# Retinoids probably don't slow skin aging

---

## 正文

人们常声称 Retinoids（retinoic acid、retinol、retinal、tretinoin、adapalene 等）既能 revert，也能 slow down skin aging。但在我看来，这似乎不对。

Reversing aging 相当困难，据我所知，只有少数事物有足够的 data 来支持 age-reversal 的 claim（[reprogramming](https://www.asimov.press/p/making-cells-young) 是我最常举的例子）。在接受 caloric restriction 及其他少数干预的 animals 中，会出现 Slowed aging，但一般而言，对这两种 effects 的默认判断都应当是“在得到相反证明之前，均无 effect”。对于“affecting aging”，我希望看到的是 reduced DNA damage 或 reduced epigenetic age；也就是说，与 UV radiation 所做的恰好相反：UV radiation 确实会通过 DNA damage 来 accelerate skin aging，而 plausibly，slowing down skin 中的 DNA damage accumulation 应当能让它更长久地 look younger。

人们相信 retinoids 能 reverse aging，是因为 retinoids 的 topical application 能让 skin look younger、increase collagen production（它会 declines with age），并使 skin 变得 thicker（reversing age-related thinning）。这并不是一个有争议的 claim，而且似乎在公众中和 scientifically 都得到了广泛认可。许多 paper 都展示了这些 effects，以下是其中之一：Kong et al.（[2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/jocd.12193?cicada_org_mdm=direct&cicada_org_src=healthwebmagazine.com&crsi=662497084)）

但人们也知道，如果停止 using retinoids，skin 就会回到“normal”。在我看来，这清楚地证明了实际上并没有发生 age-reversal：aging 是 gradually 发生的，而不是 suddenly 发生的。

有些人可能会想：“可是 retinol 正在 reversing skin thinning、collagen decrease 和 wrinkles”，不 reversing aging 就能做到这些的可能性有多大！其实，相当大。最近有一项在 mice 中开展的 study（You et al. [2023](https://www.statnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/madness-24-42.pdf)），通过 continued exposure to UV radiation 来 induced accelerated skin aging。随后，它们的 skin 接受了 retinoid 或 collagen mRNA 的 treated（分别通过 EVs 和 LNPs 进行 delivered）。这两种 treatments 都能 effective 地使 skin look younger，而两种 treatmentnts 在停止后，都未能使这些 changes 成为 permanent。那么，Collagen expression 也许 singlehandedly 就在 driving 这些 effects 中的许多部分，而我们应当都会同意，仅仅 overexpressing collagen 并不是在 de-ageing 这些 cells：

![image-20250517125523497](https://nintil.com/images/2025-05-17-retinol-does-not-slow-aging/image-20250517125523497.png)

![image-20250517125405353](https://nintil.com/images/2025-05-17-retinol-does-not-slow-aging/image-20250517125405353.png)

但许多 studies（包括前面提到的那项）都是 short term 的。Long term retinol use 则 reveals 了其他一些有趣的 changes：Thorne（[1992](https://sci-hub.se/https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2133.1992.tb16985.x)）记录到，停止 treatment 后，这些 effects 确实会消失，但需要一段时间（六个月）。Bhawan et al.（[1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8785167/)）也报告说，虽然 skin 在第一年确实会 thickens，但在之后的一段时间里又会 thins，恢复到 normal。

对于此前没有 used retinoids 的 older people，Retinoids 也能 work well，至少他们在这项 study 之前的一年内没有使用过（Kang et al. [2005](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/James-Milbauer/publication/7686280_Long-Term_Efficacy_and_Safety_of_Tretinoin_Emollient_Cream_005_in_the_Treatment_of_Photodamaged_Facial_Skin/links/56d24b4508ae059e375fa083/Long-Term-Efficacy-and-Safety-of-Tretinoin-Emollient-Cream-005-in-the-Treatment-of-Photodamaged-Facial-Skin.pdf?_sg%5B0%5D=started_experiment_milestone&origin=journalDetail&_rtd=e30%3D)）

![image-20250517130040972](https://nintil.com/images/2025-05-17-retinol-does-not-slow-aging/image-20250517130040972.png)

但我们还有这样一个问题：如果这个人一生都在 using retinoids，effects 会不会更好？如果确实如此，我们就可以说 retinoids 确实能 slow down skin aging。但截至今天，这样的 trial 似乎还没有做过。这或许是可行的：找到某个 age、终生 using retinoids 的人，然后对一个 matched age 的 retinoid-naive cohort 进行 treating，再看他们的 skins 是否 look markedly different。Conversely，也可以开展一项 trial，让 lifelong retinoid users 停止 treatment，然后看他们的 skin 是否比同 age 的平均水平 look better。

因此，根据目前已经发表的内容，我认为，如果一个人希望自己的 skin “此时此刻”（或在接下来一年内）look younger，那么 evidence 支持 using retinoids；但它并不支持为了“让我的 skin 在 50 年后相较于另一种选择 looks younger”而使用它。

不过，如果你知道相反的 evidence，请告诉我！

## 原文

Retinoids (retinoic acid, retinol, retinal, tretinoin, adapalene, etc) are commonly claimed to both revert and also slow down skin aging. But this seems wrong to me.

Reversing aging is quite difficult, and to my knowledge only a handful of things have enough data to support the claim of age-reversal ([reprogramming](https://www.asimov.press/p/making-cells-young) being my go-to example). Slowed aging occurs in animals with caloric restriction and a few other things, but in general the default for both of these effects should be "no effect until proven otherwise". The sort of thing I'd want to see for "affecting aging" is reduced DNA damage or reduced epigenetic age; ie the converse of what UV radiation does: UV radiation does accelerate skin aging through DNA damage, and plausibly slowing down DNA damage accumulation in the skin should make it look younger for longer.

People believe retinoids reverse aging because topical application of retinoids make the skin look younger, increase collagen production (which declines with age), and make the skin thicker (reversing age-related thinning). This is not a controversial claim and it seems well accepted by the public and scientifically, here's one amongst many paper that has shown these effects: Kong et al. ([2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/jocd.12193?cicada_org_mdm=direct&cicada_org_src=healthwebmagazine.com&crsi=662497084))

But it is also understood that if one stops using retinoids then the skin goes back to "normal". To me this is clear proof that there is not really age-reversal happening: aging happens gradually not suddenly.

Some might think "but retinol is reversing skin thinning, and collagen decrease, and wrinkles", what are the odds that can be done without reversing aging! Well, quite high. There's a recent study (You et al. [2023](https://www.statnews.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/madness-24-42.pdf)) in mice that induced accelerated skin aging through continued exposure to UV radiation. Then their skin was treated with a retinoid or with collagen mRNA (delivered both in EVs and LNPs). Both treatments were effective at making the skin look younger, and both treatmentnts when stopped failed to make the changes permanent. Collagen expression then, perhaps singlehandedly is whats driving a lot of these effects, and we would agree that just overexpressing collagen is not de-ageing the cells:

![image-20250517125523497](https://nintil.com/images/2025-05-17-retinol-does-not-slow-aging/image-20250517125523497.png)

![image-20250517125405353](https://nintil.com/images/2025-05-17-retinol-does-not-slow-aging/image-20250517125405353.png)

But many studies (including the one from earlier) are short term. Long term retinol use reveals some other interesting changes: Thorne ([1992](https://sci-hub.se/https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1365-2133.1992.tb16985.x)) documented that indeed after stopping treatment the effects go away but they take a while (six months). Bhawan et al. ([1996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8785167/)) reported also that while it is true that skin thickens in the first year then it thins in the period following that, returning to normal.

Retinoids also work well in older people that have not used retinoids, at least for a year before this study (Kang et al. [2005](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/James-Milbauer/publication/7686280_Long-Term_Efficacy_and_Safety_of_Tretinoin_Emollient_Cream_005_in_the_Treatment_of_Photodamaged_Facial_Skin/links/56d24b4508ae059e375fa083/Long-Term-Efficacy-and-Safety-of-Tretinoin-Emollient-Cream-005-in-the-Treatment-of-Photodamaged-Facial-Skin.pdf?_sg%5B0%5D=started_experiment_milestone&origin=journalDetail&_rtd=e30%3D))

![image-20250517130040972](https://nintil.com/images/2025-05-17-retinol-does-not-slow-aging/image-20250517130040972.png)

But we have: would the effects be even better if this person had been using retinoids their whole life? If that were true then we could say that retinoids do slow down skin aging. But that trial doesn't seem to have been done to this day. It could be doable: finding lifelong users of retinoids of a certain age, then treating a retinoid-naive cohort of a matched age, and then seeing if their skins look markedly different. Conversely, one could run a trial where lifelong retinoid users stop treatment and then see if their skin look better than average for their age.

So given what's published out there, I think the evidence supports using retinoids if one wants their skin "here and now" (or within the next year) to look younger, but it does not support its use "so that my skin looks younger in 50 years" relative to the alternative.

But if you know of evidence to the contrary, let me know!
