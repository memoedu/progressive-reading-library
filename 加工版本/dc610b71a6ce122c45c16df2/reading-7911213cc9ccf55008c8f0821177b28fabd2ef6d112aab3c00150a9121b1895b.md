## 正文

你该如何 craft 自己真正能完成的 projects？

我一生中的大部分时间都在 undertaking 各种各样的 [learning projects](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/my-projects/)。其中一些是规模庞大、雄心勃勃的尝试，持续了一年甚至更久。还有许多是 short sprints，只持续了一个月或更短的时间。

达到“good enough”的价值被严重低估了。我们推崇 mastery——赞颂那些花费数十年 honing 来之不易的 ability 的人。这当然无可厚非。但在短时间内从零达到还不错的程度，也能带来极大的满足感，而且最终可能对你的人生产生非同寻常的影响。

## 我用于 Creating Successful, Short Learning Projects 的 Strategy

在最近的一篇 [video essay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QnwkFXpSh3g) 中，我概述了自己用来 design 这类 short projects 的 three-step process，以便迅速熟悉一项 skill 或一个 topic。

基本的 strategy 是：

1.  **Draw 一个 map。** 弄清楚这项 skill 如何 works，人们如何 learn 它，以及有哪些 resources 可用。说到底，在开始任何 project 之前，你都应该先成为一个 mini-expert，了解有哪些 options 能让自己擅长它。这些 knowledge 能让你 set clear and realistic goals、pace yourself，并 avoid dead ends。
2.  **Choose 一条 path。** 决定自己先 learn 什么、后 learn 什么，以及会 ignore 这项 skill 的哪些 aspects。对 project 进行 Appropriately scoping，决定了你在一两个月后是对自己的 progress 感到满意，还是觉得完全不堪重负。
3.  **Craft 一个 plan。** 做好 planning 的关键，是仔细回顾自己过去是如何 allocate time and energy 的。好的 plans 会在你过去的 behavior 所设定的 constraints _之内_发挥作用，而不是要求你付出远远超出这些 constraints 的超常努力。

如果你学会把这三个 steps 做好，即使时间相当短，也有可能取得很大成果。

## 我犯过的三个错误

虽然谈论成功更让人愉快，但我也犯过许多错误：

1.  在我的 [portrait drawing challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/portrait-challenge/) 中，我曾以为，只需要获得更多带有 feedback 的 practice 就够了。结果，我的 progress 在进行到大约一半时 stalled 了。后来才发现，这里的 improvement 的关键不只是投入更多努力，而是先找到更好的 methods。
2.  在我规模最大的 [language learning challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/the-year-without-english-2/) 中，我曾以为大多数 languages 的难度都差不多。因此，我对 Korean 的准备严重不足，拖慢了自己的 progress。（在那次为期一年的旅行中，我 learned 了四种 languages，Korean 至今仍是其中最薄弱的一种。）如果再来一次，我会在出发旅行之前 built 一个更好的 foundation；我的好友 Olle Linge 也向 Mandarin learners [提出](https://www.hackingchinese.com/why-not-going-to-china-now-could-actually-be-good-for-your-chinese/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)了这条建议。
3.  在我的 [quantum mechanics challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/qm/) 中，我跳过了一门关于 waves 的 prerequisite class。结果，那个月我花了大量时间 relearning 已经忘了一半的 calculus，并 repeating 以前的 classes，之后才能跟上 homework。教训是：开始之前，要确保自己真的已经处于 starting point。

我提起这些 examples，不是为了自我批评——我对那些 projects 最终的结果很满意——而是因为，上面描述的 three-step process 并不是我随意挑选的 approach。每一种 strategy 都来自我自己犯过的错误。

通过 creating 一个 map，并 understanding 这项 skill 如何 works，我们就能 chart 一条 effective course。我的 portrait drawing challenge 在开始时缺少这个 map，所以我不得不在中途 draw 它。

通过 choosing 一条 path，我本会在旅行前更注重那些更难的 Asian languages 的 basic building blocks。这会让我处于一个好得多的 starting point，去应对一边 learning 一门 difficult language、一边尝试 communicate 所带来的要求。

通过 making 一个 plan，我本会从一开始就意识到，自己的 goal 不只是 learn quantum mechanics，还包括 refresh 自己已经模糊的 calculus knowledge，以及 fill in 一门缺失的 prerequisite class。这些变化会大幅调整我的 timetable。

虽然我不能说，自从 developing 这个 framework 以来，我 undertaking 的每一个 project 都一帆风顺，但我觉得，这些年来，随着自己不断犯错并纠正错误，我的 process 已经变得更加 refined。

## 原文

How do you craft projects you’ll actually finish?

I’ve spent most of my life undertaking [learning projects](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/my-projects/) of various sorts. Some were big, ambitious efforts that spanned a year or more. Many others were short sprints that lasted a month or less.

Getting “good enough” is seriously underrated. We lionize mastery—celebrating those who spend decades honing a hard-won ability. Fair enough. But going from zero to okay in a short period of time can be enormously satisfying, and it can end up having an outsized impact on your life.

## My Strategy for Creating Successful, Short Learning Projects

In a [recent video essay](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QnwkFXpSh3g), I outlined the three-step process I use to design these sorts of short projects to quickly get up to speed in a skill or topic.

The basic strategy is:

1.  **Draw a map.** Figure out how the skill works, how people learn it, and what resources are available. Essentially, before you start any project, you want to become a mini-expert on what options there are for getting good at it. This knowledge allows you to set clear and realistic goals, pace yourself, and avoid dead ends.
2.  **Choose a path.** Decide what you’ll learn first, what you’ll learn later and what aspects of the skill you’ll ignore. Appropriately scoping a project is the difference between feeling satisfied with your progress after a month or two versus feeling completely overwhelmed.
3.  **Craft a plan.** The key to good planning is to carefully reflect on how you’ve been able to allocate time and energy in the past. Good plans work _within_ the constraints set by your past behavior, rather than requiring heroic pushes well beyond them.

If you learn to do these three steps well, it’s possible to achieve a lot even in a fairly short period of time.

## Three of My Mistakes

While it’s more fun to talk about successes, I’ve made plenty of mistakes:

1.  In my [portrait drawing challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/portrait-challenge/), I assumed getting more practice with feedback was all that was needed. The result was that my progress stalled about halfway through. It turned out that the key to improvement here wasn’t just putting in more effort, but in first finding better methods.
2.  In my biggest [language learning challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/the-year-without-english-2/), I assumed most languages were about equally difficult. As a result, I severely underprepared for Korean, setting back my progress. (It’s still the weakest of the four I learned on my year-long trip.) Were I to do it again, I would have built a better foundation before traveling, advice my good friend Olle Linge [gives](https://www.hackingchinese.com/why-not-going-to-china-now-could-actually-be-good-for-your-chinese/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) for Mandarin learners as well.
3.  In my [quantum mechanics challenge](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/myprojects/qm/), I skipped over a prerequisite class on waves. The result was I spent a lot of time that month relearning half-forgotten calculus and repeating old classes before I could keep up with the homework. The lesson: make sure you’re truly at the starting point before you begin.

I bring these examples up not to be self-critical—I’m happy with how those projects eventually turned out—but because the three-step process I describe above isn’t an approach I’ve picked at random. Each strategy emerged out of my own mistakes.

By creating a map and understanding how the skill works, we can chart an effective course. This map was missing at the start of my portrait drawing challenge, so I had to draw it halfway.

By choosing a path, I would have emphasized more work on basic building blocks of the harder Asian languages before traveling. This would have put me at a much better starting point for the demands trying to communicate while learning a difficult language.

By making a plan, I would have recognized from the beginning that my goal wasn’t only to learn quantum mechanics, but to refresh my faded knowledge of calculus and fill in a missing prerequisite class. Those changes would have shifted my timetable considerably.

While I can’t say every project I’ve undertaken since developing this framework has been perfectly smooth, I feel like my process has gotten more refined over the years as I’ve made and fixed mistakes.
