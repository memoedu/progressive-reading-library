## 正文

这段文字是以下内容的一部分："_[I would never send my kids to school](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Problem_of_Schooling "Problem of Schooling")_"，作者是 [Piotr Wozniak](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Piotr_Wozniak "Piotr Wozniak") (2017)

## 教育中的主要问题

关于教育的主要问题在于这样一种信念：learning 可能会引起 displeasure，而这种 displeasure 应当被 [endured](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/The_grind_is_the_glory "The grind is the glory")，以获得更多的 learning。

有无数的 educators 相信 school 应当像 work 一样：它令人不快，但就是必须被完成。在本章中，我将说明事实恰恰相反：

**Good learning 本质上是 pleasurable**，而没有 pleasure 就没有 good learning。

这个 displeasure myth 如此普遍，以至于连好的 teachers 都相信 pain 是 learning 的一部分。

在本章中，我说明 learning 的 pleasure 是 wired into the brain 的，以及我们如何以 mankind 的 health、learning、creativity，并最终是 future 为代价，系统性地摧毁这份 [gift of evolution](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Education_counteracts_evolution "Education counteracts evolution")。

教育的主要问题也是社会的主要问题之一。通过摧毁 learning 的 pleasure，我们正在有力地促成 living 的 pleasure 的毁灭。我们建立了一个 education system，它让数百万的人被安排上一种 unhappiness 的生活。

你很可能对我的话持怀疑态度，因为 unpleasant learning 的 myth 是 [schooling](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Schooling "Schooling") 的一个强效副作用。因此本章是一次说服你的尝试。而要废除这个 myth，所需要的全部就是对 new knowledge 被 encoded in the brain 的那个简单 mechanism 的理解。

## Learn drive 与 entropy

entropy 的概念有助于理解为什么大多数 kids 在学校里学不到多少东西。

你可能还记得物理课上学过的：entropy 是 disorder 的一种度量，而 second law of thermodynamics 指出，一个 isolated system 的 entropy 永远不会减少。这就是那种我们往往会记一辈子的性感物理定律。它是 [universally applicable](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Applicability "Applicability") 的。

information theory 中有一个姊妹概念，叫做 [Shannon entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(information_theory\))。它可以被理解为 a source 所传输的 information 的平均值。例如，取一个不断向无穷远传输一串相同字母的 channel（例如一串 A："AAAAAA..."）。它完全可预测，并且携带的 entropy 为零。我们不会从这样的 channel 中学到东西。

[Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon) 在 1948 年提出了 information entropy 的概念。不久之后，scientists 就在推测，一个 information channel 的 entropy 是否可能对 brain 如何感知该 channel 的 value 产生强大的影响。1957 年，[Meyer hypothesized](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Music_and_entropy "Music and entropy") 认为 music 的 entropy 决定了对其 beauty 的 perception。他得出结论：更高的 entropy 可能导致 subjective tension，而这与更有意义的 musical moments 相关。

Meyer 的思想 [later refined](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Impact_of_syncopation_on_the_pleasure_of_music "Impact of syncopation on the pleasure of music")，以更好地理解 music 以及一般意义上的 information 的 perception。music 不只是 information。这一点通过一首歌在多次播放中仍然 entertaining 且 fun 的现象可见。但 books 很少如此。

music 是一种 universal message。如果让你在一个 radio channel 中做选择，你会很快从 noisy static 中 tune out，你也不会对 zero entropy 的 silence 感到太兴奋。然而，大多数人会对 drum 的 regular beat 作出积极反应。只要它不是被敲在 broken glass 上——我们天生就 dislike 这个——我们就会发现一个带有 regular drumbeat 的 radio channel 比一个 silent 的更有趣。这自然只会持续一段时间，直到 drumbeat 本身变得 boring 且过于 predictable。

今天，我们终于可以测试 brain 对 information entropy 的反应。Neuroimaging 显示 [anterior hippocampus responds to the entropy of a visual stream](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15896570)，并且类似的发现已在 [ventral striatum](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3403290/) 上得到证实。因此我们现在确定，brain 会对 information entropy 作出反应。这个 entropy sensor 在扫描环境以寻找 learning opportunities 时很重要。这是 underlying [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 reward 的前奏。

## information seeking 中的 prior knowledge

我们需要区分 information 与 meaning。entropy 并不是后者的一个好度量。meaning 的度量除了 information channel metric 之外，还必须涉及 brain 本身。prior knowledge 在 learning 中至关重要。想象一下，在你寻找一个有趣的 radio channel 时，你找到一个 news service。如果该 service 是用 Thai 提供的，而你不会说 Thai，你会更偏好用 English 提供的 service。从 information 的意义上说，news channels 可能具有相同的 entropy，然而你的 prior knowledge 会让你选择 English channel。Thai channel 传递的是一串 sounds，而 English channel 传递的是一串 [concepts](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Abstract_knowledge "Abstract knowledge")。如果不理解接收者的 knowledge，information entropy 几乎不能告诉我们什么。我们无法确定一个 signal-to-noise ratio。

每个 listener 都会有他或她自己偏好的 information entropy 水平。对大多数 music lovers 来说，disco 或 techno 的 regular beat 会比 drum 的 isolated beat 更有趣一些。这类 music 携带更高的平均 information 水平。对更 sophisticated 的 listener 来说，一点 syncopation 会受欢迎。然而，syncopation 需要一定程度的 prior learning。那些 music knowledge 较少的人可能会因 rhythmic complexity 的增加而感到困惑。如果 beat 中的 information 太多，就 [may no longer be possible to dance to the music](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Impact_of_syncopation_on_the_pleasure_of_music "Impact of syncopation on the pleasure of music")。对普通的耳朵来说，Wynton Marsalis 的天才可能很难被感知。Top shelf jazz music 只留给一小部分受过高等教育的 listeners，因为对大多数人群来说，随着 complexity 的增加，music 慢慢朝着 radio static 的方向 disintegrates。

## 大脑中的 Entropy detectors

大脑无法有效地检测到达 retina 或 eardrum 的信号的 entropy。就像显示器的 pixels 一样，retinal cells 并不知道自己显示的是什么。如果 detector，例如 hippocampus，要响应 entropy 而亮起，它必须作用于来自 entorhinal cortex 的 inputs（即 hippocampus 自身的 input）。那些 inputs 会在经过高度 processing 之后呈现信号。它呈现的可能不是 pixels，而是一个 concept。sensory inputs 处的高 entropy 信号会在 neural selection、completion 和 [generalization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization") 过程的早期失去其大部分 noise component。signal-to-noise ratio 将决定有多少信息丢失。noise 越大，损失越大。我们越聪明，这种 processing 就越有选择性，在那个阶段丢失的信息就越多。这很好。我们变得对细节视而不见。Pattern recognition 会像一个 deterministic function 那样起作用，而根据定义，这会导致 entropy 的下降。复杂的 patterns 可能变成简单的 concepts。那些 concepts 将为 detector，例如 hippocampus，提供实际的 input。

注意，在证明 [response of the hippocampus to signal entropy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15896570) 的实验中产生的 visual stream 具有高度的 [symbolic nature](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Abstract_knowledge "Abstract knowledge")。因此，该 stream 在 processing 中丢失的信息要少得多。那条高度简化且被 [conceptualized](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization") 的 message 将被扫描以寻找 surprisal，并为整个 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 系统提供指导。这就是为什么在这种情况下，hippocampus 似乎是在响应 input entropy。

上述推理解释了为什么低 entropy 和高 entropy 的 sensory signals 都可能无趣。经过一定程度的 processing 之后，一个高 entropy 信号可能失去其全部 noise，并向 hippocampus 传递一个低 entropy 的 input。我们于是观察到 sensory input 处存在一个 "optimum entropy" 水平的错觉。我们需要一个新概念，**[learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy")**，它将帮助我们准确地确定信号的 attractiveness。Learntropy 需要考虑到信息在被用于激活大脑中的 reward centers 之前所经历的高度 processing。Learntropy 在本文的[后面](#Learntropy)讨论。

## 信息处理的速度

在 sensory information scanning 中一个未被充分重视的因素是大脑中 information processing 的速度。

对于每一段音乐，都存在一个可容忍的 playback range，在其中音乐的 beauty 得到欣赏。高速 playback 可能令人烦躁，音乐可能变得难以 decode，因为高速超出了我们的 processing power。同一段音乐放慢速度也可能很快失去其 appeal。在 speech delivery 或 classroom lecturing 中也是如此。对于相同的信息和相同的 entropy level，我们可能实现截然不同的 signal attractiveness 水平。总是存在一个 optimum speed of delivery，而那个速度取决于所有其他为 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 提供动力的因素，包括 prior knowledge。因此，speed of delivery 是高度个体化的。

我喜欢以 1.4x 速度听 lectures。对于更有野心的内容我用 1.3x。不过我从不加速 [Fareed Zakaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fareed_Zakaria_GPS)，而是细细品味这个节目中的每一条信息。classroom lecture 中的学生没有 speed-up 或 slow-down 按钮。即使是 pause 按钮，如果有的话，也很难按下，因为它可能惹恼其他学生。

在学校里，speed of delivery 常常超过学生的 processing capacity。这导致微不足道的 learning 和高度的 stress。没有时间[享受高速列车窗外的风景](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Futility_of_schooling "Futility of schooling")。在 MIT，他们称之为 _"drinking from a firehose"_。

## 概率与知识

低 probability 的事件携带更多信息。平均信息决定 entropy。Prior knowledge 决定对 information channel 的 entropy 的感知。

如果你碰巧调到 radio news，听到 "_Janet Jackson has delivered a baby_"，你的兴趣程度将取决于该事件的 probability。如果你完全不知道 Janet Jackson 是谁，这就是一个高 probability 事件。如果大约 [350,000 women deliver babies every single day](http://www.theworldcounts.com/stories/How-Many-Babies-Are-Born-Each-Day)，这就不再是 news，既不新也不有趣。战争中第一个士兵的死亡会成为 news，但当死亡人数增加到数千时，年轻的生命就只是一个 statistic。

如果你碰巧认识 Janet Jackson 或喜欢她的音乐，baby delivery 的 probability 会急剧下降到 "_once in a lifetime_" 的水平（对 Janet 而言）。这可能让你产生兴趣。然而，如果你记忆中的 Janet 是某部古老 sitcom 里的美丽女孩，她的 baby delivery 可能归入 "_Impossible!_" 的类别。如果你意识到 Janet 已经 50 岁，并且你了解 menopause，你可能立刻对她的情况产生病态的好奇。你的 prior knowledge 决定你如何回应这条 message。不存在一个 channel 的 optimum entropy level。只存在一个适合特定大脑的 optimum entropy level。此时你可能会看到，我们需要引入一个新的派生概念，我们将在[后面](#Learntropy)称之为 **learntropy**。Learntropy 将决定一个给定 channel 对给定大脑的 attractiveness。

如果你喜欢 Janet 式的 gossip，富含那种 gossip 的 channel 将为你提供恰到好处的 surprisal 水平。它将提供 learntropy 的匹配。如果你缺乏知识或你的 priorities 不同，你就会 tune out。你的 learning priorities 也将决定你在特定领域的知识水平，以及你对任何特定 channel 及其 information entropy 的回应。

## 可预测性与 surprisal

probability 与 complexity 并不是 information perception 中仅有的组成部分。我们似乎在 predictability 与 surprise 之间寻找一种平衡。我喜欢 funk。在这种类型的 music 中，bassline 往往高度 predictable，带有 [optimum dose of syncopation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Impact_of_syncopation_on_the_pleasure_of_music "Impact of syncopation on the pleasure of music")。这使得身体动作很容易与 rhythm 同步。然而，如果 funk 不带有 surprise，它就不会有趣。正是在这里，精巧的 jazz riffs 搔动了负责 detection of surprisal 的 neural system。此外，经过数十年的 learning，我的 brain 会对一整个 database of signals 作出反应。也许会有那么一个我认得并喜欢的 backup singer voice。我的 brain 已经为 funk 做好了准备。

我喜欢 Ken Robinson 关于 creativity 的 lectures。从某方面说，它们高度 predictable。我完全同意 Robinson，所以你可以说 Robinson 喂养了我的 confirmation bias。这是 pleasurable 的。当人们同意我们时，我们喜欢说“_great minds think alike_”。但如果 Robinson 只是不断重复那些关于 schools 如何 kill creativity 的干巴巴的 mantras，他就会失去他的 appeal。entropy 可以被解释为平均预期的 [surprisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-information)。Robinson 的 delivery 带有大量美好的 surprises。他可能以不同且异常 creative 的方式描绘同样的 models。结果是，**brain 接收 new information，产生一个 generalization，并确认现有的 models**。从 new contexts 中得出的 generalizations 会提高 [knowledge coherence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_coherence "Knowledge coherence")。这是一种基于已知 model 的 message 中非常令人愉悦的 complementarity 类型。

Robinson 的 lectures 在 predictability 与 surprise 之间找到了良好的平衡。

最令人愉悦的 information channels 会不断传递那些确认现有 models 的 surprises，并把它们武装到新的 semantic twigs 上，从而可以在其上构建 new knowledge。一个摧毁现有 models 的 surprise 起初可能并不令人愉悦，但可能带来 thinking 中一场极其令人愉悦的 revolution。

打个比方，你可以把这想象成 information channel 在按摩你的 tree of knowledge 并添加新的 branches，就像一个 potter 向他完美成形的 creation 添加 [new layers of clay](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Jigsaw_puzzle_metaphor "Jigsaw puzzle metaphor")。

## 检测 surprisal

人类的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 建立在检测 [surprisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-information) 之上。这一点我们早已知道。所有关于人类与机器 learning 的 models 都以不同的名称涉及这一 concept。[Piaget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Piaget) 写过在 surprisal 的冲击下陷入 disequilibrium 的 [schemata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Piaget#Schema)。在他关于 neocortex 的 models 中，[Jeff Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Hawkins) 谈到作为 learning 与 intelligence 基础的 prediction errors。我喜欢谈论 [models](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Model "Model")、它们的 elaboration（当 new information 契合该 model 时）、contradiction（当 new information 要求对该 model 作出 changes 时），以及 [generalization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization")（当 [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting "Forgetting") 与 [memory optimization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Memory_optimization "Memory optimization") 从该 model 中雕琢出新的 quality 时）。

对于 learning 的 reward，一个新的 surprising piece of information 需要契合预先存在的 knowledge（[models](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Model "Model")、schemata、predictions 等等）。要让 reward 被传递，neural processing 是必要的。input 上的 information 需要被处理，并与 brain 中存储的 information 进行比较。brain 中 input information 的主要 processors 之一是 hippocampus。它是 brain 的 information switchboard，能够将 input 与 prior knowledge 进行比较。

测量 visual stream 的 entropy 并不一定是 information channel 的 pleasing power 的可靠指标。所有流向 hippocampus 的 information 都经过高度的 processing。一条代表美丽 beach 的 pixels 流会被处理成一系列 shapes 与 textures。这些又会对 palms、sand 与 sea 进行 model。这种高度压缩的简单 information 将决定对 information input 的原始 response。

在环境中 scanning for information 等同于 scanning for scents of food。scent 是诱人的，但只有实际的 feeding 才是真正的 reward。这就是为什么 entropy scanning 不需要是 rewarding 的。它需要做的只是导向一个 reward。如前所述，anterior hippocampus 会对 entropy 作出反应，然而 experimental design 确保了该 entropy 指的是在 input processing 期间不会丢失太多 information 的简单 shapes 的组合。与其谈论 signal entropy，我们更应关注诸如 hippocampus 这样的 information comparator 处的 input entropy。重要的不是 retinal pixels，而是如 comparator input 上所表示的 palm 的 shape。对于 comparator 而言，grayness 或 static noise 的高 entropy pattern 与 whiteness 或 silence 并无不同。它们在 input 上都会带来相同的 entropy：zero。这就是为什么我使用 [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") 这一术语来准确指称 information channel 的 attractiveness。

^pr-b5c25cfc8a5d

对 signal entropy 作出反应的 anterior hippocampus 因 Halle Berry neuron 的发现而闻名（见 [more](http://phys.org/news/2005-06-single-cell-recognition-halle-berry-neuron.html)）。利用植入一名同意的 epilepsy patient 体内的 electrodes，researchers 得以精确定位一个在各种 contexts 中一致地对 Halle Berry 的 images 作出反应的单一 neuron。同一个 neuron 也会对 Halle Berry 的 name 作出反应。与此同时，posterior hippocampus 对 Jennifer Aniston 的反应可能不那么一致（也许这表明存在一个更靠前的 neural processing 层）。

我们大多数人不知道 Halle Berry 闻起来是什么味道，而且她的 smell 可能不够独特，不足以激活 hippocampus 中的 Halle Berry neuron，然而，即便是 smell signal 也能通过 olfactory bulb、olfactory tubercle、piniform cortex 与 entorhinal cortex 中仅仅几个 synapses 快速到达那里（见图）。然而，如果一个人能听到 Halle 的 voice 的声音，它可能会在 olfactory tubercle 中与 sound signal 相遇，对 recognition 作出贡献，并导致 hippocampus 中或更下游的 neocortex 中的 Halle neuron 随后被激活。

这一切是否意味着 Halle 永久地驻留在 patient 的 hippocampus 中？由于 hippocampus 与 new memories 的 formation 之间的关联，我们更应认为 Halle 是作为 recognition 的结果而出现在 hippocampal neurons 中。她在 patient 心中的永久位置很可能位于更下游的 neocortex 中。我们现在知道，在 memory consolidation 的过程中，knowledge engrams 会 [move from the hippocampus to the neocortex](http://www.jneurosci.org/content/29/32/10087.full)。我们也相当确定这一过程发生在 [in sleep](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Memory_optimization_in_sleep "Memory optimization in sleep") 期间。我们应当在 neocortex 中寻找代表 Halle 或某人的 grandmother 的 concept neurons。最后这种可能性催生了一种假想类型的 neuron，称为 **the [grandmother cell](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Grandmother_cell "Grandmother cell")**。

在 monkeys 中，researchers 能够识别出 visual cortex 中对 faces 作出反应的 [grandmother cells](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Grandmother_cell "Grandmother cell")。在那里我们可能会找到在与 Halle 的 image 接触时更一致地 fire up 的 cells。然而，Halle 的 concept 可能仍驻留在别处，并在注意到 Halle 时被 visual cortex cells 等激活。

另一条 activation route 可能来自在 news 中听到 Halle 的 name。整个 recognition process 将由 entorhinal cortex 与 hippocampus 来 orchestrate，而最终的 Halle neuron 将在 neocortex 的 layers 中某处 light up。

要让 information rich signal 产生一个 reward，必须在 input 上检测到一个 low probability event，并通过 association 将其作为 new knowledge 编码到 cortex 中。在 anterior hippocampus 会对 entropy 作出反应之处，[activity of the extensive bilateral thalamo-cortical network would be modulated by the surprise factor](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15896570)。我们应当在那里寻找 pleasure of learning 的根源。根据 message 的类型，还可能涉及其他 comparator centers。amygdala 也被发现很可能在检测到 novel visual signals 时产生 rewards。那些对 rewarding visual stimuli 作出反应的 amygdala neurons 可能也会对 novel visual stimuli 作出反应。[Rolls hypothesized that this may implement the reward of novelty via the amygdala](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Amygdala_may_be_involved_in_rewarding_novel_input "Amygdala may be involved in rewarding novel input")。

我们知道 hippocampus 与 nucleus accumbens（brain pleasure center）直接相连。这种 connection 可能在两种 contexts 中被使用：

1.  the anticipation of pleasure 与
2.  the ultimate reward。

anticipation 会跟随对一个高 [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") signal 的 detection 而来，并会导致对 high value messages 的主动追求。由 hippocampus 检测到一个 message 之后，可能同时向 neocortex 发送 associative learning messages，并向 pleasure center 发送 reward signal。那将标志着学到新东西的时刻！

## The wow factor

在 1977 年的夏天，当 SETI researchers 在寻找 extraterrestrial intelligence 时，发现了一个来自 [Sagittarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_\(constellation\) 的不寻常 radio signal。在 cosmic space 平淡的低水平 noise 中，这个 signal 是 highly unlikely 的。Low probability 标志着 high surprisal。Astronomer Jerry Ehman 在 printout 上圈出了与这个 signal 对应的 6 个 letters，并用 "Wow!" 标记了它。

[![一份原始 computer printout 的彩色副本的扫描件，该扫描件是在 1977 年 Wow! signal 到达数年之后拍摄的](https://supermemo.guru/images/thumb/d/d3/Wow_signal.jpg/500px-Wow_signal.jpg)](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/File:Wow_signal.jpg "一份原始 computer printout 的彩色副本的扫描件，该扫描件是在 1977 年 Wow! signal 到达数年之后拍摄的")

> _**Figure:** 一份原始 computer printout 的彩色副本的扫描件，拍摄于 1977 年 [Wow! signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wow!_signal) 到来数年之后。(source: Wikipedia)_

"Wow!" 就是 brain 对 sudden discovery 的响应方式。这个时刻是 highly pleasurable 的。[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的全部目的就是在环境中寻找 wow factors。这些是最有价值的 knowledge nuggets，它们 complement 当前已知的内容：当前的 reality model。[incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") 的 pleasure 来自流入 student's brain 的 wows 的 condensed power。

到目前为止，我们已经看到了 entropy、surprisal、predictability 和 current knowledge 对 learning 的影响。在这种情况下，signal 的 mere probability 并不能完全解释它的 power。站在它背后的是 interpretation（见：[Knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network")）。在写下他的 note 的那一刻，Ehman 能够感受到其 implications 的 enormity。这是迄今为止以及此后关于存在非 human intelligence 的 intelligence 的最强有力的 evidence。如果同一个 signal 代表的是在海洋中探测到 sardines，那就不会有 "wow!"。即使在 Arctic 也不会有。

information channel 的 reliability 很重要。如果 error rate 很高，[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 可能会 weaken。当 Penzias 和 Wilson 在 1964 年发现 cosmic microwave background radiation 时，并没有 "wow!"。困惑的 researchers 接着从他们的 radio antenna 上清除 pigeon droppings。Pigeon droppings 在他们对 mysterious noise 的解释中获得了 priority。1978 年，因为他们的 discovery，Penzias 和 Wilson 获得了 Nobel Prize。

当一位 scientist 做出 discovery 时，他可能会喊出 "_Eureka!_" 并挥拳击空。他 brain 中某处的 neural network 产生了一个 [generalization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization")，从而导致发送一个 reward signal。这会进一步传播，让一位 old man 像孩子一样在 lab 里跳来跳去。

同样的事情在生命早期也会发生。一个在空房间里的 toddler 会扫描环境中的 low probability components，比如 colorful objects、new toys 等。当一个 toddler 用从桌上掉落的 spoon 做实验时，她就像一个小 scientist。然而，当 brain 做出一个 [generalization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization") "_all falling spoons make noise_" 时，她也会得到 reward。她可能会以与那位快乐的 scientist 完全相同的方式庆祝，与年龄无关。一个大大的微笑是第一个明确的迹象。

同样的快乐之事会以较小的程度发生在所有由 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 控制的 learning 形式中。我们学到的是关于一个 celebrity 还是关于一块 rock 的 chemical composition，这并不重要。事物之所以 interesting，是因为它们通过 learn drive mechanism 来 reward 这个 brain。

一个 creative process 也会产生 rewards。一个被认为 useful 的 association 是 rewarding 的。一个导致 difficult problem 的 solution 的 association 则更加 rewarding。显然存在一个 rewards 的 gradation。这个 system 可以量化 information、association 或 solution 的 probability。probability 越低，reward 越高。

## Knowledge valuation network

### Knowledge valuations

所有被 brain 处理的 granular pieces of knowledge 都会立即根据它们的 relevance、[coherence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence") 和 value 被 evaluated。我们立即知道 information 是否 understandable 和 [useful](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Applicability "Applicability")。我们也常常立即注意到它何时是 [inconsistent](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Consistency "Consistency")、[incoherent](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence") 或 irrelevant 的。

Unusual 和 surprising 的 knowledge bits 是 highly valued 的，然而，从 brain 的角度来看，probability 并不是 value 的最佳反映。存在一些 low significance 的 highly unlikely events（例如，在一个遥远 planetary system 中的 asteroid strike），也存在一些改变一个人生活的 likely events（例如，对 "_Will you marry me?_" 的回答）。

[Knowledge valuation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation "Knowledge valuation") 主要依赖于 knowledge 在实现 personal goals 中的 [applicability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Applicability "Applicability")。

### The emotional brain and the rational brain

**Knowledge valuation network** 是一个基于 knowledge 的 emotional 和 rational valuations 的 resultant 的 evaluation system。在文献中，它可能被宽泛地称为 _neural valuation circuitry_，这并不一定是 knowledge-specific 的。

在 valuation network 中，emotional valuations 会将 information 与负责 hunger、thirst、sex drive 等的 primitive brain centers 中的 rewards 连接起来。Rational valuations 将是 knowledge-based 的。一个纯粹的 emotional valuation 的例子来自对 "_Where is the nearest fast food shop?_" 的回答。Knowledge-based valuations 可能更复杂且高度 networked，即依赖于一个 subvaluations 的 network。对 "_Which book is best for my exam?_" 的回答是通过一个人的 goals 来 evaluated 的，这些 goals 包括通过 exam 从而获得一个 degree，这影响一个人的 job prospects，并有助于 lifetime goals。Emotional 和 rational valuations 在 anatomically 上是 segregated 的。Emotional valuations 来自被隐喻性地描述为 triune brain 中较老部分的东西：reptilian 和 paleommamalian structures。例如，一个由 thalamus 处理的 specific stimulus 可能会向 amygdala 发送单独的 signals 以进行 emotional evaluation，并向 neocortex 发送 signals 以进行 rational valuation。Emotional brain 在 philogenetically 上更老。Personality 和 education 决定 rational valuations 是否能 control 或 override emotional valuations。

### Decision tree in fast thinking

**Knowledge valuation network** 是决定单个知识片段价值的记忆连接网络。如果 learning 被解释为一项 task，valuation network 将决定 **perceived task value**（见：[Problem valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Problem_valuation_network "Problem valuation network")）。

^pr-db094fc13c4d

在 computational 意义上，**knowledge valuation network** 可以比作一棵 [decision tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_tree)。Goals 和 emotions 决定树根处的 core values。知识片段之间的 semantic connections 可以解释为从 goals 到 details 的 fractional value transfer。一个组织良好的 [semantic network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Semantic_network "Semantic network")，由 well-consolidated 和 well-chosen 的 knowledge 构成，只需几毫秒就能做出 expert decisions。这就是 Kahneman 所称的自动 [fast thinking](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Fast_thinking "Fast thinking")（如果你对需要 _slow problem solving_ 的难题感兴趣，见 _[How to solve any problem?](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/How_to_solve_any_problem%3F "How to solve any problem?")_）。那些构成 decision making 或 problem solving 基础的同类过程，也参与 knowledge valuation。像许多 expert decisions 一样，valuation 是快速的，而且常常在 conscious intentionality 参与度很低的情况下运行。简而言之，我们有时会拼命想知道某些事情，却无法完全解释为什么。这个过程几乎不受我们自己的控制，更不用说学校老师的控制了。对于 efficient learning，valuations 必须很高。

### Valuation network in education

Knowledge valuation network 高度专门化，而且因人而异。Reason 与 emotions 之间的平衡会不同。Goals 之间的平衡会不同。这个 valuation network 在一个 criminal 的 mind 中会以不同方式成形，在一个以人类福祉为基础、怀有崇高 goals 的 researcher 的 mind 中也会以不同方式成形。

### Valuation network in development

这个 network 的发展将取决于 personality、lifetime experience 以及 environment。Childhood trauma 或 personality characteristics，例如 impulsivity，可能增加发展出 criminal mindset 的机会。early life 中的一些 traumatic events 可能有利于发展出基于 single-minded obsessions 的 biased networks（见：[falsity vector](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Falsity_vector "Falsity vector")）。environment 和可获得的 knowledge 将决定 passions、interests、goals 以及 network subvaluations（见：[conceptualization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Conceptualization "Conceptualization")）。

所有促进 healthy brain development 的策略也将促进丰富、高度个体化且高效的 valuation network。这些将构成一个闪耀的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的基础。所有 educators 都同意，我们希望帮助 kids 良好地把握他们的 emotional life，并构建聪明、有创造力且有知识的 brains。

educational systems 的首要问题是一种 cookie-cutter approach，其中所有 kids 都以 industrial fashion 被喂以相同的 knowledge，几乎不尊重高效学习的关键组成部分：[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")。Learn drive 是一个 [perfect computational device](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Optimality_of_the_learn_drive "Optimality of the learn drive")，它将大脑中代表 knowledge 的 [semantic network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Semantic_network "Semantic network") 的当前状态与由 **knowledge valuation network** 响应环境中可获得的 information 所产生的当前 input 相匹配。如果 kid 坚持他必须看那个 YouTube video，他自己的 brain 就是最好的权威。所有 interference 都将影响未来的 independence 和 [creativity](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Creativity "Creativity")。

虽然一个 lecturing teacher 可能花 45 分钟向一个 child 喂以一长串产生低 valuations 和可忽略 memories 的 symbols，但同一个 kid，若能访问 Google，在 3-5 分钟内就会识别出具有高 valuations 且易于 coding 以终身 retention 的 pieces of information（相反观点见：[The morbid myth of Digital Dementia](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/The_morbid_myth_of_Digital_Dementia "The morbid myth of Digital Dementia")）。对于在此过程中训练有素的 kids，knowledge acquisition 的效率在 [self-learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Self-learning "Self-learning") 中可能高出一个 order of magnitude。当我说 "order of magnitude" 时，我只是在谨慎和保守。我不想招致 hyperbole 的指责。我在本文其他地方包含了几个具体比较的例子（例如 [13 years of school in a month](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Schools_are_useless_in_teaching_English!#Inefficiency_of_schooling "Schools are useless in teaching English!") 或 [1600% acceleration of learning during vacation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learning_history:_school_vs._self-directed_learning#Self-directed_acceleration "Learning history: school vs. self-directed learning")）。

在我谈到 golden nuggets of knowledge 的地方，Peter Thiel 谈到 [power law](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Thiel_on_power_law "Thiel on power law")：一小组被磨练至完美的 core skills 可以产生 power returns。

**对 learning 的 small investments** 可以对 individual lives 和整个 planet 产生 **dramatic changes**！

### Knowledge valuation in the brain

对大脑中 [knowledge valuation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation "Knowledge valuation") network 的实际 anatomical implementation 的研究，对于理解 human mind 至关重要。它对于预防 depression 和 addictions 是必不可少的。Knowledge valuation 是高效 learning、creativity 和 problem solving 的基础。

Good learning 是 pleasurable 的。food、sex 或 drugs 的 rewards 往往会饱和。Happy learning 不具有这一性质。避免 unhappy learning 是容易的。这是通过 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 本能地完成的。这就是为什么 learning 具有至高无上的 hedonic importance。它确实可以将 societies 提升到一个更快乐的新水平。

#### Orbitofrontal cortex

[knowledge valuation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation "Knowledge valuation") 的 networked nature 表明了对 cortical resources 的使用。确实，大多数 researchers 似乎倾向于相信，整个 valuations 系统可能 [centered in the orbitofrontal cortex (OFC)](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Orbitofrontal_cortex_as_the_supreme_reward_processor "Orbitofrontal cortex as the supreme reward processor")，且 abstraction 的水平向前部区域递增。关于各个 subsystems 如何影响 valuations，有许多 models 和 hypotheses（例如 common currency、common scaling、somatic marker、appraisal-by-content、multiple component、cognitive-motivational interface、parallel appraisal、locationist vs. constructionist models 等）。在 **common currency model** 中，来自所有 sub-systems（hedonic substrates）的所有 valuations 被整合，并提供 "wanting" 或 "liking" 的最终 signal。例如，(1) 来自 medial OFC (mOFC) 的 knowledge-based valuations 可能与 (2) 来自 nucleus accumbens (NA) 的 reward anticipation，以及 (3) 来自 insula 的 food appraisal messages 相结合，以影响下一餐选择 restaurant 时的 decision-making。

#### Common currency model

OFC 是一个极好的研究领域，因为许多人类兴趣线索在此汇聚：drug addictions、ahedonia、[learned helplessness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learned_helplessness "Learned helplessness")、obsessive compulsive disorder 等。**common currency model** 似乎表明，与爆发性 creativity 或爆发性 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 相关的 high，在 neurochemically 和 neuroanatomically 上与低剂量 cocaine 产生的 high 相当。

关于所有 rewards 是否都被转化为汇聚于同一类型 neurons 的 reward signal，还是保留其来源特征，存在激烈的争论。我认为这一讨论是多余的，因为 specificity 可以由个别的 [concept map](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_map "Concept map") activations 赋予，而由单一 output 产生的最终 valuation 可以构成 common currency。在所有 valuations 中，由于存在与单一 concept map activation 相对应的单一 answer，我们需要有 convergence。[Some OFC neurons](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Value-encoding_neurons_in_the_orbitofrontal_cortex "Value-encoding neurons in the orbitofrontal cortex") 似乎专门编码 high-level value。

在 [knowledge valuation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation "Knowledge valuation") 和 decision-making 中，我们需要一个单一的 boss。Redundancy 可以用来恢复 valuation system，但无法逃避 [concept neuron](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_neuron "Concept neuron") decision。我们不能有两个 decision makers，以至于在晚餐时让一只拿着 fork 的手刺向发痒的眼睛，尽管相互竞争的 neural forces 由于 computing error 使这种情景成为可能。

#### Emergence of knowledge valuations

建立 valuation network 可能通过各个 [concept maps](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_map "Concept map") 的相互作用而发生。例如，如果 exam concept 因为 job prospects [concept](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept "Concept") 而受到 valued，它们可能被 coactivated，而 employment concept 的 valuation 可能赋予 exam-related [concept map](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_map "Concept map") 一个 valuation。activation 的程度以及相关的 concept valuations 可能决定最终的 appraisal。Myelin concentration 可能在 targeting ventral striatum 的 pathways 中增加，这可能是解释 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 如何能随 learning 而 boosted（或被 [coercive learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coercive_learning "Coercive learning") a school 所 suppressed）的方式之一。orbitofrontal cortex 在决定 valuations 中的作用可能类似于 hippocampus 在建立 long-term memories 中的作用。cortex 中那些高度 connected 的区域可能扮演 switchboard 的角色，它们连接 areas of interest，随后又放弃 matchmaker 的角色，而 linked concept maps（或 centers）则发展出自己的 wiring 以实现 fast connectivity（例如在 sleep 中）。有了新的 wiring，highly valued concept 可能影响 pleasure centers，而无需 OFC 的 mediation。这样，一些 [concept cells](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_cell "Concept cell")（例如与某人最喜欢的 actor 相关的）可能仅凭 solo activation 就产生 pleasant valuations。

#### Harms of reversal learning

在 negative school conditioning 的情况下，我们可能把无关的 contexts（例如 [items](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Item "Item") 在 [SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo "SuperMemo") 中的 colors）与 low valuations 关联起来。在这个 scenario 中，white item 的 concept，或者 item 与 color 的 concept 的 coactivation，会通过提供一个强的 negative input 来 suppress valuation。从外部看，它像是一个阻断 valuations 的 cut-off signal（也许在 lateral OFC 中）。在这样的 contexts 中，concepts 的 association 仍然可能，short-term retrieval 也可能发生，然而 low valuation 会阻止 memory 的 consolidation（例如通过阻断向 long-term cortical storage 的 transfer）（见：[How school turns off memory](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/How_school_turns_off_memory "How school turns off memory")）。Reprogramming reward（例如在 SuperMemo 中 swapping template color）可能发生在 reversal learning 中。我们知道，有 OFC injury 的 animals 在 reversal learning 上受损（Mishkin 1972），这为 supreme valuation networks 的 anatomical location 增添了证据。如果我们不断 override valuation signals，我们可能最终得到 [war of the networks](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/War_of_the_networks "War of the networks")，这是我对由 schooling 诱发的 [learned helplessness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learned_helplessness "Learned helplessness") 的 origins 的 hypothetical claim。School coercion 可能被视为一种 perpetual reversal learning，它会磨损 network plasticity，导致对 decision-making 中 evaluate rewards 的能力产生 long-term adverse effects。从这个角度看，human memory 可能被视为一个具有有限 erase cycles 数的 EPROM。如果 long-term learning 被视为 synaptic substrate 的 buildup，而该 substrate 随后在 [stabilization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Stability "Stability") 的过程中被 pruned（这反过来减少 synaptogenesis），那么 reversal learning 可能导致一个 unresponsive system，在其中 learning 不再可能。

Endless fake rewards 和 micro-penalties 可能 turn off knowledge valuations，并 undermine 学校中的 long-term learning

#### Goals vs. habits

**knowledge valuation network** 在健康的 [free learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Free_learning "Free learning") 中是 central 的。相反，[passive schooling](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Passive_schooling "Passive schooling") 导致 [learned helplessness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learned_helplessness "Learned helplessness")。Coercion 把 goal-oriented behaviors 转化为对 passive habits 的接受（与在追求 goals 中磨炼出的 healthy habits 相对）。knowledge valuation network 的 output 被系统中较低的 valuations 所 suppressed（即 interest 的 concept maps 的较低 activation）。这自然导致一种不那么 joyful 的 state of mind。当 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") withers，当 curiosity 死去，life 就变成一系列以很少 reward 执行的 habits（见：[50 bad habits learned at school](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/50_bad_habits_learned_at_school "50 bad habits learned at school")）。

没有 learning 的 pleasure，human existence 就变成一套 joyless 的 habits

## Learntropy

有许多因素会影响 messages 和 information channels 如何被大脑 perceive 和 value。在前面的 sections 中我们已经注意到，大脑并不只是对 entropy 作出 respond。有许多因素会 modulate 单个 messages 的 entropy 或 surprisal 的 impact。这些因素包括：encoding、speed of delivery、pre-processing（例如 generalization、completion、recognition 等）、prior knowledge（包括 valuation、emotional valence、channel reliability 等）、optimum level（受 speed of processing 影响），以及更多。

这个过程的复杂性要求有一个更好的 concept 能够 encapsulate 所有这些 nuances。我建议使用 learntropy 这个 term 来描述一个 educational channel 或 signal 从特定 context 中个体大脑的角度来看的 attractiveness。

^pr-a7c5d5edb27d

**Learntropy 是任何 educative signal 的 attractiveness，由 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") system 所决定。**

Lectures 可以是 boring 或 attractive 的。Learntropy 从个体的角度表达它们的 attractiveness。

虽然 entropy 有一个精确的 mathematical definition，但 learntropy 大概最好通过 reward system 对从所分析的 signal 进行 learning 这一 act 的 response 来 measure。正如 entropy 取决于单个 messages 的 probability，learntropy 将取决于这些 messages（pictures、sounds、sentences 等）的 rewarding power。那个 rewarding power 将与 probability 相关联，但 valuation 将在很大程度上取决于 [knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network")。

对于 good learning 来说，存在一个 reward。然而，也存在 bad learning。存在一个 [decoding failure penalty](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Decoding_failure_penalty "Decoding failure penalty")。如果一个 student 努力去 decode 一个 message 却失败了，他就会受到 penalize。frustration 就是这样诞生的。对 learning 的 dislike 就是这样开始的。如果 learntropy 低，reward 就少，penalty 就高，net result 可能是 negative 的。如果我们把 negative reward signals 考虑在内，learntropy 实际上可能取 negative values。一个 boring lecture 可能带有 negative learntropy。它将导致 suppress 掉 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")。

高的 knowledge valuations 有助于高的 [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy")，而后者又是 attention 以及 knowledge 的 semantic slotting in 以实现 long-term retention 所必需的。在一个强大的 feedback loop 中，**learntropy 增强 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，而后者构成决定 learntropy 的 valuations 的基础**。这个 feedback loop 受到 forgetting、learned helplessness、aging、injury 以及 mental resources 的 sheer availability 的制约。通过 rational learning 和 lifestyle management，尤其是关于 [natural creativity cycle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle")，这个 equilibrium 可以在高 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") level 上维持数十年。

## Signal timing vs. learntropy

从 learning stream 中单个 messages 获得的 reward 的程度将决定 signal learntropy 的水平。一个关于 boring topic 的 lecture 将带有 low learntropy。为了 solve 一个 specific problem 而上网 surf 寻找所需的 information 的 titbits 将带有 high learntropy。

与基于 averages 的 [Shannon entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(information_theory\) 不同，learntropy 将更像是一个 trailing average，其中 recent messages 将比更早时间传递的 messages 带有更高的 weight。此外，learntropy 根植于支配 [consolidation of memory](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Two_component_model_of_memory "Two component model of memory") 的 rules，包括 [spacing effect](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spacing_effect "Spacing effect")。

一旦一个 golden nugget of knowledge 填补了 understanding 中的一个重要 gap，一个 boring lecture 的 learntropy 就会 shoot up。learntropy 的增加将与决定 knowledge valuation 的 memory trace 的 [stability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Stability "Stability") 的 expression 成正比（包括 [knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network") 中的 descending traces）。一个 golden nugget 的 impact 将随时间 wane。那些 happy discoveries 的 cumulative effect 将决定任何给定时间（例如在一个 lecture 期间）的 learntropy 水平。

上述内容表明，educators 可以 influence learntropy、enhance [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，并 enhance long-term learning outcomes。Feeding passive knowledge 是一个 bad strategy。Providing answers 应当是有选择性的，并且应当 favor 高重要性的 abstract 和 universal questions。Self-directed learning 的 free explorations 是 lifelong sustainable [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 和 lifelong learning 的最佳 formula。

所有形式的 schooling 都倾向于 suppress [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")。因此，许多 adults 可能会发现难以 internalize 关于 learntropy 在 learning 中重要性的 message。然而，在现代世界中，几乎每个人都面临着独自 solve 一个 minor technical 或 health problem 的需要。这个问题可能简单到只是对 Facebook options 中 setup 的一个 trivial change。找到一个 problem 的 solution 越难，找到 answers 的 reward 就越大。找到 answers 越难，search 和 exploration 就越 persistent 和 extensive。这些 feelings 应当为每个人所熟悉。然而，suppression [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 总是导致更少的 knowledge、更低的 self-esteem，并且所有 explorations 可能更早地 come to an end。换句话说，那些在学校或后来生活中失去 creative drive 的人，会更早 give up，或者也许甚至从不 try。在这个意义上，computers、Internet、technology 等带来的所有 technical problems 和 glitches 都有一些 positive side effect，即刺激失去的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 vestiges，即使在最 passive 的 individuals 中也是如此。唯一的要求是那些 quests 需要以一定程度的 success 结束。否则，可能发生相反的情况。penalty signal 可能导致 conditioning 一种 withdrawal from exploration。

你可以快速回答这个关于你自己的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 instant quiz。如果你在生活中面临一个 minor problem，你是 seek 一个 human expert，还是 rely on Google？如果你的 car fails，或者你的 computer crashes，或者你 get injured，或者你 got a stomach ache，你去哪里？

## Learntropy and learn drive

在一个类似于 [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting_curve "Forgetting curve") 的过程中，[learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") reward 的影响会随时间呈指数下降。就像在 [spaced repetition](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spaced_repetition "Spaced repetition") review 中一样，新的 reward 会把 learntropy 重新带回高水平。就像在 [spacing effect](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spacing_effect "Spacing effect") 中一样，更长的间隔可能会让同一条 message 变得更有 rewarding。

决定 learntropy 的 reward signal 与决定 learning 中 recall 的 consolidation signal 之间有一个重大区别：一旦你学会了某样东西，在短时间内反复 review 是毫无意义的；一旦你把 recall probability 推到 100%，你就可以让时间过去，再进行下一次 review。learntropy 的上限可能很难达到。如果你喜欢一场 lecture，那么通过一些事实或 delivery 上的变化，你可以更喜欢它。如果你记住了一个 singular memory，你无法通过在短时间内使用的技巧把它记得更好。你可以用 mnemonic techniques 重新表述这个 memory 并影响它的 durability，但一旦 recall 的 probability 是 100%，对这个 memory 来说最好的做法也许是让它在一段时间内不被使用，或者把它用在变化的 context 中，这本质上可能会导致形成新的 memories，而这些 memories 会与原来的 singular memory 形成冗余的连接。

learntropy 的 extinction 通过缺乏 reward signal 而发生。[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 extinction 是一个 [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting "Forgetting") 的问题（包括通过 brain cell loss 造成的 forgetting）。

learntropy 会在各个 message 上累加，并伴随指数下降和 diminishing returns。通过优化 rewarding messages 的 timing，我们可以把 learntropy 推高，并让 learning 成为最 pleasurable 的活动之一，与 food、sex、drugs 等的 rewards 相当。如果你怀疑，回想一下那些强迫性的 videogamers，他们真的可以在通宵玩游戏时挨饿。[Videogames](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Videogames "Videogames") 可以劫持 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，并把它与 gambling 的 reward 结合起来。gambling 的 rewards 可能也受与 learntropy 类似的下降和提升规则支配，然而，它们受制于 [variable reward](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Variable_reward "Variable reward")，这可能导致 addiction。区分 learning 的 pleasure 与有害的 addictions 很重要（见：[Addiction to learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Addiction_to_learning "Addiction to learning")）。

learntropy 会决定 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，但两者会以不同的规则维持。learn drive 是 knowledge dependent 的，因此会受制于 [spaced repetition](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spaced_repetition "Spaced repetition")。由于 [knowledge is a network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_network "Concept network")，谈论 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 optimum stimulation 大概是没有意义的。为了最大化 learn drive，我们应该从事 lifelong learning，尊重 [natural creativity cycle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle")，并照顾好 brain health（也就是 general health）。

## Optimum information delivery

在 schooling 中，我们也许可以设想一场以 optimum [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") level 进行的 lecture，其中学生不停地说“_wow! wow!_”。她以人类可能的最快速度记笔记。不过更常见的是，这场 lecture 会发出一个 high entropy signal，或者渗出 boredom。它的 learntropy 会很低，甚至是负的。

如果 optimum learntropy levels 取决于学生，那么一位 teacher 如何能 optimal 地向一个 classroom deliver knowledge？有时 universal delivery 是不可能的。在其他情况下，它困难到需要天才般的 teaching skills。对大多数 teachers 来说，lecture delivery 让大多数 kids 感到 bored 或 frustrated。

在 lecture delivery 中，幸运的少数人可能获得大部分 message。对一小部分 gifted 的人来说，这场 lecture 可能不携带任何新东西。对他们来说它是 boring 的。对其他 kids 来说，message complexity 超出了他们的 comprehension level。在这种情况下，如果他们试图 decode 它，这场 lecture 可能会令人 frustrated。一场关于 string theory 的 lecture 也许可以比作随机打乱的 English words 的 noise。Lecturing 是一种 timewasting 的练习。Nobel Prize 得主 [Carl Wieman](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Carl_Wieman "Carl Wieman") 把它比作 [blood-letting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Wieman:_Lectures_make_no_sense "Wieman: Lectures make no sense")。

为了避免 negative learntropy 带来的 frustration，students 会 tune out，就像你从我 [earlier](#Prior_knowledge_in_information_seeking) 提到的那个 Thai channel 中 tune out 一样。Children 会忽略来自 teacher 的 static noise，并 tune in 到其他携带更合适 learntropy levels 的 channels（例如课桌下手机上的 Facebook）。即使他们的 comprehension 很好，所 deliver 的 knowledge 也可能无法 complement 他们当前的 knowledge。如果它不产生 [high-quality high-value](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network") generalization，它就会被视为 obvious 或 irrelevant。

Low learntropy，即使只是偶尔出现，也会使学生条件反射地 tune out。过一段时间后，students 会发展出一个 filter，把 teacher 变成一个携带 zero entropy 和 zero learntropy 的 silent radio channel。对 lecture quality 的改进将变得徒劳。Teacher 消失了！

在 classroom setting 中，一个 student 常常无法把注意力集中到更好的 signal 上。同一个 signal 被分发给所有 students，他们可能都同样地 bored。相比之下，用好的 keywords 进行 Googling 可以用 perfectly timed low probability messages 轰炸 brain，这些 messages 会 [fit the current knowledge tree like a jigsaw puzzle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_crystallization "Knowledge crystallization")。Google 是一个非常便宜且高效的“wow!”生成器。

在 [incremental learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_learning "Incremental learning") 中，learntropy scanner 会挑选最好的 channels，优先处理它们，并采用完美的 timing 来最大化 semantic connectivity 和 memory consolidation。这应该让人容易理解为什么 **我极其快乐，我永远不会再被迫坐在 school bench 上！** 我太爱 learning 了！

以上所有例子都说明了，在识别值得 learning 的东西时，signal 与 brain 之间的 interaction 有多么错综复杂。learning 的 reward 是已知最好的 learning quality 指标。当 students 快乐时，我们就走在正确的轨道上。当 schools 成为 misery 之地时，我们就在社会尺度上失败了。

**knowledge complementarity 和 [coherence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence") 唯一可靠的 detector 是 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") system 的 neural networks。这就是为什么 knowledge 不能被 prepackaged 并强加给 students。**

这一点用 [crystallization metaphor](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_crystallization "Knowledge crystallization") 来解释。reward system 的 neural details 在以下章节中说明：[Learning rewards](#Learning_rewards)。

## Gripping lectures

我们热爱 learning，但我们通常讨厌被教。这些感受与 creativity 相关，这或许可以用这样一个事实来解释：creative elaboration 对于构成 comprehension 基础的 [pattern completion](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Pattern_completion "Pattern completion") 是必不可少的。

在 learning 中，我们决定要探究什么。[learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") 的评估严格取决于 brain 的状态以及当前的 memory activations。在 teaching 中，knowledge 被分发出去，与我们如何看待它无关。许多学生把 boring subjects 列为他们不喜欢 school 的第一原因。不是 bullying、stress 或 early waking。而是难以忍受的 boredom！我在[这里](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learning_history:_school_vs._self-directed_learning "Learning history: school vs. self-directed learning")写了 self-directed learning 与 learning at school 之间天壤之别。这一切都关乎 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")！

令我惊讶的是，有多少资源被浪费在研究如何让 kids 在 lectures 期间保持兴趣上，而显而易见的是，lectures 只是一种糟糕的教育工具。Eye contact analysis？Engagement analysis？努力量化 passion？所有 kids 都天生具备 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，我们的首要任务应当是确保我们不破坏那种 drive。[Force-feeding knowledge](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Schools_suppress_the_learn_drive "Schools suppress the learn drive") 是 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的主要破坏者。此外，还有许多 socioeconomic factors 阻止很大一部分 kids 即使在最好的情况下也无法茁壮成长。有些 kids 永远不会表现出对 learning 的 passion。在大多数情况下，这不是他们的错。只有极小一部分受到 disabilities、health 以及不那么幸运的 genetic endowments 的限制。[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 随年龄的 exponential decay 主要是由 compulsory schooling 造成的。Passive lecturing 是这一过程的一个巨大促成因素。

自然，有些 lectures 是有效的。[Khan Academy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Khan_Academy "Khan Academy") 塞满了好的例子。即使是没有 slides 的口头 lecture 也能奏效。YouTube 上的 TED talk 可以很有趣。它可以满足 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")。[MOOCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massive_open_online_course) 建立在这样一个原则之上：一位 rock-star teacher 胜过成千上万重复同一套话的普通教师。即使你只是一个 passive listener，你也能学到很多。不过有条件：你需要对 subject 有强烈的 curiosity，或者你需要喜爱 speaker，或者两者兼有。**确保 lecture 有趣的唯一可靠机制是：你需要自己选择它！** 这不过是 self-directed learning 必要性的又一个方面。

除了选择之外，在 lecturing 中，你绝对需要一个 pause button，以防你需要上厕所，或者平息饥饿感。没有什么能像憋尿那样有效地毁掉一场 lecture。最后但同样重要的是，大多数 lectures 都能从 Netflix 的 Skip Intro 功能中受益。

自然，如果你用自己的 creative thinking 甚至快速 research 来增强它，lecture 的效果会最好。这就是为什么暂停一分钟，甚至一天，可能对 learning efficiency 至关重要。与一些 psychiatrists 的说法相反，creative breaks 和 wandering mind 与 [ADHD](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/ADHD "ADHD") 毫无关系。只要它们多少有些相关，它们就是 great learning 的标志。

我使用两种方法来增量地消费 lectures。我的第一种方法是边听边锻炼。Exercise 改善 focus。好的 focus 减少了对 pause 的需求，然而，它也减少了 learning 的 creative 方面。对于最高优先级的 subjects，我使用 [incremental video](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_video "Incremental video")，在那里我可以多次暂停和继续。我甚至可以保留最重要的 lecture extracts 以供将来 review。然而，即使是 incremental video 也不是 learning 的最佳方法。它在速度和容量上无法与 [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") 竞争。有时，采用 incremental reading 并处理 lecture transcript 比听 lecture 本身更有意义。这在 fact-rich lecturing 中尤其明显。

我选择 video materials 主要基于我恰好喜欢听的 speakers。在本文的语境中，我知道你会喜欢 Ken Robinson 的 lectures！去看看：[Robinson: Schools kill creativity](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Robinson:_Schools_kill_creativity "Robinson: Schools kill creativity")！

## Learning rewards

learning 的 pleasure 可能是最令人满足的 pleasures 之一。与 eating 或 having sex 不同，learning 的 pleasure 不会随着行为而终止。learning 的 pleasure 是可持续的，只会随着参与 learning 的 networks 的 overload 而缓慢消退。它可以通过 sleep 重置回基线。learning 的 pleasure 已被证明涉及与 [pleasure of heroin or cocaine](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Biederman_model "Biederman model") 相同的 mechanisms。与 feeding 或 sex 不同，pleasurable learning 可以填满大部分清醒时间。在这个意义上，learning、creativity、problem solving 和 productivity 的 pleasures 可能是 stoic hedonic therapy 中的绝佳工具。虽然对食物的需求在健康的个体中很容易得到满足，但对 learning 的需求可能永远不会结束。[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 取决于当前 knowledge 的状态，而这个状态可以通过 learning 本身来操纵。

### Learn drive reward

我已经提到了几个例子，说明 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 如何在大脑中导致一个 reward signal。我们知道，low probability information 可以是有 reward 的。一个贡献新知识的 generalization 也是如此。一段导向某个伟大理解目标的信息片段具有很高的 value。一个 [missing piece in a jigsaw puzzle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Jigsaw_puzzle_metaphor "Jigsaw puzzle metaphor") 带有巨大的 reward。一个 obscure word，一旦被 decoded，就能让一整篇长文本从一团纠缠的句子变成一条清晰的 reasoning line。

通过一个 generalization 来 confirming 一个 model，或者为一个新的更好的 model 奠定基础，两者都感觉极好。此外，所有与强烈情绪相关联的 model confirmations 都能导致 euphoria：“_My team is the best in the world!_”，或者“_Yes! My newborn is healthy indeed!_”，或者“_Yeah! I knew that hard work will earn me that promotion!_”。然而，在讨论 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 时，我想过滤掉那层可能模糊画面的额外情绪层。我们需要记住，learning 是有 pleasure 的，无论它是否通过运用知识带来 rewards。

discovery 的 _Aha!_、_Wow!_ 或 _Eureka!_ 是 learning 中最纯粹、最终的 prize。它不需要带来来自他人的 accolades 或 praise 形式的进一步 reward。在这里，knowledge 本身就是它自己的 reward。

这个 reward 的共同分母，是把新的 highly-valued information 在 memory 中进行 encoding。

**The learn drive reward 来自 [high-value knowledge](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network")，它已准备好进行 long-term storage。**

在我们理解现实的探索中，随着大脑中存储的信息总量增加，stored knowledge 的 entropy 会下降。**随着 learning 和 modeling，理解世界的 complexity 所需的 effort 越来越少。**

### Evolution of the learn drive

科学家们说，聪明的动物玩耍得更多。我要说，更值得注意的是，玩耍得更多的物种更聪明。我 hypothesize，**[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 可能是 human brain size 爆炸的 trigger factor**。并不是鸟类或哺乳动物面临了需要更多思考的环境变化。并不是人类突然面临灭绝，除非他们 blow up 自己 cortex 的 size。可能是 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的出现，突然让 brain cells 数量那昂贵的增加得到了更好的利用。在 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 出现之前，增加 brain size 可能只会让动物多出一个头的重量要携带，以及多出一组细胞要喂养。没有 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，多出来的 brain space 可能一直未被使用，并且很可能经历浪费性的 atrophy。如果 schooling 试图 override [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，它就会促成那种进化优势的 disuse。它就会促成一个不那么聪明、不那么有创造力的社会。

如果我们把 brain size 绘制在人类进化的时间线上，我们可以在大约 2 million years ago 看到一次强劲的上升。Paleoanthropologists 倾向于把那次上升归因于饮食中更好的 brain nutrients、[cooking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catching_Fire:_How_Cooking_Made_Us_Human) 等等。

如果关于 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 出现的 hypothesis 是正确的，_Homo habilis_ 就会是这一突破起点的候选者。这可能指向从鸟类和哺乳动物简单的 procedural play drive，向更复杂的 declarative learn drive 的过渡，后者最终引导我们建立现实的 abstract models，而这些 models 是 human intelligence 的基础。_Homo habilis_ 也被 hypothesize 为导致了 [childhood dominated by brain growth](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Homo_habilis:_the_emergence_of_childhood "Homo habilis: the emergence of childhood") 的出现（从 weaning 到平均 7 years old）。

[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 在进化中较晚才出现，这暗示它并不是 neural networks 中涌现出的一种简单属性（见：[Biederman model](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Biederman_model "Biederman model")）。否则它可能很容易就在鱼类或更早的物种中出现。[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 需要一套专门的 neural structures，能够在检测到对 coherent structure of declarative knowledge 的 incremental contribution 时发送一个 reward signal。这个 signal 及其底层结构在 procedural learning 和 declarative learning 中可能不同。它对于不同类别的 sensory input 也可能不同。

### Procedural learning reward

我在 1980s 就 hypothesize 过 [circuits that might run procedural learning](http://www.super-memory.com/english/ol/ol_files/refinement_circuitry_in_stochastic_learning.jpg)。在我的 [Master's Thesis](http://super-memory.com/english/ol.htm) 中，出于无知，我用了自己的术语 ["_stochastic learning_"](http://www.super-memory.com/english/ol/ol_memory.htm)。我完全不知道，早在二十年前，也就是 1969 年，David Marr 就提出了一个 cerebellar cortex 的理论模型，与我的想法相符。在新千年里，[there is a lot of data to confirm the model](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2805361/)。

procedural learning circuit 的想法非常简单。想象你骑自行车。你运用自己的 conscious mind 去学习上车所需的各个动作，然后继续踩踏板。然而，一旦你上路了，procedural learning system 就确保你能够以最小的 neural effort 自动执行所有动作，无需 conscious supervision 的参与，或只需对一组 [command neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_neuron) 进行最小限度的 supervision。Procedural learning 将决定你的 [motor program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_program)。这个 procedural learning system 会对发送到 motor system 的 signal sequence 做出微小的随机调整（因此得名“_stochastic learning_”）。你可以把这些随机变化视为 procedural creativity。每当你的自行车失去平衡，一个 penalty signal 就会从 error-detecting network 发出，以取消所提议的 corrections。那个 penalty signal 将扮演 motor program 的 teaching signal 的角色。

在 sleep 期间，memories 会被重组，以消除对 conscious input 的需要，被简化、优化，而对 skill 贡献很低的 garbage signals 会被拒绝。每骑行一公里，signal sequence 都会通过 trial and error 得到完善。每经历一次 sleep，wrinkles 都会变得更平滑。骑自行车将变成一种 pleasure。那种 pleasure 似乎在从笨拙的 conscious rider 向 natural 的过渡中达到顶峰。

以类似的方式，随着你在电脑上打出的每一个句子，你打出的 typos 会越来越少。你知道“)”在键盘上的哪里吗？“}”呢？你打字越流畅，就越可能忘记这个细节。当对 motor sequences 的 conscious control 被拿走时，关于“)”在键盘上位置的 declarative knowledge 可能会作为“garbage”被扔掉。它不再被需要了。

### Declarative learning reward

在解释 declarative [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 时，事情要稍微复杂一些。对于 [declarative learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Declarative_memory "Declarative memory") 来说，确实存在一种 reward。有些东西就是 interesting，而找出真相是 pleasing 的。在 neural level 上，brain 会扫描 inputs 和 neural activations，以寻找具有高 [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") 的区域，这些区域能够最大程度地传递与 memory 当前状态相匹配的新 knowledge。任何具有低 probability 的有意义 message 都会被认为更具 attractive。一个明亮的 fractal pattern 会被认为 beautiful。一种灰色的 colors 随机性会被认为 boring。对于更复杂的 visual message 也是如此。一片生机勃勃的 forest 是 beautiful 的。同一片 forest 在冬天、在干旱中，或在 environmental pollution 的影响下，可能看起来不 attractive。[Steven Pinker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Pinker) 说过，我们会被那些散发着 vitality 的 images 所吸引。我不同意。这种 attraction 要广泛得多。我们可能同样会被一座死寂的 volcano 或南极洲的 frozen landscape 所吸引。我们喜爱那些能够使用 [simple models](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Abstract_knowledge "Abstract knowledge") 来表达复杂 information 的 environments、signals、messages 或 brain activations。一幅 beautiful beach 的 picture 可以由几个简单的 shapes 和 textures 来表示。

information 的 entropy 与 data 的 compressability 有关。signal processing 从 input 开始。retina 对 visual input signal 进行 100 倍的 compression。visual cortex 接收 shapes 和 relationships 的简单 representations。hippocampus 在 episodic context 中接收那个 information。那些 signals 最终可能会改变 neocortical long-term memory storage 中单个 synapse 的状态。

[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的基础是寻找在 neural networks 中表示 knowledge 的有效方式。[Learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")、[memory optimization in sleep](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Memory_optimization_in_sleep "Memory optimization in sleep") 和 [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting "Forgetting") 对于最大化 compressibility、[abstractness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Abstractness "Abstractness")、[applicability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Applicability "Applicability") 和 performance 至关重要。这就是 brain 如何确保我们能够使用简单的 representations 来看待一个复杂的世界。这就是 [human intelligence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Human_intelligence "Human intelligence") 的核心。如果 artificial intelligence 的研究人员能够为 robots 配备一种类似人类的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，在拥有足够 memory 的情况下，它们的学习能力可能是无穷无尽的。

### Reward centers in learning

在 2014 年，研究人员报告说，[activity in the nucleus accumbens was increased in the state of "high curiosity"](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Curiosity_improves_learning "Curiosity improves learning")。他们还证明了我们一直知道的事情：这种状态改善了 memory performance。此外，这种改善的 performance 还溢出到了 incidental learning，也就是那些本身不会激发 curiosity 的 learning。这项研究被 media 广泛报道，但带有错误的 interpretation："_curiosity primes the brain for better memory_"。例如，Scientific American 的标题是 "_Neuroimaging reveals how the brain’s reward and memory pathways prime inquiring minds for knowledge_"。论文本身则建议需要 "_stimulating curiosity_"。

由于 reward centers 可能参与 anticipation of pleasure，我们更应该将这项研究的结果视为一个 indicator，表明 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 与 pleasure 相关联。是 learn drive 导致了 learning。是 learning 本身是 pleasurable 的。标题应该是 "_Neuroimaging confirms that efficient learning is pleasurable_"。换句话说，顺序不是 "drive -> pleasure -> learning"，而是 "drive -> learning -> pleasure"。

与其说需要 "stimulate curiosity"，不如说需要 "develop the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")"。关键区别在于，将 stimulation 视为一种可能用于 classroom 的 quick-fix approach，而不是一个需要数月乃至数年的 long-term process。一场 advertising campaing 可能会使用廉价的 tricks 来刺激我们的 curiosity，而终生的 passion 则是 insatiable 且 unwaning 的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的公式，这是 unceasing learning 的完美 warranty。

curiosity 的状态确实会改善 attention，而这会改善整体的 learning，然而，这绝不应该被用作 classroom strategy。learning 的 Gamification 只有在 rewards 来自 target learning，而不是来自围绕 target 的 learning 时才有意义。许多面向儿童的 learning programs 使用明亮的 colors、不寻常的 sounds 或微笑的面孔来吸引 attention 以诱导 learning。然而，一旦 habituation 形成，这种形式的 artificial gamification 就不再有效。此外，incidental knowledge 不会持久。任何利用 curiosity 来激发 incidental learning 的努力都是 non-specific 且 inefficient 的。我们同样可以寄希望于 pharmacological intervention，例如使用 Ritalin，能够改善 learning。相反，learning 必须成为它自己的 reward。

nucleus accumbens 和 ventral tegmental area 参与 pleasure、anticipation of pleasure 以及 signal evaluation。来自 [knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network") 的 signals 以其 [motivational and affective valence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Brain_centers_involved_in_valuation_of_anticipated_outcomes:_nucleus_accumbens_and_VTA "Brain centers involved in valuation of anticipated outcomes: nucleus accumbens and VTA") 汇聚到这些区域。Dopamine 参与 anticipation of pleasure。由于 dopamine 参与 attention，仅仅是 anticipation of pleasure 就会因为更好地聚焦于预期能带来 pleasure 的 information 来源而改善 learning。

如果你还不信服，想想当你的 news channel 耍花招来激起你的 interest，然后说 "_find out after the break_" 时，你有多讨厌它。当他们用 "_Breaking News!_" 毁掉一切时，你会更加愤怒。Anticipation 也可能导致 frustration。只有实际的 learning 才提供 reward。从 evolution 的角度来看，只有实际的 learning reward 才有意义。我们不想仅仅因为动物看到食物就奖励它。

nucleus accumbens 中的 buzz 可以是 pleasure 的直接表达，也可能表明 pleasure seeking 的状态。最终，实际的 interpretation 对于最终的 conclusion 并不重要：**boredom 和 displeasure 是 learning 的敌人**。

对于新 knowledge 补充当前 knowledge 的 efficient learning，我们需要遵循 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")。简单来说，这意味着 learning 的 pleasure 在教育中是可取的。我们绝不应该在 displeasure 的状态下 learning（参见 [Desirable difficulty](#Desirable_difficulty)）。Painful learning 来自 brain 让学生知道，在 information theoretic 意义上，新 knowledge 不合适！它将被拒绝。Pleasure 是一个好的 guide！

从上述 neural reasoning 中，我们得出显而易见的结论：**efficient learning 的最佳 warranty 是让学生自己 learning 并追随他们自己的 passions。**

## Biederman model

### 关于阅读之乐的阅读之乐

In 2006, Irving Biederman and Edward A. Vessel, published a paper that gave me unforgettable pleasure to read. The article itself explained the pleasure of reading to me. In a paper titled "_[Perceptual pleasure and the brain](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_receptors_are_involved_in_the_pleasure_of_learning "Opioid receptors are involved in the pleasure of learning")_", Biederman hypothesized that a gradient of opioid receptors in brain structures responsible for visual perception might contribute to the pleasure of viewing nice scenes such as beautiful landscapes. Biederman's idea seemed to explain to me what I have known for ages: **learning is pleasurable**. I always liked to learn, however, I never truly understood what underlies my liking in terms of brain science. Biederman's explanation was a perfect fit and it was powerfully pleasurable. It explained something that bothered my mind for a longer while. At the moment of reading, I was very self-analytical. While reading about the pleasure of reading I was trying to "feel" how the enlightenment of reading provides the pleasure. The pleasure of reading about the pleasure of reading became unforgettable.

Biederman 和 Vessel 所提出的东西是 monumental 的。因此，为简单起见，让我把他们的思想命名为：**Biederman model**（命名按资历）。在 visual perception 中，连续的 neuron 层负责对 visual scene 进行更 abstract 的 representations。打个比方，它从 pixels 和 colors 开始，然后推进到 edges、textures 和 surfaces，再到 objects，再到 faces、places 和 collections，然后到 meaningful episodic scenes，这些在链条末端可能激活一个 "beautiful mountain" 的 representation，并以此被记住，只有少数 details 在 working memory 中超越第一印象而留存下来。一张 photograph 的数百万 pixels 会变成一个 meaningful scene，它可以用几句话 verbalize，并以此被记住多年，而 neural cost 极小。

Biederman model capitalizes on an earlier discovery ([Michael E. Lewis et al., 1981](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_receptors_form_a_gradient_along_a_processing_hierarchy)) that there is a gradient of mu\-opioid receptors along the visual perception pathway. The more meaning the neuron carries, the more opioid receptors it is likely to have. We know that opiates are rewarding and addictive. Biederman model is based on the hypothesis that this [gradient of opioid receptors is the source of perceptive pleasure](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_receptors_are_involved_in_the_pleasure_of_learning "Opioid receptors are involved in the pleasure of learning").

^pr-5ac4fd9a775a

处理 speech 和 music 也有一个类似的 hierarchical system。temporal cortex 涉及从 pitch 到 melody 处理 sounds。rhythm 的处理还涉及 [other areas of the brain](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4101486/)。很可能，所有这些 perceptive networks 都按照类似的原则运作。这是 [neuroesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroesthetics) 的研究主题。

### Opioid 与 dopamine 的 pleasure

不过，Biederman model 有一个小问题。learning 的 pleasure 可以被有意识地分析。就我自己而言，阅读 Biederman model 的 pleasure 可以被分解并追溯到该 model 的各个 components。这一事实意味着，该 pleasure 与 conscious experience 是 integrated 的。Consciousness 对 neuroscience 来说是一个出了名难啃的硬骨头。我们对 consciousness 所知的大部分要么是 speculative 的，要么基于艰难而昂贵的 experiments，其中植入 brain 的 electrodes 可以被用来 elicits 那些之后或同时可由受影响的个体报告的效果。证据似乎正汇聚到 consciousness 的 integrative model 上，其中 brain 中若干 structures 的 activation 被 integrated 并感知为 conscious self。按照那条思路，在 cortex 某处激活一个 Halle Berry neuron 并不足以把 Halle 带入一个人的 consciousness。数百万 concept neurons 可以同时被 activated，而一个 thinking mind 只能对 perceived reality 的 model 中的少数几块进行操作（见：[attention](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Attention "Attention")）。要把 Halle 带入一个人的 mind，该 activation 必须与 conscious perception 的其他 components 相 integrated，包括该 perception 的 reward。

出于这些原因，cortical neurons 中的 opioid receptors 对 learning 的 ultimate reward 不会起多大作用。一种 opiod antagonist，[naloxone, can take away some of the pleasure of music in some people](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Thrill_of_music_may_be_attenuated_with_opioid_antagonists "Thrill of music may be attenuated with opioid antagonists")。然而，learning 的 opioid pleasure 更应当产生一种首次微量使用 heroin 或 morphine 时的 mild bliss。在这个意义上，endomorphins 的 release 和 opioid receptors 的 activation 可以对 learning 的 pleasure 有所贡献。尽管如此，这种 pleasure 并不足够 specific，无法给人带来 "wow!"、"aha!" 或 "eureka!" 的一阵 jolt（Biederman 称之为 "click of comprehension"）。对于那个 ultimate learning reward，必须有一种 integrative reward experience 来自 [pleasure centers in the brain](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Neural_circuits_involved_in_liking_and_wanting "Neural circuits involved in liking and wanting")。

### association 的 pleasure

那种 discovery 的 ultimate pleasure jolt 将来自一个 meaningful association。它可以用理解 Biederman model 本身的 pleasure 来解释。当思考这个 model 时，我们在 mind 中激活两个重要的 concepts：(1) 一个 meaning 的 gradient（源自对 visual perception 所涉及的 neural structures 的理解），以及 (2) 一个 pleasure 的 gradient（源自对 visual pathways 中 opioid receptors 的 content 的观察）。一旦这两个 concepts 在 mind 中出现，就有一个 analogy 的 glue：_"gradient"_ 这个 concept。那个 glue 有助于引出那个带来一阵 pleasant enlightenment 的 association：**MEANING = PLEASURE**！这正是我在阅读 Biederman 的 paper 时所体验到的。要让那阵 jolt 发生，仅仅是与 pleasure 的 gradient 这个 concept 相关联的 opiate receptors 比与 gradient 的 mathematical underpinnings 或它与 "gradient" 一词的 association 相关联的更多，是不够的。仅仅是与 "gradient of meaning" 这个 novel concept 相关联的 opiate 比与常用的 "meaning" 一词相关联的更多，也是不够的。那阵 jolt 发生在两个 highly priced concepts 相撞之时：meaning + pleasure。

Biederman 注意到，receptors 的 gradient 一直延伸到 associative areas，包括 parahippocampal cortex。我们可能记得，在更下游，在 hippocampus 中我们发现了 [Halle Berry neuron](#Detecting_surprisal)。为了说明 opioid pleasure 与 associative pleasure 之间的区别，让我们想象在一片 beautiful beach 上遇见 Halle。当在 beach 上行走时，我们可能体验到一阵微妙的 heroin-like breeze of bliss，它来自这样的 realization：我们的 environment 在 perceptively 上是 beautiful 的：_"the beach I walk on feels great"_。一旦 Halle 出现在 horizon 上，visual analysis 可能提供另一阵 opioid pleasure 的 breeze，来自信号 _"beautiful lady approaching"_。然后 visual processing unit 可能把这位 lady 识别为 Halle，这可能激活 Halle 的 cortical representation，而它可能是 opioid-rich 的。然而，只有 Halle 与 _"my beach"_ 的 ultimate association 才会触发一个 major discovery，也许是一个 atavistic reproductive dream：_"Halle walks the same sand like me!"_。正是在这里，来自 ventral striatum 和 nucleus accumbens 的 reward 可能发挥作用，来 "liking" 这一 situation，而一阵 [dopamine](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_rewards_may_depend_on_dopamine_signals "Opioid rewards may depend on dopamine signals") 的 jolt 可能触发一个 "wanting" 的 behavioral program。那个 behavioral "wanting" program 的细节已被 censorship 从本文中删去。尽管如此，那个 program 的执行在高度发达的个体中不可避免地会被来自 prefrontal cortex 的 [executive signals](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Executive_function "Executive function") 所 halt。简而言之，向 brain 的 pleasure centers 注入 dopamine 可能给 brain 一些不体面的 ideas，而 opioid peptides 的 release 可能只是带来一种 associative bliss。

learning 的 pleasure 不需要涉及有吸引力的异性代表。Halle 出现在我的例子中，只是因为 [Halle Berry neuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmother_cell) 的发现。对于 learning 的 pleasure，所需要的只是一个 powerful 且 highly-valued 的 ideas 的 association，它激活 brain 中的 pleasure centers。每当我们学到新东西时，pleasure 就会发生，而当我们学到 high value 的东西时，那阵 jolt 最为 powerful。发现 Biederman model 的 pleasure 来自我的 [knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network") 中对 learning 本身的 pleasure 的 high valuations。High valuations 带来 high reward，这可能促进 memory（见：[Dopamine may modulate plasticity in learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Dopamine_may_modulate_plasticity_in_learning "Dopamine may modulate plasticity in learning")）

### Impact of memory on the pleasure of learning

我还要对 Biederman 关于 desensitization 的 hypotheses 补充一点，也就是 pleasure 随着 repeated exposure 而 decline。Biederman 认为，children 喜爱重复性的 [videogames](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Videogames "Videogames") 是因为 gambling factor。然而，gambling 在 adults 身上同样 potent。我假定，children 更享受重复性的 learning 是因为 [childhood amnesia](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Childhood_amnesia "Childhood amnesia")。一部分 repeat pleasure 可能来自有限的 comprehension，但另一部分则仅仅可以用加速的 forgetting 来解释。Poor comprehension 和 forgetting 是 adult 与 child brains 之间的主要 differentiators。

我们还应当注意到，pleasure of review 的大量 decline 并非来自 competitive learning，而是来自 [long term-memory consolidation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Two_component_model_of_memory "Two component model of memory")，它可能使 signals 在系统中高效流动。Competitive learning 在 pattern recognition 中可能很重要，但在 associative learning 中，正是高 [retrievability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Retrievability "Retrievability") 会削弱 repeated exposure 的 pleasure。

### Stages of learn drive evolution

当我假设人类身上出现了强大的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 时，我想到的是从 knowledge 到 reward centers 的直接 channel。它最终会是比 Biederman model 所暗示的更高层次的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")。每当涉及 receptors 时，evolution 就有一种简单而感恩的材料可供利用。Receptor gradient 最初是在 [rhesus cortex](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_receptors_form_a_gradient_along_a_processing_hierarchy "Opioid receptors form a gradient along a processing hierarchy") 中发现的。类似的 mechanisms 可能也存在于更简单的大脑、甚至更原始、缺乏 central control 的 nervous systems 中。我不知道一只 ant 在想什么、感觉如何，但找到一处绝佳的 food source 必定是某种 ant pleasure 的来源。由此我们可以得出结论：pleasure of learning 在 phylogenetically 上可能并不比 nervous system 本身年轻多少。然而，在 evolution 的过程中，这个 drive 积累了新的功能与效率层次。Playful creativity 似乎只随某些 birds 和 mammals 出现。那个 evolutionary process 可能最终在人类的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 上达到顶峰。这自然会在某个时候被实现在 thinking machines 中。理解 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的力量，对人类的生存至关重要：既在于它对 artificial intelligence 的需要，也在于 AI 转而对抗人类的威胁。

## Desirable difficulty

**Desirable difficulty** 是一个概念，它可能成为容忍学校里 learning 的 displeasure 的借口。在此我解释为什么这个借口是不公正且危险的。

Robert Bjork 可能是 learning theory 方面最好的专家。如果他对你说，difficulties 在 learning 中可以是 desirable 的，他是对的，而且这与 good learning 总是 pleasurable 这一事实并不矛盾。Desirable difficulty 是一组概念的 conglomerate，其中 learning 中的 obstacles 会带来更好的 learning。让我们在 pleasure of learning 的视角下逐一处理它们：

-   **[active recall](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Active_recall "Active recall")**：active recall 优于 passive review。Active recall 更难。这是一个 desirable difficulty。我们在学习中需要 active recall，因为它是唯一能让一个 memory engram 在 [spaced repetition](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spaced_repetition "Spaced repetition") 中被有效 reconsolidated 的 procedure。每当我们把有用的 knowledge 用于实践时，就会发生 active recall。这种使用是 pleasurable 的，因为它带来 productivity，而 productivity 是一种独立于 learning 的 reward。人类就是喜欢 [achieve goals](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Setting_goals_can_change_your_life "Setting goals can change your life")。如果 review 是人为计划的，就像在 [SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo "SuperMemo") 中那样，它就不会带来 productive act，而且很容易失去它的 appeal。所有成功的 SuperMemo 用户都把 review 与他们的 goals 联系起来。他们把每个 item 和每次 repetition 都看作通向更好未来的一步。并非所有用户都有这种想象能力。这就是为什么 SuperMemo 尽管效率惊人，却没有让人类为之倾倒。
-   **[spaced repetition](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spaced_repetition "Spaced repetition")**：如果 memory 的 [retrievability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Consolidation "Consolidation") 更低，memory [consolidation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Retrievability "Retrievability") 就更有效。这导致 [recall](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Recall "Recall") 中的 difficulty。这是一个 desirable difficulty。就像 [active recall](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Active_recall "Active recall") 一样，[review](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Review "Review") 的 reward 来自 knowledge 的运用和 productivity。在 [SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo "SuperMemo") 中，默认情况下，大多数 review 都以成功的 recall 结束，而且 difficulty 与 pleasure 之间可能存在某种联系。同样，只有一部分 SuperMemo 用户能觉得这个过程 pleasurable。那些不觉得的人通常坚持不了多久就退出了。我们告诉所有用户，让 SuperMemo 变得 fun，否则它对你就不起作用！另见：[Pleasure of knowing](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Pleasure_of_knowing "Pleasure of knowing")
-   **incremental review**：SuperMemo 提倡在 spaces 中学习。从 memory 和 creativity 的角度来看，在较长一段时间内以小块阅读一篇文章更有效率。观看视频或听讲座也是如此。这会导致为 context retrieval 而进行的小规模搏斗。然而，它带来 creative elaboration 方面的额外 bonus。它还改善 memory encoding、generalization 和 long-term memory consolidation。矛盾的是，这些额外的 difficulties 带来额外的 learning efficiency，使 [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") 成为最 pleasurable 的 learning 形式之一。
-   **learning context**：在 retrieval 中改变 context 是一种非常简单而有效的 desirable difficulty。如果 encoding 是正确的，retrieval 就会成功，它会更有效，而且会有 rewarding。如果 context change 导致 generalization 和更好的 memory encoding，learning 的有效性就会提高，learning 的 reward 也会提高。
-   **problem solving**：解决问题可以非常 pleasurable。问题越难，solution 的 pleasure 就越大。Problem solving 涉及一个 learning 过程，因为 solution 需要 intermediary steps，这些步骤会把新的 knowledge 存入 memory。所有这些步骤都是 pleasurable 的。如果 student 在 task 上挣扎而没有进展，他就什么也学不到，也得不到 reward。这个 task 变得太难了。如果 student 未能解决 problem，但在 intermediary steps 上取得了进展，即使这些步骤与 solution 无关，learning 也会存在，reward 也会存在。同样，如果 difficulty 是 desirable 的，它就会带来 reward。如果没有 reward，那么 difficulty 就显得无法逾越。因此，它既不是 rewarding 的，也不是 desirable 的。
-   **learning by doing**：learning by doing 可能涉及 play、creativity、problem solving 等等。Learning by doing 花费更多时间，而且常常带来更好的结果和更多的 reward。
-   **delayed feedback**：在某些情况下，delayed feedback 可能导致更多的 processing。用最简单的话说，如果 teacher 不告诉你你做得有多好，你可能会多琢磨一会儿。这可以有益于 memory。如果确实有益，最终效果就会是 rewarding 的。
-   **help withdrawal**：我在 [schools suppressing the learning drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Schools_suppress_the_learn_drive#Teacher_problem "Schools suppress the learn drive") 的语境中写到 help withdrawal。得不到答案的 kids 可能会变得更 curious。Curiosity 增加 learning 的 reward。那些在纠正自己错误的 reality models 时得不到帮助的 students，会因为独自解决 inconsistencies 而获得更强的 rewards。
-   **other difficulties**：能够改善 learning 的 obstacles 数量是无穷的，其中一些可能是 hormonal 性质的，一些可能涉及 motivational forces。所有这些 obstacles 的共同点似乎是某种形式的更深的 processing、memory consolidation、改善的 attention 等等。不可避免地，带来更好 learning 的 obstacles 也涉及更好的 reward。

Desirable difficulty 并不会夺走 learning 的 pleasure。恰恰相反，它使 learning 更有效、更有趣。如果 difficulty 走得太远，导致 displeasure，那么这种 difficulty 就不再是 desirable 的。这个简单的等价关系来自 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 中 reward system 的 mechanics。

^pr-f82544033d9d

注意，由于 desirable difficulty 而给高效 learning 带来的 reward bonus 并不需要对应于高 [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy")。Learntropy 是 information channel 的一个 metric。例如，active recall 与 novelty 无关。它指的是 memory reconsolidation。同样，problem solving 可能部分来自实现与 learning 无关的 goals 的需要，或者由 new knowledge 增益之外的 productivity 来 rewarding。

还要注意，上述几乎所有的 desirable difficulties 都内在地嵌入在 [incremental learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_learning "Incremental learning") 的过程中。

## Addiction to learning

### Inborn addiction

我们生来就热爱 learning。这种爱通常在 [compulsory schooling](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Compulsory_schooling "Compulsory schooling") 的岁月里迅速消退。我们越能维持对 learning 的爱，对 brain、health 以及人类的好处就越大。对 learning 的爱与 addiction 毫无关系。[addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addiction) 的定义包含因强迫性地从事某项活动而产生的不良后果。

learning 的负面副作用与益处相比微不足道。如果存在某种程度的 voracity 甚至 [compulsion](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Rage_to_master "Rage to master")，它反而能进一步放大正面效果。增强一个人对 learning 的爱是可能的。好的 learning 为进一步的 learning 提供最好的助推。

### Learning and gambling

在涉及 learning 与 gambling 的 reward systems 之间存在着紧密的联系。Gambling 与 learning new words 都会以 [similar fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striatum) 激活 [ventral striatum](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822\(14\)01207-X)。这种与 gambling 的紧密联系可能会让 learning 的图景变得混乱。一个在 slot machine 前的 gambler 学不到多少东西。Addictive videogaming 则更好。它可能相当有教育意义。许多 team game addicts 在学校里毫无进展，却达到了 English 的 fluency。对 sports news 的 addiction 也可能涉及一定程度的 learning。我是在 Africa Cup of Nations（football）期间才了解到 [Cabinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Cabinda) 的。对 Facebook updates 的 addiction 也没什么不同。它基于对特定收益的预期中的 [variable reward](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Variable_reward "Variable reward")，然而，它也可能涉及很大程度的 learning。那种 learning 可能涉及 gossip、celebrity news、fake news，或者真正有用的 learning。甚至 political poll updates 也能引起 addiction。在 Hillary Clinton 与 Donald Trump 的较量中，polls 足够均衡，从而产生了 cliffhanger effect。强迫性地查看 new polls 具有 addiction 的所有特征。然而，这种 addiction 可以带来大量的 learning。是否 [separate gambling from learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Optimality_of_the_learn_drive "Optimality of the learn drive") 取决于学生自己。Voracious learning 是好的。源自 addiction 的 learning 也可能是好的。然而，gambling 本身对人类存在带来的价值很小。这就是为什么理解 [Reward diversity in preventing addictions](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Reward_diversity_in_preventing_addictions "Reward diversity in preventing addictions") 非常重要。

### Learning and sleep

强迫性的 learning 可能侵占 sleep time，并可能助长 insomnia 和 [DSPS](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/DSPS "DSPS") 的流行。拥有强大 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 creative minds 可能会一直 learning 到凌晨。在 electric lighting 出现之前，这种对 sleep pattern 的违反是困难或不可能的。好消息是，[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 往往会随着 [network fatigue](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Homeostatic "Homeostatic") 而减弱。我们 learning 得越久，memory circuits 中的饱和程度就越高。只有 sleep 能带来缓解。这就是为什么即使是最贪婪的 learners 也会在某个时刻变得困倦并放弃 learning。如果一个读者为了一本小说而熬过整夜，这很可能是 sleep drive 不足、learning 减少以及 [variable reward](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Variable_reward "Variable reward") 增加共同作用的结果，而后者是悬念小说的典型特征。

### Learning and exercise

我听说强迫性的 learning 会导致 exercise 减少。那会很糟糕。然而，我认为更可能产生这种效果的是糟糕的 learning。好的 learning 是快乐的，并能激发额外的能量。一个快乐的孩子不应该长时间坐在书本或电脑前。一定有办法释放能量。也许我们更应该说，exercise 减少是 learning addiction 的一个标志，而好的 learning 具有 neurotrophic effects，应该让人迸发出多余的能量去消耗？

### Learning restraint

learning 有其代价，也需要时间。这就是为什么它应该审慎进行。然而，好的 learning 几乎总是一项良好的长期投资。这就是为什么我们永远不应害怕 addiction。恰恰相反，我们应该珍视并激发 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，以保障快乐的终身 learning。

## Displeasure of learning

当我声称所有的 learning 都是令人愉悦的时，我听到一片这样的声音：“_我不得不经历一场极其紧张的 exam，它给了我许多受用终生的好知识_”。这些声音把好的 learning 的 pleasure 与那些把 learning 变成许多学生噩梦的因素所带来的 displeasure 混为一谈。那些噩梦因素包括糟糕的 teachers、严厉的 parents、deadlines、stress、糟糕的 sleep、糟糕的 textbooks、过量的 volume 等等。

我听说，如果没有 deadlines 或 school-imposed goals，learning 就会被 videogames、novels、TV、hobbies、sports 等取代。这可能因许多原因而是真的。其中一些活动可能带有与 learning 无关的 pleasures。然而，它们也会因 learning 或 exercise 的原因而有益。一个全面发展的学生应该可以自由地放慢速度，把时间分配给有趣的 learning 和其他有趣的活动。缓慢的进展可能带来更多益处。

learning 的 equation 绝不可能在好的 learning 之后产生 unhappiness。责任总是在别处。所有负面因素都应该被研究并消除。

归根结底，即使存在与 exams、certificates 和 duties 相关的 displeasure，这种 displeasure 也应该由学生自己施加于自身。

**Pleasurable learning 可能被 stress、bad people、bad schools、bad textbooks 等造成的 displeasure 所掩埋**。

## Learning and procrastination

如果 learning 是最可持续的 pleasure 形式，为什么有一半的学生会 procrastinate？这几乎是普通人群中该比例的三倍。

答案很简单也很重要：学生之所以 procrastinate，是因为尽管 good learning 是一种 pleasure，但 bad learning 却极其令人不快。学校甚至大学里的大多数 assignments 都与 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的需求存在大量 mismatch。这种 learning 是 ineffective 且令人不快的。那些孩子常常在晚上玩 computer games，声称他们 _需要让大脑休息_。我怀疑他们的大脑在休息。他们实际上在做他们觉得 pleasurable 的事情。其中很大一部分 pleasure 来自 new learning。不幸的是，学校并不为 good gaming 提供学分，所以 chores-and-fun 的正弦循环会在第二天甚至同一天以 homework 开始。

我永远惊叹于有多少学生称自己 lazy。与此同时，只要这些事情是 enjoyable 的或服务于他们自己的 goals，他们就能完成许多体力或脑力上的 heroic feats。即使是那些在 SuperMemo 中记住了数千个 items 的人，也常常给自己很低的 conscientiousness 分数。learning 的 goals 可能很模糊，但即使它们非常清晰，input 与 prior knowledge 之间的 poor match 也可能导致显著的 displeasure。如果 [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") 很低，assignments 就会很 boring。如果它是负的，它们就会令人 repulsive。

high goal valuations 与 bad learning 的 negative rewards 之间的 battle 会导致 procrastination。Procrastinators 常常称自己 lazy，即使他们根本不是。

如果你认为自己在 learning 上很 lazy，你需要重新评估你的 materials 和你的 methodology。即使只是对 [natural creativity cycle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle") 的简单违反，也会扼杀 learning 的 fun。

## Learning and depression

Learning 是一种可持续且 non-addictive 的 pleasure 形式，除了时间成本之外几乎没有任何 side effects。此外，good learning 往往会占据心智，并通过提升 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 来促进更多的 learning。这意味着 learning 应当可以作为 depression 的 therapy 来使用。

### Learning at school

如果 learning 是 pleasure 和 reward 的来源，为什么我们在学龄儿童身上看到 [rampant depression](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_increase_in_depression "Incremental increase in depression")？尽管学校是 learning 的机构，但学校 [更可能促成 depression，而不是充当 remedy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/I_became_so_depressed_that_I_stopped_going_to_school "I became so depressed that I stopped going to school")。没有 learning 的自由，就很难实现 good learning。要让 learning 成为 pleasurable 的，它需要由 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 来驱动。它不能是 coercive 或 mandatory 的。它必须是 free 的。

### Impact of memory on mood

Free learning 是有趣的，然而，learning 的 pleasure 并不是使 learning 成为对抗 depression 的强大武器的原因。

Memory 是一个可能触发或抑制 depression 的因素。Memories 决定了 input signals 在大脑中如何被 routed。Memory 决定了哪些 concepts 会与 inputs 或 neural activations 相关联。Memories 决定了我们如何对一辆驶过的汽车的声音做出反应。它可能唤起一个快乐假期的 memories、Elon Musk 的 inspiration，或者一场使亲人残疾的车祸的 memories。

要让 memories 对 mood 产生显著影响，我们需要很多 memories。与 psychotherapist 坐下来谈一次，并学习一些关于大脑、我们的生活或 coping strategies 的关键 facts，是不够的。在大脑中发展出健康的 tracks 需要数月乃至数年的 learning。我们可能建立起本质上 optimistic 或本质上 pessimistic 的 associations。我们需要数千个这样的 associations 才能改变平衡。然而，即使是数年的 learning，也可能轻易被一种 pathology 或 trauma 推翻。Neurohormones 可以瞬间改变大脑运作的模式。neurohormonal profile 的切换会瞬间偏好某个 memories 子集，从而可能以负面方式影响 mood。Trauma 可以植入 memories，从而激起新的 activation 来源，压过来自其他来源的 activation。换句话说，如果一次切换改变了正在使用的 tracks，或者如果大脑中诞生了新的 activation 来源，那么一大批 good memories 也可能毫无用处。用 learning 来缓解亲近之人的死亡几乎是不可能的。

一旦 depression 袭来，受影响的个体就面临双重打击。不仅 good memories 处于防守状态。Bad memories 开始四处盘旋，促进它们自己的新 tracks 并占据上风。大脑重新编程自身，并把 mood 的平衡摆向错误的方向。当这一过程失控时，我们可能就面对 clinical depression 了。更糟的是，depressed patients 会失去他们对生活的热爱和对 learning 的热爱。

Learning 能打破这个循环吗？这可能极其困难！尊重 [circadian cycle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle") 是恢复脱轨大脑的第一步。在 circadian cycle 中，需要抓住 peak creativity window 来尝试 remedial learning。Learning 需要是 prolific、intense、effective 且 pleasurable 的。[Incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") 会非常棒，要不是它那么困难的话。对于一个在该领域毫无技能的 depressed individual 来说，[SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo "SuperMemo") 并不是 remedy。已经太晚了。在糟糕的心境下试图掌握 incremental reading 只会让事情更糟。它可能导致对 incremental reading 的 hate。

如果 learning 是可能的，它可以充当一个 refuge，这可能有助于抑制 negative memories 并建立新的 connections。从那时起，建立 knowledge 的新 tendrils 的过程就可能开始。这个应当把心智带向对世界更 optimistic 的 interpretation 的过程是缓慢而费力的。在最严重的 cases 中，它可能需要数月或数年的 hard work，而且结果没有保证。

最终的结论是，learning 并不是 panacea，然而，它可以在 therapy 中扮演重要角色。最重要的是，depression 的风险可以提前数年通过丰富而有效的 learning 来避免。那种 learning 必须在自由和尊重 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的条件下进行。简而言之，**love of learning 是通往 love of life 的一条好路。**

### Anti-depressants

我是一个医学上的 Luddite。为了一个健康的身体，我坚持这条规则“_if it ain't broke, don't fix it_”。我避免一切形式的 pharmacological intervention。我相信 [homeostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis) 的力量以及 homeostatic intervention 的危险。我使用的最强的 drugs 是 coffee 和 beer。我甚至不用 aspirin。最让我感到沮丧的是 antibiotics、painkillers、sleeping pills 和 anti-depressants 的滥用。我已经有几十年没有服用过 antibiotic 了。久到已经忘了。如果必要，我会在临终时用一次。所有 drugs 都有它们正当的用途，anti-depressants 也是如此。由于它们会导致 receptor downregulation，一旦服用，它们就会让 neurotransmitter status quo 变得更糟。这通常意味着，这种 drug 服用得越多，就越需要服用它以避免 setback。然而，在 clinical depression 的严重病例中，这些 drugs 可能会阻止那个 runaway process。它们可能会保护 brain 免于 self-injury。一旦一个 depressed patient 开始失去 brain cells，恢复之路就变得漫长而崎岖。anti-depressant therapy 开始的那一刻，如果它有效，就是利用 learning 作为 therapy 的最佳时刻。只要 brain 愿意继续，learning 就能启动那些纤细的 knowledge 卷须，它们会钩住 reality，从而产生残留的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")。在理想情况下，一旦这些 drugs 被停用，那个 learn drive 应当存活下来，开始一个与 depression 相反的过程：learning、creativity、good sleep 和 good mood 的 positive feedback。这并不容易，但非常重要。如果 drug therapy 是一个 patient 生活中唯一改变的东西，它只会作为 pathological process 中的一次中断而起作用。它不会让 brain 处于比问题开始之前更好的状态。改善需要主动的努力。没有一个健康的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，positive memories 的积累就不会开始。

### Learn drive and optimism

Toddlers 似乎表现出最旺盛的 learn drive。难怪，健康的 children 生来就是 optimistic 的。optimism 与 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 之间存在相关性。Happy mind 可能在 neurochemical 基础上充当 learn drive 的 energizer。Pessimism 肯定会充当一种 suppressant 或 filter，阻止 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的表达。在这个意义上，pessimistic mind 可能掩盖 learn drive。在 depression 中，learn drive 可能完全消失。难怪 [Dr Robert Sapolsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Sapolsky) 把 depression 称为世界上最糟糕的疾病。

似乎正在形成一种共识：schools 是 teenagers（以及 later in life）中 depression 的一个主要促成因素。其 mechanism 尚不清楚，但 [learned helplessness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learned_helplessness "Learned helplessness") 和对 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 suppression 作为这种 pathology 的可能关键因素浮现出来。

### Can learning help you?

如果你正在读这个，并且你不确定 learning 能否帮助你，那就问自己这个问题：_Are you in a good mood today?_ 如上所述，当你处于 downswing 并寻找解决方案时，你的 interpretations 会更阴暗，你可能不会觉得这段文字足够令人安慰。那么请记住 **activation energy** 这个概念：你需要迈出小小的第一步来开始，然后你可能会被一个有趣事物的 vortex 拉进去学习。

如果你今天没有心情搞 quantum mechanics，那就从琐碎的 celebrity news 或 sports news 开始。Lowly learning 总比没有 learning 好！或者，如果那些 trivia 让你更加 depressed，那就看看 [Susan Engel 这篇关于 learning 和 depression 的有趣文字](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Susan_Engel:_Learning_is_anti-depressant "Susan Engel: Learning is anti-depressant")。

最后但同样重要的是，[incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") 可能是你最好的“medication”。在一个管理良好的 reading process 中，你的 materials 会按 fun 和 meaning 很好地排定优先级。Fun learning 只需一键之遥。所需要的只是开始。理想情况下，仅仅一分钟后，你就应该沉浸在一个具有治疗作用的 _"flow"_ 中。

## Optimization of education: Global or Local?

把 pleasure 用作 education 中的 guiding light 有风险吗？

### Perfect model of education

在漫长的 schooling 岁月中，我们慢慢发展出一个关于完美 academic learning process 的想象模型，在其中我们 [set long-term goals](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Setting_goals_can_change_your_life "Setting goals can change your life")、遵循 [curriculum](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Curriculum "Curriculum")、添加重要的 knowledge 片段，并达到这样一个点：我们获得一个 college degree，在某个给定领域拥有坚如磐石的 knowledge，并得到在社会中高效运作所需的广泛 general knowledge 的支持。我们在 school system 中待得越久，就越难抽身出来，对这个模型持有客观的看法。矛盾的是，对那些在学校表现良好并开始相信自己正是凭借那个完美的 academic learning 模型才取得成功的人来说，对这个模型的验证反而最难。Smart people 在学校遭受的痛苦较少，因此，对 school system 的问题想得也较少。Successful students 将这个模型内化，并通过为未来世代提供同样固定的路径来使其永续。

**我们通过 [curriculum](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Curriculum "Curriculum") 来设计 student 的 knowledge 的那个模型是错的！** 完美 school 的模型把功劳归于 system 和 teachers，而所有实际的 learning 都应归功于 student。当 kids 大批地 fail school 时，我们倾向于责怪 kids 或他们的 parents，而一小部分 successful students 会继续为他们自己的孩子梦想着那个完美的 school 模型，并不断把这个模型强加给那些不那么幸运的人。

### 基于 learn drive 的 Optimization

与 curriculum 不同，[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 背后的 optimization mechanism 在人类 evolution 的过程中已经被 perfected。它能够把个人的 knowledge 驱动到 disentangle 科学或工程中所有 complexities 所需的水平。在 compulsory schooling 到来之前，人类已经取得了启动 [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) 或 [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) 所需的一切可以想象的 breakthroughs。[Compulsory schooling](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Compulsory_schooling "Compulsory schooling") 最初帮助把“unenlightened”的 masses 提升到了一个新的水平，然而，它正日益把自己驱入一个 optimization corner，在其中 enlightenment 被对 creative minds 的 suppression 所取代。

### 设计一个孩子的 mind

我经常从受过高等教育且非常聪明的人那里听到这种说法：education 太重要了，不能让它依赖 self-learning 或依赖 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 blindness。显然，education 如此重要，以至于我们应该用最好的科学工具、借助最好的 experts 来 globally 地 plan 它并 design 它。当我专注于 efficient learning 时，在我真正开始思考 education system 之前，我也怀着同样的 conviction。默认听从 expert opinion 是相当自然的。

受过高等教育的人常常说出以下 claims：

-   _children 没有能力进行 long-term planning，因此需要 curriculum_
-   _[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 是一种 local optimization，而我们需要 globally 地 plan education_
-   _遵循 student interests 是灾难的配方：他们最终都会沉浸在令人头脑麻木的 [videogames](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Videogames "Videogames") 中_

问题在于，education 的 global optimization 设定的 performance targets 越来越紧。Global optimization 不断采用同样低效的 learning tools，试图把更多“necessary”的 knowledge 传输到 student minds 中。结果是数百万学生的 misery。虽然 Stalin 为了 Soviet Union 的 massive achievements 而 globally 地 optimized，但正是凭借其简单 optimization algorithms 的 market economics 把西方世界提升到了新的高度。参见：[Modern schooling is like Soviet economy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Modern_schooling_is_like_Soviet_economy "Modern schooling is like Soviet economy")

当前采用的 education 的 optimization 把 knowledge tests 当作 performance 的 measure，却依赖 cramming 和 short-term memory 来在更短的时间内取得更多成果。结果，它不断失去对 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 grip。国家之间的 competition 也采用 performance tests。我们不是为实际的 long-term knowledge 而 optimize，而是为 student heads 中 knowledge turnover 的速度而 optimize。结果是 unhappy students，其 knowledge 相对于投入的时间和实际的人类 potential 而言微不足道。

### 依赖 emergence

Education 的 optimization 可以采用 [emergence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Emergence "Emergence") 这一 concept。[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 是一种 mechanism，通过它，knowledge 在没有任何 [effort from teachers](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Push_zone "Push zone")、也没有来自孩子的 pain 的情况下 self-organizing。Natural learning 可能花费很长时间，但它是 pleasurable 的，而且健康的孩子不介意整天 learning，只要这是他们自己选择的 learning。

关于基于 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 learning 的 local optimization，有两个 vital facts 我们应该牢记在心：

-   如果不依赖 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，就没有 good learning。所有 override 的尝试都会被 human memory 大规模地 rejected
-   [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 带来对 learning process 的惊人高效的 long-term optimization。1850 年代之前几乎所有的 human achievement 都是在 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 guidance 下完成的

一个 skeptic 会注意到，自 compulsory schooling 引入以来，human progress 已经加速了。他会是对的。然而，自 40 亿年前最早的生命形式出现以来，我们就一直处在 progress 的加速上升之中。我认为 Guttenberg 和 [Tim Berners-Lee](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee") 对这种加速的贡献，比可敬的 [Johann Julius Hecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Julius_Hecker) 更为重要。

基于 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 local optimization 是高度 unintuitive 的。Creation science 来自对 natural selection 的 mechanism 的类似 unintuitive feelings。基于 random mutations 的 local evolutionary optimization 怎么可能产生 human being 这样的 marvel？由上帝之手进行的 global design/optimization/guidance 似乎不可避免。今天，subscribe 到 creation science 的人更少了，然而，绝大多数人口完全不知道 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 背后是什么 mechanism，也不知道为什么忽视它是 [Prussian education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_education_system) 的首要问题。

### 树的 metaphor

只要有足够的时间和接触 knowledge-rich environments 的机会，无需 education system，个人的 knowledge 就会成长为一个庞大、全面且 [coherent body](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence")。这对所有自由且健康的个人都成立。这棵树的 size 和 quality 可能取决于一个人的 personality、interests 以及 intellectual development 的 starting point。然而，education 的主要 myths 之一是，knowledge 的 organic growth 会导致多重 biases 和 [areas of ignorance](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Ban_on_homeschooling "Ban on homeschooling")。据称，这些 blank spots 比多年 schooling 之后留下的那些更大。由于 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 computational power 以及 [emergence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Emergence "Emergence") 的 phenomenon，事实恰恰相反。我喜欢用来解释 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的力量的 metaphor 是树生长的 metaphor。

另一个能帮助解释在构建 [coherent](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Emergence "Coherence") knowledge 过程中的 [emergence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Emergence") 的隐喻是 [Knowledge crystallization metaphor](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_crystallization "Knowledge crystallization")：

### Local optimization

[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 Local optimization 会带来 human ability 与 individual's environment and goals 之间的完美匹配（见：[Optimality of the learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Optimality_of_the_learn_drive "Optimality of the learn drive")）。schooling 的 Global optimization 会 suppress [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，在将 individuals 与他们的 jobs 匹配时 defer to 被 suppressed 的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")，并导致一个不快乐的社会，其中大多数 individuals 为了自己的 comfort 而渴望 9-5 jobs，而 leadership、learning 和 responsibility 都被 delegate 给别人。相反的情况发生在 [democratic schools](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Democratic_schools "Democratic schools") 中，它们 rely on self-learning 来培养 self-determined、self-fulfilled 和 self-reliant 的 individuals，准备好接受他们所选 area of interest 中的任何 challenge。

在他那场 historic commencement speech 中，Steve Jobs 开玩笑说，在被诊断出 cancer 之前，他并不知道 pancreas 是什么。显然，他盲目的 learn drive 在他广博的 knowledge 中留下了一个 gap。即使这是真的，我也绝不会用 Steve Jobs 和他的 opus vitae 去交换 local optimization of learning 的几次失败。他那场 [inspiring speech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D1R-jKKp3NA) 的要点之一就是 follow 一个人的 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")。用他的话说，“[_the only way to do great work is to love what you do_](http://news.stanford.edu/2005/06/14/jobs-061505/)”。这个真理已被所有 wise people 重复了数千年。

### Is global optimization possible?

[Global optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_optimization) 为所有 input values 找到一个 optimum。**Global optimization of learning** 是在 department of education 的层面完成的，例如借助 [common core](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Common_core "Common core") 和 [standardized testing](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Standardized_testing "Standardized testing") 这样的 tools。Global optimization 基于一种有缺陷的 reasoning，即我们可以 design 一个 child's mind。Global optimization 也可以由那些试图 predict 一个 child's future 的 parents 来完成。

我们能提前 determine 一个 child's future 吗？如果 parents 要 globally and optimally 地 choose future，我们就会有过剩的 lawyers 和 doctors。我们还会看到 frustrated college dropouts 大幅增加。如果 governments 稍微帮点忙，并在 early age 就 optimally 地 redistribute 孩子们的 jobs，我们最终会得到 [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four) 的一个变体。很少有孩子会喜欢在 6 岁时发现他们注定要过 book-keeper 或 carpenter 的一生。Job selection 显然应该基于 love 和 passion，而不是 government decree。

那么也许应该允许孩子们 globally 地 optimize？那也不会奏效，我们最终会得到过剩的 rock musicians、professional videogamers 和 football players。

把这与通过 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 进行的 optimization 对比一下，后者几个世纪以来一直带来人类成就中最优秀的部分。

那么 [curriculum](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Curriculum "Curriculum") 是不是在通往 global optimum 的路上寻找一个 intermediary optimum 的尝试呢。Curriculum 作为什么值得知道的 guide 似乎是个好主意。当一个 kid 或 teacher 对 learning 失去 enthusiasm 时，他们可能会 consult 这个 curriculum。不过，如果 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 正处于 overdrive，为什么要 slow down 呢？有没有风险，这个 kid 将永远学不到 alcohol 的危险？这不太可能。另一方面，我不知道有哪个 curriculum 会教孩子们如何 employ [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading")。我可能有 bias，但我肯定会把这项 skill 放在 cram Kawalec 或 [Battle of Cedynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Cedynia) 的需要之前（例子取自于我自己的 curriculum）。今天我能 appreciate 已故的 Julian Kawalec。然而，由 communist authorities 强加的对他小说的 mandatory reading 对我来说是 school torture 的一个来源。你大概会想 Kawalec 是谁。我很想告诉你，但 Wikipedia 关于他成就的文章只有 [Polish only](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Kawalec)。

如果你用 curriculum 来 test student knowledge，很容易看出他们只 master 了那个 globally optimized plan 的一小部分。他们还会在此基础上加上大量他们通过 self-learning 获得的关于世界的自己的 knowledge。这导致了 good schooling 的 illusion。如果 curriculum 不是 obligatory 的，而 teachers 有更多 room 去 adapt，knowledge 的 volume 及其 coherence 都会增加。Coherence 和 speed 是 self-learning 的两个 hallmarks。更少的 kids 可能会 choose 去 solve quadratic equations，但他们会用他们认为对自己重要的其他 skills 把这个 space 填满许多倍。所有那些计划在 [STEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science,_technology,_engineering,_and_mathematics) 中发展 careers 的人，无论如何迟早都会接触到 quadratic equations。其余的人则会 fall back on 当前的 default，也就是 learn 这些 equations 然后很快 forget 它们。大多数人不知道如何 tackle quadratic equations。很少有人知道它们的 purpose。Curriculum 中的 equations 增添了 distress，以及本可以在快乐的心境中 opportunistically 高效获得的 knowledge 的 cost。

如果 global long-term optimization 是不可能的，那么以 curriculum plan 形式出现的 intermediate steps 只是不那么复杂而已。它们仍然偏离了由 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 所决定的 optimum。

唯一能高效 optimize 的方式，就是让 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 在来自 parents、mentors、peers、strangers、social media、wikipedia、Google 等等的 gentle nudges 下 determine 轨迹。Optimization of education 必须遵循 **[fundamental law of learning](#fundamental_law_of_learning)**（见下）。

## Fundamental law of learning

大多数人都知道 learning 可以是 pleasurable 的。然而，很少有人体会到这一事实对于 [future of education](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Education_reform "Education reform") 有多么重要。

只有 neuroscience 中源源不断的珍贵发现，才帮助我们看清 pleasure 在 learning 中的根本重要性。reward process 始于 [perception](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Biederman_model "Biederman model") 这一层级，并经由 associative learning，推进到 [creativity](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle")，再到 [problem solving](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/How_to_solve_any_problem%3F "How to solve any problem?")，直至 achieving goals 的终极 pleasure。在每一站，都有 pleasure signals 来 reward brainwork 的进展。

我理解 pleasure 的力量也曾经很慢。早在 1991 年，[我们](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo_World "SuperMemo World")就保守地写道：_"有一种可靠的方法可以判断某个 student 是否会在他的工作中取得成功。如果他在 long-lasting learning sessions 中找到 pleasure，他必定会做出 terrific job"_（见：[SuperMemo Decalog](http://www.super-memory.com/articles/decalog.htm)）。今天，我们意识到，pleasure 与 neural networks 中所有形式的 learning 如此内在地相关联，以至于它成为衡量 learning progress 的最佳标尺之一。

这使得我们能够表述 **fundamental law of declarative learning**：

**当没有 pleasure 时，就没有 good learning。**

当然，这条 law 需要加以限定才能精确。Good declarative learning 会产生 pleasure。这一事实可能被一些因素所掩盖，例如一点 good learning 可能隐藏在 [a mass of bad learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Unpleasant_learning_at_school "Unpleasant learning at school") 之中。Pleasure 本身并不保证 learning。我们发现的事实可能令人痛苦。有些 declarative learning 可能在 displeasure 的条件下发生（例如 fear conditioning）。Classical conditioning 常常涉及 pain。Clinical depression 会妨碍一个人去骑车的意愿，但不会破坏骑车时发生的 procedural learning。

The fundamental law of declarative learning 只是指出，获得满足 [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") 的 quality knowledge 会产生 reward signal。该 signal 的缺失表明 learning 的缺失。Dry facts 可以在没有乐趣的情况下短期地存入 declarative memory，但如果没有来自 learning 的 reward，那些 facts 就不会附着于稳固的 reality models。那些 facts 很可能被健康的 [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting_curve "Forgetting curve") 系统迅速从 memory 中清除。更糟的是，[bad and persistent engrams can cause problems with learning later in life](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Toxic_memory "Toxic memory")！任何 coherent model 在 memory 中的出现都必然会产生 reward signal。

如果你碰巧是自己把 suffering 强加于自己，你需要重新思考你的 strategies。你可能需要放慢速度，或者回到 basics，学习 mental 和 [sleep hygiene](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle") 的规则，管理你的 [stress](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Stress_resilience "Stress resilience")，学习 [20 rules of formulating knowledge](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/20_rules "20 rules")，或者也许尝试一下 [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading")。如果你不顾 pain 而坚持，你不会得到 good results 的回报。[Gladwell's 10,000 hour rule](http://www.newyorker.com/news/sporting-scene/complexity-and-the-ten-thousand-hour-rule) 也需要加以限定。没有哪位 violin virtuoso 是仅仅通过数小时练习的 suffering 中诞生的。就像 learning 一样，great music 是 love 的孩子。

另一方面，这个世界上大多数 students 的 suffering 并非他们自己的过错。Bad learning 是被从上面强加给他们的！

**Students of the world unite！** 你不再需要忍受 learning 的 pain。如果你 suffering，你有你作为 student 的基本权利去 protest。如果你 suffering，那就是有什么地方出错了！你可以停止 learning！如果有人向你要求 learning，而你并不享受它，你可以反击，并要求 pleasurable learning！这不是你那种 elitist hedonistic weak heart 的要求。这是 reason 的要求。**No pleasure, no learning！** 你的 suffering 是浪费时间、浪费健康、浪费人类的 global resources！见：[Declaration of Educational Emancipation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Declaration_of_Educational_Emancipation "Declaration of Educational Emancipation")

## The main problem in education

The main problem with regards to education is the belief that learning may cause displeasure, and that this displeasure should be [endured](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/The_grind_is_the_glory "The grind is the glory") to achieve more learning.

There are countless educators who believe that school should be like work: it is unpleasant but it just needs to be done. In this chapter, I will explain that the opposite is true:

**Good learning is inherently pleasurable**, and without pleasure there is no good learning.

The displeasure myth is so prevalent that even good teachers believe that pain is part of learning.

In this chapter, I show that the pleasure of learning is wired into the brain, and how we systematically destroy this [gift of evolution](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Education_counteracts_evolution "Education counteracts evolution") at the cost of mankind's health, learning, creativity, and ultimately future.

The main problem of education is also one of the main problems of society. By destroying the pleasure of learning we are contributing powerfully to the destruction of the pleasure of living. We have built an education system that sets millions of people up for a life of unhappiness.

Chances are you are skeptical of my words, as the myth of unpleasant learning is a potent side effect of [schooling](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Schooling "Schooling"). Therefore this chapter is an attempt to convince you. And all that is necessary to abolish this myth is an understanding of the simple mechanism by which new knowledge is encoded in the brain.

## Learn drive and entropy

The concept of entropy is helpful in understanding why most kids do not learn much at school.

You may recall from your physics class that entropy is a measure of disorder, and that the second law of thermodynamics states that the entropy of an isolated system never decreases. This is the type of sexy law of physics that we tend to remember for life. It is [universally applicable](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Applicability "Applicability").

There is a sister concept in information theory called [Shannon entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(information_theory\)). It can be understood as the average value of information transmitted by a source. For example, take a channel that is continually transmitting a string of identical letters into infinity (e.g. a string of As: "AAAAAA..."). It is entirely predictable and carries an entropy of zero. We do not learn from such a channel.

[Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon) proposed the concept of information entropy in 1948. Soon after, scientists were hypothesizing as to whether the entropy of an information channel may have a powerful impact on how the brain perceives the value of the channel. In 1957, [Meyer hypothesized](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Music_and_entropy "Music and entropy") that the entropy of music determines the perception of its beauty. He concluded that a higher entropy may result in subjective tension, which correlates with more meaningful musical moments.

Meyer's thinking was [later refined](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Impact_of_syncopation_on_the_pleasure_of_music "Impact of syncopation on the pleasure of music") to better understand the perception of music and information in general. There is more to music than just information. This is visible through the phenomena of a song being entertaining and fun for many playbacks. But this is rarely the case with books.

Music is a universal message. If you were given a choice of a radio channel, you would quickly tune out from noisy static and you would also not be too excited about zero entropy silence. However, most people will respond positively to a regular beat of a drum. As long as it wasn't being drummed on broken glass, which we are wired to dislike, we would find a radio channel with a regular drumbeat more interesting than a silent one. This will naturally last only for a while until the drumbeat itself becomes boring and too predictable.

Today, we can finally test the response of the brain to information entropy. Neuroimaging shows that the [anterior hippocampus responds to the entropy of a visual stream](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15896570), and similar findings have been confirmed for the [ventral striatum](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3403290/). Therefore we are now certain that the brain responds to information entropy. The entropy sensor is important in scanning the environment for learning opportunities. This is the prelude to the reward that underlies the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive").

## Prior knowledge in information seeking

We need to distinguish between information and meaning. Entropy is not a good measure of the latter. The measure of meaning must involve the brain itself in addition to the information channel metric. Prior knowledge is essential in learning. Imagine that in your search for an interesting channel on the radio you find a news service. If the service is delivered in Thai and you do not speak Thai, you will prefer a service delivered in English. In information sense, news channels may have the same entropy, yet your prior knowledge will make you opt for the English channel. While the Thai channel delivers a stream of sounds, the English channel delivers a stream of [concepts](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Abstract_knowledge "Abstract knowledge"). Without understanding the knowledge of the recipient, information entropy tells us little. We cannot determine a signal-to-noise ratio.

Every listener will have his or her own preferred level of information entropy. For most music lovers, the regular beat of a disco or techno will be somewhat more interesting than the isolated beat of a drum. This type of music carries a higher average level of information. For a more sophisticated listener a bit of syncopation will be welcome. However, syncopation requires a degree of prior learning. Those with lesser knowledge of music may get confused with increased rhythmic complexity. If there is too much information in the beat it [may no longer be possible to dance to the music](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Impact_of_syncopation_on_the_pleasure_of_music "Impact of syncopation on the pleasure of music"). To an average ear, the genius of Wynton Marsalis may be hard to perceive. Top shelf jazz music is reserved for only a small fraction of highly educated listeners, as for most of the population, as the complexity increases, the music slowly disintegrates into the direction of radio static.

## Entropy detectors in the brain

The brain cannot effectively detect the entropy of the signal hitting the retina or the eardrum. Like pixels of a monitor, retinal cells are not aware of what they display. If the detector, such as the hippocampus, is to light up in response to entropy, it must operate on the inputs from the entorhinal cortex (i.e. the input to the hippocampus itself). Those inputs will present the signal after a high degree of processing. Instead of pixels, it may present a concept. A high entropy signal at the sensory inputs will lose most of its noise component early in the process of neural selection, completion, and [generalization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization"). The signal-to-noise ratio will determine how much information is lost. The bigger the noise, the bigger the loss. The smarter we are, the more selective this processing will be and the more information will be lost at that stage. That's good. We become blind to detail. Pattern recognition will act like a deterministic function, which by definition, results in a drop in entropy. Complex patterns may become simple concepts. Those concepts will provide the actual input to the detector, e.g. the hippocampus.

Note that the visual stream produced in experiments that prove the [response of the hippocampus to signal entropy](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15896570) has a highly [symbolic nature](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Abstract_knowledge "Abstract knowledge"). As such, the stream will lose far less information in processing. That highly simplified and [conceptualized](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization") message will be scanned for surprisal and provide guidance to the entire [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") system. This is why, in this case, the hippocampus appears to be responding to input entropy.

The above reasoning explains why both low and high entropy sensory signals can be uninteresting. After a degree of processing, a high entropy signal may lose all its noise and deliver a low entropy input to the hippocampus. We then observe the illusion of an "optimum entropy" level at sensory input. We need a new concept, **[learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy")**, that will help us accurately determine the attractiveness of the signal. Learntropy needs to take into account the high degree of processing of information before it can activate reward centers in the brain. Learntropy is discussed [later](#Learntropy) in this text.

## Speed of information processing

An under-appreciated factor in sensory information scanning is the speed of information processing in the brain.

For every piece of music, there is a tolerable playback range where the beauty of the music is appreciated. A high speed playback can be annoying and the music may become hard to decode, as the high speed goes beyond our processing power. The same piece of music slowed down can quickly lose its appeal. The same happens in speech delivery or in classroom lecturing. For the same information and the same entropy level, we may accomplish highly different levels of signal attractiveness. There is always an optimum speed of delivery and that speed depends on all other factors that power the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), incl. prior knowledge. As such, speed of delivery is highly individual.

I like to listen to lectures at 1.4x speed. I use 1.3x for more ambitious pieces. I never speed up [Fareed Zakaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fareed_Zakaria_GPS) though, but rather relish every piece of information in this show. Students in a classroom lecture do not have a speed-up or slow-down button. Even the pause button, if available, is hard to hit as it may annoy other students.

In schools, all too often, the speed of delivery surpasses student's processing capacity. This results in negligible learning and high stress. There is no time to [enjoy the landscapes in the window of a high-speed train](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Futility_of_schooling "Futility of schooling"). At MIT they call it _"drinking from a firehose"_.

## Probability vs. knowledge

Low probability events carry more information. Average information determines entropy. Prior knowledge determines the perception of an information channel's entropy.

If you happen to tune in to radio news and you hear that "_Janet Jackson has delivered a baby_", your degree of interest will depend on the probability of the event. If you have no idea who Janet Jackson is, this is a high probability event. If some [350,000 women deliver babies every single day](http://www.theworldcounts.com/stories/How-Many-Babies-Are-Born-Each-Day), this is no longer news and is not new or interesting. The first death of a soldier in a war makes news, but when deaths incrase into the thousands, young lives become just a statistic.

If you happen to know Janet Jackson or like her music, the probability of a baby delivery drops dramatically to the level of "_once in a lifetime_" (for Janet). This can make you become interested. However, if you recall Janet as a beautiful girl from some ancient sitcom, her baby delivery may go into the category of "_Impossible!_". If you realize Janet is 50 years old, and you know about menopause, you may instantly become morbidly curious about her case. Your prior knowledge determines how you respond to the message. There is no optimum entropy level for a channel. There is only an optimum entropy level that fits a specific brain. At this point you may see that we need to introduce a new derived concept, which we will [later](#Learntropy) call **learntropy**. Learntropy will determine the attractiveness of a given channel for a given brain.

If you love Janet-like gossip, the channel rich in that gossip will provide the right level of surprisal for you. It will provide the learntropy match. If you lack knowledge or your priorities differ, you will tune out. Your learning priorities will also determine your level of knowledge in particular areas and your response to any particular channel and its information entropy.

## Predictability and surprisal

Probability and complexity are not the only components in information perception. We seem to look for a balance between predictability and surprise. I like funk. In this type of music, the bassline is often highly predictable with the [optimum dose of syncopation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Impact_of_syncopation_on_the_pleasure_of_music "Impact of syncopation on the pleasure of music"). It makes it easy to synchronize the body motion with the rhythm. However, funk would not be interesting if it did not carry surprise. This is where the sophisticated jazz riffs tickle the neural system responsible for the detection of surprisal. In addition, after decades of learning, there is a whole database of signals that my brain responds to. There may be that one backup singer voice that I recognize and like. My brain is ready for funk.

I love Ken Robinson lectures on creativity. In one way, they are highly predictable. I totally agree with Robinson, so you can say that Robinson feeds my confirmation bias. This is pleasurable. When people agree with us, we like to say "_great minds think alike_". But if Robinson just kept repeating the same dry mantras on how schools kill creativity, he would lose his appeal. Entropy can be interpreted as the average expected [surprisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-information). Robinson's delivery carries a great deal of nice surprises. He may paint the same models in a different and unusually creative way. As a result, **the brain receives new information, produces a generalization, and confirms the existing models**. Generalizations derived from new contexts increase [knowledge coherence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_coherence "Knowledge coherence"). This a very pleasing type of complementarity in a message based on a known model.

Robinson lectures find a good balance between predictability and surprise.

The most pleasing information channels will keep delivering surprises that confirm existing models and arm them in new semantic twigs on which new knowledge can be built. A surprise that destroys existing models may not be pleasing at first, but may lead to a highly pleasing revolution in thinking.

Metaphorically, you can imagine this as the information channel massaging your tree of knowledge and adding new branches like a potter who adds [new layers of clay](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Jigsaw_puzzle_metaphor "Jigsaw puzzle metaphor") to his perfectly shaped creation.

## Detecting surprisal

Human [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is based on detecting [surprisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-information). We have known that for ages. All models of human and machine learning involve that concept under different names. [Piaget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Piaget) wrote about [schemata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Piaget#Schema) that fall into disequilibrium under the impact of surprisal. In his models of the neocortex, [Jeff Hawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Hawkins) speaks of prediction errors that underlie learning and intelligence. I like to speak of [models](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Model "Model"), their elaboration (when new information fits the model), contradiction (when new information requires changes to the model), and [generalization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization") (when [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting "Forgetting") and [memory optimization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Memory_optimization "Memory optimization") sculpt out new quality from the model).

For the reward of learning, a new surprising piece of information needs to fit pre-existing knowledge ([models](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Model "Model"), schemata, predictions, or so). For the reward to be delivered, neural processing is necessary. Information on the input needs to be processed, and compared with information stored in the brain. One of the chief processors of input information in the brain is the hippocampus. It is the brain's information switchboard that is able to compare the input with prior knowledge.

Measuring the entropy of the visual stream is not necessarily a reliable indicator of the pleasing power of the information channel. All information streamed to the hippocampus undergoes a high degree of processing. A stream of pixels representing a beautiful beach will be processed into a series of shapes and textures. Those in turn will model palms, sand, and the sea. This highly compressed simple information will determine the original response to the information input.

Scanning for information in the environment is equivalent to scanning for scents of food. The scent is enticing, but only the actual feeding is a true reward. This is why entropy scanning does not need to be rewarding. All it needs to do is to lead to a reward. The anterior hippocampus responds to entropy, as noticed earlier, however experimental design made sure that the entropy refers to the combination of simple shapes that do not lose much information during input processing. Instead of speaking of signal entropy, we should rather focus on the input entropy at the information comparator such as the hippocampus. It is not the retinal pixels that matter, but the shape of the palm as represented on the comparator input. For the comparator, the high entropy pattern of grayness or static noise will not differ from whiteness or silence. They will all bring the same entropy on input: zero. This is why I used the term [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") to accurately refer to the attractiveness of the information channel.

The anterior hippocampus that responds to signal entropy is famous for the discovery of the Halle Berry neuron (see [more](http://phys.org/news/2005-06-single-cell-recognition-halle-berry-neuron.html)). Using electrodes implanted in a consenting epilepsy patient, researchers were able to pinpoint a single neuron consistently responding to images of Halle Berry in various contexts. The same neuron would also respond to Halle Berry's name. At the same time, posterior hippocampus might respond less consistently to Jennifer Aniston (perhaps an indication of a preceding layer of neural processing).

Most of us have no idea how Halle Berry smells and her smell might not be unique enough to activate Halle Berry neuron in the hippocampus, however, even the smell signal can get there fast via just a few synapses in the olfactory bulb, olfactory tubercle, piniform cortex, and the entorhinal cortex (see picture). However, if one could hear the sound of Halle's voice, it might meet the sound signal in the olfactory tubercle, contribute to recognition, and result in the subsequent activation of the Halle neuron in the hippocampus or further down in the neocortex.

[![Olfactory system anatomy](https://supermemo.guru/images/thumb/0/04/Olfactory_system_anatomy.jpg/692px-Olfactory_system_anatomy.jpg)](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/File:Olfactory_system_anatomy.jpg "Olfactory system anatomy")

Olfactory system anatomy

> _**Figure:** Olfactory system anatomy. The smell signal can get to the hippocampus fast via just a few synapses in the olfactory bulb, olfactory tubercle, piniform cortex, and the entorhinal cortex. (source: Wikipedia)_

Does it all mean that Halle resides permanently in the patient's hippocampus? Due to the association of the hippocampus with formation of new memories, we may rather think that Halle shows up in hippocampal neurons as a result of the recognition. Her permanent place in the heart of the patient is likely situated further downstream in the neocortex. We now know that in the process of memory consolidation, knowledge engrams [move from the hippocampus to the neocortex](http://www.jneurosci.org/content/29/32/10087.full). We are also pretty sure that this process is happening [in sleep](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Memory_optimization_in_sleep "Memory optimization in sleep"). It is in the neocortex that we should look for concept neurons representing Halle or one's grandmother. This last possibility gave rise to a hypothetical type of neuron called **the [grandmother cell](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Grandmother_cell "Grandmother cell")**.

In monkeys, researchers could identify [grandmother cells](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Grandmother_cell "Grandmother cell") in the visual cortex that respond to faces. There we might find cells that more consistently fire up in contact with Halle's image. However, the concept of Halle might still reside elsewhere and be activated, among others, by visual cortex cells upon noticing Halle.

Another activation route might come from hearing Halle's name on the news. The entire recognition process would be orchestrated by the entorhinal cortex and the hippocampus while the ultimate Halle neuron would light up somewhere in the layers of the neocortex.

For information rich signal to generate a reward, there must be a low probability event detected on input and encoded via association as new knowledge in the cortex. Where anterior hippocampus would respond to the entropy, the [activity of the extensive bilateral thalamo-cortical network would be modulated by the surprise factor](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/15896570). There we shall search for the roots of the pleasure of learning. There are also other comparator centers that might be involved depending on the type of the message. The amygdala has also been found to likely produce rewards when detecting novel visual signals. The same amygdala neurons that respond to rewarding visual stimuli may respond to novel visual stimuli. [Rolls hypothesized that this may implement the reward of novelty via the amygdala](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Amygdala_may_be_involved_in_rewarding_novel_input "Amygdala may be involved in rewarding novel input").

We know that the hippocampus connects directly with the nucleus accumbens (the brain pleasure center). This connection might be used in two contexts:

1.  the anticipation of pleasure and
2.  the ultimate reward.

The anticipation would follow the detection of a high [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") signal and would result in active pursuit of high value messages. Detecting a message by the hippocampus might then simultaneously send associative learning messages to the neocortex and the reward signal to the pleasure center. That would spell the moment of learning something new!

## The wow factor

In the summer of 1977, while looking for extraterrestrial intelligence, SETI researchers discovered an unusual radio signal coming from [Sagittarius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagittarius_\(constellation\)). In the bland low-level noise of cosmic space the signal was highly unlikely. Low probability marks high surprisal. Astronomer Jerry Ehman circled 6 letters corresponding with the signal on a printout and mark it with "Wow!".

[![A scan of a color copy of the original computer printout, taken several years after the 1977 arrival of the Wow! signal](https://supermemo.guru/images/thumb/d/d3/Wow_signal.jpg/500px-Wow_signal.jpg)](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/File:Wow_signal.jpg "A scan of a color copy of the original computer printout, taken several years after the 1977 arrival of the Wow! signal")

> _**Figure:** A scan of a color copy of the original computer printout, taken several years after the 1977 arrival of the [Wow! signal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wow!_signal). (source: Wikipedia)_

"Wow!" is how the brain responds to a sudden discovery. The moment is highly pleasurable. The entire purpose of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is to look for wow factors in the environment. These are the most valuable nuggets of knowledge that complement what is currently known: the current model of reality. The pleasure of [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") comes from the condensed power of wows streamed into the student's brain.

Thus far, we have seen the impact of entropy, surprisal, predictability, and current knowledge on learning. In this case, the mere probability of the signal does not fully explain its power. It is the interpretation that stands behind it (see: [Knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network")). At the moment of making his note, Ehman could sense the enormity of its implications. This had been the most powerful evidence thus far and ever since for the existence of intelligence other than human intelligence. If the same signal represented detecting sardines in the ocean, there would be no "wow!". Not even in the Arctic.

The reliability of the information channel is important. If the error rate is high, the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") may weaken. When Penzias and Wilson discovered cosmic microwave background radiation in 1964, there was no "wow!". Perplexed researchers went on to remove pigeon droppings from their radio antenna. Pigeon droppings received a priority in their explanation of the mysterious noise. In 1978, for their discovery, Penzias and Wilson received a Nobel Prize.

When a scientist makes a discovery, he may exclaim "_Eureka!_" and punch the air. A neural network somewhere in his brain has produced a [generalization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization") that results in sending a reward signal. This propagates further and makes an old man jump around the lab like a child.

The same happens early in life. A toddler in an empty room will scan the environment for low probability components like colorful objects, new toys, etc. When a toddler experiments with a spoon dropping off the table, she is like a little scientist. However, when the brain makes a [generalization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Generalization "Generalization") "_all falling spoons make noise_", she is rewarded too. She may celebrate in the exactly same way as the happy scientist, independent of the age. A big smile is the first clear sign.

The same happy thing occurs to a lesser degree in all forms of learning controlled by the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). It does not matter if we learn about a celebrity or the chemical composition of a rock. Things are interesting because they reward the brain through the learn drive mechanism.

A creative process will also produce rewards. An association deemed useful is rewarding. An association that leads to a solution to a difficult problem is even more rewarding. Clearly there is a gradation of rewards. The system can quantify the probability of information, association, or a solution. The lower the probability, the higher the reward.

## Knowledge valuation network

### Knowledge valuations

All granular pieces of knowledge processed by the brain are instantly evaluated for their relevance, [coherence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence"), and value. We instantly know if information is understandable and [useful](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Applicability "Applicability"). We also often instantly notice when it is [inconsistent](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Consistency "Consistency"), [incoherent](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence") or irrelevant.

Unusual and surprising bits of knowledge are highly valued, however, the probability isn't the best reflection of value from the brain's point of view. There are highly unlikely events of low significance (e.g. asteroid strike in a remote planetary system), and likely events that change one's life (e.g. the answer to "_Will you marry me?_").

[Knowledge valuation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation "Knowledge valuation") relies primarily on the [applicability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Applicability "Applicability") of knowledge in achieving personal goals.

### The emotional brain and the rational brain

**Knowledge valuation network** is an evaluation system based on a resultant of emotional and rational valuations of knowledge. In literature, it may be referred to broadly as _neural valuation circuitry_, which is not necessarily knowledge-specific.

In the valuation network, emotional valuations will connect information with rewards in the primitive brain centers responsible for hunger, thirst, sex drive, etc. Rational valuations will be knowledge-based. An example of a pure emotional valuation comes from an answer to "_Where is the nearest fast food shop?_". Knowledge-based valuations may be more complex and highly networked, i.e. dependent on a network of subvaluations. Answer to "_Which book is best for my exam?_" is evaluated through one's goals that include passing an exam leading to getting a degree affecting one's job prospects, and contributing to lifetime goals. Emotional and rational valuations segregate anatomically. The emotional valuations come from what has metaphorically been described as older portions of the triune brain: reptilian and paleommamalian structures. For example, a specific stimulus processed by the thalamus may send separate signals to the amygdala for an emotional evaluation, and to the neocortex for a rational valuation. The emotional brain is philogenetically older. Personality and education determine if rational valuations can control or override emotional valuations.

### Decision tree in fast thinking

**Knowledge valuation network** is the network of memory connections that determine the value of an individual piece of knowledge. If learning is interpreted as a task, valuation network will determine the **perceived task value** (see: [Problem valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Problem_valuation_network "Problem valuation network")).

In computational terms, **knowledge valuation network** can be compared to a [decision tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision_tree). Goals and emotions determine core values at the root of the tree. Semantic connections between pieces of knowledge can be interpreted as fractional value transfer from goals to details. A well-organized [semantic network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Semantic_network "Semantic network") of well-consolidated and well-chosen knowledge will need mere milliseconds to make expert decisions. This is what Kahneman calls automatic [fast thinking](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Fast_thinking "Fast thinking") (if you are interested in tough problems that require _slow problem solving_, see _[How to solve any problem?](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/How_to_solve_any_problem%3F "How to solve any problem?")_). The same kind of processes, that underlie decision making or problem solving, participate in knowledge valuation. Like many expert decisions, the valuation is fast and it is often running with low participation of conscious intentionality. In short, we sometimes die to know things without fully being able to explain why. This process is hardly under our own control, let alone the control of the teacher at school. For efficient learning, valuations must be high.

### Valuation network in development

The development of the network will depend on the personality, lifetime experience, and the environment. Childhood trauma or personality characteristics, e.g. impulsivity, may increase chances of developing a criminal mindset. Some traumatic events in early life may favor developing biased networks based on single-minded obsessions (see: [falsity vector](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Falsity_vector "Falsity vector")). The environment and the available knowledge will determine passions, interests, goals, and network subvaluations (see: [conceptualization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Conceptualization "Conceptualization")).

All strategies that promote healthy brain development will also promote rich, highly individualized, and efficient valuation network. Those will underlie a sparkling [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). All educators agree that we want to help kids have a good grip on their emotional life and build smart, creative, and knowledgeable brains.

The chief problem of educational systems is a cookie-cutter approach in which all kids are fed the same knowledge in an industrial fashion with little respect to the key component of efficient learning: the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). Learn drive is a [perfect computational device](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Optimality_of_the_learn_drive "Optimality of the learn drive") that matches the current status of the [semantic network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Semantic_network "Semantic network") representing knowledge in the brain with current input produced by the **knowledge valuation network** in response to information available in the environment. If the kid insists that he must see that YouTube video, his own brain is the best authority. All interference will affect future independence and [creativity](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Creativity "Creativity").

While a lecturing teacher may spend 45 minutes to feed a child with a long string of symbols that produce low valuations, and negligible memories, the same kid, with access to Google, within 3-5 minutes, will identify pieces of information with high valuations, and easy coding for lifetime retention (for an opposing view see: [The morbid myth of Digital Dementia](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/The_morbid_myth_of_Digital_Dementia "The morbid myth of Digital Dementia")). For kids well trained in the process, the efficiency of knowledge acquisition may be an order of magnitude higher in [self-learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Self-learning "Self-learning"). When I say "order of magnitude", I am just being cautious and conservative. I do not want to run into accusations of hyperbole. I included a couple of examples of specific comparisons in this text elsewhere (e.g. [13 years of school in a month](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Schools_are_useless_in_teaching_English!#Inefficiency_of_schooling "Schools are useless in teaching English!") or [1600% acceleration of learning during vacation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learning_history:_school_vs._self-directed_learning#Self-directed_acceleration "Learning history: school vs. self-directed learning")).

Where I speak of golden nuggets of knowledge, Peter Thiel speaks of the [power law](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Thiel_on_power_law "Thiel on power law"): a small set of core skills honed to perfection can produce power returns.

**Small investments in learning** can produce **dramatic changes** to individual lives and to the entire planet!

### Knowledge valuation in the brain

The research into the actual anatomical implementation of the [knowledge valuation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation "Knowledge valuation") network in the brain is of paramount importance for the understanding of the human mind. It is essential for prevention of depression and addictions. Knowledge valuation underlies efficient learning, creativity, and problem solving.

Good learning is pleasurable. Rewards of food, sex, or drugs tend to saturate. Happy learning does not have this property. It is easy to avoid unhappy learning. This is done instinctively via the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). This is why learning is of supreme hedonic importance. It can literally lift societies to a new happier level.

#### Orbitofrontal cortex

The networked nature of [knowledge valuation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation "Knowledge valuation") is indicative of the use of cortical resources. Indeed, most of researchers seem to lean to the belief that the entire system of valuations might be [centered in the orbitofrontal cortex (OFC)](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Orbitofrontal_cortex_as_the_supreme_reward_processor "Orbitofrontal cortex as the supreme reward processor") with the level of abstraction increasing towards anterior areas. There are many models and hypotheses on how individual subsystems affect valuations (e.g. common currency, common scaling, somatic marker, appraisal-by-content, multiple component, cognitive-motivational interface, parallel appraisal, locationist vs. constructionist models, etc.). In the **common currency model**, all valuations from all sub-systems (hedonic substrates) are integrated and provide the ultimate signal of "wanting" or "liking". For example, (1) knowledge-based valuations from medial OFC (mOFC) might combine with (2) reward anticipation from the nucleus accumbens (NA), and (3) food appraisal messages from the insula to affect decision-making in the choice of a restaurant for the next meal.

#### Common currency model

OFC is a fantastic research area due to the convergence of many lines of human interests: drug addictions, ahedonia, [learned helplessness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learned_helplessness "Learned helplessness"), obsessive compulsive disorder, etc. The **common currency model** seems to indicate that the high associated with explosive creativity or explosive [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is neurochemically and neuroanatomically comparable to the high produced by low doses of cocaine.

There is a lively dispute on whether all rewards are translated into a reward signal that converges on the same type of neurons, or if they retain the origin of their character. I think the discussion is redundant as specificity can be conferred by individual [concept map](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_map "Concept map") activations, while the ultimate valuation generated by a single output can constitute the common currency. In all valuations, we need to have a convergence due to the existence of a single answer corresponding with a single concept map activation. [Some OFC neurons](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Value-encoding_neurons_in_the_orbitofrontal_cortex "Value-encoding neurons in the orbitofrontal cortex") seem to specifically encode high-level value.

In [knowledge valuation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation "Knowledge valuation") and in decision-making, we need a single boss. Redundancy can be used to restore the valuation system, but there is no escape from a [concept neuron](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_neuron "Concept neuron") decision. We cannot have two decision makers that would make a hand with a fork stab an itching eye during a dinner even though competing neural forces make such a scenario possible due to a computing error.

#### Emergence of knowledge valuations

Building up the valuation network may occur via the interaction of individual [concept maps](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_map "Concept map"). For example, if the exam concept is valued because of the job prospects [concept](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept "Concept"), they may be coactivated, and the valuation of the employment concept may confer a valuation on the exam-related [concept map](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_map "Concept map"). The degree of the activation and the associated concept valuations may determine the ultimate appraisal. Myelin concentration may increase in pathways targeting the ventral striatum, which may be one of the ways to explain how the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") can be boosted with learning (or suppressed with [coercive learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coercive_learning "Coercive learning") a school). The role of the orbitofrontal cortex in determining valuations might be similar to the role of the hippocampus in establishing long-term memories. Those highly connected regions of the cortex may play a role of a switchboard that connect areas of interest only to relinquish the role of a matchmaker while the linked concept maps (or centers) develop their own wiring for fast connectivity (e.g. in sleep). With new wiring, highly valued concept might affect pleasure centers with no mediation from the OFC. This way, some [concept cells](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_cell "Concept cell") (e.g. associated with one's favorite actor) could generate pleasant valuations by sheer solo activation.

#### Harms of reversal learning

In case of a negative school conditioning, we may associate irrelevant contexts (e.g. colors of [items](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Item "Item") in [SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo "SuperMemo")) with low valuations. In this scenario, the concept of a white item, or the coactivation of the concept of item and color, will suppress valuation by providing a strong negative input. Outwardly it looks like a cut-off signal that blocks valuations (perhaps in the lateral OFC). In such contexts, association of concepts would still be possible, and short-term retrieval might be likely, however, low valuation would prevent consolidation of memory (e.g. by blocking the transfer to long-term cortical storage)(see: [How school turns off memory](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/How_school_turns_off_memory "How school turns off memory")). Reprogramming reward (e.g. swapping template color in SuperMemo) could occur in reversal learning. We know that animals with OFC injury are impaired at reversal learning (Mishkin 1972), which adds to the evidence for the anatomical location of the supreme valuation networks. If we keep overriding valuation signals, we might end up with the [war of the networks](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/War_of_the_networks "War of the networks"), which is my hypothetical claim on the origins of [learned helplessness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learned_helplessness "Learned helplessness") induced by schooling. School coercion might be seen as a form of perpetual reversal learning that will wear on network plasticity leading to long-term adverse effects on the ability to evaluate rewards in decision-making. In that light, human memory might be seen as an EPROM with limited number of erase cycles. If long-term learning is seen as a buildup of the synaptic substrate that is then pruned in the process of [stabilization](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Stability "Stability") (which in turn reduces synaptogenesis), reversal learning might lead to an unresponsive system in which learning is no longer possible.

Endless fake rewards and micro-penalties may turn off knowledge valuations and undermine long-term learning at school

#### Goals vs. habits

The **knowledge valuation network** is central in healthy [free learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Free_learning "Free learning"). In contrast, [passive schooling](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Passive_schooling "Passive schooling") leads to [learned helplessness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learned_helplessness "Learned helplessness"). Coercion converts goal-oriented behaviors into the acceptance of passive habits (as opposed to healthy habits honed in the pursuit of goals). The output from the knowledge valuation network is suppressed by lower valuations in the system (i.e. lower activation of concept maps of interest). This naturally leads to a less joyful state of mind. When [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") withers, when curiosity dies, life becomes a series of habits executed with little reward (see: [50 bad habits learned at school](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/50_bad_habits_learned_at_school "50 bad habits learned at school")).

Without the pleasure of learning, human existence becomes a joyless set of habits

## Learntropy

There are many factors that affect how messages and information channels are perceived and valued by the brain. In preceding sections we have noticed that the brain does not respond just to entropy. There are many factors that modulate the impact of entropy or surprisal of individual messages. Those factors include: encoding, speed of delivery, pre-processing (e.g. generalization, completion, recognition, etc.), prior knowledge (incl. valuation, emotional valence, channel reliability, etc.), optimum level (affected by speed of processing), and more.

The complexity of the process calls for a better concept that can encapsulate all those nuances. I suggest the use of the term learntropy to describe the attractiveness of an educational channel or signal from the point of view of an individual brain in a specific context.

**Learntropy is the attractiveness of any educative signal as determined by the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") system.**

Lectures can be boring or attractive. Learntropy expresses their attractiveness from the point of view of an individual.

While entropy has a precise mathematical definition, learntropy would probably best be measured by the response of the reward system to the act of learning from the analyzed signal. As much as entropy depends on the probability of individual messages, learntropy will depend on the rewarding power of these messages (pictures, sounds, sentences, etc). That rewarding power will be associated with probability, but the valuation will largely depend on the [knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network").

For good learning there is a reward. However, there is also bad learning. There is a [decoding failure penalty](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Decoding_failure_penalty "Decoding failure penalty"). If a student makes an effort to decode a message and fails, he is penalized. This is how frustration is born. This is how the dislike of learning begins. If learntropy is low, reward is little, penalty is high, and the net result may be negative. If we take negative reward signals into account, learntropy could actually assume negative values. A boring lecture could carry negative learntropy. It will result in suppressing the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive").

High knowledge valuations contribute to high [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy"), which in turn is necessary for attention and semantic slotting in of knowledge for long-term retention. In a powerful feedback loop, **learntropy enhances the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), which underlies valuations that determine learntropy**. This feedback loop is kept in check by forgetting, learned helplessness, aging, injury, and the sheer availability of mental resources. With rational learning and lifestyle management, esp. with respect to the [natural creativity cycle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle"), the equilibrium can be maintained at the high [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") level for decades.

## Signal timing vs. learntropy

The degree of reward obtained from individual messages in the learning stream will determine the level of signal learntropy. A lecture on a boring topic will carry low learntropy. Surfing the net for titbits of information needed to solve a specific problem will carry high learntropy.

Unlike [Shannon entropy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entropy_\(information_theory\)) that is based on averages, learntropy will be more of a trailing average where recent messages will carry a higher weight than messages delivered earlier in time. In addition, learntropy is rooted in rules that govern the [consolidation of memory](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Two_component_model_of_memory "Two component model of memory"), incl. the [spacing effect](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spacing_effect "Spacing effect").

The learntropy of a boring lecture will shoot up once a golden nugget of knowledge fills an important gap in understanding. The increase in learntropy will be proportional to the expression of the [stability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Stability "Stability") of the memory trace determining knowledge valuation (incl. descending traces in the [knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network")). The impact of a golden nugget will wane in time. The cumulative effect of those happy discoveries will determine the level of learntropy at any given time (e.g. during a lecture).

The above shows that educators can influence learntropy, enhance the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), and enhance long-term learning outcomes. Feeding passive knowledge is a bad strategy. Providing answers should be selective and should favor high importance abstract and universal questions. Free explorations of self-directed learning are the best formula for lifelong sustainable [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") and lifelong learning.

All forms of schooling tend to suppress the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). As a result, many adults may find it difficult to internalize the message on the importance of learntropy in learning. However, in the modern world, nearly everyone is faced with the need to solve a minor technical or health problem on their own. The problem may be as simple as a trivial change to setup in Facebook options. The harder it is to find the solution to a problem, the greater the reward in finding answers. The harder it is to find answers, the more persistent and extensive the search and exploration. Those feelings should be familiar to everyone. However, suppression of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") always results in lesser knowledge, lower self-esteem, and all explorations might come to an end earlier. In other words, those who lost their creative drive at school, or later in life, will give up earlier, or perhaps never even try. In that sense, all technical problems and glitches that come with computers, Internet, technology, etc. have some positive side effect of stimulating the vestiges of the lost [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") even in the most passive individuals. The only requirement is that those quests need to end with a degree of success. Otherwise, the opposite may happen. The penalty signal may lead to conditioning a withdrawal from exploration.

You can quickly answer this instant quiz about your own [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). If you face a minor problem in life, do you seek a human expert or you rely on Google? If your car fails, or you computer crashes, or you get injured, or you got a stomach ache, where do you go?

## Learntropy and learn drive

In a process similar to [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting_curve "Forgetting curve"), the impact of [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") reward will decline exponentially over time. Like in [spaced repetition](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spaced_repetition "Spaced repetition") review, the new reward will bring back learntropy to a high level. Like in a [spacing effect](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spacing_effect "Spacing effect"), longer breaks may result in the same message being more rewarding.

There is a major difference between the reward signal determining learntropy and the consolidation signal determining recall in learning: once you learn something, repeated review in short spaces of time is pointless, once you drive recall probability to 100%, you can let time pass before the next review. The upper limit on learntropy might be hard to reach. If you love a lecture, with some twists of facts or delivery, you can love it more. If you remember a singular memory, you cannot remember it better by tricks employed in a short space of time. You can reformulate the memory using mnemonic techniques and affect its durability, but once the probability of recall is 100%, the best thing to do for the memory might be to leave it unused for a while or employ it in varying context, which may essentially lead to developing new memories that will form redundant connections to the original singular memory.

Extinction of learntropy occurs via lack of reward signal. Extinction of [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is a matter of [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting "Forgetting") (incl. forgetting through brain cell loss).

Learntropy will be additive over individual messages with exponential decline and diminishing returns. By optimizing the timing of rewarding messages, we can drive learntropy high and make learning become one of the most pleasurable activities on the par with rewards of food, sex, drugs, etc. If you are skeptical, recall obsessive videogamers who can literally starve while playing nights. [Videogames](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Videogames "Videogames") can highjack the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") and combine it with the reward of gambling. Rewards of gambling might also be governed by similar rules of decline and boost as learntropy, however, they are subject to [variable reward](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Variable_reward "Variable reward") which can lead to addiction. It is important to distinguish between the pleasure of learning and harmful addictions (see: [Addiction to learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Addiction_to_learning "Addiction to learning")).

Learntropy will determine the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), but both will be sustained with different rules. Learn drive is knowledge dependent, and as such will be subject to [spaced repetition](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spaced_repetition "Spaced repetition"). As [knowledge is a network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Concept_network "Concept network"), speaking of optimum stimulation of [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is probably pointless. To maximize learn drive, we should engage in lifelong learning, respect [natural creativity cycle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle"), and take care of the brain health (i.e. health in general).

## Optimum information delivery

In schooling, we might envisage a lecture delivered at optimum [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") level, in which a student keeps saying "_wow! wow!_". She keeps taking down notes as fast as humanly possible. More often though, the lecture will buzz a high entropy signal or ooze boredom. Its learntropy will be low or even negative.

If optimum learntropy levels depend on the student, how can a teacher optimally deliver knowledge to a classroom? Sometimes universal delivery is impossible. In other cases, it is difficult enough to require genius teaching skills. For most teachers, lecture delivery keeps most kids bored or frustrated.

In lecture delivery, a lucky few may get most of the message. For a fraction of the gifted, the lecture may carry nothing new. For them it is boring. For other kids, message complexity goes above their comprehension level. In such cases, the lecture can be frustrating if they try to decode it. A lecture on string theory might be comparable to a noise of randomly shuffled English words. Lecturing is an exercise in timewasting. Nobel Prize winner [Carl Wieman](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Carl_Wieman "Carl Wieman") compared it to [blood-letting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Wieman:_Lectures_make_no_sense "Wieman: Lectures make no sense").

To avoid the frustration of negative learntropy, students will tune out like you tuned out from that Thai channel I mentioned [earlier](#Prior_knowledge_in_information_seeking). Children will ignore the static noise coming from the teacher and tune in to other channels that carry more appropriate levels of learntropy (e.g. Facebook on a phone under the desk). Even if their comprehension is good, the knowledge delivered may not complement their current knowledge. If it does not generate [high-quality high-value](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network") generalization, it will be considered obvious or irrelevant.

Low learntropy, even if occurring occasionally, conditions the student to tune out. After a while, students will develop a filter that will turn a teacher into a silent radio channel carrying zero entropy and zero learntropy. Improvements to lecture quality will become futile. The teacher disappears!

In a classroom setting, a student will often not be able to zero in on a better signal. The same signal is dished out to all students and they all may get equally bored. In contrast, Googling for good keywords can bombard the brain with perfectly timed low probability messages that will [fit the current knowledge tree like a jigsaw puzzle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_crystallization "Knowledge crystallization"). Google is a very cheap and efficient generator of "wow!".

In [incremental learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_learning "Incremental learning"), the learntropy scanner will pick best channels, prioritize those and employ perfect timing for maximizing semantic connectivity and memory consolidation. This should make it easy to understand why **I am extremely happy, I will never ever be forced to sit in a school bench!** I love learning too much!

All the above examples illustrate how intricate the interaction between the signal and the brain is in recognizing things worth learning. The reward of learning is the best known indicator of learning quality. When students are happy, we are on the right track. When schools are the place of misery, we are failing on a societal scale.

**The only reliable detector of knowledge complementarity and [coherence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence") are the neural networks of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") system. This is why knowledge cannot be prepackaged and imposed on students.**

This is explained using a [crystallization metaphor](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_crystallization "Knowledge crystallization"). The neural details of the reward system follow in the section: [Learning rewards](#Learning_rewards).

## Gripping lectures

We love learning, but we usually hate to be taught. Those feelings correlate with creativity, which can probably be explained by the fact that creative elaboration is essential for [pattern completion](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Pattern_completion "Pattern completion") that underlies comprehension.

In learning, we decide what to investigate. The [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") evaluation strictly depends on the status of the brain and current memory activations. In teaching, knowledge is dished out independent of what we think of it. Many students list boring subjects as their number one reason for disliking school. Not bullying, stress, or early waking. Excruciating boredom! I write about the astronomical difference between self-directed learning and learning at school [here](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learning_history:_school_vs._self-directed_learning "Learning history: school vs. self-directed learning"). It is all about the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")!

I am amazed with how many resources are wasted on research that looks for ways to keep kids interested during lectures, while it should be obvious that lectures are just a poor educational tool. Eye contact analysis? Engagement analysis? Efforts to quantify passion? All kids are equipped with natural [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") and our priority should be to ensure we do not destroy that drive. [Force-feeding knowledge](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Schools_suppress_the_learn_drive "Schools suppress the learn drive") is the prime destroyer of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). In addition, there are many socioeconomic factors that prevent a great chunk of kids to thrive even in the best circumstances. Some kids will never show passion for learning. In most cases, it is not their fault. Only a tiny fraction are limited by disabilities, health, and less fortunate genetic endowments. The exponential decay in the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") with age is caused primarily by compulsory schooling. Passive lecturing is a huge contributor to that process.

Naturally, there are lectures that work. [Khan Academy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Khan_Academy "Khan Academy") is jam-packed with good examples. Even a spoken lecture with no slides can work. A TED talk on YouTube can be fun. It can satisfy the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). [MOOCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massive_open_online_course) are founded on the principle that one rock-star teacher is better than thousands of rank-and-file teachers repeating the same mantra. You can learn a lot even if you are just a passive listener. There are conditions though: you need to be intensely curious about the subject, or you need to love the speaker, or both. **There is only one sure mechanism for ensuring the lecture is interesting: you need to choose it on your own!** This is just one more aspect of the need for self-directed learning.

In addition to choice, in lecturing, you definitely need a pause button in case you need to take a toilet break, or quiet the hunger pangs. Nothing can ruin a lecture as effectively as a bursting bladder. Last but not least, most lectures could benefit from Netflix's Skip Intro feature.

Naturally, the lecture will work best if you enhance it with your own creative thinking or even quick research. This is why pausing for a minute, or for a day might be essential for learning efficiency. Against the claims of some psychiatrists, creative breaks and a wandering mind have nothing to do with [ADHD](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/ADHD "ADHD"). As long as they are remotely relevant, they are hallmarks of great learning.

I use two methods for consuming lectures incrementally. My first method is to listen and exercise. Exercise improves focus. Good focus reduces the need for a pause, however, it also reduces the creative aspect of learning. For subjects of highest priority, I use [incremental video](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_video "Incremental video") where I can pause and resume multiple times. I can even keep the most important lecture extracts for future review. However, even incremental video isn't the best approach to learning. It cannot compete in speed and volume with [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading"). Sometimes it makes better sense to employ incremental reading and process the lecture transcript than to listen to the lecture itself. This is particularly visible in fact-rich lecturing.

I choose my video materials mostly on the basis of speakers who I just love to listen to. In the context of this article, I know you would love Ken Robinson lectures! ## Learning rewards

The pleasure of learning might be one of the most satisfying possible pleasures. As opposed to eating or having sex, the pleasure of learning does not terminate with the act. The pleasure of learning is sustainable and wanes slowly only with the overload of networks involved in learning. It can be reset back to the baseline with sleep. The pleasure of learning has been shown to involve the same mechanisms as the [pleasure of heroin or cocaine](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Biederman_model "Biederman model"). Unlike feeding or sex, pleasurable learning can fill most of the waking time. In that sense, the pleasures of learning, creativity, problem solving, and productivity might be great tools in stoic hedonic therapy. Whereas the need for food is easily satisfied in a healthy individual, the need for learning may never end. The [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") depends on the status of current knowledge and this status can be manipulated with learning itself.

### Learn drive reward

I have mentioned a couple of examples of how the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") leads to a reward signal in the brain. We know that low probability information can be rewarding. So can a generalization that contributes new knowledge. A snippet of information that leads to a great goal of understanding is highly valued. A [missing piece in a jigsaw puzzle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Jigsaw_puzzle_metaphor "Jigsaw puzzle metaphor") carries a great reward. One obscure word, once decoded, can make a whole long text switch from a tangle of sentences into a clear line of reasoning.

Confirming a model via a generalization or laying foundations for a new better model both feel great. In addition, all model confirmations associated with strong emotions can lead to euphoria: "_My team is the best in the world!_", or "_Yes! My newborn is healthy indeed!_", or "_Yeah! I knew that hard work will earn me that promotion!_". However, when discussing the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), I would like to filter out that extra emotional layer that may obscure the picture. We need to remember that learning is pleasurable independent of whether it brings rewards from employing the knowledge.

The _Aha!_, _Wow!_ or _Eureka!_ of discovery is the purest and ultimate prize in learning. It does not need to entail further reward in accolades or praise from others. Here, the knowledge is its own reward.

The common denominator of this reward is the encoding of new highly-valued information in memory.

**The learn drive reward comes from [high-value knowledge](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network") ready for long-term storage.**

In our quest to understand reality, while the total amount of information stored in the brain increases, the entropy of stored knowledge drops. **With learning and modeling, it takes less and less effort to understand the complexity of the world.**

### Evolution of the learn drive

Scientists say that smart animals play more. I say that it is even more interesting to note that species that play more are smarter. I hypothesize that the **[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") may have been the trigger factor in the explosion of the human brain size**. It is not that birds or mammals faced a change in environment that required more thinking. It is not that humans suddenly faced extinction had they not blown up the size of their cortex. It may have been the emergence of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") that suddenly allowed better usage of the expensive increase in the number of brain cells. Before there was the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), adding brain size might leave an animal with an extra head weight to carry and an extra set of cells to feed. Without the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), the extra brain space might remain unused and likely undergo wasteful atrophy. If schooling attempts to override the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), it will contribute to the disuse of that evolutionary advantage. It will contribute to society that is less smart and less creative.

If we plot the brain size over the timeline of human evolution, we can see a powerful upswing around 2 million years ago. Paleoanthropologists tend to attribute that swing to better brain nutrients in the diet, [cooking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catching_Fire:_How_Cooking_Made_Us_Human), and the like.

If the hypothesis on the emergence of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is correct, _Homo habilis_ would be a candidate for the starting point of the breakthrough. This could point to the transition from a simple procedural play drive of birds and mammals towards a more sophisticated declarative learn drive that ultimately leads us to building abstract models of reality, which underlie human intelligence. _Homo habilis_ has also been hypothesized to lead to the emergence of [childhood dominated by brain growth](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Homo_habilis:_the_emergence_of_childhood "Homo habilis: the emergence of childhood") (from weaning to an average of 7 years old).

The late arrival of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") in evolution would suggest that it is not a simple property emergent in neural networks (see: [Biederman model](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Biederman_model "Biederman model")). Otherwise it might easily show up in fish or earlier. The [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") requires a dedicated set of neural structures that are able to send a reward signal at the point of detecting an incremental contribution to a coherent structure of declarative knowledge. This signal and the underlying structure might differ in procedural learning and declarative learning. It might also differ for different classes of sensory input.

### Procedural learning reward

I hypothesized about [circuits that might run procedural learning](http://www.super-memory.com/english/ol/ol_files/refinement_circuitry_in_stochastic_learning.jpg) back in the 1980s. In my [Master's Thesis](http://super-memory.com/english/ol.htm), out of ignorance, I used my own term ["_stochastic learning_"](http://www.super-memory.com/english/ol/ol_memory.htm). I had no idea that two decades earlier, back in 1969, David Marr proposed a theoretical model of the cerebellar cortex that fit my own thinking. In the new millennium, [there is a lot of data to confirm the model](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2805361/).

The idea of a procedural learning circuit is very simple. Imagine you ride a bicycle. You apply your conscious mind to learn individual moves needed to mount the bike and to then continue pedalling. However, once you are on the way, the procedural learning system makes sure you can execute all moves automatically with minimum neural effort without participation of conscious supervision or minimal supervision over a set of [command neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_neuron). Procedural learning will determine your [motor program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_program). This procedural learning system will make minor random adjustments to the sequence of signals sent to the motor system (hence the name "_stochastic learning_"). You can view those random changes as procedural creativity. Each time your bike loses balance, a penalty signal will be sent from the error-detecting network to cancel proposed corrections. That penalty signal will play the role of a teaching signal for the motor program.

During sleep, memories will be reorganized to eliminate the need for conscious input, simplified, optimized, and garbage signals that have a low contribution to the skill will be rejected. With each kilometer cycled, the sequence of signals will be perfected by trial and error. With each bout of sleep the wrinkles will get smoother. Riding a bike will become a pleasure. That pleasure seems to peek in the transition from clumsy conscious rider to a natural.

In a similar fashion, with each sentence typed on the computer, you will strike fewer typos. Do you know where ")" is on the keyboard? How about "}"? The more fluent you are in typing, the more likely you are to forget this detail. When the conscious control of motor sequences is taken away, declarative knowledge of the position of ")" on the keyboard may be thrown away as "garbage". It is no longer needed.

### Declarative learning reward

Things are a bit more complex in explaining the declarative [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). There is a definite reward to [declarative learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Declarative_memory "Declarative memory"). Some things are just interesting, and finding out the truth is pleasing. At the neural level, the brain will scan inputs and neural activations to look for areas of high [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") with the maximum delivery of new knowledge matching the current status of memory. Any meaningful message of low probability will be deemed more attractive. A bright fractal pattern will be deemed beautiful. A gray randomness of colors will be deemed boring. The same will occur in the case of a more complex visual message. A vibrant forest is beautiful. The same forest may seem unattractive in winter, in draught, or under the impact of environmental pollution. [Steven Pinker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Pinker) remarked that we are attracted to images that ooze vitality. I disagree. The attraction is much wider. We may be equally well attracted to a deathly volcano or a frozen landscape of Antarctica. We love environments, signals, messages, or brain activations that can express complex information using [simple models](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Abstract_knowledge "Abstract knowledge"). The picture of a beautiful beach can be represented by a couple of simple shapes and textures.

Entropy of information is related to the compressability of data. Signal processing begins on the input. The retina performs a 100-fold compression of the visual input signal. The visual cortex receives simple representations of shapes and relationships. The hippocampus receives that information in an episodic context. Those signals may end up changing the status of a single synapse in the neocortical long-term memory storage.

The [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is based on seeking effective ways of representing knowledge in neural networks. [Learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), [memory optimization in sleep](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Memory_optimization_in_sleep "Memory optimization in sleep"), and [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting "Forgetting") are essential to maximize compressibility, [abstractness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Abstractness "Abstractness"), [applicability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Applicability "Applicability"), and performance. This is how the brain makes sure that we can see a complex world using simple representations. That's the core of [human intelligence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Human_intelligence "Human intelligence"). If artificial intelligence researchers could equip robots with a human-like [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), given sufficient memory, their learning capacity might be inexhaustible.

### Reward centers in learning

In 2014, researchers reported that the [activity in the nucleus accumbens was increased in the state of "high curiosity"](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Curiosity_improves_learning "Curiosity improves learning"). They have also demonstrated what we have always known: this state improved memory performance. In addition, that improved performance spilled onto incidental learning, i.e. learning that would not spark curiosity on its own. This research was widely reported in media with a wrong interpretation: "_curiosity primes the brain for better memory_". For example, Scientific American headlined "_Neuroimaging reveals how the brain’s reward and memory pathways prime inquiring minds for knowledge_". The paper itself suggested the need for _"stimulating curiosity"_.

As reward centers can be involved in the anticipation of pleasure, we should rather see the results of the research as an indicator that the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is associated with pleasure. It is the learn drive that causes learning. It is learning that is pleasurable. The headline should be "_Neuroimaging confirms that efficient learning is pleasurable_". In other words, the sequence is not "drive -> pleasure -> learning", but "drive -> learning -> pleasure".

Instead of speaking of the need to "stimulate curiosity", which should rather speak of the need to "develop the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")". The key difference is in perceiving stimulation as quick-fix approach that might be used in a classroom as opposed to a long-term process that takes months and years. An advertising campaing may use cheap tricks to stimulate our curiosity, while a lifelong passion is a formula for insatiable and unwaning [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), which is a perfect warranty for unceasing learning.

It is true that the state of curiosity will improve attention and this will improve overall learning, however, this should not ever be used as a classroom strategy. Gamification of learning makes sense only if rewards come from target learning, not from learning that surrounds the target. Many learning programs for children use bright colors, unusual sounds or smiling faces to attract attention to induce learning. However, once habituation sets it, this form or artificial gamification stops being effective. Moreover, incidental knowledge does not last. Any effort to employ curiosity to spark incidental learning is non-specific and inefficient. Equally well we might hope that pharmacological intervention, e.g. with Ritalin, could improve learning. Instead, learning must be its own reward.

The nucleus accumbens and the ventral tegmental area are involved in pleasure, in anticipation of pleasure, and in signal evaluation. The signals from the [knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network") converge into those areas in both their [motivational and affective valence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Brain_centers_involved_in_valuation_of_anticipated_outcomes:_nucleus_accumbens_and_VTA "Brain centers involved in valuation of anticipated outcomes: nucleus accumbens and VTA"). Dopamine is involved in the anticipation of pleasure. As dopamine is involved in attention, anticipation of pleasure alone would lead to improved learning due to a better focus on the source of information that is expected to deliver the pleasure.

If you are unconvinced, think of how much you hate your news channel when they do their tricks to pique your interest, and then say "_find out after the break_". You can get even more livid when they ruin it all with "_Breaking News!_". Anticipation can lead to frustration too. Only actual learning provides the reward. Only actual learning reward makes sense from the point of view of evolution. We do not want to reward an animal for the mere sight of food.

The buzz in the nucleus accumbens can be a direct expression of pleasure or might also indicate the state of pleasure seeking. In the end, the actual interpretation does not matter for the ultimate conclusion: **boredom and displeasure are the enemies of learning**.

For efficient learning in which new knowledge complements current knowledge, we need to follow the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). In simple terms, this means that the pleasure of learning is desirable in education. We should never learn in the state of displeasure (cf. [Desirable difficulty](#Desirable_difficulty)). Painful learning comes from the brain letting the student know that, in information theoretic sense, the new knowledge does not fit! It will be rejected. Pleasure is a good guide!

From the above neural reasoning we derive the obvious, **the best warranty of efficient learning is to let students learn on their own and follow their own passions.**

## Biederman model

### Pleasure of reading about the pleasure of reading

In 2006, Irving Biederman and Edward A. Vessel, published a paper that gave me unforgettable pleasure to read. The article itself explained the pleasure of reading to me. In a paper titled "_[Perceptual pleasure and the brain](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_receptors_are_involved_in_the_pleasure_of_learning "Opioid receptors are involved in the pleasure of learning")_", Biederman hypothesized that a gradient of opioid receptors in brain structures responsible for visual perception might contribute to the pleasure of viewing nice scenes such as beautiful landscapes. Biederman's idea seemed to explain to me what I have known for ages: **learning is pleasurable**. I always liked to learn, however, I never truly understood what underlies my liking in terms of brain science. Biederman's explanation was a perfect fit and it was powerfully pleasurable. It explained something that bothered my mind for a longer while. At the moment of reading, I was very self-analytical. While reading about the pleasure of reading I was trying to "feel" how the enlightenment of reading provides the pleasure. The pleasure of reading about the pleasure of reading became unforgettable.

What Biederman and Vessel proposed is monumental. Let me therefore name their thinking for simplicity: the **Biederman model** (name choice by seniority). In visual perception, successive layers of neurons are responsible for more abstract representations of the visual scene. Metaphorically speaking, it starts from pixels and colors, then it moves on to edges, textures and surfaces, then to objects, then to faces, places, and collections, and then to meaningful episodic scenes that, at the end of the chain, may activate a representation of a "beautiful mountain", and be remembered as such with only a few details perpetuated beyond the first impression in working memory. Millions of pixels of a photograph will turn into a meaningful scene that can be verbalized in just a few sentences and remembered as such for years, at a very little neural cost.

Biederman model capitalizes on an earlier discovery ([Michael E. Lewis et al., 1981](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_receptors_form_a_gradient_along_a_processing_hierarchy)) that there is a gradient of mu\-opioid receptors along the visual perception pathway. The more meaning the neuron carries, the more opioid receptors it is likely to have. We know that opiates are rewarding and addictive. Biederman model is based on the hypothesis that this [gradient of opioid receptors is the source of perceptive pleasure](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_receptors_are_involved_in_the_pleasure_of_learning "Opioid receptors are involved in the pleasure of learning").

There is a similar hierarchical system for processing speech and music. A temporal cortex involves processing sounds from pitch to melody. The processing of the rhythm involves yet [other areas of the brain](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4101486/). Chances are, all those perceptive networks work along similar principles. This is the study subject of [neuroesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroesthetics).

### Opioid vs. dopamine pleasure

There is a slight problem with the Biederman model though. The pleasure of learning can be analyzed consciously. The pleasure of reading about Biederman model, in my own case, could be decomposed and tracked down to individual components of the model. This fact implies that the pleasure is integrated with conscious experience. Consciousness is a notoriously hard nut to crack for neuroscience. Most of what we know about consciousness is either speculative or based on hard and expensive experiments in which electrodes implanted in the brain can be used to elicits effects that can later, or concurrently, be reported by the affected individual. The evidence seems to be converging on the integrative model of consciousness in which an activation of several structures in the brain gets integrated and perceived as conscious self. In that line of thinking, activating a Halle Berry neuron somewhere in the cortex is not enough to bring Halle to one's consciousness. Millions of concept neurons can get activated at the same time and a thinking mind can only operate on a few pieces of the model of the perceived reality (see: [attention](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Attention "Attention")). To bring Halle to one's mind, the activation must get integrated with other components of conscious perception, including the reward of the perception.

For those reasons, opioid receptors in cortical neurons will not do much for the ultimate reward of learning. An opiod antagonist, [naloxone, can take away some of the pleasure of music in some people](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Thrill_of_music_may_be_attenuated_with_opioid_antagonists "Thrill of music may be attenuated with opioid antagonists"). However, the opioid pleasure of learning should rather produce a mild bliss of first-time micro-dose heroin or morphine use. In that sense, release of endomorphins and activation of opioid receptors can make a contribution to the pleasure of learning. Nevertheless, this pleasure isn't specific enough to give one a jolt of "wow!", "aha!" or "eureka!" (Biederman calls it "click of comprehension"). For that ultimate learning reward, there must be an integrative reward experience coming from the [pleasure centers in the brain](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Neural_circuits_involved_in_liking_and_wanting "Neural circuits involved in liking and wanting").

### Pleasure of association

That ultimate pleasure jolt of discovery will come from a meaningful association. It can be explained using the pleasure of understanding the Biederman model itself. When thinking about the model, we activate two important concepts in our minds: (1) a gradient of meaning (derived from understanding neural structures involved in visual perception), and (2) a gradient of pleasure (derived from the observation on the content of opioid receptors in visual pathways). Once these two concept come up in mind, there is a glue of analogy: the concept of _"gradient"_. That glue helps bring up the association that gives a jolt of pleasant enlightenment: **MEANING = PLEASURE**! That's exactly what I experienced when reading Biederman's paper. For that jolt to happen, it is not enough that there are more opiate receptors associated with the concept of the gradient of pleasure than with gradient's mathematical underpinnings or its association with the word "gradient". It is not enough that there is more opiate associated with the novel concept of "gradient of meaning" than with the often used term "meaning". The jolt happens when those two highly priced concepts collide: meaning + pleasure.

Biederman noticed that the gradient of receptors proceeds far into the associative areas, incl. the parahippocampal cortex. We may remember that further downstream, in the hippocampus we have found the [Halle Berry neuron](#Detecting_surprisal). To illustrate the difference between the opioid pleasure and the associative pleasure, let us imagine meeting Halle on a beautiful beach. While walking on a beach, we may experience a delicate heroin-like breeze of bliss, which comes from the realization that our environment is perceptively beautiful: _"the beach I walk on feels great"_. Once Halle shows up on a horizon, visual analysis may provide another breeze of opioid pleasure coming from the signal _"beautiful lady approaching"_. Then the visual processing unit may identify the lady as Halle, which might activate cortical representation of Halle, which could be opioid-rich. However, only the ultimate association of Halle and _"my beach"_ would trigger a major discovery, perhaps an atavistic reproductive dream: _"Halle walks the same sand like me!"_. This is where the reward from the ventral striatum and the nucleus accumbens might come to play in "liking" the situation, and a jolt of [dopamine](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_rewards_may_depend_on_dopamine_signals "Opioid rewards may depend on dopamine signals") might trigger a behavioral program of "wanting". The details of that behavioral "wanting" program have been cut out from this text by censorship. Nevertheless, execution of that program would inevitably be halted in highly-developed individuals by [executive signals](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Executive_function "Executive function") from the prefrontal cortex. In short, an injection of dopamine in the pleasure centers of the brain may give the brain some indecent ideas, while the release of opioid peptides might just result in an associative bliss.

The pleasure of learning does not need to involve attractive representatives of the opposite sex. Halle showed up in my example only because of the discovery of the [Halle Berry neuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandmother_cell). For the pleasure of learning, all that is needed is a powerful and highly-valued association of ideas that activates the pleasure centers in the brain. The pleasure happens each time we learn something new, and the jolt is most powerful when we learn something of high value. The pleasure of discovering the Biederman model came from high valuations of the pleasure of learning itself in my [knowledge valuation network](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_valuation_network "Knowledge valuation network"). High valuations lead to high reward, which may facilitate memory (see: [Dopamine may modulate plasticity in learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Dopamine_may_modulate_plasticity_in_learning "Dopamine may modulate plasticity in learning"))

### Impact of memory on the pleasure of learning

I would also add to Biederman's hypotheses on desensitization, i.e. the decline in pleasure with repeated exposure. Biederman suggests that children love repetitive [videogames](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Videogames "Videogames") because of the gambling factor. However, gambling is no less potent in adults. I posit that children enjoy repetitive learning more because of [childhood amnesia](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Childhood_amnesia "Childhood amnesia"). Some of the repeat pleasure may come from limited comprehension, but some will simply be explained by accelerated forgetting. Poor comprehension and forgetting are the primary differentiators between the adult and the child brains.

We should also notice that a great deal of decline in pleasure of review will come not from competitive learning but from [long term-memory consolidation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Two_component_model_of_memory "Two component model of memory") that might result in signals flowing efficiently in the system. Competitive learning may be important in pattern recognition but in associative learning, it will be high [retrievability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Retrievability "Retrievability") that will undermine the pleasure of repeated exposure.

### Stages of learn drive evolution

When I hypothesized on the emergence of powerful [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") in humans, I had in mind the direct channel from knowledge to reward centers. It would ultimately be a higher level of [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") than the one implied by the Biederman model. Each time receptors are involved, evolution has a simple and grateful material to work with. Receptor gradient has originally been discovered in a [rhesus cortex](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Opioid_receptors_form_a_gradient_along_a_processing_hierarchy "Opioid receptors form a gradient along a processing hierarchy"). Similar mechanisms might be involved in simpler brains or even more primitive nervous systems deprived of central control. I have no idea what an ant thinks or how it feels, but finding a great food source must definitely be a source of some kind of ant pleasure. From this we can conclude that the pleasure of learning might not be much phylogenetically younger than the nervous system itself. However, in the course of evolution, the drive has built up new layers of functionality and efficiency. Playful creativity seems to emerge only with some birds and with mammals. That evolutionary process might have ultimately peaked as human [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). This will naturally, at some point, be implemented in thinking machines. Understanding the power of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") will be vital for survival of humanity: both in its need for artificial intelligence and the threat of having AI turn against mankind.

## Desirable difficulty

**Desirable difficulty** is a concept that might be an excuse for tolerating the displeasure of learning at school. Here I explain why this excuse is unjust and dangerous.

Robert Bjork might be the best expert on learning theory. If he tells you that difficulties can be desirable in learning, he is right and it does not stand in contradiction to the fact that good learning is always pleasurable. Desirable difficulty is a conglomerate of concepts in which obstacles in learning lead to better learning. Let's tackle those one by one in the light of the pleasure of learning:

-   **[active recall](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Active_recall "Active recall")**: active recall is superior to passive review. Active recall is harder. This is a desirable difficulty. We need active recall in learning because it is the only procedure by which a memory engram can be effectively reconsolidated in [spaced repetition](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spaced_repetition "Spaced repetition"). Active recall occurs each time we employ useful knowledge in practice. This use is pleasurable because it leads to productivity, which is a reward independent of learning. Humans simply love to [achieve goals](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Setting_goals_can_change_your_life "Setting goals can change your life"). If review is planned artificially, like in [SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo "SuperMemo"), it does not lead to a productive act and it may easily lose its appeal. All successful users of SuperMemo link the review with their goals. They see each item and each repetition as a step to a better future. Not all users have this imaginative capacity. This is why SuperMemo has not swept mankind off its feet despite its amazing efficiency.
-   **[spaced repetition](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Spaced_repetition "Spaced repetition")**: memory [consolidation](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Consolidation "Consolidation") is more effective if [retrievability](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Retrievability "Retrievability") of memory is less. This leads to difficulty in [recall](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Recall "Recall"). This is a desirable difficulty. Like with [active recall](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Active_recall "Active recall"), the reward of [review](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Review "Review") comes from the employment of knowledge and productivity. In [SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo "SuperMemo"), by default, most of review ends with successful recall and there might be some link between difficulty and pleasure. Again, only a subset of users of SuperMemo can find this process pleasurable. Those who don't usually do not last long and drop out. We tell all users, make SuperMemo fun, or it won't work for you! See also: [Pleasure of knowing](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Pleasure_of_knowing "Pleasure of knowing")
-   **incremental review**: SuperMemo advocates learning in spaces. It is more efficient from the point of view of memory and creativity to read an article in small portions over a longer period of time. The same refers to watching a video or listening to a lecture. This results in minor battles for context retrieval. However, it brings an extra bonus in creative elaboration. It also improves memory encoding, generalization, and long-term memory consolidation. Paradoxically, those extra difficulties result in extra learning efficiency that makes [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") one of the most pleasurable forms of learning.
-   **learning context**: changing the context in retrieval is a very simple and effective type of desirable difficulty. If the encoding is correct, retrieval will be successful, it will be more effective and it will be rewarding. If context change leads to generalization and better memory encoding, the effectiveness of learning will increase and the reward of learning will increase.
-   **problem solving**: solving problems can be very pleasurable. The harder the problem, the greater the pleasure of a solution. Problem solving involves a learning process as the solution requires intermediary steps that result in storing new knowledge in memory. All those steps are pleasurable. If the student struggles with the task and makes no progress, he will learn nothing and receive no reward. The tasks turns out too difficult. If the students fails to solve the problem, but makes progress with intermediary steps, even if they are unrelated to the solution, the learning will be there and the reward will be there. Again, if the difficulty is desirable, it will lead to a reward. If there is no reward, the difficulty appeared insurmountable. As such, it is neither rewarding nor desirable.
-   **learning by doing**: learning by doing may involve play, creativity, problem solving and more. Learning by doing takes more time and often brings better results and more reward.
-   **delayed feedback**: delayed feedback, in some circumstances, may result in more processing. In simplest terms, if the teacher does not tell you how well you have done, you may wonder for a while longer. This can benefit memory. If it does, the ultimate effect will be rewarding.
-   **help withdrawal**: I write about help withdrawal in the context of [schools suppressing the learning drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Schools_suppress_the_learn_drive#Teacher_problem "Schools suppress the learn drive"). Kids who receive no answers may become more curious. Curiosity increases the reward of learning. Students who do not receive assistance in correcting their false models of reality, get stronger rewards for resolving inconsistencies on their own.
-   **other difficulties**: the number of obstacles that can improve learning is endless, some of those can be hormonal in nature, some can involve motivational forces. The common denominator of all those obstacles seems to be some form of deeper processing, memory consolidation, improved attention, and more. Inevitably, obstacles that lead to better learning also involve better reward.

Desirable difficulty does not take away the pleasure of learning. Just the opposite, it makes learning more effective and more fun. If difficulty goes too far, and it results in displeasure then the difficulty is no longer desirable. This simple equivalence comes from the mechanics of the reward system in [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive").

Note that reward bonus for efficient learning due to desirable difficulty does not need to correspond to high [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy"). Learntropy is a metric for an information channel. Active recall, for example, is unrelated to novelty. It refers to memory reconsolidation. Similarly, problem solving may in part come from the need to achieve goals unrelated to learning, or be rewarded by productivity other than gains in new knowledge.

Note also that nearly all of the above desirable difficulties are inherently wired into the process of [incremental learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_learning "Incremental learning").

## Addiction to learning

### Inborn addiction

We are born in love with learning. That love usually wanes fast during the years of [compulsory schooling](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Compulsory_schooling "Compulsory schooling"). The longer we can sustain the love of learning, the bigger the benefit for the brain, health, and mankind. Love of learning has nothing to do with addiction. The definition of [addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addiction) includes adverse consequences that are a result of compulsive engagement in an activity.

Negative side effects of learning are tiny in comparison to benefits. If there is a degree of voracity or even [compulsion](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Rage_to_master "Rage to master"), it can boost the positive effects even further. It is possible to boost one's love of learning. Good learning provides the best boost to further learning.

### Learning and gambling

There is a close connection between the reward systems involved in learning and in gambling. Gambling and learning new words both activate the [ventral striatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striatum) in a [similar fashion](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822\(14\)01207-X). This close connection with gambling may confuse the picture for learning. A gambler at a slot machine does not learn much. Addictive videogaming is better. It can be pretty educational. Many team game addicts achieve fluency in English having made no progress at school before. Addiction to sports news may also involve a degree of learning. I learned about [Cabinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Cabinda) only during the Africa Cup of Nations (football). Addiction to Facebook updates is not different either. It is based on [variable reward](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Variable_reward "Variable reward") in anticipation of specific gains, however, it can also involve a great degree of learning. That learning may involve gossip, celebrity news, fake news, or actual useful learning. Even political poll updates can cause an addiction. In the battle between Hillary Clinton and Donald Trump, the polls were balanced enough to produce the cliffhanger effect. Compulsive checks for new polls have all hallmarks of an addiction. This kind of addiction, however, can lead to a great deal of learning. It is up to the student to [separate gambling from learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Optimality_of_the_learn_drive "Optimality of the learn drive"). Voracious learning is good. Learning derived from an addiction may be good too. However, gambling on its own brings little value to human existence. This is why it is very important to understand [Reward diversity in preventing addictions](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Reward_diversity_in_preventing_addictions "Reward diversity in preventing addictions").

### Learning and sleep

Obsessive learning may encroach on sleep time, and may contribute to the epidemic of insomnia and [DSPS](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/DSPS "DSPS"). Creative minds with powerful [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") may stay up learning till the early morning hours. This violation of sleep pattern was difficult or impossible before the arrival of electric lighting. The good news is that the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") tends to wane with [network fatigue](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Homeostatic "Homeostatic"). The longer we learn, the greater the degree of saturation in memory circuits. Only sleep can bring relief. This is why even most voracious learners tend to get sleepy and give up learning at some point. If a reader skips the night over a novel, this may be a likely combination of insufficient sleep drive, reduced learning, and increased [variable reward](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Variable_reward "Variable reward") that is typical of suspenseful fiction.

### Learning and exercise

I hear that obsessive learning can lead to less exercise. That would be bad. However, I think that it is bad learning that is more likely to have this effect. Good learning is joyous and sparks extra energy. A happy kid should not survive long sitting over a book or over a computer. There must be a way to vent energy. Perhaps we should rather say that reduced exercise is a hallmark of learning addiction, while good learning has neurotrophic effects and should make one burst with extra energy to burn?

### Learning restraint

Learning has its cost and it takes time. This is why it should be judicious. However, good learning is nearly always a good long-term investment. This is why we should never fear an addiction. Just the opposite, we should cherish and stoke up the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") to provide for happy lifelong learning.

## Displeasure of learning

When I claim that all learning is pleasurable, I hear a chorus of voices like "_I had to go through an awfully stressful exam that gave me lots of good knowledge for life_". Those voices confuse the pleasure of good learning with the displeasure of factors that turn learning into a horror for many students. Those horror factors are bad teachers, harsh parents, deadlines, stress, bad sleep, awful textbooks, excess volume, and more.

I hear that without deadlines or school-imposed goals, the learning would be replaced with videogames, novels, TV, hobbies, sports, etc. This might be true for many reasons. Some of those activities may carry pleasures unrelated to learning. However, they will also be beneficial for reasons of learning or exercise. A well-rounded student should be free to slow down, allocate his time for fun learning and other fun activities. Slow progress might bring more benefit.

There is no way the equation of learning could produce unhappiness in the wake of good learning. The blame will always be elsewhere. All negatives should be studied and eliminated.

In the ultimate account, even if there is a displeasure related to exams, certificates and duties, this displeasure should be imposed on the student by herself.

**Pleasurable learning can be buried in displeasure caused by stress, bad people, bad schools, bad textbooks, and more**.

## Learning and procrastination

If learning is the most sustainable form of pleasure, why do half of the students procrastinate? This is nearly a triple of the figure for the general population.

The answer is simple and important: students procrastinate because as much as good learning is a pleasure, bad learning is highly unpleasant. Most of assignments at school or even college carry a great deal of mismatch with the needs of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). This kind of learning is ineffective and unpleasant. Those kids will often play computer games in the evening claiming they _need to rest their brains_. I doubt their brains are at rest. They actually do jobs that they find pleasurable. A great deal of that pleasure comes from new learning. Unfortunately, there are no credits at school for good gaming, so the sinusoidal cycle of chores-and-fun begins on the next day or even the same day with homework.

I never stop being amazed how many students call themselves lazy. At the same time they can do many heroic feats of physical of mental work as long as these are enjoyable or serve their own goals. Even those with thousands of memorized items in SuperMemo often give themselves low conscientiousness scores. Goals of learning can be hazy, but even if they are crystal clear, poor match between the input and prior knowledge can result in significant displeasure. If [learntropy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learntropy "Learntropy") is low, assignments can be boring. If it is negative, they will be repulsive.

The battle between high goal valuations and negative rewards of bad learning will result in procrastination. Procrastinators often call themselves lazy even if they are nothing but.

If you think you are lazy about learning, you need to re-evaluate your materials and your methodology. Even simple violations of the [natural creativity cycle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle") can kill the fun of learning.

## Learning and depression

Learning is a sustainable and non-addictive form of pleasure with hardly any side effects other than cost in time. In addition, good learning tends to absorb the mind, and promote more learning by boosting the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). This means that learning should be employable as therapy in depression.

### Learning at school

If learning is a source of pleasure and reward, why do we see [rampant depression](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_increase_in_depression "Incremental increase in depression") in kids of school age? Despite being institutions of learning, schools are [more likely to contribute to depression than to act as a remedy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/I_became_so_depressed_that_I_stopped_going_to_school "I became so depressed that I stopped going to school"). Without the freedom to learn, it is hard to achieve good learning. For learning to be pleasurable, it needs to be powered by the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). It cannot be coercive or mandatory. It must be free.

### Impact of memory on mood

Free learning is fun, however, the pleasure of learning is not what makes learning a great weapon against depression.

Memory is a factor that may trigger or suppress depression. Memories determine how input signals get routed in the brain. Memory determines what concepts get associated with inputs or neural activations. Memories determine how we react to the sound of a passing car. It may bring up the memories of a happy vacation, the inspiration of Elon Musk, or memories of a car accident that crippled a loved one.

For memories to have a significant impact on mood, we need many of them. It is not enough to sit down a session with psychotherapist and learn a few key facts about the brain, our lives, or coping strategies. It takes months and years of learning to develop healthy tracks in the brain. We may build associations that are inherently optimistic or inherently pessimistic. We need thousands of such associations to swing the balance. However, even years of learning may easily be overturned by a pathology or trauma. Neurohormones can instantly change the mode in which the brain works. A switch in neurohormonal profile will instantly give preference to a subset of memories that may affect mood in a negative way. Trauma can plant memories that will stoke up new source of activation that will override activation from other sources. In other words, an armament of good memories may count for nothing if a switch changes the tracks in use or if a new source of activation is born in the brain. It is hardly possible to mitigate the death of a close person with learning.

Once depression hits, the affected individual faces a double whammy. Not only are good memories on defense. Bad memories start circling around facilitating their own new tracks and gaining upper hand. The brain reprograms itself and swings the balance of mood in a wrong direction. When this process becomes a runaway, we may have a clinical depression at hand. To complete bad news, depressed patients lose their love of life and their love of learning.

Can learning disrupt this cycle? It can be extremely hard! Respect for [circadian cycle](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle") is the first step towards recovering the derailed brain. In the circadian cycle, peak creativity window needs to be captured to attempt remedial learning. Learning needs to be prolific, intense, effective, and pleasurable. [Incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") would be fantastic if it was not that difficult. For a depressed individual with no skills in the department, [SuperMemo](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo "SuperMemo") is no remedy. It is too late. Trying to master incremental reading in a bad state of mind could only make matters worse. It could result in a hate of incremental reading.

If learning is possible, it can act as a refuge, which might help suppress negative memories and build new connections. As of that point, the process of building new tendrils of knowledge may begin. This process that should take the mind towards a more optimistic interpretation of the world is slow and laborious. In most severe cases, it may take months or years of hard work and the outcome is not guaranteed.

The ultimate conclusion is that learning is not a panacea, however, it can play an important role in therapy. Most of all, the risk of depression can be staved off years in advance by rich and effective learning. That learning must proceed in conditions of freedom and respect for the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). In short, **love of learning is a good way towards the love of life.**

### Anti-depressants

I am a medical Luddite. For a healthy body, I stick to the rule "_if it ain't broke, don't fix it_". I avoid all forms of pharmacological intervention. I believe in powers of [homeostasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeostasis) and dangers of homeostatic intervention. The strongest drugs I use are coffee and beer. I do not even use aspirin. I am most dismayed by the misuse of antibiotics, painkillers, sleeping pills and anti-depressants. It has been decades since I last took an antibiotic. Long enough to forget. I will use one on a death bed if necessary. All drugs have their legitimate use and so do anti-depressants. As they result in receptor downregulation, once taken, they make the neurotransmitter status quo worse. This usually means, the more the drug is taken, the more it needs to be taken to avoid a setback. However, in severe cases of clinical depression, the drugs may stop the runaway process. They may protect the brain from self-injury. Once a depressed patient starts losing brain cells, the road to recovery becomes long and bumpy. The moment anti-depressant therapy begins, if it works, is the best moment to use learning as therapy. As long as the brain is willing to proceed, learning can start up those delicate tendrils of knowledge that will hook onto reality to produce vestigial [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). In the ideal case, once the drugs are withdrawn, that learn drive should survive to begin a process that is a reverse of depression: positive feedback of learning, creativity, good sleep, and good mood. This is not easy, but it is very important. If drug therapy is the only thing that changes in a patient's life, it will work only as a break in the pathological process. It will not set the brain in a better state than the one from before the problem started. Improvements require active effort. Without a healthy [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), building up positive memories will not begin.

### Learn drive and optimism

Toddlers seem to show the most exuberant learn drive. No wonder, healthy children are born optimistic. There is a correlation between optimism and the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). Happy mind might act as an energizer of the learn drive on the neurochemical basis. Pessimism will definitely act as a suppressant or filter that will prevent the expression of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). In that sense, pessimistic mind may mask the learn drive. In depression, the learn drive may disappear entirely. No wonder [Dr Robert Sapolsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Sapolsky) called depression the worst disease in the world.

A consensus seems to emerge that schools are a major contributor to depression among teenagers (and later in life). The mechanism isn't clear, but [learned helplessness](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learned_helplessness "Learned helplessness") and the suppression of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") emerge as possible keys to the pathology.

### Can learning help you?

If you are reading this, and you are not sure learning can help you, ask yourself the question: _Are you in a good mood today?_ As mentioned above, when you are on a downswing and looking for a solution, your interpretations are darker, and you may not find this text comforting enough. Remember then about the concept of **activation energy**: you need a little first step to begin, and you may then be pulled in by a vortex of interesting things to learn.

If you are in no mood for quantum mechanics today, start from petty celebrity news, or sports news. Lowly learning is better than no learning! Alternatively, if trivia make you even more depressed, see [this fun-to-read text from Susan Engel about learning and depression](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Susan_Engel:_Learning_is_anti-depressant "Susan Engel: Learning is anti-depressant").

Last but not least, [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") might be your best "medication". In a well-managed reading process, your materials are nicely prioritized by fun and meaning. Fun learning is just one-click away. All it takes is to start. Ideally, after just a minute, you should be immersed in a therapeutic _"flow"_.

## Optimization of education: Global or Local?

Is there a risk in using pleasure as a guiding light in education?

### Perfect model of education

Over long years of schooling, we slowly develop an imaginary model of a perfect academic learning process in which we [set long-term goals](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Setting_goals_can_change_your_life "Setting goals can change your life"), follow the [curriculum](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Curriculum "Curriculum"), add important pieces of knowledge, and get to the point when we receive a college degree with rock solid knowledge in a given area supported by extensive general knowledge needed for an efficient function in society. The longer we stay in the school system, the harder it is to step away and have an objective view of that model. Paradoxically, verification of that model comes hardest to those minds who do well at school and start believing they have succeeded thanks to that perfect model of academic learning. Smart people suffer less pain at school, and, as a result, think less of the problem of the school system. Successful students internalize the model and perpetuate it by providing the same fixed path for future generations.

**The model in which we design student's knowledge via [curriculum](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Curriculum "Curriculum") is wrong!** The model of a perfect school gives credit to the system and the teachers, while all actual learning should be credited to the student. When kids fail school in droves, we tend to blame the kids, or their parents, while a small fraction of successful students will continue dreaming of the perfect school model for their own kids, and keep pushing the model on the less fortunate ones.

### Optimization based on the learn drive

Unlike the curriculum, the optimization mechanism behind the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") has been perfected in the course of human evolution. It is capable of driving individual knowledge to the level needed to disentangle all complexities of science or engineering. Before the arrival of compulsory schooling, mankind has achieved all imaginable breakthroughs needed to start [Enlightenment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_Enlightenment) or [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution). [Compulsory schooling](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Compulsory_schooling "Compulsory schooling") has originally helped to lift the "unenlightened" masses to a new level, however, it is increasingly driving itself into the optimization corner in which enlightenment is replaced by suppression of creative minds.

### Designing a child's mind

I hear this all the time from highly educated and very smart people that education is too important to let it rely on self-learning or on the blindness of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). Apparently, education is so important that we should plan it and design it globally with the best tools of science and using the best experts. While I was preoccupied with efficient learning, and before I really started thinking about the education system, I lived with the same conviction. It is quite natural to default to expert opinion.

Highly educated people often utter the following claims:

-   _children are incapable of long-term planning, therefore a curriculum is needed_
-   _[learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is a type of local optimization, while we need to plan education globally_
-   _following student interests is a recipe for disaster: they will all end up immersed in mind-numbing [videogames](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Videogames "Videogames")_

The problem is that global optimization of education sets performance targets that keep getting tighter. Global optimization keeps employing the same inefficient learning tools in an attempt to transfer more "necessary" knowledge to student minds. The outcome is misery for millions of students. While Stalin optimized globally for massive achievements of the Soviet Union, it was the market economics with its simple optimization algorithms that lifted the western world to new heights. See: [Modern schooling is like Soviet economy](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Modern_schooling_is_like_Soviet_economy "Modern schooling is like Soviet economy")

Currently employed optimization of education uses knowledge tests as the measure of performance, but relies on cramming and short-term memory to achieve more in a shorter period of time. As a result, it keeps losing its grip on the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). Competition between nations also employs performance tests. Instead of optimizing for actual long-term knowledge, we optimize for the speed of knowledge turnover in student heads. The result is unhappy students with knowledge that is tiny relative to the time invested and to the actual human potential.

### Reliance on emergence

Optimization of education can employ the concept of [emergence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Emergence "Emergence"). The [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is a mechanism by which knowledge is self-organizing with no [effort from teachers](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Push_zone "Push zone"), and no pain from a child. Natural learning may take long hours, but it is pleasurable, and healthy kids don't mind learning all day long as long as this is learning of their own choosing.

There are two vital facts we should hold in mind in reference to the local optimization of learning based on the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"):

-   without a reliance on the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), there is no good learning. All attempts at override will be massively rejected by human memory
-   [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") brings amazingly efficient long-term optimization of the learning process. Nearly all human achievement before the 1850s has been accomplished with the guidance of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive")

A skeptic would notice that human progress has accelerated since the introduction of compulsory schooling. He would be right. However, we have been on an accelerating ascent of progress ever since the emergence of the first forms of life 4 billion years ago. I see Guttenberg and [Tim Berners-Lee](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Tim_Berners-Lee "Tim Berners-Lee") as more significant contributors to that acceleration than that of the respectable [Johann Julius Hecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Julius_Hecker).

Local optimization based on the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is highly unintuitive. Creation science comes from a similar unintuitive feelings about the mechanism of natural selection. How can a local evolutionary optimization based on random mutations lead to a marvel of a human being? Global design/optimization/guidance by the hand of God seems unavoidable. Fewer people subscribe to the creation science today, however, a vast majority of the population has no idea what mechanism underlies the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), and why ignoring it is the chief problem of the [Prussian education system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_education_system).

### The tree metaphor

Given enough time and access to knowledge-rich environments, without the need for an education system, the knowledge of an individual grows into a large, comprehensive, and [coherent body](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence"). This is true of all free, and healthy individuals. The size and the quality of the tree may depend on one's personality, interests, and the starting point of the intellectual development. However, one of the chief myths of education is that the organic growth of knowledge leads to multiple biases and [areas of ignorance](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Ban_on_homeschooling "Ban on homeschooling"). Those blank spots are allegedly larger than those that remain after years of schooling. Due to the computational power of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), and the phenomenon of [emergence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Emergence "Emergence"), the opposite is true. The metaphor I like to use to explain the power of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is that of a tree growth.

Another metaphor that can help explain the [emergence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Emergence "Emergence") in building up [coherent](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence") knowledge is the [Knowledge crystallization metaphor](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Knowledge_crystallization "Knowledge crystallization"):

For most kids, knowledge never builds sufficient [coherence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence") and falls apart due to [interference](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Interference "Interference") (i.e. fast [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting "Forgetting")). However, it is superior in [coherence](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Coherence "Coherence"). This is why it is more applicable in [problem solving](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/How_to_solve_any_problem%3F "How to solve any problem?")_

### Local optimization

Local optimization of the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") leads to a perfect match between human ability and individual's environment and goals (see: [Optimality of the learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Optimality_of_the_learn_drive "Optimality of the learn drive")). Global optimization of schooling suppresses the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"), defers to the suppressed [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") when matching individuals with their jobs, and results in an unhappy society where most individuals crave 9-5 jobs for their comfort where the leadership, learning, and responsibility are delegated to someone else. The opposite happens in [democratic schools](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Democratic_schools "Democratic schools") which rely on self-learning to produce self-determined, self-fulfilled and self-reliant individuals ready to accept any challenge in their chosen area of interest.

In his historic commencement speech, Steve Jobs joked that before he was diagnosed with cancer, he did not know what the pancreas was. Apparently, his blind learn drive left a gap in his extensive knowledge. Even if this was true, I would never trade Steve Jobs and his opus vitae for a few failures of the local optimization of learning. One of the main points of his [inspiring speech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D1R-jKKp3NA) was to follow one's [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive"). In his words "[_the only way to do great work is to love what you do_](http://news.stanford.edu/2005/06/14/jobs-061505/)". This truth has been repeated by all wise people for millennia.

### Is global optimization possible?

[Global optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_optimization) finds an optimum for all input values. **Global optimization of learning** is done at the level of the department of education, e.g. by means of tools such as [common core](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Common_core "Common core") and [standardized testing](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Standardized_testing "Standardized testing"). Global optimization is based on the flawed reasoning that we can design a child's mind. Global optimization can also be done by parents who attempt to predict a child's future.

Can we determine a child's future in advance? If parents were to choose future globally and optimally, we would have a surplus of lawyers and doctors. We would also have a major increase in frustrated college dropouts. If governments were to help a bit and redistribute the jobs for kids optimally at early age, we would end up with a variant of [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteen_Eighty-Four). Few kids would love to find out at the age of 6 they are set for a life as a book-keeper or a carpenter. Job selection should obviously be based on love and passion, not a government decree.

Perhaps kids should then be allowed to optimize globally? That would not work either, we would end up with a surplus of rock musicians, professional videogamers, and football players.

Contrast this with optimization via the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") that has delivered the best of human achievement for centuries.

Is then a [curriculum](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Curriculum "Curriculum") an attempt to find an intermediary optimum on the way to a global optimum. Curriculum as a guide to what is worth knowing seems like a good idea. When a kid or a teacher runs out of enthusiasm for learning, they might consult the curriculum. If the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") is in overdrive though, why slow down? Is there a risk the kid will never learn the dangers of alcohol? This isn't too likely. On the other hand, I am not aware of a curriculum that teaches kids how to employ [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading"). Fewer kids might choose to solve quadratic equations, but they would fill up that space many times over with other skills they consider important to them. ## Fundamental law of learning

Most people know that learning can be pleasurable. However, very few people appreciate how important this fact is for the [future of education](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Education_reform "Education reform").

Only a constant stream of precious findings in neuroscience helps us see the fundamental importance of pleasure in learning. The reward process begins at the level of [perception](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Biederman_model "Biederman model"), and proceeds via associative learning, to [creativity](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle"), to [problem solving](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/How_to_solve_any_problem%3F "How to solve any problem?"), and the ultimate pleasure of achieving goals. At each station there are pleasure signals to reward the progress of brainwork.

I was slow to understand the power of pleasure too. Back in 1991, [we](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/SuperMemo_World "SuperMemo World") wrote conservatively: _"There is a sure way to tell if a given student will be successful in his work. If he finds pleasure in long-lasting learning sessions, he is bound to do a terrific job"_ (see: [SuperMemo Decalog](http://www.super-memory.com/articles/decalog.htm)). Today, we realize that the pleasure is so inherently associated with all forms of learning in neural networks that it emerges as one of the best yardsticks in measuring learning progress.

This makes it possible to formulate the **fundamental law of declarative learning**:

**When there is no pleasure, there is no good learning.**

Naturally, this law needs to be qualified to be precise. Good declarative learning results in pleasure. This fact can be masked by factors such as the fact that a bit of good learning can hide in [a mass of bad learning](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Unpleasant_learning_at_school "Unpleasant learning at school"). Pleasure itself is no warranty of learning. Facts that we discover can be distressing. Some declarative learning may occur in conditions of displeasure (e.g. fear conditioning). Classical conditioning often involves pain. Clinical depression will impede one's inclination to take on biking, but will not ruin the procedural learning that occurs while biking.

The fundamental law of declarative learning simply states that the acquisition of quality knowledge that satisfies the [learn drive](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Learn_drive "Learn drive") will produce a reward signal. Absence of that signal is an indication of the absence of learning. Dry facts can be committed short-term to declarative memory without having fun, but those facts will not adhere to solid models of reality if there is no reward from learning. Those facts are likely to be eliminated from memory fast by a healthy system of [forgetting](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Forgetting_curve "Forgetting curve"). Even worse, [bad and persistent engrams can cause problems with learning later in life](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Toxic_memory "Toxic memory")! The emergence of any coherent model in memory will inevitably produce a reward signal.

If you happen to impose the suffering on yourself on your own, you need to rethink your strategies. You may need to slow down, or go back to basics, learn the rules of mental and [sleep hygiene](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Natural_creativity_cycle "Natural creativity cycle"), manage your [stress](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Stress_resilience "Stress resilience"), learn the [20 rules of formulating knowledge](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/20_rules "20 rules") or perhaps give [incremental reading](https://supermemo.guru/wiki/Incremental_reading "Incremental reading") a try. If you persist despite pain, you will not be rewarded with good results. [Gladwell's 10,000 hour rule](http://www.newyorker.com/news/sporting-scene/complexity-and-the-ten-thousand-hour-rule) also needs to be qualified. No violin virtuoso has ever been born out of sheer suffering through hours of practice. Like with learning, great music is a child of love.

On the other hand, most of students of this world suffer of no fault of their own. Bad learning is imposed on them from above!

## Summary: Pleasure of learning

-   human brain naturally tunes in to "interesting information" in the environment
-   learning and discovering new things is rewarding
-   many educators subscribe to the dangerous myth that learning may cause displeasure and still be effective
-   surprisal is highly valued in new knowledge acquisition
-   predictability and surprisal may both add to attractiveness of the information channel
-   attractiveness of the information channel depends on the prior knowledge
-   information delivered to the brain must account for prior knowledge. This factor makes universal delivery, e.g. via lecturing, very difficult
-   attractiveness of the information channel depends on the speed of delivery and the speed of processing
-   the speed and complexity of information delivery in learning must to tailored to individual needs
-   the encoding of a new high value associative memory occurs simultaneously with sending a signal to reward centers in the brain
-   failed tailoring of information channels in schooling leads to lack of reward
-   learning provides a unique type of sustainable pleasure that may have therapeutic value
-   for systemic reasons, schooling usually fails to tune in to child interests
-   unrewarding nature of schooling is the chief cause of near-universal dislike of "learning" at school
-   by destroying the pleasure of learning we contribute to creating an unhappy society
-   the fundamental law of declarative learning states: **No pleasure, no learning!**
