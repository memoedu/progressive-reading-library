# Email thoughts on metascience

---

## 正文

_这段文字略经删节，摘自我于 July 17 2022 写给 Heidi Williams 的一封邮件中的笔记，围绕 metascience、_ _一次 metascience workshop，以及我与 Kanjun Qiu 的工作随想而成。_ _这些笔记本身又删节自我的个人笔记。_

-   这里的许多内容适用于几乎所有 metascience，尽管是在这次特定 workshop 的背景下记下的。其中许多内容也围绕 Kanjun Qiu 和我即将完成的一篇广泛讨论 metascience 的 essay 展开随想（我希望很快把 draft 发给你）

-   对我来说，关键的 question（以及 implicit promise）仍然是：我们能否在 human beings make scientific discoveries 的方式上实现 transformative improvements？我仍不确定。在 in-principle process 这一层面，我绝对 \[编者注，应为：/几乎/\] 确信：我们可以做得好得多。但在我们的 institutional ecosystem 能否作出 necessary changes 这一层面，我心存疑虑。我非常希望能在其中一个方向上更加确信。没有这份确信，我对自己该如何贡献力量有很多不确定。我也不太知道怎么才能放下这个该死的问题！它_确实_是一个 civilizational problem。我想，这听起来像是狂妄自大，但我认为这句话在字面上是正确的。

-   大多数 metascience events 从根本上说都是 incremental 的。“如何 fix 某个 problem？”现场往往有许多 operators，他们有时会把现有 institutions 的 operational details 误认为 science 的 fundamental facts。也可以采取一种与之互补、基于 first-principles imaginative design 的 point of view。把两者结合起来会很有意思。从长远来看，我认为 metascience 从根本上说是一门 imaginative design discipline（或者应当以此为志向）。

-   这次 workshop 没有怎么讨论 insiders 与 outsiders 之间的 difference。early modern science 最重要的 innovations 之一，是让 ideas 的 change 得以 decentralize，因此，如果 outsiders 拥有 improved ideas，他们就可能 win over incumbents。在 science 中，这在 ideas 层面运作得相当好，但在 social processes 层面却完全行不通。两个待在车库里的 grad students，不可能仅仅因为自己的 approach 更好，就 start 一所新的 university / funder，并在 25 年间将其 grow 到能够 replace Harvard / NIH 的程度。即便 new ventures 能够被 started，那也是因为某个拥有 power 或 wealth 的人_喜欢_这个 idea，而不是因为它好。Brian Josephson 获得 Nobel Prize（凭借他在 21 岁时提出的一个 idea），不是因为 physics 的前辈们_喜欢_他；他获奖是因为 nature 给出了肯定的回答。

-   与此密切相关的是：许多关于 metascience 的常见 framing，都把它置于为现有 institutions 提供建议、实质上 incremental 的 advisory role。也就是说，这是一种根本上 centralized 的 point of view。但 Netflix 的出现，并不是因为 Blockbuster 听取了 J-PAL-for-entertainment 的建议，而是源于一场 massive crisis，Blockbuster 曾竭尽所能地忽视这场 crisis。人们会反驳：“哦，可那些 startups \[Arc, Altos, 等等\] 呢？”但它们也不是在试图凭借更好来取胜。它们只是依靠 massive infusion of cash，而这笔钱在令人担忧的程度上 independent of 工作的 quality。quality of process 与 growth 之间几乎没有 feedback loop。

-   让我惊讶的是，metascience 没有 canon。随便一个人都可以来到这样的活动，听懂几乎所有谈话。它是一个 proto-field，而不是一个 field；随便一个人若去参加 organic chemistry（或其他什么学科）的 conference，2 分钟后就会听得一头雾水。这不是有意作出 critique，而是一项 observation（而且，和任何 proto-field 一样，这也是一个巨大的 creative opportunity）。

-   与此相关的是：从某种意义上说，这个 field（几乎）没有 results：没有什么是 non-trivial、surprising，而且具有足够 strong 的 evidence，能够 drive 乃至 force action 的。再说一次：它是一个 proto-field，而不是一个 field。不过，我非常喜欢你提出的那一点：其中_确实有_许多有意思的 facts。这极其重要，而我以前并没有理解到它的力量。我最近一直在反复思考这一点。

-   就上一点而言，我所知道的唯一真正 strong 的 exception，是 social psychology 中关于 replication（的缺乏）的 results。这些 results 足够 strong，足以 create 一场 crisis，进而带来一场至今仍在持续的 renaissance。从某种意义上说，这组 results 几乎就是目前 metascience 的全部 canon。Crisis 对 change 至关重要。另外，我对这个 question 深感着迷：怎样才能在 metascience 中 obtain 第二个（在这个意义上的）“strong result”？某种足够 strong、能够 force 一场 crisis 和 change 的东西……

-   这在一定程度上是一次 operators 的活动。我想起 Alan Kay 的 observation：你可以 master Linux，却没能学到多少关于 computers 的东西，因为它是一个巨大的 big ball of mud。同样，许多人把 operating contingent science funding（一个 big ball of mud，尽管其中有许多有意思的 structure）误认为是在触及 science 的某种 fundamental 内容。

-   与此相关的是：总体而言，人们与 science 的脱节程度让我惊讶。几乎没有人谈到它为什么重要、“good”意味着什么，或者 discovery ecosystem 的 goals 应当是什么。大多只是相信“更多 high impact science 就是 good”。但从纸上的涂画、laboratory 里的 devices，到 humanity 的 long-run evolution，其间有一条贯穿始终的联系。然而，这种 connection 几乎完全缺席。我怀疑，只要让某一类 scientist 或 philosopher of science 到场，请他们作某一类 grounding talk，就很容易 remedied 这个问题。

-   我说了很多 critical remarks，但当然，它们都有乐观的另一面：这里有很多唾手可得的成果！

## 原文

_Lightly abridged excerpt from notes in an email to Heidi Williams, July 17 2022, riffing on metascience,_ _a metascience workshop, and my work with Kanjun Qiu._ _Those notes are, in turn, abridged from my personal notes._

-   Much of this applies to almost all metascience, though noted in the context of this particular workshop. Much of it also a riff on Kanjun Qiu's and my forthcoming broad essay on metascience (which I hope to send to you soon in draft)

-   For me, the key question (and implicit promise) remains: can we achieve transformative improvements in the way human beings make scientific discoveries? I'm still unsure. At the level of in-principle process I'm absolutely \[ed, should be: /almost/\] certain: we can do vastly better. At the level of: can our institutional ecosystem make the necessary changes, I have doubts. I'd very much like to arrive at more conviction, one way or the other. Absent that conviction, I have a lot of uncertainty about how to personally contribute. I also don't quite know how to leave the damn problem alone! It _is_ a civilizational problem. I guess that sounds like hubris, but think it's literally correct.

-   Most metascience events are fundamentally incremental. "How to fix such-and-such a problem?" There's often lots of operators present, and they sometimes mistake operational details about existing institutions for fundamental facts about science. There's a complementary first-principles imaginative design point of view possible, too. It'd be fun to mash up both. Over the long run, I think metascience is (or ought to aspire to be) fundamentally an imaginative design discipline.

-   Absent from the workshop was much discussion of the difference between insiders and outsiders. One of the most important innovations of early modern science was to decentralize change in ideas, so if outsiders had improved ideas they could win over incumbents. This works pretty well in science at the level of ideas, but doesn't work at all at the level of social processes. Two grad students in a garage can't start a new university / funder and grow it to replace Harvard / the NIH over 25 years, merely because their approach is better. And even to the extent that new ventures can be started it's because someone in a position of power or wealth _likes_ the idea, not because it's good. Brian Josephson didn't win the Nobel Prize (for an idea he had at age 21) because the elders of physics _liked_ him, he won because nature said yes.

-   Strongly related: Much of the common framing of metascience places it in an essentially incremental advisory role to existing institutions. That is, it's a fundamentally centralized point of view. But Netflix didn't come about because Blockbuster took advice from J-PAL-for-entertainment, it came about due to a massive crisis which Blockbuster did everything it could to ignore. People will retort "oh, but what about startups \[Arc, Altos, etc\]?" But those aren't trying to win by being better, either. They're merely relying on a massive infusion of cash which is (to a worrying degree) independent of the quality of the work. There's almost no feedback loop between quality of process and growth.

-   I find it striking that there is no metascience canon. A rando could show up to an event like this and understand nearly all the conversation. It's a proto-field, not a field; a rando at an organic chemistry (or whatever) conference would be lost after 2 minutes. This isn't intended as a critique; it's an observation (and a huge creative opportunity, as with any proto-field).

-   Related: there's a sense in which the field has (almost) no results: nothing non-trivial and surprising and with strong enough evidence to drive and even force action. Again: it's a proto-field, not a field. I very much like your point that there _are_, however, many interesting facts. This is tremendously important, and not something I had previously understood the power of. I've been thinking about it a lot.

-   The only really strong exception to the last point I know of is the results about (lack of) replication in social psychology. Those results were strong enough to create a crisis, and then an ongoing renaissance. In some sense, that set of results is almost the entire canon for metascience right now. Crisis is essential to change. Also: I'm fascinated by the question: how would one obtain a second "strong result" (in this sense) in metascience? Something strong enough to force a crisis and change…

-   It was in part an operators' event. I'm reminded of Alan Kay's observation that you could master Linux and learn little about computers: it's a great big ball of mud. Similarly, lots of people mistake operating contingent science funding (a big ball of mud, albeit one with lots of interesting structure) for anything fundamental about science.

-   Related: I was in general struck by how detached people were from science. Almost nothing about why it matters, what "good" means, or what the goals of a discovery ecosystem ought to be. Mostly just faith that "more high impact science is good". But there's a through line connecting squiggles on paper and devices in a laboratory to the long-run evolution of humanity. Yet that connection was almost entirely absent. I suspect this is easily remedied by putting a certain type of scientist or philosopher of science in the room, and asking them to give a a particular type of grounding talk.

-   I've made a lot of critical remarks, but of course they all have an optimistic flipside: there's a lot of low-hanging fruit here!
