# Leaving Wave, joining Anthropic

---

## 正文

上周五是我在 [Wave](https://www.wave.com/) 担任 CTO 的最后一天，为不可思议的 ~8 年画上了句号。这些年里，我在职业和个人方面的成长、收获的喜悦与意义，都超出了自己曾经的期望。

这是我做过的最艰难的决定。我对 Wave 的使命与发展方向依然同样激动。而且，大部分重要工作还在前方等着我们：到目前为止，在我们的 potential markets 中，只有一个市场经我们转移的资金超过了 GDP 的 70%。✻✻ 请注意，这个 metric 的 ceiling 远高于 100%，因为有些 money transfer 类型（例如赠礼）并不 count towards GDP。最让我难受的是，我将与许许多多亲爱的朋友、鼓舞人心的同事和了不起的导师告别。

这是一生难得的机会——帮助壮大 Wave 的 engineering team 让我学到了极多东西；而通过参与 build 对数百万 underserved people 至关重要的东西，我有幸产生了超出自己以往预期的 positive impact。很难形容 Wave 对我的意义有多大，不过我希望以后能再写一篇文章，多表达一些。

但在过去几个月里，我有了两个关键的变化，让我开始考虑做些别的事情。

首先，我终于感觉 Wave 已经到了这样一个阶段：engineering work 要顺利进行，不再非我不可。我们 hired 并培养了许多其他出色的人，他们能接手我工作的每一个部分——building product、让它 scale、hiring，以及 building teams and organizations——而不需要我提供太多意见。

其次，最近 AI 的 progress 让我相当担心：这一 transition 是否正朝着有利于社会的方向发展。例如，[large language models](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/2023/01/26/what-are-large-language-models-used-for/) 正在以[比任何人预期都更快的速度，在新的 tasks 上 becoming human-level](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.07785.pdf)，但我们几乎不知道如何对它们的 behavior 提供任何 guarantees；我们已知最好的 safety measures，例如 [ChatGPT](https://openai.com/blog/chatgpt/) 上采用的那些，也都[很容易被 bypass](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7fYxxtZqjuYXhBA2D/testing-ways-to-bypass-chatgpt-s-safety-features)。对于 capability 处于 ChatGPT-ish level 的 model，这令人担忧，但并非 catastrophic。然而，如果你把 [scaling laws](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/midXmMb2Xg37F2Kgn/new-scaling-laws-for-large-language-models) 向未来 extrapolate 一段[短得令人惊讶的时间](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/AfH2oPHCApdKicM4m/two-year-update-on-my-personal-ai-timelines)，情况就开始显得[可怕](https://www.cold-takes.com/ai-could-defeat-all-of-us-combined/)了。†† 如果想更 rigorous、更 in-depth 地了解这背后的 reasoning，我推荐 Holden Karnofsky 关于 [The Most Important Century（最重要的世纪）](https://www.cold-takes.com/most-important-century/)及[其 implications](https://www.cold-takes.com/tag/implicationsofmostimportantcentury/) 的系列文章。Holden 是说服我认识到 mitigating risks from advanced AI 之重要性的主要人物之一：他是我认识的最谨慎、最 rigorous 的思考者之一，所以，当他的看法从“这似乎很离谱，也不太可能”转变成“我把大部分时间都花在担忧这件事上”时，确实引起了我的注意。

正因如此，虽然改变方向从来没有什么_好_时机，但现在似乎是迈出这一步最不坏的时机。

* * *

我查看了哪些 organizations 正在从事 AI alignment，并从中筛选出那些看起来最能用上我在 Wave 培养的 engineering leadership skills 的 organizations，最后明显脱颖而出的是 [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/)。Anthropic builds large language models，与 ChatGPT 背后的那个类似，‡‡ Anthropic 没有同类的 public product，但确实有一个（“Claude”）正处于 private beta，你可以看到有人[在 twitter 上](https://twitter.com/search?q=anthropic%20claude)发布 examples。并针对它们开展 [safety research](https://www.anthropic.com/#papers)——例如，[对它们进行 training，让它们更加 helpful、更少 harmful](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)，以及[更加 honest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221)。

> 补充说明，以免助长任何潜在的 [information cascade](https://www.lesswrong.com/tag/information-cascades)：我想强调，这并不意味着我个人强烈认可 Anthropic 所做的一切，而且我现阶段对 Anthropic 的期待，很大程度上来自我信任的其他人的 vetting，而不是我自己的 first-principles reasoning。
> 
> 具体来说，例如，Anthropic 很可能至少在一定程度上 accelerating 了为 develop transformative AI 而展开的[“穿越雷区的竞赛”](https://www.cold-takes.com/racing-through-a-minefield-the-ai-deployment-problem/)。他们试图审慎地考虑如何 balance 这一 trade-off（例如，[暂缓其 chat model 的 release](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PTzsEQXkCfig9A6AS/transcript-of-sam-altman-s-interview-touching-on-ai-safety?commentId=KZSQgHQPM6n8vA2Ka)），向我提供建议的人大多认为，他们的存在值得付出这份代价，但也有一些人相当不确定。
> 
> 我希望自己最终能够基于 first-principles，对我认可或不认可的事情形成自己的看法，但这里牵涉的因素很多，而我对这个领域还不太熟悉，所以我预计需要一段时间，才能充分形成自己的理解。

除了他们的使命，Anthropic 最让我兴奋的是团队的素质：我最初听说他们，是因为他们雇用了[更多](https://colah.github.io/)的[人](https://thume.ca/)，让我[觉得](https://nelhage.com/)[特别](https://twitter.com/catherineols)[酷](https://nicholasschiefer.com/)，超过了[任何](https://kipp.ly/blog/)[其他](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=6-e-ZBEAAAAJ&hl=en)[公司](https://twitter.com/danielaamodei)。§§ Wave 除外；它有一个不公平的优势，因为 product/engineering team 里的每个人我都认识。这样的高标准，让他们在短短的时间（~2 年）里，以这样的规模，做出了令人惊叹的成绩！

虽然 Anthropic 的团队目前还相对较小，但正在迅速壮大。我很期待尝试运用在 Wave 学到的东西，帮助他们应对这种快速成长。我还不确切知道自己最后会做什么，但我的计划是先到那里，从看起来最有用的事情开始做，再以此为基础 iterate，就像上次一样。

今年晚些时候，我还会搬到湾区（很可能是伯克利），到现场工作，所以也想结交新朋友——如果你在附近，也有兴趣见面，就来[打个招呼](https://www.benkuhn.net/hi/)吧！

## 原文

Last Friday was my last day as CTO at [Wave](https://www.wave.com/), capping an incredible ~8 years filled with more professional and personal growth, joy, and meaning than I could have hoped for.

This was the hardest decision I’ve made. I’m still just as excited about Wave’s mission and trajectory. And most of the important work is still ahead of us: as of yet there’s only one of our potential markets where we move over 70% of GDP.✻✻ Note that the ceiling for this metric is much more than 100%, because some types of money transfer (e.g. gifts) don’t count towards GDP. Most wrenchingly, I’ll be saying goodbye to a huge number of dear friends, inspiring colleagues, and incredible mentors.

It’s been the opportunity of a lifetime—helping grow Wave’s engineering team has taught me a tremendous amount, and by helping build something that’s important to millions of underserved people, I’ve been lucky to have more positive impact than I ever expected to be able to. It’s hard to describe how much Wave has meant to me, though I hope to capture more in a future post.

But over the last few months, I’ve had two key shifts that made me think about working on something else.

First, it finally feels like Wave is at a point where I’m not critical for engineering work to go smoothly. We’ve hired and grown lots of other amazing people who can take over every part of my job—building product, making it scale, hiring, and building teams and organizations—without needing much input.

Second, recent progress in AI has made me pretty worried about whether that transition is on track to go well for society. For example, [large language models](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/2023/01/26/what-are-large-language-models-used-for/) are [becoming human-level at new tasks faster than anyone expected](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.07785.pdf), but we don’t know how to provide almost any guarantees about their behavior; our best known safety measures, like those on [ChatGPT](https://openai.com/blog/chatgpt/), are [easy to bypass](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/7fYxxtZqjuYXhBA2D/testing-ways-to-bypass-chatgpt-s-safety-features). For a model with a ChatGPT-ish level of capability, this is concerning but not catastrophic. But if you extrapolate the [scaling laws](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/midXmMb2Xg37F2Kgn/new-scaling-laws-for-large-language-models) out a [surprisingly short time](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/AfH2oPHCApdKicM4m/two-year-update-on-my-personal-ai-timelines), things start to look [scary](https://www.cold-takes.com/ai-could-defeat-all-of-us-combined/).†† For a more rigorous, in-depth overview of the reasoning behind this, I recommend Holden Karnofsky’s series of articles on [The Most Important Century](https://www.cold-takes.com/most-important-century/) and [its implications](https://www.cold-takes.com/tag/implicationsofmostimportantcentury/). Holden is one of the main people who convinced me of the importance of mitigating risks from advanced AI: he’s one of the most careful and rigorous thinkers I know, so when he updated from “this seems wild and unlikely” to “I’m spending most of my time worrying about this,” it really got my attention.

Because of this, while there’s never a _good_ time to change direction, now felt like the least-worst time to take a leap.

* * *

When I looked at what organizations were working on AI alignment, and filtered down to the ones where it looked like the engineering leadership skills I’ve built at Wave could be most useful, the clear standout ended up being [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/). Anthropic builds large language models, similar to the one behind ChatGPT,‡‡ Anthropic doesn’t have a comparable public product, but they do have one (“Claude”) in private beta, and you can find people posting examples [on twitter](https://twitter.com/search?q=anthropic%20claude). and does [safety research](https://www.anthropic.com/#papers) on them—for example, [training them to be more helpful, less harmful](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862), and [more honest](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221).

> Sidebar to avoid contributing to any potential [information cascade](https://www.lesswrong.com/tag/information-cascades): I want to emphasize that this doesn’t mean I personally strongly endorse everything Anthropic does, and that much of my excitement about Anthropic at this point comes from the vetting of other people I trust, not from my own first-principles reasoning.
> 
> In particular, for example, Anthropic probably has at least some effect of accelerating the [“race through the minefield”](https://www.cold-takes.com/racing-through-a-minefield-the-ai-deployment-problem/) to develop transformative AI. They try to be thoughtful about how to balance this trade-off (e.g. [holding back the release of their chat model](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PTzsEQXkCfig9A6AS/transcript-of-sam-altman-s-interview-touching-on-ai-safety?commentId=KZSQgHQPM6n8vA2Ka)), and most of the people I got advice from thought their existence was worth the trade, but some were quite uncertain.
> 
> I intend to get to the point where I can have my own first-principles takes on what I do/don’t endorse, but there are a lot of moving parts here and I’m not very familiar with the space, so I expect it to take a while for me to flesh out my understanding.

Aside from their mission, the thing that makes me most excited about Anthropic is the quality of the team: I originally heard of them because they employed [more](https://colah.github.io/) [people](https://thume.ca/) I [thought](https://nelhage.com/) were [super](https://twitter.com/catherineols) [cool](https://nicholasschiefer.com/) than [any](https://kipp.ly/blog/) [other](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=6-e-ZBEAAAAJ&hl=en) [company](https://twitter.com/danielaamodei).§§ Besides Wave, which has an unfair advantage since I knew everyone on the product/engineering team. That high bar has let them do an impressive amount for their size in a short time (~2 years)!

While Anthropic’s team is relatively small right now, it’s growing quickly, and I’m excited to try to help navigate that rapid growth using what I’ve learned at Wave. I don’t know exactly what I’ll end up doing, but my plan is to show up, start working on whatever seems most useful, and iterate from there, the same way I did last time.

I’ll also be moving to the Bay Area (likely Berkeley) later this year to work onsite, so will be in the market for new friends—[say hi](https://www.benkuhn.net/hi/) if you’re in the area and interested in meeting up!
