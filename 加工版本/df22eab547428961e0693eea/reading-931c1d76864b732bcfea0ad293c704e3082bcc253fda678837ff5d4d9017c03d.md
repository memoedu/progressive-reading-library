简短说一声：Larissa MacFarquhar 的书 _Strangers Drowning_ 再过几天就要出版了，现在已经[可以在 Amazon 预订](http://www.amazon.com/Strangers-Drowning-Grappling-Impossible-Overpowering-ebook/dp/B00SI02AWA/?tag=s4charity-20)。这本书是一系列关于“extreme do-gooders”的人物特写，这些人的生活完全由自己的 morals 感所驱动，到了在大多数人看来近乎疯狂的地步——比如 adopting 二十个 disabled children，或把自己的全部 income donating 给 charity，又或者拒绝洗碗，因为花在洗碗上的时间，就没有用来 averting 数十亿只鸡的 suffering。

我无比期待这本书周二到来。我的社交圈经常抽象地谈论 morality，但大多不是从人们实际上如何践行它的角度来谈。我很想看看 MacFarquhar 笔下的人物如何思考、如何生活。

到目前为止，书评都赞不绝口。如果你想先睹为快，各家刊物上已经有一些节选或相关文章：

-   [_The Children of Strangers_](http://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2015/08/03/the-children-of-strangers)，讲述了一个 adopted 了许多孩子的家庭。

-   [《卫报》的一篇文章](http://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/sep/22/extreme-altruism-should-you-care-for-strangers-as-much-as-family)，主要讲 Julia Wise（也顺带提到了 effective altruism community 中的其他一些人）。

## 原文

A short note: Larissa MacFarquhar’s book _Strangers Drowning_ is coming out in a few days and is [up for preorder now on Amazon](http://www.amazon.com/Strangers-Drowning-Grappling-Impossible-Overpowering-ebook/dp/B00SI02AWA/?tag=s4charity-20). It’s series of profiles of “extreme do-gooders,” people whose lives are completely driven by their sense of morals, to the point of seeming crazy to most people—like adopting twenty disabled children, or donating their entire income to charity, or refusing to do the dishes because time spent doing dishes was time not spent averting the suffering of billions of chickens.

I’m incredibly excited for the book to come on Tuesday. My social circles talk abstractly about morality a lot, but mostly not from the perspective of how people actually live it. I’m interested to see how MacFarquhar’s subjects think and live.

The reviews so far have positively glowed. If you want a preview, there are some excerpts/related pieces floating around various publications:

-   [_The Children of Strangers_](http://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2015/08/03/the-children-of-strangers) about a family that adopted a huge number of kids.

-   [A Guardian piece](http://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/sep/22/extreme-altruism-should-you-care-for-strangers-as-much-as-family) principally focusing on Julia Wise (and incidentally mentioning some other folks in the effective altruism community as well).
