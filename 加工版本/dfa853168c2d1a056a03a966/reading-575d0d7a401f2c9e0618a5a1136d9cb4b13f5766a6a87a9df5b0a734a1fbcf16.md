# 当 work 毫无意义时，work 的未来

每个人都在担心 AI 是否会取代自己。

但我不禁觉得，human experience 中有些东西是无法被取代的。

我不禁觉得，人类仍然会想要 work。也就是说，他们仍然会想要 create something，与他人 share it，并通过获得某种形式的 currency 作为回报，让自己的 contribution 得到认可。这是 human nature 的一个 fundamental aspect，但令人遗憾的是，随着 productivity 成了我们的 God，“work”已经变成了一个被剥去 meaning 的肮脏词汇。

我不太担心 industrial factory-style work 被 automated。我们都知道，那个没有灵魂的深坑会把人变成什么样。

我更担心 creative work。

那个 future 会是什么样？

money 会变得 obsolete 吗？

AGI 会写出所有 prose 并创作所有 art 吗？

如果 struggle、status 和 curiosity 是 meaning 的 generators，而 AGI 承诺要把我们从这些东西中解脱出来，那我们该怎么办？

AGI 有什么东西是无法取代的？如果有的话。

在一个没有 scarcity 的 world 里，你如何成为那个 scarce good？

我想分享关于 work 的 future 的 6 个想法，谈谈作为 creative，你必须优化的 skills 和 traits，以及最终如何在一个令人望而生畏的 future 里 live a meaningful life。我会分享自己的想法，以及我认为具有相关分量的 theories。

如果你是一位 creative，想知道 AI 是否会取代你的 work，那么这篇文章就是写给你的。

我们开始吧。

### I – meaning crisis 让 meaning 值得付费

Meaning 已经成了 scarce good。

这一点不难看出来。

不过，要理解我们是如何陷入这场混乱的，简单回顾一下历史会有所帮助。

你看，reality 会随着时间推移，朝着更大的 complexity 发展。当事物变得过于 complex 或 chaotic 时，新的 ordered structure（或一个包容各个部分的新整体）就必须出现。

几个简单的例子：

Letter → Word → Sentence → Paragraph

Atom → Cell → Molecule → Organism

Matter → Life → Mind → Spirit

reality 就是这样 structured 的。

谈到 meaning 时，我想聚焦于 societies 如何 evolved，这可以从两个方面来看：

**1)** **过去 societies 的 techno-economic base（或 dominant mode of production）以及所用的 specific technology。**

_Foraging → Horticultural → Agrarian → Industrial → Informational → Whatever comes next (Intelligence?)_

**2)** **这些 societies 中人们的 dominant worldview 和 value structure（他们的 meaning-making system）。**

_Premodern → Modern → Postmodern → Whatever comes next (Metamodern?)_

为了简洁起见，我们可以快速讲过前几个阶段。

Foraging societies 是 hunter-gatherers。很简单。在 horticultural societies 中，男人是 hunters，女人则照料小块土地。在 agrarian societies 中，食物来自大规模 farming，由此产生大量 surplus，让男人可以少 work 一些，从而有更多时间去 explore、discover 和 conquer。

这些 societies 的 technologies 是这样发展的：

_Axes, spears, and fire → Hoe and digging sticks → Animal drawn plow (from human labor to animal labor)._

这些 societies 大多都处于 **premodern** worldview 之中。

换句话说，meaning 由更高力量（elders、scriptures、kings、priests）赋予。别人告诉你该相信什么。你选择 conform。Agency 是一种可能让你被杀害或驱逐的 trait。

随后 **Industrial** Age 到来，food production 来自 mechanized agriculture。更少的 farmers 养活更多的人，food 变成了 commodity。主要 technologies 是 steam、coal 和 oil。_Human and animal labor 的需求大幅减少。_

> 这里有一个关键 insight：society 的 techno-economic base 会创造条件，让新的 worldview 和 value system 能够大规模形成。

industrial society 让重视 rationality 的 **modern** worldview 得以确立。Meaning 通过 reason、science 和 evidence 被发现，而非从 tradition 继承。Nature 不再神秘，progress 取代 divine，merit 取代 birthright。

事情就是从这里开始走偏的。

*   首先，productivity 从很小的年纪起就被过度强调。你一出生，别人就已经期待你去上学、找工作，成为机器中的一个齿轮。从一开始，你就被像 robot 一样 programmed。

*   其次，我们与 tribe、village 和 community 脱离了联系。我们独自置身于一个充斥着 artificial connection 的庞大 digital world。

*   第三，religious 和 spiritual frameworks 被 universe 的 mechanical model 取代。Meaning 不再由 divine 赋予。

*   第四，我们开始把 agency 外包给 institutions，并忘记了 self-directed work 的价值。9-5 jobs 取代了 artisans 和 farmers 的 work。

这些都导致 _what we do and how we work_ 遭到扭曲。Work 被拆解成简单、重复的 tasks，让 workers 对整个 process 一无所知，因此无法独立复现（这就是为什么 generalists 总能胜过 specialists，[正如我们讨论过的](https://letters.thedankoe.com/p/if-you-have-multiple-interests-do)）。

换句话说，meaningless work 成了我们唯一的 focus 和生存手段。严重到我们无法用其他方式看待这一切；当 AI 把这种 cancer 从我们身上移除时，许多人会遭遇严酷的觉醒。

接下来是 **Information** Age，computer 的发明使我们进一步脱离 labor。如今，我们大多坐在办公桌前 work，而不是照料田地，并让 machines 承担繁重的工作。

如今的 dominant worldview 是 **Postmodern,** 我们已经开始 deconstruct 之前的一切。

postmodernism 的关键 insight 是 _aperspectivalism_，也就是说，没有任何 perspective 享有特权。所有 views 都有其所处的位置、局限性和 context。这是一项了不起的成就，因为我们现在知道，_one view_ 并非唯一真实且绝对的 view（无论在 religious、political 等方面），不必人人都 conform 于它。

然而，这项成就反而进一步加速了 meaning crisis，严重到我们开始醒悟：有些事情必须改变。

它还带来了一个矛盾，而这个矛盾必须在我们即将进入的下一个 development stage（age of intelligence）中解决：

如果没有任何 perspective 比另一个更好、更真实或发展得更成熟，那么声称所有 perspectives 都同样有效，_本身就是一种你声称更好的 perspective_。这是一种 value ranking。你通过创造自己的 hierarchies 来 deconstruct 所有 hierarchies。

> Postmodernism 正确地认识到没有 perspective 是绝对的，却错误地得出结论：没有 perspective 更好。

事实上，有些 perspectives 确实更好。

这为什么重要？

首先，_meaning_ 实际上是这样 evolved 的：

Meaning 来自 “Up There”（the Gods）→ Meaning 来自 “Out There”（productivity 和 progress）→ Meaning 来自 “Nowhere”（supposed equality）→ 接下来是什么（Meaning 来自 “In Here?”）

其次，_taste_ 是一项核心 skill，在未来会很重要；它要求你判断一件事比另一件更好。它要求你有所排除。

第三，_agency_ 也正在成为一项核心 survival skill。过去真正有意识地 embody 它的人寥寥无几。

第四，你个人的 _perspective_ 或许正是 AGI 无法取代的那一样东西。它或许就是你在 creative work 中的 competitive edge。

第五，creatives 是 society 的 meaning-architects；如果连同洗澡水一起把婴儿也泼掉（jobs），结果可能是灾难性的。

最后，我们正处于 chaos 的中心，新的 structure 必须随之出现。我们可以参与塑造 future。这是我大量写作的一个重要原因。

此外，productivity 已不再是可靠的 identity。

没人知道该走哪条 path，或该学什么 skill。

Meaning 已降至历史最低点，certainty 和 security 也是如此。

那么，下一个 stage 是什么？

我们_能_学些什么，才能确保自己不会在进入这个新 world 时处于巨大劣势？

要理解这一点，我们必须理解正在出现的 techno-economic base（artificial intelligence），因为它会创造条件，让下一个 worldview 得以形成，进而决定我们重视什么，甚至进一步决定_我们做什么_。

### II – AI（以及 meaninglessness）的加速

AI 承诺让我们摆脱所有 labor。

至少，炒作的全部内容以及大家关注的都是这个。

它承诺提供必需品，让我们不再生活在 scarcity 中。

但无论从哪方面看，它实现这一点的方式只会 _increases the scarcity of meaning._ 许多人，包括我自己，都会从某种 labor 中获得 meaning。我一点也不反 AI，但我看不到一个 AI 会从 creative work 中移除 craft 的世界。

但为什么？

work 为什么会消失？

jobs 的 economic function 又会由什么取代？

要全面了解情况，David Shapiro 的 [Post Labor Economics](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLV3Fr1UUO9bFTYDqL9KIjrdlurcoMlLxg) 是一个有用的 theory，可以帮助我们理解未来可能是什么样子。

每个人都在思考的问题是：

自从 jobs 成为一种常态（以 wage 工作）以来，companies 支付 workers，workers 花掉 money，companies 赚到 money，这个 cycle 就这样持续下去。

但后来 AI 出现了，并威胁到这一切。

作为一家公司，让 machine 完成你所有 work 的想法很诱人。Humans 成本高、复杂、情绪化，而且有 legal risk。直到现在，唯一的选择就是雇用 humans。

一旦 AI 和 robots 对某个 job 跨过“better, faster, cheaper, safer”的 threshold（姑且假设它们确实能达到那一点；眼下我们只能猜测），继续让 humans 做这项 work 在经济上就不 rational 了。Irrational 并不意味着 immoral 或 optional。我不是来评判这样做的 morality 的。

这之所以不 rational，是因为 hoe 取代了 digging stick。animal drawn plow 取代了 hoe（并让一类新的 explorers 和 conquerers 得以出现）。Industrial machines 取代了 plow。随着每一次 evolution，使用这些 tools 的个人，其 power 和 potential 都有所提升。在今天，一个人经营的 business 可以比过去许多 corporations 更精简、更 profitable。你可以学习任何东西、构建任何东西；但即使拥有这种 power，大多数人仍会什么都不做，而那些行动的人会彼此陷入无休止的 competition，让我们所有人都在追问，一个人的 competitive advantage 究竟在哪里。

当然，AI 取代所有 jobs 会带来一个大问题。

_如果所有人都被解雇，就没有人有 money。没有人有 money，就没人会买任何东西。没人买任何东西，economy 就会崩溃。_

我们会发现，上面这句话适用于有 jobs 的人，而不是有 companies 的人；这让我不禁想起 Naval 的这句话：

> 这个星球上有近 7B 人。希望有一天，也会有近 7B 家 companies。

现在，大多数人担心的是，如果没有 job，他们怎么获得报酬。households 获得 money 只有以下三种方式：

1.  **Wages** – 有人付钱给你，换取你的 labor（employer、customer、self-employment）

2.  **Transfers** – Government 付钱给你（UBI、Social Security、SNAP、Medicare）

3.  **Capital Income** – 你的 assets 为你带来收入（dividends、rent、interest、appreciation）

如果第一种方式崩溃，第二种和第三种就必须承担重担。但单靠 transfers 会造成 political instability，并使 price signals 失灵。它根本无法作为整体解决方案。

也就是说，一个潜在解决方案是扩大 capital participation，让普通人拥有能产生 income 的 assets。

为简洁起见，我不会列出所有相关的 proposed mechanisms。因为事实是，有些人，尤其是 creatives，仍然会想 work，并希望自己的 work 得到认可。这正是我们要讨论的。

就我个人而言，我不想坐在那里，只领取一笔基本水平的 cash。我的 life 中，meaning 很大一部分来自在各个 life domains 中实现 personal 和 professional growth，而 growth 需要 agency；如果 money 从 productivity metric 转变为一种 _表达 agency 的方式_，那就是我想走的 path。

我建议你进一步了解 [Shapiro 的 ideas](https://www.youtube.com/@DaveShap)，并关注他，等他那本关于这个 topic 的全面著作 Labor Zero 出版。

幸运的是，即使 automation 更优，一些 jobs 仍可能继续存在。这些 jobs 的基础是 humans 特别要求由其他 humans 来做：

*   High-liability roles（出了问题有人担责的岗位）

*   Statutory positions（法律要求必须由 humans 担任的职位）

*   **Experience economy**（bartenders、boutiques、art）

*   **Meaning makers**（帮助人们 navigate human experience）

*   Relationship/trust jobs（sales、diplomacy、negotiation）

作为一名日常创作者，我想聚焦第三和第四种选择。

_这正是 humans 愿意支付溢价的东西。_

但在讨论如何加入新的 meaning economy，或需要哪些 high-level skills 之前，我们漏掉了一件关键的事：meaning 最初是如何生成的。

### III – meaning 的 evolution 与 anatomy（robots 与 humans）

> 未来的优雅之处，不在于 man 与 machine 的对抗，而在于两者的 division of labor：silicon 打磨 necessity 的粗糙边缘，让 carbon 得以 ascends to meaning。我们将废除 baristas，将 chefs 奉为典范；让 agents 沉默，让 actors 谢幕再返场。两种领域中驱动这一切的，都是同一种自利本能——消除摩擦，保留叙事——它推动着一个这样的世界：最枯燥的杂务由 circuits 完成，最有滋味的 stories 则由会流血的人讲述。
>
> – Chris Paik

历史上大多数时候，humans 都是仰望天空来寻找 meaning——或者说，寻找活下去的理由。

“在上方。”

Meaning 是 _被给予的_。

后来，据说我们变聪明了。我们把 productivity 奉为 God。我们从 science 和 reason 中寻找 meaning。

“在外部。”

Meaning 是 _靠努力获得的_。

如今，我们变得过于聪明，反而不利于自己。我们试图在 relativity 中寻找 meaning，却没能找到。

“无处。”

Meaning 被 _解构了_。

迈向未来时，你的工作就是拾起这些碎片。你必须接受自己身为 creator 的角色。成为 tool builder。成为 explorer。成为 problem solver。如果你把太多 agency 外包给 machines，它们很乐意让你接上集体的 dopamine IV。

现在，meaning 必须 _被生成_。

你如何生成 meaning？

嗯，看看是什么造成了 meaninglessness 会有所帮助。

首先，meaninglessness 源于 stagnation。如果你对生活无所作为——就像许多人在 jobs 消失、自己又恰好有足够的钱维持生活后会做的那样——你并不会停留在原地。你会慢慢陷入 chaos。Entropy 也能在 mind 中观察到。

其次，isolation 会加剧 meaninglessness。没有 tribes 和 villages，我们比以往任何时候都更孤独。盯着屏幕，等着下一次 paycheck，好让你继续买买买。

于是我们可以说，meaning 的两大支柱是 _向前运动的感觉，以及与比自己更宏大的事物建立连接。_

Progress 和 contribution。

很长一段时间里，我们把两者都外包了。employer 给予 progress，divine authority 给予 connection。如今，两者都掌握在你手中，而许多人并不会因此感到自在。

Progress 和 contribution 通过 creative problem solving 得以实现。你设定一个 future aim，找出阻碍你前进的问题，通过 experimentation 创造 solution，再把它传给处境相似的另一个人；如果那个人认为它足够有价值，就会以另一种 value 作为交换回馈给你。

当然，这还不能帮助我们理解，如果所有 jobs 都消失，creatives 要如何生存；我们马上就会谈到这一点。

我的主张是，你必须成为 creator。你必须建立自己的东西。你必须走自己的路。你必须表达自己的 agency。无论如何都不要坐等那一天——等着世界承诺替你解决所有问题，因为我向你保证，它不会。

除了 meaning 的两大支柱，还有三个 generators：

1.  **Struggle** – progress 的 engine。你选择为之 struggle（或在意）的东西，就是你的 purpose。

2.  **Curiosity** – progress 的方向。为了 solve problems 而非线性地选择 attention。

3.  **Status** – contribution 的证明。完成这个 loop 的 recognition。

把这些都放在一起，你就得到一个 _story_。

_Stories，真实的 stories，才会让人愿意支付溢价。_

因为 human brain 就是一台 story engine。

我们就是这样理解这个世界的。

当某件事本该很快却进展缓慢时，我们会感到厌烦。

我们讨厌 DMV 的长队。我们讨厌迟到、还爱闲聊的 Uber Driver。我们讨厌在 hold 上等 30 minutes，只为询问一个本该很简单的 support question。我们讨厌快餐订单出错。

但我们愿意花大价钱飞越半个地球，到一家 five star restaurant 用餐。

在剧院看一出催人泪下的 play 时，我们会坐在座位边缘，屏息凝神。

如果任务关乎 speed、accuracy 或 utility，就交给 machines。但当我们的 mind 不再处于“必须做完某件事”的状态时（当我们投入 leisure 时，未来这种时光会很多），我们渴望的就完全不同了。

我们渴望失败的可能性。

我们渴望那个流血之人的 lessons。

我们渴望 story、drama、novelty、myth 和 meaning，也愿意为此付出真金白银。

整个 economy 都将围绕这一点建立起来，而建设已经开始。

那么，_你_究竟有什么独特之处？我们又如何能确定 machines 不会取代这一切？

### IV – creator economy = meaning economy

如果你没有 job 支付的收入，也不觉得收集刚好足以满足 necessities 的 passive income 有意义，那么还剩下什么？

_让那些相信你所做之事、并希望看到它在世界上出现得更多的人付钱给你。_

你追求自己所_care_的事，并激励人们也去关心它。

那么，Attention 就是 creatives 将要争夺的 scarce resource。

我们已经看到这种情况在 creator economy 中上演。

Elon Musk 是个显而易见的例子，他懂得 Attention 的原始力量。借助它，他确实能塑造 future。他能调动 capital、talent 和 networks。他是确保自己毕生事业——成为 interplanetary——得以实现的所有资源中最重要的资源。

Mr. Beast 也是如此。他是 attention capture 的高手，会把这些注意力重新投入 production，从而带来更多 Attention。

想想今天你如何获取 education、news 和 knowledge。大部分内容都来自 social media 上的 creators，他们把提供自己 point of view 当作工作。Centralized education 和 information 仍然广泛存在，但这种情况不会持续太久。

不过，我不想成为下一个 Mr. Beast。你大概也不想承受 Musk 面临的风险和批评。你只是想追求自己的 interests，并与一群和你一样在乎你的 vision 的人分享，就像你的 brain 天生就适合这样。你想要 generate 并分享 meaning。

关于 creator economy，最大的误解是它是一场 winner takes all 的战场。事实远非如此。

Justin Welsh 是我在这个领域的一位朋友，人们知道他向往平静的生活。他想要有意义的 work、充足的家庭时间，以及不受 financial stress 影响、按自己意愿行事的能力。他过得相当不错，而且完全能够掌控自己“工作”的时长。

但你甚至不必做到那么大。有很多人虽然 followings 不多，收入却足以过上很好的生活。他们不会每天苦干 16 小时，也不在乎成为 billionaires。他们只是有自己认为有意义的东西，并把它分享出来（同时搭配让他们能够获得 Attention 的特定 skills，后文会谈到）。你每天都能看到这些人。顺便说一句，如果你想成为 billionaire 或者极端长时间工作，请便，有些人喜欢这样的生活方式，我无意告诉你该怎么生活。

“But Dan，dead internet 又该怎么说？AI 难道不会用 brain rot 和 slop 淹没这个领域吗？”

是的，确实会。

这有什么不好？

你的意思是，所有人都在用平庸的 content 淹没这个领域，所以脱颖而出从未如此容易？我认为，人们对 AI slop 的大多数担忧，都源于不理解 Attention 如何运作。

_一旦人人都能瞬间做到，它就会瞬间变得一文不值。_

当然，任何人都可以让 AI 写 1000 条 posts 或 10 篇 articles，去碰运气赌 algorithm lottery，但你究竟多常看到“slop”真的 go viral？如果它真的 viral 了，那不就说明它不是 slop 吗？

Slop 存在于一个 spectrum 上。上周我 mega viral 时，有人把我的 article 贬成“不过是一堆 self-help 陈词滥调”；但如果你真的读过这篇 article，就知道这种说法有些牵强——毕竟其中还有关于 psychology、epistemology 和 human behavior 的章节。没有任何 AI prompt 能生成完全相同的那篇 article，甚至也无法生成这篇 article。倒不是说它是地球上最深刻的写作，但这让我不禁想问，为什么一点 self-help 就能让某些人勃然大怒——难道 self-help 不能帮到你吗？聪明人往往会对自己擅长的 domains 存在偏见，这是一种相当低效的思维方式。坦白说，一旦一段 content 变得“popular”，自认为 anti-mainstream 的人就会立刻找理由讨厌它。一个人的 slop 是另一个人的珍宝，反之亦然。

撇开 Slop 不谈，你能使用 ChatGPT，对吧？_那你为什么还在读这篇文章？_是因为你不知道该在 chatbot 里输入什么吗？是因为生活中不只有回答那些你_知道_该问的问题吗？你觉得有一天 ChatGPT 会把这篇一模一样的 article 像摆在银盘上一样递给你吗？认真想想这些问题，再开始说一切都注定完蛋。

事实是，skills 正在被提升到更高一层的 abstraction。

在页面上打字或给画作添加笔触这样的 manual task 仍然重要，因为把所有东西都汇总到 chat input 里，会让那些需要 personal touch 的 work 失去 agency；但它们的重要性仍不及完成这些事情的 artist 的 mind。

marketing、persuasion、writing、digital art、programming 等 skills 仍然重要，但最能取得成果的人是在更高层次上运作。

他们是在 human level 上运作。

### V – 最后一道可防御的 moat 就是_you_

作为一名 writer，过去几年我一直在深入思考这件事。

AI 终究能完全取代 creatives 和 artists 吗？无论怎么解释，这似乎都相当 bleak。

AI 终究能写出我写的东西吗？

AI 终究能创作出一位著名 musician 的音乐吗？

AI 终究能制作出 Spielberg 水准的 film 吗？

AI 终究能像 Steve Jobs 借助 Apple 那样打造一家公司吗？

我得出的答案是……能，但不能。

很多人会争辩说，AI 能生成优美的 essay 或 film，尤其是随着 models 越来越好；但他们忽略了某个至关重要的东西。

我们把这称为**The Swap Test：**

_如果你可以交换 creator 和 creation，而它们的价值仍然一样，那么 AI 就能取代它。_

如果 creation 只有在由_you_创作时才成立，那就是你的 edge。它承载着你的 perspective、你的 situation 和你的 taste。

你看，某些_work 类型_有一个 objective floor。

说到 writing，让 AI 写 documentation、总结多个 information sources，或把 instructions 写得更清楚，完全没问题。说真的，我甚至会主张，AI 可以把你原始想法的 brain dump 整理成可 publish 的内容；不过，有些人也许还想表达自己特有的 style。

换句话说，如果 speed 和 efficiency 比 voice 更重要，你可以把 control 交给 AI。

但说到写一篇讲述失去父母后悲伤经历的 personal essay，或写一篇主张 minimalism 是 creatives 陷阱的 article，起作用的东西就不一样了。

起作用的是你的_point of view_。

你知道，这是这个星球上最独特的东西，没有其他人能获得它。

这是 beliefs、ideas 和 experiences 的汇聚，它们从你出生那天起便逐渐形成，_无法逆转或重新模拟_。

所以问题来了：如果任何人只需一个 prompt 就能复制你的 writing、product、music 或 art，你什么时候才应该_不_把 control 交给 AI？

答案就在于 AI 无法获取的东西。

*   AI 无法_从_你的 perspective 思考。它只能思考关于你的 perspective，或以你的 perspective 的 style 思考。

*   AI 无法复制你的 energy signature。它无法选择此刻该关注什么，以及这件事对_you_为何重要。

*   AI 无法进行 genuine sensemaking。它无法决定 information 意味着什么、什么重要，或该如何 framing。

*   AI 没有_trajectory_。它没有死亡带来的 stakes。即使 mortality 成为一个已解决的问题，对人类而言，一个 moment 一旦过去，就结束了。AI 可以模拟 stories，但 time 不像对我们那样，充当一种 natural compression algorithm。

*   AI 没有 evolving taste。它不会回顾自己一年前写的东西并表示不同意，也不会成长。

换句话说，AI 可以复制任何东西，但在某件事已经发生之前，它无法复制接下来会发生什么。

作为 creative，你拥有一个始终 evolving 的 perspective。AI 总是在追赶过去的你，却追不上现在的你。

等到 AI 复制你的时候——这可能转瞬即成——你已经继续向前了。

“great work”被视为一个 moving target，会随 society 和 culture 不断演变。对 creatives 而言，发现自己所想的过程本身就是创作过程。

_如果 AI 此刻能完美地像我一样写作，那么作为一个固执的人类，我就不再想写这类东西了。我在乎的东西会立刻改变。_

当我试着让 AI 像我一样写作时，我立刻就不喜欢了。因此，_我不会写那种东西。_

那么，进入 work 的 future 时，究竟有什么变化？

其实什么都没有。我们终于开始揭示最初真正重要的东西。人们如此担忧 AI slop 的大举涌现，却没意识到，高供给的 goods 会变成 commodities。human perspective 无法被 commoditized，因此它始终会要求 premium。

这一切听起来合情合理，也很美好，但并不实用。

那么，此刻你能开始做些什么，才能确保自己创造出一个值得付费的 perspective？

### VI – 后劳动时代的技能栈

未来的 skill stack 不能限定在 career specific skills 上。

我们都知道，AI 能写 content、code 和 music。

更重要的是，developing 能让这些东西具有人性的 skills。

这封信越写越长，所以我会简要说明。我已经在后续写作中拓展这些 skills，今后也会继续拓展。现在，这份清单只是供你了解，好让你开始留意 refine、acquire 和 refine 它们的机会。

skill stack，更准确地说是 skill hierarchy，大致如下：

**1) Agency – The meta skill that creates a story worth telling**

Agency 是无需 permission 或外部 prompting 就能 act 的能力。

换句话说，这是你 create unique story 的能力。这是你为人生设定 trajectory，并做出数十亿个大大小小的 decisions、让自己向前、向后或向两侧移动的能力。每一个这样的 decision 都蕴藏着 acquire knowledge pieces 的机会，而这些 knowledge pieces 随后会沉淀到你 _choose_ 创造的东西中。

Agency 是通过我们之前讨论过的 3 generators of meaning 来实践的：struggle、status 和 curiosity。

Struggle 要求你做出 deliberate choices、reject conformity、step into the arena、create the potential for failure，并 develop a character arc。留在 society 希望你走的 default path 上，肯定会让你被取代。

未来，Status 仍会围绕 money 运转，但 money 会从 productivity metric 转变为表达 agency 的 tool。你 acquire 的 money 越多（并不是说你非得 uber rich），拥有的 options 就越多，你的 decision making 也就能越 unique。你的 story 的 complexity 会随 money 增加。

Curiosity 是你具体如何 filter signal from noise。你越容易分心、越麻木，就越难注意到内心的核心正把你拉向人生中一条 novel path。

**2) Taste – The skill of discernment**

如今，AI 正在制造 infinite library problem。

如果一座 library 包含了每一本可能的 book，以及 characters 的每一种 permutation，那么它就会包含所有 truths、所有 falsehoods 和所有 gibberish。

这座 library 实际上毫无用处，因为 statistically impossible 找到 meaningful text。

_没有 curation 或 structure 的 Information 就是 noise。_

Curation 和 structure 来自你的 taste。

此外，AI 也会制造 infinite monkey problem。

给一群 monkeys 无限的时间，让它们随机 typing，它们可能会 produce Shakespeare，但这种 output 毫无意义，因为背后没有 intention 或 selection。

要 develop taste，你必须 build 属于自己的东西。

因为如果 taste 是 discern 什么值得保留、什么不值得的能力，而你又无法完全 control 这个 process，那么你就会被另一个人的 taste 塑造。

作为 creative，curation 是比 creation 重要得多的 skill。

**3) Perspective – Increasing your human capacity**

Developmental psychology 表明，human values 和 worldviews 会随着时间经过 predictable stages 演变。

我们在这封信开头就谈到了这一点。

通常，这些 stages 遵循一个简单的 pattern：你的 mind 会变得不那么 conformist、ideological 和 dogmatic——前提是你没有陷入停滞。

换句话说，你的 mind 会 expand。你能够 make sense of higher level systems 和 constructs。你能够容纳 greater complexity of thought，而不会为了掩饰理解不足，就把它标记为“wrong”。

换句话说，你会收获更多 perspective。你不会从狭隘的 lens 看待世界；这会 hinder 你的 curation 能力，进而 limit 你的 taste 的 value。

问题在于，正如我们在 social media 上看得很清楚的那样，绝大多数人口（记录显示约为 50%）都处于高度 conformist 的 stage of development。

也就是说，大多数人没有实现 genuine agency 所需的 cognitive capacity。他们只是听说自己应该 become high agency，然后 conform to that idea，而这是一种 low agency behavior。

我们以前已经多次谈过 ego development theory，我也会继续谈下去；但以下是实际 increase 你 greater perspective capacity 的 80/20：

*   你不要试图“level up”。你只需允许自己陷入迷失方向或失去 equilibrium 的状态，并随着时间推移（而不是拒绝这种状态），让它成为你的新常态。

*   例如：当你得到一份新工作，却觉得自己做不到，因为你当前的 mind 无法 metabolize 它时，你就会 _learn._ 新的人际关系或让自己接触新的 cultures，也是一样。

*   你能做的最糟糕的事，就是 double down on your current perspective，因为这会让你封闭自己，不再 growth。

如今什么都讲究快，所以人们会过快地试图 resolve pain 或 dissonance。

他们把 political opinion 当作 law，于是现在所有其他人都是错的。

或者，他们经历了 false transformation，却没有真正 embody 更高的 stage，只是 _act_ 得像处于更高 stage 一样。我们经常在 hippies 身上看到这种情况。他们把 spirituality 当作 status symbol，用来掩饰自己庞大的 ego。

**4) Persuasion – The ability to get people to care**

你创造了某样东西。

某样你在意的东西。

但大多数 creatives 到此为止。他们只是觉得自己可以 post、write、produce，然后人们不知怎的就会 magically find 到它。或者，他们觉得只要写得 intelligent 或 clever，就足以表明自己的 ideas 值得别人 follow。实际上，除非你能 persuade 人们，让他们明白这对自己的生活有何益处，否则没人会在意。

当我们生活在村庄和部落里时，你会被 assigned 一个 role。你 produce 必要的东西，其他人则从中受益。

在当今世界，你似乎孤身一人。你必须付出努力，才能让自己的 work 传播开来。

因为你可以拥有世界上最好的 book、product、painting 或 music，但如果你不理解 attention mechanisms（marketing、sales、social media、advertising），就没人会看到它，也没人会在意到愿意付钱给你。Art 必须与 business 融合。

我们不必深入讲“如何”学习这些 skills，因为它们在这个 skill hierarchy 中层级较低是有原因的。

你会通过 build 自己的东西、犯错并纠正错误，自动学会它们；而只有 development 更高的人才能有效做到这一点。

简而言之：如果你没有得到想要的 results，就需要 experiment、learn 并 iterate，直到得到为止。这就是你如何变得能有效做好任何事。

**5) Technical Know-How – Utilizing AI and tools**

这封信可能看起来像是在反对 AI，但我可以向你保证，事实并非如此。

你不必选边站队。

而且拜托，我们刚刚才谈过 developing perspective 这件事。

事实是，每个人都会在某种程度上与 AI 一起 work，包括那些发誓反对 AI 的 creatives（他们一边在 X 上发帖说自己有多讨厌 AI，一边直接给 Grok 的 algorithm 添料，干得好。）

一如既往，我能给出的最好建议就是 experiment。

_试着在你 build 下一个 project 时使用 AI。_

问问 AI，你可以把哪些不需要 personal touch 的 tasks 交给它。

如果你愿意，就把这封信 feed 给它，描述一下你想 create 什么，然后开始试试。看看它能否提供一些 cognitive offload，让你的 mind 能更自由地专注于重要的事情。

我就说到这里吧，因为我写得累了，想必你读到这里也累了。

感谢你给予关注。

– Dan

You can learn anything and build anything, but even with that power, most people will do nothing, and those who do will be in endless competition with each other, making us all ask where one’s competitive advantage lies.
>
> But of course, there’s a huge problem with AI replacing all jobs.
>
> _If everyone gets fired, no one has money. If no one has money, no one buys anything. If no one buys anything, the economy collapses._
>
> As we’ll find, the above sentence applies to people with jobs, not people with companies, and I can’t help but think about this quote from Naval:
>
> > There are almost 7B people on this planet. Someday, I hope, there will be almost 7B companies.
>
> Right now, most people are worried about how they’ll get paid if they don’t have a job. The only three ways households get money is as so:
>
> 1.  **Wages** – Someone pays you for your labor (employer, customer, self-employment)
>
> 2.  **Transfers** – Government pays you (UBI, Social Security, SNAP, Medicare)
>
> 3.  **Capital Income** – Your assets pay you (dividends, rent, interest, appreciation)
>
> If the first collapses, the second and third have to carry the weight. But transfers alone create political instability and lose price signals. It just won’t work as an overall solution.
>
> Meaning, one potential solution would be broadening capital participation. Regular people would own income-generating assets.
>
> To keep this brief, I will not list all of the proposed mechanisms for this. Because the truth is that some people, especially creatives, will still want to work and be acknowledged for their work. That’s what we’re here to talk about.
>
> Personally, I do not want to sit around and collect a baseline level of cash. Much of the meaning in my life comes from growing personally and professionally across various domains of life, and growth requires agency, and if money shifts from a productivity metric to a _way to express agency_, that is the path I would want to pursue.
>
> I would recommend digging further into [Shapiro’s ideas](https://www.youtube.com/@DaveShap) and folllowing him for when his comprehensive book on this topic, Labor Zero, comes out.
>
> Fortunately, there are jobs that may persist even when automation is superior. These jobs are based on humans specifically demanding other humans:
>
> *   High-liability roles (where there’s someone to blame)
>
> *   Statutory positions (legally required humans)
>
> *   **Experience economy** (bartenders, boutiques, art)
>
> *   **Meaning makers** (helping people navigate the human experience)
>
> *   Relationship/trust jobs (sales, diplomacy, negotiation)
>
> As an everyday creative, I want to focus on the third and fourth option.
>
> _That is what humans will pay a premium for._
>
> But before we talk about how to join the new meaning economy or what high-level skills are required, we’ve missed something critical, which is how meaning is generated in the first place.

> ### III – The evolution and anatomy of meaning (robots vs humans)
>
> > The elegance of the future is not in man versus machine but in their division of labor: silicon sanding the rough edges of necessity so carbon can ascend to meaning. We will abolish baristas and canonize chefs, silence agents and encore actors. It is the same selfish instinct in both arenas—purge friction, preserve narrative—driving a world where the driest chores are done by circuits and the juiciest stories are told by people who bleed.

> For most of history, humans found meaning - or a reason to live - by looking up to the sky.
>
> “Up there.”
>
> Meaning was _given._
>
> Then, we got smarter, supposedly. We made productivity our God. We looked to science and reason for meaning.
>
> “Out there.”
>
> Meaning was _earned._
>
> Now, we’ve become too smart for our own good. We looked for meaning in relativity and couldn’t find it.
>
> “Nowhere.”
>
> Meaning was _deconstructed_.
>
> Going into the future, it is your job to pick up the pieces. You must accept your role as the creator you are. The tool builder. The explorer. The problem solver. If you outsource too much of your agency to the machines, they will be just fine having you hooked up to a collective dopamine IV.

> Now, meaning must be _generated_.
>
> How do you generate meaning?
>
> Well, it helps to look at what creates meaninglessness.
>
> First, meaninglessness stems from stagnation. If you do nothing with your life - like many will once jobs are gone and they have just enough to stay afloat - you do not stay the same. You slowly fall into chaos. Entropy can be observed in the mind, too.
>
> Second, meaninglessness is amplified by isolation. Without tribes and villages, we are the loneliest we’ve ever been. Screen in face waiting for the next paycheck so you can buy buy buy.
>
> We could then say that the two pillars of meaning are _the feeling of forward movement and a connection to something greater than yourself._
>
> Progress and contribution.
>
> For the longest time, we outsourced both. Progress was given by an employer and connection was given by a divine authority. Now, both are in your hands, and that will not sit well with a lot of people.
>
> Progress and contribution are activated through creative problem solving. You have an aim for the future, you identify a problem preventing you from moving forward, you create a solution through experimentation, and you pass that on to someone else in a similar position, and if that person deems it valuable enough, they will exchange another form of value back with you.
>
> Of course, that doesn’t help with understanding how creatives will survive if all jobs are gone, we’re getting to that.
>
> What I am arguing is that you must become a creator. You must build your own thing. You must take your own path. You must express your agency. Do not, by any means, sit around waiting for the day when the world promises to solve all your problems, because I promise that it won’t.
>
> Beyond the two pillars of meaning, there are three generators:
>
> 1.  **Struggle** – The engine of progress. What you choose to struggle for (or care about) is your purpose.
>
> 2.  **Curiosity** – The direction of progress. The non-linear choice of attention to solve problems.
>
> 3.  **Status** – The proof of contribution. The recognition that completes the loop.
>
> When you put those all together, you get a _story._
>
> _Stories, authentic stories, are what will demand a premium price tag._
>
> Because the human brain is a story engine.
>
> That’s how we make sense of the world.
>
> We hate when something is slow when it should be fast.
>
> We hate long lines at the DMV. We hate the small-talking Uber Driver that’s late. We hate sitting for 30 minutes on hold to ask what should be a simple support question. We hate when our fast food order is wrong.
>
> But we will pay top dollar to fly across the world to dine at a five star restaurant.
>
> We sit at the edge of our seat while watching a tear-jerking play at the theatre.
>
> When the task is about speed, accuracy, or utility, leave it to the machines. But when our mind leave the state of needing to do something just to get it done (when we engage in leisure, which there will be plenty of in the future) we crave something entirely different.
>
> We crave the potential for failure.
>
> We crave the lesson of the man who bleeds.
>
> We crave the story, drama, novelty, myth and meaning, and we pay good money for it.
>
> An entire economy will be built around this, and the construction has already started.
>
> The question then is, what is so unique about _you?_ And how can we be sure this won’t be replaced by machines?

> ### IV – The creator economy = the meaning economy
>
> If you are not paid from a job, and you do not find meaning in collecting just enough passive income to cover your necessities, what’s left?
>
> _To get paid from people who believe in what you are doing and want to see more of it in the world._
>
> You pursue what you _care_ about and inspire people to care about it too.
>
> Attention, then, is the scarce resource that creatives will be competing for.
>
> We’ve already been seeing this play out in the creator economy.
>
> Elon Musk, as an obvious example, understands the raw power of attention. He can quite literally shape the future with it. He can marshal capital, talent, and networks. He resource of all resources to ensure that his life’s work of becoming interplanetary is carried out.
>
> Mr. Beast is another. He is a master of attention capture, reinvests that back into production, which leads to more attention.
>
> Think about how you get your education, news, and knowledge today. Most of it is on social media from creators who have taken it on as their job to provide their point of view. Centralized education and information is still at large, but not for long.
>
> However, I don’t want to be the next Mr. Beast. You probably don’t want the risk and criticism of Musk. You simply want to pursue your interests and share it with a tribe of people who care about your vision just as much as you do, just like your brain is wired for. You want to generate and share meaning.
>
> The biggest misconception of the creator economy is that it’s a winner takes all battleground. That couldn’t be any further from the truth.
>
> Justin Welsh, a friend in the space, is known for wanting a quiet life. He wants meaningful work, ample family time, and the ability to do what he wants without financial stress. He does quite well for himself and has full control over how long he “works.”
>
> But you don’t even have to go that big. There are plenty of people with small followings who make more than enough to live well. They don’t grind 16 hours a day. They don’t care about becoming billionaires. They just have something they deem meaningful and share it (while pairing it with the specific skills that allow them to garner attention, as we will discuss). You see these people every day. And by the way, if you want to become a billionaire or work extreme hours, be my guest, some people love that way of life and I’m not here to tell you how to live.

> ### V – The last defensible moat is _you_
>
> As a writer, I’ve been thinking about this a lot over the past few years.
>
> Will AI ever be able to fully replace creatives and artists? That seems kind of bleak no matter which way you spin it.
>
> Can AI ever write what I write?
>
> Can AI ever create the music of a famous musician?
>
> Can AI ever produce a Spielberg level film?
>
> Can AI ever build a company like Steve Jobs could with Apple?
>
> The answer I came to was... yes, but no.
>
> Many will argue that AI can generate a beautiful essay or film, especially as the models get better, but they’re missing something crucial.
>
> Let’s call this **The Swap Test:**
>
> _If you could swap the creator and the creation would be just as valuable, then AI can replace it._
>
> If the creation only works because _you_ made it, then that’s your edge. It carries your perspective, your situation, and your taste.
>
> You see, certain _types_ of work have an objective floor.
>
> When it comes to writing, it’s perfectly fine if AI writes documentation, summarizes multiple sources of information, or makes instructions clearer. Hell, I would even argue that AI can take a brain dump of your original thoughts and structure them into something publishable, but some people may have a style they want to get across as well.
>
> In other words, if speed and efficiency matter over voice, you can hand off control to AI.
>
> But when it comes to writing a personal essay about grief after losing a parent, or writing an article arguing why minimalism is a trap for creatives, there’s something different at play.
>
> Your _point of view_ is at play.
>
> You know, the most unique thing on this planet that nobody else has access to.
>
> The culmination of beliefs, ideas, and experiences that _can’t be reversed or resimulated_ that has been forming since the day you were born.
>
> So that begs the question, if anyone can copy your writing, product, music, or art with a single prompt, when should you _not_ hand over control to AI?
>
> It lies in what AI cannot access.
>
> *   AI cannot think _from_ your perspective. It can only think about your perspective or in the style of your perspective.
>
> *   AI cannot replicate your energy signature. It cannot select what to focus on and why it matters to _you_ right now.
>
> *   AI cannot perform genuine sensemaking. It cannot decide what information means, what’s important, or how to frame it.
>
> *   AI does not have a _trajectory._ It does not have the stakes of death. And even if mortality becomes a solved problem, once a moment passes for humans, it’s over. AI can simulate stories, but time does not serve as a natural compression algorithm like it does for us.
>
> *   AI does not have evolving taste. It does not look back on it’s writing from a year ago and disagree with it. It does not grow.
>
> In other words, AI can copy anything, but it cannot copy what happens next until it has already happened.
>
> You, as a creative, have a perspective that is always evolving. AI is always chasing where you were but not where you are now.
>
> By the time AI copies you, which could be instantly, you have already moved on.
>
> What is considered “great work” is a moving target that evolves with society and culture. For creatives, the act of discovering what they think is the process.
>
> _If AI could perfectly write like me, right now, then I, being a stubborn human being, would not want to write about that anymore. What I care about would instantly change._
>
> When I try to get AI to write like me, I immediately dislike it. Therefore, _I would not write that thing._
>
> So what’s changed going into the future of work?
>
> Nothing, really. We’re finally just uncovering what mattered in the first place. People are so worried about the onslaught of AI slop without realizing that goods in high supply become commodities. The human perspective cannot be commoditized, therefore it will always demand a premium.
>
> All of that makes sense and sounds nice, but it’s not very practical.
>
> So what can you start doing, right now, to ensure that you create a perspective worth paying for?

> ### VI – The post-labor skill stack
>
> The skill stack of the future cannot be pinned down to career specific skills.
>
> We all know that AI can write content, code, and music.
>
> What’s much more important is developing the skills that make those things human.
>
> Since this letter is getting long, I’m going to keep this brief. I already have and will continue to expand on these skills in my future writing. For now, this list is just for awareness so you can begin noticing opportunities to refine acquire and refine them.
>
> The skill stack, which is more of a skill hierarchy, looks like so:
>
> **1) Agency – The meta skill that creates a story worth telling**
>
> Agency is the ability to act without permission or outside prompting.
>
> In other words, it’s your ability to create a unique story. It’s your ability to set a trajectory for your life and make the billions of small and large decisions that move you forward, backward, and side to side. Within each of those decisions lies an opportunity to acquire pieces of knowledge that are then deposited in what you _choose_ to create.
>
> Agency is practiced through the 3 generators of meaning we discussed prior: struggle, status, and curiosity.
>
> Struggle requires you to make deliberate choices, reject conformity, step into the arena, create the potential for failure, and develop a character arc. Staying on the default path society wants you on is a surefire way to get replaced.
>
> Status, in the future, will still revolve around money, but money will shift from a productivity metric to a tool to express agency. The more money you acquire (not that you need to get uber rich) the more options you have, and the more unique your decision making can be. The complexity of your story increases with money.
>
> Curiosity is how you, specifically, filter signal from noise. The more distracted and numb you become, the less you can notice your core pulling you toward a novel path in life.
>
> **2) Taste – The skill of discernment**
>
> AI right now is creating the infinite library problem.
>
> If a library contained every possible book with every permutation of characters, it would contain all truths, all falsehoods, and all gibberish.
>
> The library is effectively useless because finding meaningful text is statistically impossible.
>
> _Information without curation or structure is noise._
>
> The curation and structure come from your taste.
>
> Further, AI also creates the infinite monkey problem.
>
> Given infinite time, a bunch of monkeys typing randomly could produce Shakespeare, but the output is meaningless because there’s no intention or selection behind it.
>
> To develop taste you must build something of your own.
>
> Because if taste is the ability to discern what’s worth keeping vs what’s not, and you do not have full control over that process, you are being molded by the taste of another.
>
> As a creative, curation is a far more important skill than creation.
>
> **3) Perspective – Increasing your human capacity**
>
> Developmental psychology illustrates that human values and worldviews evolve through predictable stages over time.
>
> We learned this back up at the top of this letter.
>
> Typically, these stages follow a simple pattern: your mind becomes less conformist, ideological, and dogmatic - as long as you do not get stuck.
>
> In other words, your mind expands. You’re able to make sense of higher level systems and constructs. You’re able to house greater complexity of thought without labeling it as “wrong” as a defense mechanism for a lack of understanding.
>
> In other other words, you collect more perspective. You do not see the world from a narrow lens, which hinders your ability to curate, which limits the value of your taste.
>
> The problem, as we see very clearly on social media, is that most of the population (documented at around 50%) lives in a highly conformist stage of development.
>
> Meaning, most people don’t have the cognitive capacity for genuine agency. They simply here that they should become high agency and conform to that idea, which is a low agency behavior.
>
> We’ve talked about ego development theory plenty of times before, and I will continue talking about it, but here’s the 80/20 of what actually increases your capacity for greater perspective:
>
> *   You do not try to “level up.” You simply allow yourself to become disoriented or knocked out of equilibrium and let that become your new normal with time (and without rejecting it).
>
> *   Example: When you get a new job and feel like you can’t do it, because your current mind can’t metabolize it, you _learn._ Same goes for new relationships or exposing yourself to new cultures.
>
> *   The worst thing you can do is double down on your current perspective, because that’s how you close yourself off to growth.
>
> People try to resolve pain or dissonance to quickly because in today’s world, everything is quick.
>
> They take the political opinion as law and now everyone else is wrong.
>
> Or, they experience a false transformation and _act_ like the higher stage without actually embodying it. We see this all the time in hippies. They use spirituality as a status symbol to mask their massive ego.
>
> **4) Persuasion – The ability to get people to care**
>
> You have something you’ve created.
>
> Something you care about.
>
> But most creatives stop there. They simply think they can post, write, produce and somehow people will magically find it. Or, they think that sounding intelligent or clever in their writing is enough to signal that their ideas are worth following. In reality, nobody cares unless you can persuade them as to how it’s beneficial to their lives.
>
> When we lived in villages in tribes, you were assigned a role. You produced what was necessary and others benefited from it.
>
> In today’s world, you’re seemingly all alone. You must put in effort to get your work spread.
>
> Because you can have the best book, product, painting, or music in the world, but if you don’t understand the attention mechanisms (marketing, sales, social media, advertising) nobody is going to see it, and nobody is going to care enough to pay you. Art must merge with business.
>
> We don’t need to go into “how” to learn these skills, because they are lower in this skill hierarchy for a reason.
>
> You learn them automatically by building your own thing, making mistakes, and correcting those mistakes, and only a person of higher development can do that effectively.
>
> In a nutshell: if you aren’t getting the results you want, you need to experiment, learn, and iterate until you do. That’s how you become effective at anything.
>
> **5) Technical Know-How – Utilizing AI and tools**
>
> This letter may seem like it’s against AI, but I can assure you that is not the case.
>
> You don’t need to pick a team.
>
> And c’mon, we just talked about that with developing perspective.
>
> The truth is, everyone is going to be working with AI in some capacity, even the creatives who swear against it (while posting on X about how much they hate AI, feeding straight into Grok’s algorithm, well done.)
>
> The best advice I can give, as always, is to experiment.
>
> _Try to use AI for the next project you build._
>
> Ask the AI how you can pass off tasks that don’t demand a personal touch.
>
> Feed it this letter if you want, give a description about what you are trying to create, and start playing around. See if it can provide some cognitive offload so your mind is _more_ free to focus on what matters.
>

>
> Thank you for your attention.
