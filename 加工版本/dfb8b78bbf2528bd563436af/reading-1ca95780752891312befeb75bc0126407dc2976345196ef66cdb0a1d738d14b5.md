_此前：[2016](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2016)_

今年，我实现了 donating 我的 income 的 50% 的目标。这笔 donation 的 split 如下：

-   ~77% 尚未决定：我没有立即挑选 recipient，而是 donated 到一个个人的 [donor-advised fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donor-advised_fund)，这是一种 charitable savings account，让我可以现在 donate，以后再挑选 recipient。

本文其余部分会解释我的 reasoning、过去一年我的看法如何发生变化，以及我对未来的思考。

## 前言：欢迎批评

因为这是我每年所作的最重要的决定之一，所以我始终很欢迎反馈！你可以通过[本页底部的评论框](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2017/#comment-box)发表评论（私下或公开都可以）。本文适用 [Crocker’s Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocker%27s_rules)，也就是说，我承诺不会因任何建议或论证而感到冒犯。

## 选择 amount

今年，我曾考虑降低 donation target，原因有几个：

-   目前，大多数 top candidate organizations 似乎都没有特别 cash constrained，尤其是 Open Philanthropy Project 最近[已经开始](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/focus/other-areas#EffectiveAltruism)向那么多机构提供 large grants。

-   在某些情况下，拥有较大的 liquid savings 的 buffer 会很有用（例如买房、创办公司、长期失业，或总体上拥有更多 [slack](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2017/09/30/slack/)）。我目前的 liquid savings 平均约为 6 个月的 expenses——足以应对大多数 financial shocks，但还不足以安全地承担那样一笔 huge expense。

不过，新的 tax bill 意味着，今年多 donate 一些、未来几年少 donate 一些，能让我省钱。因此，即使我确实决定降低 donation goal，明年降低也比今年降低更合理。我仍在思考 2018 年的 donation goal 应该是多少。

## 进行 Splitting 并 giving 给 GiveWell

我延续了[去年](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2016)的 strategy：将 donation 的 20% giving 给 GiveWell，将 80% 给更 speculative/weird 的事情。今年，我遵循了 GiveWell 的 top-line recommendation，将 90% donating 用于由他们自行决定的 re-granting，10% 用于他们的 operating expenses。

## 向 donor-advised fund 进行 Giving

有几件事促使我推迟今年的最终决定：

1.  许多 organizations 今年较晚才发布 updates，因此我很难弄清楚谁在为哪些事情 raising 多少资金。

2.  考虑到 Open Phil 和 GiveWell Incubation Grants 已经大幅增加了 funding，我感觉 individual EA-aligned donors 似乎并没有什么明显的 role，能够区别于延伸 Open Phil 的 [last dollar](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/blog/good-ventures-and-giving-now-vs-later-2016-update#Benchmark)。

3.  相比去年，我更加认为自己的大部分 impact 来自[我的日常工作](https://www.wave.com/)，而且我很看重能够尽可能全心全意地把注意力放在这份工作上。为了作出自己满意的 donations，而持续了解足够多的 individual organizations，似乎会让我严重分心。

因此，我觉得自己在 2017 年既没有时间，也没有精力作出 high-quality donation decision，于是我用 donor-advised fund 把这个问题留待以后处理。

## 我考虑过的 Alternatives

去年，我主要 donated 给了由 Nick Beckstead 管理的 EA Giving Group donor advised fund。它后来大体上转变成了 Center for Effective Altruism 的 [EA Funds](https://app.effectivealtruism.org/funds) 中的两个。

年初，我对 EA Funds 非常兴奋——它似乎有潜力成为 global poverty 之外的 causes 中类似 GiveWell 的存在。遗憾的是，这些 funds 一直[非常缓慢地](http://effective-altruism.com/ea/1k9/ea_funds_hands_out_money_very_infrequently_should/)提供 grants，因此我很难 evaluate 它们的 track record（而且，如果我最终决定将自己的 DAF money grant 到那里，这种延迟大概也不会成为问题）。

我曾考虑，不用 donor-advised fund 推迟决定，而是用 [donor lottery](https://app.effectivealtruism.org/lotteries) 来完全避免作决定（以 high probability）。但 lottery block size 足够小，以至于我仍然会有 ~50% 的 chance 需要作决定，这似乎不值得。

[EA Grants](https://www.effectivealtruism.org/grants/) 团队似乎提供了一些相当不错的 grants，但他们似乎也从 [Open Philanthropy Project](https://www.centreforeffectivealtruism.org/blog/announcing-grant-from-the-open-philanthropy-project/) 和 Y Combinator 获得了很充足的 funding。

我曾考虑过（现在也仍在考虑）找一个我信任的人替我挑选 grants。这基本上就是我[去年](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2016)通过“EA Giving Group” donor advised fund 所做的事。那个 fund 基本上变成了尚未 distributed funds 的两个 EA Funds，而我还没有想到其他适合做这件事的人选。

## 关于未来的问题

以下是我对明年正在思考的一些事情：

-   考虑到 Open Philanthropy 的 prolific grant-making，individual EA donors 的 role 是什么？我是否应该直接接受，我的 money 与他们的 money 大体上是 interchangeable 的？

-   既然我不认为自己的 donations 是自身 impact 中非常大的 component，那么继续认真思考这件事并写下自己的 reasoning，是否值得？还是说，我只需做一些自己知道大体合理的事（例如 EA Funds）就好？

-   我最初决定继续将 donations 的 20% giving 给 GiveWell，部分原因是这样能让我的 giving 更容易向别人解释。既然我现在已经不怎么解释了，还值得继续这样做吗？

-   我应该继续 donating 自己 income 的 50%，还是试着积攒更大的 cash buffer/更多 slack？

## 尾声：一些杂七杂八的手续笔记

-   对于 donor-advised fund，我选择了 Fidelity，因为从 outside bank account 向它 transfer 似乎需要更少的 paperwork（Schwab 和 Vanguard 似乎有时要求你把 forms 传真给他们——真烦）。如果你已经在 Schwab 有其他 accounts，那么用 Schwab 大概也会很简单。

-   我把 tax withholding 搞砸了（因为 under-reporting 了我预期的 donations），结果到年底没有足够的 cash。我向一位室友 borrowing money，填补了这个缺口，但由于他的 bank 的 transfer limits，他把钱转给我的过程竟然非常麻烦。这件事的教训是：(a) 把你的 withholding 弄对；(b) 别用 Bank of America。

-   我担心在 tax refund 到账之前没有足够的 emergency cash，但我认定，如果真的发生紧急情况，遇到特别严重的事，我大概可以使用 [401k loan](http://benkuhn.net/401kloan)。

-   令人尴尬的是，对于 GiveWell donation，我不知怎么把他们的 top-line recommendation 误读成了 100% unrestricted，所以我最初把自己的 donation 标记为 unrestricted，之后不得不给他们发邮件，请他们把 allocation 改为 90% regranting；他们非常友善地照办了。我觉得自己真蠢，希望没有造成烦人的 accounting/budgeting 问题。

## 原文

_Previously: [2016](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2016)_

This year, I met my goal of donating 50% of my income. The donation was split as follows:

-   ~77% undecided: instead of picking a recipient immeditately, I donated to a personal [donor-advised fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donor-advised_fund), a type of charitable savings account that allows me to donate now and pick the recipient later.

-   21% to [GiveWell’s](http://www.givewell.org/) top charities (to be allocated at GiveWell’s discretion).

-   ~2% to support GiveWell’s operations.

(Update 2019-01-20: embarrassingly, I did the math wrong; I intended to do an 80-18-2 split. The rest of this post reflects that intention.)

The rest of this post explains my reasoning, how I’ve changed my views over the past year, and what I’m thinking about for the future.

## Preface: criticism welcome

Since this is one of the most important decisions I make every year, I always love feedback! You can comment (either privately or publicly) using the [comment box at the bottom of this page](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2017/#comment-box). For this post, [Crocker’s Rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocker%27s_rules) apply, i.e., I commit not to take offense at any suggestion or argument.

## Choosing the amount

I considered lowering my donation target this year for a couple reasons:

-   It doesn’t seem like most of the top candidate organizations are particularly cash constrained right now, especially since the Open Philanthropy Project [has started](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/focus/other-areas#EffectiveAltruism) making large grants to so many places recently.

-   In some situations it can be useful to have a large buffer of liquid savings (e.g. buying a house, starting a company, being unemployed for a long time, or generally having more [slack](https://thezvi.wordpress.com/2017/09/30/slack/)). I currently average about 6 months’ expenses in liquid savings—enough for most financial shocks but not enough to safely incur a huge expense like that.

However, the new tax bill meant that it would save money for me to donate more this year and less in future years. So even if I did decide to reduce my donation goal, it would make more sense to reduce it next year than this year. I’m still thinking about what my donation goal for 2018 should be.

## Splitting and giving to GiveWell

I continued my strategy from [last year](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2016) of giving 20% of my donation to GiveWell and 80% to more speculative/weird things. This year I followed GiveWell’s top-line recommendation of donating 90% for re-granting at their discretion and 10% towards their operating expenses.

## Giving to a donor-advised fund

A few things pushed me to defer my final decision this year:

1.  Many organizations released their updates relatively late this year, so it was hard for me to understand who was raising how much for what.

2.  Given how much Open Phil and GiveWell Incubation Grants have stepped up their funding, it doesn’t feel like there’s an obvious role for individual EA-aligned donors that is distinct from extending Open Phil’s [last dollar](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/blog/good-ventures-and-giving-now-vs-later-2016-update#Benchmark).

3.  Even more than last year, I think most of my impact comes from [my day job](https://www.wave.com/) and I value being able to focus my attention on it as completely as possible. It seemed like it would be a big distraction to keep up with enough individual organizations to make donations that I was happy with.

Because of this, I didn’t feel like I had either the time or attention to make a high-quality donation decision in 2017, so I used a donor-advised fund to kick the can down the road.

## Alternatives I considered

Last year I donated mostly to the EA Giving Group donor advised fund managed by Nick Beckstead. That mostly morphed into two of the Center for Effective Altruism’s [EA Funds](https://app.effectivealtruism.org/funds).

At the beginning of the year I was really excited about EA Funds—it seemed like it had the potential to be akin to GiveWell for causes other than global poverty. Unfortunately, the funds have been making grants [very slowly](http://effective-altruism.com/ea/1k9/ea_funds_hands_out_money_very_infrequently_should/), so it’s hard for me to evaluate their track record (and if I end up deciding to grant my DAF money there, the delay probably won’t be an issue).

Instead of deferring my decision with a donor-advised fund, I considered using a [donor lottery](https://app.effectivealtruism.org/lotteries) to avoid it entirely (with high probability). But the lottery block size was small enough that I would still have had a ~50% chance of having to make a decision, which didn’t seem worth it.

It seemed like the [EA Grants](https://www.effectivealtruism.org/grants/) team made some pretty great grants, but also like they have great funding from [the Open Philanthropy Project](https://www.centreforeffectivealtruism.org/blog/announcing-grant-from-the-open-philanthropy-project/) and Y Combinator.

I thought about (and am still thinking about) finding someone I trust to pick grants for me. That’s essentially what I did [last year](https://www.benkuhn.net/giving-2016) with the “EA Giving Group” donor advised fund. That fund basically became two of the EA Funds that haven’t distributed funds yet, and I haven’t thought of other good candidates to do this.

## Questions for the future

Here are some things I’m thinking about for next year:

-   What is the role of individual EA donors given Open Philanthropy’s prolific grant-making? Should I just accept that my money is mostly interchangeable with theirs?

-   Given that I don’t think my donations are a very large component of my impact, is it worth continuing to think hard about this and write up my reasoning? Or should I just do something I know is mostly reasonable (e.g. the EA Funds)?

-   I originally decided to continue giving 20% of my donations to GiveWell based partly on making my giving more easily explainable to other people. Since I’m not doing very much explaining anymore, is it worth keeping this up?

-   Should I continue donating 50% of my income, or try to save up a larger cash buffer/more slack?

## Coda: random logistics notes

-   For the donor-advised fund, I chose Fidelity because it looked like it required less paperwork to transfer to it from an outside bank account (it looked like Schwab and Vanguard required you to fax them forms sometimes—gross). If you already have other accounts with Schwab then Schwab would probably be simple also.

-   I screwed up my tax withholding (by under-reporting my expected donations) and ended up not having enough cash at the end of the year. I bridged the gap by borrowing money from one of my housemates, but it turned out to be really annoying for him to send me the money because of his bank’s transfer limits. The moral of the story is (a) get your withholding right, and (b) don’t use Bank of America.

-   I was worried about not having enough emergency cash until my tax refund came back, but I decided that in a true emergency I could probably use a [401k loan](http://benkuhn.net/401kloan) for anything really dire.

-   Embarrassingly, for the GiveWell donation I somehow misread their top-line recommendation as being 100% unrestricted, so I originally marked my donation as unrestricted and had to email them to ask them to change the allocation to 90% regranting, which they very graciously did. I felt really stupid and I hope I didn’t cause annoying accounting/budgeting problems.
