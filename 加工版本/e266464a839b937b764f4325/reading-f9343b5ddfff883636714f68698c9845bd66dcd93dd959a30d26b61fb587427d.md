# Effective social norms: don't eat out

---

## 正文

许多 communities 都有自己特有的 social norms。Effective altruism 还太年轻，尚未形成这些 norms——这意味着，我们仍然可以在它们 ossify 之前花时间 optimizing 它们。举个例子：我认为，对于 EA events，我们应该强烈倾向于不出去吃饭或喝东西。

出去吃饭很_贵_——花的钱会以惊人的速度累积。尽管我很少这样做，除非不去就会被排除在 social group 之外，但它通常仍是我在房租和交通之后最大的 expense，每年要花数千美元。[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/eatout/#fn:1) 此外，餐厅往往太吵，不便好好交谈；食物可能不健康；它们会 subsidize factory farming（即使你吃 vegetarian）；[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/eatout/#fn:2) 而且它们[会给父母带来诸多不便](http://www.effective-altruism.com/ea/ee/on_making_spaces_friendlier_to_parents/)——更不用说有 dietary restrictions 或 eating disorders 的人了。

如果我把全部 salary 都花在自己身上，我就不会那么在意这笔 cost；比起自己做的饭，我更喜欢餐厅的食物，因此这算是一项相当不错的 hedonic investment。但我正努力把大部分 income 存下来捐出去，在这种情况下，实际上被迫花掉其中很大一部分，否则就会错过 socialization，实在令人烦恼。对于那些 low spending _并非_ self-imposed 的人，比如学生、有 low-paying jobs 或背负 high debt 的人，这更是个问题。

* * *

要建立一种替代的 tradition，我们就需要 replicate 外出就餐的大部分或全部 upsides。尽管外出就餐要花很多钱，它仍是一种非常牢固的 tradition，这表明它有很多 upsides 需要由替代做法来提供。我也认为确实如此：

-   它让人能够使用 neutral、centrally-located 的 space
-   如果你不喜欢交谈，它能让你有些分散注意力的事情可做
-   它会 evenly distributes 安排食物和 space 所需的 costs
-   它为 event 提供了自然的 schedule 和 defined ending-point
-   它会 incentivizes 人们前来

我还没有想到什么办法，能在不付出这些 cost 的情况下 perfectly replicates 这些 advantages。（尤其是在天气较冷、无法待在户外的地方，要不花钱就获得 neutral、central 的 space，格外困难。）但下面有一些 partial solutions：

-   在户外举办你的 event。如果天气太冷，不适合这样做，而你们当地又有 college EA group，他们或许能在大学里提供 space。
-   把 event 办成 potluck，但要明确告诉大家，不必带任何东西。只要有一个人（希望不是主人，以便分担负担）答应做一道主菜，你们大概就会有足够的食物。
-   避开通常的用餐时间举办 event，并让一个人（同样，最好不是主人）带些零食，以发挥 distraction function。
-   找一个能 scales well 的食谱，让大家一起帮忙准备。
-   不吃东西，改为散步或短途徒步。
-   如果上面这些办法都行不通，去咖啡馆至少会比去餐厅花钱少。

希望这些办法足以让你的 EA event 获得外出就餐的大部分 benefits，同时不必承受它最糟糕的 downsides。不过，如果其他人有建议或 alternatives，我很愿意听听！

* * *

## 原文

Many communities have their own peculiar social norms. Effective altruism is too young to have them yet—which means we can still spend time optimizing them before they ossify. Here’s an example: I think that for EA events we should strongly prefer not to go out for food or drinks.

Going out to eat is _expensive_—it adds up incredibly quickly. Even though I rarely do it unless I’d be excluded from a social group otherwise, it’s usually my biggest expense after rent and transit, costing thousands of dollars a year.[1](https://www.benkuhn.net/eatout/#fn:1) Plus, restaurants are often too loud for good conversation; the food can be unhealthy; they subsidize factory farming (even if you eat vegetarian);[2](https://www.benkuhn.net/eatout/#fn:2) and they [makes things difficult for parents](http://www.effective-altruism.com/ea/ee/on_making_spaces_friendlier_to_parents/)—not to mention people with dietary restrictions or eating disorders.

I wouldn’t mind the cost as much if I were spending my entire salary on myself; I like restaurant food a lot better than what I make for myself, so it’s a reasonably good hedonic investment. But given that I’m trying to save most of my income to give away, it’s annoying to be essentially forced to spend a lot of it or miss out on socialization. This is even more of a problem for people whose low spending _isn’t_ self-imposed, like students or those with low-paying jobs or high debt.

* * *

To install an alternate tradition, we’d need to replicate most or all of the upsides of eating out. Even though eating out costs a lot of money, it’s still a very strong tradition, which suggests that it has a lot of upsides to replace. And I think this is true:

-   It comes with access to neutral, centrally-located space
-   It gives you something distracting to do if you don’t like conversation
-   It evenly distributes the costs of arranging for food and space
-   It provides a natural schedule and defined ending-point for the event
-   It incentivizes people to come

I haven’t come up with anything that perfectly replicates those advantages without the cost. (Especially in colder climates where you can’t go outdoors, it’s particularly hard to get neutral, central space without paying anything.) But here are some partial solutions:

-   Have your event outdoors. If it’s too cold for that and you have a local college EA group, they may be able to provide space at the college.
-   Make the event a potluck but make it clear that people don’t have to bring anything. As long as one person (hopefully not the host, to spread out the burden) commits to making a main dish, you’ll probably have enough food.
-   Hold the event outside of standard meal times, and have one person (again, preferably not the host) bring snack food to fulfill the distraction function.
-   Find a recipe that scales well and have everyone help prepare it.
-   Instead of having food, take a walk or a short hike.
-   If none of the above works, a cafe will at least cost less than a restaurant.

Hopefully these are enough to get your EA event most of the benefits of eating out, without incurring its worst downsides. But if anyone else has suggestions or alternatives, I’d love to hear them!

2.  Vegetarian meals subsidize meat because they’re sold at a higher margin than meat meals at restaurants that serve both (anecdotal evidence; can’t find a citation either way). [↩︎](https://www.benkuhn.net/eatout/#fnref:2)
