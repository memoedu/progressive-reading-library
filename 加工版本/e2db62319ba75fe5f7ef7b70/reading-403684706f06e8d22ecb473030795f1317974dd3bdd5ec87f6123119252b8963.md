    -   NIMBYs 经常遭到抨击。有很多 YIMBY associations，也有 YIMBY manifestors 之类的东西，但 NIMBYism 没有类似的 intellectual substrate。Scott Alexander 为它构建了一个，还暗示了我们在线下讨论过的一个 argument：我当时提出，city 的 residents 或许有权决定他们希望自己的 city 是什么样子。Newcomers 知道自己将面对什么，而且这件事是在 city level 上进行的，所以也不是说很难离开这些 low density areas。SF 的 housing issue 的一个 solution 是建造更多 housing。另一个则是人们搬离 SF。
-   [tech companies 中的 Economists 和 economics](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3247794)

    -   有一些有意思的内容，但对于 macroeconomics，Noah Smith [仍然是对的](https://noahpinionblog.blogspot.com/2014/01/the-most-damning-critique-of-dsge.html)。
-   [Tabarrok 关于 Liberal Radical 的帖子](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/09/liberal-radicalism-mechanism-producing-public-goods.html)，这是 Glen Weyl 等人提出的一种用于 public goods 的 mechanism。

    -   阅读时想到，或许存在一个 impossibility theorem，让你只能从以下三者中选择两个：Optimal amount of public goods provision、Optimal amount of public goods funding，以及 non-coercion。LR picks 1 和 2，Tabarrok 的 DAC picks 2 和 3。
    -   > government 将 top-up mechanism 下的 contribution levels 用作 signal，以决定 produce 多少 public good；而几乎如同魔法一般，top-up function 能让 citizens voluntarily contribute 恰好足够的 amount，从而 correctly signals 整个 society 对 public good 的 values。有意思！当然，还有几个 issues。optimal solution 是一个 Nash equilibrium，而它可能不容易找到，因为每个人都必须考虑其他所有人的 actions，才能 reach equilibrium（iterative process 或许有所帮助）。这个 mechanism 也可能 vulnerable to collusion。我们需要在 lab 和 field 中 test 这个 mechanism。尽管如此，这仍然是对 theory of public goods 和 applied mechanism design 的一项重要 contribution。

-   Common ownership（即同一 industry 中的许多 players 都由 Vanguard、Fidelity 之类的机构 owned）被认为会 decrease competition、rise prices 等。这使一些人提出要解决这一问题（例如，参见 Radical Markets 中的相关章节）。[Azar et al. (2017)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2427345.) 对 airline sector 进行了研究，其中就有这类 findings 的一个例子。一篇 Cato paper（既然是 Cato paper，你就知道他们会说什么）则认为，情况并非[如此](https://object.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/serials/files/regulation/2018/9/regulation-v41n3-4.pdf)。

-   [Alex Tabarrok](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/02/federal-regulation-not-cause-declining-dynamism.html) 前些时候发表了一项 study，没有发现 regulations 对 industry level dynamism 有多大的 effect。[新的 paper](http://sci-hub.tw/http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11149-018-9356-z) 考察了同一个 dataset，却确实发现了对 business investment 的 negative effect。这个 effect 似乎没有那么大："regulation" variable 的 1 SD increase 会 leads to business investment rate 的 5.5% decrease。不过，这也算有所发现。

-   [基于 behavioural economics 为 market 作 defence](https://jasoncollins.blog/2018/09/26/robert-sugdens-the-community-of-advantage-a-behavioural-economists-defence-of-the-market/)

    -   > 假设蛋糕放在展示柜的最前面。当普通人「Joe」走进咖啡馆时，他选了蛋糕。如果放在最前面的是水果，他就会选水果。Joe 在自己的 choice 中犯了 error 吗？我们需要问，他的 latent preference 是什么。但假设 Joe 对蛋糕和水果是 indifferent。他没有受到 labelling 的误导，对 products 或它们对健康的 effects 也没有任何 false beliefs。他只是想吃摆在展示柜最前面的东西。这个 error 的 nature 是什么？为了帮助回答这个问题，想象 SuperReasoner 也走进了咖啡馆。SuperReasoner 和 Joe 一模一样，唯一的区别是，他「拥有 Einstein 的 intelligence、Deep Blue 的 memory，以及 Gandhi 的 self-control」。（Sugden 从 [Nudge](https://jasoncollins.blog/2013/11/04/thaler-and-sunsteins-nudge/) 借用了这一组 traits）。当 SuperReasoner 遇到 prominence 不同的蛋糕和水果时，会发生什么？既然他和 Joe 一模一样，他对两者就是 indifferent。他的 feelings 也和 Joe 一样，所以也想吃摆在最前面的东西。这不是 intelligence、memory 或 self-control 的失灵。这里没有 error。相反，是 latent preference 本身就是 context dependent 的。但如果 latent preferences 本身就是 context dependent 的，你究竟要如何 determine latent preference 是什么？什么才是正确的 context？

-   Glen Weyl 为一门关于 [Radical Markets](https://www.dropbox.com/s/v04gy07ye8dcf3p/RadicalMarketsFall2018.docx?dl=0) 的 course 编写的 syllabus

Psychology（心理学）

-   我们已经知道，在更 egalitarian 的 countries 中，career choice 的 gender differences 更 greater。对于 personality 的许多 measures，[情况似乎也一样](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/10/specialization-personality-choice.html)。
    -   他们使用的 gender equality index，是对不同 indices 进行 PCA 后的 first component，而这些 indices 似乎未必与 social norms directly related。对于 gender equality，我更喜欢使用 [SIGI](https://www.genderindex.org/)。真要说的话，我预计这项 study underestimates 了 differences 随 equality 而 increase 的程度。
-   我之前在这里批评过一种流行的 moral psychology 的 model：intuitive responses 是 deontological 且 feelings-driven 的，随后可以被 deliberate、rational、cold utilitarian thinking overruled。[这篇 Paper](http://www.wdeneys.org/data/manuscript%20revision%202%20JEPG.pdf) 认为，给出 utilitarian responses 的人并不是在 supressing 这一最初的 intuition。相反，他们的 intuitions 从一开始就是 utilitarian 的。Sample size 高于我通常设定的、值得分享的 minimum threshold（100）。
-   [meta-analysis 对 psychological research 的 replicability 揭示了什么](http://www.deakin.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/1459620/PrePrint-What-Meta-Analyses-Reveal.pdf)
    -   一个有意思的 finding 是，各 field 中具有 "adequate statistical power" 的 studies 所占的 %。Statistical power（通常 represented as alpha）指的是：如果存在 effect，一项 study 有多大的可能 find out 这个 effect。或者说，如果 alternative hypothesis 是 true，那么 null hypothesis 被一项 study rejected 的 odds 是多少。
    -   正如人们可能预料的，genetics 排在第一，70% 的 studies 有超过 70% 的 power，而 consumer 和 cognitive psychology 的 literatures 则最 weakest。
    -   这意味着，increasing power（例如，更 larger sample sizes）可能有助于在目前深陷 failed-to-replicate 泥潭的 litetures 中 find 一些 effects
    -   sample size 应该有多 big？例如，如果你预计 small effect size（d=0.1），并希望有 80% power，那么 N~800-1000。如果你预计 large（d=0.8）effect size，或许只要 N=15 就可以，但这会为 p-hacking 和其他不可靠的 practies 打开方便之门。
-   关于 attractiveness 是否 correlates with broad fitness（比如说，healthy）的 [Meta-analysis](https://twitter.com/DrThomasAG/status/1049941604402941952)。这里比较的是 pure sexual-selection model 与 good-looks-as-signaling model。前一种 model 似乎更 stronger。（感谢 @DrXaverius 提供线索）

Economics

-   It used to be believed that poorer countries were not converge in terms of income per capita with richer countries.[New paper](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/10/convergence-big-time.html) shows that this was true when a landmark study on the topic was published, but that in more recent times, poorer countries are indeed catching up.

-   [Judges exposed to law and economics](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/10/law-economics-matter.html) training does change the behaviour of said judges.

    -   File this under ideas and culture can overrule incentives
-   [Steelmanning the NIMBYs](http://slatestarcodex.com/2018/10/01/steelmanning-the-nimbys/)

    -   NIMBYs get a lot of flak. There are lots of YIMBY associations, with YIMBY manifestors and stuff, but there is no similar intellectual substrate for NIMBYism. Scott Alexander makes one, and hints at an argument we discussed IRL: I suggested that residents of a city may have a right to decide how they want their city to look like. Newcomers know what to expect, and this is done at a city level, so it's not like it's hard to escape the low density areas. One solution for the housing issue in SF is for more housing to be built. Another is for people to move out of SF.
-   [Economists and economics in tech companies](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3247794)

    -   Some interesting stuff, but Noah Smith[is still right regarding](https://noahpinionblog.blogspot.com/2014/01/the-most-damning-critique-of-dsge.html)macroeconomics.
-   [Tabarrok's post on the Liberal Radical](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/09/liberal-radicalism-mechanism-producing-public-goods.html) mechanism for public goods, proposed by Glen Weyl et al.

    -   An thought had while reading is that there may be an impossibility theorem that lets you choose only two of: Optimal amount of public goods provision, Optimal amount of public goods funding, and non-coercion. LR picks 1 and 2, Tabarrok's DAC picks 2 and 3.
    -   > The government used the contribution levels under the top-up mechanism as a signal to decide how much of the public good to produce and almost magically the top-up function is such that citizens will voluntarily contribute exactly the amount that correctly signals how much society as a whole values the public good. Amazing! Naturally there are a few issues. The optimal solution is a Nash equilibrium which may not be easy to find as everyone must take into account everyone else’s actions to reach equilibrium (an iterative process may help). The mechanism is also potentially vulnerable to collusion. We need to test this mechanism in the lab and in the field. Nevertheless, this is a notable contribution to the theory of public goods and to applied mechanism design.

-   Common ownership (when many players in the same industry are owned by Vanguard, Fidelity and the likes) is thought to decrease competition, rise prices, etc. This has led to some to propose fixing this (See for example the relevant chapter in Radical Markets). An example of these findings can be found in [Azar et al. (2017)](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2427345.), who studied the airline sector. A Cato paper (being a Cato paper you know what they would say) argues that this is not the [case](https://object.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/serials/files/regulation/2018/9/regulation-v41n3-4.pdf).

-   [Alex Tabarrok](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/02/federal-regulation-not-cause-declining-dynamism.html) published a study a while ago that didn't find much of an effect of regulations on industry level dynamism. [New paper](http://sci-hub.tw/http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11149-018-9356-z), looking at the same dataset, does find a negative effect on business investment. The effect doesn't seem to be that big, a 1 SD increase in the "regulation" variable leads to a 5.5% decrease in business investment rate. Still, it's something.

-   [A defence of the market on behavioural economics grounds](https://jasoncollins.blog/2018/09/26/robert-sugdens-the-community-of-advantage-a-behavioural-economists-defence-of-the-market/)

    -   > Suppose the cake is at the front of the display. When the ordinary human “Joe” goes to the cafe, he selects the cake. If the fruit had been at the front, he would have selected the fruit. Has Joe made an error in his choice? We need to ask what his latent preference is. But suppose Joe is indifferent between cake and fruit. He is not misled by labelling or any false beliefs about the products or their effects on their health. He simply feels a desire to eat whatever is at the front of the display. What is the nature of the error? To help answer this, imagine that SuperReasoner also goes to the cafe. SuperReasoner is just like Joe except that he “has the intelligence of Einstein, the memory of Deep Blue, and the self-control of Gandhi”. (Sugden borrows this combination of traits from [Nudge](https://jasoncollins.blog/2013/11/04/thaler-and-sunsteins-nudge/)). What happens when SuperReasoner encounters cake and fruit that vary in prominence? Since he is just like Joe, he is indifferent between the two. He also has the same feelings as Joe, so feels a desire to eat whatever is at the front. This is not a failing of intelligence, memory or self-control. There is no error. Rather, the latent preference itself is context dependent. But if latent preferences themselves are context dependent, how do you ever determine what a latent preference is? What is the right context?

-   We knew that gender differences in career choice are greater in more egalitarian countries. The same [seems to be true](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2018/10/specialization-personality-choice.html) for many measures of personality.
    -   The gender equality index they use is the first component of a PCA of different indices that do not seem necessarily directly related to social norms. For gender equality, I prefer using the [SIGI](https://www.genderindex.org/). If anything, I'd expect that the study underestimates the extent to which differences increase with equality.
-   A popular model of moral psychology I've fired shots at are previously here is that intuitive responses are deontological and feelings-driven, that can then be overruled by deliberate, rational, cold utilitarian thinking. [Paper](http://www.wdeneys.org/data/manuscript%20revision%202%20JEPG.pdf) argues that those who give utilitarian responses are not supressing this initial intuition. Rather, their intuitions are utilitarian to begin with. Sample size is above the threshold I usually set as minimum to bother sharing (100).
-   [What meta-analysis reveal](http://www.deakin.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/1459620/PrePrint-What-Meta-Analyses-Reveal.pdf)about the replicability of psychological research
    -   An interesting finding is the % of studies that have "adequate statistical power" by field. Statistical power (usually represented as alpha) is how likely an study is to find out an effect if there is an effect. Or, if an alternative hypothesis is true, what are the odds that the null hypothesis will be rejected by a study.
    -   As one might expect, genetics came up first, with 70% of studies having over 70% power, while consumer and cognitive psychology literatures are the weakest.
    -   This means that increasing power (for example, larger sample sizes) may help find some effects in litetures that at the moment are failed-to-replicate quagmires
    -   How big should a sample size be? For example, if you expect a small effect size (d=0.1) and you want 80% power, then N~800-1000. If you expect a large (d=0.8) effect size, you may be okay with just N=15, but that opens the door to p-hacking and other dodgy practies.
