# Nintil - The antonymical fallacy

---

## 正文

我觉得有一种 informal  fallacy 还没有自己的名字。我试着找过，却没找到，所以我把它命名为 antonymical fallacy。也许它其实已经存在了。如果确实如此，请告诉我！它与 [False dilemma informal fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_dilemma) closely related。

我的想法是这样的：某个东西的 negation 与它的 antonym（或 antonym-like word）之间存在 difference。有些 antonyms 确实是原来那个 word 的 logical negation，但并非全部如此！

not-tall 并不 equal small（它可能是 medium sized），not-legal 并不 equal illegal（它可能是 alegal），not-deterministic 并不 mean random \[1\]，not-perfect 并不 mean imperfect（它可能具有 average quality）。

一般来说，¬P≠antonym(P)。

这种 fallacy 的 example 经常以如下方式使用：

1.  要么 P，要么 ¬P（Law of the Excluded Middle）
2.  P->Q
3.  antonym(P)->Q
4.  (P∨antonym(P))->Q（由 2,3 得出）
5.  ∴Q

如果 premise 3 是 ¬P->Q，这就是 true，但在这种 fallacy 中并非如此。或者，如果你还 prove 了 ¬(P∨antonym(P))->Q，这也是 true，但那就把事情弄得太复杂了！

这种 fallacy 的 Examples：

1.  （要么 everyone 都成为医生，要么并非 everyone 都成为医生）
2.  如果 everyone 都成为医生，我们就会死（死于饥荒，因为没人种地）
3.  如果 no one 成为医生，我们就会死（死于疾病）
4.  如果 everyone 或 no one 成为医生，我们就会死
5.  因此，我们就会死（！）

并非所有人不等于没有人：Not-everyone ≠ None。

这 ignores 了这样一点：在 doctor 与 not-doctor 的 world 之间，还存在有 some doctors、而我们能够活下去的 world。

1.  （要么一个 number 是 positive，要么它不是 positive）
2.  如果一个 number 是 positive，它就有 magnitude
3.  如果一个 number 是 negative，它就有 magnitude
4.  如果一个 number 是 positive 或 negative，它就有 magnitude
5.  因此，每个 number 都有 magnitude

不是正数不等于负数：Not positive ≠ Negative。

这 ignores 了 zero 没有 magnitude 这一事实，而且它既不是 positive，也不是 negative。

1.  （要么水在箱子的 part A 中，要么它不在 part A 中）
2.  如果水在 part A 中，水就在箱子的一个 section 中
3.  如果水在 part B 中（B 是箱子的其余部分），水就在箱子的一个 section 中
4.  因此，水在箱子的一个 section 中

不在 A 部分不等于在 B 部分：Not in part A ≠In part B

这 ignores 了箱子可能装满水的 possibility；这样一来，A 和 B 都 contain 水，因此水在两个 sections 中，而不只是其中一个。

\[1\] 关于一种被 excluded 的 logical possibility，请参见[这里](http://www.informationphilosopher.com/freedom/standard_argument.html)的“Standard Argument 的 Examples”一节，以及[这里](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/incompatibilism-theories/#1)。显然，Logical possibility 并不 entails correctness。

## 原文

I think there's an informal  fallacy that doesn't have a name of its own. I tried looking and couldn't find it, so I named it the antonymical fallacy. Maybe it does already exist. If it does, please tell me! It is closely related to the [False dilemma informal fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_dilemma).

So the idea is this: There is a difference between the negation and the antonym (or antonym-like word) of something. Some antonyms are indeed the logical negation of the original word, but not all!

not-tall does not equal small (it can me medium sized), not-legal does not equal illegal (it can be alegal), not-deterministic does not mean random \[1\], not-perfect does not mean imperfect (It can be of average quality).

In general, ¬P≠antonym(P).

The example of the fallacy is often used as follows:

1.  Either P or ¬P (Law of the Excluded Middle)
2.  P->Q
3.  antonym(P)->Q
4.  (P∨antonym(P))->Q (From 2,3)
5.  ∴Q

This is true if premise 3 were ¬P->Q, but it's not in the case of the fallacy. Or this is true if you also prove that ¬(P∨antonym(P))->Q, but that's overcomplicating things!

Examples of the fallacy:

1.  (Either everyone becomes a doctor or it isn't the case that everyone becomes a doctor)
2.  If everyone is becomes a doctor, we die (from famines, no one farms)
3.  If no one becomes a doctor, we die (from illnesses)
4.  If everyone or no one becomes a doctor, we will die
5.  Therefore, we will die (!)

Not-everyone ≠ None.

This ignores that between the doctor and not-doctor world, there are world with some doctors in which we live.

1.  (Either a number is positive or it is not positive)
2.  If a number is positive, it has magnitude
3.  If a number is negative, it has magnitude
4.  If a number is positive or negative, it has magnitude
5.  Therefore, every number has magnitude

Not positive ≠ Negative.

This ignores the fact that zero has no magnitude, and it is neither positive or negative.

1.  (Either the water is in part A of the box or it is not in part A)
2.  If the water is in part A, the water is in a section of the box
3.  If the water is in part B (B is the rest of the box), the water is in a section of the box
4.  Therefore, the water is in a section of the box

Not in part A ≠In part B

This ignores the possibility that the box may be full of water, and thus both A and B contain water, therefore the water is in both sections, not only one.

\[1\] See [this](http://www.informationphilosopher.com/freedom/standard_argument.html), section 'Examples of the Standard Argument', [this](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/incompatibilism-theories/#1) for one excluded logical possibility. Logical possibility does not entails correctness, obviously.
