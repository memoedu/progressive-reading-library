# Epigenetics（表观遗传学）

---

## 正文

最近，人们发现，仅查看 epigenome 就可能 estimate age、mortality risk 或 general health。用于此目的的 models 被称为 _epigenetic (or methylation) clocks_。

Epigenetic clocks 正日益成为 aging research 领域的 scientists 用来 measure anti-aging interventions 的 putative efficacy 的热门选择。它们或许能让人在完整的 Kaplan-Meier curves 可用之前就获得 results，而且至少表面看来，它们可以替代许多其他 biomarkers。我建议在阅读本文之前，先阅读 [The Longevity FAQ](https://nintil.com/longevity) 的入门章节以及有关 epigenetics 的章节，因为这些内容提供了更多背景。

## Epigenetics（表观遗传学）

先简要回顾一下 epigenetics：epigenome 是 DNA 的一组 modifications，它们不 alter genetic code 本身，却 regulates 其 expression，并使 cells 能够 dynamically regulate 它们的 functions，以及 maintain distinct states。Epigenetic marks 有多种 forms，主要是 CpG sites 的 methylation——即一个 methyl group 被 attached 到一个后面跟着 G 的 C 上——以及 histone modification。Histones 是 form octamers 的 proteins，DNA 就 coiled 在这些 octamers 周围。这些 histones 带有可以进行 modifications 的 tails；通常是 acetylation（它会 opens up chromatin 并 facilitates gene expression）或 methylation（它既可以充当 gene expression 的 repressor，也可以充当其 promoter）。尤其值得关注的是由 Polycomb Repressive Complex (PRC) induced 的 histone methylation，因为相对于 methylating 或 demethylating CpGs，cell 更容易将它们 switch on and off（[Bell et al., 2019](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-019-1824-y)）。

\[1\]. 不过，这会随 tissue 而 varies。在 mice 中，liver 中 66% 的 CpGs 是 methylated，lung 中则为 81.6%

在 mammalian tissue 中，这些 CpGs 的大多数 (70-80%) 都是 methylated[1](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-1)（[Janitz & Janitz, 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/B9780123757098000125)），但如果只看 CpGs 的 clusters，也就是所谓的 islands，那么在 humans 的任何一个给定 tissue 中，只有 6-8% 的 _islands_ 是 methylated（[Illingworth et al., 2008](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2214817/?report=classic)）：考虑到存在的所有 cells 种类，一个给定的 cell 只需要一部分 genes 处于 active 状态，就能执行其 functions。CpG methylation patterns 在 adult 中本不应发生太多 change。一旦 cell 已经 fully differentiated，这些 cells 似乎就认为，这些 CpG methylations 会一直安然无恙，因此它们大多会 loses 对额外 CpGs 进行 methylate 的 capacity，和/或它们 protect（maintenance methylation）那些 methylated CpG islands 的 ability 会 reduced（[Cedar and Bergman, 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg2540/)、[Jeltsch & Jurkowska 2014](https://www.cell.com/trends/biochemical-sciences/pdf/S0968-0004\(14\)00087-5.pdf)）。

Methylation pattern（甲基化模式；来源：Wikipedia）

随着我们 age，CpGs 中会出现一种 distinct pattern：原本是 methylated、而且本应保持如此的 islands 开始 lose 它们的 methyls，而本不该存在 methylation marks 的地方也出现了 extraneous methylation marks。目前尚不清楚，这是否发生在每个 cell 中，还是仅发生在一个 subset 中，因为这需要尚未开展的 single cell epigenetic sequencing studies。同样需要记住的是，researchers 迄今为寻找与 age 的 correlations 而查看的 CpGs，只是整个 genome 中全部 CpGs 的一个 small subset，通常每次只查看几百个。即使最先进的 methylation microarray（Illumina MethylationEPIC）也只能 assess 850k sites，占 CpGs total count 的 3%）。

这种 approach 看来是一条有希望的 research avenue：这种 aging 的 pattern 在不同 species 中都一定程度地 occurs，而且这种 pattern 似乎不只是对 cell 外部 damage 的 reaction，而是一种 intrinsic property；即使将 young cells transplanted 到 old body 中，或反过来，它也会 persists（[Søraas et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/acel.12897)）。

各种 conditions（例如 replicative senescence 或 repeated passaging）affect epigenetic age 的 extent，取决于用于 measure 该 age 的 clock。即便如此，results 的 interpretation 也并不显然；例如，[Horvath (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24138928/) 说，_当把 analysis restricting 到 iPS cells，或把 analysis restricting 到 ES cells 时，也能 observed 到 cell passage number 与 DNAm age 之间的 significant correlation_。从他们提供的 figures（Figure 5H）来看，我觉得情况似乎并非如此。在后来的一篇 review（[Raj and Horvath](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1535370220918329)，2020）中，同样的 data 得到了一种在我看来更合理的 interpreted 方式：_对 stem cells 进行 extensive passaging，甚至多达一百次，也不会 accompanied by epigenetic age 的 increase_。联系之后，Ken Raj 告诉我：

> 这处 reference 确实可能引起混淆。虽然 ESC proliferation 与 age 之间似乎存在 statistical correlation，但当 data points 与这些 cells 的 ages 的 spread 进行 compared 时，情况就并非如此；无论它们被 passaged 多少次，其中绝大多数都处于 pre-natal age。重要的是，我 \[Ken Raj\] 已在自己的 laboratory 中 repeated 了这个 experiment，可以明确 confirm，proliferating ESC（以及 iPSC）并不会 undergo epigenetic ageing。新的 data 很快就会发表。

由于 embryonic stem cells 是 immortal（[Saez et al. 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5840266/)），它们不会 epigenetically age 这一 result 在 a priori 上有一定道理。可能的 outcome 是，它们确实会 age，但速度非常缓慢，这在某种程度上呼应了针对 gametes 与 DNA mutations 发现的 results：它们也会遭受这些 mutations，但比其他 cells 少 orders of magnitude。

**根据 Horvath (2013) 及相关 work 中使用的 Horvath clock**，如果 senescence 是通过 radiation 或 oncogene suppression 而 induced，senescent cells 就不会在 clocks 中显示为 aged；只有那些 stay alive 足够久的 cells，才会变得 epigenetically aged。即使 cells 通过 telomerase 被 immortalized，它们仍然会 epigenetically age。同样，faster replication 并不会使 cells 更快地 age；假设你有一个 cell，它在一段时间内 replicates 自身的速度更快，而另一个 replicates 的速度较慢，随后在最后 test 这两个 populations 中 cells 的 epigenetic age，它们也会是 similarly aged。

**然而，并非所有 clocks 都是如此**，而且目前仍有人在努力 explore 各种 conditions 对 epigenetic age 的 effects，并更好地 conceptualizing epigenetic age 本身。为 clock design 而选择 measure 哪些 sites 至关重要；在后面的一个章节中，我会讨论一些 results 的 variability 如何取决于使用何种 clock，以及 methylation data 来自何种 tissue。

## 简要了解 epigenetic clocks

\[2\]. 你可以在[这里](https://galaxyproject.github.io/training-material/topics/statistics/tutorials/age-prediction-with-ml/tutorial.html)制作自己的 epigenetic clock

从 Conceptually 的角度看，epigenetic clocks 非常 simple[2](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-2) 。首先从 CpG methylation data 和一个感兴趣的 endpoint 入手，例如 age 或 mortality risk。然后 fits 一个 elastic net model（一个 mixed L1/L2 penalized linear regression），最终得到 $DNAmAge=a+b1CpG_1+b2CpG_2+...$。这有助于为这些 clocks 提出一个 conservative case：与更花哨的 statistical techniques 不同，使用 elastic nets 很难捏造 results；可供 tweak 的 hyperparameters 较少，而且由于它是一个 simple model，也更难 overfit 一个 training set。

最初感兴趣的 endpoint 是 age，因此人们尝试从 epigenome 来 predict age：首次这样的尝试（后来被称为“the original Horvath clock”的工作）来自 Bockland et al.（[2011](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/labs/pmc/articles/PMC3120753/)），考察了 88 个 CpG sites，就已实现了与 chronological age 相当不错的 correlation（.85）。

两年之后，[Hannum](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1097276512008933)（2013）在第一批 analyses 中考察了 71 个 CpGs。请注意，clocks 并非只使用 epigenome，Hannum 还使用了各种 controls，例如 age-adjusted BMI、gender、diabetes status、ethnicity，以及一个对应于 data 所取自的 dataset 的 dummy。

[Horvath](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24138928/)（2013）后来又制作了一个 clock，基于来自各种 tissues 的 353 个 CpGs samples；这次他仅使用 methylation，没有使用其他内容；但他使用了一个 function 来 transform age；这就是使用 linear models 时需要付出的代价，有时必须进行一些 feature engineering 来 improve 这个 model。这是有依据的，因为 epigenetic age 在 plotted 后，似乎确实与 age 存在 logarithmic relation（[Snir et al., 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15592294.2019.1623634?journalCode=kepi20)），起初有 rapid growth，随后便 slows down。

[Levine et al.(2018)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5940111/) 使用一个 single dataset，遗憾的是，它不像 Horvath 拼凑起来的 datasets（InCHIANTI 和 NHANES III）那样容易获取，来提出一个 composite metric，即 PhenoAge；它是一个 model 的 output，该 model 旨在根据一组 aging 的 phenotypical covariates（albumin、creatinine、glucose level、CRP、lymphocyte %、mean red cell volume、red cell distribution width、alkaline phosphatase、white blood cell count，以及 age 本身）来 predict 10-year mortality risk，因此 PhenoAge 与 age 是 correlated with 的，但并不相同；例如，在某些 tissues 中，PhenoAge 可以是高度 negative 的。此后的方法与 Horvath paper 中的 elastic net model 相同。这里使用了 513 个 CpGs。为了给 mortality analysis 找到一个与 age 不 correlated 的 metric（当然，Age 是 predicting aging 中的一个 significant confounder），他们 trained 一个 linear model，根据 PhenoAge 来 predict age，然后 calculated 它与 chronological age 之间的 difference（DNAm PhenoAgeAccel）。他们正是使用这个 measure 来 predict mortality risks。

GrimAge（[Lu et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30669119/)，2019）使用 Framingham Heart Study 作为 training set，并采用了另一组 intermediate variables：他们首先基于 methylation data 来 train 一个 model，以 predict smoking pack-years 和 blood 中各种 proteins 的 levels，然后使用这些 estimates，trained 另一个 model 来 estimate time to death。他们总共使用了 1030 个 CpGs。与 Levine paper 中一样，这里也以类似方式 derived 出一个 acceleration metric，即 AgeAccelGrim。正如后文将讨论的，在 predicting mortality 方面，这个 clock 迄今取得了最好的 results。

作为收束，值得一提的是，回顾起来，clocks 往往被 grouped 为 first generation（Horvath 和 Hannum）和 second generation（GrimAge 和 PhenoAge）。后者在 predicting morbidity 和 mortality 方面具有明显更高的 accuracy（[Levine, 2020](https://academic.oup.com/biomedgerontology/article/75/3/463/5717592)）。其中一个原因是，较早的 models 的 dataset 中存在 built-in bias：它们是在 cross-sectional data 上 trained 的（你从不同 ages 的人中取一个 sample，获取他们的 CpGs，然后 train 这个 model），而 second generation clocks 纳入了 longitudinal data（来自同一组人，并随时间持续 tracked）。例如，设想有一个 CpG 与 early death 是 strongly correlated 的。在一个 cross-sectional dataset 中，你可能只会在 younger subjects 的一个 small sample 中看到这个 CpG；因为 older 的那些人已经死亡，所以你无从知道这个 CpG 后来会有这样的 causal impact。与 GrimAge paper 中一样，这里我们也能找到一个 age acceleration metric。

查看 clocks 得出的 hazard risks 时，阅读 study 应当谨慎，因为根据 [Fransquet et al. (2019)](https://clinicalepigeneticsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13148-019-0656-7/figures/3)，Horvath 和 Hannum clocks 已被发现存在 publication bias（无论如何，都不应该将它们用于 predicting mortality，这也是更倾向于 GrimAge 和 PhenoAge 的原因）

此时你可能在想：为什么一直使用 elastic nets？为什么不试试 neural networks！当然，也已经有人这样做了，只不过是最近的事。[Levy et al. (2020)](https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12859-020-3443-8) 不仅发表了一个基于 deep-net 的 epigenetic clock，还免费提供了 processing pipeline，供其他人 reuse。不过，与 GWASes 的情况一样，nonlinear model 相比 linear models 并没有 improve 太多；CpGs 之间似乎没有那么多 interactions，因此最终在 predicting age 方面，performance 比 Horvath’s clock 更好，但也没有好太多。

还有人可能会想到，这些被称为 methylation clocks，但我一直只在谈论 CpG methylation。为什么？因为 CpG methylation 更容易获取；non CpG methylation 是 rare 的（起初甚至被认为并不存在），尽管它可能与某些 tissues 或 organs 有关，例如 brain（[Lister et al., 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3785061/)）

还有一些工作直接探讨在同一个 dataset 上 comparing 这些 clocks 的问题。在最近的一篇 preprint 中，[McCrory et al. (2020)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2020/04/28/2020.04.27.063164.full.pdf) 再次指出，second generation clocks 更善于 predict age-related conditions。不过，在 predicting all-cause mortality 方面（这里进行的是 10 year followup），只有 GrimAge 表现突出。AgeAcccelGrim score 每有一个 standard deviation increase，未能活过 10 年的 odds 就 increases 18%。PhenoAge 比 first generation clocks 稍好一些，但最终这种 increase 是 statistically nonsignificant 的。该 paper 在 examining 每一个 clock 时都给出了两个 models，一个是 unadjusted 的，另一个则针对其他一些 covariates 进行了 adjusted（age、“social class trajectory”、smoking history、physical activity、hazardous drinking 或 BMI）。应用这些 controls 后，relations 会 weaken。应该没有必要 control for age，因为 acceleration measures 按其 construction 就与 age 不 related。Controlling for 其他 factors 则可能存在问题。

例如，考虑 BMI。BMI、epigenetic age acceleration 和 mortality 之间的 causal association 是什么？到目前为止，我们一直只在谈论 correlations，因此各种 causal diagrams 都与我们的发现 compatible。可能是：

## causality 朝哪个方向 run？

在这里，我们希望直接 manipulate 这个 epigenome，看看是否能 observe 一个 aged phenotype。如果不能，那就意味着这个 aged epigenome 仅仅是一个 epiphenomenon，而不是可以在 drug development 中 causally 用作 target 的东西。直接这样做的 Experiments 尚未开展，因此我们只有 indirect evidence。

David Sinclair 提到了一个使用“[ICE mice](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2019/10/21/808659.full.pdf)”（Inducible Changes to the Epigenome）开展的 experiment；这些是 genetically engineered mice，能够按需 express 一个 causes DNA double stranded breaks 的 gene。根据 Sinclair 的 theory，sirtuins——它们所做的是 deacetylate proteins，主要是 histones；这又使 chromatin 能够更 tightly packed，从而 reducing 周围 deacetylated site 的 gene expression——通常在 specific sites 发挥 diminish gene expression 的作用。但当出现 DNA (double-stranded) break 时，break 处就需要 deacetylation，这会将 sirtuins 吸引过去以协助 repair，但这会 reduces 它们在其他位置的 deacetylating activity：本应 silent 的 Genes 随后变得 expressed。

因此，ICE mice 使人们能够通过 damaging 这个 genome 来间接 damage 这个 epigenome。与未被 induced DNA damage 的 ICE mice control group 相比，它们在 epigenetically 意义上 older 了 50%。在 Phenotypically 层面，它们 showed 各种 age-related conditions，例如 fur、body mass、muscle strength 的 loss，或 arthritis 的 increase 和 sight 的 loss。

\[3\]. 我在 Longevity FAQ 中提到了一些实现这一点的方法，adeno-associated viruses，以及在 antibiotic doxycycline 存在时 [genetic cassette 会 becomes active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetracycline-controlled_transcriptional_activation)，就是其中两种。

\[4\]. 事实证明，只用 OSK factors 就能得到相同的 results。Sinclair 团队认为 Myc 是一个不必要的 oncogene。

\[5\]. Sinclair 声称 effect 已经完全消失，但从 study 中相关的 figure 来看，这一点完全不清楚；相反，看起来更像是 assay 为 underpowered

另一个 example 是 [epigenetic reprogramming](https://nintil.com/longevity#epigenetic-cellular-reprogramming)：通过 Genetically engineering，使 mice 能够 inducibly and transiently[3](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-3) express 一组 genes——OSKM factors - 它们通常会将 cell 推向 undifferentiated state——使 progeroid mice 的 maximum and median lifespan 得以 increase（分别为 20 和 33%）。这在 epigenetic clocks 中表现为 rejuvenation（例如 Sinclair lab 这篇 [preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/710210v1.full.pdf) 中的 Figure 2e 和 Figure 2h）。Salk Institute 的 Izpisua-Belmonte lab 所发表的 OSKM paper 使用一种 antibiotic 来 trigger 这些 OSKM factors，因此人们可能会认为，或许是 antibiotic causes 了 lifespan 的 improvement，但他们恰好为此设置了 controls。在 Sinclair lab 的 paper 中，同样是在 mice 中，他们还考察了 OSK[4](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-4) 是否通过 remodeling 这个 epigenome 来发挥 rejuvenating effects，因此他们尝试 knock-out [Tet1](https://epigenie.com/key-epigenetic-players/important-dna-methylation-factors/ten-eleven-translocation-tet-enzymes/) 或 Tet2（promote demethylation 的 enzymes），结果 effect 被 greatly reduced[5](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-5)，这 suggesting 这些 effects 是通过 epigenetic changes 被 mediated 的。在这篇 paper 中，他们对 mice 的 optic nerve 造成 injury，然后使用 OSK factors 来 treat 其 eye。除了 observing 这个 nerve 的 regeneration，他们还 observe 到了由 epigenetic clock measured 的 rejuvenation；反过来，在 inflicting damage 后，他们 observe 到了 epigenetic age 的 increase。当 human neurons 的 axons 遭受 damage、随后接受 treated 时，也出现了同样的情况。但 damage 听起来并不像 aging，那么为什么它会在 clocks 中表现出来？

关于什么 drives 这些 clocks 的 ticking，至少存在三种 competing views：一种认为，这仅仅是 epigenetic noise，它会 randomly gradually degrades 这个 epigenome 的 informational content。而另一种认为，它 reflects 一组 programmed instructions，这些 instructions 要么 causes aging（这是 programmed aging 阵营的主张），要么仅仅是对 aging 的 response。

直观来看，Sinclair 团队说 epigenetic aging 是 epigenome 中 noise 的 increasy，似乎并不正确。毕竟，如果 clocks 确实有效，而且它们在不同 individuals 中 picking up 的是 _相同的 CpGs_，那就意味着 aged epigenomes 是 similar 的；如果这只是 noise，我们就会预期每个 individual 都 exhibit 一个 distinct pattern[6](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-6)。此外，这不仅限于 humans，人们已经能够 design 跨 species 有效的 clocks，而 Horvath [声称](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LuQKXux8UlE)，他今年将拥有一个对他 tested 的所有 mammals 都有效的 clock。但这个 outcome 并没有那么令人惊讶：毕竟，methylated sites 往往会 lose methylation，而 unmethylated sites 则往往会 gain 它。这并不是 random gaussian noise，而是一种将 epigenome 的 informational content 带向混沌的 disregulated homogeneity 的 noise。

\[6\]. 人们可能会认为，如果这只是 noise，那么我们就能够 pick up 这个 dispersion 的 increase。但用 linear model 很难做到这一点，\*如果 noise 是 gaussian 的话\*（或者，如果每个 CpG 处的 noise 与其他 CpG 的 noise 并不 correlated，那就完全不可能）

那么，evidence 说明了什么？是 noise causes 了 degraded epigenome？还是说，这是一个 regulated process？

\[7\]. 一些 clocks（例如 Zhang 的）会 pick up 与 diabetes 或 cardiovascular disease related 的 sites。Raj and Horvath (2020) 说，总体而言，在不同 clocks 中，被 picked up 的 sites 都位于一些 genes 附近，这些 genes 受到 polycomb repressive complex 的 regulated（这是一种通过 methylating histones 来充当 silencer 的 protein），而且这些 genes 参与 cell cycle（growth、proliferation、death）。随着这些 sites 随 age 而变得 methylated——其原因尚不清楚——cell 会 loses plasticity：虽然受到 PRC regulated 的 histone 中的 methyl 较容易 remove，但由 methyltransferase 放置在附近 CpG 中的 methyl 却并非如此。

Field et al. ([2018](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.cell.com/molecular-cell/pdf/S1097-2765\(18\)30642-7.pdf&sa=D&ust=1591732669904000)) 指出，被 clocks picked up 的 CpGs 并没有 localized 在任何特定位置，它们所在的 islands 分布各处，其中一些是 shared 的，另一些则是 tissue-specific[7](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-7)。与 Sinclair paper 中的情况一样，并不一定与 age tied 的 conditions 和 damage 会使 epigenome 看起来 older（在 mice 中是 ovariectomy 或 high fat diet，在 humans 中则是 insomnia 或 stress）；反过来，已知能够 promote lifespan 的 interventions（例如 rapamycin 或 CR）会 slows down 这些 clocks。这篇 paper 引用的一些 authors 提出，所 observed 的 patterns 与一种 informational loss model 是 consistent with 的，在这种 model 中，methylation 变得更加 uniform：本应 methylated 的 Sites 变得 unmethylated，反之亦然。

然而，其他 authors 指出，这与 clock 从 embryo 一直到 puberty 所 pick up 的 changes 并不 concordant。按照这种观点，aging 应当是 programmed 的，因为 life 如此早期缺乏 damage，就会 rule out 这些 methylations 的 changes 是对 damage 的 response。Field et al. 不接受这一 explanation，因为还有其他 explanations，而且 programmed aging（在 group-selection 意义上）很难从 evolutionary 角度 explain。

相比之下，Horvath ([2013](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/gb-2013-14-10-r115)) 提出了一个 epigenetic maintenance system (EMS)，认为 clocks 可能 picking up 的正是它：这些 changes 可能与 system 工作的强度 tied。在 in utero 的 maturation 期间，由于发生了许多 changes，clock 会 ticks faster，但这意味着 EMS 必须做额外工作，才能使 epigenome 保持 stable。

最近，Raj and Horvath ([2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1535370220918329)) 似乎不再接受所 observed 的 patterns 是由 damage 或 noise 导致的这一观点

> 这里值得稍作展开，考虑这一观点的一个重要 implication。关于 ageing，一个直观的 assumption 是，它并非 life 的 intrinsic property，而是一种 extrinsically induced feature，“wear and tear” 一词概括的正是这一点。epigenetic ageing clock 在 embryonic stem cell stage 之后很快就 starts ticking，这一事实促使我们重新审视这一观点。evidence 表明，与 ageing related 的 processes 在 life 开始时就已经启动。对 specific CpGs 的 DNA methylation changes 的参与表明，这是一个非常 precise process，与 entropy 或 random- ness 并不 consistent，而后两者正是 damage 的 nature。不过，重要的是要考虑到，这些 changes 是为了 develop 这个 organism。因此，ageing process 是一个从 embryonic stem cells state 之后开始、一直延续到 death 的 continuum。所以，ageing 的 essence 很可能在于理解：为什么 developmental processes 会无意中 contribute to 这个 organism 的 ageing。需要注意的是，虽然 wear and tear 或 damage-related events 似乎并不是 epigenetic ageing 的“prime-mover”，但它们确实仍然 affect epigenetic ageing 的 rate。

相反，clock 的 ticking 可能与 stem cells 向 non-stem cells 的 differentiation related

> 我们可以 posit，clock 的 ticking 是 asymmetric stem cell division 的一种 measure，也就是说，当其中一个 daughter cells become 一个 transit-amplifying cell 时，它的 DNA methylation profile changes，而这表现为 tissue 的 methylation profile 的 alteration——也就是 clock 的 ticking。由于 stem cells 的 abundance 很 low，而且 undergo infrequent division，随时间发生的 tissues 的 DNA methylation changes 会非常 small，而这确实正是对 epigenetic clock 的 ticking 所 observed 到的。这种情形意味着，如果将 stem cells 从 tissues 中 isolated 出来（不含 transit- amplifying cells 和 fully differentiated cells）并进行 analyzed，那么它们的 DNA methylation profiles 所 indicate 的 epigenetic age 应当比 tissue 的 younger 得多。最近一份 report 确实 observed 到了这一点，表明从 mice 中 isolated 的 muscle stem cells 在 epigenetically 意义上要 younger 得多，而且这与它们来源 tissue/animal 的 ages 无关。同样，stem cells 所在的 small intestine 和 colon crypts，其 epigenetic age 比不含 stem cells 的 tissue 上部 younger。虽然这些本身并不 constitute proof，但它们仍然与这一观点 consistent：epigenetic clock 的 ticking 是由伴随 stem cells 向 non-stem cells（例如 transit-amplifying cells）的 differentiation 而发生的 methylation changes 所 constituted 的。$$...$$ multi-tissue age predictor 显示，这一 difference 的平均值为 0.032，相当于 cell population 的 3.2%。这 suggests，epigenetic age 的 increase 来自一个 population 中极小 percent 的 cells 的 methylation profiles 的 changes。

不过，这不可能是全部解释。如果 clock 的一次 tick 发生在 stem cells replicate 时，那么 cells 的 damage 为什么会使 non-stem cells age？对此的解释可能是，所 observed 的 pattern 存在不止一个 cause；其中一个 component 可能与 stem cell differentiation 和/或 symmetric replication 期间 epigenetic information 的 loss related，另一个则可能是对 damage 的 response。目前，人们正在努力对 clocks 所 picking up 的不同种类的 sites 进行 categorize 和 cluster，并 assess 这些 sites 的 functionality（[Liu et al., 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2019/01/04/512483.full.pdf)）。

最后，Raj 和 Horvath 关于 clocks 中 observed patterns 不易与“wear and tear”相协调的 assertion 并不令人信服。如果 aging 正如他们所说，与 stem cells 中 epigenetic information 的 lossy replication 有关，那么在最初的 embryonic stem cells 刚刚 formed 后，这一 mechanism 就仍会是 active 的。我们从出生时起就会在 aging。

## 与其他方法相比，这些 clocks 有多好？

最后，有必要将 clocks 与实现相同用途的其他 methods 进行 compare。对于 assessing chronological age，正如前面所述，correlations 如此之高，足以使相关 clocks（如 Horvath’s）即使不是 assess age 的最佳 method，也跻身最佳之列。在 original Horvath clock 中，average error 是 ~ 3.6 years。

\[8\]. 这并不意味着对于具体情况，我们找不到更好的 markers。例如，假设你是 50 岁，并被 diagnosed 为 pancreas cancer（5-year survival 是 7%）。一般而言，在某一年因 old age 而 dying 的 odds 会是 0.35%，所以在 5 years 后 dead 的 chances 是 1.73%，由此 yielding 的 HR 为 93%/1.73%=53

对于 mortality，事情变得更有意思。Sarah Constantin 在[这里](https://thelri.org/blog-and-news/biomarkers-for-predicting-mortality/)汇编了各种 biomarkers 的 Hazard Ratios (HR) 列表。这里的思路是，取一个 biomarker，在此处即 epigenetic age 的一个 measure，选取 scorers 中 top 5% 和 bottom 5%，并对各 group 在给定 period 内 die 的可能性求 ratio。好的 biomarker 应有 high ratio，意味着它能够 effectively discriminate 具有 higher 和 lower risk 的人。差的 biomarker 会有 HR=1，此时知道 marker 的 value 并不能提供任何关于 relative risk 的 information。话虽如此，GrimAge clock 是用于 predict mortality 的最佳 clock，因此来看它：在 original paper 中，AgeAccelGrim（expected epigenetic age 与 real age 之间的 Difference）中 top 95 与 bottom 5 之间的 hazard ratios 的 ratio 是 4。这使它直接位居列表榜首，成为一般情况下最好的 predictor[8](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-8) 。作一个直观比较，对于每天吸 10 支烟的 smokers，相对于 nonsmokers，smoking vs not smoking 的 mortality hazard ratio 是 1.9（[Jacobs et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10218754/)），因此这再次表明，当 clocks 针对所关注的 endpoints 进行 trained 时，它们确实是 reliable tools。

## 已知有效的 longevity interventions 会 affect 这些 clocks 吗？

如果我们知道 rapamycin 会 extends mice 的 lifespan，那么给它们 rapamycin 后，我们应该在 clocks 中看到它们的 predicted lifespan 出现 increase，与在 rats 的一个 cohort 中 empirically observed 的结果相似。实际会这样吗？

起初，似乎并不会。[Thompson et al. (2018)](https://www.aging-us.com/article/101590/text) 为 mice constructed 了 4 个 multi-tissue clocks，发现其 accuracies 与其他 lifespan clocks 相当，约为 80%。这些 results 有些 heterogeneous：他们发现 caloris restriction 有 positive effects：calorically restricted mice 比以 regular diet 喂养的 mice 要 younger 5 months（按 clocks 衡量）。对于 rapamycin，他们没有发现 effect；他们将这一事实归因于 small sample size（每个 group 只有 4 只 mice），或使用了不同的 sequencing technique。

maximum lifespan 已有大幅（65%）increases 的 Ames dwarf，也表现为 epigenetically younger，不过作者指出，并非所有 clocks 都 picked this up。我想在这里放入 rapamycin 的 figure，也借此指出，这一 result 还可能是由单个 outlier 导致的 statistical fluke：

![image-20200716173031853](https://nintil.com/images/2020-06-09-epigenetic-clocks/image-20200716173031853.png)

如果去掉 Chow group 中那个异常的 outlier，就会 observed 到 rapamycin 的 result。不过，其 effect 最终仍比 caloric restriction 的小。鉴于此，我们还不应就此否定这些 clocks；应该查看更多 studies。而且，这个 clock 并不是我们想用来 assess lifespan interventions 的那个：我们想要的是类似 GrimAge、专门 trained 来 predict years of life remaining 的东西。

幸好还有更多 studies：

[Wang et al. (2017)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-017-1186-2) 使用了 107 个 mice methylomes 和 148 个 CpGs，并应用 normalization 以处理不同 sequencing methods 的影响，developed 了一个 trained 来 predict age 的 clock。在这里，他们发现 differences 普遍更大：对于 age-matched mice，caloric restriction 的 effect 是 epigenetic age 的 9.4 decrease，而 rapamycin 则是 6 months，这表明 CR mimetics 或许不如 CR 本身。Young Drwarf Ames mice 比 regular Ames mice 略微 epigenetically younger，而 older mice 则 younger 15 months，这一 result 在直观上更符合这些 mice 的 extended lifespan。

[Petkovich et al. (2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1550413117301687) 制作了一个 blood-based clock，并发现 calorically restricted mice 的 epigenetic ages 要 lower 20%；Dwarf mice 看起来 younger 50%，与所报告的 extended lifespan 一致。

在另一个 mice multitissue clock 中，[Meer et al. 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/40675) 则按 tissue 拆分了他们的 predictions。对于 Snell Dwarf 或 CR mice，blood-based clock predicted 了其 lifespan advantage。奇怪的是，对于 Ames Dwarf mice（与 Petkovich 相反），lifespan effect 只在 liver clock 中被发现，作者将这一事实归因于

> 其他 DNAm estimators 使用的 CpG sites 中，covered 在这些 samples 中的 fewer than 90%，而在其他 intervention tests 中，所有 clocks 都有 high sites representation：每个 sample 都 more than 90%，on average 则 more than 95%。未能在这些 samples 中 detect 到 aging 的 slowdown，另一个原因可能是 DNAm levels 是使用 WGBS 来 estimated 的。虽然 liver methylation clock 同时使用 WGBS- 和 RRBS-produced samples 进行 trained，但这里研究的另外三个 models 并未使用 WGBS-produced samples 进行 training，因此预计它们在这些 samples 上会 perform worse。

在完全不同的 context 中（因此也更难 extrapolate），rapamycin 在 human cells in vitro 的 clocks 中也有体现（[Lujan et al. 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/803676v1.full.pdf), [Horvath et al., 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6555449/)）。

所以，是的，从涉及 cohort studies 和 experimental manipulations 的更 rigorous tests 来看，methylation clocks 是有效的。

## 结论

即使只 measured 了 epigenome 中少量的 CpGs，也已经能够 construct 出能 accurately track age 和 health 的 clocks。我们仍然不确切知道这些 clocks 为什么 work，只知道它们确实如此。有一些有趣的 evidence 指出，aged epigenome 中所见的 pattern 至少有一部分是 causal，而不只是 tissue 或 organism 的 overall condition 的 reflection，因此我们可能很快就会看到 epigenome 成为 novel drugs 的 target。

## 原文

It has recently been found possible to estimate age, mortality risk, or general health by looking merely at the epigenome. The models used to do so are referred to as _epigenetic (or methylation) clocks_.

Epigenetic clocks are increasingly becoming a popular choice for scientists in the field of aging research to measure the putative efficacy of anti-aging interventions. They may make it possible to get results before full Kaplan-Meier curves are available, and they could serve, at least seemingly, as a replacement for a host of other biomarkers. I recommend reading the introductory sections of [The Longevity FAQ](https://nintil.com/longevity) as well as those about epigenetics before reading this post as it gives some more context.

## Epigenetics

As a quick recap about epigenetics, the epigenome is a set of modifications to the DNA that do not alter the genetic code itself but regulates its expression and that enable cells to dynamically regulate their functions and maintain distinct states. Epigenetic marks take various forms, chiefly methylation of CpG sites—this is when a methyl group is attached to a C that is followed by a G—and also histone modification. Histones are proteins that form octamers around which the DNA is coiled. These histones have tails to which modifications can be made; typically acetylation (which opens up the chromatin and facilitates gene expression) or methylation (which can act both as a repressor and promoter of gene expression). Of particular interest is histone methylation induced by the Polycomb Repressive Complex (PRC), as these ones are easier for the cell to switch on and off relative to methylating or demethylating CpGs ([Bell et al., 2019](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-019-1824-y)).

\[1\]. Though this varies by tissue. In mice, 66% of CpGs are methylated in the liver, 81.6% in the lung

Most of these CpGs (70-80%) are methylated in mammalian tissue[1](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-1) ([Janitz & Janitz, 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/B9780123757098000125)), but if one breaks looks only at clusters of CpGs, known as islands, only 6-8% of the _islands_ are methylated in any one given tissue in humans ([Illingworth et al., 2008](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2214817/?report=classic)): Given all the kinds of cells there are, a given cell only needs a fraction of the genes to be active to do its functions. CpG methylation patterns are not meant to change much in the adult. Once the cell is fully differentiated, the cells seems to assume everything is going to stay fine with these CpG methylations, and so it mostly loses the capacity to methylate additional CpGs, and/or their ability to protect (maintenance methylation) the methylated CpG islands is reduced ([Cedar and Bergman, 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrg2540/), [Jeltsch & Jurkowska 2014](https://www.cell.com/trends/biochemical-sciences/pdf/S0968-0004\(14\)00087-5.pdf)).

![img](https://nintil.com/images/2020-06-09-epigenetic-clocks/DNAme_landscape.png)

Methylation pattern (Source: Wikipedia)

As we age, a distinct pattern emerges in the CpGs: The islands that were methylated and supposed to stay so begin to lose their methyls, and extraneous methylation marks appear too where they are not supposed to be. It is still unclear if this happens in every cell or in a subset, as that would require single cell epigenetic sequencing studies that have not been done yet. And it is also important to remember that the CpGs that researchers have looked at until now for correlations with age are a small subset of the set of CpGs across the entire genome, looking at a few hundred at a time in general. Even the most advanced methylation microarray (the Illumina MethylationEPIC) can only assess 850k sites, 3% of the total count of CpGs).

The approach seems a promising research avenue: This pattern of aging occurs to some extent across species and this pattern seems to not be a mere reaction to damage outside of the cell but an intrinsic property that persists even when the young cells are transplanted to an old body or vice versa ([Søraas et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/acel.12897)).

The extent to which various conditions (such as replicative senescence or repeated passaging) affect epigenetic age depend on the clock that is being used to measure said age. And even then the interpretation of the results is not obvious; for example [Horvath (2013)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24138928/) says that _A significant correlation between cell passage number and DNAm age can also be observed when restricting the analysis to iPS cells or when restricting the analysis to ES cells_. This does not seem to me to be the case from the figures they have (Figure 5H). In a later review ([Raj and Horvath](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1535370220918329), 2020) the same data is interpreted in what seems to me a more reasonable way, _extensive passaging of stem cells up to even a hundred times is not accompanied by increase in epigenetic age_. When contacted, Ken Raj told me that

> The reference can indeed cause confusion. While there appears to be a statistical correlation between ESC proliferation and age, this is not the case, when the data points are compared to the spread of ages of these cells, for which the vast majority are of pre-natal age regardless of the number of times they are passaged. Importantly I \[Ken Raj\] have repeated this experiment in my laboratory and can categorically confirm that proliferating ESC (and also iPSC) do not undergo epigenetic ageing. The new data will soon be published.

As embryonic stem cells are immortal ([Saez et al. 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5840266/)) the result that they do not epigenetically age makes some a priori sense. The probable outcome is that they do age, but very slowly, in a way echoing the results found for gametes and DNA mutations: They suffer them, but orders of magnitude less than the rest of cells.

**According to the Horvath clock used in Horvath (2013) and related work**, senescent cells don’t show up as aged in the clocks if senescence was induced via radiation or oncogene suppression; it is only in those cells that stay alive for long enough that they get epigenetically aged. Even if the cells are immortalized with telomerase, they still epigenetically age. Likewise, faster replication doesn’t make cells age faster; say you have a cell that in a period of time replicates itself faster and another that replicates slower, you then test the epigenetic age of cells in both populations at the end and they are similarly aged.

**However this is not the case for all the clocks**, and there are ongoing efforts to explore the effects of various conditions of epigenetic age, and better conceptualizing epigenetic age itself. The choice of sites to measure for clock design matters greatly; in a later section I discuss the variability of some results depending on what clock, and on what tissue, the methylation data is obtained from.

## A brief walk through the epigenetic clocks

\[2\]. You can make your own [here](https://galaxyproject.github.io/training-material/topics/statistics/tutorials/age-prediction-with-ml/tutorial.html)

Conceptually, epigenetic clocks are very simple[2](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-2) . One starts from CpG methylation data and an endpoint of interest, such as age or mortality risk. Then one fits an elastic net model (a mixed L1/L2 penalized linear regression) so ultimately one gets $DNAmAge=a+b1CpG_1+b2CpG_2+...$. This is good to frame a conservative case for the clocks: Unlike with fancier statistical techniques it is hard to fudge results using elastic nets; there are fewer hyperparameters to tweak, and being a simple model it is harder to overfit a training set.

The endpoint of interest initially was age, so one tries to predict age from the epigenome: The first such attempt (What later would be referred to as "the original Horvath clock") was Bockland et al. ([2011](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/labs/pmc/articles/PMC3120753/)), looking at 88 CpG sites, and already achieving a decent correlation (.85) with chronological age.

Two years after that is what [Hannum](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1097276512008933) (2013) did looking at 71 CpGs in the first batch of analyses. Note that clocks don’t just use the epigenome, Hannum also used various controls like age-adjusted BMI, gender, diabetes status, ethnicity, and a dummy for the dataset the data was taken from.

[Horvath](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24138928/) (2013) later did another clock based on 353 CpGs samples from various tissues; this time he used exclusively methylation and nothing else; but he used a function to transform age; this is the price to pay when working with linear models, one sometimes has to do some feature engineering to improve the model. This is justified because it does seem like epigenetic age, when plotted, has a logarithmic relation with age ([Snir et al., 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15592294.2019.1623634?journalCode=kepi20)), initially there is rapid growth, then it slows down.

[Levine et al.(2018)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5940111/) used a single dataset that unfortunately is not as easily available as the patchwork of datasets that Horvath put together (InCHIANTI and NHANES III) to come up with a composite metric, PhenoAge; as the the output of a model that aims to predict the 10-year mortality risk out of a set of phenotypical covariates of aging (albumin, creatinine, glucose level, CRP, lymphocyte %, mean red cell volume, red cell distribution width, alkaline phosphatase, white blood cell count, and age itself), so PhenoAge is correlated with, but not the same as, age; for example PhenoAge can be highly negative in some tissues. From here it’s the same elastic net model as in the Horvath paper. Here 513 CpGs were used. To find a metric to a mortality analysis that is not correlated with age (Age being a significant confounder in predicting aging, of course), they trained a linear model to predict age from PhenoAge, and then calculated the difference between that and chronological age (DNAm PhenoAgeAccel). It is this measure that they used for predict mortality risks.

GrimAge ([Lu et al.](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30669119/), 2019) used the Framingham Heart Study as training set and a different set of intermediate variables: They first train a model on methylation data to predict smoking pack-years and the levels of various proteins in blood, then using those estimates they trained another model to estimate time to death. In total, they used 1030 CpGs. As in the Levine paper, here an acceleration metric, AgeAccelGrim is similarly derived. As is discussed later, this clock has so far achieved the best results when it comes to predicting mortality.

As a wrapup, it’s worth mentioning that in retrospective, clocks tend to be grouped into first generation (Horvath and Hannum) and second generation (GrimAge and PhenoAge). The latter have a markedly higher accuracy predicting morbidity and mortality ([Levine, 2020](https://academic.oup.com/biomedgerontology/article/75/3/463/5717592)). One reason for this is that the earlier models have a built-in bias in their dataset: They are trained on cross-sectional data (You take a sample of people of various ages, get their CpGs and train the model) while the second generation clocks include longitudinal data (From the same set of people, tracked over time). For example, imagine there is one CpG that strongly correlated with early death. In a cross-sectional dataset you may only see that CpG in a small sample of the younger subjects; as the older ones would be dead, so you would have no way to know that that CpG later on has such a causal impact. As in the GrimAge paper, here we also find an age acceleration metric.

When looking at hazard risks from the clocks one should take care when reading the study, as publication bias has been found, per [Fransquet et al. (2019)](https://clinicalepigeneticsjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13148-019-0656-7/figures/3) for the Horvath and Hannum clocks (Which in any case one should not use for predicting mortality, that’s why one’d prefer GrimAge and PhenoAge)

At this point you may be thinking: Why keep using elastic nets? Why not throw neural networks at it! Of course, that has also been done, albeit only recently. [Levy et al. (2020)](https://bmcbioinformatics.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12859-020-3443-8) not only published a deep-net based epigenetic clock, but also made freely available the processing pipeline for other to reuse. However, as is the case with GWASes, a nonlinear model doesn’t improve much on the linear models; it seems there aren’t that many interactions between CpGs so at the end of the day performance is better than Horvath’s clock at predicting age, but not that much.

Something else someone might think is that these are referred to as methylation clocks, yet I’ve only been talking about CpG methylation. Why? Because it’s easier to get CpG methylation; non CpG methylation is rare (initially thought not to exist even), though it may be relevant in some tissues or organs like the brain ([Lister et al., 2013](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3785061/))

There is also work that directly addresses the question of comparing the clocks on the same dataset. In a recent preprint, [McCrory et al. (2020)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2020/04/28/2020.04.27.063164.full.pdf) again remark that second generation clocks work better to predict age-related conditions. However, when it comes to predicting all-cause mortality (In a 10 year followup in this case), it is only GrimAge that shines. One standard deviation increase in the AgeAcccelGrim score increases the odds of not making it past 10 years by 18%. PhenoAge does a bit better than the first generation clocks but the increase ends up being statistically nonsignificant. The paper presents two models when examining each clock, one unadjusted and another adjusted for some other covariates (age, “social class trajectory”, smoking history, physical activity, hazardous drinking, or BMI). The relations weaken when one applies these controls. It shouldn’t be necessary to control for age as the acceleration measures by construction are not related to age. Controlling for the other factors could be problematic.

As an example, consider BMI. What is the causal association between BMI, epigenetic age acceleration, and mortality? So far we’ve been just talking about correlations, so various causal diagrams are compatible with what we have found. It could be:

![img](https://nintil.com/images/2020-06-09-epigenetic-clocks/image2.png)

This graph actually represents two possibilities: BMI only causes AgeAccel (In which case controlling for BMI does nothing to the association between AA and mortality), and BMI causes mortality directly and also through AgeAccel (In which case controlling for BMI would get a better estimate of the AA-Mortality correlation).

But it could also be like this:

In this case AgeAccel causes mortality partially through BMI (Imagine the damaged epigenome disregulates metabolism). In that case controlling for BMI would attenuate a legitimate correlation and would leave us with just the direct channel (If there is one).

It could also be

In this case there would be a correlation between mortality and AgeAccel but it would not be causal! This leads us to the question of causality. Is an aged epigenome causing aging? Or is it aging that causes an aged epigenome?

## In which way does causality run?

Here we would want to manipulate the epigenome directly and see if we observe an aged phenotype. If we don’t then it means that the aged epigenome is a mere epiphenomenon and not something one can causally use as a target in drug development. Experiments to directly do this have not been done yet, so we only have indirect evidence.

David Sinclair mentions an experiment done with “[ICE mice](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2019/10/21/808659.full.pdf)” (Inducible Changes to the Epigenome), genetically engineered mice that express on demand a gene that causes DNA double stranded breaks. On Sinclair’s theory, sirtuins—what they do is deacetylate proteins, mostly histones; and in turn this allows the chromatin to be more tightly packed, reducing gene expression around the deacetylated site—are typically acting to diminish gene expression in specific sites. But when there is a DNA (double-stranded) break, there is a need for deacetylation in the break which draws the sirtuins there to aid in the repair, but this reduces their deacetylating activity elsewhere: Genes that should be silent then become expressed.

Thus the ICE mice allow one to indirectly damage the epigenome through damaging the genome. Compared to a control group of ICE mice that had not been induced DNA damage, they were 50% older epigenetically. Phenotypically they showed all sorts of age-related conditions as loss of fur, body mass, muscle strength, or increase in arthritis and loss of sight.

\[3\]. In the Longevity FAQ I mention some ways this can be achieved, adeno-associated viruses and a [genetic cassette that becomes active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetracycline-controlled_transcriptional_activation) in the presence of antibiotic doxycycline are two forms.

\[4\]. Turns out you can get the same results with just the OSK factors. The Sinclair team deems Myc an unnecessary oncogene.

\[5\]. Sinclair claims the effect is completely gone but this is not clear at all from the relevant figure in the study; rather it looks like the assay is underpowered

Another example is [epigenetic reprogramming](https://nintil.com/longevity#epigenetic-cellular-reprogramming): Genetically engineering mice that inducibly and transiently[3](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-3) express a set of genes—OSKM factors - that would generally move the cell towards an undifferentiated state—led to an increase in maximum and median lifespan (20 and 33% respectively) in progeroid mice. This shows up as rejuvenation in the epigenetic clocks (An example would be Figure 2e and Figure 2h of this [preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/710210v1.full.pdf) from the Sinclair lab). The OSKM paper from the Izpisua-Belmonte lab at the Salk Institute uses an antibiotic to trigger the OSKM factors, so one might think that perhaps the antibiotic is what causes the improvement in lifespan, but they had controls for precisely that. In the Sinclair lab paper, again in mice, they also looked at whether or not OSK[4](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-4) is having its rejuvenating effects through remodeling the epigenome, so they tried to knock-out [Tet1](https://epigenie.com/key-epigenetic-players/important-dna-methylation-factors/ten-eleven-translocation-tet-enzymes/) or Tet2 (enzymes that promote demethylation) and the effect was greatly reduced[5](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-5) , suggesting that the effects were mediated through epigenetic changes. In the paper, they cause an injury to the optic nerve in mice and then they treat the eye with the OSK factors. Besides observing regeneration of the nerve, they observe rejuvenation as measured by an epigenetic clock; and conversely upon inflicting damage they observe an increase in epigenetic age. The same is true for human neurons when damage is done to their axons and then treated. But damage does not sound like aging, so why does it show up in the clocks?

There are at least three competing views as to what drives the ticking of these clocks: One is that it is just epigenetic noise that randomly gradually degrades the informational content of the epigenome. But another is that it reflects a programmed set of instructions that either causes aging (this is what the programmed aging camp claims) or that merely is a response to aging.

Naively, it would seem like the Sinclair group is not right in saying that epigenetic aging is an increasy in noise in the epigenome. After all, if the clocks work and they are picking up _the same CpGs_ across individuals, that means aged epigenomes are similar; if it were just noise we would expect each individual to exhibit a distinct pattern[6](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-6) . Furthermore it’s not just humans, it has been possible to design clocks that work across species, and Horvath [claims](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LuQKXux8UlE) he will have one this year that works for all mammals he tested. But this outcome is not that surprising: After all, methylated sites will tend to lose methylation and unmethylated sites, to gain it. It's not random gaussian noise but instead noise that brings the informational content of the epigenome to a mushy disregulated homogeneity.

\[6\]. One might think that if it's just noise then we can pick up the increase in dispersion. But this is hard to do with a linear model \*if the noise is gaussian\* (Or outright impossible if the noise at each CpG is not correlated to the others)

So what does the evidence say? Is it noise that causes a degraded epigenome? Or is it a regulated process?

\[7\]. Some clocks (Like Zhang’s) pick up sites related to diabetes or cardiovascular disease. Raj and Horvath (2020) says that in general, across clocks, sites picked up are near genes that are regulated by polycomb repressive complex (A protein that acts as a silencer by methylating histones), and these genes are involved in the cell cycle (growth, proliferation, death). As these sites get methylated with age - the reason why they do is unknown - the cell loses plasticity: While the methyl in the histone regulated by the PRC is easier to remove, the one placed in a nearby CpG by a methyltransferase is not.

Field et al. ([2018](https://www.google.com/url?q=https://www.cell.com/molecular-cell/pdf/S1097-2765\(18\)30642-7.pdf&sa=D&ust=1591732669904000)) note that the CpGs that are picked up by the clocks are not localized anywhere in particular, the islands they are in are all over the place, with some being shared and others being tissue-specific[7](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-7) . As in the Sinclair paper, conditions and damage that are not necessarily tied to age make the epigenome look older (ovariectomy or a high fat diet in mice, in humans, insomnia or stress); conversely interventions that are known to promote lifespan (like rapamycin or CR) slows down the clocks. Some authors referenced in the paper have suggested that the patterns observed are consistent with an informational loss model where methylation becomes more uniform: Sites that should be methylated become unmethylated and vice versa.

However other authors have pointed out that this is not concordant with the changes the clock pick up all the way from the embryo through puberty. On this view, aging would be programmed, as the lack of damage so early in life would rule out the changes in methylations being a response to it. Field et al. dismiss this explanation as there are other explanations available, and programmed aging (in the group-selection sense) is hard to explain on evolutionary terms.

In contrast, Horvath ([2013](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/gb-2013-14-10-r115)) proposed an epigenetic maintenance system (EMS) which the clocks would be picking up: The changes would be tied to how hard the system is working. During maturation in utero, with many changes happening, the clock ticks faster, but this would mean that the EMS has to do extra work to keep the epigenome stable.

More recently, Raj and Horvath ([2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1535370220918329)) seems to reject the idea that the patterns observed are due to damage or noise

> It is worth digressing a little at this point to consider an important implication of this notion. An intuitive assumption of ageing is that it is not an intrinsic property of life, but an extrinsically induced feature, which is encapsulated in the term “wear and tear”. The fact that epigenetic ageing clock starts ticking very soon after the embryonic stem cell stage, prompts a re-assessment of this perspective. The evidence suggest that processes related to ageing are already afoot at the start of life. The involvement of DNA methylation changes to specific CpGs indicates that this is a very precise process that is inconsistent with entropy or random- ness, which is the nature of damage. It is important to consider, however, that these changes are meant to develop the organism. Hence, the ageing process is a continuum which begins after the embryonic stem cells state through to death. Therefore, the essence of ageing might well lie in understanding why developmental processes inadvertently contribute to ageing of the organism. It is important to note that although wear and tear or damage-related events appear not to be the “prime-mover” of epigenetic ageing, they do nevertheless affect the rate of epigenetic ageing.

Instead, the ticking of the clock may be related to the differentiation of stem cells into non-stem cells

> One could posit that the ticking of the clock is a measure of asymmetric stem cell division, i.e. when one of the daughter cells become a transit-amplifying cell, its DNA methylation profile changes and this is manifested as alteration of the methylation profile of the tissue—the ticking of the clock. As stem cells are in low abundance and undergo infrequent division, the DNA methylation changes of tissues that occur in time would be very small, which is indeed observed for the ticking of the epigenetic clock. This scenario would suggest that were stem cells to be isolated from tissues (free of transit- amplifying cells and fully differentiated cells) and analyzed, their DNA methylation profiles would indicate much younger epigenetic age than the tissue. This was indeed observed in a recent report showing that muscle stem cells isolated from mice were epigenetically much younger independently of the ages of the tissue/animal from which they were derived. Likewise, small intestine and colon crypts, in which stem cells reside have younger epigenetic age than the upper portion of the tissue which are devoid of stem cells. Although these do not by themselves constitute proof, it is nevertheless consistent with the notion that ticking of the epigenetic clock is constituted by methylation changes that accompany the differentiation of stem cells into non-stem cells (such as transit-amplifying cells). \[...\] The multi-tissue age predictor shows this difference to be on average, 0.032, which translates to 3.2% of the cell population. This suggests that increase in epigenetic age is contributed by changes of methylation profiles in a very small percent of cells in a population.

This cannot be the whole story though. If a tick in the clock occurs when stem cells replicate, why would damage to cells age non-stem cells? The solution to this is that probably there is more than one cause for the observed pattern; one component might be related to the loss of epigenetic information during stem cell differentiation and/or symmetric replication, another could be a response to damage. There is an ongoing effort to categorize and cluster the different kinds of sites that the clocks are picking up and to assess the functionality of those sites ([Liu et al., 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2019/01/04/512483.full.pdf)).

Finally, Raj and Horvath's assertion that observed patterns in the clocks are not easily reconcilable with "wear and tear" is not compelling. If aging is, as they say, related to lossy replication of epigenetic information in stem cells then that mechanism would still be active right after the first embryonic stem cells are formed. We would be aging right from when we are born.

## How good are the clocks, compared to something else?

To finish off, it is important to compare the clocks to other methods to do what the clocks do. For assessing chronological age as noted earlier the correlations are so high so as to put the relevant clocks (Like Horvath’s) among the top if not the top method to assess age. In the original Horvath clock, the average error is ~ 3.6 years.

\[8\]. This doesn’t mean that for concrete cases we can’t find better markers. For example say you are 50 and are diagnosed with pancreas cancer (5-year survival is 7%). The odds of dying on a given year from old age in general would be 0.35%, so 1.73% chances of being dead after 5 years, yielding a HR of 93%/1.73%=53

For mortality things get more interesting. Sarah Constantin has compiled [here](https://thelri.org/blog-and-news/biomarkers-for-predicting-mortality/) a list of Hazard Ratios (HR) for various biomarkers. The idea here is to take a biomarker, in this case a measure of epigenetic age, take the top 5% and the bottom 5% scorers and do a ratio of how likely each group is to die on a given period. A good biomarker should have a high ratio, meaning that it is able to effectively discriminate between those that have higher and lower risk. A poor biomarker would have a HR=1, where knowing the value of the marker doesn’t give any information at all about relative risk. With that said, the GrimAge clock is the best clock to predict mortality so looking at it, the ratio in hazard ratios between the top 95 and bottom 5 in AgeAccelGrim (Difference between expected epigenetic age and real age), from the original paper is 4. This places it right at the top of the list as the best predictor there is in general[8](https://nintil.com/epigenetic-clocks/#sidenote-8) . For an intuitive comparison, the hazard ratio for mortality of smoking vs not smoking is 1.9 for smokers of 10 cigarettes a day relative to nonsmokers ([Jacobs et al., 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10218754/)), so this again shows that clocks are indeed reliable tools when they are trained on the endpoints of interest.

## Do longevity interventions known to work affect the clocks?

If we know rapamycin extends the lifespan of mice, if we give them rapamycin we should see an increase in their predicted lifespan in the clocks similar to that empirically observed for a cohort of rats. Does this happen?

At first, it seemed that no. [Thompson et al. (2018)](https://www.aging-us.com/article/101590/text) constructed 4 multi-tissue clocks for mice, finding accuracies comparable to other lifespan clocks of around 80%. The results were somewhat heterogeneous: They found positive effects for caloris restriction: calorically restricted mice were 5 months younger (per the clocks) than mice fed a regular diet. With rapamycin they didn't find an effect, fact they attribute to the small sample size (Just 4 mice in each group) or using a different sequencing technique .

Ames dwarf, which have achieved large (65%) increases in their maximum lifespan showed up as being epigenetically younger too, however the authors note that not all the clocks picked this up. I want to include here the rapamycin figure to also bring up the fact that the result might also have been a statistical fluke due to a single outlier:

![image-20200716173031853](https://nintil.com/images/2020-06-09-epigenetic-clocks/image-20200716173031853.png)

If you take out the odd outlier in the Chow group, then a result for rapamycin would be observed. Still, the effect ends up being smaller than for caloric restriction. Given this, we shouldn't disregard the clocks just yet; we should look at more studies. And also, this clock is not the one we want to assess lifespan interventions: We want something that's like GrimAge, trained to predict specifically years of life remaining.

Fortunately there are more studies:

Using 107 mice methylomes and 148 CpGs and normalization applied to account for the different sequencing methods, [Wang et al. (2017)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13059-017-1186-2) they developed a clock trained to predict age. Here they found uniformly larger differences: 9.4 decrease in epigenetic age as the effect of caloric restriction for age-matched mice, and 6 months for rapamycin, showing that perhaps CR mimetics may not be as good as CR itself. Young Drwarf Ames mice were slightly epigenetically younger than regular Ames mice, with older mice being 15 months younger, a result more intuitively coherent with the extended lifespan of these mice.

[Petkovich et al. (2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1550413117301687) did a blood-based clock and found 20% lower epigenetic ages for calorically restricted mice; Dwarf mice appeared to be 50% younger, consistent with teir reported extended lifespan.

In yet another mice multitissue clock, [Meer et al. 2018](https://elifesciences.org/articles/40675) instead split their predictions by tissue. For Snell Dwarf or CR mice the blood-based clock predicted its lifespan advantage. Oddly for Ames Dwarf mice (contrary to Petkovich) the lifespan effect was only found in a liver clock, a fact the authors attribute to the fact that

> fewer than 90% of CpG sites used by other DNAm estimators were covered in these samples, while in the other intervention tests all clocks had high sites representation: more than 90% for each sample and more than 95% on average. Another reason for the failure to detect slowdown of aging in these samples might be that DNAm levels were estimated using WGBS. While the liver methylation clock was trained on both WGBS- and RRBS-produced samples, three other models studied here did not use WGBS-produced samples for training and thus they were expected to perform worse on these samples.

In a completely different context (and thus harder to extrapolate), rapamycin also shows up in the clocks in human cells in vitro ([Lujan et al. 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/803676v1.full.pdf), [Horvath et al., 2019](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6555449/)).

So yes, looking at more rigorous tests involving cohort studies and experimental manipulations, methylation clocks work.

## Conclusion

Even with a small number of the CpGs of the epigenome measured, it has been possible to construct clocks that accurately track age and health. We still don't know exactly why the clocks work, just that they do. There is some interesting evidence pointing out to at least part of the pattern seem in the aged epigenome being causal, not just a reflection of the overall condition of the tissue or organism, so we may soon see the epigenome becoming a target for novel drugs.

If you want to continue reading about this, Bell et. al's [review](https://genomebiology.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s13059-019-1824-y) (from where I extract the table below) and Raj and Horvath 2020 are the best starting points.
