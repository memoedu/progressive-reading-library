# Interface abusers

---

## 正文

我现在正在 DC 一栋租下来的联排住宅里参加 Wave 公司的团建，昨晚我的房间冷得要命。今晚，同事找到了我们这一层的 thermostat，我问她能不能把它 turn it off。

“我不确定有没有办法做到，”她说。

原来，我们的 thermostat 是一台 Nest，而 Nest thermostats 简直_完全让人摸不着头脑_，你得胡乱试上一堆操作，直到其中某个操作弄出点有意思的动静。

如果你做出 Nest 所 affords 的那个显而易见的操作，也就是 twist the edge（我这里说的“显而易见”可相当宽泛），你就能 control the temperature。但你没法把它 turn it off。要把它 turn it off，你得先 bringing up 某种“main menu”；而要 bring up 这个 main menu，你得 _pushing on the screen_（顺便说一句，对于其他任何 non-touchscreen device，比如 TVs 或 laptops，这恰恰是你_不该_做的事）。当然，没有任何东西会告诉你可以 push on the screen——你只能凭直觉猜到这样能行。

我用 mobile apps 时也遇到这个问题。想从一个由 entries 组成的 list 中 delete 某个东西？也许你应该对它 swipe right！或者 swipe left，再 press 随之出现的“delete” button！或者 long-swipe left，因为 short-swipe 会做别的事！又或者，你得对那个 item 做一次 long press，来 bring up 一个包含“delete”的 menu？再不然，你可以对 list 顶部的“edit” text 进行 tap on，然后 check off 那个 item，再 tap 那个不知从哪儿冒出来的“delete” button。（但愿你注意到了它！）

不管答案是什么，有一点你尽可以确信：光看 interface，你会_毫无头绪_。人们似乎已经被 mobile apps 所 offer 的那些令人兴奋的新“user interface conventions”彻底冲昏了头，忘了你的 app 得让那些没有背熟眼下时髦的神奇 gestures 的人也能看懂。

## 原文

I’m at a Wave company retreat right now in a rented-out townhouse in DC, and my room was freezing last night. Tonight my coworker found the thermostat for our floor and I asked if she could turn it off.

“I’m not sure if there’s a way to do that,” she said.

It turned out that our thermostat was a Nest and Nest thermostats are _totally inscrutable_ until you do a bunch of random things and one of them does something interesting.

If you do the obvious thing that the Nest affords and twist the edge (and I use “obvious” loosely here), you can control the temperature. But you can’t turn it off. The way you turn it off is by bringing up some kind of “main menu”, and the way you bring up the main menu is by _pushing on the screen_ (which by the way is exactly _not_ what you’re supposed to do on every other non-touchscreen device, like TVs or laptops). Of course, nothing lets you know that you can push on the screen—you just have to intuit that it works.

I have this problem with mobile apps too. Want to delete something from a list of entries? Maybe you should swipe right on it! Or swipe left and press the “delete” button that appears! Or long-swipe left because short-swipe does something else! Or maybe it’s a long press on the item to bring up a menu that includes “delete”? Or, alternately, you could tap on the “edit” text at the top of the list, then check off the item and tap the “delete” button that appeared out of nowhere. (I hope you noticed it!)

Whatever the answer is, the one thing that you can count on is that you’ll have _no idea_ from just looking at the interface. People seem to have gotten completely carried away by the exciting new “user interface conventions” that mobile apps offer, and forgotten that your app needs to be understandable to someone who hasn’t memorized the currently-fashionable magic gestures du jour.
