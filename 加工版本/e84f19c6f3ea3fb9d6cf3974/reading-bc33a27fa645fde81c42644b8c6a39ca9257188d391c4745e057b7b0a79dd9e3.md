### 关于 DENYING PLAINTIFFS 针对与 ADOBE、APPLE、GOOGLE 和 INTEL 的 SETTLEMENTS 提出的 MOTION FOR PRELIMINARY APPROVAL 的 ORDER

现提交本 Court 审理的是三位 class representatives，即 Mark Fichtner、Siddharth Hariharan 和 Daniel Stover（以下简称“Plaintiffs”）针对与 Defendants Adobe Systems Inc.（“Adobe”）、Apple Inc.（“Apple”）、Google Inc.（“Google”）和 Intel Corp.（“Intel”）（以下简称“Remaining Defendants”）的 Class Action Settlement 提出的 Motion for Preliminary Approval。参见 ECF No. 920。该 Settlement 规定，class 获得 \$324.5 million 的 recovery，以换取 antitrust claims 的 release。第四位 class representative Michael Devine（“Devine”）已 filed 一份 Opposition，主张 settlement amount 是 inadequate。参见 ECF No. 934。Plaintiffs 已 filed 一份 Reply。参见 ECF No. 938。Plaintiffs、Remaining Defendants 和 Devine 于 June 19, 2014 出席了 hearing。参见 ECF No. 940。此外，多名 Class members 已提交信函，分别支持和反对该 proposed settlement。ECF Nos. 914, 949-51。本 Court 在考虑了 briefing、信函、hearing 中提出的 arguments 以及本 case 的 record 后，基于下述理由，对该 Motion for Preliminary Approval 予以 DENIES。

### I. 背景与 PROCEDURAL HISTORY

Michael Devine、Mark Fichtner、Siddharth Hariharan 和 Daniel Stover 以个人身份并代表由所有 similarly situated 者组成的 class，针对其前 employers Adobe、Apple、Google、Intel、Intuit Inc.（“Intuit”）、Lucasfilm Ltd.（“Lucasfilm”）和 Pixar（统称“Defendants”）提出 antitrust claims。Plaintiffs allege，Defendants 通过一系列不 solicit 彼此 employees 的 bilateral agreements，entered into 一项 overarching conspiracy，违反了 Section 1 of the Sherman Antitrust Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1，以及 Section 4 of the Clayton Antitrust Act, 15 U.S.C. § 15。Plaintiffs 主张，该 overarching conspiracy 由一系列共六项 bilateral agreements（Pixar-Lucasfilm、Apple-Adobe、Apple-Google、Apple-Pixar、Google-Intuit 和 Google-Intel）构成，并 suppressed 了 Defendants 的 employees 的 wages。

构成本 consolidated action 的五起 cases 最初在 California Superior Court filed，随后被 removed 至 federal court。参见 ECF No. 532 at 5。这些 cases 由 Judge Saundra Brown Armstrong 作 related 处理，她还 granted 了一项将这些 related actions transfer 至 San Jose Division 的 motion。参见 ECF Nos. 52, 58。在被 assigned 给署名 judge 后，这些 cases 依照 parties 的 stipulation 被 consolidated。参见 ECF No. 64。Plaintiffs 于 September 23, 2011 filed 一份 consolidated complaint，参见 ECF No. 65，Defendants 对此共同 moved to dismiss，参见 ECF No. 79。此外，Lucasfilm 于 October 17, 2011 filed 一份单独的 motion to dismiss。参见 ECF No. 83。本 Court 对该 joint motion to dismiss 作 granted in part 和 denied in part 处理，并 denied 了 Lucasfilm 的单独 motion to dismiss。参见 ECF No. 119。

Plaintiffs 于 October 1, 2012 filed 一份 motion for class certification。参见 ECF No. 187。该 motion 寻求对由全部七名 Defendants 的所有 employees 组成的 class 进行 certification，或者作为替代，对仅由这七名 Defendants 的 technical employees 组成的较窄 class 进行 certification。在完整的 briefing 和一次 hearing 后，本 Court 于 April 5, 2013 denied class certification。参见 ECF No. 382。本 Court 担忧，Plaintiffs 的 documentary evidence 和 empirical analysis 不足以 determine，就 antitrust impact 这一 issue 而言，common questions predominated over individual questions。参见 id. at 33。此外，本 Court 表示担忧，该 class certification motion 中针对 technical employees 所组成的 class 的 analysis 不足。Id. at 29。本 Court 给予 Plaintiffs leave to amend，以回应本 Court 的担忧。参见 id. at 52。

Plaintiffs 于 May 10, 2013 filed 其 amended class certification motion，仅寻求 certify 由 technical employees 组成的较窄 class。参见 ECF No. 418。Defendants 于 June 21, 2013 filed 其 opposition，ECF No. 439，而 Plaintiffs 于 July 12, 2013 filed 其 reply，ECF No. 455。针对该 amended motion 的 hearing 定于 August 5, 2013 举行。

在 class certification 最初已被 denied、而 amended motion 尚处于 pending 状态期间，Plaintiffs 于 July 12 and 30, 2013 与 Pixar、Lucasfilm 和 Intuit（以下简称“Settled Defendants”）settled。参见 ECF Nos. 453, 489。Plaintiffs 于 September 21, 2013 filed 一份 motion，寻求对与 Settled Defendants 的 settlements 给予 preliminary approval。参见 ECF No. 501。无人就该 motion filed opposition，本 Court 在 October 21, 2013 举行 hearing 后，于 October 30, 2013 granted 该 motion。参见 ECF No. 540。本 Court 于 May 1, 2014 举行 fairness hearing，ECF No. 913，并于 May 16, 2014 在存在五项 objections 的情况下，对这些 settlements 及随附的 attorneys' fees、costs 和 incentive awards 的 requests 给予 final approval，ECF Nos. 915-16。针对 Settled Defendants 的 Judgment 于 June 20, 2014 entered。ECF No. 947。

在 Settled Defendants settled 后，本 Court 于 October 25, 2013 通过一份 86 页的 order，granted Plaintiffs 的 amended class certification motion，并 certified 了由七名 Defendants 的 technical employees 组成的 class（以下简称“the Class”）。参见 ECF No. 532。Remaining Defendants 根据 Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(f)，向 Ninth Circuit petitioned，寻求对该 order 进行 review。在完整的 briefing 后，其中包括 National and California Chambers of Commerce 与 National Association of Manufacturing filed 一份 amicus brief，敦促 Ninth Circuit grant review，Ninth Circuit 于 January 15, 2014 denied review。参见 ECF No. 594。

与此同时，Remaining Defendants 在本 Court 共 filed 五份 motions for summary judgment，并 filed motions，寻求 strike 和 exclude Plaintiffs 关于 antitrust impact 和 damages 的 principal expert Dr. Edward Leamer 的 testimony；他 opined，Class 的 total damages 超过 \$3 billion，即若不存在 anti-solicitation agreements，Class members 本应获得的 wages。[1](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:1) 本 Court 于 March 28, 2014 denied 这些 motions for summary judgment，并于 April 4, 2014 denied 该 motion to exclude Dr. Leamer，且在很大程度上 denied 该 motion to strike Dr. Leamer 的 testimony。ECF Nos. 777, 788。

Plaintiffs 的 counsel 与 Remaining Defendants 的 counsel 于 April 24, 2014 向本 Court 发送一封 joint letter，表示双方已达成 settlement。参见 ECF No. 900。该 settlement 达成于 Final Pretrial Conference 前两周，距离 trial 原定 commence 的日期尚有一个月。[2](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:2): 收到该 joint letter 后，本 Court vacated 了 trial date 和 pretrial deadlines，并为 preliminary approval 制定了 schedule。参见 ECF No. 904。在 counsel 发出信函后不久，媒体披露了 settlement 的 total amount，本 Court 随后收到三位个人针对媒体关于 settlement amount 的报道而提交的信函，反对该 proposed settlement；这三人不包括 Devine。[3](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:3) 参见 ECF No. 914。Plaintiffs 按照本 Court 的 schedule，于 May 22, 2014 filed 其 Motion for Preliminary Approval。参见 ECF No. 920。Devine 于 June 5, 2014 filed 一份 Opposition。[4](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:4) 参见 ECF No. 934。Plaintiffs 于 June 12, 2014 filed 一份 Reply。参见 ECF No. 938。本 Court 于 June 19, 2014 举行 hearing。参见 ECF No. 948。在 hearing 后，本 Court 收到一名 Class member 反对该 proposed settlement 的信函，以及两名 Class members 支持该 proposed settlement 的信函。参见 ECF Nos. 949-51。

### II. LEGAL STANDARD（法律标准）

Court 必须根据 Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(e) review class action settlements 的 fairness。该 Rule 规定：“certified class 的 claims、issues 或 defenses 只有在获得 court 的 approval 后，才可以被 settled、voluntarily dismissed 或 compromised。”该 Rule 要求 Court“以 reasonable manner 向所有将受该 proposal bound 的 class members direct notice”，并进一步规定，如果 settlement“将 bind class members，court 只有在 hearing 之后，并 finding 其是 fair、reasonable 且 adequate 时，才可以 approve 它”。Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(e)(1)-(2)。Court 对 class action settlements 进行 supervision 的主要目的是确保“该 agreement 不是 negotiating parties 的 fraud 或 overreaching 的产物，也不是他们之间 collusion 的产物”。Officers for Justice v. Civil Serv. Comm'n of City & Cnty. of S.F., 688 F.2d 615, 625 (9th Cir. 1982)。

District courts 已将 Rule 23(e) interpreted 为要求通过 two-step process 对 class action settlements 进行 approval：“Court 首先 determines 拟议的 class action settlement 是否值得 preliminary approval，然后，在向 class members 发出 notice 后，再 determines 是否有理由给予 final approval。”Nat'l Rural Telecomms. Coop. v. DIRECTV, Inc., 221 F.R.D. 523, 525 (C.D. Cal. 2004)。在 final approval 阶段，Ninth Circuit 曾指出：“对 settlement proposal 进行 assessing，需要 district court balance 多项 factors：plaintiffs 的 case 的 strength；进一步 litigation 的 risk、expense、complexity 和可能的 duration；在整个 trial 期间 maintaining class action status 的 risk；settlement 中 offered 的 amount；已完成的 discovery 的 extent 和 proceedings 的 stage；counsel 的 experience 和 views；是否有 governmental participant；以及 class members 对拟议的 settlement 的 reaction。”Hanlon v. Chrysler Corp., 150 F.3d 1011, 1026 (9th Cir. 1998)。

与这些用于 final approval 的 well-established、non-exhaustive factors 相比，关于 district court 在 preliminary approval 阶段 reviewing settlement 时必须 apply 的 standard，相关 appellate authority 相对较少。一些 district courts 呼应 commentators 的看法，指出相关 inquiry 是该 settlement 是否“falls within the range of possible approval”或“处于 range of reasonableness 之内”。In re Tableware Antitrust Litig., 484 F. Supp. 2d 1078, 1079 (N.D. Cal. 2007)；另见 Cordy v. USS-Posco Indus., No. 12-553, 2013 WL 4028627, at \*3 (N.D. Cal. Aug. 1, 2013)（“如果拟议的 settlement 看来是 serious、informed、non-collusive negotiations 的产物，没有 obvious deficiencies，没有 improperly grant preferential treatment 给 class representatives 或 class 的 segments，并且 falls with the range of possible approval，那么给予 settlement Preliminary approval 并向拟议的 class 发出 notice 就是 appropriate 的。”（已省略内部引号））。为了开展这一 analysis，Court“必须 consider plaintiffs 的 expected recovery，并将其与 settlement offer 的 value balanced against”。In re Nat'l Football League Players' Concussion Injury Litig., 961 F. Supp. 2d 708, 714 (E.D. Pa. 2014)（已省略内部引号）。

### III. DISCUSSION（讨论）

根据本次 settlement 的 terms，尚未 opted out 且之后也不 opt out 的 Class members 将 relinquish 其就本 case 中 at issue 的 claims 针对 Remaining Defendants file suit 的 rights。作为交换，Remaining Defendants 将支付总计 \$324.5 million, of which Plaintiffs' counsel may seek up to 25% (approximately \$81 million）的 attorneys' fees，以及每位 class representative \$1.2 million in costs, and \$80,000 的 incentive payments。此外，如果超过 4% 的 Class members 在收到 notice 后 opt out，该 settlement 允许 Remaining Defendants 对其必须支付的 total amount 进行 pro rata reduction。[5](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:5) 如果 Court grant 所有 requested deductions，且不再有进一步的 opt-outs，Class members 将从本次 settlement 中平均获得约 $3,750[6](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:6)。[7](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:7)

Court finds，total settlement amount falls below the range of reasonableness。Court 担忧，尽管自一年前以来，case 一直朝着有利于 Class 的方向 progressed，但 Class members 从本次与 Remaining Defendants 达成的 settlement 中按 proportional basis recover 的金额，却低于一年前与 Settled Defendants 达成的 settlement。对于这一 reduced figure，Counsel 唯一的解释是 Plaintiffs 的 case 存在 weaknesses，使 Class 面临 substantial risk of non-recovery。然而，一年前 Plaintiffs 与 Settled Defendants settled 时，这种 risk 就已存在，而且当时甚至更大，因为当时 class certification 已被 denied。

Court 首先根据每项 settlement 达成时存在的 facts，将本次与 Remaining Defendants 达成的 settlement 与同 Settled Defendants 达成的 settlements 进行 comparing。随后，Court 将 discusses Plaintiffs 的 case 的 relative strengths 和 weaknesses，以 assess 本次 settlement 的 reasonableness。

## A. 与最初的 Settlements 进行 Comparison

### 1\. 对 Settlement Amounts 进行 Comparing

Court finds，与 Settled Defendants 达成的 settlements 提供了一个有用的 benchmark，可以据此 analyze 本次 settlement 的 reasonableness。与 Settled Defendants 达成的 settlements 形成了一项总计 \$20 million. See ECF No. 915 at 3. In approving the settlements, the Court relied upon the fact that the Settled Defendants employed 8% of Class members and paid out 5% of the total Class compensation during the Class period. See ECF No. 539 at 16:20-22 (Plaintiffs' counsel's explanation at the preliminary approval hearing with the Settled Defendants that the 5% figure "giv[es] you a sense of how big a slice of the case this settlement is relative to the rest of the case"). If Remaining Defendants were to settle at the same (or higher) rate as the Settled Defendants, Remaining Defendants' settlement fund would need to total at least \$380 million 的 fund。这一 number 源于以下事实：Remaining Defendants 在 Class period 内支付了 Class compensation 的 95%，而 Settled Defendants 在 Class period 内仅支付了 Class compensation 的 5%。[8](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:8)

在关于本次 Motion 的 hearing 上，Remaining Defendants 的 counsel 提出，相关 benchmark 不是 total Class compensation，而是 total Class membership。这样将得出 Remaining Defendants 的 benchmark figure 为 \$230 million[0](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:92-divided-by-8-is-11-5-11-5-times-20-million-is-230-million). At a minimum, counsel suggested, the Court should compare the settlement amount to a range of \$230 million 至 \$380 million, within which the instant settlement falls. The Court rejects counsel's suggestion, which is contrary to the record. Counsel has provided no basis for why the number of Class members employed by each Defendant is a relevant metric. To the contrary, the relevant inquiry has always been total Class compensation. For example, in both of the settlements with the Settled Defendants and in the instant settlement, the Plans of Allocation call for determining each individual Class member's pay out by dividing the Class member's compensation during the Class period by the total Class compensation during the Class period. ECF No. 809 at 6 (noting that the denominator in the plan of allocation in the settlements with the Settled Defendants is the "total of base salaries paid to all approved Claimants in class positions during the Class period"); ECF No. 920 at 22 (same in the instant settlement); see also ECF No. 539 at 16:20-22 (Plaintiffs' counsel's statement that percent of the total Class compensation was relevant for benchmarking the settlements with the Settled Defendants to the rest of the case). At no point in the record has the percentage of Class membership employed by each Defendant ever been the relevant factor for determining damages exposure. Accordingly, the Court rejects the metric proposed by counsel for Remaining Defendants. Using the Settled Defendants' settlements as a yardstick, the appropriate benchmark settlement for the Remaining Defendants would be at least \$380 million，比本次 settlement 所提供的金额高出超过 \$50 million。

Remaining Defendants 的 Counsel 还提出，以最初的 settlements 进行 benchmarking 是 inappropriate 的，因为 Remaining Defendants 的 settlement numbers 的 magnitude 远远超过 Settled Defendants 的 settlements 中 at issue 的 numbers。这一 argument 所 premised on 的想法是：对 Class caused 更多 damage、并通过 suppressing 更大部分的 class compensation 而 benefited 更多的 Defendants，应当比 caused 较少 damage、并从 allegedly wrongful conduct 中 benefited 较少的 Defendants 支付得更少。这一 argument 是 unpersuasive 的。据 alleged，Remaining Defendants 获得了 anti-solicitation agreements 的 benefit 的 95%，并在 lost compensation 方面 caused 了 Class 所 suffered 的 harm 的 95%。因此，Remaining Defendants 应当支付至少 95% 的 damages，但根据本次 settlement，他们并不会如此支付。

Court 还指出，如果 Plaintiffs 在 trial 中就其超过 \$3 billion damages claim, antitrust law provides for automatic trebling, see 15 U.S.C. § 15(a), so the total damages award could potentially have exceeded \$9 billion。尽管 Ninth Circuit 尚未 determined，antitrust cases 中的 settlement amounts 必须与 Plaintiffs requested 的 single damages award 相 compared，还是与 automatically trebled damages amount 相 compared，见 Rodriguez v. W. Publ'g Corp., 563 F.3d 948, 964-65 (9th Cir. 2009)，但本次 settlement 将使 total recovery 达到 Plaintiffs 的 expert 所 proposed 的 single damages 的 11.29%，或 treble damages 的 3.76%。具体而言，Dr. Leamer 已将 Defendants 的 allegedly unlawful conduct 给 Class 造成的 total damages calculated 为 \$3.05 billion. See ECF No. 856-10. If the Court approves the instant settlements, the total settlements with all Defendants would be \$344.5 million。这一 total 将相当于 Dr. Leamer opines Class 所 suffered 的 single damages 的 11.29%；如果 Dr. Leamer 的 damages figure 被 trebled，则相当于其 3.76%。

### 2\. 相对的 Procedural Posture

考虑到两次 settlements 之间本案 procedural posture 的变化，Remaining Defendants 相对于 Settled Defendants 所获得的 discount 尤其令人不安；Court 原本预期，这些变化应当 increased 而非 decreased Plaintiffs 的 bargaining power。具体而言，在 Settled Defendants settled 时，Plaintiffs 在其 case 中正处于特别 weak 的阶段。虽然 Plaintiffs 已经挺过了 Defendants 的 motion to dismiss，但 Plaintiffs 的 motion for class certification 已被 denied，尽管是 without prejudice。Plaintiffs 已就 class certification motion 重新提交书面论辩，但在与 Settled Defendants settled 时，尚无对其有利的 class certification ruling。如果 Court 最终 granted certification，Plaintiffs 也不知道 Ninth Circuit 是否会 grant Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(f) review 并 reverse 该 certification。因此，在当时，Defendants 拥有 significant leverage。

相比之下，在最初的 settlements 达成后，本案的 procedural posture 急剧转向有利于 Plaintiffs 的方向。具体而言，Court 尽管面对 Defendants 的强烈 objections，仍 certified 了 Class。在那份 granting class certification 的 86 页 order 中，Court 多次将 Plaintiffs 的 evidence 称为“substantial”和“extensive”，并表示其“无法 identify 出一个处于 class certification stage 的 case，其 documentary evidence 的程度能达到 Plaintiffs 在本案中所 presented 的程度。”ECF No. 531 at 69. 此后，Ninth Circuit denied 了 Defendants 根据 Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(f) 对 class certification order 进行 review 的 request。本 Court 还 denied 了 Defendants 的五项 motions for summary judgment，并 denied 了 Defendants 要求 exclude Plaintiffs 关于 antitrust impact 和 damages 的 principal expert 的 motion。本次 settlement 达成时，距 final pretrial conference 仅两周，距 trial 仅一个月；在该 trial 中，原本将 presented 对 Defendants 不利的 evidence。

总之，在本次 settlement 达成时——Class 已经被 certified，针对 class certification 的 appellate review 已被 denied，Defendants 的 dispositive motions 以及要求 exclude Dr. Leamer 的 testimony 的 motion 也已被 denied——Plaintiffs 所处的 position 远比其与 Settled Defendants 达成 settlements 时更为 stronger；而在当时，class certification 已被 denied。Court 原本预期，这种 procedural posture 的转变应当 increased Plaintiffs 的 bargaining power，因此，最近这些 amount 按比例反而更低的 settlements 更令人不安。

## B. Plaintiffs 的 Case 的 Strength

Court 现在转而考察 Plaintiffs 针对 Remaining Defendants 的 case 的 strength，以 evaluate 该 settlement 的 reasonableness。

在关于本次 Motion 的 hearing 上，Plaintiffs 的 counsel contended，本次 settlement 按比例低于先前 settlements 的原因之一，是针对 Settled Defendants（尤其是 Lucasfilm 和 Pixar）的 documentary evidence 比针对 Remaining Defendants 的 documentary evidence 更为 compelling。首先，Court 指出，关于 Settled Defendants 的 relevant evidence 在针对 Remaining Defendants 的 trial 中将是 admissible，因为 Plaintiffs allege 存在一个 included 所有 Defendants 的 overarching conspiracy。因此，关于 Lucasfilm 和 Pixar 在该 overarching conspiracy 的 creation 及 intended effect 中所起 role 的 evidence 将是 admissible。

不过，Court 指出，Plaintiffs 所言属实：Lucasfilm 和 Pixar 的 executives 对 alleged conspiracy 的 nature 和 goals 作出了特别 clear 的 statements。具体而言，Edward Catmull（Pixar President）在其 deposition 中 conceded，之所以存在 anti-solicitation agreements，是因为 solicitation“messes up 了 pay structure。”ECF No. 431-9 at 81. 同样，George Lucas（前 Lucasfilm Chairman of the Board 和 CEO）表示：“我们不能与其他 companies 陷入 bidding war，因为我们没有应付这类事情的 margins。”ECF No. 749-23 at 9.

然而，Remaining Defendants 的 documents 中也有同样 compelling 的 evidence。Google 和 Apple 的情况尤其如此，其 executives extensively discussed 并 enforced 了 anti-solicitation agreements。具体而言，正如本 Court 先前的 orders 中已详尽讨论的那样，Steve Jobs（Apple 的 Co-Founder、Former Chairman 和 Former CEO，Pixar 的 Former CEO）、Eric Schmidt（Google Executive Chairman、Member of the Board of Directors 和前 CEO）以及 Bill Campbell（Intuit Board of Directors 的 Chairman、Apple 的 Co-Lead Director，以及 Google 的 advisor）是 creating 和 enforcing 这些 anti-solicitation agreements 的 key players。Court 现在转而考察针对 Remaining Defendants、finder of fact 很可能认为 compelling 的 evidence。

有 substantial 且 compelling 的 evidence 表明，Steve Jobs（Apple 的 Co-Founder、Former Chairman 和 Former CEO，Pixar 的 Former CEO）即使不是 alleged conspiracy 中唯一的 central figure，也是其中的一位 central figure。数名 witnesses 在其 depositions 中，就 Jobs 先生在 anti-solicitation agreements 中的 role 作出了 testified。例如，Eric Schmidt（Google Executive Chairman、Member of the Board of Directors 和前 CEO）表示，Jobs 先生“认为你们不应该 hiring 彼此的，你知道，technical people”，并且“在 \[Jobs 先生的\] 看来，我们 calling in 并 hiring people 是 inappropriate 的。”ECF No. 819-12 at 77. Edward Catmull（Pixar President）表示，Jobs 先生“非常坚决地要 protecting 他的 employee force。”ECF No. 431-9 at 97. Sergey Brin（Google Co-Founder）testified：“我认为 Jobs 先生的看法是，大家不应该惹他生气。我认为，让他生气的事情就是——会是 hiring，你知道——诸如此类。”ECF No. 639-1 at 112. 因此，将有 ample evidence 表明，Jobs 先生参与了将 Lucasfilm 与 Pixar 之间最初的 anti-solicitation agreement expanding 至本案其他 Defendants 的过程。在这些 agreements 被 extended 后，Jobs 先生在 enforcing 这些 agreements 方面发挥了 central role。以下四组特定 evidence 很可能令 fact-finder 认为 compelling。

首先，在听说 Google 正试图 recruit Apple 的 Safari team 的 employees 后，Jobs 先生威胁了 Brin 先生；据 Brin 先生回忆，他说：“如果你 hire 这些人中的任何一个，那就意味着开战。”ECF No. 833-15.[9](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:10) 在发给 Google 的 Executive Management Team 以及 Bill Campbell（Intuit Board of Directors 的 Chairman、Apple 的 Co-Lead Director，以及 Google 的 advisor）的一封 email 中，Brin 先生建议：“在我们有机会讨论之前，先不要向 Apple 的新人 make 任何新的 offers，也不要 contact 他们 \[原文如此\]。”Id. Campbell 先生随后写信给 Jobs 先生：“Eric \[Schmidt\] 告诉我，他已直接介入，并坚决 stopped 了一切 recruit Apple 任何人的 efforts。”ECF No. 746-5. 正如 Brin 先生在其 deposition 中 testified 的那样：“Eric 作了一个——你知道，一个——你知道，至少是某种——与 Bill 谈过，让他转告 Steve，好让他平静下来。”ECF No. 639-1 at 61. 正如 Schmidt 先生所说：“Steve 不高兴，而 Steve 的不高兴绝对 influenced 了我们在 recruiting practice 上所作的 change。”ECF No. 819-12 at 21. Danielle Lambert（Apple 的 Human Resources 负责人）也作出了相应安排，以履行 Apple 在 anti-solicitation agreements 中的义务，指示 Apple 的 recruiters：“请把 Google add 到你们的 'hands-off list。我们最近 agreed 不从彼此那里 recruit，因此，如果你们听说他们正针对我们开展任何 recruiting，请务必告诉我。”ECF No. 746-15.

其次，其他 Defendants 的 CEOs 出于对 Jobs 先生的畏惧和顺从，maintained 了 anti-solicitation agreements。例如，在 2005 年考虑是否与 Apple enter into 一项 anti-solicitation agreement 时，Bruce Chizen（前 Adobe CEO）担忧，如果 Adobe 不与 Apple enter into 一项 anti-solicitation agreement，就会 loss“top talent”；他说：“如果我告诉 Steve，现在可以随意行动（senior managers 除外），他会 deliberately poach Adobe，只为证明自己的意思。以我对 Steve 的了解，他会盯上我们一些 top Mac talent，比如 Chris Cox，而且会设法让他们受到诱惑而来（extraordinary packages 加上 Steve 的游说）。”[10](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:11) ECF No. 297-15.

这就是 Apple-Adobe agreement 的 genesis。具体而言，在 Jobs 先生于 May 26, 2005 向 Chizen 先生抱怨 Adobe 正在 recruiting Apple employees 后，ECF No. 291-17，Chizen 先生回应说：“我以为我们已 agreed 不 recruit 任何 senior level employees . . . . 我建议我们继续这样。可以讨论。能够 agree 会很好。”Id. Jobs 先生并不满意，回复时威胁要派 Apple 的 recruiters 去找 Adobe 的 employees：“好吧，我会告诉我们的 recruiters，他们可以自由 approach 任何不是 Sr. Director 或 VP 的 Adobe employee。我对你的 position 理解得正确吗？”Id. Chizen 先生立即让步：“我宁愿 agree，不 actively solicit 任一 company 的任何 employee . . . . 如果你同意，我会告诉我的人。”Id.（强调为原文所有）。第二天，Theresa Townsley（Adobe Vice President Human Resources）向她的 recruiting team 宣布：“Bruce 和 Steve Jobs 已达成一项 agreement，我们不得 solicit 任何 Apple employees，反之亦然。”ECF No. 291-18（强调为原文所有）。Adobe 随后将 Apple 列入其“禁止接触的 $$c$$ompanies”名单，该名单指示 Adobe employees 不得 cold call Apple employees。ECF No. 291-11.

Google 为回应 Jobs 先生，采取了更激烈的行动。例如，在 2007 年 Google 的 engineering team 中一名 recruiter contacted 一位 Apple employee 后，Jobs 先生将该消息 forwarded 给 Schmidt 先生，并表示：“如果你们的 recruiting department 能停止这样做，我会非常高兴。”ECF No. 291-23. Google 的回应是将该 recruiter 作为“公开警示的例子”，并“在一小时内 terminat\[ing\] \[the recruiter\]。”Id. 这一公开举动的目的是“（希望能够）防止今后再发生。”Id. 该 recruiter 被 terminated 后，Schmidt 先生给 Jobs 先生发了 email，向其道歉，并告知 Jobs 先生该 recruiter 已被 terminated。Jobs 先生将 Schmidt 先生的 email forwarded 给 Apple 的一位 human resources official，只说了：“:)。”ECF No. 746-9.

在这次 termination 的一年前，Google 同样认真对待了 Jobs 先生的担忧。具体而言，在 2006 年，Jobs 先生给 Schmidt 先生发 email 说：“有人告诉我，Googles \[原文如此\] 新成立的 cell phone software group 正在我们的 iPod group 中 relentlessly recruiting。如果确实如此，你能制止吗？”ECF No. 291-24 at 3. Schmidt 先生将此 forwarded 给 Google 的 Human Resources professionals 后，Arnnon Geshuri（Google Recruiting Director）准备了一份详细 report，称一次 extensive investigation 并未发现对 anti-solicitation agreement 的 breach。

同样，在 2006 年，一位 Google executive 向 Jobs 先生发送 email，询问 Google 是否可以 hire 三名已离开 Apple 的 engineers 到拟设的 facility 工作，而 Jobs 先生回应说：“我们强烈希望你们不要 hire 这些人。”此后，Google 取消了在 Paris 开设 Google engineering center 的 plans。ECF No. 814-2. 整个往来始于 Google 请求 Steve Jobs 允许其 hire Apple engineer Jean-Marie Hullot。record 并不明确 Hullot 先生当时是在职还是已离职的 Apple employee。一位 Google executive 联系 Steve Jobs，询问 Google 是否可以向 Hullot 先生 make an offer，而 Jobs 先生没有及时回应这位 Google executive 的请求。这时，这位 Google executive 转而求助于 Intuit 的 Board Chairman Bill Campbell，希望他能够代表 Google 与 Jobs 先生沟通。具体而言，这位 Google executive 指出，Campbell 先生“在 Apple 和 Google 的 board 任职，所以 Steve 很可能会回他的电话”。ECF No. 428-6. Campbell 先生联系 Jobs 先生的当天，Jobs 先生就回应了这位 Google executive，要求进一步了解这位 Apple engineer 究竟将从事什么工作。ECF No. 428-9. Jobs 先生满意后，表示这项 hire“我没有意见”。Id. 然而，两周后，当 Hullot 先生和一位 Google executive 请求 Jobs 先生允许 hire 四名 Hullot 先生以前在 Apple 的 colleagues 时（三人是已离职的 Apple employees，另一人已发出即将离开 Apple 的 notice），Jobs 先生迅速回应，表示这些 hires 不可接受。ECF No. 428-9. Google 立即取消了该 plan，而这位 Google executive 恭敬地回应 Jobs 先生说：“Steve，鉴于你强烈希望我们不要 hire 那些 ex-Apple engineers，Jean-Marie 和我决定不再开设 Google Paris engineering center。”Id. 这位 Google executive 还把 email thread forwarded 给了 Brin 先生、Larry Page（Google Co-Founder）和 Campbell 先生。Id.

第三，Jobs 先生曾试图将 anti-solicitation agreements expand 至 Palm（但没有成功），甚至以 litigation 相威胁。具体而言，Jobs 先生致电 Edward Colligan（Palm 的前 President 和 CEO），要求 Colligan 先生 enter into 一项 anti-solicitation agreement，并威胁说，如果 Palm 拒绝，就对 Palm 提起 patent litigation。ECF No. 293 ¶¶ 6-8. Colligan 先生通过 email 回应，告诉 Jobs 先生，Jobs 先生“提出让我们 agree，两家公司都不 hire 对方的 employees，而不顾 individual 的意愿，这个 proposal 不仅是 wrong，而且很可能是 illegal”。Id. at 4-5. Colligan 先生接着说：“我们不能 dictate 一个人在哪里工作，也不应尝试这样做。我不能仅仅因为一些人目前为 Apple 工作，就 deny 那些选择在 Palm 谋生的人这样做的 right，而我也不希望你对目前的 Palm employees 这样做。”Id. at 5. 最后，Colligan 先生写道：“因为一名 employee 决定离开 Apple，就以 patent lawsuit 威胁 Palm，实在太过分了。lawsuit 不会符合我们任何一方的 interests，也不会阻止 employees 在我们两家公司之间 migrating . . . . 我们双方最终都只会付给许多 lawyers 大笔的钱。”Id. at 5-6. Jobs 先生向 Colligan 先生作了如下回复：“这不能令 Apple 满意。”Id. at 8. Jobs 先生接着写道：“当你说‘我们双方最终都只会付给许多 lawyers 大笔的钱’时，我相信你明白我们各自公司的 financial resources 存在 asymmetry。”Id. Jobs 先生最后说：“我的建议是，在你就此作出最终 decision 之前，先看看我们的 patent portfolio。”Id.

第四，Apple 的 documents 为 Plaintiffs 的 theory of impact 提供了 strong support，即 rigid wage structures 和对 internal equity 的 concerns 本会促使 Defendants 对 compensation structures 作出 structural changes，以 mitigate solicitation 本会造成的 competitive threat。Apple 的 compensation data 显示，在 Class period 的每一年，Apple 都有一套“job structure system”，其中包括按照分配给所有 salaried employees 的一组 discrete 的 company-wide job levels，以及适用于“Top”、“Major”、“National”和“Small” geographic markets 的四组相关 base salary ranges，对其 workforce 进行 categorizing 和 compensating。ECF No. 745-7 at 14-15, 52-53; ECF No.517-16 ¶¶ 6, 10 & Ex. B. 每个 salary range 都有“min”、“mid”和“max” figure。参见 id. Apple 还创建了一种名为“Merlin”的 Human Resources 和 recruiting tool，这是一个用于 tracking employee records 和 performance 的 internal system，并要求 managers 将 employees grade 在四个 pre-set levels 之一。参见 ECF No. 749-6 at 142-43, 145-46; ECF No. 749-11 at 52-53; ECF No. 749-12 at 33. 正如 Tony Fadell（前 Apple Senior Vice President、iPod Division 负责人及 Steve Jobs 的 advisor）所解释的，Merlin“会说，这是 employee，这是 level，这里是 salary ranges，而通过那个 tool，我们当时就——我们明白了 boundaries 是什么”。ECF No. 749-11 at 53. 超出这些规定的“guidelines”还需要额外 approval。ECF No. 749-7 at 217; ECF No. 749-11 at 53（“而如果我们要超出那个范围，那么我们就必须找来一群人，让他们 approve 超出那个 range 的任何事项。”）。

对 internal equity 的 Concerns 也贯穿 Apple 的 compensation program。Steven Burmeister（Apple Senior Director of Compensation）testified，internal equity——Burmeister 先生将其 defined 为 employee 的 compensation 是否“基于 individual 的 contribution，相对于你的 group 中或整个 organization 中的其他 employees 而言是 fair”这一 notion——inheres 于 managers 在 determining starting salaries 时考虑的部分 guidelines 中，“即使不是全部”。ECF No. 745-7 at 61-64; ECF No. 753-12. 事实上，正如 Patrick Burke（前 Apple Technical Recruiter 和 Staffing Manager）所解释的，在 Apple hiring 一名新的 employee 时，将“candidate”与其将加入的 team 中的其他人进行“compar\[ing\]”，“是我们给予什么 salary 的最大 determining factor”。ECF No. 745-6 at 279.

针对 Google 的 evidence 同样 compelling。Email evidence reveals，Eric Schmidt（Google Executive Chairman、Member of the Board of Directors 及前 CEO）因 violations of anti-solicitation agreements 而 terminated 至少两名 recruiters，并威胁要 terminate 更多人。如上所述，有 direct evidence 表明，在一名 recruiter 试图 solicit 一名 Apple employee 后，Schmidt 先生应 Steve Jobs 的要求 terminated 了这名 recruiter。此外，在发给 Bill Campbell（Chairman of Intuit Board of Directors、Co-Lead Director of Apple 及 Google 的 advisor）的一封 email 中，Schmidt 先生表示，他指示对另一名 Google recruiter 实施 for-cause termination；该 recruiter 曾试图 recruit 一名 eBay 的 executive，而 eBay 在 Google 的 do-not-cold-call list 上。ECF No. 814-14. 最后，如下文将更详细讨论的，Schmidt 先生告知 Paul Otellini（Intel 的 CEO 及 Member of the Google Board of Directors），Schmidt 先生会 terminate 任何 recruited Intel employees 的 recruiter。

此外，Google maintained 一份正式的“Do Not Call” list，将 Apple、Intel 和 Intuit 列在一起，并获得了 top executives 的 approved。ECF No. 291-28. 该 list 还包括其他 companies，例如 Genentech、Paypal 和 eBay。Id. “Do Not Call” list 的一份 draft 被提交给 Google 的 Executive Management Group；该 committee 由 Google 的 senior executives 组成，包括 Schmidt 先生、Larry Page（Google Co-Founder）、Sergey Brin（Google Co-Founder）和 Shona Brown（前 Google Senior Vice President of Business Operations）。ECF No. 291-26. Schmidt 先生 approved 了该 list。参见 id.；另见 ECF No. 291-27（Schmidt 先生发来的 email 写道：“这看起来很好。”）。此外，有 evidence 表明，Google executives 知道 anti-solicitation agreements 可能导致 legal troubles，但仍继续推行这些 agreements。当 Brown 女士询问 Schmidt 先生，他是否对与 Google 的 competitors sharing 关于“Do Not Call” list 的 information 有任何 concerns 时，Schmidt 先生回应说，他更希望“verbally 进行 shared，因为我不想 create 一份日后可能据以对我们 sued 的 paper trail？”ECF No. 291-40. Brown 女士回应：“orally 这样做有道理。我同意。”Id.

Google 对来自 Facebook 的 competition 的 response，也 demonstrates 了 alleged conspiracy 的 impact。Google 长期以来一直担心 Facebook hiring 对 retention 的 effect。例如，在发给 Google top executives 的一封 email 中，Brin 先生于 2007 年表示，“facebook phenomenon creates 了一个真正的 retention problem”。ECF No. 814-4. 一个月后，Brin 先生宣布了一项 policy：对于任何收到 Facebook 的 offer 的 Google employee，在一小时内 making counteroffers。ECF No. 963-2.

在 March 2008，Arnnon Geshuri（Google Recruiting Director）发现，non-party Facebook 一直在对 Google 的 Site Reliability Engineering（“SRE”）team 进行 cold calling。Geshuri 先生的第一个 response 是建议联系 Sheryl Sandberg（non-party Facebook 的 Chief Operating Officer），以便“请她 put a stop to 针对我们 SRE team 的 targeted sourcing effort”，并“consider establishing 一项 mutual 'Do Not Call' agreement，specifies 我们不会对彼此进行 cold-call”。ECF No. 963-3. Geshuri 先生还建议“在 internally 进行 look\[ing\]，并对 SRE group 的 attrition rate 进行 review\[ing\]”，表示：“我们也许应该 consider 额外的 individual retention incentives 或 team incentives，以使 SRE 的 attrition 尽可能 low。”Id.（强调为后加）。最后，另一个建议是“发起一场 aggressive campaign，向他们的 company 进行 call into，并 go after 他们的人——毫无限制。我们会毫不松懈，势不可挡。”Id. 对此，Bill Campbell（Chairman of Intuit Board of Directors、Co-Lead Director of Apple 及 Google 的 advisor）以 Google 的 advisor 身份建议：“谁应该联系 Sheryl [Sandberg](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/or%20Mark%20[Zuckerberg])，以达成 cease fire？我们必须达成 truce。”Id. Facebook 拒绝了。

在 2010 年，Google 以一次“Big Bang”来 altered 其 salary structure，以回应 Facebook 的 hiring，这为 Plaintiffs 的 theory of antitrust impact 提供了额外 support。具体而言，在一段时期内有大量 employees 从 Google 流向 Facebook 之后，Google 开始 study Facebook 对 Google employees 的 solicitation。ECF No. 190 ¶ 109. 开始这项 study 一个月后，Google 宣布了其“Big Bang”，其中包括将其所有 salaried employees 的 base salary increase 10%，并向所有 employees 立即提供 \$1,000 的 cash bonus。ECF No. 296-18. Laszlo Bock（Google Senior Vice President of People Operations）解释说，Big Bang 的 rationale 包括：(1) “对 rising attrition 作出 responsive 的回应；”(2) 支持 higher retention，因为“higher salaries generate higher fixed costs；”以及 (3) “非常 strategic，因为 start-ups 没有足以 match 的 cash flow，而 big companies 则 (a) 太担心 internal equity 和 scalability，因而无法这样做，且 (b) 没有这样做所需的 margins。”ECF No. 296-20.

Google 的其他 documents 为 Plaintiffs 的 theory of antitrust impact 提供了进一步的 evidence。例如，Google 的 Chief Culture Officer 表示：“对 companies 进行 $$c$$old calling 以 recruit 是意料之中的，除非它们在我们的 'don't call' list 上。” ECF No. 291-41。此外，Google 发现，尽管 referrals 是最大的 source of hires，“agencies 和 passively sourced candidates offer$$ed$$ 最高的 yield。” ECF No. 780-8。假如存在 active solicitations，employees 之间就会发生 information 的 spread——这对 Plaintiffs 的 theory of impact 至关重要——Google 的 evidence 也 demonstrated 了这一点。例如，一名 Google employee 表示：“这种事不可能保密。获得 counter offers 的人会交谈，不仅与 Googlers 和 ex-Googlers 交谈，也与向他们发出 offers 的 competitors 交谈（希望能 improving 这些 offers），而那些 competitors 也会交谈，把它当作 recruit 更多 Googlers 的 tool。” ECF No. 296-23.

Google 的 wage structure 和 internal equity concerns 也 support Plaintiffs 的 theory of impact。Google 有许多 job families，job families 内有许多 grades，而 grades 内又有许多 job titles。参见，例如，ECF No. 298-7, ECF No. 298-8；另见 Cisneros Decl., Ex. S (Brown Depo.) at 74-76（讨论 Google utilized 的 salary ranges）；ECF No. 780-4 at 25-26（testifying，Google 的 2007 salary ranges 与 2004 salary ranges 大体具有相同的 structure）。在整个 Class period，Google utilized 具有 minima 和 maxima，以及 means 或 medians 的 salary ranges 和 pay bands。ECF No. 958-1 ¶ 66；参见 ECF No. 427-3 at 15-17。正如 Shona Brown（前 Google Senior Vice President, Business Operations）所解释的：“如果你 discussed 一个 specific role \[在 Google\]，你就能明白，那个 role 处于某个 job ladder 的一个 specific level。” ECF No. 427-3 at 27-28; ECF No. 745-11。Frank Wagner（Google Director of Compensation）testified，他可以通过 internal company website locate Google 的 jobs 所对应的 target salary range。参见 ECF No. 780-4 at 31-32（“问：如果你想 identify 某个 grade 内某个 job 的 target salary 会是多少，你能否上网或去某个地方 . . . 调出那个 job family 和那个 grade 所对应的数值？ . . . 答：能。”）。此外，Google 认为 internal equity 是一个重要目标。Google utilized 一个 salary algorithm，部分目的是“通过 managing salaries，使其处于 reasonable range 内，来 $$e$$nsur\[ing\] internal equity。” ECF No. 814-19。而且，由于 Google “strive$$d$$ 实现 overall salary distribution 的 fairness”，“具有 low salaries 的 high performers \[会\] 获得比具有 high salaries 的 high performers 更大的 percentage increases。” ECF No. 817-1 at 15.

此外，Google analyzed 其 equity compensation，并将其与 Apple、Intel、Adobe 和 Intuit 等 companies 的 equity compensation 进行 compared；它根据这些 companies 满足诸如属于 “high-tech company”、“high-growth company” 和 “key labor market competitor” 等 criteria，将每一家 designated 为 “peer company”。ECF No. 773-1。在 2007，部分基于对 Google 与其 peer companies 的比较 analysis，Bock 先生和 Dave Rolefson（Google Equity Compensation Manager）写道：“我们最大的 labor market competitors 正在 significantly exceeding 它们自己的 guidelines，以便在 talent 方面 beat Google。” Id.

最后，Google 自己的 documents undermine 了 Defendants 关于 lack of antitrust impact 的 principal theory，即 compensation decisions 会是 one off，而不是 classwide。Alan Eustace（Google Senior Vice President）在评论关于 competition for workers 的 concerns 和 Google 对 counteroffers 的 approach 时指出：“有时候，对 compensation 作出 changes 是有道理的，即使这会在你目前的 comp 中 introduces discontinuities，因为这样可以留住你最好的 people，并向 hiring company 传达一个信息：我们会争取留住自己最好的 people。” ECF No. 296-23。由于 recruiting “几个真正出色的人”可能促使“许许多多其他人 \[去\] follow”，Eustace 先生 concluded：“你不能让自己成为其他 companies 的 rich target。” Id。按照他的说法，“long-term . . . 正确的 approach 不是把这些 situations 当作 one-offs 来 deal with，而是对 compensation 采取一种 _systematic approach_，使任何人都很难 get a better offer。” Id.（强调为后加）。

对于 anti-solicitation agreements 之前 Google 对 labor market 的 impact，Meg Whitman（前 eBay CEO）作出了最贴切的概括；她曾致电 Schmidt 先生，“谈论 \[Google 的\] hiring practices。” ECF No. 814-15。正如 Eric Schmidt 告诉 Google 的 senior executives 的那样，Whitman 女士说：“Google 已经成了整个 valley 的谈资，因为 \[你们\] 正在全面 driving up salaries。” Id。这次谈话一年后，Google 将 eBay added 到其 do-not-cold-call list。ECF No. 291-28.

针对 Intel，也存在 compelling evidence。对于 Intel executives 提出的关于 enforcement of the anti-solicitation agreement 的 requests，Google 的反应与上述 Google 对 Steve Jobs 要求 enforce 这些 agreements 的反应相似。例如，Paul Otellini（Intel 的 CEO 和 Google Board of Directors 的 Member）在 September 26, 2007 收到一项 internal complaint，涉及 Google 成功 recruiting Intel 的 technical employees，ECF No. 188-8（“Paul，我有这么多人流失到了 Google . . . . 我们正在 countering，但觉得应该让你知道。”）；随后，Otellini 先生将这封 email forwarded 给 Eric Schmidt（Google Executive Chairman、Board of Directors 的 Member 和前 CEO），并说：“Eric，你能帮帮忙吗？？？” Id。Schmidt 先生答应了，并把这封 email forwarded 给他的 recruiting team，后者为 Schmidt 先生 prepared 了一份关于 Google activities 的 report。ECF No. 291-34。第二天，Schmidt 先生回复 Otellini 先生：“如果我们发现有 recruiter called into Intel，我们就会 terminate 那名 recruiter”，这与针对 violations of the Apple-Google agreement 所采取的 remedy 相同。ECF No. 531 at 37。在另一封发给 Schmidt 先生的 email 中，Otellini 先生说：“抱歉又为这个话题打扰你，但 Google 持续 recruit 我们的 key players，让我的人非常不安。” 参见 ECF No. 428-8.

此外，Otellini 先生知道，anti-solicitation agreement 可能在 legally 方面带来麻烦。具体而言，Otellini 先生在给另一名 Intel executive 的一封关于 Google-Intel agreement 的 email 中表示：“让我说清楚。我们没有 signed 任何东西。eric 和我之间有一个 handshake 'no recruit'。我不希望此事被广泛知晓。” Id.

而且，有 evidence 表明，Otellini 先生知道 Intel 并非 party 的那些 anti-solicitation agreements。具体而言，Sergey Brin（Google Co-Founder）和 Google 的 Schmidt 先生都 testified，他们应该曾告诉 Otellini 先生，Google 与 Apple 有一项 anti-solicitation agreement。ECF No. 639-1 at 74:15（“我确信我们应该提起过这件事$$。$$”）；ECF No. 819-12 at 60（“我确信我在某个时候和 Paul 谈过这件事。”）。Intel 自己的 expert testified，由于 Otellini 先生是 Google 的 board 的成员，他很可能知道 Google 的其他 bilateral agreements。ECF No. 771 at 4。Intel 与 Apple 在同一天被 added 到 Google 的 do-not-cold-call list，这一事实进一步 suggests Intel participation in an overarching conspiracy。ECF No. 291-28.

此外，尽管 Intel 和 Google 是 competitors for talent，Otellini 先生还是将一份讨论包括 Apple 和 Intel 在内的 peer companies 的 bonus plans 的 Google document “从 Google lifted”了过来。Cisneros Decl., Ex. 463。真正的 competitors for talent，如果没有 agreements not to compete，不太可能 share 如此 sensitive bonus information。

此外，与 antitrust impact 相关的 key documents 也 implicate Intel。具体而言，Intel 认识到了 cold calling 的重要性，并在其 “Complete Guide to Sourcing” 中表示：“对 candidates 进行 \[Cold\] $$c$$alling，是 recruit 最 efficient 和 effective 的 ways 之一。” ECF No. 296-22。Intel 还针对其他“通常被认为与 Intel comparable 的 tech companies”进行 benchmarked compensation；Intel 将这些 companies defined 为“semiconductor、software、networking、communications 和 diversified computer companies 的 $$b$$lend。” ECF No. 754-2。根据 Intel 的说法，在 2007，这些 comparable companies 包括 Apple 和 Google。Id。正如 Plaintiffs 所 contend 的，这些 documents suggest，anti-solicitation agreements 导致了 Class members 的 wages 的 structural depression，而不是 individual depression。

而且，Intel 有一套 “compensation structure”，其中包含 job grades 和 job classifications。参见 ECF No. 745-13 at 73（“我们将 jobs break 为 three categories 之一——我们称它们为 job families——R&D、tech 和 nontech，还有很多 . . . .”）。该 company 根据 employees 的 skills 和 experience，将他们 assigned 到某个 grade level。ECF No. 745-11 at 23；另见 ECF No. 749-17 at 45（解释 Intel 的每个人都被 assigned 一个类似于 job grade 的 “classification”）。Intel 在整个 company 范围内 standardized 其 salary ranges；每个 range applied 于 multiple jobs，而大多数 jobs spanned multiple salary grades。ECF No. 745-16 at 59。Intel 进一步将其 salary ranges broke down 为 quartiles，而 Intel 的 compensation 遵循“一种 bell-curve distribution，其中大多数 employees 处于 middle quartiles，而处于 bottom 和 top quartiles 的 percentage 小得多。” Id. at 62-63.

Intel 还 used 一个 software tool，向 managers 提供有关 employee 的 pay range 的 guidance；该 tool 也会考虑 market reference ranges 和 merit。ECF No. 758-9。正如 Randall Goodwin（Intel Technology Development Manager）所解释的：“如果 tool recommended 了某个方案，而我们觉得想要作出一项超出其 guidelines 的 proposed change，我们就会写一些 justification。” ECF No. 749-15 at 52。同样，Intel 定期 ran reports，以 showing 其 employees 的 salary range distribution。ECF No. 749-16 at 64.

这些 evidence 也 supports Intel 的 wage structure 的 rigidity。例如，在一份 2004 Human Resources presentation 中，Intel 表示，尽管“Intel 的 Meritocracy philosophy 希望实现 $$c$$ompensation differentiation”，“short and long term high performer differentiation 是 questionable 的。” ECF No. 758-10 at 13。事实上，Intel 指出：“根据对 '99 data 的 analysis，$$l$$ack of differentiation 在过去一直存在。” Id. at 19。Intel identifies 了以下 key “$$v$$ulnerability $$c$$hallenges”：(1) “$$m$$anagers 在 $$f$$ocal 时 distinguish 的 (in)ability”——“actual merit increases 相较于 system generated increases 是 significantly reduced 的”，“对 key players 的 retention 的 $$l$$ong term threat”；(2) “对 HPs \[high performers\] 的 actual pay differentiation \[几乎没有\]”；以及 (3) “\[没有\] explicit strategy 来 differentiate。” Id. at 24（强调为后加）。

此外，Intel 使用 internal equity，“来 determine new hires 和 current employees 的 wage rates，使其 correspond to 每项 job 对 Intel 的 relative value。” ECF No. 749-16 at 210-11; ECF No. 961-5。为协助这一 process，Intel 在向 new employees making offers 时，使用一个会 generates “Internal Equity Report” 的 tool。ECF No. 749-16 at 212-13。用 Ogden Reid（Intel Director of Compensation and Benefits）的话来说：“我们的 culture 在很大程度上都在高喊 egalitarianism . . . . 虽然我们嘴上支持 meritocracy，但我们实际上更相信在 broad bands 内 treating everyone the same。” ECF No. 769-8.

Intel 的一份 2002 年的 human resources document——早于这些 anti-solicitation agreements——承认存在“hired 和 acquired Intel employees 之间在 base salaries/EB targets 的 alignment 方面持续存在的 inequities”，以及“这两个 populations 内与 accurate job grading 有关的 parallel issues”。ECF No. 750-15. 对此，Intel 计划：(1)“对具有 'critical skills' 的 job families 的 exempt job grade assignments 进行 Review。酌情 Make adjustments”；以及 (2)“对 inequities 的 perception 进行 Validate . . . . 对 employees 的 impact 进行 Scope。酌情 Recommend adjustments。”Id. Intel 的一份 human resources document 证实，在 2004 年或前后，“$$n$$ew hire salary premiums drove salary range adjustment。”ECF No. 298-5 at 7（着重为后加）。

Intel 会“将某个 Intel job code in grade 与某个 market survey job code in grade 进行 match”，ECF No. 749-16 at 89，并将其用作 determining 自身“own focal process or pay delivery”的 process 的一部分，id. at 23。如果 job codes fell below midpoint 上下某个 percent 的范围，该公司便作出“special market adjustment$$s$$”。Id. at 90.

来自 Adobe 的 Evidence 也 suggests，Adobe 知道其 anti-solicitation agreements 的 impact。Adobe personnel 认识到，就 recruiting 而言，“Apple 会是一个值得考察的极佳 target”，但知道他们不能这样做，因为“遗憾的是，Bruce \[Chizen（前 Adobe CEO）\] 和 Apple CEO Steve Jobs 有一项不 poach 彼此 talent 的 gentleman's agreement。”ECF No. 291-13. Adobe executives 也是 entered into、enforced 并试图 expand 这些 anti-solicitation agreements 的 high-ranking executives 群体中不可分割的一部分。具体而言，Chizen 先生发现 Apple 正在 recruiting Macromedia 的 employees（Macromedia 是一个 separate entity，Adobe 后来会 acquire 它），于是通过给 Jobs 先生发送一封 email，帮助确保 Apple 在 Adobe 对 Macromedia 的 acquisition 之后，仍会 honor Apple 与 Adobe 和 Macromedia 双方已有的 anti-solicitation agreements。ECF No. 608-3 at 50.

Adobe 认为 Google 和 Apple 是其最主要的 competitors for talent 之一，并对 Adobe 是否正在“winning the talent war”表示担忧。ECF No. 296-3. Adobe 还认为自己处于一场“从 benefits 角度而言的六马竞赛”中，其他“马匹”包括 Google、Apple 和 Intuit。参见 ECF No. 296-4. 2008 年，Adobe 将其 compensation 与包括 Google、Apple 和 Intel 在内的九家公司进行 benchmarked。ECF No. 296-4; cf. ECF No. 652-6（显示，在 2010 年，Adobe 将 Intuit 视为“direct peer”，将 Apple、Google 和 Intel 视为“reference peers”，尽管 Adobe 实际上并未将 compensation 与后面这些公司进行 benchmark）。

然而，尽管将其他 Defendants 视为 competitors，来自 Adobe 的 evidence suggests，Adobe 知道自己并非 party 的那些 bilateral agreements。具体而言，Adobe 与其他 Defendants shared confidential compensation information，尽管 Adobe 将至少一部分其他 Defendants 视为 competitors，而且与它们没有 bilateral agreement。例如，Intuit 和 Adobe 的 HR personnel exchanged 标为“confidential”的 information，内容涉及各 firm 那一年会给予多少 compensation，以及给予哪些 employees。ECF No. 652-8. Adobe 和 Intuit shared confidential compensation information，尽管这两家公司之间没有 bilateral anti-solicitation agreement，而且 Adobe 将 Intuit 视为 direct competitor for talent。在不存在 overarching conspiracy 的情况下，这样的 direct competitors for talent 不太可能 share 如此 sensitive compensation information。

与此同时，Google circulated 一封 email，其中明确讨论了其“budget 与其他 tech companies 是 comparable 的”，并将 Google 的 merit budget increases 的 precise percentage 与 Adobe、Apple 和 Intel 的相应 percentage 进行了 compared。ECF No. 807-13. Google 掌握了 Adobe 的 merit budget increases 的 precise percentage，尽管 Google 与 Adobe 之间没有 bilateral anti-solicitation agreement。competitors 之间对 sensitive compensation information 的这种 sharing，是存在 overarching conspiracy 的进一步 evidence。

Adobe 认识到，在没有 anti-solicitation agreements 的情况下，pay increases 将是必要的，这与 Plaintiffs 的 theory of impact 相呼应。例如，Adobe 担心一名 employee——因其 technical skills、intelligence 和 collaborative abilities 而被称为“star performer”——可能离开 Adobe，因为“只要他愿意，他就能轻易在别处找到一份很好的 job”，因此考虑如何最好地 retain 他。ECF No. 799-22. 在此过程中，Adobe 对这名 employee 已经在其他四家公司 interviewed，并与在那里工作的朋友沟通过这一事实表示担忧。Id. 因此，Adobe 指出，这名 employee“知道自己在 market 中的 value”，而且这名 employee 的大学朋友“每年赚的钱比他多约 $15k”。Id. 对此，Adobe 决定立即给予这名 employee 一次 pay raise。Id.

另有 evidence 支持 Plaintiffs 的 theory of impact：Adobe 的每个 job position 都被 assigned 一个 job title，而每个 job title 在 Adobe 的 salary structure 内都有一个 corresponding salary range，其中包括 salary minimum、middle 和 maximum。参见 ECF No. 804-17 at 4, 8, 72, 85-86. Adobe 预期其现有 employees 的 salaries 的 distribution 会 fit“一条 bell curve”。ECF No. 749-5 at 57. 为帮助 managers 在 setting 和 adjusting salaries 时保持在 prescribed ranges 内，Adobe 配备了一个 online salary planning tool 以及 salary matrices，它们依据 market salary data 向 managers 提供 guidelines。参见 ECF No. 804-17 at 29-30（“基本上，salary planning tool 中 populated 了某位 manager 的 employee information，也就是其 team 中的 employees \[sic\]。你可以大致查看他们目前的 compensation。它会向他们显示其目前所处 role 的 range . . . . 该 tool 还能够提供一些我们 recommend 的 guidelines，涉及 managers 可以如何考虑 spending 其 allocated budget。”）。如果 employees 低于 minimum recommended salary range，Adobe 的 practice 是“作为 annual review 的一部分，将他们 adjust 至 minimum”，并“对他们 red flag”。Id. at 12. 对 salary ranges 的 Deviations 也会引发与 managers 的谈话，在这些谈话中，Adobe 的 officers 解释说：“我们设定 minimum 是有原因的，因为我们认为你需要处于这个 range 内，才能保持 competitive。”Id.

Internal equity 在 Adobe 很重要，在其他 Defendants 也是如此。正如 Debbie Streeter（Adobe Vice President, Total Rewards）所解释的，Adobe“始终将 internal equity 作为一个 data point 来 look$$ed$$ at，因为如果你打算从外部 hire 某个人，而此人的收入 . . . 高于一名现有的 high performer employee，你就需要在把此人招进来之前知道这一点。”ECF No.749-5 at 175. 同样，在考虑是否 extend 一项 counteroffer 时，Adobe 建议“ALWAYS 应当考虑 internal equity。”ECF No. 746-7 at 5.

此外，Donna Morris（Adobe Senior Vice President, Global Human Resources Division）表示担忧的是“compression 所造成的 internal equity 问题（market driving 新 hires 的 pay 高于现有 employees 的 pay）”。ECF No. 298-9（“现实是，由于 market 越来越 aggressive，new hires 正在要求达到或高于 midpoint 的 base pay。”）。Adobe personnel 表示，由于 fixed budget，他们可能无法立即应对这一问题，“但如果 market 仍然 aggressive，可以针对 FY2006 考察 \[compression\]。”[11](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:12) Id.

### D. Plaintiffs 的 Case 中的 Weaknesses

Plaintiffs contend，尽管这些 evidence 是 compelling，但 Plaintiffs 的 case 也存在使 trial 具有 risky 性质的 weaknesses。Plaintiffs contend，这些 risks 是 substantial。具体而言，Plaintiffs 指出，他们向 jury presenting 其 case 时本会面临以下 challenges：(1) 鉴于若干对 Defendants 彼此之间没有 anti-solicitation agreements，convincing jury 在七名 Defendants 之间 find 一个 single overarching conspiracy；(2) 鉴于 Defendants 打算让六名 expert economists 出庭，attack Plaintiffs 的 experts 的 methodology，proving damages；以及 (3) overcoming Class members 的 compensation 在过去十年中尽管 economy sluggish 却仍已 increased 这一事实，并鉴于 Class members 被认为拥有的以及实际拥有的 wealth，overcoming 普遍的 anti-tech worker sentiment。Plaintiffs 还指出尚未解决的 legal issues，例如 pending motions in limine，以及 pending motion，旨在 determine 应当 apply per se 还是 rule of reason analysis；这些可能有助于 Defendants present 一个主张 bilateral agreements 具有 pro-competitive purpose 的 case。参见 ECF No. 938 at 10-14。

本 Court 承认，如果 Plaintiffs proceed to trial，他们会面临 substantial risks。尽管如此，鉴于上述 evidence，本 Court 无法 conclude 此次 settlement amount 处于 range of reasonableness 之内，尤其是在将其与同 Settled Defendants 达成的 settlements 以及 litigation 后续的发展相比较时。本 Court 进一步指出，record 中存在能够 mitigate Plaintiffs 的 case 中至少一部分 weaknesses 的 evidence。

关于 proving overarching conspiracy，若干项 evidence undermine Defendants 关于 bilateral agreements 彼此 unrelated 的 contentions。重要的是，Steve Jobs（Apple 的 Co-Founder、Former Chairman 和 Former CEO）和 Bill Campbell（Intuit Board of Directors 的 Chairman、Apple 的 Co-Lead Director，以及 Google 的 advisor）这两个人，亲自 entered into 或 facilitated 本 case 中的每一项 bilateral agreements。具体而言，在 Jobs 先生担任 Pixar 的 executive 时，他与 George Lucas（Lucasfilm 的 former Chairman 和 CEO）created 了 Lucasfilm 与 Pixar 之间最初的 anti-solicitation agreement。此后，Apple 在 Jobs 先生的领导下，与 Pixar entered into 一项 agreement；如下文所述，Pixar 的 executives 将其与 Lucasfilm-Pixar agreement 作了 compared。如上文所述，又是 Jobs 先生联系 Sergey Brin（Google Co-Founder）和 Eric Schmidt（Google Executive Chairman、Member of the Board of Directors 和 former CEO），以 create Apple-Google agreement。这项 agreement 是在 Campbell 先生的协助下 reached 的；他是 Intuit 的 Board Chairman、Jobs 先生的朋友，也是 Google 的 advisor。Apple-Google agreement 曾在 Google Board meetings 上被讨论，Campbell 先生和 Paul Otellini（Intel 的 Chief Executive Officer 和 Member of the Google Board of Directors）都出席了这些会议。ECF No. 819-10 at 47。在 Brin 先生与 Otellini 先生之间以及 Schmidt 先生与 Otellini 先生之间进行讨论后，Intel 被 added 到 Google 的 do-not-cold-call list 中。随后，Campbell 先生利用自己在 Google 的影响力，成功 lobby Google，将他担任 Chairman of the Board of Directors 的 Intuit add 到 Google 的 do-not-cold-call list 中。参见 ECF No. 780-6 at 8-9。此外，Jobs 先生 entered into Apple-Google agreement 后仅仅两个月，Apple 就向 Bruce Chizen（Adobe 的 former CEO）施压，要求其 enter into 一项 Apple-Adobe agreement。ECF No. 291-17。正如这番讨论所表明的，Jobs 先生和 Campbell 先生是与 alleged conspiracy 每一步的 formation 联系最密切的人，因为他们参与了每一个 links 的 forming 过程。

鉴于这小群 negotiated 并 enforced anti-solicitation agreements 的 executives 具有 overlapping nature，他们知道自己的 firms 并非 party 的其他 bilateral agreements，也就不足为奇了。例如，Google 的 Brin 先生和 Schmidt 先生均 testified，他们应当曾告诉 Intel 的 Otellini 先生，Google 与 Apple 有一项 anti-solicitation agreement。ECF No. 639-1 at 74:15（“我确信我们应该提到过它$$。$$”）；ECF No. 819-12 at 60（“我确信自己曾在某个时候与 Paul 谈过这件事。”）。Intel 自己的 expert testified，Otellini 先生很可能凭借自己在 Google 的 board 上的 membership，知晓 Google 的其他 bilateral agreements。ECF No. 771 at 4。此外，Google recruiters 知道 Adobe-Apple agreement。Id.（Google recruiter 的 notation 称 Apple 与 Adobe 有“严格的 'hands-off policy”）。另外，Google 的 Schmidt 先生 testified，基于他对 Jobs 先生的了解，认为 Jobs 先生“应该已向其他人 extended \[anti-solicitation agreements\]”，将是“fair to extrapolate”的。此外，正是这同一群 top executives，如上文所述，曾尝试将 agreement expand 到 Silicon Valley 的其他 companies，例如 eBay、Facebook、Macromedia 和 Palm，其中既有成功也有失败；这表明这些 agreements 既不是 isolated，也不是 one off agreements。

另外，这六项 bilateral agreements 包含 nearly identical terms，precluding 每一对 Defendants affirmatively soliciting 对方的任何 employees。ECF No. 531 at 30。此外，如上文所述，Defendants recognized 了这些 agreements 的 similarity。例如，Google 在其“do-not-cold-call” list 上将 Apple、Intel 和 Intuit 归在一起。此外，Google 的“do-not-cold-call” list 表明，Apple-Google agreement 与 Intel-Google agreement 在同一天 commenced。最后，Lori McAdams（Pixar Vice President of Human Resources and Administration）在一封电子邮件中 explicitly compared 了这些 anti-solicitation agreements，表示：“从现在起生效，我们将 follow 与 Apple 的一项 gentleman's agreement，它与我们的 Lucasfilm agreement 是 similar 的。”ECF No. 531 at 26。

关于 Plaintiffs 必须 rebut Defendants 所提出的 anti-solicitation agreements aided collaborations、因而是 pro-competitive 的 contentions 这一 contention，并不存在将 anti-solicitation agreements 与任何 collaboration links 起来的 documentary evidence。所有 memorialize collaboration agreements 的 documents 都没有 mentions 那些 broad anti-solicitation agreements，而所有 memorialize broad anti-solicitation agreements 的 documents 也都没有 mentions collaborations。此外，就连 Defendants 的 experts 也 conceded，与 collaborations 关系最密切的人并不知道 anti-solicitation agreements。ECF No. 852-1 at 8。另外，Defendants 的 top executives 自己也 acknowledge 不存在任何 collaborative purpose。例如，Adobe 的 Chizen 先生 admitted，Adobe-Apple anti-solicitation agreement“并不 limited to Apple 与 Adobe 正在 collaborating 的任何 particular projects”。ECF No. 962-7 at 42。此外，U.S. Department of Justice（“DOJ”）也 determined，这些 anti-solicitation agreements“并非 ancillary to 任何 legitimate collaboration”，“其范围 broader than 任何 collaborative effort 的 formation 或 implementation 所 reasonably necessary 的范围”，并且“disrupted 了 labor setting 中 apply 的 normal price-setting mechanisms”。ECF No. 93-1 ¶ 16; ECF No. 93-4 ¶ 7。DOJ concluded，Defendants entered into 的 agreements 属于 restraints of trade，依照 antitrust laws 是 per se unlawful。ECF No. 93-1 ¶ 35; ECF No. 93-4 ¶ 3。因此，尽管 Defendants 自本 litigation 开始以来一直 claimed，这些 anti-solicitation agreements 存在与 collaborations 相关的 pro-competitive purposes，尽管所谓的 collaborations 是 Defendants 的 motions for summary judgment 的核心，Defendants 仍未能 produce persuasive evidence，表明这些 anti-solicitation agreements 与 collaborations related 或是 pro-competitive。

### IV. CONCLUSION（结论）

本 Court 处理本 case 已近三年，在此期间，Court 在 adjudicating 不仅 substantive motions，而且数量庞大的 sealing requests 时，已 reviewed 大量 documents。经过这些工作，Court 无法 conclude 本次 settlement falls within the range of reasonableness。正如本 Court 在其 summary judgment order 中所述，七名 Defendants 之间存在 overarching conspiracy 的 evidence 十分充分，包括“各项 agreements 的 $$t$$he similarities、entered into 并 enforced 这些 agreements 的少数相互交织的 high-level executives、Defendants 对其他 agreements 的 knowledge、Defendants 之间乃至没有 bilateral anti-solicitation agreements 的 firms 之间对 confidential compensation information 的 sharing 和 benchmarking，以及 Defendants 对 anti-solicitation agreements 的 expansion 和 attempted expansion。” ECF No. 771 at 7-8。此外，正如上文及本 Court 的 class certification order 中所讨论的，关于 Defendants 的 rigid wage structures 和 internal equity concerns 的 evidence，加上 Defendants 自身 executives 的 statements，在 establishing anti-solicitation agreements 的 impact——即 Class-wide depression of wages——方面，很可能具有 compelling 的作用。

鉴于这些 evidence，Court 对本次与 Remaining Defendants 达成的 settlement 比与 Settled Defendants 达成的 settlements proportionally lower 这一事实感到不安。自那些 settlements 以来，case 朝着有利于 Plaintiffs 的方向发展，这进一步加重了这一担忧。在那些 settlements 达成时，Defendants 仍可能在本 Court 面前 defeated class certification，仍可能 successfully sought appellate review 以及对任何 class certification 的 reversal，仍可能在 summary judgment 上 prevailed，或者仍可能在 exclude Plaintiffs 的 principal expert 的尝试中 succeeded。相比之下，本次 settlement 达成时，距离原定 trial commence 的时间仅有一个月，而且 Defendants 的这些机会均已消失。尽管 trial 的 unpredictable nature 无疑会给 Plaintiffs 带来 challenges，但 Defendants 的 exposure 更为 substantial，不仅涉及可能超过 \$9 billion 的 damages，也涉及其他 collateral consequences，包括本 Order 中讨论的 evidence，以及其他本会被揭示的 evidence 和 testimony 将受到的高度关注。从一年前与 Settled Defendants 达成 settlements 时算起，procedural history 以及 trial 的临近本应 increased，而非 decreased，Plaintiffs 的 leverage。

Court 承认，Class counsel 一直是 Class 的 zealous advocates，并自行 funded 本 litigation，以对抗资源极其充裕的 adversaries。此外，Plaintiffs 的 case 中很可能存在 counsel 无法向本 Court reveal 的 weaknesses 和 challenges。尽管如此，Court concludes，与 Settled Defendants 相比，Remaining Defendants 至少应当 pay 其 fair share；Settled Defendants 与 Plaintiffs resolved 其 case 时，litigation 尚处于 Defendants 对 Plaintiffs 拥有多得多的 leverage 的阶段。

基于上述理由，Court DENIES Plaintiffs 就与 Remaining Defendants 达成的 settlements 提出的 Motion for Preliminary Approval。Court 另将 Case Management Conference 定于 September 10, 2014 的 2 p.m.。

### II. LEGAL STANDARD

The Court must review the fairness of class action settlements under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(e). The Rule states that "\[t\]he claims, issues, or defenses of a certified class may be settled, voluntarily dismissed, or compromised only with the court's approval." The Rule requires the Court to "direct notice in a reasonable manner to all class members who would be bound by the proposal" and further states that if a settlement "would bind class members, the court may approve it only after a hearing and on finding that it is fair, reasonable, and adequate." Fed. R. Civ. P. 23(e)(1)-(2). The principal purpose of the Court's supervision of class action settlements is to ensure "the agreement is not the product of fraud or overreaching by, or collusion between, the negotiating parties." Officers for Justice v. Civil Serv. Comm'n of City & Cnty. of S.F., 688 F.2d 615, 625 (9th Cir. 1982).

District courts have interpreted Rule 23(e) to require a two-step process for the approval of class action settlements: "the Court first determines whether a proposed class action settlement deserves preliminary approval and then, after notice is given to class members, whether final approval is warranted." Nat'l Rural Telecomms. Coop. v. DIRECTV, Inc., 221 F.R.D. 523, 525 (C.D. Cal. 2004). At the final approval stage, the Ninth Circuit has stated that "\[a\]ssessing a settlement proposal requires the district court to balance a number of factors: the strength of the plaintiffs' case; the risk, expense, complexity, and likely duration of further litigation; the risk of maintaining class action status throughout the trial; the amount offered in settlement; the extent of discovery completed and the stage of the proceedings; the experience and views of counsel; the presence of a governmental participant; and the reaction of the class members to the proposed settlement." Hanlon v. Chrysler Corp., 150 F.3d 1011, 1026 (9th Cir. 1998).

In contrast to these well-established, non-exhaustive factors for final approval, there is relatively scant appellate authority regarding the standard that a district court must apply in reviewing a settlement at the preliminary approval stage. Some district courts, echoing commentators, have stated that the relevant inquiry is whether the settlement "falls within the range of possible approval" or "within the range of reasonableness." In re Tableware Antitrust Litig., 484 F. Supp. 2d 1078, 1079 (N.D. Cal. 2007); see also Cordy v. USS-Posco Indus., No. 12-553, 2013 WL 4028627, at \*3 (N.D. Cal. Aug. 1, 2013) ("Preliminary approval of a settlement and notice to the proposed class is appropriate if the proposed settlement appears to be the product of serious, informed, non-collusive negotiations, has no obvious deficiencies, does not improperly grant preferential treatment to class representatives or segments of the class, and falls with the range of possible approval." (internal quotation marks omitted)). To undertake this analysis, the Court "must consider plaintiffs' expected recovery balanced against the value of the settlement offer." In re Nat'l Football League Players' Concussion Injury Litig., 961 F. Supp. 2d 708, 714 (E.D. Pa. 2014) (internal quotation marks omitted).

### III. DISCUSSION

Pursuant to the terms of the instant settlement, Class members who have not already opted out and who do not opt out will relinquish their rights to file suit against the Remaining Defendants for the claims at issue in this case. In exchange, Remaining Defendants will pay a total of $324.5 million, of which Plaintiffs' counsel may seek up to 25% (approximately $81 million) in attorneys' fees, $1.2 million in costs, and $80,000 per class representative in incentive payments. In addition, the settlement allows Remaining Defendants a pro rata reduction in the total amount they must pay if more than 4% of Class members opt out after receiving notice.[5](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:5) Class members would receive an average of approximately $3,750[6](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:6) from the instant settlement if the Court were to grant all requested deductions and there were no further opt-outs.[7](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:7)

The Court finds the total settlement amount falls below the range of reasonableness. The Court is concerned that Class members recover less on a proportional basis from the instant settlement with Remaining Defendants than from the settlement with the Settled Defendants a year ago, despite the fact that the case has progressed consistently in the Class's favor since then. Counsel's sole explanation for this reduced figure is that there are weaknesses in Plaintiffs' case such that the Class faces a substantial risk of non-recovery. However, that risk existed and was even greater when Plaintiffs settled with the Settled Defendants a year ago, when class certification had been denied.

The Court begins by comparing the instant settlement with Remaining Defendants to the settlements with the Settled Defendants, in light of the facts that existed at the time each settlement was reached. The Court then discusses the relative strengths and weaknesses of Plaintiffs' case to assess the reasonableness of the instant settlement.

## A. Comparison to the Initial Settlements

### 1\. Comparing the Settlement Amounts

The Court finds that the settlements with the Settled Defendants provide a useful benchmark against which to analyze the reasonableness of the instant settlement. The settlements with the Settled Defendants led to a fund totaling $20 million. See ECF No. 915 at 3. In approving the settlements, the Court relied upon the fact that the Settled Defendants employed 8% of Class members and paid out 5% of the total Class compensation during the Class period. See ECF No. 539 at 16:20-22 (Plaintiffs' counsel's explanation at the preliminary approval hearing with the Settled Defendants that the 5% figure "giv[es] you a sense of how big a slice of the case this settlement is relative to the rest of the case"). If Remaining Defendants were to settle at the same (or higher) rate as the Settled Defendants, Remaining Defendants' settlement fund would need to total at least $380 million. This number results from the fact that Remaining Defendants paid out 95% of the Class compensation during the Class period, while Settled Defendants paid only 5% of the Class compensation during the Class period.[8](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:8)

At the hearing on the instant Motion, counsel for Remaining Defendants suggested that the relevant benchmark is not total Class compensation, but rather is total Class membership. This would result in a benchmark figure for the Remaining Defendants of $230 million[0](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:92-divided-by-8-is-11-5-11-5-times-20-million-is-230-million). At a minimum, counsel suggested, the Court should compare the settlement amount to a range of $230 million to $380 million, within which the instant settlement falls. The Court rejects counsel's suggestion, which is contrary to the record. Counsel has provided no basis for why the number of Class members employed by each Defendant is a relevant metric. To the contrary, the relevant inquiry has always been total Class compensation. For example, in both of the settlements with the Settled Defendants and in the instant settlement, the Plans of Allocation call for determining each individual Class member's pay out by dividing the Class member's compensation during the Class period by the total Class compensation during the Class period. ECF No. 809 at 6 (noting that the denominator in the plan of allocation in the settlements with the Settled Defendants is the "total of base salaries paid to all approved Claimants in class positions during the Class period"); ECF No. 920 at 22 (same in the instant settlement); see also ECF No. 539 at 16:20-22 (Plaintiffs' counsel's statement that percent of the total Class compensation was relevant for benchmarking the settlements with the Settled Defendants to the rest of the case). At no point in the record has the percentage of Class membership employed by each Defendant ever been the relevant factor for determining damages exposure. Accordingly, the Court rejects the metric proposed by counsel for Remaining Defendants. Using the Settled Defendants' settlements as a yardstick, the appropriate benchmark settlement for the Remaining Defendants would be at least $380 million, more than $50 million greater than what the instant settlement provides.

Counsel for Remaining Defendants also suggested that benchmarking against the initial settlements would be inappropriate because the magnitude of the settlement numbers for Remaining Defendants dwarfs the numbers at issue in the Settled Defendants' settlements. This argument is premised on the idea that Defendants who caused more damage to the Class and who benefited more by suppressing a greater portion of class compensation should have to pay less than Defendants who caused less damage and who benefited less from the allegedly wrongful conduct. This argument is unpersuasive. Remaining Defendants are alleged to have received 95% of the benefit of the anti-solicitation agreements and to have caused 95% of the harm suffered by the Class in terms of lost compensation. Therefore, Remaining Defendants should have to pay at least 95% of the damages, which, under the instant settlement, they would not.

The Court also notes that had Plaintiffs prevailed at trial on their more than $3 billion damages claim, antitrust law provides for automatic trebling, see 15 U.S.C. § 15(a), so the total damages award could potentially have exceeded $9 billion. While the Ninth Circuit has not determined whether settlement amounts in antitrust cases must be compared to the single damages award requested by Plaintiffs or the automatically trebled damages amount, see Rodriguez v. W. Publ'g Corp., 563 F.3d 948, 964-65 (9th Cir. 2009), the instant settlement would lead to a total recovery of 11.29% of the single damages proposed by Plaintiffs' expert or 3.76% of the treble damages. Specifically, Dr. Leamer has calculated the total damages to the Class resulting from Defendants' allegedly unlawful conduct as $3.05 billion. See ECF No. 856-10. If the Court approves the instant settlements, the total settlements with all Defendants would be $344.5 million. This total would amount to 11.29% of the single damages that Dr. Leamer opines the Class suffered or 3.76% if Dr. Leamer's damages figure had been trebled.

### 2\. Relative Procedural Posture

The discount that Remaining Defendants have received vis-a-vis the Settled Defendants is particularly troubling in light of the changes in the procedural posture of the case between the two settlements, changes that the Court would expect to have increased, rather than decreased, Plaintiffs' bargaining power. Specifically, at the time the Settled Defendants settled, Plaintiffs were at a particularly weak point in their case. Though Plaintiffs had survived Defendants' motion to dismiss, Plaintiffs' motion for class certification had been denied, albeit without prejudice. Plaintiffs had re-briefed the class certification motion, but had no class certification ruling in their favor at the time they settled with the Settled Defendants. If the Court ultimately granted certification, Plaintiffs also did not know whether the Ninth Circuit would grant Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(f) review and reverse the certification. Accordingly, at that point, Defendants had significant leverage.

In contrast, the procedural posture of the case swung dramatically in Plaintiffs' favor after the initial settlements were reached. Specifically, the Court certified the Class over the vigorous objections of Defendants. In the 86-page order granting class certification, the Court repeatedly referred to Plaintiffs' evidence as "substantial" and "extensive," and the Court stated that it "could not identify a case at the class certification stage with the level of documentary evidence Plaintiffs have presented in the instant case." ECF No. 531 at 69. Thereafter, the Ninth Circuit denied Defendants' request to review the class certification order under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 23(f). This Court also denied Defendants' five motions for summary judgment and denied Defendants' motion to exclude Plaintiffs' principal expert on antitrust impact and damages. The instant settlement was reached a mere two weeks before the final pretrial conference and one month before a trial at which damaging evidence regarding Defendants would have been presented.

In sum, Plaintiffs were in a much stronger position at the time of the instant settlement—after the Class had been certified, appellate review of class certification had been denied, and Defendants' dispositive motions and motion to exclude Dr. Leamer's testimony had been denied—than they were at the time of the settlements with the Settled Defendants, when class certification had been denied. This shift in the procedural posture, which the Court would expect to have increased Plaintiffs' bargaining power, makes the more recent settlements for a proportionally lower amount even more troubling.

## B. Strength of Plaintiffs' Case

The Court now turns to the strength of Plaintiffs' case against the Remaining Defendants to evaluate the reasonableness of the settlement.

At the hearing on the instant Motion, Plaintiffs' counsel contended that one of the reasons the instant settlement was proportionally lower than the previous settlements is that the documentary evidence against the Settled Defendants (particularly, Lucasfilm and Pixar) is more compelling than the documentary evidence against the Remaining Defendants. As an initial matter, the Court notes that relevant evidence regarding the Settled Defendants would be admissible at a trial against Remaining Defendants because Plaintiffs allege an overarching conspiracy that included all Defendants. Accordingly, evidence regarding the role of Lucasfilm and Pixar in the creation of and the intended effect of the overarching conspiracy would be admissible.

Nonetheless, the Court notes that Plaintiffs are correct that there are particularly clear statements from Lucasfilm and Pixar executives regarding the nature and goals of the alleged conspiracy. Specifically, Edward Catmull (Pixar President) conceded in his deposition that anti-solicitation agreements were in place because solicitation "messes up the pay structure." ECF No. 431-9 at 81. Similarly, George Lucas (former Lucasfilm Chairman of the Board and CEO) stated, "we cannot get into a bidding war with other companies because we don't have the margins for that sort of thing." ECF No. 749-23 at 9.

However, there is equally compelling evidence that comes from the documents of the Remaining Defendants. This is particularly true for Google and Apple, the executives of which extensively discussed and enforced the anti-solicitation agreements. Specifically, as discussed in extensive detail in this Court's previous orders, Steve Jobs (Co-Founder, Former Chairman, and Former CEO of Apple, Former CEO of Pixar), Eric Schmidt (Google Executive Chairman, Member of the Board of Directors, and former CEO), and Bill Campbell (Chairman of Intuit Board of Directors, Co-Lead Director of Apple, and advisor to Google) were key players in creating and enforcing the anti-solicitation agreements. The Court now turns to the evidence against the Remaining Defendants that the finder of fact is likely to find compelling.

There is substantial and compelling evidence that Steve Jobs (Co-Founder, Former Chairman, and Former CEO of Apple, Former CEO of Pixar) was a, if not the, central figure in the alleged conspiracy. Several witnesses, in their depositions, testified to Mr. Jobs' role in the anti-solicitation agreements. For example, Eric Schmidt (Google Executive Chairman, Member of the Board of Directors, and former CEO) stated that Mr. Jobs "believed that you should not be hiring each others', you know, technical people" and that "it was inappropriate in \[Mr. Jobs'\] view for us to be calling in and hiring people." ECF No. 819-12 at 77. Edward Catmull (Pixar President) stated that Mr. Jobs "was very adamant about protecting his employee force." ECF No. 431-9 at 97. Sergey Brin (Google Co-Founder) testified that "I think Mr. Jobs' view was that people shouldn't piss him off. And I think that things that pissed him off were—would be hiring, you know—whatever." ECF No. 639-1 at 112. There would thus be ample evidence Mr. Jobs was involved in expanding the original anti-solicitation agreement between Lucasfilm and Pixar to the other Defendants in this case. After the agreements were extended, Mr. Jobs played a central role in enforcing these agreements. Four particular sets of evidence are likely to be compelling to the fact-finder.

First, after hearing that Google was trying to recruit employees from Apple's Safari team, Mr. Jobs threatened Mr. Brin, stating, as Mr. Brin recounted, "if you hire a single one of these people that means war." ECF No. 833-15.[9](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:10) In an email to Google's Executive Management Team as well as Bill Campbell (Chairman of Intuit Board of Directors, Co-Lead Director of Apple, and advisor to Google), Mr. Brin advised: "lets \[sic\] not make any new offers or contact new people at Apple until we have had a chance to discuss." Id. Mr. Campbell then wrote to Mr. Jobs: "Eric \[Schmidt\] told me that he got directly involved and firmly stopped all efforts to recruit anyone from Apple." ECF No. 746-5. As Mr. Brin testified in his deposition, "Eric made a—you know, a—you know, at least some kind of—had a conversation with Bill to relate to Steve to calm him down." ECF No. 639-1 at 61. As Mr. Schmidt put it, "Steve was unhappy, and Steve's unhappiness absolutely influenced the change we made in recruiting practice." ECF No. 819-12 at 21. Danielle Lambert (Apple's head of Human Resources) reciprocated to maintain Apple's end of the anti-solicitation agreements, instructing Apple recruiters: "Please add Google to your 'hands-off list. We recently agreed not to recruit from one another so if you hear of any recruiting they are doing against us, please be sure to let me know." ECF No. 746-15.

Second, other Defendants' CEOs maintained the anti-solicitation agreements out of fear of and deference to Mr. Jobs. For example, in 2005, when considering whether to enter into an anti-solicitation agreement with Apple, Bruce Chizen (former Adobe CEO), expressed concerns about the loss of "top talent" if Adobe did not enter into an anti-solicitation agreement with Apple, stating, "if I tell Steve it's open season (other than senior managers), he will deliberately poach Adobe just to prove a point. Knowing Steve, he will go after some of our top Mac talent like Chris Cox and he will do it in a way in which they will be enticed to come (extraordinary packages and Steve wooing)."[10](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:11) ECF No. 297-15.

This was the genesis of the Apple-Adobe agreement. Specifically, after Mr. Jobs complained to Mr. Chizen on May 26, 2005 that Adobe was recruiting Apple employees, ECF No. 291-17, Mr. Chizen responded by saying, "I thought we agreed not to recruit any senior level employees . . . . I would propose we keep it that way. Open to discuss. It would be good to agree." Id. Mr. Jobs was not satisfied, and replied by threatening to send Apple recruiters after Adobe's employees: "OK, I'll tell our recruiters that they are free to approach any Adobe employee who is not a Sr. Director or VP. Am I understanding your position correctly?" Id. Mr. Chizen immediately gave in: "I'd rather agree NOT to actively solicit any employee from either company . . . . If you are in agreement I will let my folks know." Id. (emphasis in original). The next day, Theresa Townsley (Adobe Vice President Human Resources) announced to her recruiting team, "Bruce and Steve Jobs have an agreement that we are not to solicit ANY Apple employees, and vice versa." ECF No. 291-18 (emphasis in original). Adobe then placed Apple on its "\[c\]ompanies that are off limits" list, which instructed Adobe employees not to cold call Apple employees. ECF No. 291-11.

Google took even more drastic actions in response to Mr. Jobs. For example, when a recruiter from Google's engineering team contacted an Apple employee in 2007, Mr. Jobs forwarded the message to Mr. Schmidt and stated, "I would be very pleased if your recruiting department would stop doing this." ECF No. 291-23. Google responded by making a "public example" out of the recruiter and "terminat\[ing\] \[the recruiter\] within the hour." Id. The aim of this public spectacle was to "(hopefully) prevent future occurrences." Id. Once the recruiter was terminated, Mr. Schmidt emailed Mr. Jobs, apologizing and informing Mr. Jobs that the recruiter had been terminated. Mr. Jobs forwarded Mr. Schmidt's email to an Apple human resources official and stated merely, ":)." ECF No. 746-9.

A year prior to this termination, Google similarly took seriously Mr. Jobs' concerns. Specifically, in 2006, Mr. Jobs emailed Mr. Schmidt and said, "I am told that Googles \[sic\] new cell phone software group is relentlessly recruiting in our iPod group. If this is indeed true, can you put a stop to it?" ECF No. 291-24 at 3. After Mr. Schmidt forwarded this to Human Resources professionals at Google, Arnnon Geshuri (Google Recruiting Director) prepared a detailed report stating that an extensive investigation did not find a breach of the anti-solicitation agreement.

Similarly, in 2006, Google scrapped plans to open a Google engineering center in Paris after a Google executive emailed Mr. Jobs to ask whether Google could hire three former Apple engineers to work at the prospective facility, and Mr. Jobs responded "\[w\]e'd strongly prefer that you not hire these guys." ECF No. 814-2. The whole interaction began with Google's request to Steve Jobs for permission to hire Jean-Marie Hullot, an Apple engineer. The record is not clear whether Mr. Hullot was a current or former Apple employee. A Google executive contacted Steve Jobs to ask whether Google could make an offer to Mr. Hullot, and Mr. Jobs did not timely respond to the Google executive's request. At this point, the Google executive turned to Intuit's Board Chairman Bill Campbell as a potential ambassador from Google to Mr. Jobs. Specifically, the Google executive noted that Mr. Campbell "is on the board at Apple and Google, so Steve will probably return his call." ECF No. 428-6. The same day that Mr. Campbell reached out to Mr. Jobs, Mr. Jobs responded to the Google executive, seeking more information on what exactly the Apple engineer would be working. ECF No. 428-9. Once Mr. Jobs was satisfied, he stated that the hire "would be fine with me." Id. However, two weeks later, when Mr. Hullot and a Google executive sought Mr. Jobs' permission to hire four of Mr. Hullot's former Apple colleagues (three were former Apple employees and one had given notice of impending departure from Apple), Mr. Jobs promptly responded, indicating that the hires would not be acceptable. ECF No. 428-9. Google promptly scrapped the plan, and the Google executive responded deferentially to Mr. Jobs, stating, "Steve, Based on your strong preference that we not hire the ex-Apple engineers, Jean-Marie and I decided not to open a Google Paris engineering center." Id. The Google executive also forwarded the email thread to Mr. Brin, Larry Page (Google Co-Founder), and Mr. Campbell. Id.

Third, Mr. Jobs attempted (unsuccessfully) to expand the anti-solicitation agreements to Palm, even threatening litigation. Specifically, Mr. Jobs called Edward Colligan (former President and CEO of Palm) to ask Mr. Colligan to enter into an anti-solicitation agreement and threatened patent litigation against Palm if Palm refused to do so. ECF No. 293 ¶¶ 6-8. Mr. Colligan responded via email, and told Mr. Jobs that Mr. Jobs' "proposal that we agree that neither company will hire the other's employees, regardless of the individual's desires, is not only wrong, it is likely illegal." Id. at 4-5. Mr. Colligan went on to say that, "We can't dictate where someone will work, nor should we try. I can't deny people who elect to pursue their livelihood at Palm the right to do so simply because they now work for Apple, and I wouldn't want you to do that to current Palm employees." Id. at 5. Finally, Mr. Colligan wrote that "\[t\]hreatening Palm with a patent lawsuit in response to a decision by one employee to leave Apple is just out of line. A lawsuit would not serve either of our interests, and will not stop employees from migrating between our companies . . . . We will both just end up paying a lot of lawyers a lot of money." Id. at 5-6. Mr. Jobs wrote the following back to Mr. Colligan: "This is not satisfactory to Apple." Id. at 8. Mr. Jobs went on to write that "I'm sure you realize the asymmetry in the financial resources of our respective companies when you say: 'we will both just end up paying a lot of lawyers a lot of money.'" Id. Mr. Jobs concluded: "My advice is to take a look at our patent portfolio before you make a final decision here." Id.

Fourth, Apple's documents provide strong support for Plaintiffs' theory of impact, namely that rigid wage structures and internal equity concerns would have led Defendants to engage in structural changes to compensation structures to mitigate the competitive threat that solicitation would have posed. Apple's compensation data shows that, for each year in the Class period, Apple had a "job structure system," which included categorizing and compensating its workforce according to a discrete set of company-wide job levels assigned to all salaried employees and four associated sets of base salary ranges applicable to "Top," "Major," "National," and "Small" geographic markets. ECF No. 745-7 at 14-15, 52-53; ECF No.517-16 ¶¶ 6, 10 & Ex. B. Every salary range had a "min," "mid," and "max" figure. See id. Apple also created a Human Resources and recruiting tool called "Merlin," which was an internal system for tracking employee records and performance, and required managers to grade employees at one of four pre-set levels. See ECF No. 749-6 at 142-43, 145-46; ECF No. 749-11 at 52-53; ECF No. 749-12 at 33. As explained by Tony Fadell (former Apple Senior Vice President, iPod Division, and advisor to Steve Jobs), Merlin "would say, this is the employee, this is the level, here are the salary ranges, and through that tool we were then—we understood what the boundaries were." ECF No. 749-11 at 53. Going outside these prescribed "guidelines" also required extra approval. ECF No. 749-7 at 217; ECF No. 749-11 at 53 ("And if we were to go outside of that, then we would have to pull in a bunch of people to then approve anything outside of that range.").

Concerns about internal equity also permeated Apple's compensation program. Steven Burmeister (Apple Senior Director of Compensation) testified that internal equity—which Mr. Burmeister defined as the notion of whether an employee's compensation is "fair based on the individual's contribution relative to the other employees in your group, or across your organization"—inheres in some, "if not all," of the guidelines that managers consider in determining starting salaries. ECF No. 745-7 at 61-64; ECF No. 753-12. In fact, as explained by Patrick Burke (former Apple Technical Recruiter and Staffing Manager), when hiring a new employee at Apple, "compar\[ing\] the candidate" to the other people on the team they would join "was the biggest determining factor on what salary we gave." ECF No. 745-6 at 279.

The evidence against Google is equally compelling. Email evidence reveals that Eric Schmidt (Google Executive Chairman, Member of the Board of Directors, and former CEO) terminated at least two recruiters for violations of anti-solicitation agreements, and threatened to terminate more. As discussed above, there is direct evidence that Mr. Schmidt terminated a recruiter at Steve Jobs' behest after the recruiter attempted to solicit an Apple employee. Moreover, in an email to Bill Campbell (Chairman of Intuit Board of Directors, Co-Lead Director of Apple, and advisor to Google), Mr. Schmidt indicated that he directed a for-cause termination of another Google recruiter, who had attempted to recruit an executive of eBay, which was on Google's do-not-cold-call list. ECF No. 814-14. Finally, as discussed in more detail below, Mr. Schmidt informed Paul Otellini (CEO of Intel and Member of the Google Board of Directors) that Mr. Schmidt would terminate any recruiter who recruited Intel employees.

Furthermore, Google maintained a formal "Do Not Call" list, which grouped together Apple, Intel, and Intuit and was approved by top executives. ECF No. 291-28. The list also included other companies, such as Genentech, Paypal, and eBay. Id. A draft of the "Do Not Call" list was presented to Google's Executive Management Group, a committee consisting of Google's senior executives, including Mr. Schmidt, Larry Page (Google Co-Founder), Sergey Brin (Google Co-Founder), and Shona Brown (former Google Senior Vice President of Business Operations). ECF No. 291-26. Mr. Schmidt approved the list. See id.; see also ECF No. 291-27 (email from Mr. Schmidt stating: "This looks very good."). Moreover, there is evidence that Google executives knew that the anti-solicitation agreements could lead to legal troubles, but nevertheless proceeded with the agreements. When Ms. Brown asked Mr. Schmidt whether he had any concerns with sharing information regarding the "Do Not Call" list with Google's competitors, Mr. Schmidt responded that he preferred that it be shared "verbally\[,\] since I don't want to create a paper trail over which we can be sued later?" ECF No. 291-40. Ms. Brown responded: "makes sense to do orally. i agree." Id.

Google's response to competition from Facebook also demonstrates the impact of the alleged conspiracy. Google had long been concerned about Facebook hiring's effect on retention. For example, in an email to top Google executives, Mr. Brin in 2007 stated that "the facebook phenomenon creates a real retention problem." ECF No. 814-4. A month later, Mr. Brin announced a policy of making counteroffers within one hour to any Google employee who received an offer from Facebook. ECF No. 963-2.

In March 2008, Arnnon Geshuri (Google Recruiting Director) discovered that non-party Facebook had been cold calling into Google's Site Reliability Engineering ("SRE") team. Mr. Geshuri's first response was to suggest contacting Sheryl Sandberg (Chief Operating Officer for non-party Facebook) in an effort to "ask her to put a stop to the targeted sourcing effort directed at our SRE team" and "to consider establishing a mutual 'Do Not Call' agreement that specifies that we will not cold-call into each other." ECF No. 963-3. Mr. Geshuri also suggested "look\[ing\] internally and review\[ing\] the attrition rate for the SRE group," stating, "\[w\]e may want to consider additional individual retention incentives or team incentives to keep attrition as low as possible in SRE." Id. (emphasis added). Finally, an alternative suggestion was to "\[s\]tart an aggressive campaign to call into their company and go after their folks—no holds barred. We would be unrelenting and a force of nature." Id. In response, Bill Campbell (Chairman of Intuit Board of Directors, Co-Lead Director of Apple, and advisor to Google), in his capacity as an advisor to Google, suggested "Who should contact Sheryl [Sandberg](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/or%20Mark%20[Zuckerberg]) to get a cease fire? We have to get a truce." Id. Facebook refused.

In 2010, Google altered its salary structure with a "Big Bang" in response to Facebook's hiring, which provides additional support for Plaintiffs' theory of antitrust impact. Specifically, after a period in which Google lost a significant number of employees to Facebook, Google began to study Facebook's solicitation of Google employees. ECF No. 190 ¶ 109. One month after beginning this study, Google announced its "Big Bang," which involved an increase to the base salary of all of its salaried employees by 10% and provided an immediate cash bonus of $1,000 to all employees. ECF No. 296-18. Laszlo Bock (Google Senior Vice President of People Operations) explained that the rationale for the Big Bang included: (1) being "responsive to rising attrition;" (2) supporting higher retention because "higher salaries generate higher fixed costs;" and (3) being "very strategic because start-ups don't have the cash flow to match, and big companies are (a) too worried about internal equity and scalability to do this and (b) don't have the margins to do this." ECF No. 296-20.

Other Google documents provide further evidence of Plaintiffs' theory of antitrust impact. For example, Google's Chief Culture Officer stated that "\[c\]old calling into companies to recruit is to be expected unless they're on our 'don't call' list." ECF No. 291-41. Moreover, Google found that although referrals were the largest source of hires, "agencies and passively sourced candidates offer\[ed\] the highest yield." ECF No. 780-8. The spread of information between employees had there been active solicitations—which is central to Plaintiffs' theory of impact—is also demonstrated in Google's evidence. For example, one Google employee states that "\[i\]t's impossible to keep something like this a secret. The people getting counter offers talk, not just to Googlers and ex-Googlers, but also to the competitors where they received their offers (in the hopes of improving them), and those competitors talk too, using it as a tool to recruit more Googlers." ECF No. 296-23.

The wage structure and internal equity concerns at Google also support Plaintiffs' theory of impact. Google had many job families, many grades within job families, and many job titles within grades. See, e.g., ECF No. 298-7, ECF No. 298-8; see also Cisneros Decl., Ex. S (Brown Depo.) at 74-76 (discussing salary ranges utilized by Google); ECF No. 780-4 at 25-26 (testifying that Google's 2007 salary ranges had generally the same structure as the 2004 salary ranges). Throughout the Class period, Google utilized salary ranges and pay bands with minima and maxima and either means or medians. ECF No. 958-1 ¶ 66; see ECF No. 427-3 at 15-17. As explained by Shona Brown (former Google Senior Vice President, Business Operations), "if you discussed a specific role \[at Google\], you could understand that role was at a specific level on a certain job ladder." ECF No. 427-3 at 27-28; ECF No. 745-11. Frank Wagner (Google Director of Compensation) testified that he could locate the target salary range for jobs at Google through an internal company website. See ECF No. 780-4 at 31-32 ("Q: And if you wanted to identify what the target salary would be for a certain job within a certain grade, could you go online or go to some place . . . and pull up what that was for that job family and that grade? . . . A: Yes."). Moreover, Google considered internal equity to be an important goal. Google utilized a salary algorithm in part for the purpose of "\[e\]nsur\[ing\] internal equity by managing salaries within a reasonable range." ECF No. 814-19. Furthermore, because Google "strive\[d\] to achieve fairness in overall salary distribution," "high performers with low salaries \[would\] get larger percentage increases than high performers with high salaries." ECF No. 817-1 at 15.

In addition, Google analyzed and compared its equity compensation to Apple, Intel, Adobe, and Intuit, among other companies, each of which it designated as a "peer company" based on meeting criteria such as being a "high-tech company," a "high-growth company," and a "key labor market competitor." ECF No. 773-1. In 2007, based in part on an analysis of Google as compared to its peer companies, Mr. Bock and Dave Rolefson (Google Equity Compensation Manager) wrote that "\[o\]ur biggest labor market competitors are significantly exceeding their own guidelines to beat Google for talent." Id.

Finally, Google's own documents undermine Defendants' principal theory of lack of antitrust impact, that compensation decisions would be one off and not classwide. Alan Eustace (Google Senior Vice President) commented on concerns regarding competition for workers and Google's approach to counteroffers by noting that, "it sometimes makes sense to make changes in compensation, even if it introduces discontinuities in your current comp, to save your best people, and send a message to the hiring company that we'll fight for our best people." ECF No. 296-23. Because recruiting "a few really good people" could inspire "many, many others \[to\] follow," Mr. Eustace concluded, "\[y\]ou can't afford to be a rich target for other companies." Id. According to him, the "long-term . . . right approach is not to deal with these situations as one-offs but to have a _systematic approach_ to compensation that makes it very difficult for anyone to get a better offer." Id. (emphasis added).

Google's impact on the labor market before the anti-solicitation agreements was best summarized by Meg Whitman (former CEO of eBay) who called Mr. Schmidt "to talk about \[Google's\] hiring practices." ECF No. 814-15. As Eric Schmidt told Google's senior executives, Ms. Whitman said "Google is the talk of the valley because \[you\] are driving up salaries across the board." Id. A year after this conversation, Google added eBay to its do-not-cold-call list. ECF No. 291-28.

There is also compelling evidence against Intel. Google reacted to requests regarding enforcement of the anti-solicitation agreement made by Intel executives similarly to Google's reaction to Steve Jobs' request to enforce the agreements discussed above. For example, after Paul Otellini (CEO of Intel and Member of the Google Board of Directors) received an internal complaint regarding Google's successful recruiting efforts of Intel's technical employees on September 26, 2007, ECF No. 188-8 ("Paul, I am losing so many people to Google . . . . We are countering but thought you should know."), Mr. Otellini forwarded the email to Eric Schmidt (Google Executive Chairman, Member of the Board of Directors, and former CEO) and stated "Eric, can you pls help here???" Id. Mr. Schmidt obliged and forwarded the email to his recruiting team, who prepared a report for Mr. Schmidt on Google's activities. ECF No. 291-34. The next day, Mr. Schmidt replied to Mr. Otellini, "If we find that a recruiter called into Intel, we will terminate the recruiter," the same remedy afforded to violations of the Apple-Google agreement. ECF No. 531 at 37. In another email to Mr. Schmidt, Mr. Otellini stated, "Sorry to bother you again on this topic, but my guys are very troubled by Google continuing to recruit our key players." See ECF No. 428-8.

Moreover, Mr. Otellini was aware that the anti-solicitation agreement could be legally troublesome. Specifically, Mr. Otellini stated in an email to another Intel executive regarding the Google-Intel agreement: "Let me clarify. We have nothing signed. We have a handshake 'no recruit' between eric and myself. I would not like this broadly known." Id.

Furthermore, there is evidence that Mr. Otellini knew of the anti-solicitation agreements to which Intel was not a party. Specifically, both Sergey Brin (Google Co-Founder) and Mr. Schmidt of Google testified that they would have told Mr. Otellini that Google had an anti-solicitation agreement with Apple. ECF No. 639-1 at 74:15 ("I'm sure that we would have mentioned it\[.\]"); ECF No. 819-12 at 60 ("I'm sure I spoke with Paul about this at some point."). Intel's own expert testified that Mr. Otellini was likely aware of Google's other bilateral agreements by virtue of Mr. Otellini's membership on Google's board. ECF No. 771 at 4. The fact that Intel was added to Google's do-not-cold-call list on the same day that Apple was added further suggests Intel's participation in an overarching conspiracy. ECF No. 291-28.

Additionally, notwithstanding the fact that Intel and Google were competitors for talent, Mr. Otellini "lifted from Google" a Google document discussing the bonus plans of peer companies including Apple and Intel. Cisneros Decl., Ex. 463. True competitors for talent would not likely share such sensitive bonus information absent agreements not to compete.

Moreover, key documents related to antitrust impact also implicate Intel. Specifically, Intel recognized the importance of cold calling and stated in its "Complete Guide to Sourcing" that "\[Cold\] \[c\]alling candidates is one of the most efficient and effective ways to recruit." ECF No. 296-22. Intel also benchmarked compensation against other "tech companies generally considered comparable to Intel," which Intel defined as a "\[b\]lend of semiconductor, software, networking, communications, and diversified computer companies." ECF No. 754-2. According to Intel, in 2007, these comparable companies included Apple and Google. Id. These documents suggest, as Plaintiffs contend, that the anti-solicitation agreements led to structural, rather than individual depression, of Class members' wages.

Furthermore, Intel had a "compensation structure," with job grades and job classifications. See ECF No. 745-13 at 73 ("\[W\]e break jobs into one of three categories—job families, we call them—R&D, tech, and nontech, there's a lot more . . . ."). The company assigned employees to a grade level based on their skills and experience. ECF No. 745-11 at 23; see also ECF No. 749-17 at 45 (explaining that everyone at Intel is assigned a "classification" similar to a job grade). Intel standardized its salary ranges throughout the company; each range applied to multiple jobs, and most jobs spanned multiple salary grades. ECF No. 745-16 at 59. Intel further broke down its salary ranges into quartiles, and compensation at Intel followed "a bell-curve distribution, where most of the employees are in the middle quartiles, and a much smaller percentage are in the bottom and top quartiles." Id. at 62-63.

Intel also used a software tool to provide guidance to managers about an employee's pay range which would also take into account market reference ranges and merit. ECF No. 758-9. As explained by Randall Goodwin (Intel Technology Development Manager), "\[i\]f the tool recommended something and we thought we wanted to make a proposed change that was outside its guidelines, we would write some justification." ECF No. 749-15 at 52. Similarly, Intel regularly ran reports showing the salary range distribution of its employees. ECF No. 749-16 at 64.

The evidence also supports the rigidity of Intel's wage structure. For example, in a 2004 Human Resources presentation, Intel states that, although "\[c\]ompensation differentiation is desired by Intel's Meritocracy philosophy," "short and long term high performer differentiation is questionable." ECF No. 758-10 at 13. Indeed, Intel notes that "\[l\]ack of differentiation has existed historically based on an analysis of '99 data." Id. at 19. As key "\[v\]ulnerability \[c\]hallenges," Intel identifies: (1) "\[m\]anagers (in)ability to distinguish at \[f\]ocal"—"actual merit increases are significantly reduced from system generated increases," "\[l\]ong term threat to retention of key players"; (2) "\[l\]ittle to no actual pay differentiation for HPs \[high performers\]"; and (3) "\[n\]o explicit strategy to differentiate." Id. at 24 (emphasis added).

In addition, Intel used internal equity "to determine wage rates for new hires and current employees that correspond to each job's relative value to Intel." ECF No. 749-16 at 210-11; ECF No. 961-5. To assist in that process, Intel used a tool that generates an "Internal Equity Report" when making offers to new employees. ECF No. 749-16 at 212-13. In the words of Ogden Reid (Intel Director of Compensation and Benefits), "\[m\]uch of our culture screams egalitarianism . . . . While we play lip service to meritocracy, we really believe more in treating everyone the same within broad bands." ECF No. 769-8.

An Intel human resources document from 2002—prior to the anti-solicitation agreements—recognized "continuing inequities in the alignment of base salaries/EB targets between hired and acquired Intel employees" and "parallel issues relating to accurate job grading within these two populations." ECF No. 750-15. In response, Intel planned to: (1) "Review exempt job grade assignments for job families with 'critical skills.' Make adjustments, as appropriate"; and (2) "Validate perception of inequities . . . . Scope impact to employees. Recommend adjustments, as appropriate." Id. An Intel human resources document confirms that, in or around 2004, "\[n\]ew hire salary premiums drove salary range adjustment." ECF No. 298-5 at 7 (emphasis added).

Intel would "match an Intel job code in grade to a market survey job code in grade," ECF No. 749-16 at 89, and use that as part of the process for determining its "own focal process or pay delivery," id. at 23. If job codes fell below the midpoint, plus or minus a certain percent, the company made "special market adjustment\[s\]." Id. at 90.

Evidence from Adobe also suggests that Adobe was aware of the impact of its anti-solicitation agreements. Adobe personnel recognized that "Apple would be a great target to look into" for the purpose of recruiting, but knew that they could not do so because, "\[u\]nfortunately, Bruce \[Chizen (former Adobe CEO)\] and Apple CEO Steve Jobs have a gentleman's agreement not to poach each other's talent." ECF No. 291-13. Adobe executives were also part and parcel of the group of high-ranking executives that entered into, enforced, and attempted to expand the anti-solicitation agreements. Specifically, Mr. Chizen, in response to discovering that Apple was recruiting employees of Macromedia (a separate entity that Adobe would later acquire), helped ensure, through an email to Mr. Jobs, that Apple would honor Apple's pre-existing anti-solicitation agreements with both Adobe and Macromedia after Adobe's acquisition of Macromedia. ECF No. 608-3 at 50.

Adobe viewed Google and Apple to be among its top competitors for talent and expressed concern about whether Adobe was "winning the talent war." ECF No. 296-3. Adobe further considered itself in a "six-horse race from a benefits standpoint," which included Google, Apple, and Intuit as among the other "horses." See ECF No. 296-4. In 2008, Adobe benchmarked its compensation against nine companies including Google, Apple, and Intel. ECF No. 296-4; cf. ECF No. 652-6 (showing that, in 2010, Adobe considered Intuit to be a "direct peer," and considered Apple, Google, and Intel to be "reference peers," though Adobe did not actually benchmark compensation against these latter companies).

Nevertheless, despite viewing other Defendants as competitors, evidence from Adobe suggests that Adobe had knowledge of the bilateral agreements to which Adobe was not a party. Specifically, Adobe shared confidential compensation information with other Defendants, despite the fact that Adobe viewed at least some of the other Defendants as competitors and did not have a bilateral agreement with them. For example, HR personnel at Intuit and at Adobe exchanged information labeled "confidential" regarding how much compensation each firm would give and to which employees that year. ECF No. 652-8. Adobe and Intuit shared confidential compensation information even though the two companies had no bilateral anti-solicitation agreement, and Adobe viewed Intuit as a direct competitor for talent. Such direct competitors for talent would not likely share such sensitive compensation information in the absence of an overarching conspiracy.

Meanwhile, Google circulated an email that expressly discussed how its "budget is comparable to other tech companies" and compared the precise percentage of Google's merit budget increases to that of Adobe, Apple, and Intel. ECF No. 807-13. Google had Adobe's precise percentage of merit budget increases even though Google and Adobe had no bilateral anti-solicitation agreement. Such sharing of sensitive compensation information among competitors is further evidence of an overarching conspiracy.

Adobe recognized that in the absence of the anti-solicitation agreements, pay increases would be necessary, echoing Plaintiffs' theory of impact. For example, out of concern that one employee—a "star performer" due to his technical skills, intelligence, and collaborative abilities—might leave Adobe because "he could easily get a great job elsewhere if he desired," Adobe considered how best to retain him. ECF No. 799-22. In so doing, Adobe expressed concern about the fact that this employee had already interviewed with four other companies and communicated with friends who worked there. Id. Thus, Adobe noted that the employee "was aware of his value in the market" as well as the fact that the employee's friends from college were "making approximately $15k more per year than he \[wa\]s." Id. In response, Adobe decided to give the employee an immediate pay raise. Id.

Plaintiffs' theory of impact is also supported by evidence that every job position at Adobe was assigned a job title, and every job title had a corresponding salary range within Adobe's salary structure, which included a salary minimum, middle, and maximum. See ECF No. 804-17 at 4, 8, 72, 85-86. Adobe expected that the distribution of its existing employees' salaries would fit "a bell curve." ECF No. 749-5 at 57. To assist managers in staying within the prescribed ranges for setting and adjusting salaries, Adobe had an online salary planning tool as well as salary matrices, which provided managers with guidelines based on market salary data. See ECF No. 804-17 at 29-30 ("\[E\]ssentially the salary planning tool is populated with employee information for a particular manager, so the employees on their team \[sic\]. You have the ability to kind of look at their current compensation. It shows them what the range is for the current role that they're in . . . . The tool also has the ability to provide kind of the guidelines that we recommend in terms of how managers might want to think about spending their allocated budget."). Adobe's practice, if employees were below the minimum recommended salary range, was to "adjust them to the minimum as part of the annual review" and "red flag them." Id. at 12. Deviations from the salary ranges would also result in conversations with managers, wherein Adobe's officers explained, "we have a minimum for a reason because we believe you need to be in this range to be competitive." Id.

Internal equity was important at Adobe, as it was at other Defendants. As explained by Debbie Streeter (Adobe Vice President, Total Rewards), Adobe "always look\[ed\] at internal equity as a data point, because if you are going to go hire somebody externally that's making . . . more than somebody who's an existing employee that's a high performer, you need to know that before you bring them in." ECF No.749-5 at 175. Similarly, when considering whether to extend a counteroffer, Adobe advised "internal equity should ALWAYS be considered." ECF No. 746-7 at 5.

Moreover, Donna Morris (Adobe Senior Vice President, Global Human Resources Division) expressed concern "about internal equity due to compression (the market driving pay for new hires above the current employees)." ECF No. 298-9 ("Reality is new hires are requiring base pay at or above the midpoint due to an increasingly aggressive market."). Adobe personnel stated that, because of the fixed budget, they may not be able to respond to the problem immediately "but could look at \[compression\] for FY2006 if market remains aggressive."[11](https://danluu.com/google-wage-fixing/#fn:12) Id.

### D. Weaknesses in Plaintiffs' Case

Plaintiffs contend that though this evidence is compelling, there are also weaknesses in Plaintiffs' case that make trial risky. Plaintiffs contend that these risks are substantial. Specifically, Plaintiffs point to the following challenges that they would have faced in presenting their case to a jury: (1) convincing a jury to find a single overarching conspiracy among the seven Defendants in light of the fact that several pairs of Defendants did not have anti-solicitation agreements with each other; (2) proving damages in light of the fact that Defendants intended to present six expert economists that would attack the methodology of Plaintiffs' experts; and (3) overcoming the fact that Class members' compensation has increased in the last ten years despite a sluggish economy and overcoming general anti-tech worker sentiment in light of the perceived and actual wealth of Class members. Plaintiffs also point to outstanding legal issues, such as the pending motions in limine and the pending motion to determine whether the per se or rule of reason analysis should apply, which could have aided Defendants' ability to present a case that the bilateral agreements had a pro-competitive purpose. See ECF No. 938 at 10-14.

The Court recognizes that Plaintiffs face substantial risks if they proceed to trial. Nonetheless, the Court cannot, in light of the evidence above, conclude that the instant settlement amount is within the range of reasonableness, particularly compared to the settlements with the Settled Defendants and the subsequent development of the litigation. The Court further notes that there is evidence in the record that mitigate at least some of the weaknesses in Plaintiffs' case.

As to proving an overarching conspiracy, several pieces of evidence undermine Defendants' contentions that the bilateral agreements were unrelated to each other. Importantly, two individuals, Steve Jobs (Co-Founder, Former Chairman, and Former CEO of Apple) and Bill Campbell (Chairman of Intuit Board of Directors, Co-Lead Director of Apple, and advisor to Google), personally entered into or facilitated each of the bilateral agreements in this case. Specifically, Mr. Jobs and George Lucas (former Chairman and CEO of Lucasfilm), created the initial anti-solicitation agreement between Lucasfilm and Pixar when Mr. Jobs was an executive at Pixar. Thereafter, Apple, under the leadership of Mr. Jobs, entered into an agreement with Pixar, which, as discussed below, Pixar executives compared to the Lucasfilm-Pixar agreement. It was Mr. Jobs again, who, as discussed above, reached out to Sergey Brin (Google Co-Founder) and Eric Schmidt (Google Executive Chairman, Member of the Board of Directors, and former CEO) to create the Apple-Google agreement. This agreement was reached with the assistance of Mr. Campbell, who was Intuit's Board Chairman, a friend of Mr. Jobs, and an advisor to Google. The Apple-Google agreement was discussed at Google Board meetings, at which both Mr. Campbell and Paul Otellini (Chief Executive Officer of Intel and Member of the Google Board of Directors) were present. ECF No. 819-10 at 47. After discussions between Mr. Brin and Mr. Otellini and between Mr. Schmidt and Mr. Otellini, Intel was added to Google's do-not-cold-call list. Mr. Campbell then used his influence at Google to successfully lobby Google to add Intuit, of which Mr. Campbell was Chairman of the Board of Directors, to Google's do-not-cold-call list. See ECF No. 780-6 at 8-9. Moreover, it was a mere two months after Mr. Jobs entered into the Apple-Google agreement that Apple pressured Bruce Chizen (former CEO of Adobe) to enter into an Apple-Adobe agreement. ECF No. 291-17. As this discussion demonstrates, Mr. Jobs and Mr. Campbell were the individuals most closely linked to the formation of each step of the alleged conspiracy, as they were present in the process of forming each of the links.

In light of the overlapping nature of this small group of executives who negotiated and enforced the anti-solicitation agreements, it is not surprising that these executives knew of the other bilateral agreements to which their own firms were not a party. For example, both Mr. Brin and Mr. Schmidt of Google testified that they would have told Mr. Otellini of Intel that Google had an anti-solicitation agreement with Apple. ECF No. 639-1 at 74:15 ("I'm sure we would have mentioned it\[.\]"); ECF No. 819-12 at 60 ("I'm sure I spoke with Paul about this at some point."). Intel's own expert testified that Mr. Otellini was likely aware of Google's other bilateral agreements by virtue of Mr. Otellini's membership on Google's board. ECF No. 771 at 4. Moreover, Google recruiters knew of the Adobe-Apple agreement. Id. (Google recruiter's notation that Apple has "a serious 'hands-off policy with Adobe"). In addition, Mr. Schmidt of Google testified that it would be "fair to extrapolate" based on Mr. Schmidt's knowledge of Mr. Jobs, that Mr. Jobs "would have extended \[anti-solicitation agreements\] to others." ECF No. 638-8 at 170. Furthermore, it was this same mix of top executives that successfully and unsuccessfully attempted to expand the agreement to other companies in Silicon Valley, such as eBay, Facebook, Macromedia, and Palm, as discussed above, suggesting that the agreements were neither isolated nor one off agreements.

In addition, the six bilateral agreements contained nearly identical terms, precluding each pair of Defendants from affirmatively soliciting any of each other's employees. ECF No. 531 at 30. Moreover, as discussed above, Defendants recognized the similarity of the agreements. For example, Google lumped together Apple, Intel, and Intuit on Google's "do-not-cold-call" list. Furthermore, Google's "do-not-cold-call" list stated that the Apple-Google agreement and the Intel-Google agreement commenced on the same date. Finally, in an email, Lori McAdams (Pixar Vice President of Human Resources and Administration), explicitly compared the anti-solicitation agreements, stating that "effective now, we'll follow a gentleman's agreement with Apple that is similar to our Lucasfilm agreement." ECF No. 531 at 26.

As to the contention that Plaintiffs would have to rebut Defendants' contentions that the anti-solicitation agreements aided collaborations and were therefore pro-competitive, there is no documentary evidence that links the anti-solicitation agreements to any collaboration. None of the documents that memorialize collaboration agreements mentions the broad anti-solicitation agreements, and none of the documents that memorialize broad anti-solicitation agreements mentions collaborations. Furthermore, even Defendants' experts conceded that those closest to the collaborations did not know of the anti-solicitation agreements. ECF No. 852-1 at 8. In addition, Defendants' top executives themselves acknowledge the lack of any collaborative purpose. For example, Mr. Chizen of Adobe admitted that the Adobe-Apple anti-solicitation agreement was "not limited to any particular projects on which Apple and Adobe were collaborating." ECF No. 962-7 at 42. Moreover, the U.S. Department of Justice ("DOJ") also determined that the anti-solicitation agreements "were not ancillary to any legitimate collaboration," "were broader than reasonably necessary for the formation or implementation of any collaborative effort," and "disrupted the normal price-setting mechanisms that apply in the labor setting." ECF No. 93-1 ¶ 16; ECF No. 93-4 ¶ 7. The DOJ concluded that Defendants entered into agreements that were restraints of trade that were per se unlawful under the antitrust laws. ECF No. 93-1 ¶ 35; ECF No. 93-4 ¶ 3. Thus, despite the fact that Defendants have claimed since the beginning of this litigation that there were pro-competitive purposes related to collaborations for the anti-solicitation agreements and despite the fact that the purported collaborations were central to Defendants' motions for summary judgment, Defendants have failed to produce persuasive evidence that these anti-solicitation agreements related to collaborations or were pro-competitive.

### IV. CONCLUSION

This Court has lived with this case for nearly three years, and during that time, the Court has reviewed a significant number of documents in adjudicating not only the substantive motions, but also the voluminous sealing requests. Having done so, the Court cannot conclude that the instant settlement falls within the range of reasonableness. As this Court stated in its summary judgment order, there is ample evidence of an overarching conspiracy between the seven Defendants, including "\[t\]he similarities in the various agreements, the small number of intertwining high-level executives who entered into and enforced the agreements, Defendants' knowledge about the other agreements, the sharing and benchmarking of confidential compensation information among Defendants and even between firms that did not have bilateral anti-solicitation agreements, along with Defendants' expansion and attempted expansion of the anti-solicitation agreements." ECF No. 771 at 7-8. Moreover, as discussed above and in this Court's class certification order, the evidence of Defendants' rigid wage structures and internal equity concerns, along with statements from Defendants' own executives, are likely to prove compelling in establishing the impact of the anti-solicitation agreements: a Class-wide depression of wages.

In light of this evidence, the Court is troubled by the fact that the instant settlement with Remaining Defendants is proportionally lower than the settlements with the Settled Defendants. This concern is magnified by the fact that the case evolved in Plaintiffs' favor since those settlements. At the time those settlements were reached, Defendants still could have defeated class certification before this Court, Defendants still could have successfully sought appellate review and reversal of any class certification, Defendants still could have prevailed on summary judgment, or Defendants still could have succeeded in their attempt to exclude Plaintiffs' principal expert. In contrast, the instant settlement was reached a mere month before trial was set to commence and after these opportunities for Defendants had evaporated. While the unpredictable nature of trial would have undoubtedly posed challenges for Plaintiffs, the exposure for Defendants was even more substantial, both in terms of the potential of more than $9 billion in damages and in terms of other collateral consequences, including the spotlight that would have been placed on the evidence discussed in this Order and other evidence and testimony that would have been brought to light. The procedural history and proximity to trial should have increased, not decreased, Plaintiffs' leverage from the time the settlements with the Settled Defendants were reached a year ago.

The Court acknowledges that Class counsel have been zealous advocates for the Class and have funded this litigation themselves against extraordinarily well-resourced adversaries. Moreover, there very well may be weaknesses and challenges in Plaintiffs' case that counsel cannot reveal to this Court. Nonetheless, the Court concludes that the Remaining Defendants should, at a minimum, pay their fair share as compared to the Settled Defendants, who resolved their case with Plaintiffs at a stage of the litigation where Defendants had much more leverage over Plaintiffs.

For the foregoing reasons, the Court DENIES Plaintiffs' Motion for Preliminary Approval of the settlements with Remaining Defendants. The Court further sets a Case Management Conference for September 10, 2014 at 2 p.m.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
