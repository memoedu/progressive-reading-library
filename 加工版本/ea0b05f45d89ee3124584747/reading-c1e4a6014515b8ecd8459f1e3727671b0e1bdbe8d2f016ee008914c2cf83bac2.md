# Mini Blog Post 22: The world is full of wasted motion — Neel Nanda

## 正文

_（Meta note：这篇文章在生活建议中，明显偏向“optimisation 真棒”的一端。我认为 optimisation 总体而言很好，但也有常见的 pitfalls，意识到它们并避开它们很重要。为了取得平衡，这篇文章适合与我关于_ [_naive optimisation 的 dangers_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise)_（以及该如何应对），以及_ [_给自己留下 Slack 和 spare capacity 的 importance_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/38-slack)_的文章一起阅读，而不是试图 optimise 生活中的每一刻。更多想法请见后记）_

引言

80/20 rule，或者说 [Pareto Principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle)，是 economics 中的一条“rule”，它说的是，80% 的 value 来自 20% 的 effort。把这样一个 fuzzy、ill-defined statement 称为 rule，很奇怪。而且也很容易想出 counter-examples。但这是关于世界的一项 empirical observation，以我的经验来看，它往往是 true。

而且我认为，这是一个_极其_有价值的 idea——远比乍看之下更有价值。这是我的 worldview，以及我生活方式中_关键_的一部分。我也认为，它是我取得成功的重要 underlying factor。关键的 insight 是：**总有一个 clever hack**。世界并不 fair。Effort 并没有按照应有的方式 distributed。这并不是因为人们愚笨，或者不肯努力。**这就是世界的 default state**。将你的 effort 进行 Allocating，而且做到 efficiently，是_很难的_——如果不是这样，Pareto Principle 就不会 hold。而且，**这也是_你_所面对的世界的 default state**。

我在这里的 underlying model 是，我们**一生中的大部分时间都困在 bad local optima 中**。我们有一套 default actions，有做事和 solving problems 的 standard ways，用来应对遇到的问题。这些比什么都没有_好得多_，但远远算不上 optimal。因此，要从一个 local optima 中_脱身_，你需要培养这样的 skill：能够 **noticing** 自己何时身处其中，能够以 **creative** 的方式找到更好的办法，并通过 **implementing** 它，转移到更好的那个。在实际生活中，这个 insight 在我脑海中形成了一种_确信_：总有一个 clever hack，一个 efficient solution。对于那些感觉 slow、effortful 或 inefficient 的事情，我有一种强烈的审美上的排斥。

而要 _solving_ 这个问题，既不 obvious，也不 easy！你需要足够 creative。有时我找不到更好的办法，做得甚至比 naive solution _更糟_。而且，你永远找不到真正的 optimal solution，因为世界是 complex 的，一切都是 uncertain 的——我的新 toolkit 也只是把我带到其他 local optima。你可能因此感到不堪重负，对任何不够完美的东西都不满意。但我发现，自己很容易对此[感到兴奋](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)——我的内心深处有一部分始终在_奋力向前_。它 Focused 于 progress，而不是 end point。**世界充满了 inefficiency 和 wasted motion，但_你_可以做得更好**。

请注意，我提出的 claim，比这个 Principle 显然 implies 的内容广泛得多。它只是说，还有大量 value 没有被获取，而大多数人都错过了。我则 assert，只要投入足够的 effort 和 skill，你_就能_找到它。这是关于世界的 empirical claim，而不是仅靠 logic 就能得出的结论。我对此深信不疑，因为它对我有效。而我写这篇文章的 goal，是概述它的一些常见 manifestations，这种 mindset 从内在感受起来是什么样，以及它可能出错的方式。

这是我之前许多文章背后的关键 insight——找出世界的 default state 中存在哪些 wasted motion，并 overcome 它们：

-   [进行 Run experiments，并尝试新事物](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/the-value-of-experiments)，认识到 value of information。这能让你摆脱 bad local optima

-   始终保持 results focused：[做到 deliberate](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/be-deliberate)，并始终牢记你的 goal。Build [systems](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)，以 effortlessly 地采取正确的 actions。不再只是[尝试](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)，而要开始行动。并了解 [optimise 究竟意味着什么](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise)

-   进行 Prioritise：[Find 你的 true goals](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/prioritisation)，并[采取最能 lead to 它们的 actions](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/2glo4ww7hd035m56cc5l7zq9r7bxhc)——否则，你就处在世界的 default state 中，生活中充满 wasted motion

-   造成 wasted motion 的一个原因是 _passive_。培养 [Doing Things 的 identity](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things)，overcome [什么都没做的 illusion](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-15-the-illusion-of-doing-nothing)，并学会如何[迈出第一步](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-21-taking-the-first-step)

-   当你对生活中的 problems 感到 helpless 时，要 Notice 到这一点，并提醒自己：[problems 是用来 fixing 的](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)

    -   [社交技能（Social skills）](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations)

    -   [共情（Empathy）](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-14-minds-should-make-sense)

    -   [教学（Teaching）](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-18-how-to-teach-things-well)

    -   [做一个友善的人](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-10-seek-positive-externalities)

    -   [内在动机（Intrinsic motivation）](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)

    -   [真实信念（True beliefs）](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-13-beliefs-are-for-true-things)

-   如果你想[改善这个_世界_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-8-what-altruism-means-to-me)，就要学会 overcome 其中的 wasted motion

学习（Learning）

对我来说，这方面一个非常突出的 case 是 learning，尤其是 maths。关键的 insight 是：**learning 是一个 information compression 的 process**。

Maths 往往被作为一连串 rigorous、formal information 来 taught。你会看到一长串 unmotivated、incomprehensible proofs，并被要求详细 study 和 remember 它们。但归根结底，我_并不_在意 remembering 一个 proof。我在意的是 building _intuitions_。是 Concepts、ideas，以及 motivation。是在脑海中建立一个 big picture framework，让我能从中 _generate_ 一门 course 的重要部分。

**每个 proof 的 80% 都是垃圾**——那是一连串不假思索的 algebra，以及做那些 obvious 的事。当我的 goal 是 _learn_ 时，我想找到那 20% _insightful_ 的部分，也就是那些我自己很难想出来的 steps

在实践中，这表现为一种确信：一切都可以 distilled 成关键的 ideas 和 insights，以及对 implementation details 的不耐烦。有时 implementation details _确实_重要！稍微 learn 一些，并且能够 generate 它们，是件好事。但我发现，大多数时候，这种确信让我能略过所有无关的废话，找到真正重要的东西。

一些建议：

-   每次遇到一个 idea，都问问：我为什么要在意这个 idea？

-   追求 big picture understanding——focus 于 concepts，而不是 details

-   如果某件事让你觉得 hard，就问：“这重要吗？”如果不重要，就继续往下，或者寻求帮助——你会花 80% 的时间追逐那 20% 最 hard 的 details——先确认这值得你花时间。

-   每次看到一个 proof，都把它 compress 成尽可能少的 bullet points。

-   每当你 solve 一个 hard problem，都问自己：“我现在知道了什么，使这个 problem 下次应该感觉 _easy_？”——这些就是这个 problem 要教给你的关键 lessons

-   用一种[迫使你做到 brevity 和 clarity 的 format](https://dynalist.io/d/ToOhEKlz9qC2PmpjgyTpXQJz)来 Write notes，因为你很容易陷入只是把一切照抄下来的陷阱——要避免 wasted motion，你需要 identify 什么才_重要_。Learning 是一个 information compression 的 process。

这里的根本要点是，我的 goal 不是能够 regurgitate proofs，而是达到 _mastery_。而 mastery 的感觉，不是把每个 detail 都 memorised，而是达到这样一种程度：我不_需要_把每个 detail 都 memorised，因为我知道哪些 details 重要，哪些可以以后 looked up，哪些可以放心 forgotten about。正是这种不耐烦的冲动，以及对 efficiency 的追求，让我能_快得多_地达到 mastery。

而且我发现，这些 skills 能很好地 transfer 到其他 fields！并不是对所有领域都如此——比如，我会是个相当糟糕的 doctor——但我发现，所有吸引过我的 fields（这里有很大的 selection bias）都有 underlying concepts 和_重要的_ ideas。在脑海中保有这种不耐烦、对 efficiency 的渴望，以及 big picture view，很有用。记得 _prioritise_，能让我 learn 得好得多。

在关于 [teaching](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-18-how-to-teach-things-well) 的文章中，我更详细地概述了自己对这一切的看法；我也在[这里](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations)概述了自己对通过交谈 learning concepts 的想法。支持这一 belief 的另一点 empirical data 是，我认为它让我成为了好得多的 teacher，而且也收到了这样的反馈。

保持 results focused

wasted motion 的一个关键 source，是没有[做到 deliberate](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/be-deliberate)。这很合理！**世界的 default state 就是 wasted motion**。这意味着，如果你想做到 focused 和 efficient，就得不断与 entropy 对抗。而始终盯住自己的 true goal，是从众多 options 中找到正确 path 的关键第一步。因此，第一个关键 skill 是保持 **results-focused**——做到 grounded，并始终 focused 于你想达成的东西。

而且，你并不总能知道自己的 true goal 是什么！对此 overconfident 也是一种 failure mode！但即使你对自己的 true goals 感到 confused，也应该始终有_某个东西_作为目标——哪怕只是为了 learn 更多，变得不那么 confused。

去掉那些扯淡的东西

如果你注意到，自己与 goal 之间存在 barriers，却看不出这些 barriers 有什么充分的 justifications，那么一个通常值得尝试的办法，就是试着绕过它们，看看会发生什么。这_不会_总是 work，而且如果你要尝试，**务必警惕 downside risk**；但我发现，它 work 的频率高得出奇。

我运用这一做法取得很多成功的一个方向，是 social 方面的事情：

-   如果我想和某个人成为朋友，并就有意义的事情展开有趣的 conversations，我是 _能_ 做到的。你不必困在乏味的 small talk 中——如果你们都想进行更好的 conversation，不妨尝试[询问那些让你由衷感到兴奋的事情](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations)

-   如果你正在和朋友争论——跳过那些鸡毛蒜皮的破事，记住你们是一伙的。清楚地 communicate 你的 preferences，倾听对方的 preferences，并尝试共同寻找 mutually best solution

-   如果我想[成为更友善的人](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-10-seek-positive-externalities)，让身边的人过得更好，我是 _能_ 做到的。如果我想更经常地给予别人有意义的赞美，我是 _能_ 做到的。这感觉有点奇怪，但确实管用。

-   如果我不理解某个人如何 thinks，而这又造成了 tension，我完全可以直接询问，保持好奇，并 _尝试_ 理解对方，直到对方的 [mind 对我而言 makes sense](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-14-minds-should-make-sense)

-   **风险提醒**：这些都是会 violate social norms 的 actions，不同的人对此有不同的 comfort levels。我发现，自己身边往往是一些适合这种做法的朋友——这不一定能 generalise。但就我个人而言，我发现这种 approach 让我快乐了 _很多_，而且对我管用。

这里的 underlying lesson 是：要 break out of 默认的 local optima，你就需要做些不同的事情。一条路径是，留意你的 intuitions 何时在阻碍你，并 test 其中究竟有没有重要的东西。这就是 [problems 是用来 fixing 的](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)背后的 key insight——那些 _感觉_ impossible 的事情是 wins 的一个重要来源，因为它们往往并不是 impossible。如果不走出去尝试，就极难判断哪些 problems 是 _确实_ impossible to solve 的。

这很难 balance，我有[一整篇文章](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-20-emotions-are-bayesian-evidence)讨论这种 mindset 如何导致 failure modes，比如 ignoring “irrational” emotions——我也承认，这条建议有些 contradictory。但我发现，这是一种值得有所保留地 cultivate 的有用 mindset。对我而言，它构成了成为[一个 Actually Does Things 的人](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things)的 core component——能够看穿 helplessness 和 impossibility 的感觉，并愿意尝试。

Go Meta：转向元层面

另一个人们会忽略的重要 efficiency boosts 来源，是 **going meta** 的 skill。这些可以分成几类：

-   做到 meta-cognitive——觉察自己如何 think，以及自己如何生活

    -   [Introspection：内省](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-20-emotions-are-bayesian-evidence)

    -   定期 reflecting 自己的生活

    -   留意 biases，并 [seeking truth](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-13-beliefs-are-for-true-things)

    -   [Finding intrinsic motivation：找到内在动机](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)

-   Self-improvement 与 growth——随时间 compound 的小事

    1.  [Noticing 生活中的 problems，并能够 solve 它们](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)

    2.  [在 getting past 自己的 limitations 时做到 Being creative](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)

    3.  留意 inefficiencies，并[对它们进行 optimising](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise)

    4.  Learning 如何 learn

    5.  [Good systems：良好的系统](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)

    6.  Prioritisation：确定优先级

    7.  [开始着手 hard things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-21-taking-the-first-step)

-   Meta-skills——作为许多不同 processes 基础的 skills

    -   Writing well：写好文章

    -   [Good communication：良好的沟通](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-18-how-to-teach-things-well)

    -   [Good listening：良好的倾听](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations)

    -   Creative problem solving：创造性地解决问题

    -   Thinking in models：运用模型思考

人类有 _很强的_ short-term bias，而 developing go meta 的能力，在当下很少会让人直观地觉得重要。然而，从 long-term 来看，它 _显然_ 很重要——小的 gains 会累积，而 meta abilities 上的小幅 gains 会在 _各个方面_ manifest。因此，这里有很多人们会忽略、却能带来丰厚回报的东西。

从链接的数量，你大概也能看出，我会刻意花 _很多_ 心思考虑这件事。而且，尽管确实有可能做得过头，我发现它给我带来了 _丰厚的_ 回报。

在 getting meta 时，很容易迷失方向，不再 grounded，意识到这一点很重要。但我认为，投入 effort 来 developing _良好的_ meta-skills 是无比宝贵的，应该成为一项重要的 priority——尤其是在年轻时！

会 _compound_ 的 Skills 尤其 valuable——变得更善于 growing、identifying problems、reflection。理解自己的 bottlenecks，并能够 overcome 它们。

Implementation：付诸实施

最后，也是最重要的部分，是 **implementation**。吸收所有这些 ideas，并认同世界充满 inefficiency，这很容易。但随后又把它忘掉，没有真正为此 _take action_。这可以分成两个部分。

首先，当你注意到自己有些卡住了——事情是 inefficient 的，感觉本来可以做得更好——**就为此做点什么**。你的 standard approach 不会奏效，不能只是更加努力。你需要做到 creative。我的主要 tools：

-   设置一个 5 minute 的计时器，仔细考虑 alternatives

    -   最大的 failure modes 之一就是过早放弃——完整的 5 minutes 长得出人意料。计时器是其中一个重要的 component

    -   这并不总是 _足够_，但我发现，它往往能给我一个 idea 的萌芽，让我可以继续思考并充实它

-   仔细考虑我以前针对 similar problems 用过的 creative solutions

-   把我对这个 problem 的 thoughts 进行 Brain-dump——哪里出了问题，为什么它是 inefficient 的？尝试尽可能详细地理解它

    -   向朋友或 rubber duck 进行 Explaining，可能会有所帮助

-   向别人征求建议

而且，如果上述做法奏效了，你 identified 一个有意思的 solution idea，**就对它进行 implement**。仅仅拥有 creative idea 还不够，你需要为此 _做_ 点什么。这一步的感觉远没有那么令人满足，但我竟然在这么多 problems 上都栽在了这一步，实在 _令人惭愧_——其他事情总是显得更重要，solution 从不显得紧迫，它也不是 _肯定_ 会奏效，等等。

这正是练习 [taking the first step 的 skill](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-21-taking-the-first-step)的绝佳时机！实际上，我已经建立了 systems，每周花 2 hours 来 implementing 自己想到的所有 creative ideas，所以我只需要把 ideas 添加到一个 queue，届时再逐一查看。

风险提醒

我认为，这整篇文章都应该附上一条相当醒目的风险提醒——这种 mindset **极其 limited**，而且**并非总是奏效**。我发现，它让我养成了相当差的 work ethic——有些 problems 确实是 hard 的，也没有 clever solution，而我觉得做这些事情很困难。对事情进行 optimising 也有显著的 costs——它会消耗相当多的时间和精力——而且很难妥善地 prioritise。我发现，自己很容易为了给一个 task 寻找 automated solution，花上采用笨拙但无聊的方式做完它所需时间的 3 倍——有一次实习期间，我花了好几个小时 learning 如何使用 regular expressions，在我的 comments 的每个 paragraph 的 end 添加一个 full stop。

而且，[正如我昨天概述的那样](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-20-emotions-are-bayesian-evidence)，这种 mindset 会促使我 dismissing 任何我看不出其背后有 robust causal mechanism 的东西，这是一种极其 unconservative、reckless 的 mindset。有时，一些重要的事情对我而言并不 legible——比如信守承诺、诚实，以及倾听 “irrational” emotions 的重要性。

实际上，conservatism 和 ruthlessly seeking efficiency 之间存在一种 balance。我觉得后一种 mindset 极其 valuable，但对我来说有些过于自然，因此我努力寻找恰当的 balance。不过，_balance_ 才是这里的关键词。过于 reckless 会有 costs，但 [reckless 得不够也有 costs](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-15-the-illusion-of-doing-nothing)——有时，你确实漏掉了一个 clever hack；有时，Chesterton’s Fence _并没有_ 发挥什么重要作用，如果你意识不到这一点，就会错失机会。总体而言，cultivating 这种 mindset 给我的生活带来了压倒性的积极影响。

由于 Law of Equal but Opposite Advice，在这样一个需要 balance 的话题上给出建议尤其困难。这是一个 spectrum，而 optimal point 很难指出。有些人需要更多地 seek efficiency，另一些人则需要更加 conservative、restrained。我的 _猜测_ 是，大多数人 seek efficiency 得还不够，但我也确实知道这方面的 counter-examples！

而且，找到恰当的 balance 很难！你需要始终保持 grounded。对事情进行 Test，尝试新事物，但也要保持 uncertain，并愿意承认自己可能是错的。当别人提出反对你的 arguments 时，要做好忽略它们的准备，但也要 _倾听_，看看它们是否有道理。留意 reckless overconfidence 的感觉，问问自己，它是否真的在让你更接近自己的 goals。[**让自己 Ground 于 pursuit of truth 之中**](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-13-beliefs-are-for-true-things)**。**

Conclusion：结语

总体而言，随着时间推移，这些 beliefs 在我心里逐渐积累成了一种 _conviction_：总有一个 clever hack。这并不总是 true！但世界的 default state 是，我感到 _helpless_。Problems 感觉 impossible，寻找更好方法的念头让人不知所措，我也始终没能 take the first step。正是这种 semi-irrational conviction，让我能够跨过自己的 intuitions，break out of 我的 local optima。

因此，下次当你注意到生活中的某件事感觉 inefficient，而自己内心的一部分在想“肯定有更好的方法”时，**倾听那个部分**。认真对待它，忽略那些犹豫，尝试做到 creative。如果你能全心全意地寻找一个 clever hack，它说不定真的会奏效。

## 原文

_(Meta note: This post falls pretty far on the ‘yay optimisation’ side of life advice. I think optimisation is generally great, but has common pitfalls, and it’s important to be aware of these and avoid them. To balance this, this post goes well with my posts on_ [_the dangers of naive optimisation_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise) _(and what to do about this), and_ [_on the importance of leaving yourself Slack and spare capacity_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/38-slack)_, rather than trying to optimise every last moment of your life. Introduction

The 80/20 rule, or the [Pareto Principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_principle) is a “rule” in economics, saying that 80% of the value comes from 20% of the effort. It is weird to call this a rule, when it’s such a fuzzy, ill-defined statement. And it’s easy to come up with counter-examples. But this is an empirical observation about the world, and in my experience it tends to be true.

And I think this is an _incredibly_ valuable idea - far more so than it seems at first glance. This is a _key_ part of my worldview, and how I live my life. And I consider it an important underlying factor in ways I’ve been successful. The key insight is this: **there is always a clever hack**. The world is not fair. Effort is not distributed as it should be. And this isn’t because people are dumb, or not trying. **This is the default state of the world**. Allocating your effort efficiently is _hard_ - if it wasn’t, then the Pareto Principle would not hold. And **this is the default state of the world for _you_**.

My underlying model here is that we spend **most of our lives stuck in bad local optima**. We have a set of default actions, standard ways of doing things and solving problems we come across. And these are _way_ better than nothing, but nowhere near optimal. So to get _out_ of a local optima, you need to develop the skill of **noticing** when you’re in one, being **creative** to find a better way, and **implementing** that to move to a better one. In practice, this insight gets stored in my mind as a _conviction_ that there is a clever hack, an efficient solution. I have a strong aesthetic preference against things that feel slow, effortful, or inefficient.

And _solving_ this is not obvious, nor easy! You need to be creative. Sometimes I can’t find anything better, and do _even worse_ than the naive solution. And you’ll never find a truly optimal solution because the world is complex and everything is uncertain - my new toolkit just gets me to other local optima. And it’s possible to feel overwhelmed by this, and dissatisfied with anything short of perfect. But this is something I find it easy to [be excited about](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about) - there is a core part of me that is always _striving_. Focused on progress, not on the end point. **The world is full of inefficiency and wasted motion, but _you_ can do better**.

Note that I’m making a far broader claim than the Principle obviously implies. It just says that there is a lot of value left on the table, which most people miss. I assert that you _can_ find it, with sufficient effort and skill. And this is an empirical claim about the world, not something you can logic your way into. It’s something I feel very convinced of, because it works for me. And my goal in this post is going to be to outline some of the common ways this manifests, what the mindset feels like from the inside, and ways this can go wrong.

This is the key insight underlying a lot of my previous posts - find ways that the default state of the world is wasted motion, and overcome them:

-   [Run experiments and try new things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/the-value-of-experiments), appreciate the value of information. This breaks you out of bad local optima

-   Be constantly results focused: [Be deliberate](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/be-deliberate), and always keep your goal in mind. Build [systems](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower) to take the right actions effortlessly. Stop [trying](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button) and start doing. And learn [what it really means to optimise](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise)

-   Prioritise: [Find your true goals](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/prioritisation) and [take the actions that best lead to them](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/2glo4ww7hd035m56cc5l7zq9r7bxhc) - because otherwise you are in the default state of the world, and your life is full of wasted motion

-   One cause of wasted motion is being _passive_. Cultivate the [identity of Doing Things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things), overcome the [illusion of doing nothing](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-15-the-illusion-of-doing-nothing) and learn how to [take the first step](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-21-taking-the-first-step)

-   Notice when you’re being helpless about problems in your life, and remind yourself that [problems are for fixing](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)

    -   [Social skills](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations)

    -   [Empathy](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-14-minds-should-make-sense)

    -   [Teaching](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-18-how-to-teach-things-well)

    -   [Being a nice person](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-10-seek-positive-externalities)

    -   [Intrinsic motivation](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)

    -   [True beliefs](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-13-beliefs-are-for-true-things)

-   If you want to [improve the _world_](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-8-what-altruism-means-to-me), learn to overcome the wasted motion there

Learning

One very salient case of this for me is learning, especially maths. The key insight is that **learning is a process of information compression**.

Maths is often taught as a stream of rigorous, formal information. You see a long stream of unmotivated, incomprehensible proofs, and are expected to study these in detail and remember them. But at the end of the day, I do _not_ care about remembering a proof. I care about building _intuitions_. Concepts, and ideas, and motivation. A big picture framework in my head, from which I can _generate_ the important parts of a course.

**80% of every proof is garbage** - it’s a series of mindless algebra, and doing the obvious thing. When my goal is to _learn_, I want to find that 20% that is _insightful_, the steps that I would struggle to come up with myself

In practice, this manifests as a conviction that everything can be distilled down to key ideas and insights, and an impatience with implementation details. And sometimes the implementation details _are_ important! It’s good to learn them a bit, and be able to generate them. But I find that most of the time, this conviction lets me cut past all the irrelevant crap, and find what actually matters.

Some tips:

-   Every time you encounter an idea, ask why do I care about this idea?

-   Seek the big picture understanding - focus on concepts not details

-   If something feels hard, ask “is this important?” And if not, move on, or ask for help - you’ll spend 80% of your time chasing the 20% of the hardest details - check it’s worth your time first.

-   Every time you see a proof, compress it into as few bullet points as possible.

-   Whenever you solve a hard problem, ask yourself “what do I know now, such that this problem should feel _easy_ next time” - those are the key lessons the problem have to teach you

-   Write notes in a [format that forces you to brevity and clarity](https://dynalist.io/d/ToOhEKlz9qC2PmpjgyTpXQJz), because it’s easy to fall into the trap of just copying down everything - to avoid wasted motion, you need to identify what _matters_. Learning is a process of information compression.

The underlying point here is that my goal isn’t to be able to regurgitate proofs, it’s to achieve _mastery_. And mastery does not feel like having every detail memorised, it feels like getting to the point where I don’t _need_ to have every detail memorised, because I know which details matter, which can be looked up later, and which can be safely forgotten about. And by having this impulse to impatience, and striving towards efficiency, I can get to mastery _far_ faster.

And I’ve found that these skills transfer pretty well to other fields! Not universally - I’d be a pretty awful doctor for example - but I’ve found that all fields that have caught my interest (major selection bias) have underlying concepts and _important_ ideas. That it’s useful to have this sense of impatience, and this desire for efficiency and a big picture view in my mind. That by remembering to _prioritise_ I can learn far better.

I outline my views on all this in more detail in my post on [teaching](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-18-how-to-teach-things-well), and I outline my thoughts on learning concepts from conversation [here](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations). A further bit of empirical data for this belief is that I think it makes me a substantially better teacher, and get feedback to this effect.

Be results focused

One key source of wasted motion is a failure to [be deliberate](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/be-deliberate). And this makes sense! **The default state of the world is wasted motion**. This means that there is a constant fight against entropy, if you want to be focused and efficient. And keeping your eye on your true goal, is the key first step to finding the right path, out of the many options you have. So the first key skill is to be **results-focused** - be grounded, and be ever focused on what you’re aiming for.

And you don’t always get to know what you’re true goal is! And being overconfident about this is also a failure mode! But even if you’re confused about your true goals, you should always have _something_ you’re aiming at - even if it’s just to learn more and become less confused.

Cut out the bullshit

And something often worth trying, if you notice barriers between you and your goal, yet can’t see good justifications behind the barriers, is to see what happens if you try skipping past them. This will _not_ always work, and **beware downside risk** if you try this, but I’ve found it works far more than it has any right to.

One approach where I’ve had a lot of success with this centre on social things:

-   If I want to become friends with somebody, and have interesting conversations about something meaningful, I _can_. You don’t have to be stuck in dull small talk - if you’re both interested in having a better conversation, try [asking about the things you find genuinely exciting](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations)

-   If you’re having an argument with a friend - skip past all of the petty bullshit and remember that you’re on the same team. Clearly communicate your preferences, listen to their’s, and try to work together for the mutually best solution

-   If I want to [be a nicer person](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-10-seek-positive-externalities), and improve the lives of those around me, I _can_. If I want to give people meaningful compliments more often, I _can_. And this feels kinda weird, but works.

-   If I don’t understand how somebody thinks, and this is a source of tension, I can just ask, and be curious, and _try_ to understand them until their [mind makes sense](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-14-minds-should-make-sense)

-   **Health warning**: These are all actions that violate social norms, which different people have different comfort levels with. I’ve found I tend to surround myself with friends where this works well - this won’t necessarily generalise. But I personally have found this approach makes me a _lot_ happier, and works for me.

The underlying lesson here: To break out of the default local optima, you need to do something different. And one path is to notice when your intuitions are holding you back, and to test whether there’s something important there or not. This is the key insight behind [problems are for fixing](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing) - a key source of the wins are the things that _feel_ impossible, because they often aren’t. It’s incredibly hard to tell which problems are _actually_ impossible to solve, without going into the world and trying things.

This is difficult to balance, and I have a [whole post](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-20-emotions-are-bayesian-evidence) on how this mindset can lead to failure modes like ignoring “irrational” emotions - and I concede that this advice is somewhat contradictory. But I’ve found this is a useful mindset to cultivate, with hesitation. To me, this forms a core component of being a [person who Actually Does Things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/become-a-person-who-actually-does-things) - the ability to look past the feeling of helplessness and impossibility, and be willing to try things.

Go Meta

Another important source of efficiency boosts that people miss is the skill of **going meta**. These break down into a few categories:

-   Being meta-cognitive - aware of how you think, and how you live your life

    -   [Introspection](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-20-emotions-are-bayesian-evidence)

    -   Regularly reflecting on your life

    -   Noticing biases, and [seeking truth](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-13-beliefs-are-for-true-things)

    -   [Finding intrinsic motivation](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-11-live-a-life-you-feel-excited-about)

-   Self-improvement and growth - small things that compound over time

    1.  [Noticing problems in your life, and being able to solve them](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-7-problems-are-for-fixing)

    2.  [Being creative about getting past your limitations](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-6-stop-pressing-the-try-harder-button)

    3.  Noticing inefficiencies and [optimising them](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-9-what-it-means-to-optimise)

    4.  Learning how to learn

    5.  [Good systems](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-19-on-systems-living-a-life-of-zero-willpower)

    6.  Prioritisation

    7.  [Getting started on hard things](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-21-taking-the-first-step)

-   Meta-skills - skills that underlie a lot of different processes

    -   Writing well

    -   [Good communication](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-18-how-to-teach-things-well)

    -   [Good listening](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-5-how-to-learn-from-conversations)

    -   Creative problem solving

    -   Thinking in models

Humans have a _major_ short-term bias, and developing the ability to go meta rarely feels viscerally important in the moment. Yet it’s _clearly_ important in the long-term - small gains add up, and small gains to meta abilities manifest _everywhere_. So this is a good source of things people miss, that can pay major dividends.

As you can probably tell from the number of links, this is something I make a point of thinking about a _lot_. And, while it’s possible to go too far, I’ve found that this has paid _major_ dividends for me.

It’s easy to get lost and stop being grounded while getting meta, and it’s important to be aware of this. But I think effort spent developing _good_ meta-skills is invaluable, and should be a major priority - especially when you’re young!

Skills that _compound_ are especially valuable - getting better at growing, identifying problems, reflection. Understanding your bottlenecks, and being able to overcome them.

Implementation

The final, and most important part, is **implementation**. It’s easy to absorb all of these ideas, and agree that the world is full of inefficiency. But then forget about it, and fail to really _take action_ on this. This breaks down into two parts.

First, when you notice you’re a bit stuck - things are inefficient, things feel like they could be better - **do something about it**. Your standard approach will not work, you can’t just try harder. You need to be creative. My main tools:

-   Set a 5 minute timer, and think through alternatives

    -   One of the biggest failure modes is giving up too early - a full 5 minutes is a surprisingly long term. The timer is an important component of this

    -   This isn’t always _enough_, but I find it often gives me the seeds of an idea I can think on more, and flesh out

-   Think through creative solutions to similar problems I’ve used before

-   Brain-dump my thoughts on the problem - what’s going wrong, why is it inefficient? Try to understand it in as much detail as possible

    -   Explaining it to a friend or rubber duck can be helpful

-   Ask somebody for advice

And, if all that worked, and you identified an interesting solution idea, **implement it**. It’s not enough to just have the creative idea, you need to _do_ something about it. This step feels far less rewarding, but it’s _embarrassing_ how many problems I’ve failed at this step for - other things always feel more important, the solution never feels urgent, it’s not _definitely_ going to work, etc.

This is an excellent time to practice the [skill of taking the first step](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-21-taking-the-first-step)! In practice, I have systems in place where I spend 2 hours every week implementing all the creative ideas I had, so I just need to add ideas to a queue that I’ll go over then.

Health Warning

I think this entire article should come with a pretty big health warning on it - this mindset is **extremely limited** and **doesn’t always work**. I find it contributes to me having a pretty poor work ethic - some problems are genuinely hard and don’t have a clever solution, and I find it difficult to do these. And there’s significant costs to optimising things - it consumes meaningful time and energy - and it’s hard to prioritise this well. I find it easy to spend 3 times as long finding an automated solution to a task versus doing it a dumb but boring way - I once spent several hours during an internship learning how to use regular expressions to add a full stop to the end of every paragraph in my comments.

And [as I outlined yesterday](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-20-emotions-are-bayesian-evidence), this mindset motivates dismissing anything where I can’t see a robust causal mechanism behind it, a highly unconservative and reckless mindset. And sometimes there are important things which are not legible to me - like the importance of keeping promises, honesty, and listening to “irrational” emotions.

In practice, there’s a balance between conservatism and ruthlessly seeking efficiency. I find the latter mindset incredibly valuable, but a bit too natural, and try to find the right balance. But _balance_ is the key word here. There are costs to being too reckless, but also [costs to not being reckless enough](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-15-the-illusion-of-doing-nothing) - sometimes there _is_ a clever hack that you’re missing, sometimes Chesterton’s Fence _isn’t_ doing anything important, and if you don’t realise this you’re missing out. And on the whole, cultivating this mindset has been an overwhelmingly positive thing in my life.

Giving advice on a balanced topic like this is especially hard, due to the Law of Equal but Opposite Advice. This is a spectrum, and the optimal point is hard to point to. Some people need to seek efficiency more, others need to be more conservative and restrained. My _guess_ is that most people don’t seek efficiency enough, but I also definitely know counter-examples to this!

And getting the right balance is hard! You need to always be grounded. Test things, and try new things, but be uncertain and open to being wrong. When people make arguments against you, be prepared to ignore them, but also _listen_, and see if there’s anything to them. Notice the feeling of reckless overconfidence, and ask whether it’s truly bringing you closer to your goals. [**Ground yourself in the pursuit of truth**](https://www.neelnanda.io/blog/mini-blog-post-13-beliefs-are-for-true-things)**.**

Conclusion

Overall, these beliefs have built up over time in me as a _conviction_ that there is always a clever hack. And this isn’t always true! But the default state of the world is that I feel _helpless_. Problems feel impossible, the idea of finding a better way feels overwhelming, and I never manage to take the first step. Having this semi-irrational conviction is what gets me to step past my intuitions, and break out of my local optima.

So, next time you notice something in your life that feels inefficient, and part of yourself thinks “surely there’s a better way”, **listen to that part**. Take it seriously, ignore the hesitations, and try to be creative. And if you can wholeheartedly search for a clever hack, it just might work.
