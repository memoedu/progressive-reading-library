# Random selection of development economics ideas

## 正文

受 _Poor Economics_ 启发，下面这些想法似乎可能对 development economics 有帮助。（不知道它们已经被尝试到了什么程度，而且其中大多数都有明显的 caveats。）

-   Credit bureaus。Microcredit institutions 仍然不得不 charge 高得离谱的 interest rates，部分原因是很难 distinguish good loan prospects 与 bad 的那些。Centralized credit information 可能有助于让更 creditworthy 的 borrowers 获得 cheaper capital。

-   结构更合理的 finance。许多 poor 仍然使用 moneylenders，而不是 microcredit，因为 microcredit 有 fixed payments 和 group liability structure，这与 high-variance business income 很不匹配。此外，如果你因为 diminishing returns to capital 而 strongly risk averse，那么 using leverage 通常不是个好主意，而大多数 microbusinesses 的情况正是如此。

    某种为 businesses 获取 equity financing 的方式或许能对此有所帮助。（当然，我看不出有什么容易的办法——enforcement 会是一场噩梦，除非你也许是 M-PESA，而且该 business 的所有 transactions 都通过你的 network 进行。）Revolving credit 也可能有帮助，不过 leverage 的那些问题仍然适用。

-   更 Cheaper、更 efficient 且 transparent 的 auditing。由 unaccountability 导致的 Corruption 似乎是个相当大的问题，而它一直是个问题，部分原因可能是，目前获取足够 granular 的 data 来 expose individual people misbehaving（例如 absenteeism、embezzlement），成本仍然很高。

-   对 moving to a city 的 friction 进行 Reducing。Cities 往往有更好的 schools 和 job opportunities，但 housing conditions 却远不那么 pleasant 和 healthy，而且搬过去的费用很高（如果失去 city job，还会有风险）。这也是一个更好的 financing options 可能有所帮助的领域。

-   更好的 access to information。_Poor Economics_ 中反复出现的一个主题是，人们会错过 productive investment opportunities，仅仅因为他们不知道这些机会。（这就是为什么，尽管大家都在抱怨 net neutrality，我却真的认为 Internet.org 有潜力成为非常棒的东西。）

## 原文

Inspired by _Poor Economics_, here are some thing that seem like they might be helpful for development economics. (No idea to what extent they’ve been attempted, and there are obvious caveats to most of them.)

-   Credit bureaus. Microcredit institutions still have to charge ridiculously high interest rates, partly because it’s hard to distinguish good loan prospects from bad ones. Centralized credit information might be helpful in allowing more creditworthy borrowers to get cheaper capital.

-   Better-structured finance. Many of the poor still use moneylenders instead of microcredit because microcredit has fixed payments and group liability structure, which is a poor match for high-variance business income. Also, using leverage is generally a bad idea if you’re strongly risk averse from having diminishing returns to capital, which is the case for most microbusinesses.

    Some way of getting equity financing for businesses might help with this. (I see no easy way to do this, of course—enforcement would be a nightmare, unless maybe you’re M-PESA and the business does all their transactions through your network.) Revolving credit might also be helpful, though the problems with leverage still apply.

-   Cheaper, more efficient and transparent auditing. Corruption through unaccountability seems to be a pretty big problem, and part of the reason it keeps being a problem may be that it’s currently expensive to get granular enough data to expose individual people misbehaving (e.g. absenteeism, embezzlement).

-   Reducing the friction of moving to a city. Cities tend to have better schools and job opportunities, but vastly less pleasant and healthy housing conditions, and it’s expensive to move there (and risky if one loses one’s city job). This is another area where better financing options might be helpful.

-   Better access to information. A common theme of _Poor Economics_ was that people would miss out on productive investment opportunities simply because they didn’t know about them. (This is why, despite everyone’s complaints about net neutrality, I actually think Internet.org has the potential to be really awesome.)
