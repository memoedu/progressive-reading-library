# Mini Blog Post 3: Become a person who Actually Does Things — Neel Nanda

---

## 正文

世界上有两种人：真正做事的人，以及不做事的人。

好了，那句没必要如此挑衅的开场白说完了，我究竟是什么意思？要 achieve 一个 goal，可以分成两个部分：弄清楚需要做什么，然后真正去做。

这么说听起来完全是老生常谈。但我觉得，这里面有一个非常重要、却并不显而易见的 point。我心里有一部分坚信，achieving 我的 goals 的主要 bottleneck，在于足够 _smart_，想出最好的 plan，获取所有必要的 information。这确实没错！如果你做的事情本身就很烂，那么你再擅长做事也没用。但如果我从不据此采取 _act_，那么我的 plan 再好，_也_没有用。

而这一点_真的_很容易被忽略。我们很容易总想着：“我明天可以做这个”“这不是 priority”，或者“这绝不可能奏效”。难就难在，有时这些想法确实是对的。但我发现，除非我主动付出努力，否则我就是那种_总会_冒出这些想法的人。我永远不会真正为自己的 goals 采取行动，改变自己的生活。

这主要表现为：我总能意识到自己生活中的 problems，而且往往会持续很长时间。我会注意到自己锻炼得不够，不喜欢正在学的课程，非常不喜欢单身的状态，没有足够的时间做那些最让我感到充实的事。但在我脑海里，“这是一个 problem”和“我打算为此_做_些什么”之间却不会产生 connection。并不是我仔细想过这个 problem，conclude 自己什么都做不了，而是我从一开始就根本不觉得这里有什么 question 需要提出！而且我觉得，这个 problem _远远_不只存在于我身上——我在大多数朋友身上都或多或少看到过这种情况，我认为它极其普遍。在“事情不是我想要的样子”和“我其实可以为此_做_些什么”之间，似乎存在一道 mental block。

我觉得这真的很糟糕，而且可能是阻碍你前进的最大 meta-problems 之一。但我写这篇文章，_不是_为了抱怨它有多糟糕，而是因为我认为，这是一个 _solvable_ 的 problem。成为一个会做事的人，也就是一个 **agent**，是一项 _skill_，而且我认为它是一项 trainable skill。这很难，也不会事事奏效，但仍有很大的进步空间。这也是我尝试 developing 过的最有价值的 skills 之一。

主要的 symptom 是：_在当下_，为自己的 desires 采取行动从不让人觉得 urgent。它从不让人觉得 important，总可以往后拖。或者，你从不觉得它是 _possible_ 的，这个 problem 仿佛只是生活中无法改变的事实。因此，solution 必须围绕如何_在当下_ solving 这个 problem 展开。对我奏效的 solution，是把成为一个 agent 纳入自己的 identity。把做事当作一条 principle，不是因为所做的事一定是 perfect action，而是因为，比起总在等待 perfect opportunity 的生活，我选择的是一种自己会_做事_的生活。

留意那些小 problems，并 fix 它们。留意大家什么时候都不喜欢正在做的事，并成为第一个说出来的人。留意水壶什么时候空了，并主动把它装满。留意自己什么时候说“哦，我明天可以做这个”，然后今天就去做。留意自己什么时候想“我应该找个时间做这件事”或者“我一直想学杂耍”，然后_真正去做_。留意什么时候某件事是 inefficient 的，留意那些没人做的事，然后_成为那个去做的人_！

这样做的 point 在于，我通过_改变自己正在回答的 question_，避开了令人动弹不得的 perfectionism 和 uncertainty。即使我做的不是 right thing，也没有_关系_，因为我正在做的事本身并没有那么 important。我可以让那些令人动弹不得的想法停下来，不是顺着它们的思路逐一回答，而是意识到：我今天作出的 choices，会 affect 我在余生中成为怎样的人。而这_远比_我今天生活中的那些鸡毛蒜皮的破事 important。因此，我 train 自己真正做事的 habit。弄清楚什么才是 _right_ 的事，同样是一项 vital skill，但可以放在其次。不要让 perfect 成为 good 的敌人。

即使在那些日复一日的琐事中，我_确实_作出了 wrong 或 imperfect 的 choice，也没什么关系。重要的是，当那个千载难逢的 opportunity 到来时，_我是那种会抓住它的人_。一千次微小的 losses，与那次真正要紧的 big win 相比，根本不算什么。

如果你只从这篇文章里带走一点，那就记住这个：下次当你为一个 choice 苦苦纠结，或者放过一个 opportunity 时，留意这一刻。然后问自己，不是“什么才是 right decision”，而是“哪个 decision 会让我更接近_我想成为的那种人”。_

## 原文

There are two kinds of people in the world: People who actually do things, and people who don’t.

Now I’ve got the unnecessarily provocative opening line out of the way, what do I actually mean by this? To achieve a goal, this breaks down into two parts: figure out what needs to be done, and actually do it.

As stated, this sounds completely trivial. But I think there’s a really important and non-obvious point in here. Some part of me is convinced that the main bottleneck for achieving my goals is being _smart_, coming up with the best plan, getting all of the necessarily information. And this is true! It doesn’t matter how good you are at doing things, if what you’re doing is crap. But it _also_ doesn’t matter how good my plan is if I never _act_ upon it.

And it’s _really_ easy to miss this point. It’s easy to always think “I can do this tomorrow” or “this is not a priority” or “this would never work”. And it’s hard, because sometimes those thoughts are correct. But I find that, unless I put in active effort, I’m the kind of person who’ll _always_ have those thoughts. I’ll never actually act upon my goals, change things about my life.

One of the main symptoms of this is that I’m always aware of problems in my life, and will often be aware of them for a very long time. I’ll notice that I don’t exercise enough, that I’m not enjoying the courses I’m studying, that I really dislike being single, that I don’t have enough time to do what I find most fulfilling. But there won’t be a connection in my mind from “this is a problem” to “what am I going to _do_ about it”. It’s not that I think through the problem, and conclude that I can’t do anything, it’s that it never feels like there’s a question to be asked in the first place! And I think this is a problem that extends _far_ beyond me - I notice some amount of this in most of my friends, and I think it’s incredibly widespread. There seems to be a mental block between “things are not as I want them to be” and “I can actually _do_ something about this”.

I think this really sucks, and can be one of the biggest meta-problems holding you back. But the reason I’m writing this post is _not_ to complain about how much it sucks, it’s because I think this is a _solvable_ problem. Being the kind of person who does things, an **agent**, is a _skill_, and I think it is a trainable skill. And this is hard, and won’t work perfectly, but there’s a lot of room for progress. And this is one of the most valuable skills I’ve ever tried developing.

The main symptom is that, _in the moment_, acting upon your desires never feels urgent. It never feels important, and can be put off. Or it never feels _possible_, the problem just feels like a fact of life. And so a solution must center on solving the problem _in the moment_. And the solution that worked for me, is to make it part of your identity to be an agent. Make it a point of principle to do things, not because the thing is necessarily the perfect action, but because I choose the life where I _do_ things, over the life where I always wait for the perfect opportunity.

Notice the small problems, and fix them. Notice when everyone isn’t enjoying what they’re doing, and be the first person to voice this. Notice when the jug of water is empty, and be the one to fill it. Notice when you say “oh, I can do this tomorrow” and do it today. Notice when you think “I should get round to this some time” or “I’ve always wanted to learn juggling” and _actually do it_. Notice when something is inefficient, notice the thing nobody is doing, and _be the person who does it_!

The point of this, is that I avoid the paralysing perfectionism and uncertainty by _changing the question I am answering_. It doesn’t _matter_ if I’m not doing the right thing, because what I’m doing isn’t that important. I can close off the paralysing thoughts, not by answering them on their own terms, but by realising that the choices I make today affect the kind of person I’ll be for the rest of my life. And that that’s _far_ more important than whatever trivial bullshit is going in my life today. And thus, I train the habit of actually doing things. Figuring out the _right_ thing to do is also a vital skill, but can come second. Don’t let the perfect be the enemy of the good.

And it doesn’t even matter if I _do_ make the wrong or imperfect choice in all of the day-to-day mundanity. What matters is that, when that one golden opportunity comes along, _I am the kind of person who will take it_. A thousand tiny losses are nothing against the big win that really counts.

If there’s one thing you take from this post, let it be this: notice the next time you agonise over a choice, or pass up an opportunity. And ask yourself not “what is the right decision” but rather “which decision will get me closer to _the kind of person I want to be”._
