## 正文

自上一篇链接帖以来，我发现的一些看起来很有意思的 papers 和 articles。

-   [Nuclear fusion 即将到来](https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2019/01/nuclear-fusion-commercialization-race.html)。早在 2014 年，我就[对 fusion 表示乐观](https://nintil.com/2014/09/04/energia-de-fusion-nuclear-como-vamos/)，而那些以 commercial power plants 为目标的 companies 中，有些给出的期限略显乐观。不过，四年后修订的日期看起来倒也没有那么糟：fusion 不再总是还要等 20 年了：

    -   General Fusion 原本希望在 2020 年拥有自己的 reactor（2014 年的说法），如今他们声称将在十年内实现 commercialisation（<2028）
    -   TAE 原本瞄准 2020-2025 年，而他们似乎正按计划推进，表示计划在 2023 年开始 commercialisation。
    -   Commonwealth Fusion Systems 是那家还要等 20 年的，但我写原帖时它还不存在。
    -   LPP 已经取得了一些进展，但他们独特的 approach 似乎还没有接近 commercial grade。
-   [一篇新的 paper](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/01/declining-labor-force-growth-explains-declining-dynamism.html)认为，较低的 labour force growth 就能很好地 explained US economy 的「dynamism」的 decline，而无须诉诸 market power 的说法。

    -   关于同一话题的 [Twitter thread](https://twitter.com/lymanstoneky/status/1082179267855814656)
-   对 Facebook 这样的 large corporations 来说，[你的 data](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/01/trying-sell-data.html)值多少钱？基本上一文不值。正如你可能预料的那样。

    -   这就是为什么我认为 Radical Markets framework 中整个「data as labor」部分是最薄弱的，而且远远弱于其他部分。没错，从 aggregate 来看，data 可能很有 valuable，但这又是 diamonds/water paradox；虽然可以通过建立 user cartels（Lanier 称之为 MIDs）来 exploit corporations，但你为什么要那么做呢？
    -   Eline Chivot 对这项 proposal 的 flaws 写了一篇[很好的 writeup](https://www.datainnovation.org/2019/01/paying-users-for-their-data-would-exacerbate-digital-inequality/)。
-   针对 Stubborn Attachments 的 critiques 并不多，但[这里](http://williamnava.com/reply-tyler-cowens-stubborn-attachments/)有一篇。（并不是说我同意其中的一切，总体而言我非常喜欢 SA！）相关 quote：

    -   > 有一个 problem，我还没见过 political philosophers 直接 addressed，但我怀疑它既困难又重要（先说明一下：我远不是 political philosophy 方面的 expert。完全有可能已经有人 addressed 过，只是我没见过。如果是这样，那这一整节可就闹笑话了）。假设有人问我的 political philosophy，我说：「我是一个 Awesomist。我相信一种 political system，其中每个人都拥有过上精彩生活所需要的一切。」Intuitively，我们并不想反对这个 position；相反，我们想说，它根本算不上一个严肃的 position（如果算的话，我们就都应该成为 Awesomists！）。但如果我们要说 Awesomism 不是一个真正的 position，就需要一个 reason。它没有满足成为严肃 position 的哪些 requirements？

    -   或者换句话说：Stubborn Attachment frameworks 是 heavily underdetermined 的。例如，如果考虑 inequality，应该有多少 inequality？就 economic growth 而言，我对此的回答是：它[并不重要。](https://nintil.com/2017/06/26/on-the-effects-of-inequality-on-economic-growth/) 的确，比如说，一些 studies 发现 redistribution 是 detrimental 的，但如果存在 growth enhancing 或 growth neutral 的做法，我也不会感到意外。

    -   Tyler 经常让读者去他的博客查看具体的 policy proposals。[Noah](https://noahpinionblog.blogspot.com/2019/01/book-review-stubborn-attachments-by.html) Smith 对这本书的理解是，它暗示应该增加对 basic research 和 green tech 的 government investment。

    -   看待 Growth policy 时，必须考虑这一事实：一旦你的 [economy going](https://nintil.com/2018/11/19/on-the-constancy-of-the-rate-of-gdp-growth/)，economic growth 在 long term 似乎就相当 constant。我在关于 [Mazzucato 的 entrepreneurial state](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6yVfnHpyohc)的 presentation 中提到，WWII 之后，US basic science 的 funding 确实出现了 massive increase。这对 growth，或者说对具体 technologies 的 improvement，有什么作用？没有。这不令人惊讶吗？多年前，我查阅过 science funding 与各种 outcomes 方面的 literature，但我想自己并没有就那个具体而狭窄的 issue 写过帖子。我应该写一篇。在此之前，你可以先看[我的 summary](https://katalepsisblog.wordpress.com/2015/09/29/handbook-of-the-economics-of-innovation-some-sort-of-summary/)，内容是 economics of innovation 的 handbook

-   Sabine Hossenfelder 在 NYT 发表了一篇 opinion piece，[argues against CERN](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/01/23/opinion/particle-physics-large-hadron-collider.html) 的 propaganda。反对 funding science，即使只是针对一个非常具体的 case（一台新的 supercollider），也是极不受欢迎的，和主张减少 healthcare 或 education funding 差不多，然而这才是正确的看法：[新的 collider 应该 put on hold](https://twitter.com/skdh/status/1085509499010646016)，至少目前如此。

    -   她因为敢于表达自己的看法而[遭到骚扰](https://twitter.com/skdh/status/1088330458251907073)
-   [关于 optimal taxation 的 discussion](http://www.aei.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/-should-the-top-marginal-income-tax-rate-be-73-percent_085518416524.pdf)：当一些 economists 说 X 是 optimal tax rate 时，这个说法是从哪里来的？

-   DeepMind 在 [Starcraft 2](https://deepmind.com/blog/alphastar-mastering-real-time-strategy-game-starcraft-ii/) 中获胜，让我输掉了一场打赌：我原本赌这不会在 2018 年发生（难过！）。[接下来是什么？](https://nintil.com/2016/04/18/future-challenges-for-ai/)几年前，我在这里提过几个例子：Magic The Gathering、automated engineering，或 entrepreneurship。但到了这个阶段，有一点越来越清楚：如果能获得一套 fixed、easily knowable 的 rules，并拥有大量 computational power，那么相关 setting 最终就会被攻克。这使 games 成为 AI 容易得手的猎物。在 real world situations 中，这就更难了，而且你无法用相当于 200 hundred 年的 experience 来 train 一个 agent。例如，如果你想 produce music，你试图 optimize 的是什么？你可以像 GANs 那样，尝试 copy 和 learn 一个 distribution，但最终的 value function 存在人们的脑海中，存在于人们认为什么是 good 或 bad music 的判断里。当然，你可以从人们那里获得 input，但速度不可能 arbitrarily high，除非也许能在 music 正被 produced 时 reads brainwaves，并相应地 adjusts music。例如，想象一下你想拥有一个 automated CEO。要把你的 system plug 到什么 simulator 上？用什么 rules？这并不 imply 这件事做不到，只是说目前的 approaches 不会奏效。

## 原文

Collection of papers and articles that I’ve spotted since my previous links post that seem interesting.

-   [Nuclear fusion is coming](https://www.nextbigfuture.com/2019/01/nuclear-fusion-commercialization-race.html). Back in 2014, I was [showing optimism about fusion](https://nintil.com/2014/09/04/energia-de-fusion-nuclear-como-vamos/), and the deadlines given by some of those companies aiming to get at commercial power plants were somewhat too optimistic. The revised dates, four years later don't look that awful though: fusion is not always 20 years into the future anymore:

    -   General Fusion wanted to have their reactor in 2020 (2014), now they claim commercialisation within ten years (<2028)
    -   TAE was aiming for 2020-2025, and they seem to be on track, saying they have plans to begin commercialisation in 2023.
    -   Commonwealth Fusion Systems is the one 20 years in the future, but wasn't around when I wrote the original post.
    -   LPP has made some progress, but their unique approach doesn't seem close to commercial grade yet.
-   [New paper](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/01/declining-labor-force-growth-explains-declining-dynamism.html)argues that the decline in "dynamism" of the US economy can be explained quite well by lower labour force growth, without need to resort to a market power story.

    -   [Twitter thread](https://twitter.com/lymanstoneky/status/1082179267855814656) about the same topic
-   How much is [your data](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2019/01/trying-sell-data.html) worth to large corporations like Facebook? Basically nothing. As you might expect.

    -   This is why I think the whole "data as labor" side of the Radical Markets framework is by far the weakest. Sure, in the aggregate data may be very valuable, but this is the diamonds/water paradox again, and while one can exploit corporations by means of having user cartels (what Lanier calls MIDs), why would you want to do that?
    -   Eline Chivot has a [good writeup](https://www.datainnovation.org/2019/01/paying-users-for-their-data-would-exacerbate-digital-inequality/) of the flaws of this proposal.
-   Not many critiques of Stubborn Attachments have been penned, but [here](http://williamnava.com/reply-tyler-cowens-stubborn-attachments/) is one. (Not that I agree with all of it, overall I like SA a lot!) Relevant quote:

    -   > There is a problem that I haven’t seen directly addressed by political philosophers, but which I suspect is a difficult and important one (caveat: I’m far from an expert on political philosophy. It’s entirely possible that it has been addressed and I just haven’t seen it. If so, oops on this whole section). Suppose I am asked for my political philosophy and I say: “I’m an Awesomist. I believe in a political system in which everyone has what they need to live awesome lives”. Intuitively, we don’t want to disagree with this position; rather, we want to say that it isn’t a serious position at all (if it were, we should all be Awesomists!). But if we’re going to say Awesomism isn’t a real position, we need a reason. What requirements for being a serious position does it fail to meet?

    -   Or to put it in other words: The Stubborn Attachment frameworks is heavily underdetermined. For example, if one considers inequality, how much inequality should there be? My answer to this, regarding economic growth: it [doesn't matter.](https://nintil.com/2017/06/26/on-the-effects-of-inequality-on-economic-growth/) True it is that, say, redistribution has been found to be detrimental in some studies, but I wouldn't be surprised that there are ways to do it that are growth enhancing or growth neutral.

    -   Tyler has often referred readers to his blog for specific policy proposals. [Noah](https://noahpinionblog.blogspot.com/2019/01/book-review-stubborn-attachments-by.html) Smith read the book as implying that there should be more government investment in basic research and green tech.

    -   Growth policy has to be seen in the light of the fact that economic growth seems quite constant over the long term, once you have your [economy going](https://nintil.com/2018/11/19/on-the-constancy-of-the-rate-of-gdp-growth/). I mentioned in my presentation on [Mazzucato's entrepreneurial state](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6yVfnHpyohc) that after WWII, there was indeed a massive increase in funding of US basic science. What did that do to growth, or for that matter improvement in specific technologies? Nothing. Isn't this surprising? I looked at the literature on science funding and various outcomes years ago but I think I didn't produce a post about that specific narrow issue. I should. In the meantime, you can have [my summary](https://katalepsisblog.wordpress.com/2015/09/29/handbook-of-the-economics-of-innovation-some-sort-of-summary/) of the handbook of the economics of innovation

-   Sabine Hossenfelder gets an opinion piece at the NYT, [argues against CERN](https://www.nytimes.com/2019/01/23/opinion/particle-physics-large-hadron-collider.html)propaganda. Arguing against funding science, even if it's a very specific case (a new supercollider) is highly unpopular, on par with arguing for less healthcare or less education funding, yet this is the correct view:[a new collider should be put on hold](https://twitter.com/skdh/status/1085509499010646016)for now.

    -   She [was harassed](https://twitter.com/skdh/status/1088330458251907073)for daring express her views
-   [A discussion of optimal taxation](http://www.aei.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/11/-should-the-top-marginal-income-tax-rate-be-73-percent_085518416524.pdf): When some economists talk about X being the optimal tax rate, where does that come from?

-   DeepMind beats[Starcraft 2](https://deepmind.com/blog/alphastar-mastering-real-time-strategy-game-starcraft-ii/), making me lost a bet that it would not happen in 2018 (Sad!). [What's next?](https://nintil.com/2016/04/18/future-challenges-for-ai/) Here I suggested years ago a few examples: Magic The Gathering, automated engineering, or entrepreneurship. But at this stage, one thing is increasingly clear: If one has access to a fixed, easily knowable, set of rules, and has a lot of computational power then the setting in question will eventually fall. This makes games easy prey for AI. In real world situations, this is harder and you can't train an agent with 200 hundred years worth of experience. If you want to, for example, produce music, what are you trying to optimize? You can try to copy and learn a distribution, like GANs do, but the ultimate value function is in the heads of people, and in what people think is good or bad music. And sure you can get input from people, but not at arbitrarily high speeds, except perhaps if one reads brainwaves as the music is being produced and adjusts the music accordingly. For example, imagine one wants to have an automated CEO. What's the simulator to plug your system to? What rules? This does not imply it can't be done, it's just to say that the current approaches wouldn't work.

And there we go into “But what do we mean by communism?”, “What do we mean by work?”. Then we get into “Which countries count as communist?”, “Which measures should we used to think about ‘working’?”, “Which time period?”. Etc. Sketching their boundaries and working with concepts is a true art.\[...\] So what I do is try to get different interpretations of the meanings and see if we can say something ‘For most plausible interpretations’ or if we have to resort to ‘In this sense, this happened, in that other sense, these other things happened’.
