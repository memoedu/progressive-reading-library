# Random faith（随机的信仰）

---

## 正文

这些说法有多少是真的？（**指 revision 之前的那些！**）

我的 prior belief 是，religion 对 variables of interest 会有 nonexistent 或 weak effects。Religion 常常与 social class 和/或 ethnicity intertwined，因此必须查看 data 的不同 breakdowns，并在可能的情况下跨时间和国家进行 compare。

（编辑：本文同时考察 individual level 和 aggregate levels。在 individual level，更容易 discern causality，而 aggregate level 有助于我们考虑 emergent effects）

## Random faith（随机的信仰）

要看某件事 causes 什么，一个好办法是采用 randomised control trial。然而，要强迫人们 at random 地 believe in 某一种 religion，似乎很困难。另一种办法或许是，请人们遵循某个 adherent 或那个 religion 会遵循的 behaviours（涉及 prayer、common rituals、dietary requirements 等方面）。

另一个选择是考察出生时便 separated、并在除 religion 外所有 relevant demographic variables 都 similar 的 families 中 raised 的 twins。

还必须指出，第一种 approach 或许有助于确切看清，religion 所 cause 的 bundle of behaviours 中，哪个 component 与哪个 outcome linked，除非 sheer belief 才是 causal force。

这也暗示了一种 conceptual difficulty：设想有人发现，Mormonism leads to 比如说 lower lifetime poverty，而 causal mechanism 是 high savings rate，它能 builds up 一笔 reserve of cash，以备遭遇逆境。于是，有人可能会 argue，真正起作用的是 savings，而不是 religion。但接着，也可以 argue，有些 individuals 太 irrational，无法自行 save；对他们而言，一种 irrationality 或许可以用另一种来对抗：如果没有 religion，他们就会 save 得更少。

## 这些我以前就说过了

在[我对 The Elephant in the Brain 的 review](https://nintil.com/2018/01/16/this-review-is-not-about-reviewing-the-elephant-in-the-brain/) 中，我指出，religion 的 effects 似乎被夸大了，或者并不 generalisable：

总体而言，大多数 literature 在性质上是 correlational，尝试 causal analysis 的很少。这次 review 中出现了一些有趣的 heterogeneous patterns，而 SH 的 core conclusion 总体上是 correct：Religion 对 individuals 并非 harmful，而且在某些情况下，它确实似乎有 positive effect，不过要考虑下文讨论中的 caveats。

对于 **physical** \*\*health related outcomes，\*\*大多数 papers 确实 find，在不同国家的一系列 measures 上，religious people 都更 healthier，但 causality 总体上一直难以 ascertain。一篇最近试图处理这些问题的 paper，最终却在 European countries 加上 US 的 sample 中 finding 了 negative relationship（[Berggren and Ljunge, 2017](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2989449)）。因此，conclusion 可能是，healthier people 更 religious，但如果你找来一个人，把 religion “apply”到他身上，得到的却是 worse health。我承认，这是一项 non-peer reviewed work，但它是迄今唯一已发表、试图超越 correlations 的 work。由于它尝试考察 causality，必须给予它一些 weight，但其余 literature 也依然存在，因此我还不会仅凭那篇 paper 就断言 religion 是 harmful。

对于 **mental health 和 happiness**，Wink et al. 的 paper 关注 elderly。起初没有发现 relation，但进一步查看后，一个特别的 relationship 出现了：在 poor health 的人中，religion reduced depression，但对于 good physical condition 的人，则出现了 opposite effect。

最近的一篇 paper（[Diener and Tay, 2011](http://www.davidmyers.org/davidmyers/assets/the%20religion%20paradox.pdf)）试图回答这个问题：如果 religion 这么好，为什么人们正在离开它？无论在 individuals 之间还是国家之间，随着 education、wealth 和 intelligence 的 scales 越来越高，都会发现 religion 越来越少。但他们为什么要离开它？SH 说过，individuals 通常知道什么对自己好（我也同意）。会不会在这里，individuals 知道自己正在将 earnings、health 和 happiness 与 truth 进行 trading off？

这篇 paper 发现——正如人们可能预料的那样——生活在 diffcult conditions 中会使人更 religious。在 controlling for life circumstances 之后，无论在 US 还是更广泛的世界范围内，都发现了 religion 与 subjective wellbeing (SWB) 之间的 Positive correlations。但在 less religious countries 中，则没有 relation，这一 pattern 与其他 studies similar。（[Snoep, 2007,](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs10902-007-9045-6) [Stavrova, Fetchnhauer, and Schlösser, 2013](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0049089X12001536)）。因此，**如果你周围的人也 religious，而你能更好地融入他们，或许 religion 就会使你 well adjusted；但如果他们不 religious，就不会。**

对于 **earnings**，有趣的是，Steen 的 paper 按 religion 对 results 进行 decomposes。Catholic men 的 earn 比 Protestant men 更多，而 Jews 的 earn 还要更多。对于 religion 的 salubrious effects，通常的 explanation 似乎是，它通过 belief 带来更多 subjective happiness，更重要的是，通过拥有 stronger supportive community 发挥作用。但这里我们发现，不同 religions 之间存在 differences，而且重要的是，non-religious men 的 earn 与 Protestants 一样多。或许 catholics 有一种 Protestants 所没有的 super-strong work ethic（？）

在另一个国家 Canada（[Dilmaghani, 2011](https://dx.doi.org/10.1108/03068291211188875)），研究发现 religion 总体上有 negative effect，但 Jews 再次拥有 higher wages。（这[说得通](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jewish_intelligence)）。不过，在一篇更近的 paper 中，按 province 对 results 进行 decomposing 后，发现了 differential effects：religion 在 religious areas 中 predicted higher wages，但在 less religious areas 中则 predicted lower wages。（[Dilmaghani, 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12122-016-9239-y)）。这与 religion-to-fit-in 的 idea 一致。

类似地，在一个 German sample 中（[Cornelissen and Jirjahn, 2012](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/88668/1/2012-03.pdf)）——不过请注意，这是一篇 working paper——研究发现 religion 对 wages 有 negative effects，但奇怪的是，由 religious parents raised、随后成为 atheist，却 raised earnings。

回到 US 的 Steen paper，Steen 本人在 SH 提到的那项 study 之后若干年，又做了一项 study（[Steen, 2004](https://dx.doi.org/10.1108/03068290410529399)）。研究再次发现，Catholics 的 earning 更多。对于一种 generic religion-is-good-for-earnings theory，这似乎不是它会 predict 的情况。（背后会不会存在难以 discern 的 ethnic correlations？）

在更 macro level，除了 national scale 上 income 与 religion 之间[众所周知的 inverse correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_and_religion#/media/File:Religion_economy.png)，US 的 county level studies 也显示，religion 有 negative 或 neutral effect（[Rupasingha and Chilton, 2009](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268109001498)）

最后，最近一项在 national level（Western countries 中）考察 causal effects 的 study（[Herzer and Strulik, 201](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00036846.2016.1251562?journalCode=raec20)6）发现，income reduces religiosity，同时 lower religiosity increases income。

**总体而言**，religion 看来确实有一些 positive effects，它们通过 increase community participation 而 materialise。然而，关于 changes of faith 会 causally inducing 这些 effects 的 evidence 是 nonexistant，因此，我不会建议任何人为追求更好的生活而尝试 acquire belief（Hanson 在[这里](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2012/05/what-use-far-truth.html)似乎就是这样建议的）。

最后补充一点，如果考虑 non-linear relationships，上述某些 findings 可能会改变：有人 argue，如果 compare highly religious 和 highly atheistic 的人（这里的 highly 指他们有 high conviction），这两个 groups 的 mental health 和 wellbeing 都比 weakly religious 的人更好（正如 [Galen, 2015](http://researcherslinks.com/current-issues/Atheism-Wellbeing-and-the-Wager-Why-Not-Believing-in-God-With-Others-is-Good-for-You/9/16/120/html)，以及 Galen and Kloet [2011](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13674676.2010.510829) 所指出的）。

### 但是……

以上是几个月前作为一篇更广泛文章的一部分完成的 literature 的 general review，因此它并没有达到可能达到的 thorough 程度。另外，那些 papers 针对的是一般意义上的 religion，而不是 mormons 这一 particular case。

\[编辑 2019-01-02：这个 RCT 中描述的 effects 在被 measured 两年后会 fade out，见[这里](https://twitter.com/albrgr/status/1212495616590467072)\]

除了上面的 papers，我还必须提到自我写下那些内容以来发表的一些 papers：[Bryan, Choi and Karlan](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24278) (2018) 发现，向 ultra-poor Filipinos teaching Protestant values 会 raises 他们的 income（幅度为 9%！）。这是一个 pre-registered RCT，具有各个 levels 的 randomization，以及 controls。他们 measure 了许多 variables，并发现 received 这个 intervention 的人比没有 received 的人更 religious，而且他们的 incomes increased。他们还在 6 months 后的 followup survey 中使用了另一家 surveying organization，以避免 biased responses。他们甚至 studied 了 households 内的 variables，以了解 income 的 increase 是否 concentrated 在得到 treatment 的 family member 身上，结果确实如此。唯一令人困惑的地方可能是，其他 variables（assets 或 consumption）并没有 increase。作者在 paper 中讨论了这些以及其他问题，我觉得这些 results 很有说服力。仅仅这篇 paper 就改变了我认为 religion 不可能有 substantial positive effects 的 belief：现在我认为它更有可能做到这一点。不过，由于我对 pre-school interventions literature 的了解，还得等待一两年，看看这些 effects 是 permanent，还是会 wash out；而且，正如作者所承认的，这可能无法 transferable 到其他 cultures 和 settings。

他们 posit 的 causal channel 是 grit 的 increase（即 Duckworth's grit 中的含义）。Grit 作为一种 construct 最近受到质疑（见这篇 [meta-analysis)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/27845531) 但那只是因为它 picking up 的是已经 established 且 similar 的东西：conscientiousness，因此这并不 invalidate 他们的 findings。

这篇 paper 还考察了 alcohol、cigarettes 的 consumption 的 changes，以及与 partner 围绕 alcohol consumption 发生的 arguments。对于接受 Protestantism treatment 的 group，在这些方面都没有发现 significant effects。

在 developed countries 方面，还有我本应纳入的 [Gruber](https://www.degruyter.com/view/j/bejeap.2005.5.issue-1/bejeap.2005.5.1.1454/bejeap.2005.5.1.1454.xml) (2005) 和 [Gruber and Hungerman (2008)](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/123/2/831/1930888?redirectedFrom=PDF)。Gruber 发现，在 US，religious density 的 10% increase 会 leads to household income 的 0.91% increase。他称这个 effect 是 substantial，但在我看来它似乎没有那么大，至少与 Bryan et al paper 中的 effect 相比是如此。第二篇 paper 发现，repealing 那些 ban Sundays 的某些 activities 的 laws（"blue laws"），会 lead to cocaine 和 marihuana consumption 的 increase。（例如，在他们的 sample 中，11% 的 sample 在过去一个月曾 consumed marihuana。在他们的 model 中，repealing 这些 laws 的 effect 是额外增加 3.2%）。还要注意，这里的 models 虽然得到了 economically and statistically significant results，但对任何 given case 的 predictive power 都很低（R2~0.1）。

最近的另一篇 paper，[Kuran (2018)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20171243)，analyses 了 Islam 对 economic performance 的 effects，发现它总体上是 negative。一方面，这与 religion 在 aggregate level 与 less developed countries correlated 的 finding 一致，也许 religion 对你是好的，但对我们是坏的。又或许，这是 Islam 所特有的东西。

如果上面的一些 papers 让人觉得 religion 对 very poor 有效、对 very rich 却无效，那么也有一篇涉及这个问题的 paper（[Bettendorf and Dijkgraaf, 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268110000235)），它认为，实际上，poor countries 中的人会因为更多 religion 而看到自己的 incomes reduced。（再次强调，这是一项 correlational study）

[Juck, Bentley and Lawson (2018)](http://advances.sciencemag.org/content/advances/4/7/eaar8680.full.pdf) 在一篇此前在 Nintil 这里链接过的 paper 中发现，secularization preceded economic growth（而且 tolerance for individual rights 是一个更好的 predictor）。religion 是否对你是好的、对我们却是坏的，是某种 prisoner dilemma？

[Park](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3167635) (2018) 使用一种 exploits law changes 的 semi-experimental design，发现了 US 的 religion-health association 是 non-causal 的 evidence（具体来说，是 "blue laws" 的 repeal，这些 laws restrict 周末的 secular activities，从而 decreasing religious activity

最 comprehensive 的一篇，尽管只针对 economic development 这一特定 topic，大概是 [Basedau, Gobien and Prediger (2018)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/joes.12250)，一篇真正的 literature survey。眼下正值大家讨论 Stubborn Attachments，而 economic growth 又是 Nintil 经久不衰的 topic，因此我必须提到它。首先必须提及的一点是，它似乎认为 religion 并没有消失，因为 world population 中并不 religious 的 % 正在略微 shrinking。但这具有误导性：原因是 highly religious populations 的 expanding 速度快于 non-religious populations。不过，在各 countries 内部，religion 如果说有什么变化，那也是在下降。

除此之外，这篇 paper 在将 religion breaking down 为 components 方面做得相当好，它分别考虑 ideas、practice 或 identity，并考察 -specific- causal mechanisms，而不是停留于单纯的 correlation。

这篇 paper 的 findings 是

-   关于 religion->ethics/thrift/workethic/->economic growth，没有 clear evidence
-   没有 evidence 表明 Religion-> work ethic -> economic growth
-   没有 evidence 表明 Protestantism -> economic growth
-   Religion->resource consumption in religious activity-> less growth（即 Religion 经由 religious activity 中的 resource consumption 导致更少的 growth）
-   Religion->anti-innovation attitudes（即 Religion 导致 anti-innovation attitudes）
-   Religion-> prevention of harmful behaviour (drugs, alcoholism)（即 Religion 带来对 harmful behaviour 的 prevention，涉及 drugs、alcoholism）
-   Religion->discipline-> more years of education（即 Religion 经由 discipline 带来更多年的 education）
-   关于 religion->prosociality，evidence 是 Inconclusive 的

最后，我们可以更深入地看看 subjective well-being (SWB)。虽然 self-report 是一种 imperfect measurement technique，而且 subjective variables 本身就很难 measure，但这一个 variable 应该能够将 religion 对人的 effects 汇集到一起。

一篇早期的 meta-analysis（[Witter et al., 1985](http://www.jstor.org/stable/3511048)）发现 religion 对 SWB 有 small (d=0.16) effect。我没能找到后来关于同一 topic 的 meta-analysis。

迄今为止的 results，加上我阅读的其他 papers，尤其是那个 RCT，已经使我的看法朝上述方向转变：我 underestimated 了 religion 的 causal effects。不过，这可能还不够：由于 models 的 explanatory power 很低（以 R2 measured），"更 religious" 并不必然会 lead to 任何一个 given person 的 quality of life 的确定 improvement，因此这是在承担 expected loss（如果一个人并不觉得 religious activity 本身 intrinsically rewarding，那么 engaging in 它就有 cost），以换取 better outcomes 的微小机会。在 high level，也就是对 economic growth 重要的 level，religion 仍未被证明是它的 driver。因此，除了“当心 alcohol 和 drugs”这类一般建议之外，建议任何一个人尝试变得更 religious，并不是一个好建议。

## Mormons（摩门教徒）

接下来，我将 analyse Mormonism。原因在于，它是一种具有 prescriptive health code 的 religion（不摄入 tea、coffee、alcohol、tobacco 或 drugs），而且其 members 的 adherence 程度很高，不像比如 Catholics。举个轶事性的例子，在 Spain，几乎人人都会 self-identify 为 catholic，但在日常生活中，其中许多人都与 self-identified atheists indistinguishable。

可以通过两种方式 define 谁是或不是 Mormon：通过 self-identification，以及通过 behaviour 的 observation。假设没有多少践行其生活方式却不相信其 belief 的 mormon weeaboos，那么后者是前者的 subset，几乎是一个 analytical truth。因此，comparing 这两个 groups，可以让我们了解 sheer belief 的 effects，以及 religion 的 community aspects 的 effects。

Mormons 生活在世界各地，尽管人们大多将其与它的发源地 Utah 联系在一起，但多数 mormons [生活在 US 以外（根据 LDS numbers）](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons)。在 US 内，其中三分之一生活在 Utah。不过，按 population 的 percentage 来看，[Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_membership_statistics_\(United_States\))（在一定程度上，American Samoa 和 Idaho 也是如此）是 mormons 最多的 state：[55% 的 population](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/religious-tradition/mormon/) 属于 LDS church。在 US 以外，[Tonga 的 self-declared LDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_in_Tonga) membership 为 18% (2011)，而 [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Samoa) 略低于 Tonga。

不过，还必须将 effective、practicing mormonism 与 passive（仅有 belief）的 mormonisn 区分开来：LDS-Recognised mormons 中，[超过一半](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_membership_statistics) 可能并不 religiously active。

## Mormons 与 Americans

可以先 compare US 内 mormons 的生活与其他 Americans，以及同住 Utah 的 Utahns 的生活。

正如人们可能预料的那样，mormons [具有](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2016/09/30/6-facts-about-u-s-mormons/) 关于 marriage、religion 和 family life 的 traditional beliefs（不过，他们也无法免于 [moral progress](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2015/12/18/most-u-s-christian-groups-grow-more-accepting-of-homosexuality/) 不断向前的影响）。从 Demographically 来看，mormons 更 younger，而且大多（85%）是 white。与这些 beliefs 一致，[mormons](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2015/05/22/mormons-more-likely-to-marry-have-more-children-than-other-u-s-religious-groups/) 更常 marry，have more kids，也 tend to marry 其他 mormons。

就 income 而言，mormons 在 wealthy 人群中似乎并未 overrepresented；真要说有什么差异，那就是他们在 poor 人群中略微 underrepresented。如果说有哪些 religions 表现突出，那应当是 Judaism 和 Hinduism。与其他 groups 相比，他们[也并非特别 educated](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/educational-distribution/by/religious-tradition/)。有意思的是，如果看 [degree of belief](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/belief-in-god/by/religious-tradition/)，这两种成功的 religions 更可能拥有对其 God's existence 并不那么确信的 members，甚至还有许多 members 直言自己并不 believe in God，却仍 identify 为该 religion 的一员。Mormons 与 Jehovah's Witnesses 同为 [most engaged group](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/attendance-at-religious-services/by/religious-tradition/)：多达 77% 的人声称每周至少 attend religious services 一次。但与 JW's 相反，[mormons 是最支持](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/views-about-size-of-government/by/religious-tradition/) reducing the size of government 的 group，其中许多人相信，给予 poor 人群的 g[overnment aid](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/views-about-government-aid-to-the-poor/by/religious-tradition/) 会 harms 他们。

到目前为止，这似乎并不是一个极其突出的 group：正如 Razib Khan 早在 2010 年所说，[Mormons 是普通的 white 民众。](http://www.unz.com/gnxp/mormons-are-average/)

他承认，他们的“floor”（处于 poverty 的 %，或者 education 不超过 high school 的 %）可能是人们将他们视为 exemplar group 的原因；而我为上一段查阅的 Pew data 也确实仍然呈现这种情况。不过，考虑到 Mormon group 的 racial composition 中 white 更多，而 whites 的 poverty rate 更低，就必须对此进行 correct。

为什么会这样？除了 religious values，LDS Church 还有[自己的 welfare system](https://www.economist.com/united-states/2002/02/14/bishops-move)，它依靠信任运转：一位 local community leader（bishop）负责 managing congregation 的 **"fast offerings"**，其金额可能相当可观；而且 bishop 拥有充分权限，可以按自己认为合适的任何方式发放这笔钱，不附带条件，也不涉及 bureaucracy。至少在 Utah，Church 拥有规模庞大的 [charity operation](https://providentliving.lds.org/bishops-storehouse?lang=eng)。

我找到了一些专门研究 mormons 的 studies：

关于 delinquency，我首先找到一篇有意思的 paper（[Kelly et al, 2015](http://www.baylorisr.org/wp-content/uploads/Religion-Delinquency-and-Drug-Use1.pdf)），不过他们只在一个段落中简略提到了 mormons：

> Stark, Kent, and Doyle (1982) 再次讨论了这个问题，并 suggested，这些 contradictory findings 很可能源于被 studied 的 community 的 moral（即 religious）makeup。作者们 predicted，religion 会在 moral communities 中 deter delinquency，但对于居住在 secularized communities 的 individuals，religiosity 几乎没有或完全没有 effect。“moral communities” hypothesis 为理解以下情况提供了重要的 theoretical framework：为什么某些 studies 发现 religion reduced delinquency，而其他 studies 则发现 religion 对 delinquency 没有 significant impact (Stark, 1996; Stark, Kent, & Doyle, 1982)。例如，在 Utah 和 Idaho 的 Mormon Wards 等 religious communities 中开展的 delinquency studies，得出了 religious commitment measures 与 delinquency 之间的 inverse relationship (Albrecht et al., 1977)。相反，像 Richmond, California 这样报告较低 church membership rates 的 communities，则未能得出这种 inverse relationship (Hirschi & Stark, 1969)。

但这个段落很有意思：它又增加了一个需要关注的 variable：也许 religion 在较小的 communities 中 reduces crime（在那里，你偷窃的对象极有可能是你认识的人），但在较大的 communities 中却不会。

[Jarvis (1977)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19485565.1977.9988300) studied Canada 的 mormon mortality rates，将它们与当地 non-LDS population 作 comparing，并发现 mortality rates 全面更 lower。[King(1990)](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00986405) 那篇 compared Utahn mormons 与 non-mormons 的 study 也发现了同样的情况。差不多同一时期的一篇 review [Jarvis & Northcott (1987)](https://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0277-9536\(87\)90039-6) 提到，当时已开展的每项 study 都显示 mormons 具有这种 mortality advantage，其中甚至包括一项针对 Maoris 的 study（！）

[一项 study（Bacon,](https://dx.doi.org/10.1001/jama.1957.62980020009013) 1957）顺带提到，mormon 中的 drinkers 的确很少，但其中的 drinkers 比 non-mormons drink 得多得多——这一 finding 几十年后得到 replicated（[Michalak et al., 2007](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0376871606002997)）；在 California，也发现 Mormons 具有更 lower mortality rates（[Enstrom & Breslow, 2008](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/17920112)）。

人们可能认为，这种 mortality rates 的 advantage 仅仅源于 tobacco abstinence，或许还有 alcohol abstinence。所引用的一些 works 确实提到，Mormons 的 mortality rates 甚至比仅根据 non-smoking 所能预期的还要 lower；或者，他们在那些似乎并不与 smoking associated 的 sites 发生的 cancer（例如 colon cancer），其 cancer mortality 也更 lower。不过，较近的 work 确实指出，smoking 也是涉及这些 cancer types 的 causal factor（[Parkin, 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/bjc2011475)）。

说了这么多，所有这些对今后还有多大意义？Tobacco smoking rates 已经大幅下降。但仅仅 religious participation 也可能仍有 health benefits。前面那篇 Park paper 声称，这种 relation——它确实存在——并不是 causal 的，而 Gruber 则说存在 causal relation；不过，他 studied 的是 income 和 drug use，而不是 health。

就 health 而言，literature 毫无疑问地明确指出了 Mormon advantage，其中 abstinence from tobacco，以及——我推测——alcohol，是 causal factors，此外或许还有 religious participation 本身的作用。我不会在这里更深入地探讨 religious participation，因为这个 topic 应当考虑所有 religious participation 来 studied，而不只是 mormons。

考察 higher level data 或许也有价值，因为某些 trends 在 individual level 可能很难看出来。

因此，到这里，我 ran 了一些 models（OLS 和 GBM），尝试寻找 mormonism 与 poverty、drug 及 ~alcohol related mortality~ mortality due to alcohol poisoning 之间的 county-level associations（controlling for income、race，以及其他 religions 的 adherents 所占的 share），另外还考察了 suicides。稍后会详细介绍。

对此有一些 evidence：Utah 的 alcohol **poisoning deaths,** 在 US 排名[第七](https://ibis.health.utah.gov/indicator/complete_profile/AlcConBinDri.html)，尽管其 binge drinking rate 比 US 的 average 低 6 percentage points。其 suicide rate 在 US 为[第五高](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/population-health/us-states-ranked-by-suicide-rate.html)（在 2008 年）。其他 Mormon-heavy states，如 Idaho 或 Wyoming，在后一个 metric 上也排名靠前。Wyoming (2nd) 和 Utah (5th) 的 [adolescent suicide](https://www.statista.com/statistics/666791/states-with-highest-number-of-adolescent-suicidal-deaths-in-us/) 排名也很高。Utah 的 opioid addiction 也有 [high rate](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2016/may/26/utah-mormons-prescription-painkiller-addiction)。为什么会这样？当然，可能在这些 states 中，mormons 的 rates of death 更 lower，而 non-mormons 更 higher，因此，生活在 mormon area 的 non-mormon，处境可能真的很糟！我们不知道。这些 statistics 是 aggregated 的。也可能正如 Guardian 文章指出的那样，原因在于 LDS Church 对其 members 的要求：

> “LDS church 是其中一个很大的因素。我每周都会去 church，也看得到困难在哪里。他们让人觉得自己应当完美，而人们会感到自卑，仿佛自己无法达到别人期望他们达到的标准。因此，他们开始 using prescription painkillers，不是为了 address pain、physical pain，而是为了应对伴随自卑感而来的 mental issues。你就是无法应付别人期望你成为的样子，以及期望你去做的所有事情。”

这些 statistics 与 individual-level data 对照时，似乎显得 paradoxical。当然，我并不是第一个注意到这一点的人，

> Utah 是 America 最 happiest 的 state。
> 
> [他们有最低的 divorce rate、最高的 job satisfaction、最低的 median work hours、最高的 rate of volunteerism](http://www.alternet.org/personal-health/5-happiest-states-america-and-5-saddest)。这里非常 healthy——[低 smoking rates、clean drinking water](http://www.gallup.com/poll/156449/Utah-Poised-Best-State-Live.aspx)，还有[美不胜收的 national parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah)，吸引 population 到户外度过时光。就连 Utah 在网上的形象也洋溢着欢快乐观的气息。一项[最近的 Twitter study](http://www.cnn.com/2013/02/19/tech/social-media/twitter-happiness/) shows，Utahns 经常使用“彩虹”“美妙”和“圣诞节”这样的词，足以让你觉得这个 state 住满了乐天派。
> 
> 但这只是情况的一半。不知为何，虽然 Utah 按许多 measures 被 ranked 为最 happiest 的 state，它也被 ranked 为最 saddest 的 state。
> 
> Utah 的 [rates of depression 比其他任何 state 都更高](http://usatoday30.usatoday.com/news/health/2007-11-28-depression-suicide-numbers_N.htm)。相应地，它的 antidepressant drug use 在 US ranks #1，rate 是 [national average 的两倍](http://articles.latimes.com/2002/feb/20/news/mn-28924)。事实上，Utah 的 [rates of mental illness as a whole](https://web.archive.org/save/https://www.ksl.com/article/28934395) 最高，近 1 in 4 residents 在过去一年中 experiencing 过 mental disorder。他们在 ~[bankruptcy filings 中是 #1](http://archive.sltrib.com/story.php?ref=/business/ci_3390415)，~ ~[pornography consumption 中是 #1](http://www.deseretnews.com/article/705288350/Utah-No-1-in-online-porn-subscriptions-report-says.html?pg=all)，~ 而最令人痛心的是，~~[child sexual abuse 中是 #1](http://fox13now.com/2016/02/17/rate-of-sex-abuse-in-utah-among-worst-in-the-nation-advocacy-group-pledges-support-for-survivors/)，尽~~管存在广泛的 under-reporting。Under-reporting 在 suicide cases 中也是一个问题，而 suicide 是 Utah 11-17 岁 young men 最 common 的 cause of death。在 50 个 states 中，Utah 的 [suicide rate 是 #4 highest](http://www.sltrib.com/news/2935514-155/as-utah-suicide-rates-climb-experts)。（[Utah 的 Paradox](http://www.funnellsinutah.com/news/2016/8/1/the-utah-paradox)）

编辑 \[01-12-19\] 在他们 cite 的 paper 中，Utah ranks first，然而 LDS members 数量 second highest 的 state——Idaho——却 ranks at the bottom。另外，按某个受欢迎的 pornography [site](https://www.pornhub.com/insights/utahs-porn-viewers) 的 measured 结果，情况也并非如此。如今 Utah 的 brankrupcy filings 也是第 [number](https://www.valuepenguin.com/bankruptcy-filings-state) 4，而不是 1。至于 child sex abuse，[这](https://www.acf.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/cb/cm2017.pdf)也不是真的。

我必须指出，这种情况让我想起 Nordic countries 的情形。Mormons 难道是 cryptonordics 吗？但原因不一定是 Mormonism。有人 proposed，那些 areas 更高的 suicide rates 是由于它们更高的 altitude（[Ha & Tu, 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/15/4/671)）。Utah 及其 surrounding areas（过去由 LDS-run 的 State of Deseret）恰好处于 high elevation。（[这并不是](https://scholar.google.co.uk/scholar?hl=en&as_sdt=0%2C5&q=altitude+and+suicide&btnG=)一篇孤立的 paper）。因此，需要把 altitude 加为 control，因为 state level 的 fixed-effects（我的 models 最初版本所用的）可能把 state-level mormonism 的 good effects（对哪些 laws 得以 passed 的 control）与 state-level altitude 的 negative effects 混在了一起。

回到 models：我尝试了不同方法，包括 OLS、按 population weighted 的 OLS，以及 boosted trees（使用 LightGBM），既尝试直接 predict 相关 variable，也尝试先 regressing the variable on income（一个非常 strong predictor），再在其余 analysis 中处理 residual。对于 poverty，我用了两个 poverty metrics，一个来自 American Community Survey，另一个来自 Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates progam。两者的 Results 相似。在最终的 models 中，controlled for 的 variables 是 income、population、\[non-hispanic whites, blacks, asians, hispanics, native americans\] 的 percentage、state 中 mormons 的 %、county 的 altitude、state-level fixed effects，以及 % mormon。这些都处于 county level，因此无法据此对 individuals 作出任何判断..

你可以在 [OpenNintil repo](https://github.com/jlricon/open_nintil/tree/master/mormons) 中找到这些 models 的细节。如有疑问，应当更信任 GBM，因为它 more accurately predicts underlying values。例如，下方 plots 展示的就是 alcohol-**poisoning**\-related mortality 的 prediction 情形。

![alcohol\_gbm\_ols.png](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2019/01/05-1.png)

在这个 case（alcohol-**poisoning**\-related mortality）中，两个 models 的 prediction 大致相同，我为 variable of interest calculate 了一个 elasticity。对于 OLS，这是一个 single value，3.76e-4。这意味着，如果把一个 mormon population（其中每个人都是 mormon）完全 converted 为 atheism（我们把这称为 'the counterfactual'），那么预期 attributable to alcohol **poisoning** 的 mortality 会 decrease 0.375 points。average rate 是每 100k population 2.97，因此从整体来看，这是一个 negligible amount，即便它是一个 7% increase。实际上，OLS model 中的 coefficient 甚至并非 statistically significant。（我把下方 plot trimmed 为只包含 mormons >10% 的 populations）。

对于由 drug abuse 导致的 mortality，models 给出了不同答案：OLS 会 predict，counterfactual 中有 4.42 points 的 decrease（这一次 coefficient 是 significant），而 GBM predicts 1.62 points 的 increase。average rate 是每 1000 中 9.07：

Drug use 也呈现出 diverging estimates；OLS 会 predict drug use decrease 4.4 points，而 GBM 则会 predict 平均 increase 1.6 points（baseline average 是 9.07，因此这些 effects 是 significant）

Suicide 是另一个 outcome of interest：在这个 case 中，两种情况下的 effects 都是 negative：在 counterfactual 中，相对于每 100k 中 16.21 的 mean value，存在 1.37 (OLS) / 7.8 (GBM) points 的 decrease。

如有疑问，我选择 GBM model，而对于上述 outcomes，我们看到 mormonism 对 drug abuse 是 weakly good，对 suicides 是 bad，对 alcohol-**poisoning**\-related deaths 则并非 significant。这并不是说，例如，mormonism 不会通过 alcohol 产生其他 effects；一个 heavily drunk 的人可能还活着，却无法 productive。

## Conclusion（结论）

在这篇文章中，我提供了一些关于一般意义上的 religion 及其对一系列 outcomes 的 effects 的 evidence。对我来说，最有意思的部分是 RCT 和关于 economic growth 的 literature survey。这个 RCT shows，至少在那个特定 case 中，religious belief（与诸如 health norms 之类的东西 separated）能够带来 positive outcome（这里是对 income 而言）。\[编辑 2019-02-01：不过，如上所述，这些 effects 在两年后 fade out\]

至于这篇博文的主要话题 mormonism，首先要注意的是获取 quality data 的困难。最 clear 的 evidence 来自一些较早的 mortality studies：当时，无论在 US 境内还是境外，mormons 都明显比同一地区的其他人 healthier。如今，情况就没那么 clear 了。tobacco 的 negative effects 比 alcohol 的更明显。alcohol consumption 究竟会产生什么作用，足以另写一整篇文章，因为我记得，关于它的 effects 存在 conflicting evidence：虽然大家一致认为 elevated consumption 会带来 negative consequences，但对于 low doses，我见过的 evidence 分别指向 tiny negative、neutral 和 slightly positive effects。 根据 Pew study，mormons 往往比 non-mormons 稍微 less poor，不过这还没有 controlling for confounders。

在 county level 上考察 data 时，一个不同的 pattern 变得突出起来：首先，根据从网上搜集的 anecdotic evidence，以及随后对一组 outcomes 与 mormonism 和其他 variables 之间的 relations 进行 modeling，似乎在 aggregate 上，mormonism 的 effects 是 mixed：不知出于什么原因，它与 alcohol **poisoning** 所致 mortality 的 increase 存在 correlated 关系。其 mechanism 可能是，在存在 strong social norms 反对 alcohol 或某种 drug 的地方，safe consumption 的 culture 无法 develop，最终形成一种 skewed distribution：大多数人不 consuming，少数人却在 binging。看看其他 countries，[尚不清楚](https://ourworldindata.org/alcohol-consumption) [alcohol-positive culture](https://web.archive.org/web/20190301041537/http://www.pewglobal.org/2014/04/15/global-morality/table/alcohol-use/) 与更多 alcohol-related disorders 所致 deaths 之间是否存在 association。

我不会建议把 conversion 到 strict religion 作为一种 anti-poverty measure；至少在现阶段，我甚至不会建议 complete alcohol abstinence（而且这话是我这个很少喝酒的人说的\*！），除非某人已经有，或预计会有，alcohol 方面的 problems。我们需要更多 research，而这里最好的 evidence 将来自更多 RCTs 或 twin studies；甚至有可能，已有的 twin studies 已经 measured religion，因此无需再进行新的 sample gathering，就可能 isolate 它的 effect。

理想情况下，现在会有人拿着我的 dataset 和 notebooks，尝试一些不同的做法，希望还能用更多 data 加以 enhancing。如果你发现不同的 results，请务必告诉我！

## Caveats 和 limitations（注意事项与局限）

-   dataset 的 size 应当意味着有足够的 statistical power 来 detect “big” effect，但更 subtler 的 effects 可能没有被 captured。
-   我可能未能 control for 相关 variables 的其他 major covariates
-   这个 analysis 仅限于 US。
-   如 OpenNintil 笔记中所指出的，已知这个 "mormon" variable 存在 biased upwards 的问题。但如果这种 bias 是 constant，它应该不会对 analysis 产生太大影响。
-   可能存在 community level 的 effects，而它们在 county level 并不存在；我的 analysis 无法 capture 这些 effects。
-   同样，population 中的 mormon 与 non-mormon subsets 之间可能存在我无法 capture 的奇特 interaction effects
-   在这个 analysis 的基础上再进一步，可以进行 Structural Equation Modeling（例如使用 lavaan）
-   更好的 feature engineering 可能会带来对 OLS coefficients 更好的 estimates。我用 GBM 来支持这些 results，因为它们不像 OLS 那样需要那么多 data formatting。
-   这主要是对 developed countries 和现代的 exploration。因此，它并不适用于 tribes 或 smaller scale societies。

## 原文

How much truth is there to these statements? (**The ones before the revision!**)

My prior belief is that religion will have nonexistent or weak effects on the variables of interest. Religion is often intertwined with social class, and/or ethnicity, and so one has to look at diverse breakdowns of the data and compare if possible across time and countries.

(Edit: This post looks at both individual level and aggregate levels. At the individual level it is easier to discern causality, while the aggregate level helps us consider emergent effects)

## Random faith

A good way to see what something causes is with a randomised control trial. However, it seems difficult to force people to believe in one or other religion at random. Instead, it may be possible to ask people to follow the behaviours that an adherent or that religion would follow (in matters of prayer, common rituals, dietary requirements, and so on).

Another alternative is to look at twins separated at birth, and raised in families similar in all relevant demographic variables but religion.

It is also important to remark that the first approach may be useful to see exactly what component of the bundle of behaviours that religion cause is the one linked with which outcome, unless it is sheer belief that is the causal force.

This also hints at a conceptual difficulty: Imagine that it is found that Mormonism leads to, say, lower lifetime poverty, the causal mechanism being a high savings rate that builds up a reserve of cash for when adversity hits. Then, one could argue that it is the savings, not the religion that is doing the job here. But then, it could be argued, that there are individuals that are too irrational to save on their own, and that for them, one irrationality might be fought with another: absent religion they would save less.

## Been there, said that

In [my review of The Elephant in the Brain](https://nintil.com/2018/01/16/this-review-is-not-about-reviewing-the-elephant-in-the-brain/) I noted that the effects of religion seem overblown, or not generalisable:

Overall, most of the literature is correlational in nature, and attempts at causal analysis were few. Some interesting heterogeneous patterns emerged from the review, and SH’s core conclusion is correct in general: Religion is not harmful to individuals and in some cases it does seem to have a positive effect, with the caveats that follow the discussion below.

For **physical** \*\*health related outcomes, \*\*most papers indeed find that religious people are healthier across a range of measures in different countries, but causality has been in general difficult to ascertain. A recent paper that tries to address these concerns ended up finding a negative relationship instead for a sample of European countries plus the US ([Berggren and Ljunge, 2017](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2989449)). So the conclusion may be that healthier people are more religious, but that if you take a person and “apply” religion to it, you get worse health. I acknowledge this is a non-peer reviewed work, but it is the only published so far that tries to go beyond correlations. Some weight has to be given to this one for trying to look at causality, but the rest of the literature is still there, so I wouldn’t claim religion is harmful just because of that paper yet.

For **mental health and happiness**, the Wink et al. paper focuses on the elderly. Initially no relation was found, but on a deeper look, a peculiar relationship emerged: Among those in poor health, religion reduced depression, but the opposite effect obtained for those in good physical condition.

A recent paper ([Diener and Tay, 2011](http://www.davidmyers.org/davidmyers/assets/the%20religion%20paradox.pdf)) tried to answer the question that, if religion is so good, why are people leaving it? As one climbs higher in the scales of education, wealth, and intelligence, across individuals and countries, one finds less religion. But why are they leaving it? SH have said that individuals generally know what is good for them (and I agree). Could it be here that individuals know that they are trading off earnings, health, and happiness against the truth?

The paper found that – as one might expect – living in diffcult conditions makes one more religious. Positive correlations were found between religion and subjective wellbeing (SWB) after controlling for life circumstances, in the US and in the world more broadly. But in less religious countries, there was no relation, a pattern similar to other studies.  ([Snoep, 2007,](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs10902-007-9045-6) [Stavrova, Fetchnhauer, and Schlösser, 2013](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0049089X12001536)). So **maybe religion makes you well adjusted if you fit in better with those who surround you if they are also religious, but not if they do not.**

For **earnings**, Steen’s paper interestingly decomposes the results by religion. Catholic men earn more than Protestant men, and Jews earn even more. The usual explanation for the salubrious effects of religion seems to operate through either more subjective happiness due to belief, and more importantly, through having a stronger supportive community. But here we find differences withing religions, and importantly, that non-religious men earn as much as Protestants. Maybe catholics have a super-strong work ethic that Protestants do not have (?)

In a different country, Canada ([Dilmaghani, 2011](https://dx.doi.org/10.1108/03068291211188875)), religion in general is found to have a negative effect, but again Jews have higher wages. (This makes [sense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jewish_intelligence)). But in a more recent paper, decomposing results by province, differential effects were found: religion predicted higher wages in religious areas, but lower wages in less religious areas. ([Dilmaghani, 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12122-016-9239-y)). This goes with the religion-to-fit-in idea.

Similarly, in a German sample ([Cornelissen and Jirjahn, 2012](https://www.econstor.eu/bitstream/10419/88668/1/2012-03.pdf)) – but note that this is a working paper – religion was found to have negative effects on wages, but oddly being raised by religious parents and then becoming and atheist raised earnings.

In the US, coming back to the Steen paper, Steen  himself did another study some years after the one mentioned by SH ([Steen, 2004](https://dx.doi.org/10.1108/03068290410529399)). Catholics were again the ones found to be earning more. For a generic religion-is-good-for-earnings theory, this doesn’t seem to be what it would predict. (May there be hard to discern ethnic correlations behind?)

At a more macro level, besides the [notorious inverse correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_and_religion#/media/File:Religion_economy.png) between income and religion at a national scale, studies at a county level in the US show negative or neutral effect of religion ([Rupasingha and Chilton, 2009](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268109001498))

And finally, a recent study ([Herzer and Strulik, 201](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00036846.2016.1251562?journalCode=raec20)6) looking at a national level (in Western countries) considering causal effects find that both income reduces religiosity, and that lower religiosity increases income.

In **general**, it appears that religion does have some positive effects that materialise themselves through increase community participation. However, evidence on changes of faith causally inducing those is nonexistant, thus I wouldn’t recommend anyone to try to acquire belief (As Hanson seems to recommend [here](http://www.overcomingbias.com/2012/05/what-use-far-truth.html)) in pursuance of a better life.

As a final note, some of the findings above may change if one considers non-linear relationships: It has been argued that if one compares highly religious and highly atheistic (Where highly here means that they have high conviction), both of those groups have better mental health and wellbeing compared to those who are weakly religious (As noted by [Galen, 2015](http://researcherslinks.com/current-issues/Atheism-Wellbeing-and-the-Wager-Why-Not-Believing-in-God-With-Others-is-Good-for-You/9/16/120/html), and Galen and Kloet  [2011](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/13674676.2010.510829) ).

### But...

The above was a general review of the literature, done a few months ago as part of a wider article, so it is not as thorough as it could possibly be. Also, those papers were for religion in general, not the particular case of mormons.

\[EDIT 2019-01-02: The effects described in this RCT fade out two years after they are measured, see [here](https://twitter.com/albrgr/status/1212495616590467072)\]

Besides the papers above, I have to mention some that were released since I wrote that: [Bryan, Choi and Karlan](https://www.nber.org/papers/w24278) (2018) found that teaching Protestant values to ultra-poor Filipinos raises their income (by 9%!). It's a pre-registered RCT with randomization at various levels, and controls. They measure many variables, and find that those who received the intervention were more religious and had their incomes increased, in contrast with those that did not. They also used a different surveying organization for the followup survey 6 months later to avoid biased responses. And they even studied the variables within households to see if the increase in income was concentrated in the family member that got the treatment, and it was. The only puzzle may be that other variables (assets, or consumption) did not increase. The authors address these and other points in the paper and the results seems convincing to me. This paper alone has shifted my belief that religion cannot have substantial positive effects: Now I think it is more likely that it can. Nonetheless, because of my experience with the pre-school interventions literature, one'd have to wait one or two years to see if the effects are permanent, or if they wash out, and, as the authors acknowledge, this may not be transferable to other cultures and settings.

The causal channel they posit is the increase of grit (As in Duckworth's grit). Grit has come under attack recently as a construct (See this [meta-analysis)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/27845531) but that's just because it's picking up something already established and similar: conscientiousness, so this does not invalidate their findings.

The paper also looked at changes in consumption of alcohol, cigarettes, and arguments with a partner about alcohol consumption. No significant effects were found for those for the group treated with Protestantism.

In developed countries, there's [Gruber](https://www.degruyter.com/view/j/bejeap.2005.5.issue-1/bejeap.2005.5.1.1454/bejeap.2005.5.1.1454.xml) (2005) and [Gruber and Hungerman (2008)](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/123/2/831/1930888?redirectedFrom=PDF), which I should have included. Gruber found, in the US that a 10% increase in religious density leads to a 0.91% increase in household income. He calls that effect substantial, but it doesn't seem that big to me, at least not compared to the one from the Bryan et al paper. The second paper finds that repealing laws that ban certain activities on Sundays ("blue laws") lead to an increase in cocaine and marihuana consumption. (In their sample, for example, 11% of the sample had consumed marihuana in the past month. The effect of repealing the laws in their model is an additional 3.2%). Note also that the models here, while obtaining economically and statistically significant results, have low predictive power for any given case (R2~0.1).

Another recent paper, [Kuran (2018)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/jel.20171243) analyses the effects of Islam on economic performance, finding it generally negative. On the one hand, this goes with the finding that religion correlated with less developed countries at an aggregate level, perhaps religion is good for you but bad for we. Or perhaps it's something specific to Islam.

If it seemed like religion works for the very poor but not for the very rich, from some papers above, there is also a paper for that ([Bettendorf and Dijkgraaf, 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167268110000235)), arguing that actually those in poor countries would see their incomes reduced from more religion. (Again, this is a correlational study)

[Juck, Bentley and Lawson (2018)](http://advances.sciencemag.org/content/advances/4/7/eaar8680.full.pdf) in a paper previously linked here at Nintil, found that secularization preceded economic growth (and that tolerance for individual rights was an even better predictor). Is religion good for you but bad for us, a prisoner dilemma of sorts?

[Park](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3167635) (2018) finds evidence that the religion-health association in the US is non-causal, using a semi-experimental design that exploits law changes (especifically, the repeal of "blue laws", that restrict secular activities on weekends, thus decreasing religious activity

The most comprehensive one, albeit it's only for the specific topic of economic development is probably [Basedau, Gobien and Prediger (2018)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/joes.12250), a proper literature survey. It's Stubborn Attachments season, and economic growth is an evergreen Nintil topic, so I had to mention it. One thing I have to mention from the start is it seems to suggest that religion is not disappearing, because the % of world population that is not religious is shrinking somewhat. But this is misleading: This is because highly religious populations are expanding faster than non-religious populations. But within countries, religion if anything is going down.

Other that that, the paper is quite good in breaking down religion into components, considering separately ideas, practice, or identity, and looking at -specific- causal mechanisms instead of stopping at mere correlation.

What the paper finds is that

-   No clear evidence about religion->ethics/thrift/workethic/->economic growth
-   No evidence that Religion-> work ethic -> economic growth
-   No evidence that Protestantism -> economic growth
-   Religion->resource consumption in religious activity-> less growth
-   Religion->anti-innovation attitudes
-   Religion-> prevention of harmful behaviour (drugs, alcoholism)
-   Religion->discipline-> more years of education
-   Inconclusive evidence about religion->prosociality

Finally, we can have a deeper look at subjective well-being (SWB). While self-report is an imperfect measurement technique and subjective variables are intrinsically hard to measure, this one variable should gather in one place the effects of religion on people.

An early meta-analysis ([Witter et al., 1985](http://www.jstor.org/stable/3511048)) found a small (d=0.16) effect of religion on SWB. I couldn't find later meta-analysis on the same topic.

The results so far, with the extra papers I've looked, especially the RCT, have moved my opinion in the direction I mentioned above: I underestimated the causal effects of religion. However, that may not be enough: Because of the low explanatory power (as measured by R2) of models, "be more religious" will not necessarily lead to a sure improvement in the quality of life of any one given person, thus imposing an expected loss (the cost of engaging in religious activity if one does not find it intrinsically rewarding) to get a tiny chance of better outcomes. At a high level, the level that matters for economic growth, religion still has not proven to be a driver of it. Thus recommending any one person to try to become more religious is not a good recommendation, beyond generic recommendations of "be careful with alcohol and drugs".

## Mormons

In what follows, I will analyse Mormonism. The reason for this is that it is a religion with a prescriptive health code (no tea, coffee, alcohol, tobacco, or drugs), and one that has a high degree of adherence within its members, unlike, say, Catholics. Anecdotically, for example, almost everyone in Spain will self-identify as a catholic, but on a day to day basis many would be indistinguishable from self-identified atheists.

There are two ways one could define who is or who is not a Mormon: By self-identification, and by the observation of behaviour. It is almost an analytical truth that the latter is a subset of the former, assuming that there are not many mormon weeaboos who walk the walk but don't believe the belief. Thus, comparing both groups could give us an idea of the effects of sheer belief and the effects of the community aspects of religion.

Mormons live all over the world, and even though it is mostly associated to its birthplace, Utah, most mormons [live outside of the US (according to LDS numbers)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons). Within the US, a third are in Utah. By percentage of population, however, [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_membership_statistics_\(United_States\)) (And to some extent, American Samoa and Idaho) is the state with the most mormons:[55% of the population](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/religious-tradition/mormon/) belongs to the LDS church. Outside of the US, in [Tonga, self-declared LDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_in_Tonga) membership is 18% (2011), and [Samoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Samoa) slighly lower than Tonga.

However, one also has to consider effective, practicing mormonism, from passive (Just belief) mormonisn:[More than half](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints_membership_statistics) of LDS-Recognised mormons may not be religiously active.

## Mormons and Americans

An initial starting point is to compare the lives of mormons within the US with the rest of Americans, and fellow Utahns.

As one might expect, mormons [have](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2016/09/30/6-facts-about-u-s-mormons/) traditional beliefs regarding marriage, religion, and family life (Though they are not immune to the relentless march of [moral progress](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2015/12/18/most-u-s-christian-groups-grow-more-accepting-of-homosexuality/)). Demographically, mormons are younger, and mostly (85%) white. In accordance with those beliefs, [mormons](https://www.pewresearch.org/fact-tank/2015/05/22/mormons-more-likely-to-marry-have-more-children-than-other-u-s-religious-groups/) marry more, have more kids, and tend to marry other mormons.

Regarding income, it does not seem that mormons are overrepresented among the wealthy, if anything they are somewhat underrepresented amongst the poor. If there are religions that excel those would be Judaism and Hinduism. They are [not also particularly educated](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/educational-distribution/by/religious-tradition/), compared with other groups. Interestingly, if one looks at [degree of belief](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/belief-in-god/by/religious-tradition/), the two successful religions are more likely to have members that are not that convinced about their God's existence,and even have many members who straight say that they don't believe in God, but still identify as part of that religion. Together with Jehovah's Witnesses, Mormons are the [most engaged group](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/attendance-at-religious-services/by/religious-tradition/): As many as 77% of them claim to attend religious services at least once a week. But contrary to JW's, [mormons are the group most in favor](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/views-about-size-of-government/by/religious-tradition/) of reducing the size of government, with many believing that g[overnment aid](https://www.pewforum.org/religious-landscape-study/compare/views-about-government-aid-to-the-poor/by/religious-tradition/) for the poor harms them.

So far, it does not look like an extremely remarkable group: As Razib Khan put it back in  2010, [Mormons are average white folk.](http://www.unz.com/gnxp/mormons-are-average/)

He admits that their "floor" (% in poverty, or % with no more than a high school education) may be what leads to people's perception of them as an exemplar group, and it is true that this continues to be the case in the Pew data I've looked at for the previous paragraph. However, given the more white racial composition of the Mormon group, and that the poverty rate among whites is lower, one would have to correct for this.

Why that could be? Besides the religious values, the LDS Church has [its own welfare system](https://www.economist.com/united-states/2002/02/14/bishops-move) that runs on trust: a local community leader (bishop) is in charge of managing the congregation's **"fast offerings"**, which can amount to a large sum, and has full authority to give away that money in any way the bishop sees fit, no strings attached and no bureaucracy involved. At least in Utah, the Church owns a massive [charity operation](https://providentliving.lds.org/bishops-storehouse?lang=eng).

There are a few studies I found that look at mormons in particular:

For delinquency I find an interesting first paper ([Kelly et al, 2015](http://www.baylorisr.org/wp-content/uploads/Religion-Delinquency-and-Drug-Use1.pdf)), though they barely mentions the mormons in a paragraph:

> Stark, Kent, and Doyle (1982) returned to the issue and suggested that these contradictory findings were likely the result of the moral (i.e., religious) makeup of the community being studied. The authors predicted religion would deter delinquency in moral communities, but there would be little or no effect of religiosity on individuals residing in secularized communities. The ‘‘moral communities’’ hypothesis provided an important theoretical framework for understanding why religion reduced delinquency in some studies, whereas other studies found religion had no significant impact on delinquency (Stark, 1996; Stark, Kent, & Doyle, 1982). For example, studies of delinquency in religious communities such as Mormon Wards in Utah and Idaho yielded an inverse relationship between religious commitment measures and delinquency (Albrecht et al., 1977). Conversely, communities reporting lower church membership rates such as Richmond, California, failed to generate the inverse relationship (Hirschi & Stark, 1969).

But it's an interesting paragraph: it adds one more variable that one has to pay attention to: Perhaps religion reduces crime in smaller communities (Where you would be stealing from someone you most likely know) but doesn't on larger ones.

[Jarvis (1977)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/19485565.1977.9988300)studied mormon mortality rates in Canada, comparing them to the local non-LDS population, and found mortality rates lower across the board. This was also the case in [King(1990)](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00986405) the study by that compared Utahn mormons vs non-mormons. A review around the same time [Jarvis & Northcott (1987)](https://dx.doi.org/10.1016/0277-9536\(87\)90039-6) mentions that every study done so far shows this mortality advantage for mormons, including a study done on Maoris (!)

[A study(Bacon,](https://dx.doi.org/10.1001/jama.1957.62980020009013) 1957) mentions in passing that indeed there are few drinkers among the mormon, but those who are drinkers, drink much more compared to non-mormons- a finding replicated decades later ([Michalak et al., 2007](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0376871606002997)) and in California too Mormons were found to have lower mortality rates ([Enstrom & Breslow, 2008](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pubmed/17920112)).

One might think that this advantage in mortality rates is just from tobacco abstinence, or perhaps alcohol abstinence. Some of the works cited do mention that Mormons have even lower mortality rates than one might expect just from non-smoking, or that they have lower cancer mortality from cancer in sites that don't seem to be associated with smoking (e.g. colon cancer). However more recent work does point to smoking also being a causal factor involved in those types of cancer as well ([Parkin, 2010](https://www.nature.com/articles/bjc2011475)).

All that said, how relevant is all this going forward? Tobacco smoking rates have plummeted. But there may be still health benefits from just religious participation. The Park paper above claimed that this relation -because there is one- is not a causal one, while Gruber said that there is, but he studied income and drug use, not health.

So far as  health goes, the literature clearly points out to a Mormon advantage, with no doubt, with abstinence from tobacco -and I assume- alcohol as causal factors, plus maybe religious participation as such. I won't look deeper into religious participation here, as this is a topic that should be studied considering all religious participation, not just mormons.

It may also be worth looking at higher level data, because some trends may be hard to see at the individual level.

So at this point, I ran some models (OLS and GBM) to try to find county-level associations between mormonism and poverty and drug and ~alcohol related mortality~ mortality due to alcohol poisoning (controlling for income, race, and share of adherents of other religions), plus suicides. More on that later.

There's some evidence of this: Utah ranks [seventh](https://ibis.health.utah.gov/indicator/complete_profile/AlcConBinDri.html) in the US in deaths from alcohol **poisoning deaths,** even though the binge drinking rate is 6 percentage points lower than the average of the US. The suicide rate was the [fifth highest](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/population-health/us-states-ranked-by-suicide-rate.html) (in 2008) in the US. Other Mormon-heavy states like Idaho or Wyoming also rank high in the latter metric. Wyoming (2nd) and Utah (5th) are also high in [adolescent suicide](https://www.statista.com/statistics/666791/states-with-highest-number-of-adolescent-suicidal-deaths-in-us/). Utah also has a [high rate](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2016/may/26/utah-mormons-prescription-painkiller-addiction) of opioid addiction. Why could this be? It can be, of course, that the rates of death are lower for mormons in these states, and higher for the non-mormons, thus being non-mormon in a mormon area might turn out to be really bad for you! We don't know. These statistics are aggregated. It can be that, as pointed out in the Guardian article, the LDS Church impositions on their members:

> “The LDS church is a big part of it. I go to church every week and I see where the challenge is. They make people feel that they should be perfect and they feel inferior, like they can’t live up to the standards of what they expect them to live up to. So they start using prescription painkillers not to address pain, physical pain, but the mental issues that go along with feeling inferior. That you just cannot cope with all the things you’re expected to be and to do.”

These statistics, when contrasted with the individual-level data, seem paradoxical. I am not the first one noting this, of course,

I have to remark that the situation reminds me of what happens with Nordic countries. Are Mormons cryptonordics? But it need not be Mormonism. Some people have proposed that the higher suicide rates in those areas is due to their higher altitude ([Ha & Tu, 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/1660-4601/15/4/671)). Back to the models: I tried different things, OLS, OLS weighted by population, and boosted trees (using LightGBM), both trying to predict the relevant variable directly, and regressing the variable on income (a very strong predictor) and then working with the residual in the rest of the analysis. For poverty, I used two poverty metrics, one from the American Community Survey and another one from the Small Area Income and Poverty Estimates progam. Results were similar in both. In the final models, the variables controlled for are income, population, percentage of \[non-hispanic whites, blacks, asians, hispanics, native americans\], % of mormons in the state, altitude of the county, state-level fixed effects, and % mormon. This is all at a county level, so nothing can be said about individuals..

You can find details about these models in the [OpenNintil repo](https://github.com/jlricon/open_nintil/tree/master/mormons). In case of doubt, the GBM is to be trusted more, as it more accurately predicts the underlying values. The average rate is 2.97 per 100k population, so this is a negligible amount in the grand scheme of things, even when it is a 7% increase. ![drugs\_gbm\_ols\_elast (conflicted).png](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2019/01/05-3.png)

An interesting factor that came out of feature importance analysis here was population: It trumped income as the most powerful predictor of mortality due to drugs. (For alcohol, population was a minor factor, while the % of native americans was the dominant factor)

Drug use also shows diverging estimates; OLS would predict a decrease in drug use of 4.4 points, and GBM, an increase of 1.6 points on average (baseline average is 9.07 so these effects are significant)

Suicide was another outcome of interest: In this case, the effects turn out to be negative in both cases: in the counterfactual, there is an decrease of 1.37 (OLS) / 7.8 (GBM) points, above a mean value of 16.21 per 100k.

In case of doubt, I go with the GBM model, and for the outcomes above we see that mormonism is weakly good for drug abuse, bad for suicides, and not significant for alcohol-**poisoning**\-related deaths. This is not to say that, for example, mormonism has no other effects via alcohol, someone who is heavily drunk may be alive, and yet not being able to be productive.

\[EDIT 01-12-19\]: A [paper](https://academic.oup.com/aje/article/155/5/413/171404) I did not cite but that I should, using better data than what I was able to find, shows that there is a negative relation between LDS membership and suicide. Thus the result above for suicide is less likely to be true.

Poverty is strongly correlated with income, so here I decided to control for income first, and then model the residual with the rest of the variables. The GBM model is closer to the OLS model when fitted to the residual. In all cases coefficients are fairly small (but significant). The estimate here is that the OLS model would predict a decrease in poverty of 0.022 points, while GBM would predict a tenth of that. The baseline rate is 16.7%, so the effect is negligible. ## Conclusion

In this post I have provided some evidence about religion in general and its effects on a range of outcomes. To me the most interesting bits are the RCT and the literature survey on economic growth. The RCT shows that, at least in that particular case, religious belief (separated from, say, health norms) can have a positive outcome (here, on income). \[EDIT 2019-02-01: Although, as noted above, the effects fade out after two years\]

As for mormonism, the main topic of this blogpost, the first thing to note is the difficulty in obtaining quality data. The clearest evidence is from some old studies about mortality: back then mormons are clearly healthier than their peers in locations inside and outside the US. Nowadays, it's less clear. The negative effects of tobacco are more obvious than those of alcohol. What alcohol consumption does would be a topic for a whole new post, as I recall there is conflicting evidence about its effects: while there is consensus that elevated consumption has negative consequences, for low doses I've seen evidence pointing to tiny negative, neutral, and slightly positive effects.  From the Pew study, mormons tend to be somewhat less poor than non-mormons, albeit this is before controlling for confounders.

Looking at data at a county level, a different pattern becomes salient: First, from anecdotic evidence gathered online and then modeling the relations between a set of outcomes and mormonism and other variables, it seems that in the aggregate the effects of mormonism are mixed: For some reason it is correlated with an increase in mortality due to alcohol **poisoning**. The mechanism might be that there where there are strong social norms against alcohol, or some drug, a culture of safe consumption does not develop, and you end up with a skewed distribution of most people not consuming, and a few binging. Looking at other countries, it is[not clear](https://ourworldindata.org/alcohol-consumption) that there is an association between an [alcohol-positive culture](https://web.archive.org/web/20190301041537/http://www.pewglobal.org/2014/04/15/global-morality/table/alcohol-use/) and more deaths from alcohol-related disorders.

I wouldn't recommend conversion to a strict religion as an anti-poverty measure, and I wouldn't even recommend, at this stage at least, complete alcohol abstinence (And I say this as someone who rarely drinks\*!) unless one has, or is expected to have, problems with alcohol. More research is needed, and the best evidence here will be in the form of more RCTs, or twin studies; it could even be that existing twin studies have measured religion, and thus it may be possible to isolate its effect without the need for new sample gathering.

Ideally, now someone will take my dataset and my notebooks and try something different, hopefully enhancing it with more data. Please do tell me if you find different results!

## Caveats and limitations

-   The size of the dataset should imply enough statistical power to detect "big" effect, but subtler ones may not have been captured.
-   I may have failed to control for other major covariates of the relevant variables
-   The analysis is limited to the US.
-   The "mormon" variable, as noted in the OpenNintil notes, is known to be biased upwards. But if this bias is constant, it shouldn't affect the analysis much.
-   There may be effects at a community level that are not present at a county level; my analysis cannot capture those.
-   Similarly, there may be odd interaction effects at play between the mormon and non-mormon subsets of the population that I can't capture
-   A step up from this analysis would be doing Structural Equation Modeling (e.g. using lavaan)
-   Better feature engineering may have led to better estimates of the OLS coefficients. I backed the results up with GBM because they do not require as much data formatting as OLS.
-   This is mostly an exploration of developed countries, and modern times. So this does not apply to tribes or smaller scale societies.
