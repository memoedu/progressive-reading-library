# Oneboxing and twoboxing in Newcomb's dilemma

---

## 正文

先读[这个](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcomb%27s_paradox)。

我们把下面的内容称为 Newcomb's dilemma 的 Formulation A：

> 一天，你接到一个叫 Robert Nozick 的人的电话，告诉你，你被选中了，可以拿到一些免费的 money。他约你在某个地方见面。你到了那里，发现他带着两个 boxes。他说，欢迎来到 Newcomb's dilemma。rules 很简单：他一边 opens 第一个 box，一边说，这里有 1000$. So now you have a box and 1000$，放在一张桌子上。另一个 box 里面可能什么都没有，也可能有 1000000$. Your goal, he informs you, is to get the maximum amount of money possible. But what is actually in the box, Mr. Nozick? Well, that depends on what you do. You see, I happen to have a very accurate decision-predictor we'll call Alpha. I ran Alpha before calling you, and set up the box back then. If Alpha predicted you'd take both boxes, it will have put nothing there. But if it predicted that you'd take only the closed box, it will have placed 1000000$。你现在无论做什么，都不会 alter 这些 boxes 里的东西。嗯……好吧，还不止这些。我有个朋友，叫 Newcomb，他恰好能看到另一个 box 里面有什么。不过，他不能告诉你。但不妨想象一下，如果他能告诉你，他会说什么。Money 不会凭空出现在那里，所以，如果他告诉你 open 那个 box 并拿走 1000$，那么 box 里有没有东西都无关紧要。所以……我应该拿走 money，再 open 那个 box，对吧？即使这台 machine 是 perfect predictor，我现在做的任何事也都不会 causally affect 这些 boxes 的 content。因此，我选择 two-boxing。

现在考虑 Newcomb's Dilemma 的 Formulation B

> 一天，你接到一个叫 Robert Nozick 的人的电话，告诉你，你被选中了，可以拿到一些免费的 money。他向你讲述 Newcomb's dilemma 和 Alpha，并让你想想自己会怎么做。money 还没有放进 box。你开始 reason：嗯，最好的做法应该是，现在真的 believing 自己会 one-box，但到时候却 two-box，以此 fool 那个 predictor。不过，我做不到，predictor 会看穿我。所以，如果我 intending 去 two-box，我就会 fail。因此，无论如何，我都必须 one-box，而且要真的这么做，不能藏着别的 plans。Nozick 问：“准备好了吗？”当然，开始吧。Alpha 作出它的 prediction，然后你就面临这个 dilemma。你可以盲目遵循自己的 pre-decision ideas（并且 one-box），也可以回头改变主意，选择 two-box。问题是：如果你改变主意，那么你在 decision 之前下定决心时，是否完全 sincere？因为如果不是，Alpha 就会已经看穿你，也就不会往 box 里放 money。但是……这些 boxes 已经放好了，应该无论怎么做都无关紧要。不过，如果你正在这样想，那很可能意味着你之前并不 sincere（否则你就会直接选一个 box），所以 box 里很可能什么都没有。但你之前是 sincere 的，仍然有一个 small probability。于是，为了以防万一，你最终还是 oneboxing。

显然，我在这里给出的 reasoning 与 [Kavka's toxin puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavka%27s_toxin_puzzle) 中的 situation 相似。

现在考虑 Newcomb's Dilemma 的 Formulation C

> 一天，你接到一个叫 Robert Nozick 的人的电话，告诉你，你被选中了，可以拿到一些免费的 money。他向你讲述 Newcomb's dilemma 和 Alpha。然后，他说，two-box 的人最终会拿到 1000$ and people who one-box end up with 100000$。知道这一点之后，你面临这个 Dilemma。同样，Nozick 在给你打电话之前，就已经按照 Alpha 的要求，放入或没有放入 money。你会怎么做？这里，你会 one-box，因为实际上，发生的事情是，one-boxers 会 maximise 他们的 money。

在 case C 中，我认为你应该选择 one-boxing，这一点完全清楚。毕竟，你有 evidence 表明 one-boxers 会 win。实际上，这似乎根本不算 dilemma。Cases A 和 B 试图在作出 decision 的 point in time，以及 committment 这个 idea 上做文章。它们都呈现了这个 dilemma 中出现的 opposing intuitions。

现在，要 study 这个 problem 本身，有几个 points 应该 clarified，比如：

-   你真的能 free to choose 吗？
-   这个 predictor 有多 accurate？

也许你拥有完全的 free will，但 predictor 知道你将来会 willing 什么。也许你没有 free will，而 predictor 仍然知道。我认为，Free will 是 irrelevant 的。

重要的是 predictor 的 predict 能力。100% accuracy 意味着你根本无法 fool 这个 predictor。但即使如此，在 boxes 放好之后，你无论做什么都无关紧要，所以，显然 predictor 做什么也无关紧要。这一点在 Formulation B 中没有那么明显：在那里，predictor 是在你下定决心之后才 choses 的，所以你知道，如果你是 insincere，predictor 也会知道，因此你知道，你甚至不应该试图在将来拿走 money 并 open 那个 box。

这就引出了一个 question：是否真的可能，你 a priori 想要 one-box，却在 Alpha 的 decision 之后 a posteriori 改变主意，而这又不 implying 你一开始就有 insincerity？

另一个 question：这场 discussion 是 relevant 的吗？有一个 case 看起来不是：Case C，在那里，你有 inductive evidence 表明你应该 one-box。

我们能否有类似的 evidence 来支持 two boxing？

Newcomb's Dilemma 的 Formulation D1 与 C 一样，但你知道，所有 one-boxes 的人都会拿到 1000000$ and everyone who two-boxes gets 1001000$。

Newcomb's Dilemma 的 Formulation D2 与 C 一样，但你知道，one-boxers 什么都拿不到，而 two-boxers 会拿到 1000$

Newcomb's Dilemma 的 Formulation D3 与 C 一样，但你知道，one-boxers 什么都拿不到，而 two-boxers 会拿到 1001000$

D3 的 problem 在于，Alpha 会以一种与它本来应该采用的方式相反的方式 predicting。也就是说，如果你要 one-box，它就什么都不给你；如果你要 two-box，它就会把所有东西都给你，所以这里甚至不存在 ex-ante dilemma：two-boxing 总是 wins。

在 D2 中，Predictor 总会 gets wrong 你要 one-box 的 intention（以为你会 two-box，因此不放 money），但当你要 two-box 时，它却会 correctly predicts（并让 money 空着）。所以，这里的 predictor 遵循的是始终让 box 空着的 algorithm。就好像它根本没有 predicting。因此，这不是一个 plausible case。

在 D1 中，predictor 的 behaves 再次如同它没有 predicting：它只是始终把 money 留在 box 里。

在 cases D 中，显然，predictor 不会符合它的 description。它并没有在 predicting，所以并不存在能像 one-boxing 的 case 那样，为 two-boxing 提供支持的 evidence。因此，你应该 one-box。

接下来，我们可以有包含 mixed cases 的 cases E。在那里，我们可以 infer probabilistically 该怎么做。

但是，是否真的完全不可能存在这样的 pure（predictor 始终以同样的方式 acts）cases：其中 predictor works as expected，同时又能作为支持 two-boxing 的 evidence？

在 cases D 中，接近这一点的是第一个，其中 one-boxing 很好，但 two-boxing 更好。predictor 会 predicting 你总是会 one-box，而且至少在你确实 one-box 时，它对那些 cases 的判断是对的。但当你 two-box 时，它总是 fails。

我可能还需要多读一些这方面的东西，但如果现在让我面临这个问题，而且我知道 predictor 确实 works，我会选择 one-boxing，仅仅因为似乎很难 conceive 这样一个 world：你能有 evidence，同时表明 predictor works as advertised，又表明 two-boxing works。又或者，这个 problem 本身就是 incoherent 的。

不过，在 case A 中，我肯定会 two-box。

## 原文

Read [this](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcomb%27s_paradox) first.

Let's call what follows Formulation A of Newcomb's dilemma:

> One day, you get a call from some Robert Nozick to inform you that yo have been selected to earn some free money. He asks you to meet him somewhere. You go there and he is with two boxes. Welcome to Newcomb's dilemma, he says. The rules are simple: Here, in the first box, he says while he opens it, there are 1000$. So now you have a box and 1000$ on a table. In the other box you can have either nothing or 1000000$. Your goal, he informs you, is to get the maximum amount of money possible. But what is actually in the box, Mr. Nozick? Well, that depends on what you do. You see, I happen to have a very accurate decision-predictor we'll call Alpha. I ran Alpha before calling you, and set up the box back then. If Alpha predicted you'd take both boxes, it will have put nothing there. But if it predicted that you'd take only the closed box, it will have placed 1000000$ in it. Nothing you do now will alter what is in the boxes. Hmmm.... Well, there's more. There is a friend of mine, Newcomb, who happens to be able to see what is inside the other box. He can't tell you, though. But just imagine what he would tell you if he were able to. Money won't be magically appearing there, so if he tells you to open the box and take the 1000$, it won't matter whether there is anything in the box. So... I should take the money and open the box, right? Even if the machine is a perfect predictor, nothing I do now will causally affect the content of the boxes. Therefore, I go for two-boxing.

Consider now Formulation B of Newcomb's Dilemma

> One day, you get a call from some Robert Nozick to inform you that yo have been selected to earn some free money. He tells you about Newcomb's dilemma and about Alpha, and to think about what will you do. The money is not in the box yet. You reason: Well, the best thing to do would be be to fool the predictor by really believing now that I will one-box, but then two-box. However, I can't do that, and the predictor will see through me. So if I'm intending to two-box, I will fail. So I have to one-box no matter what, and really do it, with no hidden plans. "Are you ready?" Asks Nozick. Sure, go ahead. Alpha casts its prediction, and you are then faced with the dilemma. You can either blindly follow your pre-decision ideas (and one-box) or backtrack and change your mind, and two-box. The thing is: If you change your mind, were you totally sincere when you made up your mind before the decision? Because if you are not, then Alpha will have seen through you, and won't have put money in the box. But... the boxes have already been set, and it shouldn't matter what to do. But if you are thinking this, it probably means that you weren't sincere (you would have gone straight for one box), so probably there is nothing in the box. But there is a small probability that you were sincere. You then end up oneboxing, just in case.

Apparently, the reasoning I presented here is similar to the situation is [Kavka's toxin puzzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kavka%27s_toxin_puzzle).

Consider now Formulation C of Newcomb's Dilemma

> One day, you get a call from some Robert Nozick to inform you that yo have been selected to earn some free money. He tells you about Newcomb's dilemma and about Alpha. He then says that people who two-box end up with 1000$ and people who one-box end up with 100000$. Knowing this, you are presented with the Dilemma. As again, Nozick placed or didn't place te money as Alpha told him to do before calling you. What do you do? Here, you one-box, given that actually, what happens is that one-boxers maximise their money.

In case C, I think it is totally clear that you should go for one-boxing. After all, you have evidence that one-boxers win. This doesn't seem like a dilemma at all, actually. Cases A and B try to play with the point in time where the decision is taken, and the idea of committment. They both present the opposing intuitions that arise in the dilemma.

Now, to study the problem itself, there are a couple of points that should be clarified, like:

-   Are you really free to choose?
-   How accurate is the predictor?

Maybe you have total free will, yet the predictor knows what you will be willing in the future. Maybe you don't have free will, and the predictor still knows. Free will is irrelevant, I think.

What matters is the ability of the predictor to predict. 100% accuracy means that you can't fool the predictor at all. But even then, after the boxes are in place, it won't matter what you do, so apparently it doesn't matter what the predictor does. This is less obvious in Formulation B: There the predictor choses after you've made your mind, so you know that if you are insincere, the predictor will know too, so you know you shouldn't even attempt to get the money and open the box in the future.

This leads us to a question: Is it even possible to a priori want to one-box and a posteriori Alpha's decision change your mind without that implying your insincerity in the first place?

Other question: Is this discussion relevant? There is one case in which it doesn't seem to be: Case C, where you have inductive evidence that you ought to one-box.

Could we have similar evidence for two boxing?

Formulation D1 of Newcomb's Dilemma is as C, but knowing that everyone who one-boxes get 1000000$ and everyone who two-boxes gets 1001000$.

Formulation D2 of Newcomb's Dilemma is as C, but knowing that one-boxers get nothing, and two-boxers get 1000$

Formulation D3 of Newcomb's Dilemma is as C, but knowing that one-boxers get nothing, and two-boxers get 1001000$

The problem with D3 is that Alpha would be predicting in an opposite way  to the one it's meant to. That is, if you are going to one-box, it gives you nothing, and if you are going to two-box, it will give you everything, so here there is not even an ex-ante dilemma: two-boxing always wins.

In D2, the Predictor always gets wrong your intention to one-box (thinks that you will two-box, therefore doesn't put money), but correctly predicts when you are going to two-box (and leaves the money empty). So here the predictor is following the algorithm of always leaving the box empty. As if were not predicting. So this is not a plausible case.

In D1, the predictor behaves again as if it were not predicting: it just leaves the money always in the box.

In cases D, apparently, the predictor wouldn't fit its description. It wouldn't be predicting, so there is no evidence that could serve for two-boxing, as there is for the one-boxing case. So you should one-box.

We could then have cases E with mixed cases. There we could infer probabilistically what to do.

But is it completely true that there can be no pure (the predictor always acts the same) cases in which the predictor works as expected, and that serve as evidence for two-boxing?

From cases D, the one that would get close is the first one, in which one-boxing is great, but two-boxing is even better. The predictor would be predicting that you will always one-box, and at least when you do one-box, it gets those cases right. But it always fails when you two-box.

I probably need to read more about this, but if I were presented with this right now,  and I knew the predictor works I would go with one-boxing, just because it seems hard to conceive a world in which you could have evidence that both the predictor works as advertised, and that two-boxing works. Or perhaps, the problem itself is incoherent.

In case A, though, I would definitely two-box.
