## 正文

_也就是那个你或许会因为它改变了你的 political views 而永远记住的网页 :)_

**引言**

**0.1: 你是谁，这是什么？**

关于我，请参阅本站的“关于”部分。

这是对 [Scott Alexander 的 Non-Libertarian FAQ](https://slatestarcodex.com/2017/02/22/repost-the-non-libertarian-faq/) 的回应，最初写于 2010 年，并于 2013 年修订。请注意，他的 FAQ 不叫 _Anti_\-Libertarian FAQ。本着同样的精神，这份 FAQ 并不 defend libertarianism，而是 criticises 针对 libertarianism 提出的 arguments。

**0.2: 你是 libertarian 吗？**

这取决于你对 libertarian 的 definition。我一直不太习惯用“我是一个 X”中的任何 ideological X 来称呼自己。不过，你可以通过阅读 Michael Huemer、Loren Lomasky、Robert Nozick、Matt Zwolinski 和 Jason Brennan，了解我的想法。

关于 statism，我将 statism define 为这样一种 belief：State 拥有 political authority，或者 law 应当被 obeyed，仅仅因为它是 law，而不论其 content 是什么。如今的 Political philosophers reject 这一点，因此 statists 站在了 philosophical progress 的错误一边。不过，仍有 arguments 认为，State commands 对于其他 goals 而言是 right。也就是说，针对 murder 的 laws 是 nonproblematic，但这是因为 murder 是 wrong，而 State enforces 对它的 ban。此外，也可能有其他支持 State 的 arguments，并不 rely on political authority，即 consequentialist arguments。

那么，大多数 non-libertarians（有意思的是，也包括一些 libertarians）都是上文所 define 的 statists。Scott 并不 believe in political authority：他是 consequentialist。因此，他不是 statist。他只有在 state 是最 conducive to Greater Good™ 的 social-institutional arrangement 时，才会支持它。

**0.3 你讨厌 non-libertarians 吗？**

Hating 是 irrational 的，在 intellectual inquiry 中尤其如此。“hating”这种 mental state 不仅让你感觉更糟，还会诱使你以 biased ways 思考你所讨厌的事物。

Scott 说，他喜欢这样的 libertarianism：它是一组 hands-off policies，可以通过 arguments 说明它们比 alternatives 更有效；而他不喜欢的 libertarianism 则是“并非先 analyzing specific policies，再经常 deciding 更 laissez-faire 的 approach 是 best，而是一开始就奉行 government 不可能做对任何事、private industry 不可能做错任何事的 tenet，并用这种 faith _代替_更 careful 的 analysis。”。

他的 FAQ 是写给第二类 libertarians 的。我应该属于第三类：我确实认为 libertarianism 总体上 works better（依据 empirical and theoretical evidence），而且它 work 得不那么好的 situations，并不 justify 使用 force 来 correct 它们。如果存在它 work 得非常糟糕的 situations，那么 coercion 就会是 justified。也就是说，我相信 States 可以做对许多事，private sector 也可以做错许多事，但这本身并不能说明是否应当存在 State，或者，如果必须存在一个 State，它应当做些什么。

 economics 和 history 是一回事，moral philosophy 则是另一回事。这份 FAQ 主要讨论前者，因为 Scott 另有一份讨论后者的 FAQ：[Consequentialism FAQ](http://raikoth.net/consequentialism.html)。虽然我不是 consequentialist，但我认为 consequences 非常重要。这份 FAQ 主要讨论 empirical issues，因此，哪一种 ethical theory 才是 correct，与我在这里的目的无关。更何况，许多 [libertarians 是](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/02/26/liberalisms-overlapping-consensus/) consequentialists 或 utilitarians，这使得这场 debate 在此处更加显得 prima facie 不重要：即使 consequentialism 是 true，它最终也可能仍然 leading to libertarianism。

**0.4 这份 FAQ 会 prove free market 总是比 government 更好吗**？

不会。

我要 argue 的是，free markets 非常好，甚至比大多数 economists 所认为的还要好。而且，从长远来看，结合 non-market institutions，以及对 property rights 的恰当 definition（以 solve externalities），这是一种很难通过与 alternative system 的 comparison 来 criticise 的 (meta)system。

**0.5 为什么要写一份 Non-Non-Libertarian FAQ？**

Scott Alexander 写了一份文档，却没有公开可得的（high quality）critique；由于这份文档 well argued，语气也很友好，有些人便将文档本身，以及三年来 critique 的缺席，当作 libertarianism 是 wrong 的 proof。

如果一个像 Scott 这样诚实、rational 且 rigorous 的人，以 non-strawmanning 的方式 rejected libertarianism，而且没有人回应他的 arguments，这难道不构成一个针对 libertarianism 的 strong prima facie case 吗？确实如此！已有[一篇这样的回应](https://problemswithdemocracy.blogspot.com/2013/10/the-non-libertarian-faq-response.html)（恕我直言，不怎么好），David Friedman 也通过电子邮件给 Scott 发过[另一篇](https://daviddfriedman.blogspot.com/2015/02/better-than-facebook.html#c2008977712910407576)，[这里](http://www.daviddfriedman.com/Miscellaneous/My%20Response%20to%20a%20Non-Libertarian%20faq.html)也能找到。但仍然没有公开可得、针对整份 FAQ 逐点展开的良好回应。当然，其他地方有支持 libertarianism 的良好 argumentations（philosophy 见[这里](https://www.amazon.com/The-Problem-Political-Authority-Examination/dp/1137281650)，practical issues 见[这里](https://www.amazon.es/Revoluci%C3%B3n-Liberal-Espa%C3%B1a-Economia-deusto/dp/8423418537?ie=UTF8&*Version*=1&*entries*=0)），但它们并非专门针对这份 FAQ。

话虽如此，Scott Alexander 几年前曾[承认](http://squid314.livejournal.com/344915.html?thread=7772755)，他已经不确定自己是否还是 non-libertarian，而且这份 FAQ 针对的是不聪明的 libertarians，因此，写下我的 critique 可能显得，而且我认为确实会显得，有些用力过猛。

最后，如果我不同意一些 well argued arguments，我就[不能让](https://xkcd.com/386/)它们得不到回应。如果一个人想继续持有与他的 FAQ 不一致的 beliefs，就必须给出 reasons。我将自己的这份 reasons 清单公开给所有人，以推动 debate 向前发展。

**0.6 这份 FAQ 是如何 structured 的？**

与 Scott 的一样。我建议你将我的 FAQ 与他的一起对照阅读，因为我的 FAQ 中的 questions，大致是对 Scott 的 answers 的 summaries。如果不这样对照阅读，我想表达的内容甚至可能不太说得通。

补充一句，在这一段中，Scott 提到了 libertarians 倾向于诉诸 economic and moral arguments。如果 morality dictates libertarianism，为什么还要费心讨论 consequences？如果 libertarianism 就是 works best 的 system，仅此而已，为什么还要费心说某些事情是 morally wrong？

这一 critique 最初由 [Jeffrey Friedman (1997)](http://www.criticalreview.com/crf/jf/11%203%20libertarianism.pdf) 提出，随后引发了 debate（[Palmer 1998](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/08913819808443507)、[Friedman 1998](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/08913819808443508)）。最近，[Zwolinski](http://www.peter-boettke.com/app/download/7116937557/Zwolinski+-+A+Brief+History+of+Libertarianism.pdf) 和 [Tesón](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2011/03/classical-liberalism-defended/) 明确表述了这一点：

> 尽管 appeal to self-ownership 可能是最著名的、用于 defending rights of private property 的 libertarian strategy，但它绝不是唯一一种。一些 libertarians 试图将自己的 defense 建立在不同但同样是 deontological 的 grounds 上，例如 appeal to negative liberty，或者 autonomy。另一些 libertarians 则试图提供更 consequentialist 的 justification，方法是说明 private property 如何以 market economy 的 positive-sum transactions，replaces commons 的 zero- (or negative-) sum transactions。或者，说明 rights of private property 如何在 securing 其他重要的 civil and political rights 方面具有 instrumentally valuable 的性质。不过，大体而言，libertarian intellectual tradition 中的人们一直避免在 consequentialist 与 deontological arguments 之间 drawing sharp distinctions，而是相信，对 private property 的 respect 既是 moral duty，也是 wise social policy。因此，Libertarians，尤其是 academic philosophy 这一 discipline 之外的那些人，往往会大量使用这两类 arguments，却不总是 clearly distinguishing 它们。$$...$$
> 
> 在如此多的 libertarian arguments 中，justice 和 utility 都指向同一个方向，这无疑是一个需要 explanation 的事实。一种可能的 explanation 是 skeptical 的。按照这一路 reasoning，justice 和 utility 之所以对 libertarians 而言能够一致，是因为 libertarians 会 frame 自己的 arguments 来 fit 自己的 conclusions，而不是反过来。关于这种 skepticism 一种略带同情的表述，参见 Jeffrey Friedman, "What's Wrong with Libertarianism?," Critical Review 11, no. 3 (1997)。不过，也存在其他不那么 skeptical 的 explanations。例如，justice 和 utility 之所以不 come into conflict，是否可能是因为 justice 的 content 部分地是 utilitarian considerations 的 a function of？关于许多此类可能的 explanations 的讨论，以及对其中一种的 endorsement，参见 Roderick Long, "Why Does Justice Have Good Consequences?," http://praxeology.net/whyjust.htm \[编者注：另见[这里](http://praxeology.net/postlib.htm)和[这里](https://mises.org/library/review-ethics-social-science-moral-philosophy-social-cooperation-leland-b-yeager)\]

对于这个问题，我还没有明确的看法，但我要说，如果 libertarianism 带来非常糟糕的 consequences，就应当 abandoned。此外，如果 libertarianism 带来非常好的 consequences，却是高度 unethical 的，那么它也应当 abandoned。

### **Part A: Economic Issues（A 部分：经济问题）**

**The Argument（论证）**

_每一项 transaction 都不仅 affects seller 和 buyer，也 affects 其他所有人，而且它 assumes consumers 具有 coherent wants 和 demands。Markets 存在 problems：externalities、coordination problems、irrational choice，以及 lack of information。鉴于此，regulated market 胜过 free market。_

**The Counterargument（反论证）**

_是的，voluntary trade necessarily 使人们 ex-ante better off，但这并不 presuppose classical perfect competition 的所有 conditions。Perfect competition 给出的是 efficiency 的 sufficient conditions，而不是 necessary conditions。_

_trade 确实并非 occurs in isolation。恰恰这一点将是回答这份 FAQ 所提出的 challenges 的关键。此外，trade 不必在两个人之间进行，也可以有 multilateral trade。接下来将论证，只要一切都是 voluntary，而且 property rights 是 well defined，free market 就能 delivers。_

**1\. Externalities（外部性）**

**1.1 什么是 externality？**

[Externalities](http://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/Externalities.html) 是 imposed upon 那些并未作为 participants 参与 originated 该 cost 的 transaction 的人的 costs。尽管一些 economists 喜欢从 externalities 中 derive government interventions 的 justifications，我们仍应记住，有相当多的 externalities 是没有人主张 fixing 的，而其中一些与 pollution 一样，都是 externalities。

一个例子是 non-whites 对 white supremacists 造成的 psychological distress。如果必须 fixed 这种 externality，那么 non-whites 就会因为自身的存在而被 taxed，white supremacists 则会得到 compensated，或者采取类似的做法。相反，我们大多数人都认为，而且这样认为是正确的，white supremacism 是 wrong，应该发生的是让他们 change their preferences。这也通过使 externality 消失而 solves 了它。

另一个例子是 procreation：把更多孩子带到世界上会 alters society 中 traits（intelligence、health、creativity、kindness 等）的 distribution，而这会 affects 每一个人（[Anomaly, 2014](http://philpapers.org/archive/ANOPGA-3.pdf)）。要 fix 它，我们可能需要 positive（unproblematic）或 negative（problematic）eugenics，或者由某种 authority 进行 family planning。

大多数人不会 endorse 对 non-whites 进行 taxing 以 compensate white supremacists，也不会 endorse 通过 curtailing reproductive rights 来确保每个孩子都不是一个行走的 negative externality。

此外，我们也不会想要 fix pecuniary externalities：即通过 price system 来 affect 他人的 externalities。如果我购买橙汁，就会 marginally raise 其他 consumers 所面对的橙汁 price，因此会 negatively affects 他们。但他们的这种 loss，会被橙汁 producers 获得的 equivalent gain（以 money 的形式）所 balanced。

大多数人通常希望 fix 的是哪些（negative）externalities？就是那些基本上属于 violations of rights 的 externalities，而 libertarianism 要求这样做。

还有一些 positive externalities，但要从它们出发，提出 justify coercion 以 fix 它们的 arguments，就更加困难了。

**1.2 但是，libertarian 的 solve externalities 的方式会 fail，除非你提出的东西基本上就是一个 government**

这份 FAQ 描述的 externality 可以由 court 来 solved。你会客气地请 wasp farmer 不要在那里继续经营，因为他令人困扰；如果他不肯，你就 sue 他。也可以有 private organisations 替你进行 suing。court 会 assess wasp farmer 造成的 nuisance 是否属于 rights violation。它很可能是：neighbourhood 中的人早已住在那里，而 wasp farmer 的出现使他们以非常明显的方式 worse off。

另外，另一种可以 fix 此事的 institution 是 [Homeowner Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeowner_association)（HOA）。这些[并不是](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/11/15/the-privatepublic-distinction-a-reply-to-stringham-and-powell/) governments，却 provide 了你希望 local governments 具备的大多数 functions。

另一种是 covenants，即随房屋 property 一同 transferred 的 binding agreements。为什么 builders 会在新建 neighbourhoods 的 buildings 中纳入针对 wasp farming 的 prohibitions？显然，因为人们会更加 value 这样的 neighbourhoods。这也是 libertarian zoning restrictions 的一个 foundation。关于这方面的更多信息，请参阅 [Beito, Gordon & Tabarrok (2009)](https://www.amazon.com/The-Voluntary-City-Community-Society/dp/1598130323) 关于这一主题的出色著作。如今可能不再使用这些做法，因为 government 已经在做这些事，但在过去，这并不罕见。

**1.3 人们不会 boycott 那些 pollute 的 companies，除非大多数 consumers 都是 knowledgeable 和 altruistic**

Pollution 不会主要通过 boycott 来处理。Pollution 是 libertarian 世界中另一种会由 court-regulated 的事情。对于 minarchists，这很容易：有一个 State 在进行 regulation。对于 anarchists，这更难，但我认为仍然可行。Pollution 是 libertarian theory 的难题之一，而这一领域的工作仍在继续（[Zwolinski, 2014](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2443030)）。

无论如何，尽管 boycotts 不会是 libertarian 世界中 governance 的主要 tool，其他 institutions 可以发挥这一作用，包括一般的 consumer pressure：有一些 studies 表明，它可以 induce companies 来 change their behaviour（[Hettige et al. 1996](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0305750X96000769)）。

**1.4 因此，externalities 可以 justify 一些 regulations**

如果你愿意，就把它们叫作 regulations。它们也可以叫作 property rights 的 definitions，而这些与 libertarianism 完全 consistent，而且也是 libertarianism 所 required 的。通过把精细的 property rights definitions 排除在 libertarianism 的 definition 之外，就能在原本没有 problems 的地方为它制造 problems。

**2\. Coordination Problems（协调问题）**

**2.1 什么是 coordination problems**

[Moloch](http://slatestarcodex.com/2014/07/30/meditations-on-moloch/)。或者说，所有人都看得到 solution，却没有 coordination 来 implement 它的 situations。我曾在[别处](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/12/24/slaying-alexanders-moloch/) criticised 那种认为 coordination problems 无处不在且 unsolvable 的笼统观点。关于 fish farmers 的故事，我把自己写过的内容复制粘贴如下：

于是，我们有 1000 家 firms，每家都造成 -1$ loss to others, from an initial gain of $1000，所以它们的收益为 zero。fishermen 聚到一起，说明各自的情况。他们同意 signing 一份 conditional contract，与在 Kickstarter 上可以做的事情并无太大不同：每个人都将 pay 300$ for a filter, so that everyone makes \$700 一个月。只有所有人都 signs，这份 contract 才会是 binding；他们去 court，在那里 sign，使它成为 binding，而且可由 external agency 来 enforceable。现在，他们已经 changed their incentives：可以 choose 什么也不做（payoff=0），也可以 sign（expected payoff=700）。于是他们 sign。一旦去过 court，他们就可以 cooperate（expected payoff=700），也可以 shirk。但现在，他们的 payoff 不再是 999，而是 paying a fine，直到他们 cooperate，所以现在 cooperate 是 rational。解决了。

有人对我说：但是你用了 court！那又怎样？Libertonia 里有 courts，你原本期待什么？Third-party enforceability 是一种 valuable service。

但如果有一个固执的 fisherman，demands 其他人 pay 他，他才肯真正 sign 这份 contract，那怎么办？这里，我们就遇到了 fishermen 起初是否真的拥有 pollute water 的 right 这一问题。

那么，假设 fishermen 提出了这一问题。他们中的大多数 argue，既然 lake 是 common resource，而且让它保持 clean 符合他们所有人的 interest，就应该把它视为 common property，可以在其中 imposed rules，以确保 inner coordination。那个 hypothetical free rider 可以 argue，既然他是最先来到 river 的人，就有 right to pollute，而其他人应该 pay 他。这可能是某种 Coasean bargain。在这种情况下，如果他向其他每个人 demands payment P，他的收益就会是 700+999P。其他人的收益则会是 700-P。在 cooperative case 中，total earnings 为 700_1000=0.7 megadollars。在 noncooperative case 中，则为 700+999P+700_999-999P=0.7 megadollars，amount 相同，distribution 不同。这里的 P 会 depend on 多种 factors，但 total gains 是相同的。court system 必须处理这样的 cases，并 resolve disputes、set precedents，在 respecting rights 的同时努力确保 good outcomes。这不是一项 trivial task，但也并非 impossible。

也请阅读[这篇](http://www.owl232.net/commons.htm)。关于 fisheries，总的来说，请阅读 [Ostrom (1999)](http://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev.polisci.2.1.493)。

Coasean solution 并非 troublefree：它只有在至少不存在 transaction costs 时，才是 necessarily efficient。现实中的 bargaining 不会是 texbooklike efficient。但正如这份 FAQ 始终强调的，这些并不是仅有的 solutions。David Friedman 在[这里](http://www.daviddfriedman.com/Academic/Coase_World.html)讨论了 Coase's Theorem。

**2.1.1 但这行不通**

从 Empirically 来看，行得通。请阅读 [Leal (1998)](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1023%2FA%3A1024691919628)。这份 FAQ 提出 fishing quotas。显而易见的 libertarian solution 是 private 或 common property rights，而 fishing quotas 是对它们的一种 approximation。关于 private 与 common property rights 的 comparison，请参阅[Ostrom and Hess (2007)](http://surface.syr.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1024&context=sul)。

Atlantic Northwest cod fishery 的例子属于 _government_ failure：该 fishery 是由 government (mis)regulated 的。如果整个 fishery 都有 property rights，会发生什么？government 没有允许这种事情发生。US fishery policy 存在自身的 problems（[White, 2000](http://lsr.nellco.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1083&context=nyu_lewp)）。EU 的 Common Fisheries Policy 似乎也正在 failing（[Khallilian et al, 2010](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0308597X10000709)）。Governments 在 solving common resource coordination problems 时可能 succeed，也可能 fail，但 private 或 communal institutions 同样如此（[Tarko, 2012](http://www.iea.org.uk/sites/default/files/publications/files/IEA%20Future%20of%20the%20Commons%20web%2029-1.10.12.pdf)）。

话虽如此，即使有大量 national 和 international government regulation，如今 global fisheries 的状况仍[不容乐观](http://www.economist.com/news/leaders/21596942-new-management-needed-planets-most-important-common-resource-tragedy-high)。在跨越 national borders 的 fisheries 中 Creating common property 会与 sovereignty conflict（哈，仅仅是 fishermen，竟然告诉 States 该如何处理它们的 territorial waters！），所以在某种意义上，States 可能正在使这些 fisheries 的 governance 更加困难。

**2.1.2 但是 Fishermen 不会这样做！**

正如上文所说，从 empirically 来看，他们确实在这样做，尤其是在 non-international water regions，以及某种 rights 已经存在的时候。Formal external enforcement 将是任何 libertarian political order 的 feature，无论有没有 state，因此，需要它这一事实并不构成对 libertarianism 的 critique。

对于 international waters，事情更加棘手，以至于，正如上文指出的，States（目前）也无法处理。但我们会进一步声称这件事是 _impossible_ 吗？

**2.2 Global warming 是这一 problem 的一个 example，ozone layer depletion 和 recycling 也是如此**

影响 private sector 的同一个 problem 也影响 States。所以，这类 problems 对 libertarianism qua libertarianism 而言并不算特别 problematic，因为 States 也无法在 global scale 上 fix 它们。statist solution 会是设立一个 world-State 来 regulate 这些事项。libertarian solution 则会说，既然每个人都 makes use of the atmosphere，而为了所需的 purposes（regulate emissions of certain gases and pollutants），它又无法被 subdivided，那么它就必须是一项 single property。因此，设立一个 worldwide atmospheric regulation committee 来 solve 这场 tragedy of the commons 就会是 justified。这听起来确实很难做到，但 States 最近也没有做得好多少。

幸运的是，technology 似乎能在一个由 Nation-States 构成的世界里做到 individual incentives 无法做到的事。现在，我们正在经历一场 solar energy revolution（如果稍微乐观一些，几十年后我们还会经历一场 fusion energy revolution），最终 fossil fuel energies 将被一种 cheaper 且 cleaner 的能源所 replaced。因此，尽管这个 problem 并未 solved，它却不再是一个实际的 problem，于是一切都没问题了。

**2.3 Libertarians 说 boycotts 能 discipline corporations。但 Incentives 并不是那样 aligned 的。**

假设 markets 更加 competitive，使一个 product 与其 replacement 之间的 difference 很小。那么，也许就是 \$1 plus an ethical cost of buying from an unethical company vs $(1+x)，并向一家友善的 company 购买。如果 x 是 relatively small，这或许行得通。

不过，我并不是在 arguing boycotts 是一种非常非常强大的 tool。但那些令人难以启齿的事情可以由 courts 来 solved。我的意思是，如果有一家 company uses slave labour，任何人都可以代表 slaves，因 slavery 而 sue 它。我实在看不出这里有什么 problem。

**2.3.1 它们不起作用：[Coke example](http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/business/1448962.stm)**

这类 abuses 是 highly rare，而且在有 governments 的情况下也会发生。而这些 Governments 在某些情况下还是 corrupt 的。一些 left-libertarians 甚至 claim，它们之所以发生，_正是因为_有 governments。

而且，这个 Coke example [并不那么站得住脚](http://www.straightdope.com/columns/read/2622/did-coca-cola-torture-and-kill-workers-in-latin-america)。那并不是 Coca-Cola 本身，而是一家为 Coca-Cola 工作的 bottler；有关 alleged crime 的 evidence 是 dubious，而 lawsuit 最终被 [dismissed](http://business-humanrights.org/en/coca-cola-lawsuit-re-colombia)。

**2.3.2 Governments 能做得更好**

所提到的大多数 laws 与人们想要什么没有多大关系。除了非常特殊的 cases，人们想要什么与 government 在日常活动中做什么之间，存在明显的 disconnect。总体而言，governments 以及任何 institutional system，包括 anarchies，都受到关于什么是 right 和 wrong 的 generally accepted beliefs 的 constrained（[Long, 2008](http://praxeology.net/Anarconst2.pdf)、[Wiśniweski 2014](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2599286) ）。但在这个范围内，governments 仍有很大的余地。

实际上，其中一些 regulations 可以说是在 _privilege_ 它们所 regulate 的 corporations。

**2.4 Coordination failures 也 justify 用于 charitable causes 的 government spending**

关于 world hunger，这里是否存在 coordination failure 并不那么明确。为什么？这取决于人们所 value 的是 alleviating people's suffering，还是通过 making donations 来感觉良好；根据这一点，它可能存在，也可能不存在。在第一种情况下，它是 private good。在第二种情况下，它是 public good。在第一种情况下，你向 charitable organisation 每 donate 一 dollar，就会得到 poverty 或 hunger 的 marginal reduction。并不是说，假如除了一个人之外所有人都 donate，hunger 就不会消失。实际上，人们并没有那么在意 world hunger（[Leeson, 2014](http://www.peterleeson.com/Homo_Economicus_Lives.pdf)） 。这个 issue 很严肃，但有多少人会花时间了解如何更好地应对它（例如 Effective Altruism、了解 Givewell 等）？几乎没有人。然而，那些用于 acquire information 的 resources 是免费的。

在 OECD——我将以它作为 developed countries 的 proxy——大约有 1.3 billion 人。假设他们全都能够 donate。那么，每年就是 1.3_1_(365/4)=118.6 bn$ per year. If [Jeffrey Sachs is right](http://www.visionofearth.org/economics/ending-poverty/how-much-would-it-cost-to-end-extreme-poverty-in-the-world/), and ending poverty costs 175bn$，人们将不得不 pay 1,5$ per week for 20 years (This assumes kids will also pay, so the estimate is a lower bound). But Sachs [is not without his critics](http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/articles/A25562-2005Mar10.html). [Givewell](http://blog.givewell.org/2015/11/06/the-lack-of-controversy-over-well-targeted-aid/) accepts that sort of criticism for that sort of aid plans, while defending that their approach: bottom-up, cost-effective, step-by-step interventions doesn't have the same problems. Sadly, there are no obvious solutions to lift the world's poor out of their situation, and if there isn't, I think it's difficult to justify coercion to help towards that goal.  So far, $4.6 _trillion_ 已在过去 50 年中被 donated 用于 foreign aid。仅凭连续 20 年、每年 175bn$，就能使全球 poor 的 quality of life 获得超出 economic growth 所带来改善的 substantial increase，这真的可信吗？

最后，如果这个 argument 成立，它可能 proves too much：难道我们不应当基于 consequentialist grounds，将 welfare State eliminate 到 bare minimum，并向 absolutely poor donate 巨额资金吗？

如果人们真的那么在意，他们有一个现成的 mechanism 可以让每个人 pay：State。很少能看到 political parties 为 Massive foreign redistribution 或 open [borders](http://openborders.info/) 辩护，也很少能看到 voters 要求这些。

还有一点值得考虑：States 可能正在让这个 problem 恶化。EU 的 [Common Agricultural Policy](http://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/politics/eu-subsidies-deny-africas-farmers-of-their-livelihood-478419.html) 和 [US subsidies for biofuels](https://conversableeconomist.blogspot.com/2013/01/biofuels-and-hunger-in-low-income.html) 确实 harm 其他 countries 的 poor，同时 benefitting 其 borders 内的 interest groups。

**3\. Irrational Choices（非理性选择）**

**3.1 人并不是 rational 的。他们不会 choose 用于 reach their goals 的 best means：如果某人 by default 被 signed up into a pension plan，他就会留下。如果某人 by default 没有被 signed out，他就不会 join 它。Transaction costs 几乎为 zero，所以这不应该造成 difference。**

Governments [也是人](https://johnhcochrane.blogspot.com/2014/11/behavioral-political-economy.html)，而 coercing 人们去 implement alternative choice architectures，以 fix 已存在的 minor cases of irrationality，并不是 justified，尤其是当所提出的 solution 比 problem 更糟时。Highly problematic forms of irrationality 最终会 inducing 能够 fix 它们的 social norms 和 institutions。

这里的 arguments 似乎是这样的

> 1.  在 stringent epistemic conditions 下，Markets maximize welfare；这些 conditions 包括 apt probability estimation、stability of preferences、没有 framing effects 或 externalities 等。
>     
> 2.  在某个给定的 market 中，这些 conditions 有一个或多个并不 hold，因为 individuals 并非 fully rational。
>     
> 3.  因此，那个 market 无法 maximize welfare。 （[Boettke et al. 2013](http://mercatus.org/publication/error-obvious-coordination-puzzle-0)） 。
>     

但 markets 实际上不需要这些也能是 efficient 的。

考虑 pensions 这个 case。如果某个 behavioural argument 是 right 的，那么人们就不会为 retirement 而 save enough，我们将会有大量 relatively poor 的 elderly people。这个 hypothesis 以 [human psychology](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/ssb/v70n4/v70n4p1.html) 为 grounded，而想必，为了让这个 argument 生效，并免受 welfare-induced irrationality 这一 critique（“保护人们免受其愚行影响的最终结果，就是让世界充满傻瓜”（Spencer，见 [Zwolinski 2015](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2598818)）），我们就必须在 public pensions 出现之前，observe 到那些大量处于极糟糕境况的人。此外，public pensions 应当是因为 private pensions 不起作用，或虽然存在却是 stagnant 的，或诸如此类的原因才出现的。

那么，public pensions 为什么会出现？有许多 theories，而有一篇 paper studies 了这些 theories，发现有几种 reasons 可以解释这一点。最早 adopting 它们的 countries 是 Germany,(1889)、Denmark (1891) 和 New Zeland (1898)，最后则是 Switzerland, (1948)、Netherlands, (1947)、Argentina, (1944) 和 Japan (1942)。（[Cutler et al. 2004](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1023%2FB%3APUCH.0000035859.20258.e0)）。他并未将老年人的悲惨境况作为一种 general，甚至 prevalent explanation 来提及，所以，在 providing old-age pensions 方面存在 massive market failure 的 hypothesis，原则上被 rejected 了。还有一个 question：在 mandatory pension schemes 实施前后，老年人的 poverty rate 分别是多少？

在 public pensions 之前，已有 sickness funds。那是一些 Non-profit associations，用于 provision 包括 old-age pensions 在内的各种保障。当时 financial markets 尚未 well developed，因此不可能有我们今天所知的那种 widespread pension funds，但总体而言，人们仍设法避免了破产。（[Immergut, 1992](https://www.sowi.hu-berlin.de/de/lehrbereiche/comppol/pubb/pdfs/Immergut1992a.pdf)）。理想情况下，我们会寻找 mandatory insurance 被 introduced 前后的 statistics，~但那些很难找到~。

在 Google Scholar 上考古一番后，我找到了一些 US 在 pre-Social Security period 的 statistics；我认为，比起 Scott 的说法，这些更能 confirms 我的解释：即使没有 State intervention，elderly 也过得不错。

首先，[Weaver (1987)](http://object.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/serials/files/cato-journal/1987/11/cj7n2-15.pdf) explains 了在 State 开始照顾老年人之前，社会如何照顾他们。如上所述，当时已有几种 mechanisms，包括 savings、family care、各种 insurance、pensions 和 charity，而 Social Security 出现的 reason 并不是 systemic problem，而是 Great Depression 给 poor 带来的严酷境况。Social Security 并未作为一套 emergency measures，而是被 instituted 为 permanent，随后发展为如今的样子（[Miron and Neil, 1998](http://www.nber.org/chapters/c6897.pdf)）。一些人甚至 argue，这些 institutions 不仅足够好，而且是 economically optimal 的（[Emery, 2010](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0014498309000345)）

他们也比现在工作到更晚的年龄。但我们也知道，在 Social Security 出现之前的几十年里，retirement age 就一直在 going down，并在它出现之后继续以同样的 pace going down（[Lee, 2000](http://hosting03.snu.ac.kr/%7Echullee/pub/aging_us/jpe14.pdf)a）

那些确实 endorse public interventions 的 Papers，例如 [Valdés-Prieto (2004)](http://web.worldbank.org/archive/website00894A/WEB/PDF/SVALDE-2.PDF)，更偏 theoretical 而非 empirical，也没有 take into account [Carter and Sutch (1995)](https://core.ac.uk/download/files/153/6894713.pdf) 或 [Gratton (1997)](http://psychsocgerontology.oxfordjournals.org/content/52B/3/S117.full.pdf) 这样的 historical evidence；后者 shows，Social Security Administration 为了促使 legislation passed，从而 strengthen 其作用，overreported 了 elderly poverty。

早先关于 Social Security 的 scholarship 确实表明，elderly 当时处于 impoverished 状态，但如今的 consensus view 是，他们并非如此，尽管仍存在一些 disagreements（[Lee, 2000](http://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/viewdoc/download?doi=10.1.1.195.69&rep=rep1&type=pdf)b）。

所以

> 在 convincing evidence proves otherwise 之前，我们应当 assume，二十世纪初的大多数 older people 按当时的 standards 衡量过得相对不错，elderly 的 economic well-being 在这一时期有所 improved，而且 younger people 预期能够在 old age 实现 economic security。鉴于这些 findings，建立一个致力于向 aged 提供 generous transfers 的 welfare state，就成了一件非常令人费解的事。economic evidence 中没有任何内容 substantiates，elderly 相对于 U.S. society 的其他某个 group 存在 overwhelming need。事实上，到 1930s 后期，SSB bureaucrats 非常清楚，children 比 aged 更有可能处于 impoverished 状态。那么，Social Security 的 source 究竟是什么？Weaver 的 political economy approach identifies 出了一个意图 expansion social security program 中 old age insurance title 的 state bureaucracy。（[Gratton,  1996](http://econpapers.repec.org/article/cupjechis/v_3a56_3ay_3a1996_3ai_3a01_3ap_3a39-61_5f01.htm)）

**3.1.1 人们的 preferences 是 complex 的，而且在他们 act 时，并不会（fully）revealed。**

好。

**3.2 许多 choices 实际上并不是 choices，而是 spontaneous、unthoughtful、并不 conform to rational principles 的 reactions。将这些 irrationalities 考虑在内的 abilities 存在 unequal distribution：并非每个人都是 perfect reasoner übermensch，因此某种 paternalism 是 justified 的。**

**4\. Lack of information（信息缺乏）**

**4.1 lack of information 是什么意思？**

不存在 perfect information，acquiring 它是 costly 的。人们通常希望获得一些有关 safety、efficacy，以及 product 如何 produced 所涉及的 ethics 的 information。Governments 可以听取这些 demands 并 enforce 它们，而 markets 可以通过直接 catering to consumer's demands 来做到这一点。

**5. Just Desserts 和 Social Mobility（应得与社会流动）**

**5.1 rich 的 Children 更有可能是 rich，而 poor 的 children 更有可能是 poor**

确实如此，但程度或许没有这份 FAQ 所说的那么大。如果 intergenerational income elasticity 大致意味着有多少 [mobility](https://randomcriticalanalysis.wordpress.com/2015/06/07/understanding-socioeconomic-mobility/)，那么 Denmark 的 mobile 程度就是其 _three times_。如果不 measuring intergenerational income mobility，而是 measure rank-mobility（parents 所处的 quartile 与 child 最终所处的 quartile 之间的 Correlation），那么 US 的 upward social mobility 处于平均水平（而 downward mobility 更大），与 Europe 相当（[Corak et al. 2014](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927537114000530)）。如果你转而关注有多少 born poor 的人 stay poor，[results 确实 show](http://www.keepeek.com/Digital-Asset-Management/oecd/social-issues-migration-health/growing-unequal_9789264044197-en#page172)，US 的 mobile 程度较低：约 66.6%（或按另一种 measure，为 58.2%）的 poor 仍然 poor，而 Denmark 的这一数字是 60.5%（Compare：UK 为 62.5%，Finland 为 71.9%，Australia 为 62.5%）。如果再使用 transition matrices，你又会得到不同的 results，再次 confirm US 的 mobile 程度较低，但并没有 elasticities 所暗示的那么低：US 比 Denmark 少 10% 的 mobile 程度，而不是 300%。不过，这并不是唯一的问题：在 US 处于 poor 状态，比在 Denmark 处于 poor 状态更糟。

不过，US 并不是 libertarian 天堂，因此这对这场 debate 几乎没有帮助。不如将 Nordics 与 Switzerland、Singapore、Hong Kong 或 New Zeland 进行 compare。从 Rawlsian point of view 来看，你可能宁愿出生在这些国家之一，而不是一个 Social Democracy。举例来说，如果你按 age group 来 measure 2013 年的 [Eurostat's material deprivation rate](http://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/income-and-living-conditions/data/main-tables)，你会得到：Denmark 有 8.8% 的人属于 poor，而 Switzerland 为 _3.7%_，Germany 为 11.6%，Finland 为 8.5%，Sweden 为 4.3%，Luxembourg 为 5.6%，Norway 为 4.8%，UK 为 17.4%，Spain 为 16.9%。诚然，这些国家都有进行 redistribution 的 State，但它们的 sizes 差异极大。给定一个 desired outcome，为 achieve 它所 required 的 coercion 越少越好，这似乎是合理的。

话虽如此，我不知道有哪位 academic libertarian philosopher 会谈论这种 moocher/productive distinction，或认为 hard work 会使你 deserve 某些东西。

US 也是一个 argument，说明让 State spend 更多，并不 necessarily translate into 对 poor 而言更好的 outcomes：[US](http://www.nytimes.com/roomfordebate/2015/10/20/can-the-us-become-denmark/us-social-spending-is-already-on-par-with-scandinavia) [有一个更大的 welfare state](https://www.aei.org/publication/the-us-welfare-state-and-safety-net-are-bigger-than-you-think-but-who-are-they-helping/)[，相比 Denmark 而言](http://www.nber.org/papers/w15982)，如果你将 tax breaks 和 [其他因素](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2629585) account for 的话。问题出在哪里？Denmark 将重点[更多放在 poor 身上](http://lanekenworthy.net/2010/06/07/social-spending-and-poverty/)，而 US 更多放在 middle class 身上。

总之：Nordics 很好，但并不 optimal，也不构成反对 libertarianism 的 argument。一些 [libertarian authors](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/10/is-the-us-the-most-economically-libertarian-country/) [实际上](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2015/10/denmark-libertarian-welfare-state/) [argue 说](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/05/the-administrative-state-vs-the-social-insurance-state/)，Nordics 比 US 本身更加 libertarian！

**5.1.1 但 progressives 中的 conventional knowledge 并非如此！**

这是因为它基于不同的 methodology：measuring intergenerational elasticities of income。我们可以问不同的问题：poor parents 的 children 在 Denmark 是否比在 US 有更好的 prospects？US 的 children 与 parent income 之间的 correlation 是否比 Denmark 更高？在 US 还是在 Denmark 更容易 escape poverty？从 Welfare-wise 来看，应该问的是第一个问题。而 prima facie，他们在 Denmark 确实过得更好，但这不是因为更低的 intergenerational income elasticity，而是因为 freer markets，以及一个 designed 得更好的 welfare State。

你现在可能会问：好吧，如果你认为 Nordics 相比 US 如此 libertarian，那么假如你有一个魔法按钮，能将 US 变成一个巨型版本的 Denmark（Copying Denmark 所能提供的最好的东西，使最终结果兼具两者的优点），你会按下它吗？我的回答是会，我会按。我也会优先按下一个类似的、将 US 变成 Switzerland 的按钮，而不是 Denmark 按钮；也会优先按下 minarchist 按钮，而不是 Switzerland 按钮。而如果我们 ex-ante 确信它会像其 theorist 所说的那样 works，那么 anarchy 按钮就排在 ranking 的最前面。

**5.1.2 genetics，以及 parents 教授更好的 habits 等因素，可以 explain wealth gap 的一部分，而不只是 parents 将钱交给 children**

确实如此

**5.1.3 Social mobility 是一种 political choice，而不是既定的东西。人们是 naturally unequal 的。**

确实如此，但前面展示的 data 表明，从 welfare-wise 来看，US 相比 Europe 并没有做得 _那么_ 差。此外，有些 income elasticities 比 US 更低的国家，其 markets 比 US 更 free（按 Heritage's Index of Economic Freedom 来 measured），尤其是在 discount welfare State 的情况下。你可以同时拥有 free market 和 welfare State，也可以拥有 less free market 和更少的 welfare，使前者整体上比后者 freer，具体取决于 index 是如何 constructed 的。

**5.1.3.1 government 可以 improve social mobility**

Scott 引用了 [Mayer & Loppo (2008)](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S004727270700059X)。好吧，也许如此。上面展示的 Data 对此提出了一些疑问，但 prima facie，welfare state 会 improve poor 的 prospects，这一点非常 plausible。（改善多少，10%？500%？）

但从长远来看，它可能产生 perverse effect，例如对使人们保持在 poverty 中的那些 behaviours 进行 [incentivising](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/miscellaneous/a-review-of-adam-perkinss-the-welfare-trait/)。这不是因为 poor 的 poverty 而 blaming 他们，只是在 recognising 一种可能的 cause and effect relation。再者，如果 [Gregory Clark](https://www.theguardian.com/commentisfree/2015/feb/04/social-mobility-equality-class-society) 是对的，那么 social mobility（作广义理解）或多或少是 fixed 的，因此你只能以 improve poor 的 conditions 为目标。

**5.3 Therefore，如果 success 是 externally determined 的，就应 redistribution。**

等等，为什么？

我认为这份 FAQ 有着与 Rawls 相同的问题：他指出，没有人 deserves 任何东西（大致如此），然后由此得出，一切都可以任人取用。正如我在[另一篇文章](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/02/01/rawls-redistribucion-de-organos-e-ingenieria-psiquica/)中 argued 的那样，这个 argument 原则上也 licenses organ redistribution，以及 thought-engineering，以 ensure 人们无论愿不愿意，都会以某种方式 act，成为致力于建立一个使 worse off 变得 better off 的 society 的人。

以下是 Rawlsian view：

> 由于我们是谁以及我们做什么，大部分都受到 undeserved native endowments 和我们出生时所处的 undeserved circumstances 的极大 influenced，因此一个人不能 deserve 任何东西，或者，充其量只能 deserve 很少的东西。按照一种常见的 interpretation，Rawls 认为 desert 不应在 distributive justice 中发挥任何 role，因为这些 undeserved factors 对所有可能的 desert bases 都有重大 influence (Sher 1987, 22 ff)。（[IEP](http://www.iep.utm.edu/desert/)）

问题不在于 rejecting desert。我基本上也 reject 它。问题在于说 desert 是 entitlement 的 precondition，而这需要一个 argument。

我认为，Rejection of desert 或 merit 是当今的 mainstream philosophical view，其中也包括 libertarian academics。即使并非如此，libertarians 中的 mainstream view 也是 desert 与 entitlement 之间没有 connection。Robert Nozick 曾有这样一段著名的话：

> 如果一个 entitlement system 下的大部分 transfers 都是出于 reasons 而进行的，我们在 upholding 它的 justice 时会感到更加安心。这并不 necessarily 意味着所有人都 deserve 他们收到的 holdings。$$…$$ 我们前面指出过，关于 holdings 的 entitlement conception of justice 并不是一种 patterned conception of justice，因此也不接受 distribution in accordance with moral desert。任何人都可以将自己 is entitled to 的任何 holding 给予其他任何人，而不取决于 recipient 是否 morally deserves 成为 recipient。（[Anarchy, State, and Utopia](https://www.amazon.com/Anarchy-State-Utopia-Robert-Nozick/dp/0465051006)）

[Hayek and Milton Friedman](https://andrewlister.wordpress.com/2014/05/12/mankiw-on-just-deserts/) 也 rejected 这一点。

当然，有些 libertarians 接受 desert 和 merit 的 notion，例如 [Bryan Caplan](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2012/06/the_meritocracy.html)，但他们并不将 desert 作为其为 free markets 所作的 justification 的核心。

这种错误就是我所称的 Unjust World Fallacy。将著名的 Just World Fallacy 应用于这个例子，它会说：1. Poor people deserve 处于 poor 状态 2. 如果他们 deserve 如此，那么他们处于 poor 状态就是 just 的 3. Therefore，poor 处于 poor 状态是 just 的。

Unjust World Fallacy 则是：1. Poor people 不 deserve 处于 poor 状态 2. 如果他们不 deserve 如此，那么他们处于 poor 状态就是 unjust 的 3. Therefore，poor 处于 poor 状态是 unjust 的。

这两个 second premises 都是 mistaken 的，因为 justice 与 desert 之间没有 connection。从 lack of desert 推到 lack of entitlement 以及 redistribution 的 need，还需要 Additional arguments。

话虽如此，你可以主张存在帮助 the poor 的一般 moral duty，但不是因为他们 deserve 这种帮助，而只是因为帮助别人是 good，而且 doing more good 比 doing less good 更好，所以，如果你打算投入 resources 帮助别人，那么以 the poor 为对象，就能完成大量帮助。

**7\. Government 的 Competence**

**7.1 Government 让一个人登上了月球**

我这样说，是因为我本人尤其热爱 space 和 rocketry，也把登月视为人类最伟大的成就之一。

**7.1.1 如果这还不够，governments 还给我们带来了：全球范围内的 smallpox 和 polio eradication。US 的 Cholera 和 malaria eradication，computer、mouse、digital camera 和 email。internet，以及 regular highways。整个大陆的 Clean、free water 和 electricity。Forcing integration，以及 leading the struggle for civil rights。GPS。针对一系列 disasters 的 Accurate disaster forecasts。没有 bank runs。Nuclear power 和 game theory。**

-   Smallpox 和 polio eradication。Smallpox 这一项可以。Polio eradication 是一项 [public-private effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliomyelitis_eradication)。其他 illnesses 也曾被 nongovernmental-led efforts 针对并 successfully reduced（Dracunculiasis、Hookworm ）。目前针对 malaria 的 efforts 是 public-private 的。

-   Electricity：同上。

-   GPS：可以
-   Disaster forecasts：可以。Governments 从一开始就在从事 satellite business，而与 free services competing 是困难的。

-   Game theory，为的是避免 misusing 它。Game theory 由 von Neumann 创立，没有 government intervention。他 invented 它之后，government 才聘请他继续工作。

无论我上面说了什么，重要的是 private sector 是否也会想出那些 inventions 和 advances，而我看不出有什么理由认为不会。然后我们可以逐个案例讨论，market process 要 achieve 它们，会多花多少时间。

此外，想想 governments [做错](https://www.hawaii.edu/powerkills/MURDER.HTM)的许多事情，以及本来可能做错的事情（nuclear war）。就眼下而言，想想 US 的 over-regulation 和 excessive defense spending。

**7.1.1.1 Governments 带来了 peace，另外还有一些链接**

也许是 [guns](http://economics.wm.edu/wp/cwm_wp158.pdf) 带来的。或者是 [moral progress](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs11098-015-0588-9)。我不认为 Hobbesian logic 能成立（解释可参见 Huemer 的书）。至于那些链接，我就不逐一 debunking 了；我不认为在这里这样做会为这份 FAQ 增添多少实质内容。

**7.2 根据一项 study，Large government projects 并不比 private 的 projects 更 bloated 或 over-budget**

Scott 链接了一篇 studies transport infrastructure 的 costs 的 paper。这里的 critique 不是说 project building 会更 expensive，而是说，相比 market，它会更不 economically rational。

此外，我想强调，这项 study 适用于 transport infrastructure，而非一般意义上的 large projects。

**7.3 世界上一些最伟大的 companies 是或曾经是 state-run 的，比如 JNR 或 BBC。有时，这些 corporations 被设立，是为了 provide market 不会 provide 的 goods**

好吧。我不会深入讨论 Amtrak 或 USPO，但我认为很难以此为依据 defend 它们。如果 small urban concentrations 的 mail deliveries 有更多 delays 才更 efficient，那就这样吧。这也会成为促使他们搬到 larger cities 的 Incentive。Amtrak 也应该走同样的路。如果人们确实想要 passenger transport，他们会为此付费。Amtrak 的 problems 的一个 cause，可能是 public highways 的 oversupply。

**7.3.1 government 取得的 Advances 包括上面提到的那些，还有 radar、jet engine、satellites、fiber optics、artificial limbs 和 nuclear energy，也包括 government institutions 中取得的 advances。许多 market innovations 来自 de-facto state-owned monopolies，比如 Bell Labs。Apple 做的是 consumer packaging。De novo invention 来自负担得起 basic research 的 very large organizations。**

我已经评论过其中一些 advances。

-   Jet engines：我在[这里](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/08/28/is-war-necessary-for-economic-growth-a-review-and-some-aviation-history-love/)和[这里](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/02/09/aircraft-progress-before-and-after-world-war-ii/)写过。并非由 government invented，但在 invention 之后由它 improved。
-   Radar：并非由 government [invented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_H%C3%BClsmeyer)，但此后它对其 independently discovered 并 improved
-   Satellites：可以
-   Fiber optics：关键 achievement 由 [Corning Glass Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_fiber#History) 取得。Government 在这里的作用相对较小。
-   Artificial limbs：近些年来（post WWII），government 在这里做了大量工作。但在那之前，这个领域就已经有人在工作。
-   Nuclear energy：可以

与 Scott——以及 Schumpeter——的看法相反，innovation 并不只发生在 shielded from competition 的 large companies 中。并不存在从 more concentrated markets 指向 more innovative markets 的 causality。其他 researchers 发现了一种 U relationship，其中 largest 和 smallest firms 是 most innovative 的（[Cohen, 2010](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016972181001004X) \[sec 2.3\]）。如果我们用 R&D Magazine's 100 Awards 作为 proxy，就会发现大多数 innovations [来自 private sector](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/02/03/where-do-innovations-really-come-from/)，而且与过去不同，它们通常来自 small companies。取得 breakthrough _discoveries_（而非 inventions）的地方，是 basic-science research centers，而这些大多是 universities，还有，是的，big corporations。universities 获得了大量 government funded 支持。但它们并不必然需要如此。

关于 Apple，Scott 说的是真的。他没有声称 US government 要为 iPhone 负责，但确实有人这样说。[这里](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/08/21/mazzucato-and-the-iphone-ii-the-myth-of-the-entrepreneurial-state/)有一篇对此的回应。

**7.4 许多 government programs 似乎是 failures，这种看法基于 biased perception。**

好吧。Negativity bias 很强。同样的事情也解释了为什么有些人认为世界正在变得更糟。

**7.4.1 governments 做的许多事情不为人知，所以它的 contributions 被 understated 了**

好吧

**7.4.2 是的，有时 regulations 并不 cause better outcomes，在它们出现之前，事情就已经在改善。但要公平、一致地运用你的 skepticism。并不是 government 碰到什么就 destroys 什么；不过，即便它碰到的事情进展顺利，也可能并不是 government 的功劳。**

好吧

**7.4.3 Regulations foster social norms，比如 wearing seatbelts；如果没有这样的事情，lifesaving regulations 或许会更少**

也许吧。但要考虑到，mandatory seatbelts encourage less careful driving，因而造成更多 deaths。mandatory seatbelts 所 saved 的 Lives [may equal](http://cafehayek.com/2006/11/what_peltzman_f.html)它们造成的 lives lost。[Nonprofits](http://www.pulseprotects.com/the-national-safety-council/) 也可以发挥这种 norm creating 作用。

此外，在它们被规定为 mandatory 之前，rates of usage 就已经[在上升](http://john-adams.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2006/failure%20of%20seatbelt%20legislation.pdf)。

**8\. Healthcare（医疗保健）**

**8.1 Government-run health systems 比 private health systems 表现更好，而且更便宜。Sweden、France、Canada、UK（single payer）对比 US（private）。Public systems 在 life expectancy、infant mortality 和 cancer 方面胜出。**

我 reject 这个 fundamental premise：US 并没有 private healthcare system。或者，如果我们接受它有，那么它是 private 的，其含义就像说 Federal Reserve 是一家 private bank 一样。US healthcare system 的相当一部分是 privately owned，这是一回事。那个 sector 受到的 regulation 的 degree，以及这对它的行为产生的 effects，则是另一回事。我将在本节解释这一点。

另一个可以提出的 criticism 是，[populations 并不相同](https://randomcriticalanalysis.wordpress.com/2014/11/29/some-other-issues-with-comparing-us-healthcare-costs-and-so-called-outcomes/)，而这会使 FAQ 所呈现的 variables 上的 outcome comparisons 产生 biases。更多内容见[这里](http://www.econtalk.org/archives/2012/07/scott_atlas_on.html)。

据我所知，没有人为目前这样的 US healthcare system 辩护。这并不是因为某种隐含的 no-true-Scotsman fallacy：libertarians criticise US healthcare reason，并 propose measures 来 fix 它，是有理由的：

-   [Tax-subsidized](http://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2010/02/henry-aaron-writes-to-me.html) 的 [health insurance，它](http://mercatus.org/publication/tax-exemption-employer-provided-health-insurance)会 [distorts the market](http://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2012/12/04/166434247/the-huge-and-rarely-discussed-health-insurance-tax-break)
-   Medicare、Medicaid 等（[total spending 的 45%](https://www.cms.gov/research-statistics-data-and-systems/statistics-trends-and-reports/nationalhealthexpenddata/downloads/highlights.pdf)）
-   以及[其他因素](http://mercatus.org/publication/american-health-care-system-principles-successful-reform)

简而言之，US healthcare 的 problem 是：在 healthcare system 中，应该有人负责 rationing。在 US，却没有人这样做。与 Europe 不同，US healthcare system 的 public 部分确实因此 contribute to price inflation。

关于 healthcare，以及其中的 pure free market 会如何 work，可以说的还有很多；但这是一篇 Non-Non-Libertarian FAQ，不是为任何特定 position 提供 full defense 的地方。

**8.2 Government-run healthcare 比 market healthcare 更 efficient：Blue Cross 的 example，以及 spending vs outcomes 的 chart**

关于 Blue Cross 的 claim 有点难以 evaluate。FAQ 所链接的 chart 没有 source，不过通过一些 googling，我们找到了一本 [Peter Drucker](https://books.google.com/books?id=8S8sBgAAQBAJ&pg=PA34&lpg=PA34&dq=peter+drucker+blue+cross+canada&source=bl&ots=rGpE-z-12p&sig=8fpTpFdyCmkuGwy9Fhd9tDvK7uc&hl=es&sa=X&ved=0ahUKEwiVyvfu3KnLAhWErRoKHSbkA1QQ6AEIKDAC#v=onepage&q=peter%20drucker%20blue%20cross%20canada&f=false) 的书。Drucker 没有 cite 那些 numbers 的 source。但 [Massachusetts 的 Blue Cross](http://annual-report-2015.bluecrossma.com/?waid=null) covers 约 2.8 million 人，而 Canadian Health system 则是 27 million 人。[Health Canada](http://www.tbs-sct.gc.ca/psm-fpfm/modernizing-modernisation/stats/ssa-pop-eng.asp) 有 10403 名 employees，[而 BC 有 3600 名](https://web.archive.org/web/20110719231356/https://www.bluecrossma.com/visitor/about-us/just-facts.html)。因此，这个 claim 是 false。接下来或许有人会 argue，Canada 的 people/employee ratio 更好；对此，我会回答说，economies of scale 以及其他 factors 也在发挥作用。

由于 8.1 所述的理由，这里呈现的 chart 并不 fair。相反，你可以 [compare healthcare 的 expenditures 占 GDP 的 share 与 GDP](https://randomcriticalanalysis.wordpress.com/2014/11/24/national-healthcare-expenditure-united-states-versus-other-countries-the-us-is-not-really-an-outlier/)，并发现，取决于你如何 measure，US 并不是那么突出的 outlier（另见[这里](https://www.google.com/url?sa=t&rct=j&q=&esrc=s&source=web&cd=1&cad=rja&uact=8&ved=0ahUKEwi-0JPek6rLAhXMfxoKHWM4CMkQFggfMAA&url=http%3A%2F%2Fwww.mckinsey.com%2Finsights%2Fhealth-systems-and-services%2Faccounting-for-the-cost-of-us-health-care&usg=AFQjCNF1pPPXPkLl7bvwcUFJetUGwoDLZA&sig2=cSx44F3YcPgilLKR1Pm6DQ&bvm=bv.116274245,d.d2s)）；此外，single-payer systems 中的 costs 也一直在 increasing（某些情况下不像 US 那么多）。不过，正如我稍后会说的，这是可以预期的。

**8.3 Waiting list 不是什么大问题。Health 必须被 rationed。在 private system 中，price system 承担这个作用，但它有一个 disadvantage：如果人们想要的 healthcare 超过他们真正 need 的程度，prices 就会 spiral up。相比之下，在 public systems 中，allocation 是 based according to need。**

如果人们想 see a doctor 的次数超过他们所“need”的次数，那只是 demand 的 increase，FAQ 所描述的 mechanism 仍会同样有效地 work。如果这个 system excludes 大量 poor people，就会有 strong incentives 去寻找 alternatives，以 serve 那个 market。

对此我没有意见

**8.5 Government healthcare 因为 economies of scale 而更 effective，而且人们是 irrational，也不知道自己 need 什么 healthcare。此外，for-profit hospitals 的 outcomes 比 non-for profit hospitals 更差。**

Economies of scale 与此没有多大关系，除了这一点：如果 governments 是 single buyer（即 monopsony），就能向 companies 强加它们的 dictates。在某个 threshold 以上，insurance pool 通过增加人数所带来的 fixed-cost-sharing 很少，而且 insurance pools 始终可以选择为自己 [reinsure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinsurance)。

关于 profits/nonprofit hospitals：幸运的是，private [US hospitals](http://www.aha.org/research/rc/stat-studies/fast-facts.shtml) 中的 75% 是 non-profits（注意，该 study 并没有 compare private v public，但[这项研究有](http://blogs.sph.harvard.edu/ashish-jha/2012/08/20/profits-quality-and-u-s-hospitals/)）。这些 results 并不 robust（它们取决于 regions 和 time periods），而且 public hospitals 显然不如 private non-profits（[Eggleston et al., 2008](http://www.ps4h.org/docs12_own/Eggleston.pdf) [Koning et al. 2007](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1007%2Fs10645-007-9063-0)）。这里的 problem 也不是 profit motive：non-profit hospitals 的“owners”确实会赚取“profits”，方式是支付 [higher salaries](http://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/cwc/wages-in-profit-and-nonprofit-hospitals-and-universities.pdf)，支付给他们的 [boardmembers](http://www.healthbeatblog.com/2011/03/high-ceo-salaries-at-nonprofit-hospitals-under-scrutinyonce-again/)（以及其他 workers）

如果你想看看更接近 libertarians 所设想的 system 的东西，可以看看 [Singapore](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2008/05/quality_health.html)。是的，那里存在大量 [government intervention](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2008/01/singapores_heal.html)，但 libertarians 会 argue，这个 system 的运作方式完全可以通过 market mechanism 来 replicated。另请阅读 Caplan 关于 free market healthcare 的[这篇文章](http://econfaculty.gmu.edu/bcaplan/healthstate.htm)。

一个 healthy healthcare system 会有大量 out of pocket payments。Insurance 就应该是 insurance，只用于非常 rare、high-cost 的事情。我们甚至可以对 [public and out of pocket](http://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/social-issues-migration-health/out-of-pocket-expenditure-on-health_oopexphtl-table-en) payments 对 healthcare costs 的 impact 做一个简短的 analysis，以一篮子 hospital services 的 cost 来 measured（[Koechlin et al. 2010](http://www.oecd-ilibrary.org/social-issues-migration-health/comparing-price-levels-of-hospital-services-across-countries_5km91p4f3rzw-en%3Fcrawler%3Dtrue)）。如果我们对一系列 countries（Australia、Canada、Finland、France、Italy、Israel、Korea、Portugal、Slovenia、Sweden 和 US）进行这个 analysis，就会得到这些 charts（orange line 是 excluding US 的 fit）：

我们也可以看看 India 的 healthcare system。你可能会问，为什么？

在许多 poor countries，healthcare 大多是 private（[Forsberg et al., 2011](http://heapol.oxfordjournals.org/content/26/suppl_1/i1.long)）。India 尤其值得关注，因为其 private healthcare system 目前正在出现一些 innovations，而且人们更喜欢它，而不是那个 quality 较低、但 free 的 public system（[Das et al. 2015](http://www-wds.worldbank.org/external/default/WDSContentServer/WDSP/IB/2015/06/23/090224b082f7d787/1_0/Rendered/PDF/Quality0and0ac0e0providers0in0India.pdf)）。

这些 revolution 已经发展到这样的程度：一些 Indian hospitals 提供 [western-level quality](https://hbr.org/2013/11/delivering-world-class-health-care-affordably) 的 care，而 [cost 仅为其一小部分](http://www.forbes.com/sites/johngoodman/2016/03/02/better-care-at-one-tenth-the-cost/#1cad5d0a2fa9)，这是通过 process innovations 和 technological innovations 两者实现的（[PSP4H, 2014](http://www.psp4h.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Understanding-the-India-Low-Cost-Model-of-Healthcare-Delivery-3.pdf)）；与此同时，它们还利用较富裕 clients 支付的 fees 来 subsidise 较贫穷的 clients，从而帮助 poor。

诚然，India 不是 wealthy country，而且许多 poor people 因 health expenses 而 become indebted。但正是这种 situation spurred healthcare sector 的 innovation，而且这种 poverty situation 不会永远持续。Health outcomes 正在以良好的 pace improving（[Joumard et al., 2015](http://www.oecd.org/officialdocuments/publicdisplaydocumentpdf/?cote=ECO/WKP\(2015\)2&docLanguage=En)）。正如你可以从我的 references 中看到的，这个 system 并不 perfect。但它难道不是比你预期的 work 得更好吗？现在，想象一下这种情况出现在一个更 wealthy 的 country！

**9\. Prison Privatization（监狱私有化）**

**9.1 Private prisons 比 regular prisons 更差。它们还有 incentive 去 lobby，争取 stricter sentencing，以获得更多 prisoners，从而获得更多 income。**

这方面我不清楚。不过，US 的 private prisons 更像是 [“contracted-out”](http://www.economist.com/blogs/democracyinamerica/2010/08/private_prisons) 的 prisons，而不是 [privatised 的 prisons](http://www.slate.com/blogs/moneybox/2012/06/22/contracting_out_vs_privatizing_.html)。似乎也有[人](http://reason.org/files/d14ffa18290a9aeb969d1a6c1a9ff935.pdf)为它们 [have evidence](http://grad.mercatus.org/publication/book-review-changing-guard-private-prisons-and-control-crime-edited-alexander-tabarrok)。在 ancient Athens，它们 work 得并没有那么差（[D'Amico 2009](http://www.danieljdamico.com/DanDamico/CV_and_Publications_files/prison%20public%20choice%20proof.pdf)）。你也可以有 charity-run prisons（[Skarbek 2014](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ecaf.12089/abstract)）。

**9.2 libertarians 不相信 private prisons 也没关系**

如果 private 的那种最终确实很差，Minarchists 对这两种类型都能接受。Anarchist libertarians 如果要实行 imprisonment，就需要 private prisons。我认为，大多数 theorists 倾向于反对 imprisoning people，而是让他们 work，generate money，以 compensate 那些他们曾 aggressed against 的人。又或者，security agencies 会付钱，让 prisoners 待在那里。

**10\. Gun control（枪支管制）**

\*\*10.1 也许应该允许 guns，但应该设置一些 restrictions \*\*

确实如此。如何让 nuclear weapons 以及类似的东西不出现在街头？对 minarchists 来说，这很 trivial：拥有什么 weapons 才是 reasonable，可以由 State 来 decided。对 anarchists 来说，这确实困难得多。solution 必须是由 court system 来 ban unreasonable weapons，而且由于大多数人会 favour 这样的 measure，它很可能会被 implemented。

**11\. Education（教育）**

**11.1 但 public schools 比 private 的学校 do better，而且我们没有 public education 兴起之前的 statistics。**

幸运的是，我们确实有这样的 statistics。UK 的见（[West, 1978](http://www.jstor.org/stable/2598759?seq=1#page_scan_tab_contents)），France 的见（[Blum et al., 1985](http://www.persee.fr/doc/pop_0032-4663_1985_num_40_6_17572)）。提醒一下：Public education 在这两个 countries 都是大约从 1870 年开始的。

![2016-03-03 15-10-56 的屏幕截图](https://nintil.com/old_assets/2016/03/24-5.png) Illiteracy rate（文盲率）

此外，与 private schools 相比，government sponsored public education 在 underdeveloped countries 是一场惨重的 failure（[Tooley, 2005](http://object.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/pubs/pdf/tooley.pdf)）。

Finland 的情况：与 Spain 相比，Finland 的 per student 支出多 5%，而 PISA ratings 的 difference 是 40 points。然而，如果你 compare Finnish public 与 Spanish private schools 的 SES-adjusted ratings，Finland 与 Spain 之间的 difference 就只有 10 points（Finland 占优）。更公平的 comparison 应当涉及 Finland 本国的 students，但那里没有可供这种 comparison 的 private schools。如果你将 Macao 和 Hong Kong（>90% 在 private schools）与 Finland 进行 compare，这些国家在 PISA 中胜过这个 Nordic country（500 vs Finland 的 485-478）。

**11.2 Libertarians 相信 equality of opportunity，但这需要 regulating schools**

如果你指的是 mandated equality，我认为他们并不相信它。如上所述，genes 和 external factors 在很大程度上 determine 你的人生 chances。By default，并不存在 equality of opportunity 或 results 的 equality。

对于 Scott 讨论的 extreme cases，在 Libertarialand，某些 'regulation' 作为 avoid fraud 的 means 会是 justified。Offering 听起来像 educational services、却并非如此的东西，就像售卖烂苹果。问题是，对有些人来说，这可能还不够。如果你想要 full equality of opportunity，你就不仅需要 equalize schools，还需要 equalize food intake、parents、universities、friends，以及你一生中遇到的几乎一切。这在某种程度上是 doable，尤其是未来 cheap genetic selection 出现之后，但 philosophical question 依然存在：coerce 他人 accept 这些更 extreme 的 measures，或者 coerce 他人以获取 resources 来 implement 上述 measures，是否是 justified。

**11.3 government 应当 protect children，使其免受 parents 的伤害**

当然。但这并不是什么“特殊”的事情。Children 拥有 rights，protect 他们是 right：任何能够做到这一点的 properly structured organisation 都可以。mistreat 一个 child，甚至在考虑 parents 所拥有的 resources 后，仍不向其提供 adequate resources，都是对这个 child 的 rights 的 violation。关于 libertarianism 是否实际上出于 justice 而[要求](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/07/libertarianism-and-parental-licensing/)对生育 children 的人实行 [licensing parents](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2011/12/licensing-parents-2/)，存在一些有趣的 debates。

### Part D：Moral Issues（道德问题）

\*\*这个 Argument： \*\*_仅以 avoiding force 和 respecting rights 为基础的 Moral systems 是 incomplete、inelegant、counterintuitive，而且通常充斥着 logical fallacies。一个更 sophisticated 的 moral system，即 consequentialism，会 generates natural rights 和 non-initiation of violence 的 principles，将其作为可用于 solve coordination problems 的 heuristics，但也会详细说明在哪些 situations 下，这些 heuristics 不再 apply。government intervention 的许多 cases 就属于这种 situations，因此可能是 moral。_

**这个 Counterargument：\_ \_**_Consequentialism 存在与 absolute rights theories 相同的 problems，而且有时会 lead 到甚至更加 unintuitive 的 situations。时至今日，还没有一个 fully coherent ethical system 能在任何给定 circumstance 下 make decisions，但我们可以依靠 reflective equilibrium，使我们的 ethical intuitions 与 ethical principles 达到 cohere。_

**12\. Moral Systems（道德体系）**

**12.1 Freedom 并不是 infinitely valuable**

确实不是。

**12.1.1 Franklin 的意思并不是你所理解的那样**

好吧

**12.2 Taxation 是 theft，没错，但所有人都接受这一点。这个 argument 唯一的 force 是 emotional**

我不知道是否所有人都接受这一点，但所有人都接受：一般来说不应进行 theft，而且，如果普通人做出 governments 所实施的那些特定种类的 theft，也会受到 criticised。人们认为 governments 拥有 political authority，因此才接受它。只要普通的 theft 遭到 rejected，反对 taxes 的 argument 就有 force，而普通的 theft 确实遭到 rejected。

从策略上说，声称 taxation 是 theft，或者 [non aggression principle](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2013/12/the-nap-isnt-a-knock-down-argument-for-libertarianism/) 是 true，并没有用，也不该这样做，[因为人们](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/06/loren-lomasky-taxation-is-not-theft/)不会把它[当作 starting point](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/06/more-on-lomasky-taxation-and-reasonable-disagreement/)。

**12.3 为什么针对 innocent people 的 initiation of force 应当是 wrong？**

大多数人会说这是 wrong，而且似乎显而易见。考虑这个 thought experiment：有人告诉你，一个 government 即将对一个 innocent person use force。你可以让这次 use of force 发生，也可以阻止它。大多数人会阻止这次 use of force。一个 consequentialist 在没有更多 information 的情况下，会是 indifferent，并通过抛硬币来决定。或者，她会认为，考虑到现实世界中的 governments，针对 innocent 的 use of violence 更有可能 lead 到 bad consequences，于是选择阻止它。如果考虑这样一个 government，这种回应就会被削弱一些：它位于遥远的外星球上，而且有人明确告诉你，你对它一无所知。

也可能出现这种情况：在获得更多 information 后，all things considered，对一个 innocent 使用 force 会是 right。你愿意打一位随机路人一拳，来换取人类未来的存续吗？大多数人会愿意。

Initiation of force 是 _prima facie_ wrong，在某些 cases 中也可能是 right，但 burden of proof 落在为其辩护的人身上。

**12.3.1 有时，use of force 会有 good consequences**

有时确实如此。但你需要的是这样的 occasions：use of force 有 good consequences，而且在该 circumstance 下是 justified。如果这个 argument 成功，它所 justify 的不会是今天所理解的 government，而是一个职能范围非常狭窄的 governance institution，而且只有在 overall institutional framework 优于 alternative 时才成立。

假设有两个孩子，会因一种 rare illness 而在一年内死亡。再假设有一个人，具有特殊的 genetic makeup，因此对这种 illness 有 resistance。Science 告诉我们，如果对这个人进行 experiments，就可以在一年内 produce 一种 cure，救下这两个孩子。在这里，consequentialist 的 recommendation 是 kidnap 这个人，并对他进行 experiment。其他所有人都会反对这种 kidnapping。Consequentialists 或许想借助 rule-consequentialism 来避免这一点。我会在后面的 sections 中对此作些评论。

**12.3.2 Non-consequentialism 是 arbitrary**

很少有人遵循 fully consequence-independent 的 theories。对他人的 Noninterference 似乎是一个非常 universal 的 principle，但这并不会引导你走向 consequentialism 或 utilitarianism。

arbitrariness 的 claim 也可以用来针对 consequentialism：为什么 harm 是 bad？为什么我们每个人都应当[同等地考虑所有人的 interests](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/05/why-not-utilitarianism/)？

归根结底，如果我们将 arbitrariness 理解为“从 false 或 dubious premises 出发”，就必须把注意力放到 ethics 上，并研究相关 premises。这超出了本 FAQ 的范围。

**12.3.2.1 Principle of Non-Aggresion 是一个用来挂上既有 prejudices 的钩子**

对有些人来说，或许确实如此。的确，人们相信类似 NAP 的东西，而一种 belief 被广泛持有，会使其在是 false 时也难以消除。这个 claim 也可以用来针对 political authority。

NAP 的某些 versions 可能需要 hardcore epicycling 才能起作用。但 utilitarians 在借助 rule utilitarianism，或者更改 utility 的 definition（pleasure、preferences、welfare 等）时，也在做同样的事。归根结底，大多数 moral systems 都会与某一种 strong intuition 冲突，这并不只是 utilitarianism 或 NAP 的问题。

话虽如此，full、hardcore exceptionless NAP 确实有其 defenders，但[并不是每一个](http://www.libertarianism.org/blog/six-reasons-libertarians-should-reject-non-aggression-principle) libertarian 都赞同 FAQ 所 criticises 的那个 version。

**12.3.2.1.1 支持 NAP 的 Arguments 是 weak**

许多确实如此，尤其是 Rothbard 和 Hoppe 的（[Eabrasu 2012](http://ppe.sagepub.com/content/12/3/288.abstract)）。但也许，构成一个人 personal projects 的东西，包括 body 在内，不应遭到 aggressed against，这个想法足够 plausible，足以被 self-evidently accepted（正如 harm 应当被 avoided 的想法）。这并没有 solve 以下 issue：哪些东西会成为 property，东西在多大 extent 上是 property，以及今天是 property 的东西明天是否仍然是 property。这正是你需要 legal system 的原因。Libertarians 接受 Lockean Proviso，因此所描述的 Tristan de Cunha 的 situation 不可能发生。

**12.3.2.1.2 这个 NAP 对 force 有一个奇怪的 definition，而且 libertarians tolerate 某些 initiations of force，这个 NAP 还需要一长串 exceptions 才能 work。**

Definitions 是 arbitrary 的。'Initiation of force' 被 defined 为 violating rights，而 rights 是按照 homesteading 和 contract 的 libertarian principles 产生的。

[有些](http://volokh.com/2011/02/15/asteroid-defense-and-libertarianism/) libertarians 不 tolerate 那些 exceptions，但必须承认，大多数人确实如此。通过 NAPping 来处理 ethics 的 Problems，几十年前就被 [David Friedman](http://www.daviddfriedman.com/Libertarian/Machinery_of_Freedom/MofF_Chapter_41.html) 指出，他随后受到了 [Block (2011)](http://libertarianpapers.org/articles/2011/lp-3-35.pdf) 的 criticised（我仍然赞同 Friedman）。Rights 在 theory 中不应该 conflict，如果它们在 practice 中确实如此，courts 就必须尽力 interpret 它们。这就是 courts 现在 work 的方式，只是涉及的 rights 不同。而 consequentialist considerations 也被允许在 determining 那些 hard cases 时发挥作用。

**12.4 Consequentialism 比这个 NAP 更好**

一般而言，人们并不是 implicit 或 full consequentialists。[即使 Scott Alexander 也不是](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/01/16/the-ethics-of-scott-alexander/)。即便是 self-declared consequentialists，也不会[像那样 behave](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2014/07/the_argument_fr_1.html)，尽管这样做是 [possible](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/sep/22/extreme-altruism-should-you-care-for-strangers-as-much-as-family) 的。Consequences 很重要，其他人也很重要。但并非 infinitely 重要。

此外，capitalism 并不是由 consequentialism driven 的，而是由对 entrepreneurial arbitrage opportunities 的 rights-bounded search driven 的。人们通常并不试图 unconstrainedly maximise 他们自己（或所有人）的 welfare，尽管 economists 将 firms model 为 profit-maximising，因为这与 reality 足够接近。此外，这个 FAQ 通常描绘的 consequentialism，不同于 ethical egoism，也就是做对你自己最好的事，不顾世界上的其他人。

**12.4.1 由你 decide 哪些 consequences 是 good 的**

（Consumerism 会告诉你？啊？）我认为，这里存在一种 confusion，将 economic decisionmaking 误当成了 ethical theorising。Decisions 有 opportunity costs，而我们面临 tradeoffs。当我们思考做什么才是 right 时，这些都必须、也确实被 factored in。

**12.4.2 如果你关心其他人，就在你的 ethical calculations 中将他们考虑在内**

大多数人确实如此，但那并不是一种 purely consequentialistic 的做法。此外，这种 reasoning 对 ethical egoists，或者不关心外国人的 national consequentialists，甚至对 family consequentialists 都是 impotent 的。或者……对 white supremacist consequentialists 也是如此。

**12.4.3 Consequentialism 不会变成 war of all vs all，因为人们会同意一套 rules 来避免这种情况**

是啊，但这 undermines 了这个 FAQ 前面的 reasoning。就我个人而言，如果其他人 refrains from maximising 他们的 goods，并让我 maximise 我的，那么我就在 maximising my good optimally。我有什么 incentive 去 bind myself？我并不 control 其他人，所以我可以 calculate 每个 decision 的 expected utility，并且照样选择 maximising my good。实际上，正如我前面所 explain 的，人们很可能会 coordinate，但这个 FAQ 对 overcoming anti-coordination incentives 相当持批评态度。

**12.4.4 那不会 lead to 像 slavery、decadence 或某种 dystopia 这样的 undesirable situations**

谁知道呢。也许那些东西确实 maximise utility。也许有些人会完全认为，从 long term-discounted-welfarewise 来看，right 的做法就是让 US conquer 世界，enslave sub-100 IQ 的人，并 coordinate global resources 来 wirehead humanity，从而获得一个充满 eternal brain stimulation-induced pleasure 的未来。（[Scott rejects 的某种情况](http://slatestarcodex.com/2013/04/08/whose-utilitarianism/)）。如果这样的事得到了 utilitarianism 的 endorsed，你会赞同它，还是将其视为一个 reductio？

**12.4.5 Consequentialism 是 morality 的 gold standard，但也许仅仅通过做我们认为有 good consequences 的事来尝试 implement 它，并不是一个好主意**

如上文所指出的，即使这个 FAQ 的作者 Scott 也并不真的相信这一点：他知道它仍然遗漏了一些东西。Scott 在这里暗示了 act/rule consequentialism distinction。在下一节，我将 criticise 它。

**13\. Rights 和 Heuristics**

**13.1 Rights 是 conclusions，而不是 premises。是 Heuristics。只有当它们 bring good consequences 时，才应该 supported 它们**

每个 argument 都从某些 premises 开始。Consequentialists 有他们的，deontologists 有他们的，virtue theorists 有他们的，等等。在 consequentialism 中，rights 是（或不是）conclusions。在 deontology 中，good consequences 是遵循正确的 rights structure 所 follow（或不 follow）的 conclusions。

像‘我不应该 kill people，除非在非常特殊的 circumstances 下’这样的想法，似乎比‘我应该 maximise aggregate utility’更 initially plausible。现在，你可能会要求一个关于 rights 的 unifying principle，以便从中 deduce 它们。没有人构建出一个 works perfectly 的 moral system，但 libertarians 可能会回答，self-ownership、这个 NAP，或者你正在 arguing 这一 fact 的 implications（Hoppe），就是这样的 axiom。但正如 academic libertarians 所指出的，一个人也可以通过 rejecting 那些 axioms，或者主张其 reasoning 是 fallacious 的来回应，不过同样的话也可以用来回应 consequentialists。关于 libertarian theorists 所依赖的几种 background philosophies，请参见[这份 list](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/02/26/liberalisms-overlapping-consensus/)。更多信息请阅读[这里](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/02/libertarianism-consequentialism-and-self-ownership/)、[这里](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/02/against-utilitarianism-and-self-ownership-defenses-of-libertarianism/)和[这里](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/02/eudaimonist-libertarianism/)。

所以：Right theorists 会说，rights 的一个 reason 是，rights-respect 本身就是 good society 的 constitutive，而与 consequences 分开。由于有一些 libertarian theorists 是 pluralists，他们会说，存在一系列 goods，而不只是“happiness”或“utility”，而 rights 既是那些 goods 的一部分，也是获得它们的 means。

**13.2 Consequentialism 并不 imply 当聪明人觉得 rights 应该被 breached 时就 breaching rights，rule consequentialism 才是答案。**

有[一些 philosophical reasons](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism-rule/#OldObjRulCon) 可以用来反对 rule consequentialism。如果你很聪明，思考过这个问题，而且其他聪明人也赞同，那么如果 violate 某人的 rights 会 increases utility，这似乎就是 morally mandatory 的。否则，你就会被束缚于 respecting 在某个时间点 determined 的 right structure，并且永不 revise 它们。

**13.3 有时，你应当改变要 respect 哪些 rights：alchemist 的 example。Rights 是 warning signs。**

这并没有 solve 前面提出的 problem。如何 determine 某种 paradigm shift 是否应当 induce 对某项 right 是否仍应存在的 reconsideration？如何 determine 什么样的 circumstance 才是我们应当这样做的 circumstance？一种 solution 是 abandoning consequentialism，并直接 applying common sense morality：人们拥有 rights，而如果 consequences 确实很 bad，这些 rights 就可以被 breached。这与大多数 academic libertarians 已经采取的 approach 没有什么不同。

而且，恕我不同意 Scott，反对 practising alchemy 的 rule 是个坏主意：从事 alchemy 的人有时最终做出了有用的事情，并由此通向了 modern chemistry。

**13.3.1 Property rights 通常是 useful，但有时可以被 breached，好吧。然而，如果人们自行这样做，consequences 会很 terrible。因此，最好由一个 central agent 来 decide 关于何时 violate rights 的 uniform rules：也就是 governments。**

如果需要 governments 来 avoid societal breakdown，那么 governments 就是 justified。但真是如此吗？Minarchists 说是，anarchists 则不这么认为。为此需要 welfare state 吗？welfare state 大约在 130 年前诞生于 Germany。modern welfare state 则是 post-WWII 的产物。在它出现之前，society 似乎并没有处于 collapse 的边缘：它反而越来越好。要 decide 何时 break rules 是 justified，你需要一个 court system。许多 court systems 接受以[极其 good 的 consequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessity) 作为 excuse。

**13.3.2 Choosing 何时 violate rights 是 complicated，但我们可以为此制定一些 meta-rules：至少必须有一个行之有效的 procedure，来获取每个人的 input。Governments 就是这样的一个 example。**

Scott 提出了这个建议。Libertarians 会以 rule of law 作答：一个 common law system 可以发挥同样的作用。Governments 在 developed country 中运作良好，在许多 underdeveloped 国家中则不然。在 democracy 中，并不存在 will of the people。存在的只是 election process 所产生的 outcomes；这个 process 是由能够 vote 的人设计的（不是 children，也不是 foreigners），而且他们只 vote 一次来决定由谁 rule，而不是逐个 issue 进行 vote。除非你是 Switzerland，能够实行 large-scale direct democracy。

所以，democracy 无法通过 universalizability test，除非我们把“每个人”的 meaning 限定为“某个 country 中的每个 adult”。而且，仍然可以说，如果某件事在你看来是 right，你就应当去做；如果某件事是 wrong，就不要做。世界上的其他人会对你作出反应，而你知道这一点，就会将其考虑在内。如果因为人们挨饿而出现大量 robberies，人们就会 hire security，其他人可能会 philantrophically 为他们提供生活所需，甚至只是设计新的 business models 来 lower food prices。这个 system 最终会自行 balancing。

关于 government size 与 well-being：除了只有 rich societies 才能 sustain 一个 large State 这一事实之外，higher taxation rates（或 government spending）与 standards of living 之间并没有 clear correlation。你可以像 Kiribati、Cuba、Lesotho 或 Micronesia 那样拥有 huge governments，却生活在 poverty 中；也可以像 Hong Kong、Switzerland 或 Singapore 那样拥有 small governments，却生活在 prosperity 中。理想情况下，我们会把 Nordics 与一个拥有真正的 minarchist(<5% GDP) government 和 free markets 的地方进行 compare，但我们没有当代的 examples。

**13.4 Governments 在 acting 时可能犯错。但不 acting 可能带来更 worse 的 consequences：Holocaust 与 Rwanda 的 case。**

如果每个人从一开始就遵循 non-interventionist policy，Holocaust 就不会发生。没有 First World War，没有 Versailles Treaty，没有贫困而战败的 Germany，没有 Hitler，没有 World War II，也就没有 Holocaust。Pacifism 似乎是[应当选择的道路](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2010/04/the_common-sens.html)。

而且，由 violating rights 造成的重大 catastrophes，即便不是全部，至少大多数都是 large governments 酿成的。并不是它们有时会 violate rights：有些 governments 经常这样做。

在 war 中 using coercion 存在一种 asymmetry：要使用它，就必须 ex-ante 极其明确地知道，它将带来 desired consequences，而实际情况很少如此。那么，先前那些关于即使看起来这样做是 right，也要小心不要 violating heuristics 的说法，都到哪里去了？

\*\*13.5 Libertarians 似乎 endorse fighting terrorism，而这很 expensive；但如果把那些 money 用于 fighting cardiovascular diseases，就能 saved 更多 lives。与 CVD 相比，选择 fighting terrorism 并没有 benefit。 \*\*

我没有就此 surveyed libertarians，但这里有 Michael Huemer 的[一场演讲](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4JYL5VUe5NQ)，其中他明确指出，把大量 resources 投入 fighting terrorism 是 irrational。所以，我认为 libertarians 应当反对这样做。但我看不出它与前一个 case 有什么相似之处，除非这里的 argument 仅仅是说，killing by omission 与 directly killing 是一样的；这是一个几乎没人接受、因而必须得到 argued for 的 premise。认定 killing by omission 与 directly killing 之间没有 difference，究竟基于什么 rational grounds？

**13.6 存在这样一个 case：libertarian principles 得到了遵循，却导致 catastrophe：发生了一场 pandemic，只有一个人拥有 cure，他想收多少就收多少，最后每个人都被 enslaved**

根据 Lockean Proviso，他必须以 fair price 向每个提出请求的人 provide 那种 cure，而 coerce 他这样做也是 justified 的。

不过，这确实是一个很好的 example，说明为什么“hard” libertarianism 是 problematic 的。它的常规 varieties 不受这个 issue 的影响。

**13.6.1 好吧，这个故事并不 realistic，但它仍然是一个 reductio ad absurdum。heuristic “永不 initiate force”有效这一事实，仅限于某些 cases。libertarianism 有效的 circumstances 是 contingent 的**

这是一个很好的 reduction。但它对 academic libertarianism 并不起作用。如果世界不是现在这个样子，那么我们会把这一点纳入 violate rights 所需的 conditions 之中。

**13.7 在没有 government welfare 的 countries 中成为 poor，日子很难过。Private charity 并没有 solving third-world poverty。一些简单的 math 表明，Private charity 不太可能 increase 到所需的程度。**

大多数 developed countries 确实有某种 government welfare，因此很难、甚至不可能找到 Scott 所寻找的 poor。如前所述，即便在 Denmark 这样先进的 social democracies 中，仍然有 homeless 和非常贫困的 poor people。（也就是说，是 taxes 和 transfers 之后的 Poor）

Scott 建议的 calculations，已经在[这里](https://www.amazon.es/Revoluci%C3%B3n-Liberal-Espa%C3%B1a-Economia-deusto/dp/8423418537?ie=UTF8&*Version*=1&*entries*=0)的一本书中针对 Spain 做过了；这本书提出了一个在 50 年内 transition 到 minarchist state (5% GDP) 的详细方案，涵盖 education、healthcare、pensions 等方面的具体 proposals 和 budgets。

math 本身是 right 的，但有一个 premise 是 wrong 的：welfare 上的 spending 并不真正在 helping the poor。在 US，面向 poor 的 [welfare](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/wonk/wp/2014/01/12/no-we-dont-spend-1-trillion-on-welfare-each-year/) 包括 EITC、TANF、SSI,food stamps、housing vouchers，以及 child tax credit。总计 $212bn。因此，private sector 手中 money 的 increase，将能够 offset 所需的 charity increase。

或许还可以进一步 refinement：Scott 概述的 precise calculations，已经在前面引用的那本书中针对 Spain 完成，而 Spain 的 market-poverty rate 比 US 更 higher。而我一直作为 example 引用的 [Switzerland](http://www.cbpp.org/us-poverty-rate-is-high-after-taxes-and-transfers-compared-to-similarly-wealthy-countries)，其 market-poverty rate 为 15%（按 relative standards 计算，我没能找到 pre-market absolute poverty rates），甚至低于 US 在 post-tax and transfer 之后的水平。

**13.8 作出 bad decisions 的人并不 deserve 承受苦难**

同意。但让人们免于承受其 actions 的 consequences，在 incentivewise 上是 problematic 的。

### Part E：Practical Issues（实践问题）

\*\*这个 Argument： \*\*给予 government 一些 power 并不会把我们引向 tyranny。Libertarians 反而应该在 system 内努力，oppose 那些不起作用的 regulations，并 accept 那些起作用的 regulations。

\*\*这个 Counterargument： \*\*提出这个 argument 的不是 minarchists，而是 anarchists。问题不是 tyranny，而是 government 比它应有的规模更大。在 advanced countries，government 变得 tyrannical 的情况会很少见。相反，至少有一个 historical example：一个 country 以 limited government 为 premise 起步，最后却拥有了大得多的 government，那就是 US。[有一些](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zd5aJUVZzgw) reforms 可以用来 enhance democracies 的运作方式，但 libertarians 仍然会追求 first-best，而不是选择 second-best。其他所有人也都应该如此。

**14\. Slippery Slopes（滑坡）**

**14.1 给予 government 一些 power，并不会在长远将它变成 tyranny**

如上所述，问题不是 tyranny，而是 overreach（ 如果接受某些 government functions 的话）。

**14.1.1 如果我们让 government 自行其是，它也不会 engineer 一场走向 dictatorship 的 collapse**

我不知道是否有很多 libertarians endorsing 这种看法。我同意 Scott 对 politics 的看法。

**15\. Strategic Activism（策略性行动）**

**15.1 如果你足够聪明，能够成为 libertarian，你就应该运用自己的 intelligence 和精力来 enact 一个 proper system**

由于 votes 几乎没有 power，人们没有 incentive 去这样做。这是一个巨大的 [coordination failure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J5maguX5x8c)——正是那种被用来 criticise libertarianism 的情况。不过，它[并不是一种](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/economics/wait-a-minute-is-the-government-self-interested-or-isnt-it/) [utter failure](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/politics-government/why-we-vote-the-way-we-vote/)，原因与前面指出的 markets 中的 failures 没有那么糟糕的原因相同。

相反，聪明的 non-libertarians 可以努力帮助实现向 libertarianism 的 transition :\_)

**15.2 你不能说 government 无法 improved，有些人正在为 government 提出巧妙的 arrangements**

Futarchy 是在 replacing government 的一个部分：policymaking。Governments 仍然是由人组成的 governments，也仍然会有问题。我的意思不是说这样不会更好。improve government 当然是可能的，而且 futarchy 是一个有趣的想法，我希望看到有人尝试，但它仍然会是一个具有 political authority 的 monopoly on force，而 libertarianism 可能仍然会 dominate 它：market process 自身就会 generates information。如果不是这样，那么通过设立一个 futarchic central planner committee，就有可能 improve upon capitalism。

不过，事实仍然是，一些 countries 的 government 的 quality 似乎并没有在 improving：[rate of approval of US Congress](http://www.huffingtonpost.com/2012/08/14/congress-approval-rating-all-time-low-gallup-poll_n_1777207.html) 正处于 historical minimum，而 [Trump](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MKH6PAoUuD0) 已经展示了 democratic system [works as designed](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2016/03/democracy-works-because-it-doesnt-work/) 时会发生什么。也许是时候开始 advocating[epistocracy](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2011/04/epistocracy_and.html)，而不是 democracy 了？

而且，尽管这个问题与这番论述是 orthogonal 的，提起它也近乎在故意挑衅，但前面提到的那些巧妙的 economics 的 theorems 未必那么 true：Tetlock 的 forecasters 比 prediction markets 表现更好，尽管 prediction markets 仍然非常好。([Atanasov, et al. 2015](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2660628))

**15.3 Improving government 很难，但 libertarianism 在 politically 是 impossible 的**

旨在 reduce government 的 Incremental attempt，也比明天就 transitioning 到 libertarianism 更 viable。问题不只是 reforming government 很困难。[有些](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zd5aJUVZzgw) libertarians 确实有能使 governments 更好的 reforms，即使这些 libertarians 的 first best 是根本不要任何 government。真正的问题是，best possible government 仍然无法与 best possible libertarianism 相提并论；那么，如果一个 society 能够 overcome 围绕 politics 的众多 coordination problems，从而获得 good government，为什么它不能同样 solve 那些你最初想要 set up government 来解决的 coordination problems 呢？

**15.4 划定界限并 banning 所有 government intervention 并不起作用。如果 libertarians 想产生一些影响，他们就应该对 policies 采取 case by case approach。人们不会听那些反对每一项 policy 的人的话。**

有些 libertarians 是这样说的：US government 可以被视为对 limited government 的 theory 的 refutation。但由此得出的 conclusion 就是转而追求 anarchism。

至于后一个问题，我引用 Huemer 的话：

> 为什么本章的 proposal 并不同样是 utopian 的？为什么，相比于期待那些获知 democratic government 所 implemented 的 flawed policies 的 citizens 努力去 perfect 其 government 的 policies，期待那些确信 government 的 illegitimacy 的 citizens 努力去 abolish 其 government，会更加 realistic？
> 
> 答案是，对 government 总体上的 illegitimacy 获得认识，在 cognitively demanding 的程度上，要远远低于对某个特定 government 的 specific policy errors 获得足够认识，以至于能够制定 rational plans 来 correct 其中大多数 errors。要认识到 government 是 illegitimate 的，接受本书中的 arguments 就足够了。但要 identify 自己的 government 的大多数 specific policy errors，就需要详细熟悉数千项 statutes 和 regulations；数十个 government agencies、boards 和 commissions；以及数百位 political figures。一个人必须在一生中持续 update 这些知识，才能 take account of government 的每个 arm 的每项新 action。希望人们就单一的 philosophical principle——rejection of authority——达成 consensus，要比希望人们就大多数具体 government policies 的 specific flaws 达成 consensus realistic 得多。

**16.1 我不同意你。我应该怎样与 non-non-libertarian arguments 展开讨论，才最有可能改变你的想法？**

一般来说，友善一些。阅读 academic libertarian philosophers 的著作，也阅读 States 开始接手之前事情如何运作的 history。在充分探究 libertarian society 用来 solve 可能出现的诸多问题的 mechanisms 之前，不要 assume consequentialism entails 对 libertarianism 的 rejection。而且，不要 assume consequentialism 来为某种 political philosophy 进行 argue，这会让你的工作更困难。从共同的 moral premises 出发，在 principles 和 intuitions 之间寻求 equilibrium。正如 Scott 所说，你必须从 first principles 出发，prove 你的 standards 是 right 的。迄今还没有人成功地从零开始 deriving 一个 full moral system。因此，将来我可能会对 non-consequentialist FAQ 进行 critique。或者，你也可以 show，一个 realistic libertarian society 与 social democracy 或 alternative system 相比会是 _really bad_ 的。我认为，这应该能 convince 人们 abandon libertarianism。

## 尾声

希望你读这篇 FAQ 读得愉快。我想，libertarians（尤其是 Scott 心目中的那类 libertarian）和 non-libertarians 都能从中学到一些东西。

我其实并不指望你读完这些就成为 libertarian。我在这里并没有为 libertarianism 辩护，只是解释了 Scott 提出的 arguments 并不能 debunk libertarianism。例如，我并不指望你现在就相信 private healthcare 比 public healthcare 更好。我没有讨论 economists 在那场 debate 中提出的 arguments，也没有做完整的 literature review。但我希望已经让你相信，它可能是 workable 的。

你现在看待 libertarianism 的方式，应当介于两个极端之间：一是完全接受；二是把它看作一个有趣的想法，也许应该在遥远的一个小地方试一试。这就是 Scott [在这里](http://slatestarcodex.com/2015/03/18/book-review-the-machinery-of-freedom/)所持的当前 position。为 libertarianism 辩护通常要借助 historical examples，或者从各处挑选 examples，因为目前并没有哪个 country 是 libertarian。因此，关于它的 viability 或 desirability 的 debates 往往会无休止地持续下去，因为在这个问题上，没有像 social democrats 借助 Nordics 所拥有的那种 hard, conclusive evidence。

最后，我想回应 Scott 在前面引用的文章中说的一件事：

> 这就引出了我对 libertarianism 和 anarcho-capitalism 最深的疑问之一：它为什么应该能 work？我说的不是那种可以用具体 reasons 回答的“它为什么应该能 work”：不，monopolies 不会 form；不，人们不会经常把自己卖入 slavery；不，protection agencies 不会 form 一个新的 feudal ruling class；不，人们不会永远在 public goods problems 和 externalities 面前碰壁，却始终没有任何 market solutions 出现；也不，穷人不会饿死。我说的是那个真正属于 Outside View 的问题：“为什么恰巧不 using force，就是 solve 所有 problems 的一种 effective way？”Good governance 是一个非常非常困难的 problem。认为这个 problem 的 solution 包含 zero bits of information，只要不去干涉人们，它就会自行解决，这似乎令人震惊。即使我们同意 capitalism 通过 incentivizing companies 去做 consumers 想要的事情而 works very well，仍然有许多 peripheral issues 是它无法涵盖的。例如，Friedman 强烈支持 child rights，因为 children 应当在大多数情况下 free from 父母的 coercion，而且受到这种对待的 children 会成长得更好。现在，除了用 zero bits of information 来 solving governance，你还用 zero bits of information 解决了 optimal child-rearing。这令人印象深刻得难以置信。既然 universe 可以向我们抛出任何它想抛出的 problems，而且迄今为止，它也一直兴高采烈地利用这个权利，提出了大量形形色色而又有趣的 problems，那么，为什么这些 problems 中，没有任何一个最好由拥有 monopoly on force 的 centralized entity 来处理？从 game-theoretic perspective 来看，那似乎是一种相当 basic structure，而你却告诉我，它在现实世界中就是从不 work？难道不应该至少有一两件事，恰恰适合由 government，或者任何形式的 coercive structure 来解决吗？难道我们不能只设一个 small government，专门做_那些事_吗？

这是一个非常好的问题。在写那篇评论的几个月前，Scott 写下了如今已很有名的 [Meditations on Moloch](http://slatestarcodex.com/2014/07/30/meditations-on-moloch/)。他认为 coordination problems 无处不在，甚至 governments 也不能 fix 它们全部：它们同样受到 perverse incentives 的影响。那么，solution 就是一个 external unmoved mover，一个 _artificial god,_ 以确保人们在想要 cooperate 却缺乏相应 incentives 时，确实会 cooperate。而由于这实在很难，整篇文章都带着 existential angst 的语气，面对 unsolvable coordination problems 而非 evil 才是我们最大 problem 的想法，流露出恐惧与战栗。

对此或许有一个答案。但在考虑它之前，想一想 socialist calculation problem。想象我们回到几十年前，有人写了下面这段话给我们：

是的，markets 非常好。但见鬼，它们究竟怎么会好到让 central planning 在任何 industry 都不 work？面包不行，钢铁、煤炭或石油不行，小器件、汽车或飞机也不行。为什么？Central planning 有 advantages，比如 economies of scale、solving 一些 coordination failures，以及 reducing duplicities。为什么对于每一种可以想象的 industry，你都说 market works better？还有，为什么是 capitalistic management？为什么不是 worker ownership？你怎么知道 capitalistic ownership 在每一种情况下都 works best？

而我的回答是：如果 central planning 是 efficient 的，它就会出现在 market 中。如果 worker cooperatives 是 efficient 的，它们就会出现在 market 中。如果 worker cooperatives 是 efficient 的，它们就会出现在 market 中。market dominates 每一种 alternative，因为如果 alternative 更好，market structure 就会 transform 成那种 alternative，然后在必要时 readapt。今天，你可能有两家大型 oil companies。明天，你可能有三家或八家。后天，也许一家都没有，而我们都在使用 solar energy。与此同时，你可以有 worker owned firms、anarchosyndicalist firms（如 Valve）、拥有 extense supply chains 的 decentralised firms，或者所有事情都在 inhouse 完成的 firms（如 SpaceX）。

这基本上就是 [First Theorem of Welfare Economics](http://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2007/08/the-first-funda.html)。但接着，有人可能会说：是啊，但那只有在存在 perfect information、perfect competition，而且没有 transaction costs 时才 holds。

这里可以提出两个 counter arguments。第一个涉及 comparative institutions：Governments 并不是 benevolent social planners。按照 Soylent Green Principle of Institutional Analysis，governments 是由人组成的。optimal institutional arrangement 可能是这样一种 arrangement：通过 limiting State 的 role，允许 markets failures 存在，却不允许 governments failures 存在。

第二个更有意思的 counter argument 是，market failures 是供 entrepreneurs 去 solve 的 [profit opportunities for entrepreneurs](http://www.coordinationproblem.org/2006/05/responding_to_c.html)。并不是 markets 需要一系列 conditions 才能 work properly，而是一个允许 markets 运作的 institutional framework 会带来那些 conditions：足够的 competition、足够的 information，以及 sufficiently low transaction costs。人们能够走出自己正在参与的 games，rearrange incentives，再次进入 games，然后 cooperate。

> 如果支持 laissez-faire conditions 的 argument，是 behavioral assumptions、那些 super heroic behavioral assumptions 的 consequence，比如你是一个身处 frictionless environment 的 fully informed 且 perfectly rational chooser——很多人认为这个 argument 就是这样展开的——那么我只需要 undermine agents 的 rationality，以及 price system 作为 perfect guide 的 function，也就是那个 perfectly competitive model。
> 
> 但是，从 Adam Smith 一直到 Vernon Smith，他们从未以这种方式来表述其支持 relatively free economy 而非 interventionist economy 的 argument。他们总是用 comparative institutional analysis 来表述它；如果你看看 Adam Smith 以及他给出的各种不同 examples，就会发现，他从未依赖一个在 perfectly competitive market 中 operating 的 perfectly-informed actor，来得出支持 laissez-faire 的 argument。([Boettke](http://www.libertarianism.org/media/free-thoughts/austrian-tradition-economics))

多数人把 markets 看作一种 mechanism，把 government 看作另一种 mechanism，然后认为你必须看看在每种情况下哪一种 works best。我认为，更好的思考方式是把 markets 看作一种 meta-mechanism，让许多 sub-mechanisms（包括 government-like institutions）能够从 bottom-up 产生（[Pennington, 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259437437_Elinor_Ostrom_and_the_robust_political_economy_of_common-pool_resources)）。另一种 meta-mechanism 是让一切都处在 government 之内，这就是我们现在的情况，尽管它本身又 nested 在 international anarchy 的 state 之中。

“谁来监督监督者？”这个 problem 的 solution，不是指定特别的监督者，而是让每个人都在自己熟悉的 domain 内充当监督者。这一点，尤其是在结合一些 moral norms 时（人们并不是，而且[不应当](http://peterturchin.com/blog/2016/01/19/naked-self-interest-is-a-recipe-for-social-dissolution-a-response-to-branko-milanovic/)[是完全](http://peterturchin.com/blog/2016/01/16/herbert-gintis-societies-whose-business-leaders-have-moral-integrity-are-successful-societies-a-guest-blog/) [selfish](http://peterturchin.com/blog/2016/01/18/robert-frank-ruthless-self-interest-is-not-a-good-business-strategy-a-guest-post/) 的），使 libertarianism 成为在 theoretically 和 practically 两方面都尽可能好的 social order 和 cooperation 的 basis。

正如你在这篇 FAQ 中可能看到的，libertarians 与其他人之间的多数 disagreements 都是 empirical 的。我们应当能够研究每一种 system 会如何运作，并就此达成一致。也许总体而言 libertarianism 更好，但 society 中最穷的 5% 除外，他们的处境比在 social democracy 中略差。也许它对他们也更好。完成这一步之后，我们可以继续争论哪些 values 是 correct 的。

我的一些回答可能让人觉得像是在耍赖。我真的在为 Scott 提出的 problems 给出 libertarian answers 吗？我难道是个搪塞大师吗？

我 rejected 了对 NAP 的一些 absolutist interpretations，accepted 了一些可以由 market provided 的 regulations 的必要性，而且在某些方面赞扬 Nordics 胜过 US model，等等。我主张，即使多数 critiques 成立，也可以由 minimal state 来应对。

## 原文

_aka The webpage you will always remember for changing your political views, maybe :)_

**Introduction**

**0.1: Who are you, what is this?**

For me, see in the About section of this site.

This is a response to [Scott Alexander's Non-Libertarian FAQ](https://slatestarcodex.com/2017/02/22/repost-the-non-libertarian-faq/), initially written in 2010 and revised in 2013. Note that his FAQ is not called the _Anti_\-Libertarian FAQ. In the same spirit, this FAQ does not defend libertarianism, but criticises arguments offered against libertarianism.

**0.2: Are you a libertarian?**

That depends on your definition of libertarian. I've never been really comfortable with any ideological X's in 'I am an X'. But you can get an idea of what I think by reading Michael Huemer, Loren Lomasky, Robert Nozick, Matt Zwolinski and Jason Brennan.

On statism, I define statism as the belief that the State has political authority, or that the law ought to be obeyed just because it is law, whatever its content. Political philosophers nowadays reject this, so statists are on the wrong side of philosophical progress. However, arguments still remain for State commands being right for other goals. That is, laws against murder are nonproblematic, but that's because murder is wrong, and the State enforces its ban. Also, there may be other arguments for the State that do not rely on political authority, namely consequentialist arguments.

Then, most non-libertarians (and some libertarians, curiously) are statists as defined above. Scott does not believe in political authority: he is a consequentialist. Hence, he is not a statist. He would support the state only if it is the social-institutional arrangement most conducive to the Greater Good™.

**0.3 Do you hate non-libertarians?**

Hating is irrational, and doubly so in intellectual inquiry. The mental state of 'hating' not only makes you feel worse, but it also induces you to think in biased ways regarding that which you hate.

Now, Scott says that he likes libertarianism as a set of hands-off policies that can be argued to work better than the alternatives, and that he dislikes the libertarianism that "rather than analyzing specific policies and often deciding a more laissez-faire approach is best, starts with the tenet that government can do no right and private industry can do no wrong and uses this faith _in place of_ more careful analysis.".

He addresses the FAQ to the second type of libertarians. I would be a third type: I do think libertarianism works better in general (based on empirical and theoretical evidence)  and that the situations where it doesn't work as good do not justify the use of force to correct them. If there were situations where it worked really poorly, then coercion would be justified. That is, I believe States can do many things right, and that the private sector can do many wrong things, but that by itself doesn't say anything about whether there should be a State, or if there has to be one, what should it be doing.

One thing is economics and history, and the other is moral philosophy. In this FAQ we will deal mainly with the former, as Scott has another FAQ that deals with the latter: the [Consequentialism FAQ](http://raikoth.net/consequentialism.html). Although I'm not a consequentialist, I think consequences matter greatly. This FAQ mainly deals with empirical issues, so it isn't relevant for my purposes what the correct ethical theory is. To add to the prima facie unimportance of this debate at this point, many [libertarians are](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/02/26/liberalisms-overlapping-consensus/) consequentialists or utilitarians, so if consequentialism is true, it may end up leading to libertarianism anyways.

**0.4 Will this FAQ prove that the free market is always better than the government**?

No.

What I will argue is that free markets are very good. Even better than most economists think they are. And in the long term, coupled with non-market institutions and a proper definition of property rights (to solve externalities), this is a (meta)system that is very hard to criticise by comparison to an alternative system.

**0.5 Why write a Non-Non-Libertarian FAQ?**

Scott Alexander has written a document for which there is no publicly available (high quality) critique, and given that it is well argued, and has a friendly tone, some have taken the document itself, and the absence of critique for three years as proof that libertarianism is wrong.

If a person as honest, rational and rigorous as Scott has rejected libertarianism in a non-strawmanning way, and no one has replied to his arguments, doesn't it count as a strong prima facie case against libertarianism? It does! There is [one such reply](https://problemswithdemocracy.blogspot.com/2013/10/the-non-libertarian-faq-response.html) (not very good, imho), and David Friedman emailed Scott [another](https://daviddfriedman.blogspot.com/2015/02/better-than-facebook.html#c2008977712910407576), also available [here](http://www.daviddfriedman.com/Miscellaneous/My%20Response%20to%20a%20Non-Libertarian%20faq.html). But still there is no publicly available good response to the entire FAQ, point by point. Of course, there are good argumentations for libertarianism elsewhere (see [here](https://www.amazon.com/The-Problem-Political-Authority-Examination/dp/1137281650) for philosophy, see [here](https://www.amazon.es/Revoluci%C3%B3n-Liberal-Espa%C3%B1a-Economia-deusto/dp/8423418537?ie=UTF8&*Version*=1&*entries*=0) for practical issues), but those are not specific to this FAQ.

That said, Scott Alexander [admitted](http://squid314.livejournal.com/344915.html?thread=7772755) some years ago that he wasn't sure he's a non-libertarian anymore, and that the FAQ is aimed at non-smart libertarians, so writing my critique may seem, and I think it will seem, like an overkill.

Finally, I [cannot let](https://xkcd.com/386/) well argued arguments to stand unanswered if I disagree with them. If one wants to keep holding beliefs in disagreement with his FAQ, one must produce reasons for it. I make my list of such reasons available to everyone to move  the debate forward.

**0.6 How is this FAQ structured?**

The same way as Scott's. I recommend you to read mine in parallel with his, as the questions in my FAQ are summaries of Scott's answers, more or less. My want could even make little sense without this parallel reading.

As an additional comment, in this paragraph, Scott alludes to the tendency of libertarians to resort to economic and moral arguments. If morality dictates libertarianism, why bother with consequences? If libertarianism is the system that works best, period, why bother saying that things are morally wrong?

This critique was initially advanced by [Jeffrey Friedman (1997)](http://www.criticalreview.com/crf/jf/11%203%20libertarianism.pdf), and was debated afterwards ([Palmer 1998](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/08913819808443507), [Friedman 1998](http://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/08913819808443508)). Recently, [Zwolinski](http://www.peter-boettke.com/app/download/7116937557/Zwolinski+-+A+Brief+History+of+Libertarianism.pdf) and [Tesón](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2011/03/classical-liberalism-defended/) made this explicit:

> While an appeal to self-ownership is probably the most famous libertarian strategy for defending rights of private property, it is by no means the only one. Some libertarians have sought to base their defense on different but equally deontological grounds such as an appeal to negative liberty, or to autonomy. Other libertarians have sought to  provide a more consequentialist justification, by showing how private property replaces the zero- (or negative-) sum transactions of the commons with the positive-sum  transactions of a market economy.  Or by showing how rights of private property are instrumentally valuable in securing other important civil and political rights. For the most part, though, individuals in the libertarian intellectual tradition have refrained from drawing sharp distinctions between consequentialist and deontological arguments, believing instead that respect for private property is both a moral duty and a wise social policy.  Libertarians, especially those outside the discipline of academic philosophy, have thus tended to help themselves generously to both sorts of arguments, without always distinguishing clearly between them. \[...\]
> 
> That fact that justice and utility both point in the same direction in so many libertarian arguments is surely a fact that calls out for explanation. One possible explanation is the skeptical one. The reason justice and utility line up for libertarians, this line of reasoning goes, is because libertarians frame their arguments to fit their conclusions, rather than the other way around. See, for a somewhat sympathetic expression of this skepticism, Jeffrey Friedman, "What's Wrong with Libertarianism?," Critical Review 11, no. 3 (1997). However there are other, less skeptical explanations available as well. For instance, perhaps justice and utility do not come into conflict because the content of justice is partly a function of utilitarian considerations? See, for a discussion of many such possible explanations, and an endorsement of one, Roderick Long, "Why Does Justice Have Good Consequences?," http://praxeology.net/whyjust.htm \[Ed. See also[this](http://praxeology.net/postlib.htm) and [this](https://mises.org/library/review-ethics-social-science-moral-philosophy-social-cooperation-leland-b-yeager)\]

I don't have a defined view on this matter yet, but I will say that if libertarianism had very bad consequences, it ought to be abandoned. Also, if libertarianism had very good consequences, but it were highly unethical, it ought to be abandoned as well.

### **Part A: Economic Issues**

**The Argument**

_Every transaction affects not just the seller and buyer, but everyone else, and that it assumes that consumers have coherent wants and demands. Markets have problems: externalities, coordination problems, irrational choice, and lack of information. Given this, a regulated market trumps a free market._

**The Counterargument**

_Yes, voluntary trade makes people ex-ante better off necessarily, but this does not presuppose all the conditions of classical perfect competition. Perfect competition gives sufficient, not necessary, conditions for efficiency._

_It is true that trade occurs not in isolation. Precisely this will be the key to answer the challenges the FAQ raises. Also, trade doesn't need to be between two people, you can have multilateral trade. As long as everything is voluntary and property rights are well defined, the free market delivers, it will be argued._

**1\. Externalities**

**1.1 What is an externality?**

[Externalities](http://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/Externalities.html) are costs imposed upon people who were not participants in the transaction that originated said cost. While some economists like to derive justifications for government interventions from externalities, we should remember that there are quite a few externalities no one advocates for fixing, and yet some are as externalities as pollution.

One is the psychological distress non-whites cause to white supremacists. If that externality has to be fixed, non-whites would be taxed for existing, and the white supremacists would be compensated, or something like that. Instead, most of us agree, and rightly so, that white supremacism is wrong, and that what ought to happen is for them to change their preferences. This also solves the externality by making it vanish.

Another one is procreation: bringing more children to the world alters the distribution of traits (intelligence, health, creativity, kindness, etc) in society, and that affects everyone ([Anomaly, 2014](http://philpapers.org/archive/ANOPGA-3.pdf)). To fix it, we may require positive (unproblematic) or negative (problematic) eugenics, or family planning by some authority.

Most people would not endorse taxing non-whites to compensate white supremacists, or curtailing reproductive rights in such a way as to ensure every child is not a walking negative externality.

Also, we wouldn't want to fix pecuniary externalities: externalities that affect others via the price system. If I buy orange juice, I marginally raise the price of orange juice for the rest of consumers, so it affects them negatively. But that loss for them is balanced by an equivalent gain (in money) for orange juice producers.

What (negative) externalities are usually the ones that most people would like to fix? Those that are basically violations of rights, and that is required by libertarianism.

There are also some positive externalities, but arguments that begin from there to justify coercion to fix them are harder to make.

**1.2 But the libertarian ways to solve externalities fail, unless you propose something that basically is a government**

The externality described in the FAQ would be solved by a court. You would kindly ask the wasp farmer to stop having his business there because he's annoying, and if he doesn't, you sue him. Alternatively, there could be private organisations that could to the suing for you. The court would assess if the nuisance caused by the wasp farmer is a rights violation or not. It probably would: people in the neighbourhood were there before, and the presence of the wasp farmer leaves them worse off in a very noticeable way.

Alternatively, another institution to fix that is a [Homeowner Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeowner_association) (HOA). These [are not](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/11/15/the-privatepublic-distinction-a-reply-to-stringham-and-powell/) governments, but provide most of the functions that you would like local governments for.

Another one is covenants, binding agreements that are transferred with the property of the house. Why would builders include prohibitions against wasp farming in the buildings in their newly built neighborhoods? Because people would value such neighborhoods more, obviously. This is also a foundation for libertarian zoning restrictions. For more info on this, see [Beito, Gordon & Tabarrok (2009)](https://www.amazon.com/The-Voluntary-City-Community-Society/dp/1598130323)'s excellent book on the topic. These may not be used nowadays, because the government is already in the business of doing that, but in the past, it wasn't that rare.

**1.3 People won't boycott companies that pollute unless most consumers are knowledgeable and altruistic**

Pollution wouldn't be primarily handed via boycott. Pollution is another thing that would be court-regulated in the libertarian world. For minarchists, this is easy: there is a State doing the regulation. For anarchists, this is harder, but I think it's still doable. Pollution is one of the hard problems for libertarian theory, and work in that area continues  ([Zwolinski, 2014](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2443030)).

Anyway, while boycotts won't be a main tool for governance in the libertarian world, other institutions can play that role, including general consumer pressure: There are some studies showing that it can induce companies to change their behaviour ([Hettige et al. 1996](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0305750X96000769)).

**1.4 Therefore, externalities justify some regulations**

Call them regulations if so you wish. They can also be called definitions of property rights, which are fully consistent -and required by- libertarianism. By excluding fine property rights definitions from the definition of libertarianism, it is possible to create problems for it where there are none.

**2\. Coordination Problems**

**2.1 What are coordination problems**

[Moloch](http://slatestarcodex.com/2014/07/30/meditations-on-moloch/). Or, situations where everyone sees a solution, but there is no coordination to implement it. I criticised the broad idea of pervasive and unsolvable coordination problems [elsewhere](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/12/24/slaying-alexanders-moloch/). Regarding the story of the fish farmers, I copy-paste from myself:

So we have our 1000 firms, each one causing a -1$ loss to others, from an initial gain of $1000 each, so they are making zero. The fishermen get together and lay out their situation. They agree on signing a conditional contract, not too dissimilar from what you can do in Kickstarter: Everyone will pay 300$ for a filter, so that everyone makes $700 a month. The contract will only be binding if everyone signs it, and they go to a court and sign it there so that it becomes binding, and enforceable by an external agency. Now they have changed their incentives: They can choose to do nothing (payoff=0) or sign (expected payoff=700). So they sign. Once they have gone to court, they can either cooperate (expected payoff=700) or can shirk. But now their payoff is not 999, but paying a fine until they cooperate, so now it’s rational to cooperate. Fixed.

But you have used a court!, I'm told. So what? In Libertonia there are courts, what were you expecting? Third-party enforceability is a valuable service.

But what if there’s a stubborn fisherman who demands others to pay him to actually sign the contract. There we run into the question of whether the fishermen actually had the right to pollute the water in the first place.

So suppose that the fishermen raise this issue. Most of them argue that since the lake is a common resource, and since it’s in the interest in all of them for it to be clean, they should regard it as a common property, where rules can be imposed to ensure inner coordination. The hypothetical free rider could argue that since he was the first to get to the river, he has the right to pollute, and that others ought to pay him. This could be some sort of Coasean bargain. In this case, if he demands payment P from each other, he would be making 700+999P. Others would be making 700-P. In the cooperative case, total earnings are 700_1000=0.7 megadollars. In the noncooperative case, 700+999P+700_999-999P=0.7 megadollars, same amount, different distribution. P would depend here on a variety of factors, but total gains are the same. The court system would have to deal with cases like this, and resolve disputes, set precedents, trying to ensure good outcomes while respecting rights. No trivial task, but not an impossible one either.

Read also [this](http://www.owl232.net/commons.htm). On fisheries, In general, read  [Ostrom (1999)](http://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev.polisci.2.1.493).

The Coasean solution is not troublefree: it is necessarily efficient only if, at least, there are no transaction costs. Real life bargaining will not be texbooklike efficient. But as is highlighted through this FAQ, these are not the only solutions. David Friedman discusses [here](http://www.daviddfriedman.com/Academic/Coase_World.html) Coase's Theorem.

**2.1.1 But this doesn't work**

Empirically, yes. Read [Leal (1998)](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1023%2FA%3A1024691919628). The FAQ proposes fishing quotas. The obvious libertarian solution is private or common property rights, to which fishing quotas are an approximation. On a comparison between private and common property rights, see[Ostrom and Hess (2007)](http://surface.syr.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1024&context=sul).

The example of the Atlantic Northwest cod fishery was an example of _government_ failure: the fishery was government (mis)regulated. What would have happened had there been property rights on the whole fishery? The government didn't allow that to happen. The US fishery policy has its problems ([White, 2000](http://lsr.nellco.org/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1083&context=nyu_lewp)).The EU's Common Fisheries Policy also seems ([Khallilian et al, 2010](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0308597X10000709)) to be failing. Governments can succeed or fail at solving common resource coordination problems, but so can private or communal institutions ([Tarko, 2012](http://www.iea.org.uk/sites/default/files/publications/files/IEA%20Future%20of%20the%20Commons%20web%2029-1.10.12.pdf)).

That said, the state of global fisheries today [is dim](http://www.economist.com/news/leaders/21596942-new-management-needed-planets-most-important-common-resource-tragedy-high), even with plenty of national and international government regulation. Creating common property in fisheries that span national borders conflict with sovereignty (Ha, mere fishermen telling States what to do with their territorial waters!), so in a way States could be making their governance more difficult.

**2.1.2 But Fishermen won't do it!**

As said above, they actually, empirically, do it, especially for non-international water regions, and when rights of some sort are in place. Formal external enforcement will be a feature of any libertarian political order, with or without a state, so the fact that it is required is no critique of libertarianism.

For international waters, things are trickier, to the point that, as pointed out above, States are not able to deal with it (right now). But would we go as far as claiming it is _impossible_?

**2.2 Global warming is an example of this problem, as is ozone layer depletion and recycling**

The same problem that affects the private sector affects States. So this kind of problems are not quite problematic for libertarianism qua libertarianism, as States also can't fix them on a global scale. The statist solution would be to have a world-State to regulate these matters. The libertarian solution would be to say that since everyone makes use of the atmosphere, and that for the purposes needed (regulate emissions of certain gases and pollutants) it can't be subdivided, it has to be a single property. So it would be justified for it to exist a worldwide atmospheric regulation committee to solve this tragedy of the commons. Sounds really hard to do, but States haven't been doing that much better lately.

Fortunately, it seems that technology can do what individual incentives are not able to in a world of Nation-States. Right now, we're living through a solar energy revolution (and if we're a bit optimistic, we'll live a fusion energy revolution in some decades) that will end up with fossil fuel energies replaced by a cheaper and cleaner one. So while the problem is not solved, it stops being an actual problem, so everything is ok.

**2.3 Libertarians say boycotts can discipline corporations. Incentives aren't aligned that way.**

Suppose markets are more competitive so that the difference between one product and its replacement is slim. Then it's maybe $1 plus an ethical cost of buying from an unethical company vs $(1+x) and buying from a nice company. If x is relatively small, it may work.

Now, I'm not arguing boycotts are a really really strong tool. But those unspeakable things can be solved by courts. I mean, if there is a company that uses slave labour, anyone could sue them for slavery, on behalf of the slaves. I don't really see the problem here.

**2.3.1 They don't work: the [Coke example](http://news.bbc.co.uk/1/hi/business/1448962.stm)**

Those kind of abuses are highly rare, and they also happen with governments around. Governments that, in some cases, are corrupt. Some left-libertarians even claim they happen _because_ there are governments around.

And the Coke example [doesn't work that well](http://www.straightdope.com/columns/read/2622/did-coca-cola-torture-and-kill-workers-in-latin-america). It wasn't Coca-Cola itself, it was a bottler that worked for Coca-Cola, the evidence for the alleged crime is dubious, and the lawsuit ended up [dismissed](http://business-humanrights.org/en/coca-cola-lawsuit-re-colombia).

**2.3.2 Governments can do better**

Most of the laws mentioned had little to do with people wanting things. Except for very particular cases, there is a notable disconnect between what people want and what the government does in its day to day activities. Overall, governments, and any institutional system, anarchies included, are constrained by generally accepted beliefs about what's right and wrong ([Long, 2008](http://praxeology.net/Anarconst2.pdf), [Wiśniweski 2014](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2599286) ). But within this, governments have lots of leeway.

Some of these regulations could be actually said to _privilege_ the corporations they regulate.

**2.4 Coordination failures also justify government spending on charitable causes**

Regarding world hunger, it is not that clear that there is a coordination failure here. Why? Depending on whether people value alleviating people's suffering vs feeling good by making donations, it will or won't be. For the first case, it is a private good. For the second one, it's a public good. In the first case, for each dollar you donate to a charitable organisation, you get a marginal reduction of poverty or hunger. It's not that if everyone except one person donates, then hunger doesn't go away. People don't really care that much about world hunger in practice ([Leeson, 2014](http://www.peterleeson.com/Homo_Economicus_Lives.pdf)) . This issue is serious, yet how many people devote time to inform themselves about how to better deal with it (e.g. Effective Altruism, knowing about Givewell, etc)? Almost no one. Yet those resources to acquire information are free.

In the OECD, which I will use as proxy for developed countries, there are about 1.3 billion people. Let's assume all of them can donate. So 1.3_1_(365/4)=118.6 bn$ per year. If [Jeffrey Sachs is right](http://www.visionofearth.org/economics/ending-poverty/how-much-would-it-cost-to-end-extreme-poverty-in-the-world/), and ending poverty costs 175bn$ per year, people would have to pay 1,5$ per week for 20 years (This assumes kids will also pay, so the estimate is a lower bound). But Sachs [is not without his critics](http://www.washingtonpost.com/wp-dyn/articles/A25562-2005Mar10.html). [Givewell](http://blog.givewell.org/2015/11/06/the-lack-of-controversy-over-well-targeted-aid/) accepts that sort of criticism for that sort of aid plans, while defending that their approach: bottom-up, cost-effective, step-by-step interventions doesn't have the same problems. Sadly, there are no obvious solutions to lift the world's poor out of their situation, and if there isn't, I think it's difficult to justify coercion to help towards that goal.  So far, $4.6 _trillion_ have been donated towards foreign aid in the last 50 years. Is it really believable that just with 175bn$ per year for 20 years, there is going to be a substantial increase on the global quality of life of the poor, above that produced by economic growth?

Finally, if this argument succeeded, it may be that it proves too much: Wouldn't we have to, on consequentialist grounds, eliminate the welfare State to a bare minimum, and donate huge amounts of money to the absolutely poor?

If people really cared that much, they have a handy mechanism to make everyone pay: the State. Massive foreign redistribution, or open [borders](http://openborders.info/) is something that's hard to see defended by political parties, or asked for by voters.

One further thought is that States may be making this problem worse. The EU's [Common Agricultural Policy](http://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/politics/eu-subsidies-deny-africas-farmers-of-their-livelihood-478419.html), and [US subsidies for biofuels](https://conversableeconomist.blogspot.com/2013/01/biofuels-and-hunger-in-low-income.html) do harm the poor in other countries, while benefitting interest groups inside their borders.

\*\*2.5 & 2.5.1 We need labor regulation \*\*

We don't. Outsourced to [Bryan Caplan](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2015/09/scott_alexander_3.html).

**3\. Irrational Choices**

**3.1 People are not rational. They don't choose the best means to reach their goals: if someone is signed up into a pension plan by default, he will stay. If someone is not signed out by default, he won't join it. Transaction costs are almost zero, so it shouldn't make a difference.**

Governments are [also people](https://johnhcochrane.blogspot.com/2014/11/behavioral-political-economy.html), and coercing people to implement alternative choice architectures to fix minor cases of irrationality that exist are not justified, especially if the proposed solution is worse than the problem. Highly problematic forms of irrationality will end up inducing social norms and institutions that will fix them.

The arguments here seems to be like

> 1.  Markets maximize welfare under stringent epistemic conditions including apt probability estimation, stability of preferences, an absence of framing effects or externalities, etc.
>     
> 2.  One or more of those conditions do not hold in a given market because individuals are less than fully rational.
>     
> 3.  That market thus fails to maximize welfare.  ([Boettke et al. 2013](http://mercatus.org/publication/error-obvious-coordination-puzzle-0)) .
>     

But markets don't really need that to be efficient.

Consider the case of pensions. If one behavioural argument is right, then people won't save enough for retirement, and we'll have masses of relatively poor elderly people. This hypothesis is grounded in [human psychology](https://www.ssa.gov/policy/docs/ssb/v70n4/v70n4p1.html), and presumably, for the argument to take effect and be immune to the critique of the welfare-induced irrationality ("The ultimate result of shielding men from the effects of their folly, is to fill the world with fools" (Spencer, in [Zwolinski 2015](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2598818))), we would have to observe those masses of people in really bad conditions before the coming of public pensions. Also, public pensions will have appeared because private pensions were not working, or existed but were stagnant, or something like that.

So why did public pensions appear? There are many theories, and a paper that studies them, and finds that several reasons account for it. The first countries in adopting them were Germany,(1889), Denmark (1891) and New Zeland (1898), and the last ones were Switzerland, (1948), Netherlands,  (1947), Argentina, (1944), and Japan (1942). ([Cutler et al. 2004](http://link.springer.com/article/10.1023%2FB%3APUCH.0000035859.20258.e0)).He doesn't mention the terrible conditions of the old as a general, or even prevalent explanation, so the hypothesis of massive market failure in providing old-age pensions is in principle rejected. One question would remain: what was the poverty rate for old people before and after mandatory pension schemes?

Before public pensions there were sickness funds. Non-profit associations for the provision of , amongst others, old-age pensions. This was a time before financial markets were well developed, so there was no possibility of widespread pension funds as we know them today, and yet people managed to avoid ruin, in general. ([Immergut, 1992](https://www.sowi.hu-berlin.de/de/lehrbereiche/comppol/pubb/pdfs/Immergut1992a.pdf)). Ideally, we would look for statistics from before and after mandatory insurance was introduced,~but those a hard to come by~.

After some Google Scholar archaeology, I found some statistics from the pre-Social Security period in the US, and I think this confirms my account more than Scott's: the elderly do fine without State intervention.

First, there's [Weaver (1987)](http://object.cato.org/sites/cato.org/files/serials/files/cato-journal/1987/11/cj7n2-15.pdf) who explains how society cared about its elders before the State took care of them. There were, as described above, several mechanisms, including savings, family care, various types of insurance, pensions, and charity, and the reason why Social Security appeared was not due to a systemic problem, but due to the harsh conditions imposed on the poor by the Great Depression. Instead of being a set of emergency measures, Social Security was instituted as permanent, and then grew to be what it is now ([Miron and Neil, 1998](http://www.nber.org/chapters/c6897.pdf)). Some even argue not only that these institutions were good enough, but that they were economically optimal ([Emery, 2010](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0014498309000345))

They worked until a later age than now, too. But we also know that retirement age had been going down decades before Social Security, and continued going down at the same pace after it ([Lee, 2000](http://hosting03.snu.ac.kr/%7Echullee/pub/aging_us/jpe14.pdf)a)

Papers that do endorse public interventions, like [Valdés-Prieto (2004)](http://web.worldbank.org/archive/website00894A/WEB/PDF/SVALDE-2.PDF) are more theoretical than empirical, and don't take into account the historical evidence like [Carter and Sutch (1995)](https://core.ac.uk/download/files/153/6894713.pdf), or [Gratton (1997)](http://psychsocgerontology.oxfordjournals.org/content/52B/3/S117.full.pdf), who shows that the Social Security Administration overreported elderly poverty in order to have legislation passed to strengthen their role.

Earlier scholarship on Social Security did show that the elderly were impoverished, but now the consensus view is that they were not, though some disagreements remain ([Lee, 2000](http://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/viewdoc/download?doi=10.1.1.195.69&rep=rep1&type=pdf)b).

So

> Until convincing evidence proves otherwise, we should assume that most older people in the early twentieth century fared relatively well by the standards of the time, that the economic well-being of the elderly improved during this period, and that younger people expected to achieve economic security in old age. Given these findings, the creation of a welfare state dedicated to generous transfers to the aged becomes a great curiosity. Nothing in the economic evidence substantiates overwhelming need among the elderly, as opposed to some other group in U.S. society. Indeed, by the late 1930s, SSB bureaucrats knew full well that children were much more likely to be impoverished than the aged. What then was the source of Social Security? Weaver's political economy approach identifies a state bureaucracy intent on expansion of the old age insurance title in the social security program. ([Gratton,  1996](http://econpapers.repec.org/article/cupjechis/v_3a56_3ay_3a1996_3ai_3a01_3ap_3a39-61_5f01.htm))

**5. Just Desserts and Social Mobility**

**5.1 Children of the rich are more likely to be rich, and children of the poor are more likely to be poor**

True, but maybe not to the extent the FAQ says. If intergenerational income elasticity means, roughly, how much [mobility](https://randomcriticalanalysis.wordpress.com/2015/06/07/understanding-socioeconomic-mobility/) there is, Denmark is _three times_ more mobile. If instead of measuring intergenerational income mobility, you measure rank-mobility (Correlation between parents being in a quartile and the quartile the child ends up), the US has an average upward social mobility (and greater downward mobility), on par with Europe's ([Corak et al. 2014](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0927537114000530)). If you instead focus on how many people born poor stay poor, [the results do show](http://www.keepeek.com/Digital-Asset-Management/oecd/social-issues-migration-health/growing-unequal_9789264044197-en#page172) that the US is less mobile: around 66.6% (or  58.2%, in another measure) of the poor remain poor,  in Denmark this figure is 60.5% (Compare 62.5% for UK,, 71.9% for Finland, 62.5% in Australia). If you then use transition matrices, then you get different results, which confirm again that the US is less mobile, but not as little mobile as elasticities suggest: the US is 10% less mobile than Denmark, not 300%. This isn't the only issue, though: being poor in the US is worse than being poor in Denmark.

The US is no libertarian paradise, so this isn't hardly useful for the debate, though. Rather, compare the Nordics to Switzerland, Singapore, Hong Kong or New Zeland. From a Rawlsian point of view, you may prefer to be born in one of these countries rather than in a Social Democracy. As an example, if you measure [Eurostat's material deprivation rate](http://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/web/income-and-living-conditions/data/main-tables) by age group in 2013, you get that in Denmark 8.8% were poor, compared to _3.7%_ in Switzerland, 11.6% in Germany, 8.5% in Finland, 4.3% in Sweden, 5.6% in Luxembourg, 4.8% in Norway, and 17.4% in the UK, and 16.9% in Spain. Granted, in all of those countries there is a State that does redistribution, but their sizes wildly differ. Given a desired outcome, it seems reasonable that the less coercion required to achieve it, the better.

That said, I don't know any academic libertarian philosopher who talks about this moocher/productive distinction, or that hard work makes you deserve stuff.

The US is also an argument why having the State spend more does not necessarily translate into better outcomes for the poor: t[he US](http://www.nytimes.com/roomfordebate/2015/10/20/can-the-us-become-denmark/us-social-spending-is-already-on-par-with-scandinavia) [has a larger welfare state](https://www.aei.org/publication/the-us-welfare-state-and-safety-net-are-bigger-than-you-think-but-who-are-they-helping/)[than Denmark](http://www.nber.org/papers/w15982) if you account for tax breaks and [other things](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2629585). Where's the catch? Denmark focuses [more on the poor](http://lanekenworthy.net/2010/06/07/social-spending-and-poverty/), and the US more in the middle class.

To sum up: The Nordics are very good, but not optimal, nor an argument against libertarianism. Some [libertarian authors](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/10/is-the-us-the-most-economically-libertarian-country/) [actually](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2015/10/denmark-libertarian-welfare-state/) [argue that](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/05/the-administrative-state-vs-the-social-insurance-state/) the Nordics are more libertarian than the US itself!

You could now ask: Well, if you think the Nordics are so libertarian vis-a-vis the US, if you had a magic button to transform the US into a huge version of Denmark (Copying the best things Denmark has to offer, so that the end result is a mix of the best of both worlds), would you press it? And if we're ex-ante sure it works as its theorist say, then the anarchy button is at the top of the ranking.

**5.3 Therefore, if success is externally determined, redistribution.**

Wait, why?

I think the FAQ has the same problem Rawls had: He notes that no one deserves anything (more or less), and from there he follows that everything is up for grabs. As I argued in [another post](https://artir.wordpress.com/2015/02/01/rawls-redistribucion-de-organos-e-ingenieria-psiquica/), this argument also licenses, in principle, organ redistribution, and thought-engineering to ensure people act in such a way as to be people that work towards a society that makes the worse off better off, want it or not.

Here is the Rawlsian view:

> Since most of who we are and what we do is greatly influenced by undeserved native endowments and by the undeserved circumstances into which we are born, one cannot deserve anything, or, at best, one can deserve very little.  According to a common interpretation, Rawls believes that desert should not have any role in distributive justice, since these undeserved factors have a major influence on all would-be desert bases (Sher 1987, 22 ff).  ([IEP](http://www.iep.utm.edu/desert/))

The problem is not rejecting desert. I basically reject it. The problem is saying that desert is a precondition of entitlement, and that requires an argument.

Rejection of desert or merit, I think, is the mainstream philosophical view today, including among libertarian academics. And even if it were not, the mainstream view among libertarians is that there is no connection between desert and entitlement. It was Robert Nozick who famously said:

> We feel more comfortable upholding the justice of an entitlement system if most of the transfers under it are done for reasons. This does not mean necessarily that all deserve what holdings they receive. \[…\] We noted earlier that the entitlement conception of justice in holdings, not being a patterned conception of justice, does not accept distribution in accordance with moral desert either. Any person may give to anyone else any holding he is entitled to, independently of whether the recipient morally deserves to be the recipient. ([Anarchy, State, and Utopia](https://www.amazon.com/Anarchy-State-Utopia-Robert-Nozick/dp/0465051006))

[Hayek and Milton Friedman](https://andrewlister.wordpress.com/2014/05/12/mankiw-on-just-deserts/) also rejected that.

To be sure, there are libertarians who accept the notion of desert and merit, like [Bryan Caplan](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2012/06/the_meritocracy.html), but they don't make desert the core of their justification for free markets.

This mistake is what I've called the Unjust World Fallacy. The well-known Just World Fallacy, applied to this case says: 1. Poor people deserve being poor 2. If they deserve so, it is just for them to be poor 3. Therefore, it is just for the poor to be poor.

The Unjust World Fallacy is: 1. Poor people don't deserve being poor 2. If they don deserve so, it is unjust for them to be poor 3. Therefore it is unjust for the poor to be poor.

Both second premises are mistaken, as there is no justice and desert connection. Additional arguments are required to go from lack of desert to lack of entitlement and the need of redistribution.

That said, you can defend that there exists a general moral duty to help the poor, but not because they deserve it, but just because it is good to help others, and doing more good is better than doing less good, so if you are going to dedicate resources to helping others, if you target the poor you will get lots of helping done.

Not only they are high historically, in the US they [are](http://taxfoundation.org/sites/taxfoundation.org/files/docs/ETR1.png) [absurdly high](http://mercatus.org/publication/updated-corporate-income-tax-rates-oecd) even if you compare them to, say, the Nordics. Estimates for the effective tax rate vary, some say it has been [going down](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/3e/US_Effective_Corporate_Tax_Rate_1947-2011_v2.jpg), so that they are somewhat [lower than OECD average](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/Effective_Corporate_Tax_Rate_OECD_Countries,_2000-2005_Average.jpg) (16.1). Corporations in the [SP500](http://d35brb9zkkbdsd.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2013/02/corporatetax45.jpg), though, pay around 30% of their income, which is really high. So the companies you think of when you think of 'corporations' do pay really high taxes.

**6.3.2 We're at the left of the Laffer curve, and it is false that high tax rates discourage production, real income has low elasticity with respect to taxes. What taxes affect is taxable income, so increasing taxes x% will bring less than x% of revenue, but it's still positive.**

If overall taxation is a proxy for government size, then unfortunately the consensus is that increasing the government size (and taxation required to fund it) by 10 percentage pints leads to a 0.5%-1% less lower growth rate ([Bergh et al. 2011](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-6419.2011.00697.x/abstract), [Bergh et al. 2015](http://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joes.12103/full)).

If you measure aggregate wages and aggregate productivity, [there has been no decoupling](http://juanramonrallo.com/2014/08/el-mito-del-desacople-entre-salarios-y-productividad-en-eeuu/), if you were thinking in that.

**6.4 Trickle down doesn't work**

As said before, no serious person defends trickle-down.

Are the poor getting poorer? You need to look at full compensation, not as just income. Market income depends upon what the government does, you can't just measure it in isolation. As wages=productivity more or less holds, you can estimate that free market wages=actual compensation today. Doing that, we see that the poor [are not getting poorer, but richer](http://www.brookings.edu/blogs/social-mobility-memos/posts/2014/02/07-five-poverty-facts-reeves), at a slower pace than the rich.

**6.5 Raising taxes won't fix the deficit. But cutting lots of things, including all of welfare, won't do it either.**

US federal deficit was 438$bn in 2015 and 616$bn in 2016. Let's say 500$bn. When Scott wrote, it was 1.2$tn. If Medicaid, Medicare, and Social Security are cut, and defense is cut in half, you get savings of  1.9tn$, so technically, you can do it. Now, you wouldn't want to do it in a year, that'd be dangerous. For the case of Spain, there is a transition plan proposed[here](https://www.amazon.es/Revoluci%C3%B3n-Liberal-Espa%C3%B1a-Economia-deusto/dp/8423418537) to a minimal state (5% of GDP), and it takes 50 years to transition.

**6.6 Little taxes go to foreigners and the poor. Most taxes go to benefit middle income citizens.**

Indeed. Welfare states are not about rich-to-poor redistribution, but mostly medium class-to-medium class redistribution. Nordics included.

This is because of [Director's Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director%27s_law) (also [here](https://katalepsisblog.wordpress.com/2015/05/10/estado-democracia-redistribucion-y-los-mas-desaventajados-bleeding-heart-libertarianism/), in Spanish).

-   Smallpox and polio eradication. Okay for Smallpox. Polio eradication is a [public-private effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poliomyelitis_eradication). -   The internet: Modems, glass fibers, most communication satellites, almost all submarine cables (which carry 99% of the traffic), ISPs and their peerings, and every website of interest, is privately designed, owned, and managed. _That_ is the internet. Now, if you go to the network that preceded the internet, ARPANET, it was the work of ARPA, and a few subcontractors. I have no trouble granting the invention of the internet to ARPA. Read here [a story](https://www.amazon.com/Where-Wizards-Stay-Up-Late/dp/0684832674).
-   Regular highways: [Private highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway#History) [precede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Highway) government's, as do private roads, in general. If you mean building more of them, then yes, the government did that.
-   Clean, free water: Work initially began by private utilities, plus it isn't really free.
-   Electricity: Same as above.
-   Forcing integration, leading the struggle for civil rights: Even if this were true, you would have to weight this against flagrant violation of civil rights (think of apartheid in South Africa or segregation in the US). Regarding civil rights, I don't know what Scott exactly means, but I think civil rights extension was a thing of civil rights movements, not governments.
-   GPS: Okay
-   Disaster forecasts: Okay. Governments have been at the satellite business from the beginning, and competing with free services is hard.
-   No bank runs: Okay. But bank runs before were usually caused by government regulations, and the possibility of them happening is [a good thing](http://www.huffingtonpost.com/lauren-windsor/bank-runs-are-good-cato-director-says_b_7515292.html) and government management of money has been worse than what came before it ([Selgin et al. 2012](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0164070412000304)), and even worse when compared to fully private banking. Inflation under the FED has been higher and more volatile ([Hogan, 2015](http://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016407041400130X)). The banking system ought to be [reformed](http://www.adamsmith.org/research/reports/sound-money-an-austrian-proposal-for-free-banking-ngdp-targets-and-omo-reforms/).
-   Nuclear power: Okay. But you can imagine an alternate future where nuclear energy progressed at a slower pace, with safer, and smaller reactors, so that it gets less bad press. The general problem with innovations is that it's hard to know what would have happened if some particular agent hadn't player it's role. My position on this is that for technological improvement it doesn't matter, and that for practical implementation, it does.
-   Game theory to avoid misusing it. Game theory was birthed by von Neumann, without government intervention. After he invented it, the government subsequently hired him to continue his work.

Whatever I could had said above, what matters is whether the private sector would have come up with those inventions and advances, and I see no reason why not. We could then debate, case by case, how longer would have taken for a market process to achieve them.

Also, think about the many things governments [did wrong](https://www.hawaii.edu/powerkills/MURDER.HTM) or that could have done wrong (nuclear war). Right now, think about over-regulation and excessive defense spending in the US.

Contra Scott -and Schumpeter- innovation does not happen just at large companies shielded from competition. There is no causality pointing from more concentrated markets to more innovative markets. **8\. Healthcare**

**8.1 Government-run health systems do better than private health systems, and are cheaper. Sweden, France, Canada UK (single payer)  vs the US (private). Public systems win at life expectancy, infant mortality, and cancer.**

I reject the fundamental premise: the US does not have a private healthcare system. Or, if we accept that it does, it is private in the sense the Federal Reserve is a private bank. One thing is that a substantial part of the US healthcare system is privately owned. Another is the degree of regulation that sector has, and the effects it has over how it behaves. I will explain this in this section.

Another criticism to be made is that the [populations are not the same](https://randomcriticalanalysis.wordpress.com/2014/11/29/some-other-issues-with-comparing-us-healthcare-costs-and-so-called-outcomes/), and that biases outcome comparisons in the variables presented by the FAQ. See here for [more](http://www.econtalk.org/archives/2012/07/scott_atlas_on.html).

No one defends the US healthcare system as it is now, as far as I know. And it's not because of an implicit no-true-Scotsman fallacy: there are reasons why libertarians criticise the US healthcare reason, and propose measures to fix it:

-   [Tax-subsidized](http://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2010/02/henry-aaron-writes-to-me.html) [health insurance that](http://mercatus.org/publication/tax-exemption-employer-provided-health-insurance) [distorts the market](http://www.npr.org/sections/health-shots/2012/12/04/166434247/the-huge-and-rarely-discussed-health-insurance-tax-break)
-   Medicare, Medicaid , etc ([45% of total spending](https://www.cms.gov/research-statistics-data-and-systems/statistics-trends-and-reports/nationalhealthexpenddata/downloads/highlights.pdf))
-   And [others](http://mercatus.org/publication/american-health-care-system-principles-successful-reform)

The problem of US healthcare in a nutshell: In a healthcare system, someone should be doing the rationing. In the US, no one is. Unlike in Europe, the public part of the US healthcare system does contribute to price inflation because of that.

Lots of things could be said about healthcare and how a pure free market there would work, but this is a Non-Non-Libertarian FAQ, not a place where to present a full defense of any particular position.

A fairer comparison would involve students in Finland itself, but there are no private schools for such comparison. If you compare Macao and Hong Kong (>90% in private schools) with Finland, these countries win over the Nordic country in PISA (500 vs 485-478 of Finland).

Contrary to Scott, School ownership [does not make a difference](http://www.oecd.org/pisa/pisaproducts/pisainfocus/48482894.pdf) (also [this](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2505333) and [this](http://www.oecd.org/pisa/50110750.pdf) and [this](http://www.oecd.org/education/school/programmeforinternationalstudentassessmentpisa/34668095.pdf)) in quality if both schools have the same level of freedom to set their schooling policies, after adjusting for SES.

**11.2 Libertarians believe in equality of opportunity, but that requires regulating schools**

If by that you mean mandated equality, I think they don't believe in that. As discussed above, genes, and external factors determine to a great extent your chances in life. By default there is no equality of opportunity or of results.

Regarding the extreme cases Scott discusses, in Libertarialand, some 'regulation' would be justified as a means to avoid fraud. Offering things that sound like educational services but aren't is like selling rotten apples. The thing is, this may not be enough for some people. If you want full equality of opportunity, you need to equalize not only schools, but food intake, parents, universities, friends, and mostly everything you find in your life. This is doable to some extent, especially with the future advent of cheap genetic selection, but the philosophical question remains of whether it is justified to coerce others to accept these more extreme measures, or to coerce others to get resources to implement said measures.

**11.3 The government should protect children from their parents**

Surely. But this is not something 'special'. Children have rights, and it is right to protect them: any properly structured organisation that can do that will do. It is a violation of the rights of the child to mistreat it, or even not to give it adequate resources, given the resources of the parents. There are interesting debates about whether libertarianism actually [requires](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/07/libertarianism-and-parental-licensing/) [licensing parents](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2011/12/licensing-parents-2/) to have children as a matter of justice.

### Part D: Moral Issues

\*\*The Argument: \*\*_Moral systems based only on avoiding force and respecting rights are incomplete, inelegant, counterintuitive, and usually riddled with logical fallacies. A more sophisticated moral system, consequentialism, generates the principles of natural rights and non-initiation of violence as heuristics that can be used to solve coordination problems, but also details under what situations such heuristics no longer apply. Many cases of government intervention are such situations, and so may be moral._

**The Counterargument:\_ \_**_Consequentialism suffers from the same problems that absolute rights theories suffer from, and lead sometimes to even more unintuitive situations sometimes. As of today, there is no fully coherent ethical system to make decisions in any given circumstance, but we can rely on reflective equilibrium to make our ethical intuitions and ethical principles cohere._

**12.2 Taxation is theft, yes, but everyone accepts that. The only force this argument has is emotional**

I don't know if everyone accepts that, but everyone accepts that theft ought not be done in general, and that the particular kinds of theft governments undertake would also be criticised if regular people did them. People think governments have political authority, that's why they accept it. The argument against taxes has force insofar as regular theft is rejected, which it is.

**12.3 Why should the initiation of force against innocent people be wrong?**

Most people would say it is wrong, and seems obvious. Consider this thought experiment: You are told that a government is about to use force against an innocent person. You can make that use of force happen or not. Most people would stop that use of force. A consequentialist, given no more information, would be indifferent and flip a coin to decide. Alternatively, she would think that given real world governments, it is more likely that the use of violence against the innocent will lead to bad consequences, and chose to stop it. This response is somewhat defused by considering a government for which you are told, explicitly, that you know nothing about, in an alien planet far away.

It can be the case that, given more information, it would be right, all things considered, to use force against an innocent. Would you punch a random person in exchange of the future existence of mankind? Most people would.

Initiation of force is _prima facie_ wrong, and it can be right in some cases, but the burden of proof if on those who argue for it.

**12.3.1 Sometimes, the use of force has good consequences**

Sometimes, yes. But what you would need are occasions where the use of force has good consequences, and it is justified in that circumstance. If successful, that argument wouldn't justify a government as understood today, but a very narrow governance institution, and that only if the overall institutional framework is better than an alternative.

Suppose there are two kids who will die in a year due to a rare illness. Suppose now there is a person with a peculiar genetic makeup such that he has resistance to the illness. Science says that if experiments are performed on that person, a cure can be produce before one year to save the kids. Here, the consequentialist recommendation is to kidnap this fellow and experiment with him. Everybody else would object to such kidnapping. Consequentialists may want to avoid this by resorting to rule-consequentialism. I have some comments regarding this in the following sections.

**12.3.2 Non-consequentialism is arbitrary**

Few people follow theories that are fully consequence-independent. Noninterference with other people seems to be one very universal principle, but that doesn't lead you to consequentialism or utilitarianism.

The claim of arbitrariness could also be thrown at consequentialism: why is harm bad? Why should each of us consider everyone's[interests equally](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/05/why-not-utilitarianism/)?

In the end, if we understand arbitrariness as "beginning from false or dubious premises", we would have to focus on ethics, and study the relevant premises. That is outside of the scope of this FAQ.

**12.3.2.1 The Principle of Non-Aggresion is a hook to hang pre-existing prejudices**

Maybe for some people it is. Indeed, people believe in something like the NAP, and a belief being widely held makes it hard to eradicate if it is false. This claim can also be leveled at political authority.

Some versions of the NAP may require hardcore epicycling to work. But the same thing is done by utilitarians when they resort to rule utilitarianism, or by changing the definition of utility (pleasure, preferences, welfare, etc). In the end, most moral systems clash with some or other strong intuition, this is not a problem only for utilitarianism or the NAP.

That said, full, hardcore exceptionless NAP has its defenders, but [not every](http://www.libertarianism.org/blog/six-reasons-libertarians-should-reject-non-aggression-principle) libertarian agrees with the version of it that the FAQ criticises.

**12.3.2.1.1 Arguments for the NAP are weak**

Many are, specifically Rothbard's and Hoppe's ([Eabrasu 2012](http://ppe.sagepub.com/content/12/3/288.abstract)). But perhaps the idea that the things that form part of one's personal projects, body included should not be aggressed against is one plausible enough to be self-evidently accepted (As is the idea that harm is to be avoided). This doesn't solve the issue of what things become property, to what extent things are property, and if what is property today still is property tomorrow. That's why you need a legal system for. Libertarians accept the Lockean Proviso, so the situation described for Tristan de Cunha can't happen.

Here is A. John Simmons, who is no libertarian, but a philosophical anarchist Lockean, interpreting Locke to explain the Proviso:

> Fairness, then, seems to generate a conflict. Fair acquisition may result in resources being closed out (or in the requirement that any reminder of them be left common, thereby eliminating rightful appropriation). But fairness also demands a share for later persons. Governments must settle such conflicts. But these conflicts need not be insuperable, since later persons can get their share by getting access to a living. Fairness does not necessarily require the redistribution of initially fair acquisitions. **It requires only that persons who cannot appropriate a share are not denied access to their share or room to exercise their rights of self-preservation and self-government. Where these latters goods are precluded, later persons must be allowed to appropriate the property of earlier ones**. This conception of a “fair share limit” on appropriation, holding, and transfer seems to me a plausible addition to the mixing argument. (The Lockean Theory of Rights (1994, ch 5))

Here is Nozick

> A theory of appropriation incorporating this Lockean proviso will handle correctly the cases (objections to the theory lacking the proviso) where someone appropriates the total supply of something necessary for life (For example, Rashdall’s case of **someone who comes upon the only water in the desert** several miles ahead of others who also will come to it and appropriates it all.) A theory which includes this proviso in its principle of justice in acquisition must also contain a more complex principle of justice in transfer. **Some reflection of the proviso about appropriation constrains later actions. If my appropriating all of a certain substance violates the Lockean proviso, then so does my appropriating some and purchasing all the rest from others who obtained it without otherwise violating the Lockean proviso**. If the proviso excludes someone’s appropriating all the drinkable water in the world, it also excludes his purchasing it all. (More weakly, and messily, it may exclude his charging certain prices for some of his supply.) This proviso (almost?) never will come into effect; the more someone acquires of a scarce substance which others want, the higher the price of the rest will go, and the more difficult it will become for him to acquire it all. \[…\] The total supply could not be permissibly appropriated by one person at the beginning. His later acquisition of it all does not show that the original appropriation violated the proviso (even by a reverse argument similar to the one above that tried to zip back from Z to A). R**ather, it is the combination of the original appropriation plus all the later transfers and actions that violates the Lockean proviso. Each owner’s title to his holding includes the historical shadow of the Lockean proviso on appropriation**. This excludes his transferring it into an agglomeration that does violate the Lockean proviso and excludes his using it in a way, in coordination with others or independently of them, so as to violate the proviso by making the situation of others worse than their baseline situation. Once it is known that someone’s ownership runs afoul of the Lockean proviso, there are stringent limits on what he may do with (what it is difficult any longer unreservedly to call) “his property.” Thus a person may not appropriate the only water hole in a desert and charge what he will. Nor may he charge what he will if he possesses one, and unfortunately it happens that all the water holes in the desert dry up, except for his. This unfortunate circumstance, admittedly no fault of his, brings into operation the Lockean proviso and limits his property rights.  Similarly, an owner’s property right in the only island in an area does not allow him to order a castaway from a shipwreck off his island as a trespasser, for this would violate the Lockean proviso. \[…\] I believe that the free operation of a market system will not actually run afoul of the Lockean proviso. (Recall that crucial to our story in Part I of how a protective agency becomes dominant and a de facto monopoly is the fact that it wields force in situations of conflict, and is not merely in competition, with other agencies. A similar tale cannot be told about other businesses.) If this is correct, the proviso will not play a very important role in the activities of protective agencies and will not provide a significant opportunity for future state action. Indeed, were it not for the effects of previous illegitimate state action, people would not think the possibility of the proviso’s being violated as of more interest than any other logical possibility. (Here I make an empirical historical claim; as does someone who disagrees with this.) This completes our indication of the complication in the entitlement theory introduced by the Lockean proviso. (Anarchy, State, and Utopia)

And [here](https://docs.google.com/viewer?a=v&pid=sites&srcid=ZGVmYXVsdGRvbWFpbnxwcm9mZXNzb3JlcmljbWFja3xneDo2NTY2NzUwMTAxMzVhM2Zi) is Eric Mack, discussing the case of the island and other, similar, case.

Here are [Brennan](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2013/02/social-justice-and-the-legitimacy-of-property/) and [Zwolinski](http://www.libertarianism.org/blog/locke-nozick-justification-property), discussing the proviso. (Brennan even notes that academic libertarians [don't even use the NAP](https://www.facebook.com/cathyreisenwitz/posts/10100342701046569) as a_basis_ for libertarianism)

Arguments for private ownership of stuff don't say that because you have your body, therefore you own it. They explain why you _own_ a body, and then the rest of the things. Bodies are not qualitatively special.

Plus, banning bodily self-ownership as a foundational moral value is question begging. [Everyone agrees](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/11/self-ownership-an-uncontroversial-conclusion-among-liberals-not-a-controversial-premise/) to some version of self-ownership.

**12.3.2.1.2 The NAP has a weird definition of force, and libertarians tolerate certain initiations of force, and the NAP requires a sausage of exceptions to work.**

Definitions are arbitrary. 'Initiation of force' is defined as violating rights, and rights come to be according to the libertarian principles of homesteading and contract.

Rights in theory shouldn't conflict, if in practice they do, courts will have to try to interpret them the best they can. This is, with different rights, how courts work right now. And consequentialist considerations are allowed to play a role in determining those hard cases.

**12.4 Consequentialism is better than the NAP**

People are, in general, not implicit or full consequentialists. [Not even Scott Alexander](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/01/16/the-ethics-of-scott-alexander/). Even self-declared consequentialists do not [behave like such](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2014/07/the_argument_fr_1.html), even though it is [possible](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2015/sep/22/extreme-altruism-should-you-care-for-strangers-as-much-as-family) to do so. Consequences matter, and other people matter. But not infinitely.

Also, capitalism is not driven by consequentialism, but by rights-bounded search for entrepreneurial arbitrage opportunities. People are not usually trying to unconstrainedly maximise their (or everyone's) welfare, even if economists model firms as profit-maximising because it's close enough to reality. Plus, consequentialism in the way usually depicted in the FAQ is different from ethical egoism, or do what's best for you, disregard the rest of the world.

**12.4.1 You decide what consequences are good**

(Consumerism tells you? Huh?) I think there is a confusion here in mistaking economic decisionmaking for ethical theorising. Decisions have opportunity costs, and we face tradeoffs. Those have to, and are, factored in when we think about what's the right thing to do.

**12.4.2 If you care about other people, take them into account in your ethical calculations**

Most people do that, but that's not a purely consequentialistic thing. Plus, this reasoning is impotent vs ethical egoists, or national consequentialists who don't care about foreigners, or even against family consequentialists. Or... white supremacist consequentialists.

**12.4.3 Consequentialism won't become a war of all vs all because people will agree to a set of rules to avoid so**

Yeah, but that undermines previous reasoning earlier in the FAQ. Individually, I'm maximising my good optimally if the rest of the people refrains from maximising their goods and let me maximise mine. What incentive do I have to bind myself? I don't control other people, so I can calculate the expected utility of each decision, and go for maximising my good anyways. In reality, people would probably coordinate, as I've explained above, byt the FAQ is quite critical with overcoming anti-coordination incentives.

**12.4.4 That won't lead to undesirable situations like slavery, decadence or some dystopia**

Who knows. Maybe those things do maximise utility. Maybe some people will totally think that the right thing to do, long term-discounted-welfarewise, is for the US to conquer the world, enslave sub-100 IQ people, and coordinate global resources to wirehead humanity in order to get a future of eternal brain stimulation-induced pleasure. **13\. Rights and Heuristics**

**13.1 Rights are conclusions, not premises. Heuristics. They should only be supported if they bring good consequences**

Every argument begins with some premises. Consequentialists have theirs, deontologists have theirs, virtue theorists have theirs, and so on. In consequentialism, rights are (or not) conclusions. In deontology, good consequences are conclusions that follow (or not) from following the right rights structure.

Things like 'I shouldn't kill people, except in very particular circumstances' seem more initially plausible than 'I ought to maximise aggregate utility'. Now, you may ask for a unifying principle for rights from where to deduce them. No one has worked out a moral system that works perfectly, but libertarians may reply that self-ownership or the NAP or the implications of the fact that you are arguing (Hoppe) are such an axiom. But one can reply by rejecting those axioms, or claiming the reasoning is fallacious, as academic libertarians point out, but the same can be said of consequentialists. See [this list](https://artir.wordpress.com/2016/02/26/liberalisms-overlapping-consensus/) for several background philosophies libertarian theorists rely on. Read [this](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/02/libertarianism-consequentialism-and-self-ownership/) and [this](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/02/against-utilitarianism-and-self-ownership-defenses-of-libertarianism/) and [this](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2012/02/eudaimonist-libertarianism/) for further info.

So: Right theorists will say that, one of the reason for rights is that rights-respect is constitutive of a good society by itself, separately from consequences. Since a few libertarian theorists are pluralists, they will say that there are a series of goods, and not just "happiness" or "utility", and that rights are both part of those goods, and means to get them.

**13.2 Consequentialism doesn't imply breaching rights when it seems to smart people that they should be breached, rule consequentialism is the answer.**

There are[some philosophical reasons](http://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consequentialism-rule/#OldObjRulCon)  to be against rule consequentialism. If you are smart, and have thought about the issue, and other smart people agree, it seems that it is morally mandatory to violate someone's rights if that increases utility. If not, you would be tied to respecting the right structure determined at some instant of time, and never revise them.

**13.3 Sometimes, you should change which rights to respect: the alchemist example. Rights are warning signs.**

This doesn't solve the problem presented before. How to determine whether a paradigm shift of sorts should induce a reconsideration of whether a right should still be in place or not? How to determine what is such a circumstance in which we should do that? One solution is abandoning consequentialism and directly applying common sense morality: people have rights, and those can be breached if the consequences are really bad. This is no different from the approach most academic libertarians already take.

And pace Scott, a rule against practising alchemy was a bad idea: people pursuing alchemy ended up doing useful things sometimes, leading up to modern chemistry.

**13.3.1 Property rights are generally useful, but sometimes they can be breached, okay. But if people did that on their own, consequences would be terrible. Therefore, it is best for a central agent to decide uniform rules for when to violate rights: governments.**

If governments are needed to avoid societal breakdown, governments are justified. But is that true? Minarchists say it is, anarchists don't. Is the welfare state required for that? The welfare state was born about 130 years ago in Germany. The modern welfare state is a post-WWII creature. Before it, it doesn't seem like society was at the brink of collapse: it was getting better and better. To decide when it is justified to break rules, you need a court system. Many court systems accept[extreme good consequences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessity) as an excuse.

**13.3.2 Choosing when to violate rights is complicated, but we can have some meta-rules for that: at least must be a procedure to get everyone's input that works. Governments are one such example.**

Scott proposes this. Libertarians will reply rule of law: a common law system can play the same role. Governments work well in developed country, not so in many underdeveloped ones. In democracy, there is no will of the people. There are results that are the outcomes of an election process that is designed by people who can vote (not children, not foreigners), and who vote once on who is going to rule, not issue by issue. Unless you're Switzerland and can pull off large-scale direct democracy.

So democracy fails the universalizability test, unless we restrict the meaning of 'everyone' to 'every adult in a given country'. And still, it can be said that if something seems right to you you should do it. Or if something is wrong, not do it. The rest of the world will react to you, and you knowing this, will take it into account. If there are lots of robberies because people are hungry, people will hire security, and other people may philantrophically provide for them, or even just devise new business models to lower food prices. The system will end up balancing itself.

Regarding government size and well-being: There is no clear correlation between higher taxation rates (or government spending) and standards of living, beyond the fact that only rich societies can sustain a large State.  You can have huge governments like in Kiribati, Cuba, Lesotho or Micronesia and live in poverty, or small ones like in Hong Kong, Switzerland, or Singapore, and live in prosperity. Ideally, we would compare the Nordics to a place with a truly minarchist(<5% GDP) government, and free markets, but we don't have contemporary examples.

**13.4 Governments may make mistakes when acting. But not acting can have worse consequences: the case of the Holocaust and Rwanda.**

The Holocaust wouldn't have happened if everyone had followed a non-interventionist policy from the beginning. No First World War, no Versailles Treaty, no poor beaten Germany, no Hitler, no World War II, and no Holocaust. Pacifism seems to be [the way to go](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2010/04/the_common-sens.html).

And most, if not all, great catastrophes resulting from violating rights have been perpetrated by large governments. It's not that sometimes they violate rights: Some governments regularly do it.

There is an asymmetry in using coercion in war: to use it, it has to be ex-ante crystal clear that it is going to result in the desired consequences, and that rarely it's the case. And where did all the talk about being careful about not violating heuristics even when it seems it's the right thing to do?

\*\*13.5 Libertarians seem to endorse fighting terrorism, which is expensive, but if instead that money went towards fighting cardiovascular diseases, more lives would be saved. There is no benefit in fighting terrorism instead of CVD. \*\*

I haven't surveyed libertarians on this, but here there's [one talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4JYL5VUe5NQ) by Michael Huemer in which he explicitly makes the point that devoting massive amounts of resources to fighting terrorism is irrational. So I think libertarians should be against that. But I don't see the similarity with the previous case, unlike the argument being made is just that killing by omission is the same as directly killing, a premise that almost no one accepts, and that would have to be argued for. On what rational grounds is the decision that there is no difference between killing by omission and directly killing being made?

**13.6 There's a case in which libertarian principles are followed and a catastrophe results: there's a pandemic, and only a guy has the cure, and he charges what he wants for it, and everyone ends up enslaved**

On Lockean Proviso grounds, he would have to provide the cure at a fair price to everyone who asked for it, and it would be justified to coerce him to do so.

This, however, is a very good example of why 'hard' libertarianism is problematic. Regular varieties of it are unaffected by this issue.

**13.6.1 Okay, the story is not realistic, but it is still a reductio ad absurdum. The fact that the heuristic "never initiate force" works is only limited to some cases. The circumstances in which libertarianism works are contingent**

It's a good reduction. But it doesn't work against academic libertarianism. If the world wasn't the way it is, then we would factor that in into the conditions required to violate rights.

**13.7 Being poor in countries without government welfare sucks. Private charity is not solving third-world poverty. Private charity can't plausibly increase as much as required, as some easy math shows.**

Most developed countries do have some kind of government welfare, so it's hard or impossible to find the poor Scott is looking for. There are still homeless and really poor poor people even in advanced social democracies like Denmark, as mentioned above. (Poor after taxes and transfers, that is)

The calculations Scott suggests were made for Spain in a book [here](https://www.amazon.es/Revoluci%C3%B3n-Liberal-Espa%C3%B1a-Economia-deusto/dp/8423418537?ie=UTF8&*Version*=1&*entries*=0) that proposed a detailed transition to a minarchist state (5% GDP) in 50 years, covering specific proposals and budgets for education, healthcare, pensions, etc.

The math itself is right, but one premise is wrong: spending on welfare is not really helping the poor. In the US, [welfare](https://www.washingtonpost.com/news/wonk/wp/2014/01/12/no-we-dont-spend-1-trillion-on-welfare-each-year/) for the poor is the EITC, TANF, SSI,food stamps, housing vouchers, and a child tax credit. In total, $212bn. So the increase in money in the hands of the private sector, would be able to offset the increase in charity required.

Maybe more refinement could be done: The precise calculations outlined by Scott were done in the previously cited book for Spain, which has a higher market-poverty rate than the US. And [Switzerland](http://www.cbpp.org/us-poverty-rate-is-high-after-taxes-and-transfers-compared-to-similarly-wealthy-countries), which I've been citing as an example all along, as a market-poverty rate (by relative standards, I couldn't find pre-market absolute poverty rates) of 15%, even lower than US's post-tax and transfer.

**13.8 People who make bad decisions don't deserve to suffer**

Agree. But insulating people from the consequences or their actions is incentivewise problematic.

### Part E: Practical Issues

\*\*The Argument: \*\*Allowing some power to government won't lead us to tyranny. Libertarians should instead work within the system to oppose regulations that don't work, and accept regulations that do work.

\*\*The Counterargument: \*\*This argument is made not by minarchists, but anarchists. The problem is not tyranny, but a government larger than it should be. In advanced countries, it would be rare that the government became tyrannical. Instead, there is at least one historical example of a country that began on the premise of limited government, and ended up with quite a larger government: the US. [There are](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zd5aJUVZzgw) reforms that can be made to enhance the ways democracies work, but instead of going for a second-best, libertarians will still go for the first-best. Everyone else should follow.

**14\. Slippery Slopes**

**14.1 Giving some power to government won't convert it into a tyranny in the long run**

As explained above, the problem is not tyranny, but overreach ( if some government functions are accepted).

**14.1.1 If we let government have its way, it won't engineer a collapse into dictatorship**

I don't know if there are many libertarians endorsing this. I agree with Scott's view of politics.

**15\. Strategic Activism**

**15.1 If you are smart enough to be a libertarian, you should use your intelligence and energy to enact a proper system**

Given that votes have little power, people have no incentive to do that. This is a huge [coordination failure](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J5maguX5x8c) - of the sort that are used to criticise libertarianism. It's [not an](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/economics/wait-a-minute-is-the-government-self-interested-or-isnt-it/) [utter failure](http://www.adamsmith.org/blog/politics-government/why-we-vote-the-way-we-vote/), though, for the same reason the failures pointed out in markets aren't that bad.

Instead, smart non-libertarians can work to help make the transition to libertarianism :\_)

**15.2 You can't say government cannot be improved, there are people proposing clever arrangements for government**

Futarchy is replacing one piece of government: policymaking. Governments are still governments made of people, and will still have problems. My point isn't that this wouldn't be better. Surely it's possible to improve government, and futarchy is an interesting idea that I would like to see tried, but it will still be a monopoly on force with political authority, and libertarianism may still dominate it: the market process generates information by itself. If not for this, it would be possible to improve upon capitalism by having a futarchic central planner committee.

Nonetheless, the fact remains that the quality of government in a few countries doesn't seem to be improving: the [rate of approval of US Congress](http://www.huffingtonpost.com/2012/08/14/congress-approval-rating-all-time-low-gallup-poll_n_1777207.html) is at a historical minimum, and [Trump](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MKH6PAoUuD0) has shown what happens when a democratic system [works as designed](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2016/03/democracy-works-because-it-doesnt-work/). Perhaps it's time to start advocating[epistocracy](http://econlog.econlib.org/archives/2011/04/epistocracy_and.html), instead of democracy?

And altough this issue is orthogonal to this disquisition and raising this borders on trolling, the nifty theorems of economics mentioned are not necessarily that true: Tetlock's forecasters do better than prediction markets, altough prediction markets remain really good. ([Atanasov, et al. 2015](http://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2660628))

**15.3 Improving government is hard, but libertarianism is politically impossible**

Incremental attempt to reduce government are also more viable than transitioning tomorrow to libertarianism. The problem is not just that reforming the government is difficult. [Some](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zd5aJUVZzgw) libertarians do have reforms for making governments better, even if those libertarians don't want any government at all as a first best. The real problem is that the best possible government is still no match for the best possible libertarianism, and then if a society is able to overcome the many coordination problems that surround politics in order to get good government. Why can't it also solve the coordination problems you wanted to set up government for in the first place?

**15.4 Drawing a line in the sand and banning all government intervention doesn't work. If libertarians want to have some impact, they should take a case by case approach to policies. People won't listen to people who are against every single policy.**

Some libertarians say this: the US government could be taken as a refutation of the theory of limited government. But the conclusion is then to go for anarchism.

As for the later question, I quote Huemer:

> Why is the proposal of this chapter not similarly utopian? Why is it more realistic to expect that citizens convinced of the illegitimacy of government will work to abolish their government than it is to expect that citizens apprised of the flawed policies implemented by a democratic government will work to perfect their government’s policies?
> 
> The answer is that acquiring awareness of the illegitimacy of government in general is much, much less cognitively demanding than acquiring sufficient awareness of the specific policy errors of a particular government to enable one to make rational plans to correct most of those errors. To realize that government is illegitimate, it suffices to accept the arguments in this book. But to identify most of the specific policy errors of one’s government would require detailed familiarity with thousands of statutes and regulations; dozens of government agencies, boards, and commissions; and hundreds of political figures. One would have to update this knowledge continuously throughout one’s life to take account of each new action of each arm of the government. It is much morerealistic to hope that a consensus could be reached on a single philosophical principle, the rejection of authority, than to hope that a consensus could be reached on the specific flaws of most particular government policies.

**16.1 I disagree with you. How should I engage with non-non-libertarian arguments in a way that is most likely to change your mind?**

Being nice, in general. Read academic libertarian philosophers, and read about the history of how things worked before States began doing them. Don't assume consequentialism entails a rejection of libertarianism without having fully explored the mechanisms a libertarian society has to solve the many problems that may arise. And furthermore, don't assume consequentialism to argue for a political philosophy, it will make your job harder. Begin from shared  moral premises, and work out an equilibrium between principles and intuitions. As Scott says, you would have to prove your standards are right from first principles. So far, no one has succeeded deriving a full moral system from scratch. In the future, I might therefore do a critique of the non-consequentialist FAQ. Alternatively, you can show that a realistic libertarian society will be _really bad_ compared to a social democracy, or an alternative system. I think that should convince people to abandon libertarianism.

Don't assume smart libertarians are the [cartoon libertarians](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2013/05/why-talk-about-cartoon-libertarianism/) you find angrily commenting in internet posts or twitter. Like with every ideological group, there are [tiers of sophistication](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2014/04/libertarian-hooliganism-and-vulcanism/).

Also, I address this to libertarians: Libertarians should be aware that the best libertarian theorists are not Rothbard, Hoppe and Rand. They are not very good, actually. Some authors you should look to are: Nozick, Mack, Lomasky, Huemer, Brennan, Zwolinski, D. Friedman and if you can read Spanish, Rallo.

## Epilogue

I hope you have enjoyed this FAQ. I think both libertarians (especially the sort of libertarian Scott has in mind) and non-libertarians will be able to learn something from it.

I don't actually expect you to become a libertarian by reading this. I have not argued for libertarianism here, just explained that the arguments put forward by Scott do not debunk libertarianism. For example, I don't expect you to believe right now that private healthcare is better than public healthcare. I haven't discussed the arguments economists put forward in that debate, or done a full literature review. But I hope to have convinced you that it may be workable.

The way you should think of libertarianism right now should be between two extremes: one, full acceptance. And two, seeing it as an interesting idea that maybe should be tried in a tiny place far away. This is Scott's current position [here](http://slatestarcodex.com/2015/03/18/book-review-the-machinery-of-freedom/). Defending libertarianism is usually done by historical examples, or picking examples here and there, as there is no country that is currently libertarian. Hence, debates about its viability or desirability tend to drag on forever, as there is no hard, conclusive evidence on the matter, like social democrats have with the Nordics.

I want to finish by addressing one thing Scott says in the previously cited post:

> And this leads me into one of my deepest problems with libertarianism and anarcho-capitalism: why should it work? I don’t mean the sort of “why should it work” where you answer with specific reasons why no, monopolies won’t form, and no, people won’t routinely sell themselves into slavery, and no, protection agencies won’t form a new feudal ruling class, and no, people won’t bash their heads against public goods problems and externalities forever without any market solutions appearing, and no, the poor won’t starve to death. I mean the very Outside View question of “why is it that, by coincidence, not using force is an effective way to solve all problems?” Good governance is a really really hard problem. The idea that the solution to this problem contains zero bits of information, that it just solves itself if you leave people alone, seems astonishing. Even if we agree that capitalism works very well by incentivizing companies to do what the consumers wants, there are still a lot of peripheral issues which that just doesn’t cover. Friedman for example is a strong supporter of child rights, because children should mostly be free from coercion from their parents, and that children treated this way turn out better. Now in addition to solving governance with zero bits of information, you have solved optimal child-rearing with zero bits of information. That is implausibly impressive. Given that the universe is allowed to throw whatever problems it wants at us, and that it has so far gleefully taken advantage of that right to come up with a whole host of very diverse and interesting ones, why is it that none of these problems are best addressed by a centralized entity with a monopoly on force? That seems like a pretty basic structure from a game-theoretic perspective, and you’re telling me it just never works in the real world? Shouldn’t there be at least one or two things where a government, or any form of coercive structure at all, is just the right answer? And can’t we just have a small government that does _that_?

This is a very good question. Some months before writing that review, Scott wrote his now famous [Meditations on Moloch](http://slatestarcodex.com/2014/07/30/meditations-on-moloch/). He sees coordination problems everywhere, and not even governments can fix all them: they are also subject to perverse incentives. The solution, then, is an external unmoved mover, an _artificial god,_ to ensure people do cooperate when they want to cooperate but don't have the incentives for it. And since that is really hard, the whole piece has a tone of existential angst, of fear and trembling at the idea of unsolvable coordination problems, not evil, as our greatest problem.

There may be an answer to that. But before considering it, think of the socialist calculation problem. Imagine we're back some decades, and someone writes this to us:

Yes, markets are very good. But hell, how on Earth are they so good that central planning does not work in any industry? Not bread, not steel, coal or oil, not widgets, cars or aircraft. Why? Central planning has advantages, like economies of scale, solving some coordination failures, and reducing duplicities. Why is is that for every conceivable industry you are saying the market works better? And why capitalistic management? Why not worker ownership? How do you know capitalistic ownership works best in every single case?

And I say: If central planning is efficient, that will appear in the market. If worker cooperatives are efficient, that will appear on the market. If worker cooperatives are efficient, that will appear on the market. The market dominates every single alternative, because the market structure will transform into the alternative if the alternative is better, and then readapt if necessary. Today, you might have two big oil companies. Tomorrow, you might have three or eight. The day after tomorrow, maybe none and we're all using solar energy. At the same time, you can have worker owned firms, anarchosyndicalist firms (like Valve), decentralised firms, with extense supply chains, or firms that do everything inhouse (like SpaceX).

This is basically the [First Theorem of Welfare Economics](http://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2007/08/the-first-funda.html). But then, it's possible to say: yeah, but that holds if there is perfect information, perfect competition, and no transaction costs.

It is possible to make two counter arguments here. One, of comparative institutions: Governments are not benevolent social planners. According to the Soylent Green Principle of Institutional Analysis, governments are people. It's possible that the optimal institutional arrangement is one in which markets failures are allowed to exist, but governments failures aren't, by limiting the role of the State.

And the second, more interesting one, is that market failures are [profit opportunities for entrepreneurs](http://www.coordinationproblem.org/2006/05/responding_to_c.html) to solve. It's not that there is a series of conditions that markets need to work properly, it is that an institutional framework that allows markets to work will lead to those conditions: enough competition, enough information, and sufficiently low transaction costs. People are able to step outside the games they are playing, rearrange the incentives, get into the games again, and cooperate.

> If the argument for laissez-faire conditions was a consequence of behavioral assumptions of super heroic behavioral assumptions like you are a fully informed and perfectly rational chooser that exists in a frictionless environment, which is how a lot of people think the argument goes, then all I need to do is undermine the rationality of agents and also the function of the price system as being a perfect guide, so the perfectly competitive model.
> 
> But from Adam Smith, all the way up to Vernon Smith, that’s never the way that they couch their argument for a relatively free economy versus an interventionist economy. They always couch it in terms of comparative institutional analysis and if you look at Adam Smith and the various different examples that he gives, it’s never the case that he relies on a perfectly-informed actor operating in a perfectly competitive market yields an argument for laissez-faire. ([Boettke](http://www.libertarianism.org/media/free-thoughts/austrian-tradition-economics))

Most people see markets as one mechanism and government as another mechanism, and you have to see which one works best in any case. I think a better way of thinking is thinking of markets as a meta-mechanism, that enable many sub-mechanisms (including government-like institutions) to arise from the bottom-up ([Pennington, 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259437437_Elinor_Ostrom_and_the_robust_political_economy_of_common-pool_resources)). Another meta-mechanism is to have everything within a government, which is what we have now, altough that, in turn, is nested inside a state of international anarchy.

The solution to the "Who will watch the watchmen?" problem is not to designate special watchmen, but have everyone, within the domain known by that someone, be a watchmen. This, especially when coupled with some moral norms (people are not and [ought not](http://peterturchin.com/blog/2016/01/19/naked-self-interest-is-a-recipe-for-social-dissolution-a-response-to-branko-milanovic/)[be fully](http://peterturchin.com/blog/2016/01/16/herbert-gintis-societies-whose-business-leaders-have-moral-integrity-are-successful-societies-a-guest-blog/) [selfish](http://peterturchin.com/blog/2016/01/18/robert-frank-ruthless-self-interest-is-not-a-good-business-strategy-a-guest-post/)) is what makes libertarianism the best possible, theoretically and practically, basis for social order and cooperation.

As you may see in this FAQ, most of the disagreements between libertarians and the rest are empirical. It should be possible to study, and agree on how each system would work. Perhaps it's the case that overall libertarianism is better except for the poorest 5% of society, who are somewhat worse off than in a social democracy. Or perhaps it's also better for them. After that is done, we can resume arguing which values are correct.

Some of my answers may have felt like cheating. Am I really giving libertarian answers to the problems Scott raises? Am I a master handwaver?

I have  rejected some absolutist interpretations of the NAP, I have accepted the need for some regulations that could be market provided, and I've praised the Nordics above the US model in some aspects, and so on. I claim that most of the critiques, even if successful, can be addressed by a minimal state.

I think the reasons and explanations offered above are perfectly compatible with the kind of libertarianism proposed by academic libertarians, and so while there are serious problems for the sort of [cartoon libertarians](http://bleedingheartlibertarians.com/2013/05/why-talk-about-cartoon-libertarianism/) that may be the target of the FAQ, the arguments presented there fail to refute more rational and informed variants of libertarianism, while at the same time casting some doubt on its alternatives.
