    - *[everything is correlated](/everything)*:

        - ["Genetic link between family socioeconomic status and children's educational achievement estimated from genome-wide SNPs"](https://www.nature.com/articles/mp20152), Krapohl & Plomin 2016
        - ["LD Hub: a centralized database and web interface to perform LD score regression that maximizes the potential of summary level GWAS data for SNP heritability and genetic correlation analysis"](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/051094.full), Zheng et al 2016 (Web interface to scores of GWAS results, allowing combined inference over them all: [LD Hub](https://ldsc.broadinstitute.org/about/). Gets around privacy & scaling problems by using, not GCTA, but [LD regression](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4495769/ "'LD Score regression distinguishes confounding from polygenicity in genome-wide association studies', Bulik-Sullivan et al 2015"){#ldsc} which only needs summary statistics. LD score regression is definitely the wave of the future. GCTA was king for a few years, but the inability to use summary data is killing it; in the modern context of a thousand different funding sources, 'medical ethics', & empire building driving countless genetic silos, a method must be able to work on just summary statistics if it's to have any real uptake and get _n_ into the hundreds of thousands.)
        - ["Detection and interpretation of shared genetic influences on 42 human traits"](/doc/genetics/heritable/correlation/2016-pickrell.pdf), Pickrell et al 2016
        - ["An Atlas of Genetic Correlations across Human Diseases and Traits"](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/014498.full), Bulik-Sullivan et al 2015
        - ["Genetic contributions to self-reported tiredness"](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/047290.full), Deary et al 2016 (GCTA estimate is not too impressive, but the pleiotropy is interesting. What do you get when you sum a lot of weakly genetically-correlated variables? A giant difference.)
