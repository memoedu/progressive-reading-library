> Countless genres of art start in appropriating objects long incubated in subcultures for originally practical purposes, often becoming fashionable and collectible _because_ no longer practically relevant, such as fancy watches. This seems a little odd, and leads to weird economic situations where brands bend over backwards to try to maintain ‘authenticity’ by, say, showing that some $5,000 pair of sneakers sold to collectors has _some_ homeopathic connection to a real athlete.
> 
> With an infinite design-universe to explore, why does this keep happening and why does anyone care so much? Why, indeed, is _l’art pour l’art_ not enough and people insist on the art being for something else, even when it blatantly is not?
> 
> Because humans respond esthetically to not simply complexity or ornamentation, but to the optimal combination of these in the pursuit of some comprehensible goal, yielding constraint, uniqueness, and comprehensibility. A functional goal _keeps artists honest_, and drives the best design, furnishing an archive of designs that can be mined for other purposes like fashion.
> 
> For that reason, the choice of a goal or requirement can, even if completely irrelevant or useless, be a useful design tool by fighting laziness and mediocrity.
> 
> * * *
> 
> _In Memoriam, [Christopher Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Alexander "Christopher Alexander") (1936–862022)._

Many fashionable esthetics are derived from unfashionable traditions developed by small groups for practical purposes. Indeed, whether [“dark academia”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_academia "Dark academia") or [_gyaru_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyaru "Gyaru") (“gal”), many fashions are simply named after the groups they were copied from (as opposed to their properties). Hence such oddities as ‘American workwear’ as a fashion, resulting in flannel-wearing hipsters in [Red Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Wing_Shoes "Red Wing Shoes") boots aspiring to look like Paul Bunyan, who have never swung an axe; or Japanese military enthusiasts hunting for the [most authentic](https://www.buzzricksons.jp/ "Buzz Rickson’s") WWII-vintage-style [bomber jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_jacket "Flight jacket") or [aviator sunglasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviator_sunglasses "Aviator sunglasses"), the better to look like the American pilots who killed their grandfathers. And _why_, exactly, did we adopt the Western business [suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suit "Suit") & [necktie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necktie "Necktie") from… [“hunters and military officers”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_suits#Regency "History of suits § Regency")? (Have the wily executive memos come to life and must be stalked through the office corridors?)

The endless talk of ‘authenticity’ betrays a certain anxiety over the extent of this borrowing and hipster [LARPing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_action_role-playing_game "Live action role-playing game").

# [Drift Into Decadence](https://gwern.net/larping#drift-into-decadence "Link to section: § 'Drift Into Decadence'")

![](https://gwern.net/doc/ai/nn/transformer/gpt/dall-e/3/2023-12-27-gwern-dalle3-mechanicalpencildrawingofaredwing875moctoebootdisplayedinartgallery.jpg "Satirical illustration of a Red Wing Heritage 875 Moc Toe work boot being displayed in an art gallery.")

Drunkard’s walk. Fashion being fashion, these seeds are only the _starting point_. The fashion will keep evolving on its own, as the cool kids endlessly find new ways to [separate themselves from the uncool kids](https://gwern.net/note/fashion "‘Fashion Cycles’, Gwern 2021"), buffeted by external factors and chance, like [a famous actor wearing a watch](https://www.vanityfair.com/style/2022/03/inside-the-frenzied-world-of-rare-watches "Inside the Frenzied World of Rare Watches and the Rich People Who Love Them").

Lost purposes. What was originally functional and explicable, like the [rivets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeans#Rivets "Jeans § Rivets") on [jeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeans "Jeans") at stress points, having [lost their purpose](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/sP2Hg6uPwpfp3jZJN/lost-purposes "Lost Purposes") & selection pressure for functionality (in part driven by fashion’s version of [Dutch disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_disease "Dutch disease")—[collecting](https://gwern.net/collecting "‘What Is The Collecting Mindset?’, Gwern 2021")), can [drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_drift "Genetic drift") to strange places the original users would consider [dangerously deviant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalization_of_deviance "Normalization of deviance")—[Levi Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Strauss "Levi Strauss") had to add rivets to keep the pants together, but today’s jean designers will spangle them anywhere one pleases, no matter how awkward, if that is the fashion at the time. In the case of jeans, the use is clear enough, and the history well-documented, but in many cases of design, copies are inferior because the new ignorant designers just did not understand the value of the features that were present, or absent (eg. the American copies of the German WWII [jerrycan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerrycan "Jerrycan") were [grossly inferior](https://gwern.net/doc/www/web.archive.org/d865552bc8deedd4ec6cc126249e183b31af97d8.html "'The Little Can That Could', Daniel 1987"), and the British used captured German jerrycans while waiting for exact duplicates), illustrating cases where [blackbox evolutionary design](https://gwern.net/backstop#black-box-vs-white-box-optimization) using replication-variation-selection can beat intelligent design. As designers know, often the most important parts of a design cannot be seen and are not even part of the design, but were what was learned in the long series of iteration as they groped towards a design that seems, in retrospect, inevitable; unfortunately, as [Perlis notes](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/algorithm/1982-perlis.pdf "'Epigrams on Programming', Perlis 1982"), “You can’t communicate complexity, only an awareness of it” (which is why I maintain a [list of discarded Gwern.net features](https://gwern.net/design-graveyard "'Design Graveyard', Gwern 2010")).

Mutational decay. After a while, a fashion is an erroneous copy of a misunderstood copy of an inferior variant of a copy of an original a fashion designer once saw; the original passes away: passable, then pastiche, then parody, as the concrete physical objects themselves have long since been forgotten in [a haze of signaling](https://www.lesswrong.com/tag/simulacrum-levels "Simulacrum Levels") “I did _X_” or “we are _Y_” or “you should like _Z_” or “I am more authentic than thou”. It has become more important to seem good than to be good, and one uses cheap gimmicks like adding metal weights [to headphones](https://beneinstein.com/how-it-s-made-series-yup-our-beats-were-counterfeit-but-they-cost-about-the-same-to-make-as-the-364cc6808d18 "How It's Made Series: Yup, Our Beats Were Counterfeit (But They Cost About the Same to Make as the Real Ones)") to make them feel more substantial; it has become _l’art pour l’art_. (By the time people are even talking about ‘authenticity’ and in need of special stamps & certifications & celebrity endorsements to assure them of their virtue, this process must already be far gone.)

# [Authenticity As Anti Dutch Disease](https://gwern.net/larping#authenticity-as-anti-dutch-disease "Link to section: § 'Authenticity as Anti Dutch Disease'")

“They don’t make them like that anymore.” This is, I think, why these fashion brands make such efforts to maintain contact with reality, no matter how bizarrely distorted their markets gets by things like scalping: ‘live by the fad, die by the fad.’ With no intrinsic backing, and having traveled fully up its rear orifice, a fashion can go bankrupt and become unfashionable overnight. A grounding in a real group, with practical uses, keeps the design honest.[1](https://gwern.net/larping#fn1) A hipster may be unable to evaluate the deeper qualities of stuff, but if they buy “professional” or “commercial-grade”, free-riding off that market discipline, they can assuage the anxiety that they are are being _inauthentic_ or someone somewhere might be _exploiting_ them (ie. making a profit).

But why care about those originals? All interesting, but not an answer to the question: where the seed goes may be fairly arbitrary and one may care about maintaining fidelity to the original seed by mechanisms like policing authenticity, yes, fine—but why is the chosen original seed so consistently a borrowed folkway or subculture or profession etc., drawn from any time or place, whether the military of a conqueror or fishermen centuries ago in the North Atlantic or the fads of young Manhattanites who your grandparents might have seen as a child? It could come from anywhere, or better yet, be brand new. It’s not as if no one’s trying to invent new looks!

# [Optimization As Esthetic](https://gwern.net/larping#optimization-as-esthetic "Link to section: § 'Optimization As Esthetic'")

> For my part I believe that the [Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_ToweR "Eiffel ToweR") will possess its own beauty. Are we to believe that because one is an engineer, one is not preoccupied by beauty in one’s constructions, or that one does not seek to create elegance as well as solidity and durability? Is it not true that the very conditions which give strength also conform to the hidden rules of harmony?…Now to what phenomenon did I have to give primary concern in designing the Tower? It was wind resistance. Well then! I hold that the curvature of the monument’s 4 outer edges, which is as mathematical calculation dictated it should be…will give a great impression of strength and beauty, for it will reveal to the eyes of the observer the boldness of the design as a whole.
> 
> —[Gustave Eiffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustave_Eiffel "Gustave Eiffel"), [1887-02-14](https://web.archive.org/web/20150908052239/http://www.toureiffel.paris/en/everything-about-the-tower/themed-files/71.html "The History of the Eiffel Tower: debate and controversy, 1887 Artists’ Protest") ([_Le Temps_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Temps_\(Paris\) "<em>Le Temps</em> (Paris)") interview rebutting [the artists’ protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower#Artists%27_protest "Eiffel Tower § Artists’ protest"))

I would suggest the formal reason is that those seeds are _highly-optimized for multiple[2](https://gwern.net/larping#fn2) things_, and that being optimized is intrinsically esthetically pleasing. To borrow from [Christopher Alexander’s ideas](https://dorian.substack.com/p/at-any-given-moment-in-a-process "At Any Given Moment in a Process…we have a certain partially evolved state of a structure. This state is described by the wholeness: the system of centers, and their relative nesting and degrees of life."), his [“quality without a name”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Timeless_Way_of_Building "The Timeless Way of Building") ([C2](https://wiki.c2.com/?QualityWithoutaName)) could in this context be given the name [“end-to-end optimized”](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/end-to-end-principle/index "‘end-to-end’ directory").

Thoughtful vs thoughtless. A building with the quality without a name is a building which glows with its inner logic: it has [learned over time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_Buildings_Learn "How Buildings Learn") and, with great care, tailored to its surrounding in every way, from blending into the local architecture vernacular to its repair cost to how the sun shines on it in the morning in mid-winter to how long it takes to find the bathroom when you need to do the needful, a delightful surprise around every corner, becoming a true home for its inhabitants[3](https://gwern.net/larping#fn3); aspiring to perfection, it can reach excellence. Contrast such a building with the slovenly cookie-cutter dysfunctional buildings, constructed by organizations which do not care (assuming the architect is not outright misanthropic and [deliberately manufacturing anxiety](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.katarxis3.com/c5d24890d248b7a6a212c735abbb7c6e97de4e94.html#The%20Debate "'Contrasting Concepts of Harmony in Architecture', Alexander & Eisenman 1982")), which, being suits cut to fit all sizes, suit none (even as they mouth the slogan ‘form follows function’[4](https://gwern.net/larping#fn4)), and aiming no higher than mediocrity, lands at failure, with each surprise more unpleasant than the last.

Qualities emerge. When we look at natural objects like rivers finding the most efficient path to the ocean, or aerodynamic birds, or bones in a skeleton, we may have no idea what a fractal dimension is or what variational physics are, but we still recognize that the object is in some way intrinsically simple, its form driven by function. It will have desirable properties like symmetries, a hierarchy of features repeating but with local variations, and many rules with violations when a deeper rule is followed.

Qualities converge. When we look at a [minimal surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_surface "Minimal surface"), or Buddhist [sand mandala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_mandala "Sand mandala") or some engineering problem like a metal beam optimized by genetic algorithms to maximize stiffness while minimizing weight, we may struggle to understand why it bulges in one place and has a hole punched in another; but we say, “how _organic_\-looking!” [A power-line](https://gwern.net/doc/anime/eva/2020-fukushima.pdf "‘Noise in the landscape: Disputing the visibility of mundane technological objects’, Fukushima 2020") is as esthetic as a painting, a [space station](https://gwern.net/doc/www/localhost/157e66d66c8c7a27975a8b03bc7e83701690e5f1.html "UnseenOps 2026") fit to compare to a sonnet, and a computer chip as miraculous a work as any authored by human hands. Indeed, any optimization is enough: much [computer art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_art "Computer art"), and human art, takes the form of minimizing or maximizing some entirely arbitrary constraint. (What computer languages are called [‘beautiful’](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_beauty "Mathematical beauty")? The single-[paradigm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_paradigm "Programming paradigm") languages like [Smalltalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smalltalk "Smalltalk") or [Haskell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haskell "Haskell"), which take the premise “what if everything was _X_?” as far as possible; done well, such languages are [“a mistake carried through to perfection”](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD04xx/EWD498.html "'How do we tell truths that might hurt?', Dijkstra 1975-06-18"). And do the details of [_feng shui_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feng_shui "<em>Feng shui</em>") rules or the truth of claims about _qi_ or dragons matter half so much as the rules existing & being followed?)

Perception & learning of qualities. When we look at such an object, we may have no idea what purpose each part serves, but we respond to the whole.[5](https://gwern.net/larping#fn5) And the longer we spend with the object, the better we appreciate it, keeping the object interesting by being [novel but not _too_ novel](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/novelty/index "‘novelty U-curve’ directory"). We [gradually learn to compress it](https://gwern.net/doc/www/people.idsia.ch/0bb71a75b32dcb903ba233f64eb7751d3d80366f.html "‘Formal Theory of Creativity & Fun & Intrinsic Motivation (1990–2010)’, Schmidhuber 2010"), understanding why each part is the way it is (because there are in fact reasons, even if only as decorative spandrels), yielding esthetic pleasure. (This is also why [skeuomorphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeuomorph "Skeuomorph") and [greebles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeble "Greeble") work, especially with esthetics like [steampunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steampunk "Steampunk"): they counterfeit this sort of [depth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logical_depth "Logical depth") by stealing objects that _were_ optimized for a purpose, and then placing them at random to exploit our trust that there is a real logic to it all—at least for a short time, which for many things like SFX in movies is all they need.)

## [Have A Principle—Any Principle](https://gwern.net/larping#have-a-principleany-principle "Link to section: § 'Have A Principle—Any Principle'")

> Every task involves constraint,  
> Solve the thing without complaint;  
> There are magic links and chains  
> Forged to loose our rigid brains.  
> Strictures, structures, though they bind,  
> Strangely liberate the mind.
> 
> —[James E. Falen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Falen "James E. Falen")[6](https://gwern.net/larping#fn6)

It takes a village (and city, and country…) A bomber jacket, or Red Wing boot, or aviator sunglass, or [guernsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guernsey_\(clothing\) "Guernsey (clothing)")/[Aran sweater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aran_jumper "Aran jumper"), or [Irish pub](https://www.eater.com/2017/3/7/14839472/irish-pub-design) (or…), is the product of long evolution & design optimizing for many properties, under competing constraints. An ecosystem of traditions can [percolate novelty upwards in a multilevel design](https://gwern.net/internet-community-design "‘Internet Community Design: Slow To Fast’, Gwern 2021"), balancing exploration and exploitation.

Good artists copy… Few humans can design such objects from scratch; they optimize only a few constraints, because the designer intended only one thing, or ran out of time, or ideas, and do not reward long-term use—what you see is only what you get. Cast adrift on the sea of possibilities, they will do only a few iterations. They will stagger around at random, like a drunken sailor, and [never go far](https://gwern.net/doc/science/1986-hamming#consistent-progress). They will not build up a unique design language, driven by real needs. They will not deeply [explore](https://gwern.net/doc/reinforcement-learning/exploration/index "‘RL exploration’ directory") a design space, and come up with unobvious but right results, or refactor each component until they all hang together in a logical way. If you sat down and tried with a blank sheet of paper, you’d never invent that [A-2 bomber jacket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-2_jacket "A-2 jacket") in a single go; no Japanese collectors will care a century later about it. It is too specific, too idiosyncratic—it can only be the product of a long line of earlier jackets starting at the dawn of aviation by repurposing tweed jackets and motoring apparel, driven by the needs of flying. But when you copy from a tradition with a whole [pattern language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pattern_language "Pattern language"), you stand on the shoulders of giants.

Pick a tradition. It does not matter what tradition, if the alternative is a [“flat”](https://gwern.net/doc/www/acoup.blog/ed1b14179cecbb14be7f15ccac47d80c1d641fcf.html "'Collections: Why <em>Rings of Power</em>’s Middle Earth Feels Flat', Bret Devereaux 2022-12-16") collage of careless incompetence & cargo-culting & lowest-bidder work & myopic choice. Pick one as a guide: “At least it’s an esthetic.”[7](https://gwern.net/larping#fn7)

I wear Red Wing boots because I like their look, not because I need them; my cousin wears RW boots, because he fights wildfires—and tells me that the [RW Museum](http://solutions.redwingshoes.com/WorldsLargestBootA) in [RW, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Wing,_Minnesota "Red Wing, Minnesota") has a wall of half-destroyed boots sent in from RW wearers worldwide, such as firefighters in Japan, in exchange for replacements.
    
    If RW boots degrade, presumably they’ll let RW know. Because I won’t.
    
    RW boots also offer an interesting example of degenerate signals: an argument that used to be made by RW fans was that their boots used a [Goodyear welt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodyear_welt "Goodyear welt") and would reduce total cost in the long run. A Goodyear welt attaches the sole to the rest of the boot, which meant that the sole could be _relatively_ easily replaced when they wore out long before the rest of the boot wore out.
    
    This was useful and economical for workers wearing RW boots back in 1862164ya, and the distinctive ‘stitching around the edges’ of a Goodyear welt was a sign of quality boots—that one might wear long enough and _want_ to replace the sole on. However, if you look into actually doing it _now_, when the cost of skilled labor has gone through the roof compared to then, you’ll find that there are not many cobblers still around in America, and resoling will cost you easily $100… which is around how much a lightly-used RW boot will cost you on eBay! In practice, few people seem to resole their RW boots, beyond hobbyists getting a kick out of actually doing it…
    
    So now ‘Goodyear welt’ has become a sort of fashion symbol in boots: coasting on its history as a useful feature driven by the realities of its time, but now existing in the same liminal space of ‘luxury’ features as wine, mechanical watches, fine art, fonts, etc. An example of the characteristic rhetoric, explicitly aimed at enculturing newbies with boot shibboleths:
    
    > If you’re new to the world of quality footwear, Goodyear Welt Construction is one of the first major upgrades separating heritage boots from disposable footwear. Though expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to master, Goodyear Welt Construction creates a tightly-crafted boot that is water-resistant and wearable for years to come. It’s a better way to build a boot that combines centuries of human ingenuity with skilled craftsmanship.

3.  One might ask, what makes a home _homely_? There are many images which try to evoke homeliness or “coziness”, but they rely on cheap shortcuts. (I once asked a woman if she noticed that all ChatGPT-4o-generated images, including the two she had generated to put on the big poster we were looking at, were sepia-yellow tinted; she had not but she described ChatGPT images are looking “cozy”.)
    
    I would say that homeliness is not about surface dressing, like whether the lights are old incandescents, or if there is a sofa, or being clean, or having copper pots hanging on the wall. I have been in many places which had those without being homely in the slightest.
    
    What makes a home homely is that it is intentional and has “learned” its people, and been adapted by them in turn. A home is predictable and has no hidden paper cuts; it may seem chaotic, but there is a hidden order known to its inhabitants, who are comfortable. A home is somewhere you can walk around in the dark, where you know what was that weird sound late at night, and you automatically avoid the bad spot in the couch.
    
    Everywhere else, like a hotel room, is just a stage set by and for strangers, where we are guests, at best… No wonder we sleep poorly [the first night](https://gwern.net/doc/zeo/1966-agnew.pdf "'The First Night Effect: An EEG Study Of Sleep', Agnew et al 1966") [in a new place](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4864126/ "‘Night watch in one brain hemisphere during sleep associated with the first-night effect in humans’, Tamaki et al 2016").[↩︎](https://gwern.net/larping#fnref3)
    
4.  “[Form follows function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Form_follows_function "Form follows function")” is an excellent idea; some day, it may even be honored outside the breach.
    
    In particular, in modern architecture descending from [Adolf Loos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Loos "Adolf Loos"), I note that while Loos claimed to be inspired by the highly-optimized rationality of grain silos & water towers, his own buildings imitate only their most superficial and cosmetic aspects. American farm architecture was evolved over many decades to satisfy hard requirements of real jobs through trial-and-error by full-time engineers & architects who spent their career manufacturing many such buildings (and may well have grown up laboring on the family farm using them while thinking about how to improve them). That is _opposite_ of Loos’s work like the [Steiner House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steiner_House "Steiner House"), where the architect swoops in, [drawing lines on a blueprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seeing_Like_a_State "Seeing Like a State") to impose by fiat his eccentric geometric scheme on a single building (a scheme completely unrelated to its function as a house for someone to live in), and flees without any revision or looking back, aspiring to never do it again and learn nothing from it.
    
    Such anti-human approaches to design are all ‘form’ and no ‘function’—‘function’ is left as the problem for whatever poor sap must endure the consequences. (And they usually fail at ‘form’ too: few suburban strip malls or McMansions manage to be as ugly as the Steiner House.)[↩︎](https://gwern.net/larping#fnref4)
    
5.  Does one _need_ to [machine a stainless steel](https://lulabs.net/machining/ss-cube/ "Stainless steel rubik's cube") [Rubik’s cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubik%27s_Cube "Rubik's Cube")? No. Would one want to use it for playing Rubik’s cube, much less [speedcubing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedcubing "Speedcubing")? Also no. Does the arbitrary objective of “Rubik’s cube but steel” drive the design & creation of an object far more beautiful than he would’ve created with the remit “a beautiful steel object”? Heck yes.[↩︎](https://gwern.net/larping#fnref5)
