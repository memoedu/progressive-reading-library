Did everyone misread [Alice B. Sheldon's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Tiptree_Jr.) 1977 SF short story ["The Screwfly Solution"](https://web.archive.org/web/20080702231205/http://www.scifi.com/scifiction/classics/classics_archive/sheldon/sheldon1.html) ([WP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Screwfly_Solution))?
It seems so.
Sheldon's story has the same problem as Ted Chiang's ["Story of Your Life"](/story-of-your-life "'‘Story Of Your Life’ Is Not A Time-Travel Story', Gwern 2012"): the surface story is so easily read one way that a reader can just run roughshod over the parts that don't fit, either ignoring them or cramming a square peg into a round hole.
In Sheldon's story, no readers seems to have noticed all the hints that the mind-altering chemicals are affecting the *women* too---everyone reads it solely as "the men go berserk murdering all women, who are able to put up no resistance".

When I read "Screwfly", I thought this was fairly obvious albeit that Dr. Sheldon was perhaps, as with ["Love Is The Plan"](https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/fiction/love-is-the-plan-the-plan-is-death/) ([WP](!W "Love Is the Plan the Plan Is Death")) or ["The Psychologist Who Wouldn’t Do Awful Things to Rats"](https://weirdfictionreview.com/2016/02/101-weird-writers-39-james-tiptree-jr/) ([and related stories](/doc/fiction/science-fiction/2004-elms.pdf#page=9 "‘The Psychologist Who Empathized with Rats: James Tiptree Junior as Alice B. Sheldon, PhD § pg9’, Elms 2004 (page 9)")), expecting too much of her reader like familiarity with zoology to the point of understanding the "lordotic" reference^[One reader told me that they *did* wonder what that meant & checked, but Google took them to [the back posture problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordosis) instead---leaving them more confused than before!], and was surprised to look at the Wikipedia entry and see none of this covered; checking other reviews, none of them mention this either.
As obvious as it might seem, it seems almost all readers miss it (somewhat like missing how [[Tarkovsky's _Stalker_](/review/movie#stalker)]{#gwern-reviews-movies} retells Dostoevsky's ["Grand Inquisitor"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Grand_Inquisitor)).

# Problems With Heterosexuality

So, to dig into it, the male-only reading has several problems:

#. This is prima facie absurd.

    Women are not helpless creatures who bare their throats to be cut, have never in history acted like that.

    Certainly, Sheldon herself  certainly didn't believe that. She had an active life as an artist, traveler, Army intelligence officer (rank of Major), earning her doctorate in animal psychology, career as a SF writer under several pseudonyms, bisexual, journalist, etc.
    Sheldon is typically taken to be a highly-feminist & knowledgeable writer, and not the ignorant borderline misogynist and/or misandrist that the male-only reading implies (with its depiction of women en masse as being incompetent, helpless, and masochistic to the point of suicide). Even in terms of a "victimization Olympics" interpretation, this is extreme.

    By any sensible understanding of the world, it would take women years or decades to die out, and they might never do so; it would be perfectly possible for women (being slightly over half the world population) to take over and counter-purge men (whose economies are also collapsing), and make do without men (harvesting semen from raids, raising their children, or taking a few captives who can be shackled to safely service many women).
    Further, the men are in a losing strategic position: they are unable to replenish their population, as they must murder any women they capture, while the women can begin exponential growth of their population easily, meaning that any war of attrition is an almost guaranteed victory.
    So the alien strategy is deeply flawed, even without considering evolution.^[Such strategies are best used for total eradication or not at all, otherwise, like insecticides or antibiotics, survivors may evolve resistance. Even a *single* resistant male can result in a population becoming resistant almost overnight, given intense selection; a remarkable recent example is ["The Evolution of Sex Ratio Distorter Suppression Affects a 25 cM Genomic Region in the Butterfly _Hypolimnas bolina_", Hornett et al 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosgenetics/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgen.1004822 "'The Evolution of Sex Ratio Distorter Suppression Affects a 25 cM Genomic Region in the Butterfly <em>Hypolimnas bolina</em>', Hornett et al 2014"), which demonstrates [Fisher’s principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher%27s_principle) in the real world, when a parasite caused a 100:1 female:male sex ratio, which after a century of stasis (!), collapsed to 1:1 in *5* years once a resistant mutant finally immigrated from elsewhere. Thus, if one uses a *single* intervention affecting only one sex, one cannot simply walk away: the job must be monitored & more work done until extinction is complete.]
#. The absurdity of it is explicitly highlighted in the story, directly pointing out to the reader how "strange" it is that the women are generally so helpless and even the female protagonist is apathetic to the point of suicide (emphasis added):

    > I still can't really realize, I have to stop myself from rushing back. But you saved my life, I know that. The first trip in I got a paper, I saw where they bombed the Apostle Islands refuge. And it had about those 3 women stealing the Air Force plane and bombing Dallas, too. Of course they shot them down, over the Gulf. *Isn't it strange how we do nothing? Just get killed by ones and twos. Or more, now they've started on the refuges.… Like hypnotized rabbits.* We're a toothless race. Do you know I never said "we" meaning women before? "We" was always me and Alan, and Amy of course. Being killed selectively encourages group identification.… You see how sane-headed I am. But I still can't really realize.
    >
    > [...] The old man gave me the hooks quick and whispered to me, "Boy, them woods'll be full of hunters next week." I almost ran out. [...] No. I'll just make a good end, say up on that rock where I can see the stars. After I go back and leave this for you. I'll wait to see the beautiful color in the trees one last time.

    Indeed, it is strange how the women do nothing.
    And her analogy raises a question that the story pointedly never answers: rabbits do not hypnotize themselves, but a *predator* does---so what predator suddenly hypnotized the women "rabbits"? If one answers "the men", that seems odd: men do not normally have hypnotic powers over women.

    In fact, the odd passivity of the women is present from the start: in the first anecdote, a female epidemiologist travels to the initial outbreak, and lies down on a cot---and lets her throat be cut; the female protagonist's friend dithers and makes excuses about selling her house rather than simply fleeing; women do minimal lobbying about 'femicide' by the 'cult', etc.
    (In reality, in the 1970s, people went berserk over the wave of cults in the USA and any associated mistreatment of women especially, eg. [Charles Manson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Manson), and allegations of abuse of women have always been some of the most effective war propaganda.)
#. The reading is simplistic, and the story disappointingly shallow.

    Compare the complexity of "Love Is the Plan the Plan Is Death" to this reading of "Screwfly".
    Why is it so *long* when so much of it in the middle is just men murdering women, and could be cut to reach the punchline with no loss?
    (And why does it feel like "Love Is the Plan the Plan Is Death" is a twin story, when "Screwfly" is so one-sided?)
#. The story is written with many parallelisms and symmetries, making the lack of a parallel in male/female psychological horror striking by its absence.

    The viewpoint moves between man and woman, with the man acting and the woman reacting.
    The male protagonist succumbs to the alien attack, becoming an unreliable narrator, and while aware of his madness, commits suicide after realizing he murdered his daughter (who goes to him despite all orders not to); and then the perspective switches to the woman, who tries and fails to escape successfully, noting the oddity of how futile resistance seems, and presumably dies shortly after the ending.
#. A core male vs female parallelism is the extract from "Ian MacIntyre" (an author mouthpiece), around three-quarters of the way through, about the sex/violence mechanism the aliens exploit:

    > ...in some, the expression of aggression and copulation alternate or even coexist, an all-too-familiar example being the common house cat.
    > Males of many species bite, claw, bruise, tread or otherwise assault receptive females during the act of intercourse; indeed, in some species the male attack is necessary for female ovulation to occur...It seems therefore appropriate to speculate that the present crisis might be caused by some substance, perhaps at the viral or enzymatic level, which effects a failure of the switching or triggering function in the higher primates...In this connection it might be noted that exactly this condition is a commonplace of male functional pathology, in those cases where murder occurs as a response to and apparent completion of, sexual desire.
    > It should be emphasized that the aggression/copulation linkage discussed here is specific to the male; the female response (eg. [lordotic reflex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lordosis_behavior)) being of a different nature.

    If the male half is about attacking, then what is the female half?
    There are no less than 2 references to lordosis here, but this apparently flies over the heads of most readers, who skim over this as so much evopsych bio-babble and likely haven't seen cats mating in this age of near-universal spaying/neutering.
    "Lordosis" is about passively cooperating with being *mounted* by sticking up one's rear.[^ignorance-of-critics]
    (See also ["clipnosis"](!W "Pinch-induced behavioral inhibition") in cats.)

    So, what 'switching or triggering function' might be failing in 'the female response'?
    Or do we just ignore this entire passage as so much infodumping by Sheldon, her ["showing her homework"](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/ShownTheirWork) in prolixly explaining some quasi-scientific justification of the men-murdering-women which we are well-acquainted with by this point?

[^ignorance-of-critics]: While this might seem unreasonably obscure a reference, lordosis has been the subject of a fair amount of investigation, some of it quite macabre, like the experiments in seeing how much brain could be removed from mice or cats while still being able to mount or do lordosis (which I previously read about for my [hydrocephalus essay](/hydrocephalus "'Hydrocephalus and Intelligence: The Hollow Men', Gwern 2015")); those specific studies were a few years after "Screwfly" was published, true, but there was work before then, and anyway, it is entirely possible that Sheldon knew of such work pre-publication given her scientific specialty.

    Like Cordwainer Smith (who incidentally, like Sheldon, also worked in US intelligence) and [the "pain of space"](/scanners "‘‘Scanners Live in Vain’ as realistic SF’, Gwern 2013") or [Frank Herbert's genetics & pheromones](/dune-genetics "‘Genetics and Eugenics in Frank Herbert’s Dune-verse’, Gwern 2018"), would be another example of how literary-supremacist interpretations of SF stories, neglecting the contemporary scientific knowledge informing SF, leads to distorted interpretations.

# Swinging With Alice

All of this has a simple and satisfying explanation: it takes two to tango, and so the alien attack affects *women* as well.
In both genders, the sexual instincts are deranged: the men act to kill, and the women act to *be* killed.
(See also: vampire and monster fiction, harlequin romance, ["the love of the wolf"](/doc/psychology/personality/1994-cixous.pdf#page=8 "‘Love of the Wolf § pg8’, Cixous & Cohen 1994 (page 8)"), [fetishes](#fetish).)

This resolves the mysteries; absolves Sheldon from the charges of incompetent worldbuilding, superficial storytelling, and thorough misogyny; adds additional parallelism (the male unreliable narrator is followed by a female unreliable narrator, etc.), and considerable depth (the length is because of the passive witnessing); and explains the long jarring digression into feline mating behavior.

## Counter-Argument: The Screwfly Solution(s)

This interpretation has one problem itself: the [eponymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochliomyia_hominivorax#Control) [anti-insect intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterile_insect_technique), which is still employed today [on a mass scale](https://www.theatlantic.com/science/archive/2020/05/flesh-eating-worms-disease-containment-america-panama/611026/ "'America’s Never-Ending Battle Against Flesh-Eating Worms: Inside the U.S. and Panama’s long-running collaboration to rid an entire continent of a deadly disease',  Zhang 2020"), is about one sex only (sterile males).
The females are otherwise unaffected.
Doesn't the title of the story demand we interpret the attack as solely about males?

I disagree.
Should we take the screwfly sterile insect technique as a strict mechanical analogy/allegory, which must exactly match its inspiration?
After all, in none of the sterile insect technique applications do the sterilized insects *kill* the opposite sex, and the humans do not act like the insects.
So the analogy is only partial and can't be insisted upon as holding exactly in every detail.
Given all the problems with insisting *only* the male sex is affected, I don't find adding another point of dis-analogy to negate all the advantages of the bisexual interpretation.

A further counterargument is that there is yet another symmetry here, missed if one focuses *only* on screwflies: there are *3* examples of sterile insect technique^[Another interesting society-level chemical attack is the juvenilizing [MV-678](https://www.nytimes.com/1982/09/07/science/science-watch-carrion-bees.html "Eternal Youth Kills Ants").] are listed throughout the story---the screwfly, but also the "Spruce budworm" and the "canefly".
These 3 examples do not all operate the same way:

- female screwflies mate only once, so a sterile male only has to successfully 'mate' to eliminate a female (by using up her mating);
- while for the [Spruce budworm](!W), "It seems it blocks the male from turning around after he connects with the female, so he mates with her *head* instead.";
- and for caneflies, one interferes in massed coordinated reproduction, where you "Concentrate the pheromone, release sterilized females".

The Spruce budworm example seems to be plausible if not entirely factual (the sterile insect technique was used on the [*tobacco* budworm](!W)---an authorial error?), but I find no references to the sterile insect technique for any 'canefly', nor is there any 'canefly' which is a major human parasite.

This is striking given the other 2 examples, screwflies & budworms, are real.
Why did Sheldon feel the need to make up an apparently-fictional intervention involving modifications to *females* if she intended the male-only interpretation?

The presence of low-tech human technological attacks on males and females separately suggests that we look for a 'missing' attack, and that would be an attack on *both* sexes.
And it would then stand to reason that a higher-tech civilization (the aliens) might employ this much more effective bisexual attack on pests which are far more adaptable & difficult to exterminate than any insect: humans.

So even here, the bisexual interpretation gives a superior reading, and a further twist.

# Appendix: Sexual Reflexes, Absorption, and Immobility {#sexual-immobility-bibliography}

<div class="abstract">
[Literature review by ChatGPT-6 Astra, Claude-5.1-Fable, et al 2026.]{.editorial} The biological background to “The Screwfly Solution” extends beyond the familiar association between male sexuality and aggression: genital stimulation can also alter pain, posture, and movement, while human sexual experience can involve absorption, altered agency, and profound passivity.
This selected bibliography distinguishes experimentally demonstrated effects from proposed mechanisms and firsthand reports.

The connections to Sheldon's story are interpretive: later studies illuminate its biological allusions but cannot establish her sources or intentions.
</div>

**Lordosis**, the mating posture of many female mammals; **analgesia**, reduced pain; **tonic immobility**, inhibition of movement associated with threat; **absorption**, immersive attention; and **subspace**, practitioners' term for altered experience during BDSM, are distinguishable phenomena.
The evidence supports studying their connections; it does not establish a single female “submission reflex” explaining all of them.
Entries are grouped by subject and ordered chronologically within each group; dates refer to journal publication unless otherwise specified.

## Animal Sexual Reflexes

- [Komisaruk & Larsson 1971](https://doi.org/10.1016/0006-8993(71)90608-1 "Suppression of a Spinal and a Cranial Nerve Reflex by Vaginal or Rectal Probing in Rats"): “Suppression of a Spinal and a Cranial Nerve Reflex by Vaginal or Rectal Probing in Rats”.

    Early experimental connection between genital sensory input, suppressed withdrawal responses, and immobility in rats.
  The rectal findings depend on experimental conditions; the paper does not demonstrate a general anal-stimulation paralysis reflex.
- [Rodriguez-Sierra et al 1975](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0076442 "Vaginal Stimulation in Rats Induces Prolonged Lordosis Responsiveness and Sexual Receptivity"): “Vaginal Stimulation in Rats Induces Prolonged Lordosis Responsiveness and Sexual Receptivity”.

    Vaginal stimulation increased subsequent sexual receptivity.
  “Prolonged” describes the duration of enhanced responsiveness, not continuous immobility or a sustained lordosis posture.
- [Komisaruk & Wallman 1977](https://doi.org/10.1016/0006-8993(77)91014-9 "Antinociceptive Effects of Vaginal Stimulation in Rats: Neurophysiological and Behavioral Studies"): “Antinociceptive Effects of Vaginal Stimulation in Rats: Neurophysiological and Behavioral Studies”.

    Vaginal stimulation inhibited responses to noxious stimulation while sparing responses to gentle touch and movement elicited through the pyramidal tract.
  Establishes that genital analgesia and inability to move need not be the same effect.
- [Schwartz-Giblin et al 1984](https://doi.org/10.1016/0014-4886(84)90142-0 "Axial Electromyogram and Intervertebral Length Gauge Responses During Lordosis Behavior in Rats"): “Axial Electromyogram and Intervertebral Length Gauge Responses During Lordosis Behavior in Rats”.

    Electromyographic and mechanical measurements characterize lordosis as coordinated muscular activity.
  Useful corrective to treating the receptive posture as simple limpness or generalized paralysis.
- [Smith et al 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-9384(85)90143-X "Effects of Estrus, Estrogen-Progesterone Priming, and Vaginal Stimulation on Tonic Immobility, Dorsal Immobility, and Lordosis in the Female Rat"): “Effects of Estrus, Estrogen-Progesterone Priming, and Vaginal Stimulation on Tonic Immobility, Dorsal Immobility, and Lordosis in the Female Rat”.

    Directly compares three responses often conflated in discussions of sexual passivity.
  Vaginal stimulation facilitated tonic immobility under appropriate hormonal conditions, but these tonic-immobility episodes were brief; longer dorsal immobility was a different measurement.
- [Cunningham et al 1991](https://doi.org/10.1016/0006-8993(91)90021-M "Differential Roles of Hypogastric and Pelvic Nerves in the Analgesic and Motoric Effects of Vaginocervical Stimulation in Rats"): “Differential Roles of Hypogastric and Pelvic Nerves in the Analgesic and Motoric Effects of Vaginocervical Stimulation in Rats”.

    Nerve-section experiments separate components of the response to vaginocervical stimulation.
  Combined pelvic and hypogastric nerve cuts abolished the tested analgesic and motor effects, including immobility: causal evidence for sensory pathways, rather than an inference from appearance.
- [Rose & Flynn 1993](https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-9384(93)90123-W "Lordosis Response Components Can Be Elicited in Decerebrate Rats by Combined Flank and Cervix Stimulation"): “Lordosis Response Components Can Be Elicited in Decerebrate Rats by Combined Flank and Cervix Stimulation”.

    Components of the receptive posture remained elicitable after removal of forebrain influence.
  Demonstrates subcortical organization of a motor response; it cannot establish the animal's subjective pleasure, willingness, or conscious experience.
- [Pfaus et al 2006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physbeh.2006.07.022 "Effects of Pelvic, Pudendal, or Hypogastric Nerve Cuts on Fos Induction in the Rat Brain Following Vaginocervical Stimulation"): “Effects of Pelvic, Pudendal, or Hypogastric Nerve Cuts on Fos Induction in the Rat Brain Following Vaginocervical Stimulation”.

    Maps sensory contributions to brain activation and behavior following vaginocervical stimulation; pelvic neurectomy eliminated the observed immobility response.
  Genital feedback participates in several aspects of mating behavior, not merely increasing receptivity.

## Human Genital Analgesia and Reflex Modulation

- [Whipple & Komisaruk 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3959(85)90164-2 "Elevation of Pain Threshold by Vaginal Stimulation in Women"): “Elevation of Pain Threshold by Vaginal Stimulation in Women”.

    Two small experiments, each involving 10 women, found elevated pain thresholds during vaginal self-stimulation, particularly orgasm, without corresponding loss of ordinary touch sensitivity.
  A direct human counterpart to genital analgesia, but no test of voluntary paralysis.
- [Whipple & Komisaruk 1988](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224498809551403 "Analgesia Produced in Women by Genital Self-Stimulation"): “Analgesia Produced in Women by Genital Self-Stimulation”.

    Further testing in 10 women found elevated pain thresholds during pleasurable self-stimulation of three genital sites, and during pressure stimulation of the anterior vaginal wall.
  Extends the findings across stimulation sites; it does not establish that reduced pain entails reduced agency.
- [Martinez-Gomez et al 1988](https://doi.org/10.1016/0031-9384(88)90387-3 "Analgesia Produced by Vaginal Self-Stimulation in Women Is Independent of Heart Rate Acceleration"): “Analgesia Produced by Vaginal Self-Stimulation in Women Is Independent of Heart Rate Acceleration”.

    Vaginal-stimulation analgesia occurred without heart-rate acceleration, while exercise-induced heart-rate acceleration did not produce analgesia.
  Separates pain modulation from one autonomic measurement; it does not establish a parasympathetic explanation.
- [Bouhassira et al 1998](https://doi.org/10.1152/ajpgi.1998.275.3.G410 "Effects of Rectal Distensions on Nociceptive Flexion Reflexes in Humans"): “Effects of Rectal Distensions on Nociceptive Flexion Reflexes in Humans”.

    In 10 participants, slow rectal distension inhibited the electrically elicited lower-limb RIII withdrawal reflex; rapid distension produced a different pattern.
  Relevant human evidence for visceral modulation of a spinal reflex, obtained in a gastrointestinal experiment rather than during sex; voluntary movement was not the outcome.

## Defensive Immobility in Humans

- [Abrams et al 2009](https://doi.org/10.1002/da.20462 "Human Tonic Immobility: Measurement and Correlates"): “Human Tonic Immobility: Measurement and Correlates”.

    Among 78 undergraduates selected for reporting traumatic immobility, questionnaire scores correlated with dissociation, post-traumatic symptoms, and absorption.
  An empirical connection between absorption and immobility reports, with selection and retrospective measurement preventing causal inference.
- [Volchan et al 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsycho.2011.06.002 "Is There Tonic Immobility in Humans? Biological Evidence from Victims of Traumatic Stress"): “Is There Tonic Immobility in Humans? Biological Evidence from Victims of Traumatic Stress”.

    33 trauma-exposed participants listened to accounts of their own traumatic experiences.
  Reported immobility accompanied reduced postural sway and altered cardiac activity, providing an objective physiological correlate of the subjective reports.
- [Reichenheim et al 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3991625/ "Structural Validity of the Tonic Immobility Scale in a Population Exposed to Trauma: Evidence from Two Large Brazilian Samples"): “Structural Validity of the Tonic Immobility Scale in a Population Exposed to Trauma: Evidence from Two Large Brazilian Samples”.

    Psychometric analysis of 3,223 participants rejected the original two-factor structure and proposed shorter versions.
  A reminder that an immobility questionnaire mixes several experiences and should not be treated as a direct assay of one neural mechanism.
- [TeBockhorst et al 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037953 "Tonic Immobility Among Survivors of Sexual Assault"): “Tonic Immobility Among Survivors of Sexual Assault”.

    Qualitative interviews with seven survivors describe inability to act during assault and subsequent recurrence of related experiences.
  Relevant to immobility appearing in later sexual contexts; the accounts concern trauma-related distress rather than establishing pleasurable subspace.
- [Möller et al 2017](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/aogs.13174/full "Tonic Immobility During Sexual Assault—A Common Reaction Predicting Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder and Severe Depression"): “Tonic Immobility During Sexual Assault—A Common Reaction Predicting Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder and Severe Depression”.

    Of 298 women attending a specialist clinic after sexual assault, 70% met the study's threshold for significant tonic immobility and 48% for extreme immobility; immobility predicted worse subsequent psychiatric outcomes.
  These are clinic-sample questionnaire estimates, not population prevalence or evidence that nonresistance implies enjoyment.

## Absorption, Hypnosis, and Sexual Trance

- [Tellegen & Atkinson 1974](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0036681 "Openness to Absorbing and Self-Altering Experiences (‘Absorption’), a Trait Related to Hypnotic Susceptibility"): “Openness to Absorbing and Self-Altering Experiences (‘Absorption’), a Trait Related to Hypnotic Susceptibility”.

    Foundational account of absorption as an individual difference in immersive attention and altered self-experience.
  Its relationship with hypnotic susceptibility makes it relevant to recurrent trance-like experiences, without making the two constructs interchangeable.
- [Mosher 1980](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224498009551060 "Three Dimensions of Depth of Involvement in Human Sexual Response"): “Three Dimensions of Depth of Involvement in Human Sexual Response”.

    Distinguishes sexual role enactment, sexual trance, and engagement with a partner.
  A useful theoretical vocabulary for separating receptive absorption from submissive identity or role-playing; not an experimental demonstration of immobility.
- [Kihlstrom et al 1989](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207148908414476 "Dispositional Correlates of Hypnosis: A Phenomenological Approach"): “Dispositional Correlates of Hypnosis: A Phenomenological Approach”.

    Absorption and everyday trance-like experiences were stronger correlates of hypnotizability than relaxation or drowsiness.
  Supports distinguishing immersive attention from merely being calm, sleepy, or physically relaxed.
- [Hagenaars et al 2006](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207140500528547 "Motor and Sensory Dissociative Phenomena Associated with Induced Catalepsy: A Brief Communication"): “Motor and Sensory Dissociative Phenomena Associated with Induced Catalepsy: A Brief Communication”.

    Tactile induction of arm catalepsy in 15 women produced reported rigidity, sensory changes, and reduced awareness of the arm.
  A human touch-to-motor-change analogue, although the small study lacked an attention-matched control and did not investigate sex or whole-body immobility.
- [Cojan et al 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2009.05.021 "The Brain under Self-Control: Modulation of Inhibitory and Monitoring Cortical Networks During Hypnotic Paralysis"): “The Brain under Self-Control: Modulation of Inhibitory and Monitoring Cortical Networks During Hypnotic Paralysis”.

    Brain imaging during suggested hand paralysis found preserved motor preparation alongside altered monitoring and imagery-related activity.
  Demonstrates experimentally altered motor agency, without identifying spontaneous sexual immobility as hypnosis.
- [Sheen & Koukounas 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224490902775843 "The Role of Absorption in Women's Sexual Response to Erotica: A Cognitive-Affective Investigation"): “The Role of Absorption in Women's Sexual Response to Erotica: A Cognitive-Affective Investigation”.

    Instructions to imagine participating in an erotic film increased reported absorption, sexual arousal, and positive affect relative to detached observation.
  Experimental evidence that attentional stance changes sexual experience, rather than evidence of a fixed submissiveness trait.
- [Nielsen et al 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00751/full "Synaesthesia and Sexuality: The Influence of Synaesthetic Perceptions on Sexual Experience"): “Synaesthesia and Sexuality: The Influence of Synaesthetic Perceptions on Sexual Experience”.

    19 sexual synesthetes reported stronger altered states than 36 controls, without greater sexual satisfaction.
  A useful counterexample to equating more unusual or intense consciousness with a better sexual experience.
- [Costa et al 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2016.03.013 "Altered States of Consciousness Are Related to Higher Sexual Responsiveness"): “Altered States of Consciousness Are Related to Higher Sexual Responsiveness”.

    Questionnaire study of 116 participants linking sexual responsiveness with altered bodily, temporal, and spatial awareness.
  Supports sexual absorption as a measurable experience, but measures neither paralysis nor impaired refusal.
- [Safron 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5087698/ "What Is Orgasm? A Model of Sexual Trance and Climax via Rhythmic Entrainment"): “What Is Orgasm? A Model of Sexual Trance and Climax via Rhythmic Entrainment”.

    Proposes that rhythmic stimulation concentrates attention through neural entrainment, facilitating trance and orgasm.
  A mechanistic model for organizing observations, not a direct experimental test of entrainment or immobilization.
- [Ellero & Costa 2020](https://scielo.pt/scielo.php?pid=S1645-00862020000300782&script=sci_arttext "Altered States of Consciousness, Absorption, and Sexual Responsiveness"): “Altered States of Consciousness, Absorption, and Sexual Responsiveness”.

## BDSM and Subspace

- [Lee et al 2016](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0153126 "Altered States of Consciousness During an Extreme Ritual"): “Altered States of Consciousness During an Extreme Ritual”.

    Field study of a communal piercing ritual examining altered experience, mood, and cognitive performance in pierced and non-pierced participants.

- [Ambler et al 2017](https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000097 "Consensual BDSM Facilitates Role-Specific Altered States of Consciousness: A Preliminary Study"): “Consensual BDSM Facilitates Role-Specific Altered States of Consciousness: A Preliminary Study”.

- [Wuyts et al 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsxm.2020.01.001 "Between Pleasure and Pain: A Pilot Study on the Biological Mechanisms Associated With BDSM Interactions in Dominants and Submissives"): “Between Pleasure and Pain: A Pilot Study on the Biological Mechanisms Associated With BDSM Interactions in Dominants and Submissives”.

- [Carlström 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/1363460720964035 "Spiritual Experiences and Altered States of Consciousness—Parallels Between BDSM and Christianity"): “Spiritual Experiences and Altered States of Consciousness—Parallels Between BDSM and Christianity”.

    Draws on a 5-year Swedish ethnography, analyzing interviews with 14 of 29 participants selected for discussing spiritual or religious experiences.

    Practitioners had higher heat-pain thresholds overall, but not pressure-pain thresholds.

## Firsthand and Qualitative Accounts

- [Will 2012](https://thejourneyofwill.blogspot.com/2012/12/two-kinds-of-subspace.html "Two Kinds of Subspace"): “Two Kinds of Subspace”.

    Practitioner taxonomy with relevant reader comments: near-paralysis and incoherent speech (2014-04-08), absorption induced by gentle touch and speech (2015-01-25), and painless ordinary-sex subspace explicitly described as mentally rather than physically “boneless” (2015-04-06).

- [Will 2013](https://thejourneyofwill.blogspot.com/2013/01/why-safewords-are-not-safe.html "Why Safewords Are Not Safe"): “Why Safewords Are Not Safe”.

    Distinguishes inability to speak, forgetting a safeword, reluctance to disappoint, and impaired judgment.
  A 2019-04-26 comment alleges exploitation of a familiar state after an explicit refusal; it is relevant testimony about impaired refusal, not verified evidence of pleasure or motor paralysis during assault.
- [Witt 2013](https://www.nplusonemag.com/issue-16/essays/what-do-you-desire/ "What Do You Desire?"): “What Do You Desire?”

    Reporting on a commercial BDSM performance describes intense post-scene elation and difficulty articulating the experience.

- [Ari 2016](https://www.autostraddle.com/bottoms-up-bottoming-and-anal-sex-337254/ "Bottoms Up: Bottoming and Butt Sex"): “Bottoms Up: Bottoming and Butt Sex”.

    First-person account of anal stimulation producing whole-body relaxation, floating sensations, and reduced discursive thought, subsequently used to approach a desired submissive state.

- [Hamer 2024](https://www.tabooscience.show/040-bdsm/ "Philias: Why People Like BDSM (‘It Was Like Taking Drugs’)"): “Philias: Why People Like BDSM (‘It Was Like Taking Drugs’)”.

- [Gunnarsson 2025](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003535546 "Dynamics of Sexual Consent: Sex, Rape and the Grey Area In-Between"): _Dynamics of Sexual Consent: Sex, Rape and the Grey Area In-Between_, chapter 7, pp. 175–178.

## Related Phenomena and Limits of the Analogy

- [Pozza et al 2008](/doc/cat/psychology/2007-pozza.pdf "Pinch-Induced Behavioral Inhibition (‘Clipnosis’) in Domestic Cats"): “Pinch-Induced Behavioral Inhibition (‘Clipnosis’) in Domestic Cats”.

    Neck clips inhibited behavior in many of 31 cats, an example of tactile behavioral arrest interpreted by the authors as useful restraint.
  The paper did not establish subjective pleasure; read alongside Moody et al 2020 below.
- [Poryazova et al 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1743-6109.2009.01328.x "Weak with Sex: Sexual Intercourse as a Trigger for Cataplexy"): “Weak with Sex: Sexual Intercourse as a Trigger for Cataplexy”.

    Three of 29 patients with narcolepsy reported intercourse-triggered cataplexy.
  Demonstrates a distinct clinical route to sexual weakness or immobility, rather than showing that prolonged enjoyable trance is ordinarily cataplexy.
- [Esposito et al 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.03.041 "Infant Calming Responses During Maternal Carrying in Humans and Mice"): “Infant Calming Responses During Maternal Carrying in Humans and Mice”.

    Human infants and mouse pups reduced movement, vocalization, and heart rate during maternal carrying; causal physiological interventions were performed in mice.
  A nonsexual example of coordinated calming and reduced movement, not evidence that an infant transport response explains adult sexual trance.
- [Panero et al 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/aca0000044 "Do Actors Possess Traits Associated with High Hypnotizability?"): “Do Actors Possess Traits Associated with High Hypnotizability?”

    Acting students scored higher than music and non-arts students on absorption, imaginative suggestibility, and fantasy proneness.
  Hypnotizability itself was not tested, and the comparison cannot distinguish training effects from self-selection.
- [Meltzer et al 2017](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617691361 "Quantifying the Sexual Afterglow: The Lingering Benefits of Sex and Their Implications for Pair-Bonded Relationships"): “Quantifying the Sexual Afterglow: The Lingering Benefits of Sex and Their Implications for Pair-Bonded Relationships”.

    Diary studies of 214 newlywed couples found elevated sexual satisfaction lasting approximately 48 hours after sex.
  Here “afterglow” means lingering satisfaction, not a measured trance, euphoria, or inability to move during an encounter.
- [Janik McErlean & Osborne-Ford 2020](https://peerj.com/articles/8588/ "Increased Absorption in Autonomous Sensory Meridian Response"): “Increased Absorption in Autonomous Sensory Meridian Response”.

    A survey comparing 124 ASMR experiencers with 124 matched controls found higher absorption in the ASMR group.
  Connects the trait to pleasurable sensory immersion outside sex; neither immobility nor a shared mechanism with subspace was demonstrated.
- [Moody et al 2020](https://doi.org/10.1136/vr.105261 "Getting a Grip: Cats Respond Negatively to Scruffing and Clips"): “Getting a Grip: Cats Respond Negatively to Scruffing and Clips”.

    Cats displayed negative responses to scruffing and clip restraint compared with minimal handling.
  An important counterweight to interpreting reduced movement as relaxation, pleasure, or assent.
- [Luhrmann et al 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7865123/ "Sensing the Presence of Gods and Spirits Across Cultures and Faiths"): “Sensing the Presence of Gods and Spirits Across Cultures and Faiths”.

    4 studies involving over 2,000 participants across five countries linked absorption and culturally shaped beliefs about mental permeability to reported spiritual experiences.
  Extends absorption across experiential domains without implying that spiritual, hypnotic, and sexual states share one mechanism.
- [Semchenko et al 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672221115218 "Lordosis in Humans"): “Lordosis in Humans”.

    Two studies measured men's responses to animated female figures differing in lumbar curvature and back arching.
  Despite the title, the experiments concern perceived attractiveness, not induction of an involuntary female mating reflex.
