<!-- COMMENTARY:

GENRE AND LINEAGE:
A Marco Polo parable in the generic register of Italo Calvino, _Invisible Cities_ (1972), with secondary debts to Jorge Luis Borges's civic/theological parables ("Lottery in Babylon", "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius", "Three Versions of Judas") and to the bureaucratic flatness of medieval Eurasian travelogues (Marco Polo's _Travels_, Hyecho's _Memoir of the Pilgrimage to the Five Kingdoms of India_, Ibn Battuta's _Rihla_).
The frame clause "Sire... And he recounted:" invokes the Polo-Khan dialogue without quoting any specific Calvino city, and the tale does not imitate any specific Polo chapter.
Nearer-contemporary points of comparison, for positioning rather than borrowing: Guy Davenport's erudite miniatures; Cortázar, _Cronopios and Famas_; Milorad Pavić, _Dictionary of the Khazars_; Kate Bernheimer; Amina Cain; Jesse Ball; Anne Carson; and Eliot Weinberger's essay-as-world mode.
The piece also intersects Ted Chiang's philosophical determinism stories ("Story of Your Life", "What's Expected of Us") at the conceptual level, though the idiom is medieval rather than science-fictional.

VOICE AND TONAL CONTRACT:
The prose is declarative, enumerative, and dry.
Visible emotion is held to a minimum; civic oddities are reported as administrative facts, in the tradition of Polo's "Its tax register is..." openings.
The reader performs the moral response.
The one permitted tonal inversion in the first paragraph—"children are lifted to see the scaffold"—signals that the tale's flat register is load-bearing, and that grotesquerie will be delivered without authorial emphasis throughout.
Withholding interiority is deliberate: no citizen is named, no astrologer individuated, the Khan gives no reply, and Polo himself speaks one sentence.
No figure is available to the reader as a surrogate, so the reader has nowhere to stand except inside the city's ritual logic.

ARCHITECTURE:
Six movements, in ascending order of conceptual stakes and descending order of concreteness:

#. Geography, economy, and criminal register (terrain; taxation; crops; the scaffold inversion).
#. Planetary partition of professions (seven quarters in Chaldean order).
#. Customary law of the five non-luminary quarters (a miniature jurisprudence of binding).
#. The nativity ritual proper (the market of failed astrologers; the wax-sealed chart; the funerary reading; 3 sample horoscopes).
#. Philosophical self-inscription (the traveler's question; the citizens' reply)
#. Conclusion/exit (the gate-text; the absorption of dissent)

(Arguably, the framing story is the seventh movement, adding another 7 to the story architecture.)

The movement from census data to gate-inscription is the shape of a catechism working itself out backwards: the reader arrives at the doctrine only after inhabiting the practices it licenses.
The structure refuses the reading of the city as allegory first and place second; the place is described, the argument happens.

GEOGRAPHY AS SILK ROAD COMPOSITE:
Samarkand, felt-tent nomads, horseflesh, mulberry canals, salt, fire-temples, kariz, _qing_, Khotanese bonzes, ashen mendicants: the setting is a deliberate composite rather than a historically exact itinerary.
The point is not documentary Central Asia but the crossroads where Persian, Turkic, Mongol, Chinese, Indian, and Hellenistic systems of order can plausibly meet.
The composite is _dated_, not _located_: late Song / early Yuan vocabulary (_qing_, the Astronomical Bureau) meets earlier Samarkand (pre-Timurid, though the "turquoise towers" epithet is knowingly forward-tilted; see revision notes below).

ASTROLOGICAL ORDER:
The planetary quarters follow the Chaldean sequence—Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon—the classical order by geocentric speed, from slowest and outermost to fastest and nearest.
This is simultaneously (1) the traditional occult order of the "planetary hours" and "planetary days" used across Hellenistic, Persian, Arabic, and medieval European astrology; (2) a tonal gradient from cold/remote/grave to warm/near/quick; and (3) the order implicit in the Neoplatonic _ascent_ through the planetary spheres (reversed, so that the quarter tour descends the reader toward the Moon, which then opens onto birth, water, and dream).
The trades assigned to each quarter are not decorative:
- Saturn rules old age, stone, wells, death-work, foreigners, the marginal, and damaged or incomplete generation (stillborn).
- Jupiter rules law, treasury, judgment, oaths, and measurement, i.e. the civic forms of expansion made orderly.
- Mars rules iron, blood, surgery, butchery, and licensed violence.
- The Sun rules sovereignty, coinage, and the governor's public center.
- Venus rules dye, song, bathing, marriage, and social ornament.
- Mercury rules interpretation, commerce, theft, and writing—the media of mediation, honest or dishonest.
- The Moon rules water, birth, cyclical domestic labor, and dream record.

NUMEROLOGICAL PROGRAM:
The walls encode an astronomical diagram in masonry:
- 12 gates   → zodiacal signs
- 28 wells   → lunar mansions (Arabic _manāzil al-qamar_; Chinese _xiu_)
- 36 towers  → decans (Egyptian 10°-divisions of the zodiac)
- 60 stones  → bounds/terms (the five non-luminaries subdividing each sign: 5 × 12 = 60; also the sexagesimal minutes of arc)
- 360 niches → degrees of the ecliptic

The line "only the astrologers know how these numbers answer to the heavens" preserves esoteric authority without forced exposition.
The reader who recognizes the series feels the city's coherence directly; the reader who does not feels the presence of an ordered totality.
Both readings are serviceable—the mark of a well-placed allusion.

CUSTOMARY LAW AS A JURISPRUDENCE OF BINDING:
The five customs of the non-luminary quarters form a miniature theory of what binds what:
- Saturn (material binding): repair follows visible decay.
- Jupiter (oath binding): oaths deplete through repetition, even for the swearer.
- Mars (weapon binding): open cutting is legitimate; concealed cutting is not.
- Venus (contractual binding): enchantment (song) voids consent.
- Mercury (rhetorical binding): elegant falsehood is fined less than profitable truth.

The Sun and Moon receive no explicit law because sovereignty and birth/death already structure the whole city; two more customs would over-close the sequence.
The nativity ritual, when it arrives, can then be read as a sixth law of the same kind: _prediction_ binds only while unread.
The laws-paragraph is therefore not decoration but argument; it prepares the central paradox as a member of a series the reader has already accepted.

CENTRAL CONCEIT:
Every citizen has a natal chart cast at birth.
The wax remains sealed until the funeral.
This converts astrology from prediction-for-action into posthumous biography.
The horoscope is allowed to be true only because it never becomes advice.
The move evades the self-reference problem of prophecy—where disclosure becomes cause—by quarantining foreknowledge from action.
It is also, recognizably, a Newcomb-problem solution: the predictor is accurate _iff_ the subject does not consult the prediction, and the city institutes the sealing as a civic enforcement of the epistemic conditions under which oracular accuracy is even coherent.

PHILOSOPHICAL ARGUMENT:
The city stages compatibilism in astrological costume.
The citizens are determined—or treated as determined—but they are not puppets in their own experience because the determining description is withheld from them.
The life unfolds from character, habit, ignorance, appetite, fear, and circumstance.
Only afterward does the chart name the pattern.
Five readings, all simultaneously available:

#. *Compatibilist* (the dominant reading): determinism is preserved at the cosmic level (the chart pre-exists), freedom at the phenomenal level (the subject deliberates without foreknowledge). The ritual institutionalizes the ignorance compatibilism requires.
#. *Newcomb-structural*: disclosure would collapse the prediction. The sealing is the contract with the oracle.
#. *Stoic*: _ducunt volentem fata, nolentem trahunt_. The citizens are willing by ignorance; they consent to their nativities without knowing them. The gate inscription is explicit: "The stars obey; therefore they are free."
#. *Spinozist*: the chart is the adequate idea of the life. Ordinary humans live under inadequate ideas. The funerary reading is the moment the life is finally adequately comprehended—but by the survivors, not the subject. _Sub specie aeternitatis_ relocated from reason to ritual.
#. *Kantian (inverted)*: Kant demands that agents act under the idea of freedom regardless of the metaphysical question. The city makes this a civic ordinance: the chart exists (determinism) but the citizen must act as if it did not (freedom), and the polity maintains the sealing that makes the duality livable.

The core line is "Thus they forgive lives for happening only once".
Its grammar is precise: not "they forgive the dead", not "they forgive themselves", but "they forgive *lives*"—the category.
What is forgiven is the ontological structure of finite existence: that each life is unrepeatable, non-correctable, and read only after it is over.
The city has built a ritual that makes peace with mortality by treating the life as a closed text whose meaning is fixed but whose reading is deferred.

GATE INSCRIPTION:
"The stars obey; therefore they are free." is the city's constitutional formula.
It reverses the customary direction of the free-will debate.
Ordinarily we ask whether the agent can be free *given* cosmic necessity.
The inscription asks whether the cosmos is free *because* it obeys.
The implicit answer: freedom is not the absence of law but the complete coincidence of being and law.
A planet that perfectly expresses its orbit is free in a way a human who deliberates is not, because the planet is not divided against itself.
The citizens, by not reading their charts, refuse the division that would otherwise introduce un-freedom into their lives.
The life that conforms to its chart without knowing it does so is, by the inscription's logic, as free as Saturn.
A Stoic and Spinozist thought in miniature, rendered as city signage.

THE THREE HOROSCOPES:
The funerary readings do specific literary work.
They use technical vocabulary (domicile, exaltation, fall, combustion, Lot of Spirit, profection, cadent, retrograde, affliction from the eighth) to produce *density without exposition*.
A reader with no astrological training feels coherence; a reader with training finds real chart-arguments.
The 3 cases balance moral registers—greed, gift, and cowardice—and map onto the 3 non-luminary visible planets of traditional moral iconography: Jupiter (greater benefic, corruptible into miserliness when retrograde in wealth), Venus (lesser benefic, corruptible into thwarted beauty when combust), and Mars (malefic, corruptible into misplaced courage when cadent).
Specifically:
- **The miser**. Jupiter retrograde in the second house, afflicted from the eighth by a Saturn in fall. Jupiter is the greater benefic and rules wealth; the second house rules money; retrogradation turns expansion inward, producing hoarding rather than flow. The eighth house rules other people's money, inheritance, and death; Saturn in fall (in Aries) is the restrictive principle in its most debilitated dignity, casting a hostile aspect back at the 2nd. This is a coherent traditional signature for miserliness-under-inheritance-anxiety.
- **The singer**. Venus in her Taurus domicile (home sign, full dignity), setting (approaching the descendant, the house of public others), combust (within ~8° of the Sun, her light swallowed), joined to the Pleiades (fixed-star cluster traditionally associated with weeping sisters, with grief, and with sudden destruction), her Lot of Spirit in the house of enemies. A real gift, made invisible, tearful, and self-defeated—beauty burned by proximity to the king-star.
- **The soldier**. Mars exalted (in Capricorn, the disciplined-courage dignity) but cadent (in the 3rd/6th/9th/12th—the houses distant from the angles, where effectiveness bleeds off). His twenty-third profection—i.e. the annual time-lord technique—lands in the twelfth house, the house of isolation, hidden enemies, and self-undoing. A strong Mars placed where it cannot act. "Courage is buried with its dead" does in five words what a less compressed version would do in a paragraph.

THE CLOSING MOVE:
The bonzes of Khotan (Mahāyāna Buddhist; their _anātman_ doctrine would dissolve the "native" whose nativity is cast) and the ashen mendicants (Śaiva/Jain ascetic traditions in which ash-smearing accompanies an austerity-modulated karmic determinism) are the two specific Silk Road critics best positioned to dispute the city's compatibilism.
They are not refuted.
They are relocated—assigned to the Mercury quarter, where elegant falsehood is fined less than profitable truth.
The polity does not treat their philosophy as wrong but as urban speech, subject to Mercury's customary law.
This is a move philosophy cannot easily recover from: it accepts the argument as argument and denies it the standing of argument-*about-the-world*.
The city does not argue; it zones.

ETHICAL SHADOW:
The reader is not asked to admire or condemn the city, and the piece declines to do either on the reader's behalf.
Several costs are visible on inspection:

- *Consolation purchased at the price of agency*. If wrongdoing is always already chart-inscribed, effort, failure, and correction lose purchase during life. The miser cannot be reformed, only eulogized.
- *Moral stasis as sedation*. Executions are rare because the categories of serious crime and alterable character have atrophied together. The rarity is not virtue but anesthesia.
- *Caste grounded theologically*. The planetary partition of professions is inherited; the theological framing conceals its inheritance structure. A child born in the Saturn quarter is born a Saturn subject.
- *The quiet authoritarianism of the pluralist polity*. Dissent is not persecuted; it is *accommodated*, which is in some ways worse for the dissenter, because it denies him even the dignity of resistance.

Against all this, the citizens live, pay their taxes, are patient with strangers, mourn the dead, and console the mourners. The city works.
Whether its working is a model or a warning is the reader's question; the refusal to decide is the final ethical texture of the tale.

CONTEMPORARY RESONANCE:
The piece wears medieval astrology as costume; the underlying subject is causal contamination by prediction.
It maps cleanly onto actuarial risk scoring, algorithmic profiling, genomic risk prediction, recommendation systems, performance review, precision medicine, and any apparatus that alters the behavior it claims merely to describe.
The city's solution—quarantine foreknowledge so it cannot enter the life—is an oblique comment on these contemporary regimes.
The philosophically interesting observation is that the city is not merely fatalist: it distinguishes *determinism*, *predictability*, and *usable prediction* as 3 separate issues, and constructs a civic religion only on the third.

THE STRONGEST INCOMPATIBILIST OBJECTION:
A perfect horoscope should predict its own disclosure and the subject's reaction to it.
Classical prophecy, from Oedipus onward, often works exactly this way.
The city's rule against disclosure is therefore not logically required by determinism; it is culturally chosen.
The citizens want lives uncontaminated by explicit knowledge of their pattern.
This is less a metaphysical claim than an ethics of narration: a life should not read its own ending.
The story's best reply is not an argument but a definition: "How could it remain his nativity, had he used it as advice?"—a consulted chart is a different object.

> The customs of the quarters are these.
> <!-- Formula of a law code; also a narrative pivot from *who lives where* to *how they live there*. Six words. -->
> In Saturn's quarter no house may be repaired until a stone has fallen.
> <!-- Saturn governs stone, decay, patience, and necessity. The law makes repair wait on visible material decline: no pre-emptive maintenance, no vanity improvement. The economy is of the grudged concession to entropy, which is the Saturnian virtue. -->
> In Jupiter's quarter a man may swear the same oath no more than three times; thereafter it binds no one, not even the swearer.
> <!-- Jupiter is the oath-god (Zeus Horkios / Jove), the greater benefic, and is afflicted by excess (pomp, inflation, grandiloquence). Most systems *increase* binding through repetition; this law *depletes* binding through repetition—oaths are a finite resource, and the swearer is the first victim of their inflation. "Not even the swearer" is the crux: what is lost is not merely the listener's confidence but the swearer's own capacity to be bound. "Three" is the traditional ritual tipping-point (3× repetition as ritual completion in both IE and Semitic liturgical traditions). -->
> In Mars's quarter knives may be sharpened openly, but never hidden.
> <!-- Mars governs weapons and cutting. The moral axis is not *violence versus non-violence* (that would be too pious for a Martian quarter) but *open violence versus hidden violence*. The law legalizes blade-care as a civic act while criminalizing its concealment—the quarter's ethic is the duelist's, not the pacifist's. -->
> In Venus's quarter debts contracted by song are not recoverable.
> <!-- Venus governs song and enchantment; a debt contracted under the influence of song is contracted under enchantment, and the law treats such contracts as voided by the enchantment itself—pleasure corrupts consent. The law is also comic: it licenses a particular kind of Venerian dishonor (the singer who dines for free) in exchange for recognizing that music is not arm's-length negotiation. -->
> In Mercury's quarter a lie told elegantly is fined less than a truth told for profit.
> <!-- Mercury governs rhetoric, mediation, and the *form* of speech. The law privileges elegance over factuality and penalizes mercenary honesty—i.e. the quarter's ethic is aesthetic rather than epistemic. It also satirizes sophistry without denouncing it: sophistry is a recognized civic function, not a civic crime. The line foreshadows the closing absorption of the bonzes and mendicants into this quarter. -->
> There are also, about the outer wall, [12 gates](!W "Zodiac"), [28 wells](!W "Lunar mansion"), [36 towers](!W "Decan"), [60 boundary-stones](!W "Essential dignity"), and [360 mulberries](!W "Degree (angle)"), which I did not look for; none but the astrologers know how these numbers answer to the heavens.
> <!-- The city's walls diagram the ecliptic: 12 signs / 28 lunar mansions / 36 decans (10°-divisions) / 60 bounds-or-terms (the 5 non-luminary planets subdividing each of the 12 signs, 5×12 = 60; simultaneously the 60 sexagesimal minutes of a degree) / 360°. The pun on "60" holds both the Hellenistic astrological bounds and Babylonian/sexagesimal arithmetic. The 360th term should be niches (the standard architectural fill for the degree-count); mulberries substitute, echoing the mulberry canal of the opening paragraph, and making the final item in the astrological number-set uncountable-by-the-system. Polo's "which I did not look for" is the second of three withholdings (*shun* / *did not look for* / *hour lost*); the astrologers' claim to know how mulberries answer to the heavens is now ironic rather than awed—the city's totalizing self-account is preserved in its own voice, but the traveler's report has admitted a surplus the system cannot reach. -->
>
> <!-- P4 GLOSS:
> The fourth paragraph discloses the city's religion: a sealed natal chart, read only at the funeral.
> It begins with the market of failed astrologers (accuracy is professionally disqualifying), proceeds through the sealing/funeral ritual, and closes with 3 worked example charts.
> The Saturn-quarter-wells detail closes the planetary loop opened in paragraph 2 (the washers of the stillborn → the Saturn-quarter wells at the last washing). -->
> Only failed astrologers may sit in the [market of Mercury](!W "Bazaar"), offering [auspicious hours](!W "Electional astrology") for journeys, [lucky names](!W "Onomancy"), lawsuits, reconciliations, haircuts, and cures for [barren goats](https://psi-encyclopedia.spr.ac.uk/articles/sheep-goat-effect/ "‘Sheep-Goat Effect’, Psi Encyclopedia 2016").
> <!-- The market is Mercury's: commerce, speech for hire, fraud, translation, calculation, and the small public sale of futures.
> It is reserved for *failed* astrologers because accuracy is professionally disqualifying: a market-astrologer who can predict births would make prediction actionable, and actionable prediction is exactly what the civic religion forbids.
> The catalogue descends in gravity: journeys (life-direction, electional astrology), lucky names (identity, onomancy), lawsuits (conflict), reconciliations (repair of conflict), haircuts (bodily vanity and elective timing), and cures for barren goats (rural bathos, livestock economy, and a concealed parapsychology joke).
> The "goats" are also the goats of the sheep-goat effect: in parapsychology, sheep are believers who tend to psi-hit, while goats are skeptics who tend to psi-miss.
> This lets the comic final item echo the story's central epistemic rule: divination works only under the right stance toward it, and fails when made skeptical, commercial, or self-conscious.
> The allusion should remain hidden in the annotation/link rather than stated in the visible prose, because twentieth-century psi terminology would break the Silk Road travelogue surface. -->
> A man who correctly forecasts three births is then barred.
> <!-- "3" again (the ritual tipping-point from the Jupiter law). The threshold is concrete: too-accurate prediction is a public hazard. The civic logic: prediction must be generally true in the sealed chart and generally error-prone in the market; a diviner who bridges the two categories would collapse the system. Legibility is licensed only under conditions of non-consumption. -->
>
> <hr> <!-- second ruler: moon -->
>
> So devoted is its populace that a [horoscope](!W) is cast at birth, and the [figure](!W "Natal chart") sealed in wax.
> <!-- "So devoted" inherits from the previous sentence—devotion is why the accuracy-ban is tolerated. "Figure" is the technical term (the chart as _diagramma_, not mere paper). The wax seal is simultaneously bureaucratic (Byzantine/medieval document practice), sacramental (wax as the material of oaths and indulgences), and informational (the seal is the epistemic enforcement of non-consultation). -->
> At the funeral, when the body has been washed in water drawn from the Saturn-quarter wells and turned toward the west, the [_alchocoden_](https://www.skyscript.co.uk/glossary/alchocoden/ "'Alchocoden', Skyscript") breaks the seal and reads aloud.
> <!-- "Saturn-quarter wells": the planetary partition closes on itself—Saturn rules death-work, well-cleaners, and washers of the stillborn (P2); his wells now wash the corpse (P4). Turned toward the west: the canonical funerary orientation, also solar setting, also the direction of the gate-inscription (P6). The _alchocoden_ is the medieval-Latin form of the Arabic _al-kadkhudāh_, traditionally the "giver of years" (the planet determining length of life in the Hellenistic/Arabic synthesis); here the title is refunctionalized as the civic office of the giver-of-years at the *end* of those years, reading them out. The etymology does quiet thematic work: the giver of years is also their reader. -->
> Then the mourners marvel, or lower their faces, hearing of illnesses, journeys, inheritances, adulteries, virtues, and stupidities past correction.
> <!-- "Marvel, or lower their faces"—the two polar reactions, admiration and shame, without narrative investment in either. The Chaucerian six-item list (illnesses, journeys, inheritances, adulteries, virtues, stupidities) moves from passive condition to active failure; closing on "stupidities past correction" is the tale's first explicit statement of the irreversibility theme. The word "stupidity" is deliberately small (not *folly*, not *sin*)—the diction refuses tragic elevation. -->
> A miser is found to have had Jupiter [retrograde](!W "Apparent retrograde motion") in the [second house](!W "House (astrology)"), afflicted from the eighth by a Saturn in [fall](https://www.skyscript.co.uk/glossary/exaltation/ "'Exaltation (and Fall)', Skyscript"); a singer had Venus in her [Taurus domicile](!W "Domicile (astrology)"), setting and [combust](https://www.skyscript.co.uk/glossary/combust/ "'Combust/Combustion', Skyscript"), joined to the [Pleiades](!W), her [Lot of Spirit](!W "Arabic parts") fallen on the house of enemies; a soldier who fled three battles had Mars exalted but [cadent](https://www.skyscript.co.uk/glossary/cadent/ "'Cadent', Skyscript"), his [twenty-third profection](https://theastrologypodcast.com/2018/04/26/annual-profections-a-basic-time-lord-technique/ "'Annual Profections: A Basic Time-Lord Technique', Brennan 2018") to the twelfth house, where courage is buried with its dead.
> <!-- 3 worked charts, each a coherent traditional signature. MISER: Jupiter (greater benefic, ruler of wealth) retrograde (expansion inverted, hoarding) in the 2nd (the house of resources and movable goods); afflicted from the 8th (death, inheritance, other people's money) by Saturn in fall (Saturn in Aries, its dignity of debility—restriction at its worst)—so abundance is turned inward and attacked by the principle of denial operating from the domain of inheritance. The native hoards against death. SINGER: Venus in Taurus (her domicile, full dignity, the gift is real), setting (approaching the descendant / 7th house, the horizon of public others), combust (within ~8° of the Sun, her light devoured by the king-star), joined to the Pleiades (a fixed-star cluster associated in Arabic/medieval tradition with weeping, sudden loss, and violent destruction; the Seven Sisters of myth), her Lot of Spirit (the Hellenistic _kleros_ of inner resources and initiative, calculated from the Ascendant + Sun − Moon by day, reversed by night) fallen on the house of enemies (the 7th or 12th depending on Lot-of-Spirit conventions). The gift is present, burned, drowned, and turned against her. SOLDIER: Mars exalted (in Capricorn, his dignity of disciplined courage) but cadent (in the 3rd/6th/9th/12th, the houses removed from the angles, where effectiveness bleeds away); at age 23, his annual profection falls in the 12th house, the house of isolation, hidden enemies, and self-undoing. A strong Mars placed where it cannot act. "Courage is buried with its dead": the exhausted five-word close, doing the work of a stanza. -->
>
> <!-- P5 GLOSS:
> The fifth paragraph stages the paradox as dialogue rather than exposition, which is the Calvino move: the outsider's obvious question gets the insider's flat reply, and the flatness itself is the argument.
> The closing line ("forgive lives for happening only once") is the tale's thesis sentence. -->
> If the traveler asks why the horoscope was not read while the man lived, the people answer patiently, as if explaining [weirs](!W) to a fish.
> <!-- The traveler's question is the utilitarian objection: if the chart is true, why not use it? "Patiently" (not "embarrassedly", not "puzzledly") is the precise affect: the answer has been rehearsed over generations, and the speaker has a standing reply for foreigners. 'Water/wells to a fish' is too cliche; we instead use fish traps. The figure is no longer "you are submerged in what you're asking about" but "you are caught in what you're asking about." The weir is the mechanism that catches; the fish's incomprehension of weirs is constitutive of the fish's relation to weirs. The traveler asking why the chart isn't consulted is asking, from inside the city's mechanism, why the mechanism that catches him exists. The citizens' patience is the patience of those who have built the weir explaining it to the fish, who is the only one who can be caught by it. -->
> "How could it remain his nativity", they say, "had he used it as advice?"
> <!-- The definitional claim: a nativity is by definition the chart that is not consulted. A consulted chart becomes a plan, a regimen, a self-fulfilling or self-falsifying act—in any case a different object. The citizens treat "nativity" as a technical category of statement accurate only under the condition of non-use. The sentence is a Newcomb-problem analysis compressed into a single counterfactual. -->
> Thus they forgive lives for happening only once.
> <!-- The thesis sentence. Grammar is precise: not "they forgive the dead", not "they forgive themselves", but "they forgive *lives*"—the abstract plural, the category. What is forgiven is the ontological structure of finite existence: unrepeatability, non-correctability, and the fact that the reading comes only after the life is over. The line is Stoic-Spinozist in philosophical lineage and elegiac in tone; it is the line an editor would underline. -->
>
>
> <!-- P6 GLOSS:
> The final paragraph turns custom into constitutional theology, then neutralizes critique by incorporation.
> The western gate is the site of the inscription because the city's doctrine is legible only at the end of a life (west = sunset = funerary direction, continuing the loop from P4's "turned toward the west").
> The bonzes and mendicants are not refuted but *zoned*—the administrative move philosophy cannot easily counter. -->
>
> Atop the western gate is written: \
> *The stars obey; therefore they are free.*
> <!-- West: the gate of sunset, funerary orientation (P4), and end-of-life disclosure. The inscription is the city's constitutional paradox. It inverts the usual free-will opposition: obedience is not slavery but perfect self-expression. A planet that fully enacts its orbit is free in a way a divided human who deliberates is not. A Spinozist and Stoic thought, rendered as civic signage. The italics mark it as inscription (not speech), with no further emphasis, because the axiom is meant to sit flat on the stone. -->
>
> Against this sentence the [bonzes](!W "Bonze") of [Khotan](!W "Kingdom of Khotan") and the [ashen mendicants](!W "Sadhu") make subtle arguments.
> <!-- The two philosophical traditions best equipped to dispute the inscription: Khotanese Mahāyāna Buddhists (whose _anātman_ / no-self doctrine would dissolve the "native" whose nativity is cast, making the entire civic machinery point at a fiction) and the ash-smeared ascetic mendicants (sadhus and/or Jain ascetics, in which karmic determinism is modulated by austerity rather than consoled through ritual), i.e. the Indian and Indic-diasporic philosophical traditions travelers on the Silk Road would actually encounter. "Bonze" is the medieval European term for East Asian Buddhist monks—slightly anachronistic for a Khotanese context (Khotan's Buddhism is Central Asian Mahāyāna rather than East Asian, and "bonze" later drifts toward Japanese/Chinese connotation in European sources), but accepted here as part of the composite Polo-idiom. "Subtle arguments" (not "clever", not "futile") is chosen to confer genuine philosophical respect on the dissenters—they are not strawmen. -->
> The citizens listen courteously, assign them to the quarter of Mercury, and go on as before.
> <!-- The closing coup. 3 verbs, in sequence: *listen* (receive), *assign* (classify), *go on as before* (dismiss by continuity). The city's response to philosophy is not refutation but zoning. Dissent receives civic status under Mercury, the quarter in which elegant falsehood is fined less than profitable truth (P3)—so the dissenters' arguments are reclassified as Mercurial speech, a local civic function rather than a claim about the world. The move is simultaneously hospitable and annihilating: the critics are welcomed into the city they would unmake, and the city is not unmade. Calvino's best closes work this way—not refutation but incorporation. -->

<!-- Ending: right now the piece ends inside the city, on the city's own aphorism. Acquiescence collapses the piece. If Polo endorses the city's theology the frame becomes decoration, and worse, the Khan has no reason to hear the report: Kublai listens to Polo precisely because Polo remains foreign to each city he describes, which is what lets the cities mean something to an emperor who cannot visit them. Acquiescence would also betray the actual Polo-function in Calvino, which is that he is the one who has seen and therefore the one who has not been consoled. A Polo who accepts the Counted Stars has stopped traveling. Calvino's framing almost always returns: a sentence, sometimes only a clause, where we are back on the terrace, and the Khan has heard something, and what he has heard is not quite what Polo said. The return does not need to be long. "The Khan was silent" is enough, if the silence is specific. The current ending—"listen courteously, assign them to Mercury, and go on as before"—is the city's voice, not Polo's, and it closes the piece in the city's own terms, which is exactly the sealing you want to break. A speaking Khan takes the city away from Polo and puts it in contact with a second intelligence, which is what the frame is for. The whole _Invisible Cities_ engine is that Kublai is not a passive audience; he tests, completes, refutes, and sometimes out-imagines Polo's cities. A Khan who replies is the frame actually functioning. A Khan whose nativity was never catchable is structurally excluded from the city's consolation, and he is telling Polo this. It is also biographically plausible in the way Calvino liked (steppe birth, nomadic conditions, a real fact about Mongol versus Persianate record-keeping cultures) without being researched-feeling... The Khan in Calvino, when he speaks, often speaks in fragments or questions or in sentences that trail—his utterances have the texture of thought happening, not thought delivered. The line wants to be slightly more broken, or slightly more private, or to not quite finish. And then nothing—Polo does not reply, the scene does not continue, the piece ends on the lost hour rather than on the storm. It matches the opening—one verb doing the work, uncommented—and it gives the piece the symmetry it actually wants, which is not seven-planets-seven-quarters but *two withholdings*, one at each end: Polo shuns, the Khan's hour is lost. Neither man says why. The city in the middle explains everything about everyone, and is framed by two men who have declined to be explained. That is the piece the conceit deserves, and it's the piece that stops reading as AI, because the model will generate the city but will not generate the two silences that contain it. -->
"The hour of my birth", said the Khan, "was lost."
