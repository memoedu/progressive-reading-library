> A woman has fallen, and 2 voices dispute whether she has landed. The poem is a reverso: 15 lines read down, then the same 15 read up, with no word changed. Read down, each consolation is refused; read up, each despair is; 3 verdicts are refused both ways.
> 
> A claim standing at the left margin is asserted, and a claim hung from the line above it is denied by that line. The lines are numbered 1 to 15 on the way down and 15 to 1 on the way back, so a number names the same line on both sides of the glass. Between the stanzas stand 5 palindromes, which read the same from either side.
> 
> The title, both epigraphs, the fall, and the locked doors are [Lewis Carroll’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Carroll "Lewis Carroll"), from the first 2 chapters of [_Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice%27s_Adventures_in_Wonderland "Alice's Adventures in Wonderland").
