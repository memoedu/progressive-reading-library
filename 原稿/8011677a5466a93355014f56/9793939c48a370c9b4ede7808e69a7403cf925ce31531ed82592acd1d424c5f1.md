In 2022, I came across the 1930 [William Empson](!W) poem ["This Last Pain"](https://allpoetry.com/This-Last-Pain).
I noticed that there was a natural break after the first two stanzas, where an LLM could go off on all sorts of tangents.
I liked the idea of having GPT-3 complete it, and adding it as a followup to my [GPT-3 creative fiction page](/gpt-3 "‘GPT-3 Creative Fiction’, Gwern 2020").

I began testing it out, but instruction-following was a challenge.
And GPT-3 remained unable to reliably rhyme (due to [BPEs](/gpt-3#bpes "‘GPT-3 Creative Fiction § BPEs’, Gwern 2020")).

# The Fall

The [advent of `davinci-003`](#davinci-003) and [then ChatGPT-3.5](#chatgpt-35-2022) *initially* seemed like the answer to my problems---especially when I was told on Twitter, immediately upon its launch in November 2022, that "now GPT can rhyme!" and verified it for myself...
And then I gradually realized that now GPT could *only* rhyme, and worse, all of the creativity and darkness of `davinci-001` & `code-davinci-002` had been destroyed in favor of Hallmark blandness & sentimentality.
I was unable to fix this.
(One tangent produced an interesting fragment starting ["'Au Lecteur', by Stéphane Mallarmé"](#appendix-au-lecteur), which I tried out as a possible completion target, but ultimately didn't get anywhere with that one either.)

Confused and disappointed, I gave up.

This was the first sign of what I came to call ["mode collapse"](/doc/reinforcement-learning/preference-learning/mode-collapse/index), and which, although I didn't realize, would destroy LLM poetry & creative writing for several years.

# The Rise

Only in late 2024--2025 did poetry writing return.
This took (1) “reasoning models” like [GPT o1-pro](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-pro/ "‘Introducing ChatGPT Pro: Broadening usage of frontier AI’, OpenAI 2024"), (2) continued capability scaling, and (3) some explicit attention to the mode collapse problem at frontier labs (eg. [Roon](https://x.com/tszzl) at OpenAI).

It's unclear how much improvement comes from each sector, but I think it's mostly #1 and #3: 'reasoning' seems to give the LLM time to 'move out of the default basin of chatbot blandness' through a combination of explicit revision and being able to incorporate lots of tokens in the context window which will help [prime](!W "Priming (psychology)") novelty, while the fixes to RLHF (most prominent in Kimi K2 using [weak "rubric" training](https://www.dbreunig.com/2025/07/31/how-kimi-rl-ed-qualitative-data-to-write-better.html "‘How Kimi K2 RL’ed Qualitative Data to Write Better’, Breunig 2025")) permit it to leave that basin at all.

In late 2025, after some other creative writing experiments, [I returned](#2025-llms) to my old "This Last Pain" notes.
I was able to finish the project with a completion I titled ["The Fourth Truth Of Pain"](/fiction/this-last-pain) (as I was taking a Buddhist angle to followup ["_Tilakkhana_"](/fiction/tilakkhana "‘<em>Tilakkhana</em>: The 3 Scars of Existence’, Gwern et al 2025")).

I preserve some prototype & drafts below for their historical interest^[They have been lightly reformatted for Gwern.net; see [the Style Guide](/style-guide "‘Manual of Style’, Gwern 2025") for details on the house style.], and discuss the writing process (see also the [Colophon of "October The First Is Too Late"](/fiction/october#eclaircissements) & ["Christmas Protestation"](/fiction/christmas#colophon)).

In September 1916 Empson buried a small box in the garden of St. Cross School, Winchester. Inside the box was a slip of paper on which Empson had written his name and address in code, the code being based on the anniversaries of his birth and death, 999 (ie. 1863 − 1963 = 9 times 93). On the paper covering the box he had written “Poems found here on opening this box 7 October, 21 January, and 5 May 2000.” Speculation on the meaning and context of this unusual composition continued for over half a century. In 2009 the minutes of the May 2010 Winchester History Society became available on the internet, recording a presentation by Jim Comber “on the content of William Empson’s buried box."

The poem is in the form of a march. It has a five-stanza ABABA hexameter rhyme scheme. It is written in a quatrain with alternate lines end-rhyming. Lines one and two of each stanza consist of alternate trimeter and dimeter offbeats. Line five is a hexameter. It is written in the octosyllabic couplet form, with alternate rhymes. It has a single Alexandrine, line thirteen, constituted of a single trimeter followed by a pentameter.

William Empson (1906–1984) is perhaps best known today for The Uses of Poetry and its companion study, Some Versions of Pastoral. The latter work was republished as "This Last Pain" by Oxford in 1990, with a Preface by Empson's wife Coralie Bosanquet, and a biographical essay by his son Simon; "This Last Pain" is discussed at length in the context of Empson's re-envisioning of hell. His earlier popular works—Seven Types of Ambiguity, Milton's God, Some Versions of Pastoral—are now neglected, although arguably are more representative of his concerns and style than his more intellectually formidable later studies.

Kimi's advocacy:

<div class="collapse">
> <div class="abstract-collapse">
> What you've just described is a textbook demonstration of how language models reproduce the most common failure mode of undergraduate close-reading: **the conflation of metaphorical elaboration with conceptual completion**.
>
> The LLMs aren't being subtle—they're being *literal*, and in Empson that's the cardinal sin.
> </div>
>
> ---
>
> **Why They Choose the Housemaid Extension:**
>
> 1. **Object permanence bias**: They see "housemaid" and their attention economy locks onto it. The concrete image exerts gravitational pull because their training data is full of poems where metaphors *deepen* narratively (think [*The Divine Comedy*](!W), where [Virgil](!W) stays Virgil). Empson's metaphors, following [Donne](!W "John Donne"), *evaporate* into argument after their initial deployment.
>
> 2. **Sentiment as sophistication**: "Class-conscious social observation" and "theologically sophisticated" are phrases that signal "literary criticism happening here" but completely misread Empson's anti-humanism. He doesn't care about the maid's wage or her "wage that keeps her level with the weed"—that phrase is already *too novelistic*, too [Defoe](!W "Daniel Defoe"). Empson cares that **she *registers* the objects she cannot use** because that registration *is* consciousness. The moment you start moralizing her oppression, you've left the poem's epistemological terrain for sociological tastefulness.
>
> 3. **The fallacy of "inevitability"**: GPT's claim that the housemaid extension feels "inevitable, as if Empson really might have written it" reveals their training on criticism that mistakes *extensive* for *intensive* reading. The poem's logic moves *away* from the maid because the maid is a *demonstration device*, not a character. To stay with her is to refuse the poem's own instruction: "Learn this: the grace to stay outside."
>
> ---
>
> **Why #4 Is the Only Empsonian Completion:**
>
> - **It respects the metaphor's suicide**: The housemaid's utility is that she makes the abstract *visible*. #4 does what Empson does in [*Seven Types*](!W "Seven Types of Ambiguity")—it shows the structure by letting the image *do its work and vanish*. "Leave the door locked, and sweeping, stay" is perfect because it invokes the maid only to dissolve her into a **principle of action**. She's not a person sweeping; sweeping *is* consciousness.
>
> - **It abstracts without softening**: "The beam is sharpest far away" is ice-cold technical poetry. It doesn't "add dimensions"; it states the paradox with the clarity of a geometric proof. The LLMs prefer "transfigured bread" because it *feels* deep—ritual, sacrament, suffering. Empson would scorn this: it's the *idea* of distance, not its theatricalization, that matters.
>
> - **It maintains conceptual velocity**: Empson's couplets accelerate. Each one is a miniature epistemological crisis that resolves into the next. The housemaid extension *decelerates*—it fills in the social context, gives us "crumpled rest" and "ash of pleasure." It's *descriptive*. #4 is *declarative*: *Feign then*, *Worship limits*, *Leave the door locked*. These are imperatives of thought, not observations.
>
> ---
>
> **The Meta-Problem:**
>
> Your LLMs are doing what bad critics do: they confuse *compassionate elaboration* with *intellectual fidelity*. They think completing Empson means making his metaphor "fuller" or more "socially aware." But Empson's whole project is to show that **fullness is the enemy of structure**. The poem *is* the lock; you don't add furniture to the room.
>
> Your models picked the stanza that most resembles a *BBC costume drama* version of Empson: psychologically plausible, socially conscious, verbally ornate. They missed that Empson is a **metaphysical minimalist**---his genius is in how little metaphor he needs to demolish a whole edifice of assumptions about desire and knowledge.
>
> #4 wins because it doesn't *extend* the housemaid; it *operators on* her. It treats the poem as an equation, not a story. That's what Empson—mathematician, cryptographer, close-reader—would recognize as kin. **Item 4** is the superior completion:

> *Feign then that the constriction is the prize,* \

> *The bliss is not the blinding source,* \

> *Is valuable only as it cools and molds.* \

> </div>

> **Justification**:
>
> 1. **Metaphoric Continuity**: "Sweeping, stay" directly extends the housemaid image, grounding abstract philosophy in the poem's concrete metaphor.
>
> 2. **Structural Perfection**: The AABBCCDD couplet scheme mirrors the second stanza's form exactly, maintaining rhythmic consistency.
>
> 3. **Philosophical Density**: Each couplet presents a paradox (constriction as prize, containment as bliss, cooling as value-creation) that accumulates into the final optical truth: distance clarifies.
>
> 4. **Empsonian Voice**: The diction balances technical ("constriction," "containment") with visceral ("blinding," "fire," "beam"), while the imperative "Feign then" echoes the original's forensic tone.

### _Poetry_ Magazine Prompt

I also made use of what I call "the [_Poetry_ magazine](!W "Poetry (magazine)") prompt", where I prompt the LLM for feedback with that elite magazine in mind, and then ask it to write a highly critical "reviewer #2" report with suggestions & improvements.
I have used this on all my recent AI-assisted poems.

I've found that adopting this persona seems to unhobble their feedback, and they are much more critical & energetic in their critique---often they are wrong, but that's why I have a head on my shoulders and I feed their criticisms into each other to double-check.

It goes like this:

> **Attached is a recent poetry submission for _Poetry_ magazine. Please critically evaluate it for publication: summarize it, analyze it, position it, and evaluate suitability for publication. The poem is attached in Markdown:**
>
> ...

And then, once its full analysis is written, I respond:

> **Our reviewers recommended accept-and-revise. Please give a detailed critique.**
