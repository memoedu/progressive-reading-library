## Five Books I Liked

While I did mostly read books on the [twelve foundations](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/2024/07/09/the-twelve-foundations-for-a-good-life/) I decided to cover, I did manage to sneak in some other reading. Here were five of my favorites:

### 1\. [Proto](https://www.amazon.ca/Proto-Ancient-Language-Went-Global/dp/1639732586) by Laura Spinney

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/81RrOvqQXEL._SL1500_-674x1024.jpg)

Long before the Buddha taught the sutras or Homer recounted the Odyssey, there was a language that eventually became the words spoken by both. In riveting style, Spinney tells the tale of how Proto-Indo-European, a language that was never written down, became the seed for the mother tongues of almost half the human beings on Earth.

### 2\. [Power, Sex, Suicide](https://www.amazon.ca/Power-Sex-Suicide-Mitochondria-Meaning/dp/0198831900) by Nick Lane

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/51MmyqorEL._SL1170_-659x1024.jpg)

Why do we have sex? Why do we age and die? Why are there two sexes and not one, three or ten thousand? Why haven’t we met any alien life?   Biochemist Nick Lane argues that the answer to all of these questions is [mitochondria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrion). In this fascinating book he argues that these powerhouses inside cells are more than just batteries: they are a deeper explanation for many of the seeming mysteries of the universe.

### 3\. [Food Intelligence](https://www.amazon.ca/Food-Intelligence-Science-Nourishes-Harms-ebook/dp/B0DRBK1BZS?s=digital-text) by Julia Belluz and Kevin Hall

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/71zybu7NQCL._SL1500_-679x1024.jpg)

Most popular nutrition books are not worth reading. However, Kevin Hall and Julia Belluz manage to serve up one that is. In this book, they tackle the rise of ultra-processed foods (whose notoriety today is mostly owing to Kevin Hall’s original experiments) as well as the history of nutritional science (and nutritional quackery) itself.

### 4\. [Breakneck](https://www.amazon.ca/Breakneck-Chinas-Quest-Engineer-Future-ebook/dp/B0DXQGL4X9) by Dan Wang

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/81fcLdWxY-L._SL1500_-674x1024.jpg)

Wang offers a lens for understanding China, as compared to the United States. In the US, government is run by lawyers. In China, government is run by engineers. Wang extends this analysis to many facets of life, from China’s impressive economic growth to its challenges with population control and zero-COVID. Part political theory, part travelogue, I greatly enjoyed the book even though I don’t usually find broad generalizations of whole nations very helpful.

### 5\. [A Deepness in the Sky](https://www.amazon.ca/A-Deepness-in-the-Sky/dp/B07149CSFJ) by Vernor Vinge

![](https://www.scotthyoung.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/81NqYWJsFjL._SL1500_-1-672x1024.jpg)

After [Foundations](https://join.ingeniumcourses.com/foundations/) finished, I was happy to slip into some fun science fiction. Space fascists and capitalists plot against each other in the shadows as a spider-like alien race recreates the technological progress of the latter half of the twentieth century. Set thousands of years in the future, Vinge manages to make the humans feel alien and the aliens feel human.
