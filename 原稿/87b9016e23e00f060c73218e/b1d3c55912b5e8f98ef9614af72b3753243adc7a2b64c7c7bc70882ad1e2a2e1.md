In [September 2025](/fiction/silver-bird#pindaric-ode "‘<em>Silver Bird Above San Francisco</em> § Pindaric ode’, Gwern et al 2025"), I was intrigued by GPT-5's attempt at rendering my poem concept as a [Pindaric ode](!W), which is difficult because there is no generally agreed upon English verse form which could be considered equivalent (["Pindarics"](!W) notwithsntanding).
The draft was unsuccessful but interested me enough to revisit it periodically, including having GPT-5 invent an [alliterative](!W) Pindaric ode format (dubbed "pressure-cooker") to write [Apollonian #2](/fiction/lab-animals "‘Apollonian #1: The Counted & the Crowned’, Gwern et al 2025"), which was highly successful.

Sometime after, the [ChatGPT "Pulse"](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-pulse/) feature [suggested a new "strict" Pindaric ode](https://chatgpt.com/share/69b64671-3d34-8006-b7f3-dadc7245da23) format:

<div class="collapse">
> Here are two clean, practical ways to frame a modern Pindaric ode without losing the triadic “turn / counter-turn / stand” feel—one strict and audible, one freer but rhetorically locked.
>
> **Strict-triadic (accentual template)**
>
> Goal: keep the *audible* triad by mapping Greek cola → English stress-units so strophe & antistrophe are metrically identical and the epode “snaps” shorter.
>
> #. **[Strophe](!W) / [Antistrophe](!W)**: 3 cola-lines each, stress counts `[8, 7, 8]`, with a medial [caesura](!W) after stress 4.
> #. **[Epode](!W)**: 2 shorter lines `[6, 6]` with *final [catalexis](!W)* ([dactylic](!W) “drop” feel).
> #. **Sound**: alliteration > rhyme; [enjamb](!W) with restraint between cola; keep lineation visibly parallel across the **S**/**A**.
>
> [Scansion key](/style-guide#scansion) (pasteable as HTML comments):
>
> ~~~{.HTML}
> S1 L1: i8: 8: the1 BEST2 is3 WA4-ter5; // caesura after stress4
> S1 L2: i7: 7: NEXT2 GOLD3 that4 FLAMES5 by6 NIGHT7
> S1 L3: i8: 8: GODS1 GRANT2 BRIGHT3 GIFTS4; MEN5 PRAISE6 the7 GIVER8
> A1 L1: i8: 8: the1 BEST2 is3 WA4-ter5; // mirror S1 L1
> A1 L2: i7: 7: NEXT2 GOLD3 that4 FLARES5 at6 NOON7
> A1 L3: i8: 8: SONGS1 SPEND2 their3 BREATH4; CROWNS5 CLAIM6 the7 CLIMB8
> E1 L1: i6: 6: SHORT1 LINE2 SNAP3 toward4 REST5
> E1 L2: i6: 6: THEN1 FALL2 (un)SAID3 to4 GROUND5
> ~~~
>
> **Authoring checklist (1):**
>
> #. Write the strophe first; clone its stress map to the antistrophe.
> #. Mark the mid-line `||` break after stress 4 for the long lines.
> #. Let the epode’s last foot truncate (visual + acoustic release).
</div>

I liked the idea as a variant of the pressure-cooker, and had GPT-5 test it with Richard Lattimore's translation of [Pindar's "Olympia 2"](https://archive.org/details/odesofpindar035276mbp/page/n20/mode/1up "‘<em>The Odes Of Pindar</em> § Olympia 2’, Lattimore 1971") (from [_The Odes of Pindar_, 1971](https://archive.org/details/odesofpindar035276mbp "‘<em>The Odes Of Pindar</em>’, Lattimore 1971")), using my 'test translation' workflow.

# Test Translation

The pressure-cooker meter's development relied on a 'test translation' approach: have the LLM write out a semi-formal specification of a meter, then rewrite or 'translate' an *existing* poem (["Olympia 1"](!W)) into it, and evaluate where it struggled and how to fix the specification.[^test-translation]

[^test-translation]: This reminds me a little of using AlphaZero to investigate [possible changes to the rules of chess](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.04374#deepmind "‘Assessing Game Balance with AlphaZero: Exploring Alternative Rule Sets in Chess’, Tomašev et al 2020"), or [reconstructing lost](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352409X23001803 "‘The Digital Ludeme Project: Combining archaeological and computational methods for the study of ancient board games’, Crist & Soemers 2023") [historical game rules](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.13516 "‘<em>Foundations of Digital Archæoludology</em>’, Browne et al 2019").

> Concept → Formal Specification → Test Translation → Failure Analysis → Constraint Revision → Original Composition → Evaluation

The document shows this process working in real-time:

#. **Initial specification** (GPT-5's "Pulse" suggestion): 8b/7b/8b + 6b/6f
#. **Stress-test** ("Olympia 2" translation): Identifies "fake [ictus](!W "Scansion#Overview")", "short-line inversion", "stress-saturation"
#. **Refinement**: "Syllable corridors", caesura flexibility rules, prohibition on artificial stress

After several iterations, GPT-5 was satisfied, and produced [a final definition](#strict-pindaric-meter) and [test translation](#example-translation) ([comparison](#comparison)), below.

SCANSION KEY (Strict-Triadic / “Olympian 2” full-translation test)

Accentual beat-count (stress-count), not syllable-count.

Each TRIAD = Strophe (3 lines) + Antistrophe (3 lines) + Epode (2 lines).

STROPHE:     beats [8, 7, 8]
ANTISTROPHE: beats [8, 7, 8]  (same pattern as strophe)
EPODE:       beats [6, 6]

CAESURA:
- On 8-beat lines: caesura after beat 4, marked “||”.
- On 7-beat lines: caesura after beat 4, marked “||” (4+3 split).

PER-LINE MARKUP:
- Poem lines are in normal orthography.
- Immediately after each line: an HTML comment with the intended stress-beats.
- In comments: BEAT WORDS are in CAPS and numbered (1..N).
- Caesura shown in comments as “||” between beat 4 and beat 5 (or 4|3).

NAME NOTE:
- Proper names can carry a beat when listed in CAPS in the comment (even if Title Case in the poem line).

# Evaluation

The poem strikes me as somewhat clunky due to the required stresses and padding/compression (eg. ["Guest-loving, Akragas' tower, he stands || choice bud of a high line."]{.poem}), but has a lot of merit, and some great lines from pushing the LLM out of its comfort zone (contrast the weak lines in ["Human Perception at a Red Light"](/fiction/hays)), eg:

- ["She cut the mighty race down, hand by hand, || in kindred blood and wrath."]{.poem}
- ["Gold flowers burn on land, in trees; || the water feeds them too."]{.poem}
- ["They say whatever comes; they clack like crows || against Zeus' sacred bird."]{.poem}
- ["Success can wash the striver's striving clean || of strain and dust and ache."]{.poem}
- ["Grief, breaking out from quiet, dies at last || beneath fair fortune's weight."]{.poem}
- ["What's done, just or unjust, no Time the father || can ever leave undone."]{.poem}
- ["The others face blank evil, featureless, mute."]{.poem}

    (Notably better than the original: ["The rest look on a blank face of evil."]{.poem})
- ["They live without hard labor, troubling neither || earth nor sea for need."]{.poem}

## Refinements

A further revision might try to tweak the stress requirements; perhaps 6b/5b/6b for S/A and 4b/4f for the epode, or possibly 7b/6b/7b with the same corridors?
(Which would bring the density closer to what English naturally sustains.)

There is also an issue that the level of enjambment and 'flowing' feels weak here; in "Apollonian #1" and in Pindar, the triads are not necessarily cleanly separated units, but may rush onwards.[^flood]

A rule that encouraged (or even required) at least one cross-triad enjambment per pair of triads might solve the "epode as verdict" problem the specification flags, while also loosening the translation's tendency toward end-stopped heaviness.

[^flood]: In the famous [description of Horace](!W "Pindar#Horace's tribute"):

    <div class="poem">
    > A river bursts its banks and rushes down a \
    > Mountain with uncontrollable momentum, \
    > Rain-saturated, churning, chanting thunder--- \
    > There you have Pindar's style.
    </div>

# Strict Pindaric Meter

Definition for use in prompts:

~~~{.Markdown}
STRICT TRIADIC v2
English accentual triadic meter

PURPOSE
Strict English triadic form meant to preserve the audible turn / counterturn / stand
of Pindaric ode, while avoiding the main failures exposed by the Olympia 2 test:
fake ictus, short-line inversion, stress-saturation, and over-closing epodes.

BASIC UNIT
One triad = Strophe + Antistrophe + Epode.

LINE COUNTS
Strophe:    8b / 7b / 8b
Antistrophe: 8b / 7b / 8b
Epode:      6b / 6f

Notation:
- b = beat / ictus
- f = feminine ending: 1 final unstressed syllable after the last beat
- 4|4, 3|4, etc. = principal caesura position

CORRESPONDENCE RULE
- Strophe and antistrophe must correspond line-for-line in beat-count and order.
- Beat-count is strict.
- Syllable count is corridor-based, not exact.
- Matching caesural shape is preferred on the 8-beat lines.
- On the 7-beat line, caesura may float with syntax.
- Triad boundaries are metrical, not necessarily sentence boundaries.

SYLLABLE CORRIDORS
8b line: 11–15 syllables
7b line: 10–13 syllables
6b line: 8–11 syllables
6f line: 9–12 syllables

ANACRUSIS
- 0–2 initial unstressed syllables are allowed before the first beat of any line.

CAESURA RULES
8-beat line
- Default shape: 4|4
- One 3|5 or 5|3 variant is allowed per triad if syntax strongly demands it

7-beat line
- No fixed caesura
- Place the principal break at the strongest syntactic joint nearest beat 3 or 4
- 3|4 and 4|3 are both normal

6-beat line
- No fixed caesura
- A hinge near beat 3 is common, but optional

ICTUS RULES
- Beats fall only on natural lexical stress
- Articles, auxiliaries, prepositions, conjunctions, and unstressed pronouns do not bear ictus
  unless they are contrastively stressed in ordinary speech
- Proper names scan by ordinary English pronunciation
- Do not promote or suppress stress artificially to solve the line

SLACK RULES
- In 8b and 7b lines, each side of the principal caesura must contain at least one unstressed syllable
- In 6b and 6f lines, the line as a whole must contain at least two unstressed syllables
- No run of more than 3 consecutive ictuses is allowed
- Unstressed syllables may expand freely between beats, so long as the line stays inside its corridor

ENDING RULES
- One feminine ending is permitted in any line
- The second epode line is normally feminine: 6f

EPODIC RULE
- The epode should hinge or release, not necessarily conclude
- The 6f line may run syntactically into the next triad
- The epode is therefore lighter than a couplet “verdict”

NON-RULES
- Rhyme is optional and not part of the meter
- Alliteration, assonance, and syntactic parallelism are encouraged but not required
- Exact syllable-matching between strophe and antistrophe is not required

COMPOSITIONAL WARNING
- Proper names and catalogue passages are the main pressure point
- When names or lists accumulate, add syllabic slack rather than forcing extra ictus

SCANSION KEY
- Mark beats, not syllables
- Mark the principal caesura only
- Example notation:
    8b 4|4
    7b 3|4
    6f

MINIMAL TEMPLATE
Strophe:
  L1  8b
  L2  7b
  L3  8b

Antistrophe:
  L1  8b
  L2  7b
  L3  8b

Epode:
  L1  6b
  L2  6f
~~~
