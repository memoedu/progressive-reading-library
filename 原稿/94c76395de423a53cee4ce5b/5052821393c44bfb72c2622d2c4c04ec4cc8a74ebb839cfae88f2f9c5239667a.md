Sometimes in research, despite [my best search techniques](https://gwern.net/search "'Internet Search Tips', Gwern 2018"), I am unable to get a digital copy of a paper or book to upload to Libgen. This page lists my failures to date, and offers bounties for anyone who can get me a digital copy of them (often possible via a university ILL department, which are no longer accessible to me). Internet Archive scans are not accepted due to low quality, unless there are extenuating circumstances.

Unless otherwise specified, the current bounty is $10 for papers/theses/chapters & $20 for books/videos/miscellaneous; I can do PayPal or Amazon gift cards most easily, other payment methods will be more work. (I may pay bounty+costs if not unreasonable or you contact me in advance with the total.)

To claim a bounty, [contact me](https://gwern.net/me#contact) with the copy. (Payments are first-come-first served unless you contact me in advance so I can remove it from the list & avoid unnecessary duplication.)

Current count of filled bounties: **_47_**.

## [AI](https://gwern.net/fulltext#ai "Link to section: § 'AI'")

-   Unknown title, survey article cited on “Integrated Cognitive Systems”, pg92 of _On Machine Intelligence_, Donald Michie: \[paper\]
    
    > How long is it likely to be before a machine can be developed approximating to adult human standards of intellectual performance? In a recent poll \[8\], thirty-five out of forty-two people engaged in this sort of research gave estimates between ten and one hundred years. There is also fair agreement that the chief obstacles are not hardware limitations. \[8\]
    > 
    > -   8.  _European AISB Newsletter_, no. 9, 4 (196957ya)
    >         
    
-   Bledsoe, W. W. 196858ya. “Semiautomatic Facial Recognition”, Technical Report SRI Project 6693, Stanford Research Institute, Menlo Park, California. (as cited in Boyer’s [“In Memoriam: Woodrow Wilson Bledsoe”](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~boyer/bledsoe-memorial-resolution.pdf) which describes it as reporting a breakthrough in facial recognition and even outperforming humans to some degree, but does not seem to be publicly available) \[paper\]
    
    It is unclear where a copy might be found; [SRI International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SRI_International "SRI International") should have a copy, and, another likely place seems to be the [‘Woodrow “Woody” W. Bledsoe papers, 1949–47199630ya’](https://txarchives.org/utcah/finding_aids/00064.xml) held by the [University of Texas at Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Texas_at_Austin "University of Texas at Austin") archives, which lists a number of highly relevant-sounding entries; it clearly contains the face photographs Bledsoe used (“Faces, 1968–196957ya”, which is consistent with the [description in _Wired_](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.wired.com/3ef2583198d2bfbde0b36ca6d40db6155346025c.html "‘The Secret History of Facial Recognition: Sixty years ago, a sharecropper’s son invented a technology to identify faces. Then the record of his role all but vanished. Who was Woody Bledsoe, and who was he working for?’, Raviv 2020")), and I suspect that either “Papers on facial recognition, 1964–23198739ya” or “Facial recognition, 1969” contain or refer to the technical report (or a superseding document).
    
    (Unfortunately, the UT archive website does not include any duplication/scanning policy, requiring one to contact them. Likewise, SRI’s policy on accessing archive documents is unclear.)
