A free-verse poem about commercial aviation as a mundane miracle, developed through iterative collaboration testing frontier LLM poetry capabilities.
Its central contrast is between the ease of an ordinary journey and the material work, training, fuel, and historical losses that make such ease possible.
William Carlos Williams’s “so much depends” becomes a recurring statement of reliance, connecting mechanical specification with human vulnerability and wonder.

STRUCTURE & FORM:
The main poem contains six sections and 86 rendered verse lines, excluding this commentary, the abstract, section separators, the illustration, and the alternate ode.
Five internal horizontal rules divide the six sections; their “sun” annotations refer to the site’s sun-icon rendering.
The ascent and ground-observer stanzas share one section, rather than constituting two separate sections.
1. Assembly, lines 1–10: Pre-dawn work in a Renton hangar introduces a torqued bolt and the progression from wing-root to fuselage, metal to metal, souls to air.
2. Ascent and observers, lines 11–30: The aircraft passes Bay Area landmarks; one passenger watches while others rest, and a child and a walker see its ascent from the ground.
3. Perceptual and physiological danger, lines 31–37: Disorientation, the unreliable inner ear, visual deception, and hypoxia motivate the reliance on “instruments over instinct.”
4. Engineering dependence, lines 38–49: The Lands End observer’s view opens into the inventory of parts, calculations, checks, and fuel supporting the aircraft.
5. Historical cost, lines 50–70: The Wright flight leads into icing, fire, fuel exhaustion, disappearance, and test-pilot deaths, followed by the conversion of wreckage into procedural knowledge.
6. Coda, lines 71–86: The aircraft continues climbing while the refrain is distributed across the final passage, culminating in “bone to bird to geometry.”

The five complete occurrences of “so much depends” begin on lines 1, 29, 36, 41, and 50.
A sixth occurrence is fractured into the isolated “So,” “much,” and “depends” on lines 71, 79, and 86.
The coda spans 16 verse lines in five stanzas: three isolated-word stanzas interleaved with passages of seven and six lines.
The separation postpones completion of the refrain; the final word completes its grammar, while the absence of a closing period leaves the typographic ending open.

KEY FORMAL TECHNIQUES:
• Geographic specificity: San Bruno Mountain, Pacifica, Crissy Field, the Marin Headlands, Lands End, and the Farallones provide successive locations for the imagined ascent and its observers.
• Technical vocabulary: Torque, witness marks, castellated nuts, Jet-A, autopilot, and checklists bring the material and procedural supports of flight into the lyric register.
• Evolutionary allusion: The thrown “small bone” invokes the bone-to-spacecraft transition in 2001: A Space Odyssey; “bone to bird to geometry” recalls and transforms that image in the coda.
• The white-bird allusion: “Unstained” between sky and sea preserves the Bokusui echo identified in the earlier glossed drafts.
• Sonic patterning: Clipped stresses, repeated phrases, local alliteration, and selected rhymes coexist with variable line lengths.

• Suspended syntax: “So” and “much” acquire local meanings within the coda as well as remaining parts of the eventual refrain.
• Perspective shifts: An implied mechanic, passengers, ground observers, pilots, engineers, and a historical panorama produce a distributed view rather than a single continuous speaker’s experience.
• Metabolic metaphor: “Inhaling Jet-A” and “exhaling their debt as fire” figure the engine as a breathing organism. This is figurative language, not a literal account of fuel intake and exhaust.
• Sensory return: The hangar’s opening hum returns in the “checklists humming in the ear,” linking the coda to practiced human procedure.

REVISION OF THE DANGER PASSAGES:
The earlier stanza retains “clear air chokes,” whose three stressed monosyllables contrast apparent clarity with physiological danger.
The historical stanza no longer repeats that hypoxia image or the earlier movement toward unseen terrain.
Its opening three lines are now “pilots fighting wings of ice,” “burning in their straps,” and “gliding on with empty tanks,” retaining the previous 7 / 5 / 7 syllable counts.
The new sequence gives separate material forms to the danger: an impaired lifting surface, fire around a restrained body, and flight continuing after fuel exhaustion.
“Just vanishing” then leaves room for losses without a narrated explanation.
The subsequent “auger into desert” remains the passage’s explicit impact image.
The change distinguishes failures of perception and physiology in section 3 from the material and operational dangers accumulated in section 5, rather than expanding the poem into a catalogue of hazards.

• Observation and participation: Ground watchers see a distant shape, while most passengers miss the ascent; neither viewpoint reveals the full system on which the flight depends.

CRITICAL READING:
The poem moves repeatedly between a visible aircraft and the supports that are difficult to see from either cabin or coast.
Its opening narrows attention to one fastener; subsequent changes of viewpoint enlarge the scale to passengers, geography, bodily vulnerability, engineering practice, and historical loss.
“An inch of steel” functions as synecdoche for dependence, not as a complete description of the aircraft’s load paths.
“Tombstone books” brings death and codified knowledge into a single image.
The lone watching passenger provides a point of identification without becoming an omniscient witness.
The checkered salt ponds introduce a geometric view of the landscape before geometry becomes the coda’s endpoint.
The new “wings of ice” gives the bird imagery a dangerous material counterpart: the surface that should sustain flight becomes something the pilot must fight.
The coda returns to apparent ease and distance, but the danger passages have changed what that ease can mean.
“Bone to bird to geometry” closes the sequence of transformations; the isolated “depends” reconnects the distant image to the material reliance named at the beginning.

> Commercial aviation is a mundane miracle: hundreds of people nap in a pressurized tube and arrive on schedule.
> My poem, inspired by a June 2025 departure from [SFO](!W), praises that calm as an engineered faith in bolts, checklists, and invisible labor.

> A century of wreckage and regulation compresses into routine, until the refrain breaks into ["So— / much— / depends"]{.poem}, the missing period leaving dependency hanging in the air.

**[Strophe](!W) I**

At noon the span burns clean; the Gate’s red ribs keep their
measure. \
Past San Bruno Mountain the bank begins— \
seaward across Pacifica’s white surf, then bayward, \
clockwise the radius holds in clear air. \
Offshore, sparing South City the sound. \
Sutro pins the noon; the ponds flare white. \
Container cranes in Oakland bow and hold. \
Someone shades their eyes; a child finds the loop— \
silver ellipse, again, again— \
and thinks: that white bone holds lives. \
*Still rising.*

**[Antistrophe](!W) I**

Belts low and tight; a pressurized hush. \
The push sets in the seat and in the teeth. \
Three hundred sleepers tilt and lift in blue. \
Winglets scythe their arcs; the yaw damper steadies. \
Flaps stepping in; slats stowed; the nose writes circles. \
LNAV holds the lead; VNAV lifts the floor. \
Tablets map the climb; masks already down. \
One awake holds the window, will not blink. \
“Climb and maintain one-five thousand”, dry in the ear. \
The skin takes sun; the rivet lines run true. \
Phones pool small estuaries of blue. \
Inside: quiet thoroughness—checklist, crosscheck, callout. \
*Still rising.*

**[Epode](!W) I**

Years paid this silence: \
Kill Devil Hills—twelve seconds held, \
desert floors where test flights failed, \
regs written in blood; Whitcomb’s tuned wing; \
centuries of wreckage and of math— \
before we learned to sleep through heaven’s door; \
so this noon could be nothing.

**Strophe II**

At Land’s End a walker tracks the chalk \
inscribed on blue—clockwise—by that mote. \
Far side: Tam; far off: the Farallones like coins. \
Down-bay the ponds go pink; the freeway braids. \
Gulls sheer off; the craft climbs past their reach. \
A hand lifts—shade or blessing—for the climb, \
the salt-bright light, the clean exactness— \
a white bone between blue and blue, unstained, \
carrying strangers to meetings, funerals, \
a lover at a gate. \
*Still rising.*

**Antistrophe II**

Autothrottle keeps the bargain with the air. \
Hydraulics think in pounds; the pitot speaks in knots. \
Logbooks: o-rings, borescope, dye; night-shift hands. \
ETOPS is not a word here but a trust. \
In rows, the living cargo—errands and griefs; \
homecomings; a lover at a gate— \
lean back, go dark; the crew minds time and fuel. \
A finite engine compresses air, burns fuel, \
spends the heat as thrust, and keeps its covenant. \
*Still rising.*

**Epode II**

All this to make a bus with wings— \
miracle compressed to Monday— \
so that a noon can stay a noon. \
*Still rising.* \
Climb, then go level. Leave a thin white proof.
