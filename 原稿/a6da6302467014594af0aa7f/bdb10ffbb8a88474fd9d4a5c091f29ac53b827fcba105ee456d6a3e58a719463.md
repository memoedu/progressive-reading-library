> Why are there so many places for backdoors and weird machines in your “computer”?
> 
> Because big things are made of small things: your computer is in fact scores or hundreds, perhaps even thousands, of computer chips, many of which host [weird machines](https://gwern.net/turing-complete#security-implications) and are explicitly or implicitly capable of Turing-complete computations (many more powerful than desktops of bygone eras), working together to create the _illusion_ of a single ‘computer’.
> 
> Backdoors, bugs, weird machines, and security do not care about what you think—only where resources can be found and orchestrated into a computation.

If ‘the network is now the computer’, it is equally true that ‘the computer is now [a network](https://gwern.net/doc/www/fgiesen.wordpress.com/81a7f5e44ec4e9a55df9bb2dbd5535302ba06acb.html "Networks all the way down") (of computers)’. While perhaps the earliest computers like the Altair PC really did have just one ‘computer’ in them, one place where all Turing-complete tasks had to pass through, that era is long over, and a ‘computer’ is composed of countless components, many of which previously could have been a useful computer. The phrase “computer” is one of convenience, rather than a hard-and-fast distinction. What is important are the inputs and outputs: how capable is the system as a whole and what resources does it require and how can the components be reprogrammed?

# [Levels](https://gwern.net/computers#levels "Link to section: § 'Levels'")

No one cares if Google is implemented using 50 supercomputers, 50,000 mainframes, 5 million servers, or 50 million embedded/mobile processors, or [a mix of any of the above](https://gwern.net/doc/www/herbsutter.com/95e457bce002225653b71c2b3070eb8496f232ba.html "Welcome to the Jungle: Or, A Heterogeneous Supercomputer in Every Pocket") exploiting a wide variety of chips from custom “tensor processing units” to custom on-die silicon (implemented by Intel on Xeon chips for a number of its biggest customers) to FPGAs to GPUs to CPUs to still more exotic hardware like [custom YouTube encoding chips](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/hardware/2021-ranganathan.pdf#google "Warehouse-Scale Video Acceleration: Co--design and Deployment in the Wild") or prototype [D-Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-Wave_Systems "D-Wave Systems") quantum computers—as long as it is competitive with other tech corporations and can deliver its services at a reasonable cost. (A “supercomputer” these days mostly looks like a large number of rack-mounted servers with unusual numbers of GPUs & connected by unusually high-speed [InfiniBand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InfiniBand "InfiniBand") connections and is not that different from a datacenter.) Any of these pieces of hardware could support multiple weird machines on many different levels of computation depending on their dynamics. The system can be seen on many levels, each equally invalid but useful for different purposes. Are you a ‘single biological intelligence’ or a community/ecosystem of human cells/neurons/bacteria/yeast/viruses/parasites? And does it matter at all?

# [Computers All The Way Down](https://gwern.net/computers#computers-all-the-way-down "Link to section: § 'Computers All The Way Down'")

![XKCD #2166, “Stack”](https://gwern.net/doc/cs/2019-06-21-xkcd-2166-stack.png "XKCD comic caption: 'The Modern Tech Stack: Gotta feel kind of bad for nation-state hackers who spend years implanting and cultivating some hardware exploit, only to discover the entire target database is already exposed to anyone with a web browser.'; description: a mock OSI technical diagram listing all the conceptually different layers a modern computer system will be hacked on, from hardware vulnerabilities to government hackers to Bitcoin miners to the final malicious end-user themselves.")

[_XKCD_](https://xkcd.com/2166/ "Stack") #2166, “Stack”

Here is an example of the ill-defined nature of the question: on your desk or in your pocket, how many computers do you currently have? How many computers are in your “computer”? Did you think just one? Let’s take a closer look—it’s computers all the way down. You might think you have just the one large CPU occupying pride of place on your motherboard, and perhaps the GPU too, but the computational power available goes far beyond just the CPU/GPU, for a variety of reasons: [mass-produced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience_curve_effects "Experience curve effects") transistors and processor cores are so cheap now that it often makes sense to use a separate core for realtime or higher performance, for security guarantees, to avoid having to burden the main OS with a task, for compatibility with an older architecture or existing software package, because a [DSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processor "Digital signal processor") or core can be programmed faster than a more specialized [ASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Application-specific_integrated_circuit "Application-specific integrated circuit") can be created, or because it was the quickest possible solution to slap down a small CPU and they couldn’t be bothered to shave some pennies[1](https://gwern.net/computers#fn1)… (Halvar Flake dubs this phenomenon [“cheap complexity”](https://gwern.net/doc/www/rule11.tech/55f61d09c9f365355ed758e48376cbd7ab1626b0.pdf "Security, Moore’s law, and the anomaly of cheap complexity").) Whenever a peripheral or device is made, the [Wheel of Reincarnation](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.cap-lore.com/e99b2d16734b461a6cf003c101b50f0b0d2b449a.html "'The Wheel of Reincarnation', Norman Hardy") begins to turn. Further, many of these components can be used as computational elements even if they were not intended to be or try to hide that functionality. (For example, the [Commodore 64’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_64 "Commodore 64") [floppy drive’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_1541 "Commodore 1541") [CPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOS_Technology_6502 "MOS Technology 6502") running Commodore DOS was used as a source of spare compute power & for defeating copy-protection schemes, because it was as powerful; one can offload to the ‘co-processor’ tasks like computing fractals, 3D math routines for animating demos, computer chess…)

## [Down](https://gwern.net/computers#down "Link to section: § 'Down'")

Thus:

-   A common AMD/Intel **x86 CPU** has billions of transistors, devoted to many discrete components:
    
    -   Each of the >2 CPU cores can run independently, shutting on or off as necessary, and has its own private caches L1–L3 (often bigger than desktop computers’ entire RAM a few decades ago[2](https://gwern.net/computers#fn2), and likely physically bigger than their CPUs were to boot), and must be regarded as individuals. Further, the CPU as a whole is reprogrammable through microcode, such as to work around errors in the chip design, so what those CPUs compute changes.
        
    -   CPUs sport increasingly opaque features, such as the management engines/secure enclaves, like the [Intel Management Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Management_Engine "Intel Management Engine") (with a [JVM for programmability](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.slideshare.net/4c77aff9a14bada62fbb8b28313560666694f788.html "Platform Embedded Security Technology Revealed [slides]"); [Ruan 2014](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4302-6572-6 "Platform Embedded Security Technology Revealed [book]") & [SGX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_Guard_Extensions "Software Guard Extensions")), or [AMD’s Platform Security Processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMD_Platform_Security_Processor "AMD Platform Security Processor") (PSP) or [Android’s TEEs](https://gwern.net/doc/www/googleprojectzero.blogspot.com/9d4321093e59ba95ac0de63689182a31d5d423ff.html "Project Zero: Trust Issues: Exploiting TrustZone TEEs") or [Titan chips](https://gwern.net/doc/www/cloud.google.com/d77ebb7310ee305de2e68a3ac2e26b9916e8f0ec.html "Titan in depth: Security in plaintext"); these hardware modules typically are full computers in their own right, running independently of the host and able to tamper with it (and have contributed to terminological confusion, as the formerly simple [protection ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_ring "Protection ring") levels of 0–3 have had to be augmented with “ring −1”, [“ring −2”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_Management_Mode "System Management Mode"), and [“ring −3”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intel_Management_Engine#Ring_%E2%88%923_rootkit "Intel Management Engine § Ring −3 rootkit")). Intel’s ME runs a proprietary unauditable fork of [MINIX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minix "Minix") (an OS better known for its role in the creation of Linux), whose security implications concerned Google enough to launch a project to [remove MINIX from its CPUs](https://www.tomshardware.com/news/google-removing-minix-management-engine-intel,35876.html "Google Working To Remove MINIX-Based ME From Intel Platforms") & its Titan chips.
        
        -   any _[floating-point unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating-point_unit "Floating-point unit")_ may be Turing-complete through encoding into floating-point operations in the spirit of [FRACTRAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRACTRAN "FRACTRAN")[3](https://gwern.net/computers#fn3)
            
        -   the _[MMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_management_unit "Memory management unit")_ can be programmed into a page-fault weird machine driven by a CPU stub (see above)
            
-   **[DSP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_signal_processor "Digital signal processor") units, custom silicon**: ASICs for video formats like [h.264](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Video_Coding "Advanced Video Coding") probably are not Turing-complete (despite their support for complicated deltas and compression techniques which _might_ allow something like Wang tiles), but for example [Apple’s A9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_A9 "Apple A9") mobile system-on-a-chip goes far beyond simply a dual-core ARM CPU and GPU as like Intel/AMD desktop CPUs, it includes the secure enclave (a [physically separate dedicated CPU core](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.arm.com/20b78840ea6a9de174f57555056d7b4e6e78d142.html "Layered Security for Your Next SoC")), but it also includes an image co-processor, a motion/voice-recognition coprocessor (partially to support Siri), and apparently a few other cores. These ASICs are sometimes there to support AI tasks, and presumably specialize in matrix multiplications for neural networks; as recurrent neural networks are Turing-complete… Other companies have rushed to expand their system-on-chips as well, like Motorola or Qualcomm
    
    -   [Mark Ermolov](https://gwern.net/doc/www/localhost/72f82c05135c67ec10a72ec8a7b903d044d0c4f6.html "Ermolov 2026") notes that a given [system on a chip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_on_a_chip "System on a chip") (SoC) may have not just multiple CPUs, but easily 5 different CPU architectures all represented:
        
        > It’s amazing how many heterogeneous CPU cores were integrated in Intel Silvermont’s Moorefield SoC (ANN): x86, [ARC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARC_\(processor\) "ARC (processor)"), LMT, [8051](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCS-51 "MCS-51"), Audio DSP, each running own firmware and supporting [JTAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JTAG "JTAG") interface.
        
    -   even weirder special-purpose chips like the Macbook [Touch Bar’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacBook_Pro#Fourth_generation_\(Touch_Bar\) "MacBook Pro § Fourth generation (Touch Bar)") chips which run little [WatchOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WatchOS "WatchOS") apps & fingerprint authentication on a [Apple T1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_silicon#Apple_T1 "Apple silicon § Apple T1")/T2 SoC (using an ARM CPU) independent of the laptop OS, which can, of course, be [hacked](https://arstechnica.com/information-technology/2020/10/apples-t2-security-chip-has-an-unfixable-flaw/ "Apple’s T2 security chip has an unfixable flaw: Checkm8 vulnerability used to jailbreak iPhones hits Macs as well.") to [run Linux](https://gwern.net/doc/www/localhost/cf35cfc86201a6ebfee650426f52525fb9ae6764.html "qwertyoruiopz 2026").
        
        Apple’s “Always-on Processor” in its smartphones doesn’t just enable location tracking of the phones even when ‘turned off’, but [implements wakeup](https://gwern.net/doc/www/naehrdine.blogspot.com/681e03ff12a05479411d7458e5e6a368275ffbcd.html "Always-on Processor magic: How Find My works while iPhone is powered off") [for Siri](https://gwern.net/doc/www/machinelearning.apple.com/a5488a4a66f86b11887eb3d52a58c240406decc7.html "Hey Siri: An On-device DNN-powered Voice Trigger for Apple’s Personal Assistant") as well.
        
-   **motherboard BIOS & management engines** (on top of the CPU equivalents), typically with network access
    
    These management or debugging chips may be ‘accidentally’ left enabled on shipping devices, like the [Via C3 CPU’s](https://github.com/xoreaxeaxeax/rosenbridge "Hardware backdoors in some x86 CPUs") embedded ARM CPUs.
    
-   GPUs have >100s of simple **GPU cores**, which can run neural networks well (which are highly expressive or Turing-complete), or do general-purpose computation (albeit slower than the CPU)[4](https://gwern.net/computers#fn4)
    
-   **smartphones**: in addition to all the other units mentioned, there is an independent **[baseband processor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseband_processor "Baseband processor")** running a proprietary realtime OS for handling radio communications with the cellular towers/GPS/other things, or possibly more than one virtualized using [something like L4](https://gwern.net/doc/www/boston.conman.org/fd696d839b40efe71fa1f780825a595b2d924f97.html "Cellphone guts for the curious ..."). Baseband processors have been found with backdoors, in addition to all their vulnerabilities.
    
    Given ARM CPUs are used in most of these embedded applications, it’s no surprise [ARM likes to boast](https://gwern.net/doc/www/www.theregister.com/b61da630b357acb527d53549bc94d0e1ed90ef1d.html "Malware-flingers can pwn your mobile with Over-The-Air updates; German Fed-sponsored boffins: They have ways of hearing you talk") that “a modern smartphone will contain somewhere between 8 and 14 ARM processors, one of which will be the application processor (running Android or iOS or whatever), while another will be the processor for the baseband stack.”
    
-   [**SIM cards**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIM_card "SIM card") for smartphones are much more than simple memory cards recording your subscription information, as they are [smart cards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_card "Smart card") which can independently run [Java Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Java_Card "Java Card") applications (apparently [NFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near-field_communication "Near-field communication") chips may also be like this as well), somewhat like the JVM in the Intel Management Engine.
    
    Naturally, SIM cards can be hacked too and used for surveillance etc.[5](https://gwern.net/computers#fn5)
    
-   the **IO controllers** for tape drives, hard drives, flash drives, or SSD drives etc. typically all have ARM processors to run the on-disk firmware for tasks like hiding bad sectors from the operating system or accelerating [disk encryption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disk_encryption "Disk encryption") or doing [wear leveling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wear_leveling "Wear leveling"); these [can be hacked](https://gwern.net/doc/www/spritesmods.com/8937e399c67274967ac439f11307d0f7785fbce2.html "Hard disk hacking - Intro").
    
-   **network chips** do independent processing for [DMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_memory_access "Direct memory access"). (This sort of independence, in conjunction with the motherboard & CPU management engines, is why features like [Wake-on-LAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wake-on-LAN "Wake-on-LAN") for [netboot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_booting "Network booting") work.)
    
-   **USB/Thunderbolt cables/devices**, or motherboard-attached devices: an embedded processor on device is needed for negotiation of data/power protocols at the least for cables/batteries/chargers[6](https://gwern.net/computers#fn6), and may be even more heavy duty with multiple additional specialized processors themselves like WiFi adapters or keyboards or mice or [SD cards](https://www.bunniestudios.com/blog/2013/on-hacking-microsd-cards/ "On Hacking MicroSD Cards").
    
    In theory, most of these are separate and are at least prevented from directly subverting the host via DMA by in-between [IOMMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Input%E2%80%93output_memory_management_unit "Input–output memory management unit") units, but [the devil is in the details…](https://gwern.net/doc/www/googleprojectzero.blogspot.com/3f5fadc7549ae139add81fd5d410a72a407fab7c.html "Over The Air: Exploiting Broadcom's Wi-Fi Stack (Part 2)")
    
-   **monitor-embedded CPU** for control of displaying whatever the GPU sends it (part of [a tradition](https://gwern.net/doc/www/cva.stanford.edu/047f519fcc1b7d94c38c68c10de52278765a292c.pdf "'On the Design of Display Processors', Myer & Sutherland 1968 [the Wheel of Reincarnation]") going back to smart teletypes)
    
-   …
    

# [We Are Legion](https://gwern.net/computers#we-are-legion "Link to section: § 'We Are Legion'")

So a desktop or smartphone can reasonably be expected to have anywhere from 15 to several thousand “computers” (in the sense of a Turing-complete device which can be programmed in a usefully general fashion with little or no code running on the ‘official’ computer); which is computationally powerful enough to run many programs from throughout computing history; and which can be exploited by an adversary for surveillance, exfiltration, or attacks against the rest of the system.

None of this is unusual historically, as even the earliest mainframes tended to be multiple computers, with the main computer doing batch processing while additional smaller computers took care of high-speed I/O operations that would otherwise choke the main computers with interrupts.

In practice, aside from the computer security community (as all these computers are insecure and thus useful attack routes), users don’t care that our computers, under the hood, are insanely complex and more accurately seen as a motley menagerie of hundreds of computers awkwardly yoked together; as long as it is working correctly, he perceives & uses it as a single powerful computer.

Until it stops working as expected.
