<div class="abstract">
> It can be hard to see the gradual improvement of most goods over time, but I think one way to get a handle on them is to look at their *downstream* effects: all the small ordinary everyday things which nevertheless depend on obscure innovations and improving cost-performance ratios and gradually dropping costs and new material and... etc.
> All of these gradually drop the cost, drop the price, improve the quality at the same price, remove irritations or limits not explicitly noticed, or so on.
>
> It all adds up.
>
> So here is a personal list of small ways in which my ordinary everyday daily life has been getting better since the late 1980s/early 1990s (as far back as I can clearly remember these things---I am sure the list of someone growing up in the 1940s would include many hassles I've never known at all).
</div>

[Small stuff matters too.]{.margin-note} Progress is usually debated in terms of the big things like lifting the Third World out of poverty, eliminating child mortality[^tuberculosis], or [science](!W "List of years in science#2000s") & tech: discovering gravitational waves, creating world champion AIs, turning AIDS into a treatable rather than terminal disease, conquering hepatitis C, or curing deadly cancers with genetically-engineered T-cells.
But as cool as those big things are, and matters of life-and-death for many, such achievements tend to be remote from ordinary people, and not your everyday sort of thing (or so one hopes).
What about the *little* things in an ordinary life?

[^tuberculosis]: <span id="tuberculosis"></span> My grandmother casually horrified us a few years ago by going through the list of her dead siblings: 2 died as infants on the farm of 'summer diarrhea' ([bovine tuberculosis](!W "Mycobacterium bovis") from unpasteurized milk), an unremarkable fate in the area, and then 3 died in their teens--20s after moving to the city to work in textile factories. The rest died later. For comparison, *she* lost 1 child thus far out of 5 (stillbirth), and 0% of her >12 grandchildren/great-grandchildren.

![The [1980s Desktop](https://www.designboom.com/technology/evolution-desk-harvard-innovation-lab-09-30-2014/ "Harvard Innovation Lab visualizes 'The Evolution Of The Desk'")^[A similar example [with a 1991 Radio Shack ad](https://www.trendingbuffalo.com/life/uncle-steves-buffalo/everything-from-1991-radio-shack-ad-now/ "Everything from 1991 Radio Shack ad I now do with my phone"). Incidentally, there was a little-noted sequel to the [original video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uGI00HV7Cfw "Evolution Of The Desk"): ["Evolution of the City"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9YBOn-Fxg_4).]](/doc/technology/2015-11-harvard-innovationlabs-bestreviews-evolutionofthedesk-vi.jpg "Photograph of a 1980s-vintage desktop, created by the Harvard Innovation Labs in 2015 to demonstrate 'the evolution of the desk', which is cluttered with objects which have since been replaced by computer software/hardware/the Internet. [The seen and the unseen.]{.margin-note} When I think back, so many hassles have simply disappeared from my life, and nice new things appeared.
I remember my desk used to be crowded with things like dictionaries and pencil sharpeners, but between smartphones & computers, most of my desk space is now dedicated to cats.
Ordinary life had a lot of hassles too, I remembered once I started thinking about it.
("The past is a third world country", but America in the 1990s could also have used some improvement.)

These things rarely come up because so many of them are about removing irritations or creating new possibilities---dogs that do not bark, and 'the seen and the unseen'---and how quickly we forget that the status quo was not always so.
The hardest thing to see can be that which you no longer see.
Who asks why [lines got painted on roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_McCarroll#Highway_marking), when ["911"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/911_(emergency_telephone_number)#History) became a number you could call, or when [ambulances became more](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_ambulance#Move_to_on-scene_care) than just repurposed hearses for people to die quietly in?
I thought it would be interesting to try to remember the forgotten.
Limiting myself to my earliest relatively clear memories of everyday life in the mid-1990s, I still wound up making a decent-sized list of improvements to my ordinary life.^[Now, imagine if I could have extended this back another decade. Then another decade. Then another few decades... For broader metrics of increase in global well-being such as political freedoms, life expectancy, income, pollution, slavery, poverty etc., see [Our World in Data](https://ourworldindata.org/data), the [Performance Curve Database](https://pcdb.santafe.edu/) (handy for looking at [experience curve effects](!W)), the work of [Hans Rosling](!W) like [Gapminder](!W "Gapminder Foundation"), [Human Progress.org](https://humanprogress.org/) etc.]

# Improvements

Roughly divided by topic:

## Computers

With computers, it's hardly worth trying to enumerate the improvements on every dimension, and it might be easier to list the exceptions instead---if I made a list of a hundred things, someone would chime in with another one I'd forgotten, like easy rental rooms through [Airbnb](!W) or food delivery apps.
But nevertheless, here's a few:

Time keeps passing, so people tend to greatly underestimate cumulative inflation, and thus do not understand how many *real* prices have declined over time.</p> <p>So prices here are presented using my [Inflation.hs](/static/build/Inflation.hs "'InflationAdjuster', Gwern 2019") plugin with the current year real price first, and the (misleading) nominal price suffixed.</p></div>

- **Cheap**: electronics prices keep falling.

    These days, people whine endlessly online if a RAM or semiconductor shortage (something that happens every decade or so, as the industry has notorious boom-and-bust dynamics) means that they have to pay as much as they did a few years ago for something, but the long-term trends are dramatic.

    You can buy things like top-end VR headsets or smartphone, which will cost less in real terms than a Nintendo NES/Famicom did on its [1983 Japanese release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Nintendo_Entertainment_System#Famicom_release_in_Japan_(1983%E2%80%931984)) ([$150]($1983)[^inflation], or the USA "Action" release my family had, [$149.99]($1988)) or a Sony Walkman cassette player in 1979 ([$150]($1979)). Kids in 2020 can't even imagine having to pay [$50]($1990) for a new copy of [_Super Mario Bros. 3_](!W)^[Part of why I never got an SNES or _Super Mario Bros 3_, despite enjoying it a lot whenever I could play it with my friends. (The specific reason was that we had spent several years collecting soda cans to [recycle for the deposit](!W "Container-deposit legislation"), and when we'd finally saved up enough quarters to purchase something as expensive as a video game console, I somehow lost the big jar at our church, and no good Samaritan turned it in.)], [much less](https://arstechnica.com/gaming/2020/07/the-return-of-the-70-video-game-has-been-a-long-time-coming/ "The return of the $70 video game has been a long time coming: Top-end game pricing has never been lower when measured in constant dollars") [$34.99]($1983) for Atari 2600 _Centipede_---a far cry from paying [$5]($2019) these days for a great PC game during a Steam sale, or nothing at all for many of the most popular games like [_Fortnite_](!W). (And of course, often you would need to buy additional parts or gadgets or software, quickly amounting to more than the original PC or console itself...)
- the **Internet/Human Genetics/AI/VR** are now actually things

    Imagine dealing with the [2019--2023 coronavirus pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic) in 1989 instead.
- **VHS tapes**:

    - [Not Watching]{.smallcaps} crummy VHS tapes, period

    - [All Day]{.smallcaps}: because you won't be yelled at for tying up the (only) phone line

- **Hearing Aids** are a small fraction the size, available over-the-counter, have gone digital with multiple directional microphones (higher-quality, customizable, noise-reduction)^[I expect even greater things as more advanced signal processing technology becomes possible within battery life constraints, with [deep learning](/doc/ai/nn/2016-wang-2.pdf "Deep Learning Reinvents the Hearing Aid: Finally, wearers of hearing aids can pick out a voice in a crowded room"), and I find it intriguing how many hearing people eagerly took up Apple [AirPods](!W), which could do on-iPhone processing (benefiting from specialized ASICs & large phone batteries). Could hearing aids/earphones in the next decade become *better* than natural hearing, even among the young?], halved or more in price, become water-resistant, and even do tricks like Bluetooth
- [**Noise-cancelling headphones**](!W): while noise-canceling tech in some form has existed for a long time in prototypes and specialized systems of various sorts, it would take until 1989 for [Bose's](!W "Bose Corporation") first bulky noise-canceling headphone aimed at pilots for the [low low price](https://bose.fandom.com/wiki/Headphones#Bose_Aviation_Headset_Series_I) of [$995]($1989), and then consumers a decade later. On a long flight, they're as big an upgrade as first class (particularly if you can get a good movie going).
- **GPS**: not getting lost while frantically driving down a freeway^[People like to harp on cases of GPSes giving bad routes, but I wonder how much GPSes contribute to long-term improvements in car safety? It seems like GPSes *must* contribute to lower accident rates: I recall people arguing over maps or making insanely reckless last minute swerves as they suddenly realize that they are about to blow past the turn, or just driving around distractedly going "where *am* I‽"]; or anywhere else, for that matter
- **Universal Cables**: [USB](!W) cables mean that for connecting or recharging, we now only need to figure out ~10 different plugs instead of 1,000+ (one for every pairwise device combo)
- **Universal Fulltext**: most books and scientific papers can be (perhaps with [a little work](/search "'Internet Search Tips', Gwern 2018")) downloaded free---the 'universal library' came to pass, despite many peoples' best efforts
- **Universal Search**: search engines typically turn up the desired result in the first page, even if it's a book or scientific paper; one doesn't need to resort to ['meta-search engines'](!W "Metasearch engine") to cover a dozen search engines which each index a different tiny fraction of the Internet, or gradually building up enormous 20-clause Boolean queries to filter out noise
- **Universal Storage**: we no longer need to strategize which emails or photos or documents to delete to save space
- **RAM**: programmers able to assume users have 4GB RAM rather than 4MB RAM
- <div id="microsoft">**Microsoft Windows Hacking**: consumer computers in the '90s were a pain because they all ran Windows and if you ever connected them to the Internet, there were *so* many ways to get hacked or systems degraded. This is far less of an issue now.

    If you grew up in the 1990s, you didn't grow up in a paradise of software quality where everything Just Worked™ and we communally worshiped at the shrine of the Apple [HIG](!W "Human interface guidelines").
    You grew up in an era where you saved documents reflexively because Windows would [BSOD](!W) once a day or worse, random typing on a keyboard would crash or give you root on even Unix, a single packet could knock you off IRC, basic utilities like Sendmail would have backdoor passwords hardwired, and literally nothing was encrypted anywhere.

    If you managed to never install a bad [IE](!W "Internet Explorer") [toolbar](!W "Browser toolbar") (or get hacked by one of the countless IE vulnerabilities) and managed to track down the safe version of every application you installed (there being no kind of [package manager](!W)-based [app store](!W) as popularized in the '90s by Linux distros), you would still get hacked remotely by a worm (this was the [golden age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_computer_viruses_and_worms#1990%E2%80%931999) of Internet viruses/worms like [ILOVEYOU](!W)/[Code Red](!W "Code Red (computer worm)")/[Nimda](!W)/[SQL Slammer](!W)). At MS's nadir, people were estimating that [botnets](!W) were so active in [portscanning](!W) the Internet for vulnerable Windows systems that a new Windows installation would be hacked before it finished downloading security patches!

    In [January 2002](!W "Trustworthy computing#Microsoft and Trustworthy Computing") [Bill Gates issued a memo](https://www.nytimes.com/2002/01/17/business/stung-by-security-flaws-microsoft-makes-software-safety-a-top-goal.html "Stung by Security Flaws, Microsoft Makes Software Safety a Top Goal") and MS had its come-to-Jesus moment, making security a priority: switching to [Windows NT](!W) as a foundation (benefiting from [VMS](!W "OpenVMS") designs), rewriting old code in [memory-safe](!W "Memory safety") programming languages (especially [.NET](!W ".NET Framework")), investing heavily in [static program analysis](!W) tools (some developed by [MSR](!W "Microsoft Research")), adding free antivirus/firewall programs to Windows, increasing bug bounties, monitoring hacking more actively, releasing more free updates & moving to [SaaS](!W "Software as a service") models (enabling continuous updates), [adding telemetry to phone home about crashes](https://hardcoresoftware.learningbyshipping.com/p/061-bsod-to-watson-the-reliability "BSoD to Watson: The Reliability Journey: '20% or so of most frequently occurring crashes accounted for more than 80% of all crashes'—our discovery of the distribution of bugs and crashes") (resented for "privacy" reasons despite being necessary to create actually-private software) and in general investing far more money into security.

    [It succeeded.](https://www.nytimes.com/2015/11/18/technology/microsoft-once-infested-with-security-flaws-does-an-about-face.html "Microsoft Sheds Reputation as an Easy Mark for Hackers")</div>
- **Smartphones**: far too much to list... (eg. GPS, and careless smartphone photographs are higher-quality than most film cameras from a few decades ago, particularly in niches like dark scenes where smartphone night modes can [achieve things](!W "Computational photography") few or no non-digital film cameras were capable of)

    - *Universal Audio & Video Calls*: being 'out of range' scarcely exists anymore for urban & suburban dwellers; even rural areas have shockingly good coverage---one relative lives in a place so rural that he remembers when it didn't even have street addresses (you simply mailed the person + town and the post office knew where to take it), but gets a perfect 3G signal.
- [**Spaced Repetition**](/spaced-repetition "'Spaced Repetition for Efficient Learning', Gwern 2009") has escaped the cognitive psychology labs, and has been a great blessing to foreign-language learners, medical students, and many others
- [nuisance](!W "Patent troll") [**Software Patents**](!W "Software patents under United States patent law") have been expiring (eg. [GIF](!W), [arithmetic coding](!W), [MP3](!W))
- **Universal Media Availability:**

    - [Back Catalogue Access]{.smallcaps}: catching the tail end of a cartoon or movie on TV and being able to look it up instead of wondering for the rest of one's life what it was about. (I've looked up some series I watched as a kid, and I had some *strange* misconceptions about them due to my fragmented watching...)
    - having [Fansubs]{.smallcaps} available for all anime (no longer do anime clubs watch raw anime and have to debate afterwards what the plot was! Yes, that's actually how they'd watch anime back in the 1970s--1990s when fansubs were often unavailable)
    - everything is available [[Subtitled](!W "Subtitles")]{.smallcaps}[^subtitles], not just TV (accelerated by legislation making subtitle decoder chips mandatory in TVs [~1991](!W "Closed captioning#Legislative development in the U.S."))
    - most programs have a usable [FLOSS]{.smallcaps} equivalent and in some areas FLOSS is taken so for granted that new programmers are unaware they used to have to pay for even text editors/compilers or that Linux is Communism

[^subtitles]: <span id="subtitles"></span> Subtitles have had interesting second-order effects. Although they were intended solely for the hearing-impaired/deaf, they appear to have:

    - helped **spread English literacy** worldwide (there are [cross-country differences](/doc/culture/2018-micola.pdf "‘TV or not TV? The impact of subtitling on English skills’, Micola et al 2018") correlated with sub vs dub, and every English-speaking Scandinavian I've asked says that they benefited greatly from Scandinavian subtitles of English-dubbed video); and presumably likewise for other media exporters like India, South Korea, and Japan
    - **changed TV-watching culture** to the point where the [majority of 18--24yos](https://yougov.co.uk/topics/entertainment/survey-results/daily/2023/02/24/9a34f/3 "‘When watching TV shows or movies in your native language, do you generally prefer to have the subtitles on or off? § By Age’, YouGov 2023") use subtitles and this may be partially due to (and contributing to) the change in video-making style towards incomprehensible speech (because the makers know that viewers can, or do, understand it perfectly via subtitles, and will do it, thereby driving even more viewers to use subtitles).

        It has also enabled more 'distracted' or 'background' watching, where the viewer watches with the audio off (because they are in public or with other people), exemplified by cable television news channels like [CNN](!W) or social video like [TikTok](!W).
    - **helped AI progress** in [speech recognition](!W)/[synthesis](!W "speech synthesis") via the availability of ever-larger datasets of subtitles (often from pirate websites), which can be aligned with the audio of the respective video.

        This synergizes with technology like search engines or social media: it is *much* easier to search or analyze text subtitles as transcripts than to analyze raw audio/video. Similar to images: image search engines like [Google Images](!W) relied for a long time almost entirely on Internet assistive technology features like [`alt` image captions](!W "alt attribute") (because machine learning analysis of raw images was *so* poor prior to ~2019), as do the large text-image datasets used to train such famous neural net models as [CLIP](https://openai.com/index/clip/ "‘CLIP: Connecting Text and Images: We’re introducing a neural network called CLIP which efficiently learns visual concepts from natural language supervision. CLIP can be applied to any visual classification benchmark by simply providing the names of the visual categories to be recognized, similar to the “zero-shot” capabilities of GPT-2 and GPT-3’, Radford et al 2021") or [DALL·E](https://openai.com/index/dall-e/ "‘DALL·E 1: Creating Images from Text: We’ve trained a neural network called DALL·E that creates images from text captions for a wide range of concepts expressible in natural language’, Ramesh et al 2021").

## Technology

- **HVAC**: houses which are well-insulated & uniformly comfortably warm, and centrally-cooled, rather than leaky and using heaters or wall units^[I have not looked for statistics on this, but I also suspect *availability* of air conditioners in general has expanded greatly even since 1990, since I seem to recall AC not being universal, even in well-off areas. American visitors to Japan still note the contrast of how many homes either do not have AC or are reluctant to run it despite great need; and I notice in 1990s media, like early _Simpsons_, that lack of AC during hot summers is taken for granted---although when I watch it now, I immediately begin wondering why the Simpsons family *don't* have AC.] running constantly creating drafts and hot/cold spots
- **Solar Panels** & **Battery Storage**: enable local power which is more resilient than any grid could be
- **Showers**: hot water heaters increasingly heat water on demand, and do not run out (while sometimes shocking the bather)
- **Low-Flow Toilets**: in 1992, Congress & President George H. W. Bush [outlawed toilets that worked](!W "Energy Policy Act of 1992"), imposing an artificially low, arbitrarily defined limit that no working toilet could satisfy.

    Thus I enjoyed a common 1990s experience: everywhere I went in new buildings, I would suffer the minor humiliation & disgust of having to use a [low-flush toilet](!W)---if I didn't walk into a dirty stall, then I might have to reflush, or just look at brown streaks in the bowl. Low-flow toilets continued to suck for a long time afterwards.

    However, you may have noticed in new buildings or toilets, that the low-flush toilets are... working? Apparently, what happened was [that in 2002](https://slate.com/technology/2025/02/toilets-low-flow-trump-environment-cost-savings.html "‘Flushed Away: The crappy lie Americans still believe about their toilets’, Gibbs 2025"), engineers Bill Gauley & John Koeller organized the ["MaP" toilet-testing consortium](https://map-testing.com/performance-toilets-testing/map-contributors/) to create a rigorous toilet test (the existing one being bogus) and test enough toilets to name-and-shame s---y designs, and after 2 decades of R&D, low-flush toilets now work.
- **Stoves** which are increasingly safe and clean, because [induction](!W "Induction cooking")-based (rather than the perpetually dirty fire hazards of electric burners/gas stoves^[Fire safety, it is worth remembering, is an issue that affects *other* people, not just the cook. An anecdote: a local woman a year or two ago was frying food one windy day with her windows open for cooling---maybe AC was (still) too expensive?---when a freak gust splattered the oil, which immediately was set on fire by the gas burners. Perhaps because of improvements in the flammability of building materials, only the corner of the building burned before the fire department arrived and put it out---but that was too late to save her bedridden mother upstairs, who died of smoke inhalation.])
- [**Riding Lawn Mowers**](!W "Zero-turn mower") are affordable & common for rural people^[Given our acreage, I'm not sure when, if ever, [robotic lawn mowers](!W "Robotic lawn mower") will be an option, as much as I would love to stop mowing. For people with small lawns, I understand they work well, and have since at least the early 2000s.]
- **Power Tools** (such as drills, leaf blowers, or lawn mowers) are increasingly rechargeable-battery-powered, making them more reliable & quieter & less air-polluting

    - speaking of [Batteries]{.smallcaps}: batteries last long enough that they are increasingly built-in---remember how advertisements always had to say "batteries not included"?---so no more mad scrambles at Christmas for [AA](!W "AA battery") or [AAA](!W "AAA battery") batteries^[Incidentally, I was shocked to learn that AA/AAA batteries were introduced in 1907/1911, respectively. Hunting for a fresh battery of the right size is an *old* problem!] to power all the presents (which could easily add [$5]($1990)--[$10]($1990) to the immediate total cost, and would have to be replaced in a year).

      Given the exponential progress in battery costs & density, and wireless recharging becoming a consumer reality, it would not surprise me if within decades, small replaceable batteries become relegated to niches like extremely cheap disposable goods or specialty uses (eg. dollar-store toys, smoke alarms, flashlights), and young people start being confused what the difference between AA/AAA is or why one battery is rechargeable but another isn't in much the same way that young people no longer know how to write a check. (In 2019, a bursar, whose college doesn't take credit cards, showed me the giant sweepstakes-style novelty check she had to use as a prop to teach freshmen how a check is supposed to be filled out; it took coronavirus [to](/note/local-optima "'Local Optima & Greedy Choices', Gwern 2021") [force](/doc/economics/automation/index) them to accept alternatives.)
- **Cars**:

    - [Reliable & Efficient]{.smallcaps}: cars last longer and get better mileage^[This sort of reliability gain seems like it might be widespread. People love to complain that "they don't build them like they used to", but I am suspicious because most such comparisons appear to reflect [survivorship bias](!W) or are selective, and ignore improvements such as pollution or safety or variety---assuming they don't ignore inflation entirely. I understand this applies to other things like mowers and tractors and boats; I would swear my uncle spent more time *maintaining* the boat & tractor than we ever did *using* them. (Meanwhile, we treat the newer riding lawn mowers or cars like cr—p and yet they keep working.)]
    - all cars have electrified [Power Windows]{.smallcaps}; I don't remember the last time I had to physically crank down a car window.^[[Tim](https://x.com/darthtimothy/status/1361343608662630402) notes the many other improvements to the creature comforts & safety of using a car, gradually trickling down from luxury markets: remote keys, heated seats, motorized doors, backup cameras/proximity alerts, automatic headlights (even with [GPS](https://jalopnik.com/you-have-no-idea-how-insanely-complex-modern-headlights-1840509448 "You Have No Idea How Insanely Complex Modern Headlights Can Be I Mean Holy Crap They Have GPS"))... Reading about how much car safety has improved over the past 20 years is cheering, but also makes me anxious---apparently my beater car from 2000 is much more dangerous than I realized!]
    - [Electric Cars]{.smallcaps} are feasible rather than follies, and will be ordinary things in 5--10 years
    - [Self-Driving Cars]{.smallcaps} not long after that
- **Air Travel Democratized**: airplane flights no longer cost an appreciable fraction of your annual income^[Where do you think all the money came from for those pretty stewardesses & elaborate meals in those glamorous Pan Am flights? Even much more recently, that [$289]($1990) [average airfare in 1990](https://www.airlines.org/dataset/annual-round-trip-fares-and-fees-domestic/ "'Domestic Round-Trip Fares and Fees: Average Domestic Round-Trip Airfare: Nominal and Real ($2018)', America 2018") is not such a bargain when you inflation-adjust it to today.], and people can afford multiple trips a year.

    - A less appreciated consequence is the general [democratization of travel]{.smallcaps}: travel 'overseas' (not to Canada via visiting Niagara Falls) used to be far more exotic than most people realize now.

        ![Hard to travel without a [passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_passport).[^passports]](/doc/technology/2018-01-statista-numberofvaliduspassportsdividedbytotalusadultpopulationaccordingtodepartmentofstate.png)

        For example, in the beloved 1990 classic [_Home Alone_](!W), the protagonist Macaulay Culkin (who 4 years later played [Richie Rich](!W "Richie Rich (film)"), amusingly) must fight burglars targeting the most expensive house in the neighborhood when his entire wealthy family leaves for a flight to Paris---because you would *have* to be the richest family in a [rich neighborhood](!W "North Shore (Chicago)") to jet everyone off like that.
- **Laser Pointers** are no longer exotic executive toys or for planetariums, they're things you buy off eBay for \$1 for your cat. (Go crazy and buy three, to get colors beyond red; [I suggest blue or purple](/review/cat#far-from-the-madding-crowd).)
- **LED lights** are more energy-efficient, cooler & safer, smaller, turn on faster, last longer, and are brighter than incandescents or fluorescents

    - a particular boon for [Flashlights]{.smallcaps} (which have, oddly, become their own online subculture, perhaps similar to mechanical keyboard enthusiasts: smartphones replaced many minor flashlight uses, leaving just high-margin [collectors](/collecting "'What Is The Collecting Mindset?', Gwern 2021") competing with each other)
- <span id="movie-theater-seats"></span> <span id="movie-theater-seat"></span> **Movie Theater Seats** have become far more comfortable as movie theaters upgraded, now that they are forced to compete with DVDs/home-theaters (and then the Internet & video games):

   In particular, they upgraded their uncomfortable school-style stadium seats to *real* seats; and concession prices seem like they've increased less than inflation, making them less of a total ripoff; going is more convenient, as I remember having to *call* the movie theater for times or check the newspaper to see which of several theaters *might* have a screening at the right time (crazy, I know); nor am I particularly nostalgic for film rather than digital projection, where damage to the film might be noticeable, and one had to wait on the projectionist (and is that where dirty film booth windows kept coming from...?).
- **EU**: the European Union & single Euro currency make the EU easier to understand & travel in it much less tricky and expensive
- **Car Security**:

    - [Car Theft]{.smallcaps} is rarer (because [much harder](https://www.nytimes.com/2014/08/12/upshot/heres-why-stealing-cars-went-out-of-fashion.html "'Here’s Why Stealing Cars Went Out of Fashion', Josh Barro 2014-08-12")), and in particular, we [no longer have to worry](https://www.npr.org/2009/03/24/101998015/car-stereo-theft-a-dying-crime "Car Stereo Theft: A Dying Crime") about our car windows being smashed to steal our car radios^[Remember when [physically detaching your car radio](https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-decades-long-quest-to-design-a-car-stereo-that-cant-be-stolen/ "The Decades-Long Quest to Design a Car Stereo That Can't Be Stolen: Car stereos have traditionally been both valuable and easy to spot in an idle vehicle, making them a key target for thieves. Why aren’t they getting stolen nearly as much anymore?") to avoid leaving it in the car was considered a 100% normal thing to do? (For my younger readers: car radios/CD players used to come in two parts, the main body and a detachable "faceplate" which contained the display & control buttons; you would detach the faceplate and put it in a purse or something when you left your car, in the hopes that the endemic property crime everywhere would pass over your car as the main radio would be much less valuable on its own.)], or our GPSes
    - car [Security Alarms]{.smallcaps} no longer go off endlessly in parking lots. (It wouldn't've been a normal day in the suburbs in the 1990s without hearing at least one. I don't know if the car manufacturers fixed car alarms, or if everyone mutually agreed that this was not working out & stopped buying them. [It seems to be a mixture](https://www.popularmechanics.com/cars/a17477/why-the-hell-do-they-still-make-car-alarms/ "Why the Hell Do They Still Make Car Alarms? They add to noise pollution while failing to prevent car theft. It's time for them to go.") of activism, consumer backlash, & improved physical security/reduced theft leading to alarm sensitivity being pared way back.)
- **Radios** have minimal static
- **TVs** no longer have rabbit ears that require regular adjustment
- **LASIK** surgery has gone from an expensive questionable novelty to a cheap, routine, safe cosmetic surgery
- **[Plushies](!W)**: teddy bears & other toys are [much more cuddly and silky](https://www.vpostrel.com/articles/how-the-easter-bunny-got-so-soft "'How those plush Easter bunnies got so cuddly', Postrel 2015")
- **Clothing** has become almost "too cheap to meter", as the [Industrial Revolution](!W "Textile manufacture during the British Industrial Revolution") in textiles never stopped; employment in the US textile industry has cratered while garment per man-hour & per capita GDP in new textile-heavy economies like [Bangladesh](https://www.noahpinion.blog/p/bangladesh-is-the-new-asian-tiger) soars [as textile automation continues](https://www.wsj.com/articles/the-robots-are-coming-for-garment-workers-thats-good-for-the-u-s-bad-for-poor-countries-1518797631 "The Robots Are Coming for Garment Workers. That’s Good for the U.S., Bad for Poor Countries: Automation is reaching into trades that once seemed immune, transforming sweatshops in places like Bangladesh and bringing production back to America").

    In the USA, this delivered huge benefits as people are no longer wasted on tasks machines can do better and reducing environmental pollution thanks to de-industrialization & eliminating things like dye contaminant waste (see the [environmental Kuznets curve](!W "Kuznets curve") & general improvement in US environmental quality)---eg the idea of, say, darning socks is completely alien^[Have you ever noticed how much time even 'middle class' mothers used to spend sewing up pants or darning socks or organizing family clothes banks as recently as the 1970s or 1980s? Somewhere around then, mothers stopped teaching their daughters how to sew or make clothes---I think less because of any feminism and more because it no longer seems like a particularly worthwhile skill to learn, especially given pressure from other uses of time like sports or homework. My grandmother in the 1950s routinely made whole outfits---dresses and pants and socks---for her family, while my mother only sewed under considerable duress, and my sisters couldn't use a sewing machine at all (until one of them took up jewelry as a hobby as an adult). When I've asked about other families, this has been a common pattern.], and clothing companies routinely discard millions of pounds of clothes because it's cheaper than wasting scarce human labor reprocessing & selling them for a song, flooding Africa with discards.

    - materials science has produced constant visible-yet-invisible improvements in textiles yielding, among other things like plusher plushies, far better insulated (and cheaper) [winter coats]{.smallcaps}: instead of choosing between winter coats which make you look like the [Stay Puft Marshmallow Man](!W) or freezing (and if you get wet, freezing anyway) or exotic ultra-expensive garments aimed at mountain climbers, you can now buy ordinary (and much cheaper) winter coats which are amazingly thin and work even better to keep you warm---so much so that you have to be careful to not buy *too* well-insulated a coat, lest you swelter at the slightest exertion and be placed between the Scylla of overheating & the Charybdis of opening your coat to the freezing air to cool.
- **[Wheeled Luggage](!W)** no longer expensive or rare, but cheap & ubiquitous
- **Roach control**: this is one I don't remember, but apparently ~1990, [roach complaints in America fell drastically](https://www.theatlantic.com/podcasts/archive/2023/11/cockroach-bait-invention-combat/676167/) due to a new method of roach poisoning, provoking much public comment at the time but quickly becoming taken for granted
- **Learning to Bike**: starting ~1997, the traditional method for learning to bicycle, of spending weeks or months using training wheels which are gradually raised & removed (like I did), has been obsoleted by the ["balance bike"](!W) method, which [teaches children faster](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9096157/) & safer.

[^passports]: Graph from [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/804430/us-citzens-owning-a-passport/); Statista does not provide sources without a paid account, and media discussions of it usually only credit "[US Department of State](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/about-us/reports-and-statistics.html)"/"[US Census Bureau](!W)". So I investigated it in more detail to fact-check it.

    The graph appears to be calculated by taking the US Department of State's annual estimate of total number of valid US passports (["Valid Passports in Circulation (1989–2022)"](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/about-us/reports-and-statistics.html)) and dividing by the Census Bureau's reported US adult population; this would apparently assume that there must be only 1 passport per person and that passports for children or non-citizens are negligible.
    (In 2022, there were '151,814,305' valid US passports in circulation, against a total US population of ~332 million.) So overall, the graph seems accurate to me.

    While some suggest that passport holding has been inflated by various War on Terror-influenced requirements, particularly in 2006, I see only a moderate bump in 2006--2007 in what is otherwise a consistent secular trend, and the effect of such travel requirements may have been only to speed up passport acquisition that would've generally happened anyway.

## Society

- **Lower Dysfunctionality**: crime, violence, teen pregnancy, and abusive drug use in general kept falling, benefiting everyone (even those not prone to such things) through externalities

    - [urban life]{.smallcaps}: it is now reasonably safe and feasible to live in (most) big cities like NYC, Chicago, or DC---we're a long way from _[Taxi Driver](!W)_ and annual summer urban riots (outside California). This is a large part of why urban living has become so much more desirable (with the unfortunate consequence of urban inelasticity driving up rents, as the increases in desirability outpace the non-increases in availability).
- **War on Drugs Lost**: with the gradual admission that the War on Drugs was never a good idea, marijuana has been medicalized or legalized in many states, and psychedelics research is enjoying a renaissance; other drugs are increasingly treated in a more appropriate medical/rehabilitative framework
- **War On Smoking Won**: somewhere in the late 1990s, the decline of smoking accelerated and it largely disappeared from public life---restaurants have gone from smoking, to smoking sections, to non-smoking entirely; and smoking in public outdoors has become rare. Aside from any health benefits, this makes everywhere smell and look nicer. (And to the extent smoking is stimulating and pleasant---see next point about nicotine!)
- **Nicotine** [gum](!W "Nicotine gum") & [patches](!W "Nicotine patch") no longer require, absurdly, a doctor's prescription to buy, benefiting quitters and stimulant users alike (although moral panics & deeply irresponsible reporting about [adulterated black-market marijuana products](!W "Vaping-associated pulmonary injury") have produced severe retrogression on [vaping](!W))
- **Environment**: air quality in most places has continued to improve (and considering the growing evidence on the harms of air pollution, this may well be the single most important item on this whole page), [forest area has increased](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/AG.LND.FRST.ZS?locations=US "Forest area (% of land area) - United States, 1990–2016"), and more rivers are safe to fish in
- **Intellectual Property Maximalism** rollback: copyright terms have not and [probably](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2019/01/a-whole-years-worth-of-works-just-fell-into-the-public-domain/ "'Mickey Mouse will be public domain soon—here’s what that means: The Internet stopped another copyright extension without firing a shot', Lee 2019") [will](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2018/01/hollywood-says-its-not-planning-another-copyright-extension-push/ "'Why Mickey Mouse’s 1998 copyright extension probably won’t happen again: Copyrights from the 1920s will start expiring next year if Congress doesn’t act.', Lee 2018") not be indefinitely extended again to eternity to protect properties like Mickey Mouse or Sherlock Holmes, and in 2019, for the first time since 1998, [works entered the public domain](!W "2019 in public domain#Entering the public domain in the United States"); overreaching patents, both [software](!W "Alice Corp. v. CLS Bank International") and [genetic](!W "Biological patents in the United States#Myriad Genetics case"), have been rolled back---[_Steamboat Willie_](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I5pG1wbRKOg) is now public domain!
- **Board Games** have been revolutionized by the influx of [German/European-style games](!W "Eurogame"), liberating us from the monopoly of the [Amerigame](!W) [_Monopoly_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monopoly_\(game\))
- **Logistics** has become cheaper, faster, more reliable, and more convenient in every way:

    - [Advances In Internet & Computers]{.smallcaps} of course have superseded many logistical problems---the best-solved problem is the one you don't have in the first place
    - USPS introduced [[self-adhesive stamps](!W)]{.smallcaps} in the early 1990s, and by 2010, licking postage stamps was almost nonexistent (and not a moment too soon to guard against [SF extortion plots](/embryo-selection#glue-robbers-sequencing-nobelists-using-collectible-letters "'Embryo Selection For Intelligence § Glue Robbers: Sequencing Nobelists Using Collectible Letters', Gwern 2016")!)
    - [No More Coupon Scams]{.smallcaps}: most people recognize rebates are a scam (half the time I mailed one off, I never got anything back), and coupons too (if it's not expired, it's out of stock; if it's not out of stock, it is part of a weird bundle or forces you to buy more than you need; if it's not any of that, it's not worth the time it took to look through the giant pile in your Sunday paper, analyze each one, keep them, remember to have them, check the shelf, give it to the clerk...), or as we would call it these days, "dark patterns".

        The rise of discounters/warehouse stores/Internet shopping has largely obviated them and provided superior forms of price discrimination. If you see a coupon/rebate these days? Ignore and move on with your life (other than remembering what brand tried to trick you so you can avoid them in the future).
    - [No More Mattress Scams]{.smallcaps}: you can avoid ripoff mattress stores (typically owned by an exploitative oligopoly of mattress companies with massive margins) by ordering online, thanks to compact vacuum-compressed foam mattresses which can be shipped easily
    - the [Shipping Cost]{.smallcaps} of goods has plummeted
    - the [Shipping Speeds]{.smallcaps} have dramatically improved, especially for low-cost tiers: consider Christmas shopping from a mail-order company or website in 1999 vs 2019---you used to have to order in early December to hope to get something by Christmas (25 December) without spending [$30]($1999) extra on fast shipping, but now you can get free shipping as late as 19 December! ("'Same-day delivery'---what the hell is *that*?")
    - the shipped [Packages]{.smallcaps} are also nicer: initiatives like [Amazon's "Frustration-Free Packages"](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20171121005280/en/Amazon-Celebrates-10th-Holiday-Season-Frustration-Free-Packaging "Amazon Celebrates 10<sup>th</sup> Holiday Season of Frustration-Free Packaging -- An Invention That's Helped Eliminate 181,000 Tons of Packaging and 307 Million Boxes, and Given Millions of Customers Holidays Without 'Wrap Rage'") have led to a trend of fewer [clamshell](!W "Clamshell (container)") plastic packages which can't be opened [without cutting your hand](!W "Wrap rage")
- **Finance**: change comes slowly to consumer finance indeed compared to Wall Street, but we can note since the '90s the (half-assed) shift to chip cards and [faster payments, cheaper remittances & free checking](https://www.bitsaboutmoney.com/archive/financial-innovation-is-happening/ "Financial innovation is actually happening").

## Food

The quality of the average person's diet historically appears to be greatly overrated by nostalgia and ignorance^[Historical height time-series reflect poor nutrition, and things like [dental records](/beauty "'Progress In Beauty', Gwern 2016") and iodization shocks are difficult to reconcile with the idea that the pre-1950 USA or UK were some prelapsarian paradise of delicious foodstuffs stuffed with micronutrients & eaten only in moderation; the scrawny, [rickety](!W "Rickets"), [moronic](!W "Goitre") [1915 American](https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/2016/article/the-life-of-american-workers-in-1915.htm), for example, could look forward to a diet of lots and lots of lard & canned food, little fresh fruit/vegetables or meat, and all at the low low price of twice as much what a 2016 American would pay for more & better.], and, for all the moral panics, we have things pretty good now.
There are not many things in food that have gotten worse[^fries], and most have gotten better---a lot better.
Bright spots include:

- Beverages, particularly **Tea/Alcohol/Coffee**:

    - high-quality loose-leaf [tea widely available & popularized]{.smallcaps} by chains like [Teavana](!W)
    - [[microbrews](!W)/craft beers]{.smallcaps} have revolutionized beer varieties & availability (similar things could be said of wine, cider, and mead)
    - [safe McDonald's coffee]{.smallcaps} which doesn't [explode in one's lap](!W "Liebeck v. McDonald’s Restaurants") while trapped in a car & causing disfiguring third-degree burns requiring skin grafts
    - [mass market coffee]{.smallcaps} in general (McDonald's & Dunkin Donuts coffee in particular) no longer taste like 'instant char-fee' (similar to Starbucks popularizing relatively high quality coffee)
    - [Keurig](!W) & other [[Single-Serve](!W "Single-serve coffee container") coffee machines]{.smallcaps} which heat the water separately from the coffee-making are increasingly common, especially in hotels; this means that tea drinkers (like myself) can make tea which doesn't taste hopelessly like coffee due to ineradicable coffee contamination
- **Fast Food** in general has gotten much better: much tastier (compare [McDonald's post-2003 chicken sandwiches](!W "McDonald’s Premium line") with before), and safer, as we no longer worry about getting salmonella or _E. coli_ from our burgers^[Consider how you no longer see deaths from regular _E. coli_ outbreaks. As far as I can tell from US statistics, the total number of fast food-related _E. coli_ deaths in the 26 years since the [1993 Jack in the Box <em>E. coli</em> outbreak](!W) *still* do not exceed the number of deaths from that single Jack in the Box incident. (It is difficult to be sure because [_E. coli_ wasn't tracked until 1994](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3320345/) and good statistics only start ~2000.) In particular, despite the enormous amount of attention paid to [Chipotle's food safety](!W "Chipotle Mexican Grill#Food safety"), there appear to have been 0 fatalities. Fast food is [pretty safe](https://www.statnews.com/2016/01/15/chipotle-foodborne-illnesses-cdc/ "Chipotle, <em>E. coli</em>, and more: The surprising truths about food-borne illnesses"), and on the increasingly rare occasions anything happens, outbreaks are increasingly rapidly traced using [computers & cheap genome sequencing](https://www.cnn.com/2024/11/02/health/public-health-mcdonalds-e-coli-outbreak/index.html).]
- even **Mass-Market Grocery Stores** like Walmart increasingly routinely stock an enormous variety of foods, from sushi to goat cheese to kefir; and if you don't like those, you can probably find a more upscale one like Whole Foods, which behind the egregious (like shelves of homeopathy) host the exotic
- **['meat'](!W "Ketogenic diet") is a [fad](!W "Low-carbohydrate diet") diet**---since most nutrition research is [B](/replication "'The Replication Crisis: Flaws in Mainstream Science', Gwern 2010")[S](/causality "'Why Correlation Usually ≠ Causation', Gwern 2014") and most fad diets don't work either, it's good to have one which is at least delicious
- **[Sous-Vide](!W "Sous-vide")** cookers have gone from devices bought only by professional European chefs for thousands of dollars to a popular [$70]($2018) kitchen gadget
- **Fresh [Guacamole](!W)** can be easily bought due to pressure pasteurization (["Pascalization"](!W)), avoiding the inexorable spoilage of regular guacamole (and buying fresh avocados from the supermarket only to forget about it for a day and discovering it's ruined)
- **Resealable Packaging** on many foodstuffs reduces spoilage waste while also increasing convenience
- <span id="apples">**Better Apples** (not the computer kind): the tasteless mealy bitter-skinned so-called "[Red Delicious](!W)" apples are still dismayingly common, but now one can buy (in most supermarkets) far superior varieties of apples, such as [Honeycrisp](!W) apples (>1991) or [SweeTango](!W) apples (>2009), with fascinating new varieties like [Cosmic Crisp](!W) (ultra-long shelf life) or [Autumn Glory](!W) (caramel/cinnamon flavor) coming out every year[^apples] (thanks both to [improvements in breeding technology](/doc/genetics/selection/artificial/apple/2010-kean.pdf "'Besting Johnny Appleseed: With a few tricks, and a lot of patience, fruit geneticists are undoing the work of an American legend', Kean 2010") and [innovations](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2011/11/21/crunch "Crunch: Building a Better Apple") [in the](https://story.californiasunday.com/cosmic-crisp-apple-launch/ "The Launch: Inside the 'largest launch of a produce item in American history'") "club" business model eliminating free-riding & the Red Delicious's fatal race-to-the-bottom)</span>

    - [Seedless Fruit]{.smallcaps}: [Russ Roberts](https://www.econtalk.org/matt-ridley-on-how-innovation-works/) suggests 2 fruit examples: [seedless](!W "Seedless fruit") [grapes](!W "Grape#Seedless grapes") & watermelons.

        I am not entirely sure about this one's timing or quality: seedless grapes apparently go back centuries in [Turkey](!W "Sultana (grape)") and elsewhere, and were sold commercially in the USA well before the 1990s, with [red/black grapes in the 1950s](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2001-aug-15-fo-34311-story.html), and taste less strong than seeded grapes. And seedless (or [triploid](!W) hybrids) watermelon go back to 1939 in Japan (according to [Andrus 1971](https://naldc.nal.usda.gov/download/CAT71326739/PDF), citing Kihara & Nishiyama 1947/Kihara 1951, which are inaccessible), but commercialized perhaps around the 1970s--1980s in the USA.
- <span id="pineapples"></span> **Better Fresh Pineapples**: apparently, only starting 1996 could you reliably [buy a whole fresh pineapple](https://www.wired.com/story/pink-pineapples-grocery-store-pinkglow-genetically-modified/ "‘WTF Is With the Pink Pineapples at the Grocery Store‽’, Mullin 2024") at the grocery store which would taste good (itself only the latest in a [long history of pineapple progress](https://worksinprogress.co/issue/king-of-fruits/))

    - *Mangos*: [a HNer](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=43765862) claims that mangoes are another surprisingly-recent fruit that was once highly exotic and largely unobtainable pre-1990s. (I can't remember offhand when I started seeing mangoes, although I like them, so I'm not sure when this transition happened. I do feel like I remember seeing fresh mangoes in the mid-2000s as a novelty, so it's probably a valid entry, but I could use some better referencing on this one.)
- **Better Brussels Sprouts**: [Brussels sprouts](!W) [no longer taste](https://www.soci.org/Chemistry-and-Industry/CnI-Data/2010/24/Brussels-a-bittersweet-story "'Brussels: a bittersweet story', O'Driscoll, 2010-12-20") [quite so bad](https://www.npr.org/sections/thesalt/2019/10/30/773457637/from-culinary-dud-to-stud-how-dutch-plant-breeders-built-our-brussels-sprouts-bo "From Culinary Dud To Stud: How Dutch Plant Breeders Built Our Brussels Sprouts Boom") due to artificial selection against the [glucosinolates](!W)
- **Better Sausages**: you no longer need to cook sausages to death, because [trichinosis](!W) is now rare.

[^fries]: One clearcut exception would be [French fries](!W). They have gotten much worse, after [McDonald's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_McDonald%27s_products#Fries) phased out its famous franchise-defining [tallow](!W) fries in 1990.

    I am old enough to have eaten some but not to remember them; when I asked my relatives about whether the 'new' fries were as good as the old ones, their eyes lit up remembering them, and they unanimously agreed the old ones were much better. (Occasional attempts like 'duck fat fries' are good but don't match them.) They would drive to McDonald's just to get an order of fries.

    No fries sold anywhere seem to match the originals. Hobbyists have [reinvented the recipe](https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/original-mcdonalds-french-fry-recipe), possibly even the exact secret recipe, but there was much more to it than simply 'fry in beef tallow' (like air-drying the potatoes in the desert), so DIY fries are either pale imitations or a labor of love.
[^apples]: The rate at which new apple varieties come out is absurd. It seems every other time I do my grocery shopping at Walmart/Aldi, I find a new variety on the shelves due to new varieties, staggered seasonality, and spreading availability. At one point, bemused, I went around to every grocery store I could find just to check their apple selection: at the 6^th^ store, I was *still* finding a variety the others did not have! (In 2021 alone, I found "Cosmic Crisp", "WildTwist", "Ruby Frost", and "Rock-It".)

    The availability means I can't easily do blinded comparisons like [I did for mineral water](/water "'Self-Blinded Mineral Water Taste Test', Gwern 2017"), but as of August 2021, I rank the eating apples I've tried thus far as (in descending order): SweeTango, Honeycrisp, WildTwist, Granny Smith, Golden Delicious, Juici, Ruby Frost, Cosmic Crisp, Pazazz, Braeburn, Smitten, Kanzi, Jazz, Fuji, Kiku, SnapDragon, Ambrosia, Cripps Pink, Envy Rck-It, Lady Alice, SugarBee, McIntosh, Cameo, Paula Red, Ginger Gold, Red Delicious.
