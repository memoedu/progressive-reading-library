<!-- COMMENTARY

TITLE: "The Gift of the Amygdali" layers three references:

#. O. Henry's "The Gift of the Magi"—a story about gifts made useless by well-intentioned but ironic excess, which is Batman's error (iatrogenically doubling the antitoxin dose "for good measure").
#. The amygdala, the brain's fear-processing center, from Greek _amygdalē_ ("almond"): hence "a smell of almonds" when Scarecrow's toxin enters the pipes. The mock-Latin plural _amygdali_ (as if from second-declension _amygdalus_, although the proper Latin would be _amygdalae_) gives it the clinical register of Dr. Crane's academic life.
#. Brand & Yancey's _Pain: The Gift Nobody Wants_ (1993), cited in the abstract, which argues that pain is a protective signal whose absence is catastrophic— the thesis of the whole story, transposed from pain to fear.

The title thus reads as "the gift of the fear-centers": fear itself as the gift no one wants but everyone needs, destroyed by the hero who thinks he is helping.

STRUCTURE:

- Faux 1980s comic-script surface: splash portraits, narrator captions, stage directions, bold emphatics, and centered chorus cards.
- Act 1 is the trap and false antidote.
- Act 2 is proof by montage: gambling, speeding, childcare, rooftop jumping, suicide, and gang revenge.
- Act 3 reveals Batman as the last and worst exemplar.

KEY FORMAL TECHNIQUES:

- T. S. Eliot supplies both scaffold and diction: _The Waste Land_ (https://poets.org/poem/waste-land) opens the action; _The Hollow Men_ (https://poets.org/poem/hollow-men) supplies Scarecrow's "hollow"/"shadow"/"twilight kingdom", the refrains, the stars, and the final whimper.
- William Shakespeare is spliced into prose rather than quarantined as quotation: _Macbeth_ in the pipe scene; _Hamlet_ in the "infinite space" and suicide vignettes.
- 'Red' spreads both explicitly (red, rust, scarlet, crimson, cardinal) and implicitly (danger zone, stop lights, flames, Russia, tomato juice, Tylenol, fire hydrant, red sky).
- Sound play shadows the plot: say/may, rock/dock, you/new, dust/rust.
- Bird imagery threads outward from Scarecrow: cape-or-wing, Sparrow, Starling, Swift, Willet, Old Crow, cardinal.
- Water: Waterworks → pipes → water → "just water" (Sparrow) → bath (Willet) → Swift (Hudson river) → fire hydrant → the antitoxin dispersal medium. Water is the delivery system for both the disease and the cure. "Fear death by water", Eliot's Sosostris prophetically warns the reader... but Gotham fails to.
-->

# Act 1

On any ordinary summer night, [the Tombs](!W "The Tombs") [Gotham Waterworks](!W "Collect Pond") would be silent as the broken stones of their namesake, save for the gurgle of water passing through to the City of Fog.
<!-- GLOSS: "City of Fog" is a Gothamized modernist city-label, close to Eliot's foggy "Unreal City." "The Tombs" is the long-standing colloquial name for the Manhattan Detention Complex. The original 1838 jail was built on the filled-in site of Collect Pond, Manhattan's colonial-era freshwater source—a prison founded on a ruined water supply. "Tombs Gotham Waterworks" thus fuses carceral and hydrological infrastructure, prefiguring a plot in which the city's water system becomes the vehicle for both poisoning (Scarecrow's toxin) and the worse cure (Batman's antitoxin). -->

_A hallway of large pipes.
Sharp shadows hide the nooks and crannies.
The hint of a cape---or wing?---around the corner, through the mess._
<!-- GLOSS: "cape—or wing?" starts the bird field that runs from Scarecrow to Sparrow, Willet, the cardinal, and Gus's fantasy of flight. -->

But not tonight, for the **Scarecrow** is on the loose and the **Caped Crusader** hunts the darkness!

[Scarecrow]{.smallcaps} \[slowly\]: ["What are the roots that clutch, what branches grow / out of this stony rubbish? Son of man, / you cannot say."]{.poem} <!-- Free accentual triplet: WHAT are the ROOTS that CLUTCH, what BRANCHes GROW / out of this STONy RUBbish? SON of MAN, / you CANnot SAY. [_The Waste Land_; the quotation is continued far enough to let Scarecrow address Batman as "Son of man"; say/may@41] -->

_Flash to Batman, eyes asquint, watching an empty corridor intently for come what may._

[Scarecrow]{.smallcaps} \[chanting\]: ["There is shadow under this red rock, / come in under the shadow of this red rock..."]{.poem} <!-- Free accentual couplet: THERE is SHADow UNder this RED ROCK / COME in UNder the SHADow of this RED ROCK. [_The Waste Land_; the first explicit red; rock/dock@45] -->

_Batman lunges, swinging on a batarang, crossing from catwalk to catwalk, hiding under a dock._

[Scarecrow]{.smallcaps} \[loudly\]: ["I will show you something different from either / your shadow at morning striding behind you, / or your shadow at evening rising to meet you..."]{.poem} <!-- Free accentual triplet: I will SHOW you SOMEthing DIFferent from EIther / your SHADow at MORNing STRIDing beHIND you / or your SHADow at EVEning RISING to MEET you. [_The Waste Land_; you/new@49] -->

_Batman freezes, silhouetted against a cathedral-like circular window by a floodlight.
That light was new._

[Scarecrow]{.smallcaps} \[softly\]: ["I will show you... **_fear in a handful of dust_**."]{.poem} <!-- Free-verse cadence: I will SHOW you... FEAR in a HANDful of DUST. [_The Waste Land_; dust/rust@53] -->

_Scarecrow blows downwards, gently.
The powder mixes in the multitudinous pipes, making the green one red---swirls and spirals and drains---and is gone, leaving a smell of [almonds](!W "Amygdala") and a stain like rust._ <!-- _Macbeth_ splice + tail cadence: MAK1-ing2 the3 GREEN4 one5 RED6. "making the green one red" import blood-guilt into the water system; bitter 'almond' references cyanide/amygdala (originating from the Greek word _amygdalē_ or _amýgdalon_, which means "almond".) dust/rust@51/53. -->

[Batman]{.smallcaps}: "Scarecrow!"

_Closeup.
The mask of the Scarecrow, stuffed with straw---a hollow man draped over a nest of open pipes and knobs, his costume._

_Back to Scarecrow.
He looks like your favorite uncle or college professor, in another life, perhaps.
He has lifted up his palms to his face as if he were praying (were it not for the indelible traces of [umber](!W) on one)._
<!-- GLOSS: "stuffed with straw"/"a hollow man" makes the overlap with Eliot's "The Hollow Men" explicit. "A penny for the Old Guy"—the stuffed effigy burned on Guy Fawkes Night. Scarecrow is literally a stuffed effigy ("headpiece filled with straw"), making him the story's Old Guy. "Umber" is literally a 'dust', a brown earth pigment, but "indelible" imports the Lady Macbeth guilt-stain ("Out, damned spot") into the same Macbeth field as the "green one red" splice seven lines earlier. -->

_From out of nowhere the Masked Avenger plummets downward---`POW!`---roundhouse punching the Scarecrow, sending him flying.
Robin leaps, and ties him up._
<!-- GLOSS: `POW!` keeps the register camp-comic even inside the literary allusions. -->

[Batman]{.smallcaps} \[angry\]: "What have you **done‽**"

_Scarecrow throws up his bound arms in exultation.
The new floodlight catches the straw fringe of his sleeves and throws his long shadow across Batman and his twilight kingdom._

[Scarecrow]{.smallcaps} \[triumphant\]: "You're too late!
This is the center of the water network.
The fear amplifier toxin is already spreading through the city.
Gotham is **done**."
<!-- GLOSS: The shadow falling on the "twilight kingdom" compresses _The Hollow Men_'s "Falls the Shadow" and "death's twilight kingdom." It also echoes the classic radio/pulp hero "The Shadow". -->

_Closeup. He is too late, and angry. Batman scowls and shakes his head._

But Batman knows the Scarecrow is **right**.
The dose would spread in minutes, especially with [this heat wave](!W "New York City blackout of 1977")---he fears the worst!

_The vigilante's cowl suddenly lifts._

But then a thought strikes Batman!
The Scarecrow was a consummately careful researcher before he went bad...

_Batman kneels, rummaging through the bags, hurriedly searching... searching... finding!_

Would he really have created a fear amplifier toxin---without an **anti**-toxin?
<!-- GLOSS: Batman's fatal syllogism: a toxin must imply an antitoxin. any researcher working with aerosolized toxins in an enclosed space would keep an antidote for self-protection. Batman's error is not in finding the antitoxin but in the dose, perhaps making it just a toxin of a different kind... After all, "the dose makes the poison". (This is true of 'fear' or 'pain' or 'guilt' as well: you need some, but not too much. And Batman feared too little his casual dosage.) -->

_The Scarecrow staggers up, receiving a gut punch for his trouble._

[Scarecrow]{.smallcaps}: "Wait---wait, no---**ugh**!"
<!-- GLOSS: The sincerity of Scarecrow's protest is the clue that Batman is about to make things worse. -->

_Ignoring his self-serving cries, Batman holds two bags of blue powder at arm's-length, and empties them into the same pipe._
<!-- GLOSS: blue being the usual extreme opposite to red, of course, and associated with police; Batman is drawn with black and dark blues. Neurotoxins require notoriously tiny doses, and Batman lacks the Scarecrow's protective gear (whatever that might be) or his built-up tolerance, so Batman has been poisoned here, but Robin, far away, has not (yet). -->

"One to **negate** the fear toxin, and one for [**good measure**](!W "The dose makes the poison")."
<!-- GLOSS: "good measure" is the disastrous phrase; the city is ruined by over-correction. -->

[Batman]{.smallcaps}: "Pray this reduces the damage, Scarecrow."

[Scarecrow]{.smallcaps}: "You **fool**. You have no idea what you've done."

[Batman]{.smallcaps}: "I've **saved** the city."

# Act 2
<!-- GLOSS: Act 2 switches to proof by exempla. Each vignette isolates a different protective use of fear, while the red sequence shifts from named colors to culturally coded reds. The vignettes escalate in danger, from financial ruin to mass mayhem -->

<!-- GLOSS: The skyline portrait widens the frame: Gotham itself is now the subject of the experiment. -->

_The Gotham skyline._

_A well-dressed businessman looks at his cards in an elegantly scarlet underground casino, elegantly suited thugs in the background._
<!-- GLOSS: The first vignette begins with an explicit "scarlet", then lets later scenes carry red more obliquely. -->

Dan Sparrow didn't get where he is now by not taking risks or by indulging when he needed to be sharp, no sir.
Just water for him, thank you.
Tonight, he feels good about his [luck with the cards](!W "Gambler's ruin")---**very good**.
<!-- GLOSS: Dan Sparrow's prudence ("Just water") is exactly what dooms him to gambler's ruin, as he will never quit no matter how up he is; the surname also keeps the avian motif in play. -->

_A [Mustang](!W "Phaethon") streaks through the empty streets.
A closeup of the speedometer in the danger zone.
It swerves around a corner, almost flipping. Gripping the wheel, a young man grins a crazed rictus._
<!-- GLOSS: The speedometer's danger zone is implicitly red. In Greek myth, Phaethon fearlessly tried to drive Apollo's horse-drawn chariot, but cannot control the horses and is killed for his hubris. -->

After a quick dinner, Rick [Starling](!W "Starling#Distribution, habitat and movements") decided to run an errand across town.
Usually there is time for decisions and revisions at the yellow light, but not tonight.
There's so little traffic that he wonders---Do I dare?
Yes---he punches it up... and up... and up some more.
The young man feels like a god behind the wheel and is exhilarated---**what could be better** than this?
Driving through the sky while the stop lights streak by like dying stars?
<!-- GLOSS: The stop lights are another unstated red, and "dying stars" answers the opening "fading stars." Rick Starling echoes the 'star' theme and the semi-apocryphal Shakespearan/Central Park origin of starlings in Manhattan is a useful echo of recklessness. "time for decisions and revisions"/"Do I dare?" echoes "The Lovesong of J. Alfred Prufrock". -->

_The inside of a cozy apartment.
A young housewife is relaxing with her tea & talk show on TV; through the door we see a bath with steam rising and a small child in it, and flames licking a pot on a stove in a kitchen through the other door._
<!-- GLOSS: More hidden red: the stove flames. -->

Mary Willet is juggling cooking dinner with a hot bath for James and watching [Johnny Carson](!W); one must be so careful these days.
She pauses to watch one bit on Russia.
How funny! Why not watch a little longer?
**There's nothing** to worry about.
<!-- GLOSS: Willet is a water bird (hence the bath); weak T. S. Eliot "fear death by water" reference: "Fear death by water. / I see crowds of people, walking round in a ring. / Thank you. If you see dear Mrs. Equitone, / Tell her I bring the horoscope myself: / One must be so careful these days." "Russia" adds a Cold War red to the sequence, and raises the question of whether 'mutually assured deterrence' could in a world without fear, where no one worried about anything. "If we were bees, ants, or Lacedaemonian warriors, to whom personal fear does not exist and cowardice is the most shameful thing in the world, warring would go on forever. But luckily we are only men—and cowards." —Erwin Schrodinger, <i>Mind and Matter</i> -->

_A middle-aged man stretches out in a lounger on the roof of an apartment building, glass of tomato juice in hand, smoking a fat blunt, the Hudson a dark ribbon below._
<!-- GLOSS: Tomato juice is another red object before the cardinal makes the color literal again. The Hudson recovers "death by water" for this vignette through geography rather than allusion. -->

Ever since he was a child, Gus [Swift](!W "Swift (bird)") dreamed of **flying like a bird**, a king of infinite space---but he had bad eyes.
Idly, he sees a cardinal fly from roof to roof, and remembers. <!-- _Hamlet_ fragment + cadence: a1 KING2 of3 IN4-fi5-nite6 SPACE7. The rewrite swaps Hamlet's "bad dreams" for "bad eyes"; tomato juice and the cardinal keep the red/bird programs running. Swift is the consummate flight bird (they sleep on the wing, barely able to land). -->

_A small bare hotel room, lit by a single hanging bulb.
A not-unattractive woman in a dress contemplates the debris of makeup, cups, and a dead bottle of [Old Crow](!W "Old Crow (bourbon)") on her night stand._

[Ofelia](!W "Ophelia") had nothing to live for after [Hammond](!W "Prince Hamlet") left her.
She'd have crossed to that other undiscovered kingdom long ago, but to go alone into the dreams beyond had always puzzled her nerve.
Now she looks at the [Tylenol](!W "Chicago Tylenol murders") **with direct eyes**.
<!-- GLOSS: This vignette quadruple-weaves Shakespeare, T. S. Eliot, 1980s true crime, and the story's avian motif into pulp narration.
1. _Hamlet_: 'Ofelia' (Ophelia) and 'Hammond' (Hamlet); "undiscovered" ("undiscovered country"); "dreams beyond" ("what dreams may come"); "puzzled her nerve" (a hardboiled mutation of "puzzles the will"). Ophelia's traditional death by drowning also maintains the overarching water motif.
2. _The Hollow Men_: Eliot's "Those who have crossed / With direct eyes, to death's other Kingdom" is spliced directly into the text ("crossed to that other... kingdom", "with direct eyes"). Without fear, Ofelia is no longer a hollow man avoiding the gaze; she finally has "direct eyes".
3. The 1982 Chicago Tylenol murders: The laced Tylenol capsules contained potassium cyanide, which smells of almonds. This completes the loop with the Scarecrow's fear-toxin ("smell of almonds" / amygdala) from Act 1, while Tylenol's iconic branding provides another unstated consumer red.
4. The Bird Motif: Specifying "a dead bottle of Old Crow" bourbon provides the vignette's bird reference. This triple-links to the overarching avian chain (Sparrow, Willet, cardinal), to Ophelia's drowning garland of "crow-flowers" in _Hamlet_, and to the Scarecrow himself (who wears "crowskin" as his deliberate disguise in _The Hollow Men_). -->

_Outside on the streets.
It is a sultry hot summer night, and Gotham is a pressure-cooker._

Even the young men, strutting in their crimson [Converses](!W "Chuck Taylor All-Stars") to their boomboxes, lack their usual insouciance and are restless tonight.
A group of gangsters dancing in the water of a fire hydrant they've 'borrowed' look at each other.
Switchblades flick.
They move out.
They've remembered **unfinished business** elsewhere.
<!-- GLOSS: The montage ends by making the coded red explicit again: crimson shoes, a stereotypically red fire hydrant, and contaminated water feeding revenge. -->

_City skyline._

Across Gotham, weary men and women are realizing, for the first time in their lives... they live in **a world without fear**.
They're not worried about crime, retirement, or being damned to Hell.
But what they don't yet realize about this world is...

# Act 3

_The outside of the Tombs. Dark, without a star in the sky._
<!-- GLOSS: The star-motif closes here: fading stars → dying stars → no star. The phrase also leans back toward _The Hollow Men_'s dying-star imagery, marking arrival in the starless condition of "The Hollow Men"'s "death's other Kingdom" -->

The night is long.
<!-- GLOSS: Eliot-like nocturne echo of "Life is very long." ("Hollow Men") rather than a direct quotation; at minimum it continues the night/twilight/star chain. -->

_Inside.
The Scarecrow lies silently in front of Robin, looking at Batman hunched over the pipes._

[Robin]{.smallcaps}: "I can hear a siren. [Sirens](!W "Siren (mythology)")."
<!-- GLOSS: Robin is the audience surrogate, who watches helplessly. "Sirens" functions dually. Literally, it signals the cascading emergency response as Gotham's citizens kill themselves through fearlessness. Mythologically, it invokes the Sirens of the _Odyssey_ who lure sailors to their deaths—continuing the "fear death by water" motif originating from the Waterworks. Without fear, the citizens happily answer the Sirens' call, where an Odysseus was wise enough to fear them and bind himself to the mast lest he succumb. -->

_Robin looks uncertainly at Batman._

[Robin]{.smallcaps}: "Shouldn't---shouldn't we do something?"

_Batman turns away to look at Robin, the Gotham sky behind him now tinged slightly red._
<!-- GLOSS: The red has reached the sky. -->

_A siren wails close. Batman's hand moves to his grapnel gun---and falls away._

_Robin starts for the door. Batman does not follow._

[Robin]{.smallcaps}: "Batman?"

_Closeup. Batman is smiling?_

[Batman]{.smallcaps}: "No, Robin, old chum, **I'm... afraid... not**."
<!-- GLOSS: "old chum" is the decisive Adam West/Burt Ward note; the pun makes fearlessness Batman's unmaking. The ellipsis pauses enable 3 simultaneous readings: (1) conventional declining ("I'm afraid not"); (2) self-diagnosis ("I am... afraid... not"—I am no longer afraid); (3) the ellipses suggest Batman discovering his own condition in real time, the pauses registering dawning horror at his own absence of horror; camp Batman can be read as existentialist and horrifying in many ways, similar to other re-readings of older American pop culture like _Garfield Without Garfield_. Reading (3) is the cruelest and most dramatically interesting, and it's the one that makes the "old chum" Adam West register land—camp delivery of existential catastrophe. It proves the thesis of Brand & Yancey's _Pain: The Gift Nobody Wants_ at the psychological level: Batman, the ultimate avatar of hyper-vigilance and weaponized fear (who wears bat outfits to terrorize criminals etc.), is neutralized entirely by being "cured" of it. -->
