> American TV often chooses ugliness as an aesthetic, not forced by budgets.
> 
> Shows like _Ren & Stimpy_ are deliberate in their grotesqueness, creating a specific visceral response. However, other series like _The Simpsons_ stagnate, using bigger budgets not to improve but to polish, losing originality.
> 
> This split—ugliness as artistic choice versus mediocrity from laziness—highlights broader creative trends in animation.

Ugly American television animation is systematically _profoundly_ ugly, in a way that you don’t seem to see in, say, Japanese or French animation. I have mentioned a number of ugly cartoons like [_Beavis and Butt-Head_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beavis_and_Butt-Head "Beavis and Butt-Head"), but they can get much, much worse than examples like [_Rugrats_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugrats "Rugrats"), [_Ed, Ed, and Eddy_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed,_Ed,_and_Eddy "Ed, Ed, and Eddy"), [_Family Guy_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Guy "Family Guy")/[_American Dad!_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dad%21 "American Dad!")/[_Bob’s Burgers_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob%27s_Burgers "Bob's Burgers"), [_Big Mouth_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Mouth_\(American_TV_series\) "Big Mouth (American TV series)") (or anything for [Adult Swim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Swim "Adult Swim") like [_The Problem Solverz_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Problem_Solverz "The Problem Solverz")): it is difficult to imagine [Squigglevision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squigglevision "Squigglevision")[1](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#fn1) anywhere but American TV.

# [Ugly Is Valid](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#ugly-is-valid "Link to section: § 'Ugly is Valid'")

Now, ‘ugly’ is not necessarily bad; it is often a legitimate esthetic. _Ren & Stimpy_, for example, or the [Rat Fink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rat_Fink "Rat Fink") character, or the rotoscoping of the anime [_The Flowers of Evil_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flowers_of_Evil_\(manga\) "The Flowers of Evil (manga)") (or whatever [_My Neighbors the Yamadas_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Neighbors_the_Yamadas "My Neighbors the Yamadas") is), or some Soviet/Russian animation & all [“limited animation”](https://animationobsessive.substack.com/p/breaking-away-from-disney-animation#%C2%A7minimalist-movement "'Breaking Away from Disney Animation: An introduction to limited animation', Animation Obsessive 2024-09-02") (see also [R. Crumb](https://gwern.net/review/crumb "‘Review Of <em>Crumb</em>’, Gwern 2024")): I find these literally unwatchable and as a kid sometimes had to change the channel after a few minutes of _Ren & Stimpy_, and I was unable to get through more than a few episodes of _Flowers_, but I would still say that they are artistically valid and I would not wish them out of existence. These uncompromisingly pursue their style, and if these mutants or freaks of nature do not work out, they nevertheless have their place. Sometimes this might be motivated partially by budget, and becomes an artistic constraint.

# [Defectpunk](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#defectpunk "Link to section: § 'Defectpunk'")

When I watch shows like those, they are clearly different from a _Simpsons_, much less a Squigglevision. They are delighting in the esthetic experience of revulsion (cf. [“corpse meditation”](https://gwern.net/doc/japan/art/2005-kanda.pdf "'Behind the Sensationalism: Images of a Decaying Corpse in Japanese Buddhist Art', Kanda 2005")), and often exploiting the visceral horror: _Ren & Stimpy_ is teetering on the edge of sanity (and it is no surprise that [its creator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kricfalusi "John Kricfalusi") has issues). They have the courage of their esthetic convictions, and [worship Nurgle](https://gwern.net/doc/www/antifandom.com/ccd6a2a332f28ba49e094be5cfe02361f2c321af.html "Nurgle Warhammer 40k Wiki"): if you gave them more money, they would simply make it _more so_. Whereas for the others, it does seem as much laziness and low budgets (the explicit motivation for Squigglevision or the Hanna-Barbera style): if it looks like garbage to begin with, who can tell if you take even more shortcuts or copy? (If those styles are so great, why is this so idiosyncratic to _TV animation_? Why do even the movie & video game versions move as far away from the originals as possible?)

# [Ugly As Choice](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#ugly-as-choice "Link to section: § 'Ugly as Choice'")

But the other kind of ugliness is not necessary, but a choice. Low budgets do not force ugly animation or design, as most of these are hideous even as static images due to character design (which is inexpensive), and one can animate cleanly if not expensively for TV series[2](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#fn2), with time-honored tricks like careful reuse of high-quality sequences (“[the](https://gwern.net/doc/anime/2015-chang.pdf "‘The Ritualization of the ‘Bank System’ in Japanese TV Animation with Hero or Heroine’, Chang & Tseng 2015") [bank](https://gwern.net/doc/anime/eva/2021-kirkegaard-2.pdf "‘Play it Again, Hideaki: Using the Cel Bank in <em>Neon Genesis Evangelion</em>’, Kirkegaard 2021b")”) or static but gorgeous paintings by specialist [background artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Background_artists "Background artists"). And ugly animation can have high production values: [_Rick & Morty_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_%26_Morty "Rick & Morty") & [_South Park_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park "South Park") are expensive but ugly, and _The Simpsons_ animation budget escalated by many orders of magnitude going from [Matt Groening’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Groening "Matt Groening") early [_Life in Hell_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_in_Hell "Life in Hell") drawings & [Tracey Ullman show shorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simpsons_shorts "The Simpsons shorts") to the 2000s episodes (or contemporary episodes), but it still has the same ugly yellow people (although in its defense, the animation itself is now often a lot better than it has to be).[3](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#fn3)

# [Lazy Mediocrity](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#lazy-mediocrity "Link to section: § 'Lazy Mediocrity'")

What is contemptible about this second kind of ugliness is that it wallows in its own mediocrity and laziness, its self-satisfaction, its belief that it is ugly as an esthetic rather than a mindless default.

Whatever was good about the original [“comix”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_comix "Underground comix") movement which seems to be historically upstream of a lot of this, was lost on its epigones, who unable to imitate the substance, imitated some of the style, but less and less.[4](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#fn4) Such esthetic fashions running on fumes can never be refuted, they can only fade away as people get tired of [LARPing](https://gwern.net/larping "‘Why Do Hipsters Steal Stuff?’, Gwern 2022") an esthetic by mimicking its superficial features and losing its essence.

# [Proof by Stagnation](https://gwern.net/ugly-anime#proof-by-stagnation "Link to section: § 'Proof by Stagnation'")

So that suggests one way to distinguish between shows that are ugly for good reasons and for bad reasons: how do they take advantage of increasing budgets, or the equivalent, technological progress that allows for higher quality at the same budget? Do they fix the ugliness & shortcuts, or do they at least make it _more so_—or do they settle for the same thing as before? If we look at _Simpsons_, despite huge increases in both budget & animation technology from the 1980s to the 2020s, it remains about the same, just with the harsh edges sanded off—sacrificing everything that was of value about Groening’s almost hallucinogenic early artwork (like the unforgettably creepy anthropomorphic bunnies). Whereas other long-running shows that could be criticized, like [_One Piece_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece "One Piece"), have drastically upgraded their animation: the early episodes of _One Piece_ look terrible, but the [most recent episodes have jaw-dropping _sakuga_](https://fullfrontal.moe/sakuga-espresso-one-piece-1074/) (like the recent homages to rubber hose animation as the protagonist’s final superpowers), showing that they cared but were too constrained before (probably by scheduling) to do better.

Because it is not about money but taste, the best we can hope for is that the bad imitations will slowly die off as the original ugly shows fade into history and no longer become the status quo, and new TV animators will be inspired by better shows.
