The _[Structure and Interpretation of Computer Programs](!W)_. What can be said about it that hasn't been said a thousand times before? It is perhaps the single greatest computer science textbook, and surely one of the preeminent [Lisp](!W "Scheme (programming language)") or [functional programming](!W) textbooks. It is renowned for its imaginative exercises and mind-blowing techniques.

So it's pretty good.

Best of all, it's freely available and people are working through SICP all the time. It's tailor-made for autodidacts!

# Resources

There's an embarrassment of riches for the prospective SICP student.

#. Some time ago [Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=428248) got up its gumption and collectively started doing SICP. [Their wiki](https://hn-sicp.pbworks.com/w/page/9077106/FrontPage) is an excellent one-stop shop for SICP links; for example, most of the following links are redundant with their link directory. They also maintain moderately active [mailing list](https://groups.google.com/g/hacker-news-reads-sicp) and IRC channels (`irc://irc.libera.chat/#SICP`)
#. First is the book itself, [available online](https://mitp-content-server.mit.edu/books/content/sectbyfn/books_pres_0/6515/sicp.zip/sicp.html) in its entirety or as a [PDF](https://media.githubusercontent.com/media/sarabander/sicp-pdf/master/sicp.pdf); it includes all the graphics in a nicely formatted hypertext and is licensed under [CC-BY-NC](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/3.0/).

    - The site also supplies some larger programming tasks; see its ["Sample Programming Assignments"](https://mitp-content-server.mit.edu/books/content/sectbyfn/books_pres_0/6515/sicp.zip/psets/index.html) and the [OCW](!W "MIT OpenCourseWare") [materials](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-001-structure-and-interpretation-of-computer-programs-spring-2005/).
#. With the book, one can [watch the actual lectures](https://groups.csail.mit.edu/mac/classes/6.001/abelson-sussman-lectures/) of the MIT course taught by Abelson & Sussman. These too are free for the download and CC-licensed.
#. One particularly nice MIT service was allowing public access to their (now defunct?) "online tutor" (formerly `http://icampustutor.csail.mit.edu/6.001-public/`), which is basically a web interface to the slideshows & text, as well as a forms-based interface to the problem sets -- for example, a true-false question like '`(define 6 2)` is a legal Scheme expression' or 'what does `(+ -3 -5)`' evaluate to. Useful in conjunction with the larger questions that the textbook provides for checking on how well you understand the details^[Although caution can be warranted. One question asks what `(+)` evaluates to. In Haskell I know it'd evaluate to `(+)`, and I suspect it'd be an error in Scheme -- which it is in Dr. Scheme -- but the accepted answer is 0! [Common Lisp](!W) apparently also has this unprincipled hack of quietly implementing [monoidal](/sicp/1-3#monoids) behavior.].
#. One needs a Scheme environment. One of the best, and very well suited for SICP, is Dr. Scheme or PLT Scheme or [Racket](https://racket-lang.org/) (as it's now called). It is Free, and available in a Linux distro near you.
#. Perhaps one isn't using Scheme? Your solutions may not be obviously correct. You can double-check yourself by looking at [solutions in other languages](https://web.archive.org/web/20100704002207/http://www.codepoetics.com/wiki/index.php?title=Topics:SICP_in_other_languages); another wiki with solutions is [Ivan Veselov's SICP wiki](https://web.archive.org/web/20130725040434/http://sicp.org.ua/sicp).
#. A blogger named Lispy is [occasionally working](https://lispy.wordpress.com/category/scheme/) through SICP.
#. Finally, companions on the SICP are always good. I can recommend the [blogging journey of Eli Bendersky](https://eli.thegreenplace.net/tag/sicp), in which he documented working through the entirety of SICP. It's an impressive achievement, and one I look to as a model.

# My plan

I'm going to try to work through SICP in both [Haskell](!W "Haskell (programming language)") and Scheme. The 2 languages represent the premier languages of functional programming, embodying one-half of the divide between dynamic/runtime typechecking and static/compiletime typechecking.

While I do it, I, like Eli, am going to write my experiences up. This serves many purposes - it encourages me to do it because I will have tangible products; it will further my experience in writing [Markdown](!W) and exercise [Gitit](https://github.com/jgm/gitit/tree/master) functionality (ie. be a sort of "[Eating one’s own dog food](!W)"), force me to think critically about what I'm reading (lest there be unseemly many short gushing sections), and write well enough for the putative audience.
