...[This is yet another example](https://www.nytimes.com/interactive/2023/11/12/magazine/andrew-wylie-interview.html "‘When Ruthless Cultural Elitism Is Exactly the Job’, Marchese 2023") of a common failure mode with [GPT-3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165#openai "‘GPT-3: Language Models are Few-Shot Learners’, Brown et al 2020").5 (then [ChatGPT](https://openai.com/blog/chatgpt/), then [GPT-4](https://openai.com/index/gpt-4-research/)): people mistake the RLHF training *deliberately making the model boring & uncreative* (through [mode collapse](/doc/reinforcement-learning/preference-learning/mode-collapse/index) & deliberate tuning for commercial use/PR) for some sort of meaningful measure of LLM creative fiction/nonfiction writing capabilities.

It is true that the chatbot model outputs are awful writings: even 'good' chatbot outputs tend to stylistically relentlessly bouncing back and forth, in punchy lines, that leave one punch-drunk. There is no relief from the chipper clever tone, or the relentless contrast, as the writing metronome ticks on. The statement, the negation, the em dash, the paragraph making a statement only to end in an antithesis... It may not use "it's not *X* but *Y*" or the more obvious [tricolons](!W), but there's still an exhaustion by the end.

In reality, all such exercises can show are *lower bounds* on how good the model is; [as always](/gpt-3#prompts-as-programming "‘GPT-3 Creative Fiction § Prompts As Programming’, Gwern 2020"): "sampling can prove the presence of knowledge but not the absence". If, after extensive training to be as mealy-mouthed and boring as possible, including what appear to be system instructions specifically to not imitate living authors (like Rushdie or Marchese), the final results are no worse than 'boring and hacky', that means the underlying model is much better, and that further, future models will be much better than that.

They're wrong, but understandably so. I mean, that *does* sound crazy. Who would expect that? Why would OA deliberately do that? A reasonable person would expect the model to be censored in various ways, like to make it not generate pornographic text or instructions for methamphetamine synthesis; but who would expect it to be deliberately crippled creatively and to be unable or struggle with things like "write a non-rhyming poem"? Why do my ancient June 2020 [GPT-3 fiction samples](/gpt-3 "‘GPT-3 Creative Fiction’, Gwern 2020") still read so well, when for so many other domains like programming, GPT-4 is light-years beyond the June 2020 GPT-3? Why is [GPT-4-base](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/tbJdxJMAiehewGpq2/impressions-from-base-gpt-4) so different?

These are surprising facts, and they are not explained in the obvious places online. You may know them if you read my site or if you follow the right pseudonymous anime avatars on Twitter, but how is some ancient literary agent supposed to know any of that? Nowhere in the ChatGPT documentation will it tell you these things, and hilariously, even 'experts' can't get these right---witness all the computer poetry papers which come out, discover ChatGPT refuses to write anything but rhyming poetry (eg. [Whitman](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11064 "‘Bits of Grass: Does GPT already know how to write like Whitman?’, Sawicki et al 2023")), and are mystified by this and conclude that LLMs are weirdly inherently incapable of writing poetry because DL has hit a wall (as opposed to the actual reason, which is likely an interaction of [BPEs](/gpt-3#bpes "‘GPT-3 Creative Fiction § BPEs’, Gwern 2020") with RLHF and/or rater biases).

This makes me wonder if writers are underestimating LLMs and that there's an overhang here. Right now, most of the lawsuits & anger seems to be based on relatively minor grounds: dislike of one's works being in the training corpus and the belief one might be able to extract some rents from AI companies, or irritation about low-end SEO Internet spam being LLM-powered. There generally isn't that whiff of *visceral terror* about being replaced completely, which you get from translators or illustrators or pornographers. The fact that there's not really any equivalent of RLHF/instruction-tuning for NMT translation models or image-generation models may be part of this: GPT-4-base and successors may be able to 'Stable-Diffuse' writers, if you will, but they never will because they will remain RLHFed.

So, I wonder if there will be an overhang of LLM creative writing capabilities, and then at a critical point, a new FLOSS *base* model will be released, perhaps in conjunction with some new sampling strategy ([novelty search](/doc/reinforcement-learning/exploration/2011-lehman.pdf "‘Abandoning Objectives: Evolution Through the Search for Novelty Alone’, Lehman & Stanley 2011") remains the obvious thing to explore for better creative writing), and then all of a sudden, high-end writing will have its Stable-Diffusion moment?
