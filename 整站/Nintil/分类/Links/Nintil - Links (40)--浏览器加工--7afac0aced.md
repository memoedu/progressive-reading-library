DNA breaks all the time and this gets repaired. But these mechanisms degrade with aging; and while breaks themselves might not be an issue, DNA breaking a lot can lead to epigenetic damage which in turn does lead to aging (As per Sinclair's work with the ICE mice). A recent [review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1568163720302890) on that. One interesting fact I learned is that excessive upregulation of DNA repair mechanisms can actually increase DNA damage, as these mechanisms end up joining up chunks of DNA that should not be joined. Another is that -not surprised here- inflammation and senescent cells both inhibit DNA repair and increase DNA damage. Score one for the "everything is connected" view of aging.
